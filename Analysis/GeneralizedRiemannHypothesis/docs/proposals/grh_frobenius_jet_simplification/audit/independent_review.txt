# Independent stress test of the finite-jet replacement

Baseline: `VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt`, commit
`9050b04000647d588d0c78d945180abcd3b0bcf7`.
All source paths below are relative to
`Analysis/GeneralizedRiemannHypothesis/Lean/grh/src/`.

## Verdict and scope

The proposed replacement survives the checks below. The raw exponent of a
resolution theta product supplies both actual consumers of the expanded
`couplingSum` normal form, with the same valuations, factorial factors and
error terms. The necessary replacement is an interface change: retain an
exact factorization with an integral raw exponent, and prove a finite
null-moment condition for upper bounds and a first-gradient condition for
inert-prime sharpness. Do not merely erase `couplingSum` from the current
theorem statements while retaining their hypotheses.

This note is a mathematical/source-level review. It does not claim a
completed integrated Lean refactor, a successful full-project replay, or a
measured reduction in kernel-checking time.

## 1. The coefficient ring really can be nonreduced

The source uses the actual quotient

\[
 k=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}/(\pi_p),\qquad
 \pi_p=\texttt{piPrimeInt d},
\]

not the residue field of the valuation ring. This is explicit in
`FormalGroup/Exponent.lean`, `reducePiPrime` and its following instances
(lines 495–513):

* The quotient is nontrivial because `norm_piPrime_lt_one` implies that
  `piPrimeInt d` is not a unit.
* It has characteristic `p` because `piPrimeInt_dvd_natCast d` states
  `piPrimeInt d ∣ (p : valRingCp p)`.
* `reducePiPrime_eq_iff` identifies equality in the quotient with divisibility
  of the difference by `piPrimeInt d`.

The quotient is generally nonreduced. For example, choose a square root
`a` of `π_p` in `C_p`. Then `v(a)=v(π_p)/2`, so its class is nonzero in
`O/(π_p)` but its square is zero. Frobenius therefore must not be assumed
injective. Its additivity and multiplicativity are sufficient throughout
the proposed proof.

This matches the source's existing congruence infrastructure.
`Lib/General/Algebra/SeriesCongruences.lean`, `dvd_sum_pow_sub_sum`, already
passes to `R ⧸ Ideal.span {π}` for an arbitrary commutative ring `R` and
`π ∣ p`, establishes characteristic `p`, and applies Frobenius additivity.
It requires no field, reducedness, perfection or noetherianity hypothesis.
`C_dvd_iff_forall_dvd_coeff` equates constant-series divisibility with
coefficientwise divisibility. The one-variable analogue is used in
`map_reducePiPrime_eq_iff` in `Exponent.lean`.

The raw exponent lemmas to retain are
`exists_seriesToCp_eq_exponentSeries_of_inert` (line 420) and
`exists_seriesToCp_eq_exponentSeries_of_ramified` (line 446). They assert
divisibility by `PowerSeries.C (piPrimeInt d)`, exactly the strong
coefficientwise congruence needed here. Reduction only modulo the maximal
ideal would be insufficient for the upper divisibility bound.

## 2. The weights are Frobenius-fixed for the actual application

Every weight in the relevant theta products is a natural multiplicity:

* `CMLattice.rescale_thetaProd_eq_mul_expSubst`,
  `FormalGroup/ThetaProducts.lean` line 405, takes `m : I → ℕ`.
* `piPrimeInt_dvd_sum_linearSeries_sub_coupling`, line 244, takes
  `m : J → ℕ` and `IsResolution u m`.
* `Parameters.exists_hasNormalForm`, line 443, quantifies over
  `m : J → ℕ`.
* `Sharpness/Cases.lean`, `exists_hasNormalForm_resolutionForms`, uses
  `c : J → ℕ` for the tight-frame multiplicities.

After reduction these weights lie in the prime field, so
`m_i^(p^a)=m_i`. This is essential. The pair identity below does not hold
without change for arbitrary coefficients in `k` that are not fixed by
Frobenius. The scalar `λ` is allowed to be arbitrary and is kept outside
Frobenius; no equality `λ^p=λ` is used.

## 3. Finite Frobenius argument and the exact cutoff

Let `k` be any commutative characteristic-`p` ring, let

\[
 J_B(k)=k[z_1,\ldots,z_n]/(z_1,\ldots,z_n)^{B+1},
 \qquad I=(z_1,\ldots,z_n),
\]

and let `L_i∈I`. The family is finite. Write `F(x)=x^p`,
`b(U,V)=Σ_i m_i U_i V_i`, and

\[
 M_e=b(L,F^eL)=\sum_i m_i L_i^{p^e+1}.
\]

For all nonnegative `a,b`,

\[
 b(F^aL,F^bL)
 =M_{|a-b|}^{p^{\min(a,b)}}.                 \tag{1}
\]

This follows from the Frobenius homomorphism and the fixed weights. It
does not use any cancellation or injectivity. Since `L_i∈I`, `M_e=0`
automatically if `p^e+1>B`.

For `q=p^r`, the equation `Y−Y^q=L` has the unique solution in `I`

\[
 Y=\sum_{a=0}^{h-1}L^{q^a}\quad(q^h>B).      \tag{2}
\]

Existence follows by telescoping. If `D` is the difference of two
solutions, `D=D^q=D^{q^h}=0`. This uniqueness argument is valid in the
nonreduced quotient because it uses nilpotence of the variable ideal, not
injectivity of Frobenius on coefficients.

In the inert case, `q=p²`. Thus `Y` is a finite sum of even Frobenius
iterates of `L`, whereas `FY` is a finite sum of odd iterates. If every
odd `M_e` with `p^e+1≤B` vanishes, every term of `b(Y,FY)` vanishes by
(1). Hence `λ b(Y,FY)=0`.

In the ramified case, `q=p`. If every `M_e`, including `M_0`, with
`p^e+1≤B` vanishes, (1) gives both `b(Y,Y)=0` and `b(L,L)=0`, hence
`λ(b(Y,Y)−b(L,L)/2)=0`. The `e=0` hypothesis must be retained. All
actual nonsplit primes are odd; the inverse of `2` is therefore
available.

The argument also works after arbitrary integral zero-constant
substitutions `z↦Z`: the resulting `L_i(Z)` still belong to `I`. They
need not remain linear. The current upper-bound consumer assumes
divisibility of the relevant coupling series globally; this is stronger
than the finite-degree vanishing needed here. Its cutoff `e≤log_p B`
includes every needed exponent, because `p^e+1≤B` implies `p^e≤B`.

## 4. Exact factorization and integrality threshold

Keep the one-curve data supplied before the expansion into two-index
monomials:

\[
 \theta(\varpi z)=W_0(z)\exp(c\Xi(z)),
 \quad c=\varpi^{p+1}/p,
 \quad W_0,\Xi\in\mathcal O[[z]],\quad \Xi(0)=0.
\]

`FormalGroup/Exponent.lean`, `exists_rescale_theta_eq_mul_expSubst`
(line 240), provides the factorization, while the raw congruence lemmas
provide integral `Ξ`. The scaled parameter from
`Lib/CM/FormalGroup/FormalParameter.lean`, `exists_scaled_formalParam`
(line 224), already supplies `Y−Y^q≡z (mod π_p)` before its final,
stronger coefficient-formula field. That equation, rather than the
stronger infinite coefficient formula, suffices.

For a resolution, retain the literal raw exponent

\[
 \Psi(z)=\sum_i m_i\Xi(L_i(z)).
\]

`rescale_thetaProd_eq_mul_expSubst` gives the exact product
factorization, with integral prefactor. Any factor `H` in the
multiseries ring becomes integral after the same rescaling by
`rescale_mem_range_of_mem_multiSeriesRing` and is absorbed into that
prefactor. The moment congruence, which is retained unchanged, gives
`M_e≡Γ_e (mod π_p)`.

If the finite Frobenius argument shows all coefficients of `Ψ(Z)` of
degree at most `B` divisible by `π_p`, then the existing lemma
`Divisibility/NormalForm.lean`, `norm_coeff_expSubst_smul_le_one`
(line 131), directly proves integrality of the exponential in those
degrees, provided

\[
 v(c\pi_p)\ge\frac1{p-1}.
\]

The source has equality, not a newly imposed slack condition:
`Parameters.norm_pow_succ_div_mul_piPrimeInt`,
`FormalGroup/ThetaProducts.lean` line 114. Explicitly:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 &v(\varpi)&v(\pi_p)&v(c\pi_p)\\\hline
 \text{inert}&1/(p^2-1)&1&1/(p-1)\\
 \text{ramified}&1/(2p-2)&1/2&1/(p-1).
\end{array}
\]

The bound is sufficient at equality: the factorial denominators satisfy
`v_p(k!)≤k/(p−1)`. There is no loss in the derivative order, no enlarged
charge and no changed error term. Finite integrality of the exponential
is enough; its coefficients in higher degrees need not be integral.

## 5. Precise inert gradient formula and jet precision

In characteristic `p`, with the raw inert reduction

\[
 \overline\Psi=\overline\lambda\sum_i m_iY_i^{p+1},
 \qquad Y_i-Y_i^{p^2}=L_i,
\]

differentiation gives `dY_i=dL_i` and

\[
 d\overline\Psi
 =\overline\lambda\sum_i m_iY_i^p\,dL_i
 =\overline\lambda\sum_{\substack{e\ge1\\e\ {\rm odd}}}dM_e.
                                                        \tag{3}
\]

The last sum is finite in each degree: `Y_i^p` has, degreewise, exactly
the terms `L_i^{p^e}` for positive odd `e`. Differentiating the moment
congruence gives

\[
 d\overline\Psi
 =\overline\lambda\sum_{e>0\ {\rm odd}}d\overline\Gamma_e.
                                                        \tag{4}
\]

For `Γ_e=Σ_x z_x^{p^e}z_{pr(e,x)}`, its derivative in `z_y` is

\[
 \partial_y\overline\Gamma_e
 =\sum_{x:\,pr(e,x)=y}z_x^{p^e},             \tag{5}
\]

because `e>0` and the derivative of each `p^e`-power vanishes. Formula
(5) remains correct if a partner happens to equal its starting variable.

**The off-by-one point:** ordinary differentiation is not a map from
`J_B(k)` to itself. For instance, the zero class of `z^{B+1}` can have a
nonzero derivative class in degree `B`. To determine a derivative
through degree `D`, use the original series through degree `D+1`, or
the well-defined linear derivative map `J_{D+1}(k)→J_D(k)`. Equivalently,
differentiate in the full formal-series ring and then truncate. To
establish the selected leading gradient at degree `p^{e_τ}`, use raw
exponent precision `p^{e_τ}+1`.

This is not a derivative-order loss repeated once for each later
differentiation. The source's generic recurrence retains the exact
formal series throughout; only their residues are replaced by the
selected gradient product. See the next section.

For completeness, the ramified analogue is

\[
 d\overline\Psi=
 \overline\lambda\left(\tfrac12dM_0+
             2\sum_{e\ge1}dM_e\right).
\]

No ramified sharpness consumer currently needs this extra statement.

## 6. Unit leading coefficient and the unchanged sharpness argument

For the canonical scaled parameter, `Y=z+O(z^{p²})` modulo `π_p` in
the inert case. Thus

\[
 [z^{p+1}]\Xi\equiv\lambda\pmod{\pi_p}.
\]

`FormalGroup/Exponent.lean`,
`CMLattice.norm_coeff_exponentSeries_eq_one` (line 566), proves that
this coefficient has norm one. Since `v(π_p)>0`, `λ` is a unit. This
derivation precedes and bypasses the expanded normal form; it must not
be obtained by invoking the final theorem one intends to remove.

Now let `Φ₀,e` be the existing `IsCouplingFamily`. For a selected
`τ∈Φ₀`, put `y_τ=pr(e_τ,τ)`. Kill all variables whose places are
outside `Φ₀`, then reduce to the residue field of `O`. Equations
(4)–(5) and the existing no-earlier-partner condition imply

\[
 \left.\overline{\partial_{y_\tau}\Psi}\right|_{\Phi_0}
 =\overline\lambda\,z_\tau^{p^{e_\tau}}
       +\text{terms of total degree }>p^{e_\tau}.         \tag{6}
\]

This is exactly the `IsLowestTerm` input proved for `couplingSum` in
`Sharpness/Couplings.lean`,
`IsCouplingFamily.isLowestTerm_pderiv_couplingSum` (line 201). Its
downstream use needs only (6) with a unit coefficient, not the full
support of `Ψ`. The coefficient can depend on `τ`, as after scaling;
the product of these coefficients is still a unit.

The differential part is already generic in the source. If
`F=W exp(c Ψ)`, with integral unit `W`, integral zero-constant `Ψ`,
and `|c|>1`, then

\[
 \partial F=(c\,\partial\Psi+B)F\qquad(B\text{ integral}).
\]

For integral `g`,

\[
 \partial(gF)=c\,g'F,\qquad
 g'=g\,\partial\Psi+c^{-1}(\partial g+gB).
\]

Since `c^{-1}` belongs to the maximal ideal, reduction gives
`ḡ'=ḡ·(∂Ψ)̄`. Therefore `j` successive derivatives produce an
integral multiplier whose residue is the product of the `j` selected
gradients. There is no need to know `j` successive derivatives of
the finite-jet approximation. This is already proved by
`exists_pderiv_mul_expSubst`, `exists_pderiv_mul_eq_smul` and
`exists_jet_mul_prod_X` (lines 108, 126, 148).

Given a cancelling-copy product `U` with `U(0)=1`, the coefficient
norm conclusion of `IsCouplingFamily.norm_coeff_pairExponent`
(line 358) therefore remains exactly

\[
 |\nu''!|\,
 \left|[z'^{\nu'}z''^{\nu''}]
      F(z'+z'')\prod_{c\ne0}F(\delta_cz')\right|
 =|c|^{\sum_\tau j_\tau}.
\]

The source's subsequent factorial/Legendre estimates, the least
valuation bound and its global error budget remain unchanged. This
argument works with the existing six-copy norm cancellation or with
the separately proposed two-copy cancellation.

## 7. Rescaling: transport the consumer contracts

For integral `α`, variable rescaling gives

\[
 F(\varpi\alpha z)=W(\alpha z)\exp(c\Psi(\alpha z)).
\]

The upper condition is preserved because
`Γ_e(αZ)=α^{p^e+1}Γ_e(Z)`. Inert gradient coefficients become
`λ α^{p^e+1}`. When `α` is a unit, these remain units, and (6) is
preserved.

If `ϖ'=αϖ` has the same norm as `ϖ`, then `α` is a unit. To normalize
the scalar as `c'=ϖ'^{p+1}/p`, use

\[
 W'(z)=W(\alpha z),\quad
 \Psi'(z)=\alpha^{-(p+1)}\Psi(\alpha z),\quad
 c'=\alpha^{p+1}c.
\]

The new selected coefficient is
`λ α^{p^e-p}`, still a unit. The null-moment contract is also preserved.

Do **not** assert that the normalized one-variable resolvent remains
unchanged under such rescaling. If `Y−Y^q=z`, then
`Y'(z)=Y(αz)/α` satisfies

\[
 Y'-\alpha^{q-1}(Y')^q=z,
\]

which generally differs from `Y'−(Y')^q=z`. Either retain the
canonical `ϖ` and transport the consumer contracts, or allow this
twisted resolvent. The first option is shorter. The present
`HasNormalForm.exists_of_norm_eq` has no external caller in the
checked tree, but preserving its natural analogue poses no difficulty.

## 8. Uniformity in resolutions and actual consumers

The canonical one-curve `ϖ,Ξ,λ` can be chosen once for the CM curve and
the prime. The multivariate raw exponent `Ψ=Σ_i m_iΞ(L_i)` is allowed
to depend on the resolution. The checked consumers do not require
these raw exponents to agree for different resolutions.

`Vacuum/Divisibility.lean`, `exists_hasNormalForm_jetSeries`
(lines 170–203), expresses each standard datum as one theta product
times an integral multiseries factor `H`; it then rescales variables
by the integral scalar `ι_p(c·periodFactor)`. There is no step that
forms a sum of unrelated raw exponential factorizations. Thus the
resolution-dependent factorization and its upper certificate suffice.

`Divisibility/Count.lean` uses the normal-form interface at its final
charged-direction substitutions. Retain the charged frame construction
and `dvd_subst_coupling_directionSubst_of_charges`; replace only the
call to `HasNormalForm.norm_coeff_subst_le_one` by the finite
null-moment integrality theorem. The numerical argument is unaffected.

`Sharpness/Cases.lean`, `exists_hasNormalForm_resolutionForms`
(lines 245–272), obtains the factorization for a resolution and
rescales variables by the unit `taylorScale`. Section 7 proves exactly
the required transport. The raw `W` is a unit because the theta
product has constant term one; the generic source lemma
`isUnit_of_rescale_thetaProd_eq` already establishes this without a
coupling-sum assumption.

## 9. Why this does not repeat the existing proposals

The closest archived proposal is
`docs/proposals/cm_norm_theta_simplification/report.tex`. Its lines
194–195 explicitly say that it keeps the normal form, coupling
coefficient identity, factorial losses and eventual main term.
Its coupling section assumes the existing normal-form hypotheses
(around lines 582–608), and improves the number of cancelling copies.
The present proposal changes those hypotheses and eliminates the
two-index exponent representation, while using whatever cancelling
product that proposal supplies.

The discrete Hecke/Smith, integral flag-row, signed-gain, Gram-adjoint,
orbital-exchange, sparse-cutoff, twist-selection and arithmetic-label
proposals concern different interfaces. The finite-jet argument keeps
their improved downstream statements and does not re-propose them.
No global novelty claim is made for Frobenius, nilpotent resolvents or
lowest-term multiplication; the contribution is the weaker sufficient
interface and the proof that both local consumers can use it without
changing any bound.

## 10. Recommended integration acceptance criteria

1. Prove the finite characteristic-`p` pairing and resolvent lemmas over
   an arbitrary commutative ring, with natural weights explicitly.
2. Package the canonical one-curve raw exponent before any expanded
   `primePowerSeries`/`couplingSum` theorem.
3. Prove upper divisibility through a requested degree, using `O/(π_p)`.
4. Prove inert gradient lowest terms, with one additional degree of
   exponent precision, and then pass to the residue field for units.
5. Generalize the sharpness coefficient theorem from `couplingSum`
   to an arbitrary integral exponent with these lowest-gradient terms.
6. Migrate the three consumer files above, preserve their final
   numerical statements, and only then remove unused expansion and
   infinite-sum lemmas.

The distinction between the quotient `O/(π_p)` and the residue field,
the natural weights, the ramified zeroth moment, and the one-degree
derivative precision are the material correctness checks. All are
satisfied by the inspected source inputs.

## 11. Independent review of the finite regression checker

Reviewed
`deliverable/grh_frobenius_jet_simplification/checks/check_finite_jets.py`
without changing it or adding tests. No indexing or algebraic error was
found. `length_for(q,B)` returns a length with `q^h>B`, including the
empty resolvent for `B=0`. The inert pairing indexes
`abs((2a+1)−2b)` and `min(2a+1,2b)` match the pair of odd/even
Frobenius iterates. The ramified correction is `−M_0/2`. Gradient tests
compute the exponent in degree `B+1`, differentiate to degree `B`, and
include exactly the positive exponents satisfying `p^e≤B` (odd only
in the inert case). The deliberately non-Frobenius-fixed scalar `λ`
stays outside all Frobenius operations.

The reported total is arithmetically consistent: 29 parameter cases
times three trials, with 16 pairing, six resolvent, two uniqueness,
one inert, one ramified and four gradient assertion groups per trial,
plus four negative controls, equals 2,614 groups. These are exact finite
regressions, not a proof of the universal theorem or a full-project
replay.

One boundary when pruning declarations: `Sharpness/OneCurve.lean:298`
still uses the final coefficient-congruence field of
`exists_scaled_formalParam`. The new local-exponent route can ignore
that field, but it should be retained unless this separate consumer is
also adapted. The two-index `primePowerSeries` coefficient machinery
itself occurs only in `Lib/General/Algebra/SeriesCongruences.lean` and
`FormalGroup/Exponent.lean` in the checked tree.
