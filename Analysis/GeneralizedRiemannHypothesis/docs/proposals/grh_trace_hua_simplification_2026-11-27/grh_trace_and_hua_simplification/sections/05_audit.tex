\section{Comparison with the existing proposal bundles}\label{sec:audit}

The exclusion audit uses the same source pin as the mathematical review.
The proposal directory contains 28 bundles, 43 TeX files, and 136 files with
extensions \code{.tex}, \code{.md}, or \code{.txt}. The primary-text screening
covers 92 papers, README summaries, and implementation plans. The archive records
their hashes and a 28-row comparison matrix.
A final check of a later revision added three new bundles to the exclusion review;
Section~\ref{sec:late-audit} records that update separately.

Every primary README summary was read. TeX headings and named future directions
were screened; relevant statements and future directions were read directly;
the complete primary text was searched for the two mechanisms and source targets.
The closest proposals were compared by their mathematical contracts, not just
their titles. This is a targeted semantic audit of the requested corpus, not
a line-by-line verification of every supplied proposal or a priority search in
the mathematical literature.

\subsection{The closest existing work is about different operators}

The existing \proposal{gram_adjoint_simplification}{Gram-adjoint proposal}
uses an inverse Weyl--Fock Gram matrix in an integral comparison operator.
The branching part of \proposal{grh_simplification}{the compact simplification bundle}
uses a calibrated adjoint, Schur's lemma, and a highest vector.
Neither retains the negative dual-frame Gram matrix in the real-place volume
ratio or integrates its fixed-column determinant. The occurrence of ``Gram''
or ``Schur'' in both discussions does not identify the mathematical mechanism.

The \proposal{discrete_hecke_simplification}{discrete Hecke} and
\proposal{edge_hecke_simplification}{edge Hecke} proposals shorten local
elementary-divisor arguments. They do not reconstruct the cuspidal trace from
line-parahoric operators. The proposed trace theorem changes the premise of
\code{LineRelation.trace_mul_mean_eq}, in a different part of the proof.

The \proposal{projective_incidence_grh}{projective incidence proposal}
changes a finite-field second moment, and
\proposal{centered_theta_lift_simplification}{centered theta-lift completion}
changes the rank recurrence after moment estimates are known. The real-place
majorant lies in the input to those estimates and retains its old numerical output.
These changes can therefore coexist.

The search for the trace targets returned only two irrelevant occurrences: an
unrelated finite-field kernel statement and a sentence saying another proposal
leaves the trace formula unchanged. The search for the real-place source names
and mechanisms returned no hits in the screened primary corpus. These searches
support the audit; the source-contract comparison above is the substantive
reason for treating the replacements as distinct.

In particular, the proposed work does not repackage the already suggested
rank-height optimization, exterior-power lower bound, sparse-block construction,
real-axis selector, or raw-exponent Frobenius-jet approach as a new research direction.
It also does not propose the recent false-lift descent simplification already
present at the chosen source revision.

\keyresult{\textbf{The supported distinctness claim is local and precise.}
No matching replacement of the line-parahoric moment-recovery contract or the
fixed-column real-place Gram integral was found in the audited bundles.
The familiar general tools---finite interpolation, the spectral theorem, Schur
complements, and matrix beta integrals---are not claimed as new mathematics.}

The two retired directories are the finite-quotient class-count bundle and the
two-order-tests bundle. Their removal does not make either idea new again.
All 26 surviving baseline proposal bundles are unchanged. The new reports,
summaries, and relevant further directions do not propose either replacement
developed here.

All 13 source files in the original target-file audit, together with
\code{lean-toolchain}, \code{lakefile.toml}, and \code{lake-manifest.json}, have
identical Git blob identities at the two snapshots. Thus this late update changes
the exclusion corpus without invalidating the local source interfaces or checked
dependency pins. The archive includes \code{audit/latest_proposal_addendum.md},
its manifest, and \code{results/freshness_check.json}.
