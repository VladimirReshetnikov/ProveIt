# Independent test of two candidate FirstOrder simplifications

## A. Square-zero / socle formulation

Let `A = O[x_j : j in J]/(x_j^2 : j in J)` for a finite set J and any commutative ring O. For a subset S write x_S for the product of its variables. Then:

1. The x_S are an O-basis.
2. `[x_J](ab)` is a perfect O-bilinear pairing, because x_S pairs to 1 precisely with x_(J\S).
3. `Ann_A(x_S) = (x_j : j in S)`.
4. `x_J a = a(0) x_J`.

Suppose disjoint blocks I_e partition J and P_e is their prefix union. Given elements d_e, Γ_e in A with

    x_(P_e) (d_e - Γ_e x_(I_e)) = 0,

induction gives

    product_(k<e) d_k = (product_(k<e) Γ_k) x_(P_e).

Consequently

    product_e d_e = (product_e Γ_e(0)) x_J.

This is a clean, short replacement for the telescoping proof in `FittingBlock.coeff_mul_prod_of_mem_colon` (lines 537–562), but not a new underlying mathematical mechanism. `FittingBlock.nonDivisorIdeal` (503ff) is already exactly `(x_j^2)`. The source colon ideals are pullbacks of the annihilators in item 3. Its `CharacterRows.correction_mem_colon` and `add_eq_mul_prod` supply precisely the hypotheses above.

The source's hard work is not the multiplication identity. It is proving the character block Fitting membership from `HasTriangleCost`, obtaining row matrices realizing those Fitting elements, lifting the row matrices to common tied rows under every character, and producing a common trace-ideal multiplier by the integral Ribet method. Neither socle pairing nor the displayed induction supplies these missing constructions. At present the quotient refactor yields a more conceptual explanation and perhaps a short local lemma, but does not support a significant whole-module deletion claim.

The later operator argument in `AuxiliaryPrimeTie` reads only squarefree coefficients via the convolution

    sum_(S subset J) N_(J\S) HeckeAction(F_S),

so its coefficient-reading portion is compatible with this quotient. However this does not establish a replacement for the exact common-multiplier membership upstream.

## B. Traces plus Newton identities with a factorial

The suggested strategy is to avoid denominator-free Amitsur/characteristic-polynomial integrality by recovering coefficients from traces, clearing factorial denominators, and absorbing the extra factor into the arbitrary auxiliary precision ν.

This does not presently work. `RibetMethod.exists_ribet_element_of_tied_rows` constructs

    f = trace(ρ_1 P(ρ_0))

where P(0) equals the relevant block determinant. On the genuine lifts, ρ_1ρ_0=0 and `trace(ρ_1)=μ_0`, hence `f = μ_0 P(0)`. Multiplying P by an integer a to clear Newton denominators multiplies the actual final coefficient c by a. Thus its valuation changes by v(a) in `RibetLemma.exists_isCommonRibetElement_addVal_le` (551ff), and this is a charged denominator budget. The size of the tied-row block may depend on the large family of eigensystems and its generators. A factorial in that dimension is not among the exported bounds.

By contrast increasing ν absorbs an additive congruence error that disappears after a fixed nonzero cancellation. It cannot divide the final operator by a nonunit without a new proof of integral divisibility. One needs an additional exact saturation/cancellation theorem, not merely larger ν.

An elementary integral obstruction explains why this is serious. Over O=Z_p and R=O[t], let A=t I_p. Every positive power trace equals p t^k. The subring generated by these traces is O + p t O[t], whereas `det A = t^p` is not in that subring. In characteristic p all the power traces are zero and the determinant is still t^p. Newton trace formulas alone lose precisely the integral information that the characteristic-polynomial hypotheses and Amitsur theorem retain.

This example is not a counterexample to the original theorem: its original hypotheses include the full characteristic polynomials of group elements. It is a counterexample to treating their integral extension to block determinants as an innocuous consequence of traces with no cost.

## Novelty audit

No existing proposal gives either a square-zero Fitting/Ribet theorem or a Newton-trace replacement of Amitsur. Some proposal audit prose lists reorganization of Fitting/Hecke annihilator constructions as a future opportunity. The Newton divided-difference proposal concerns torsion points and is unrelated. Therefore a genuinely stronger replacement at this interface would be distinct, but these two easy formulations do not yet establish one.
