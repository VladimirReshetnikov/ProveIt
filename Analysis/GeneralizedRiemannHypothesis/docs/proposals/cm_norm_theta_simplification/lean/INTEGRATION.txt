Stage 1: abstract the existing cancellation interface
----------------------------------------------------
Current definitions use Fin n -> Z_p in scaledThetaProd. The new inert
multiplier is generally in an unramified quadratic extension of Q_p, hence
in C_p, and MUST NOT be restricted to Z_p. Change or wrap this interface as

    scaledThetaProdCp C mu c delta
      = product_i (C.thetaProd mu c).rescale (fun _ => delta_i),
    delta : Fin n -> C_p.

Provide the following generic consequences:

1. Its constant coefficient is 1.
2. It equals the product over linear forms mu_j of the one-variable product
   product_i theta(delta_i z), substituted at mu_j and raised to c_j.
3. If (delta_i,s_i) belongs to normPairClosure d for every i and sum s_i=0,
   then the product belongs to C.multiSeriesRing mu.
4. Under those hypotheses it has an inverse in C.multiSeriesRing mu.

For (4), invert the one-variable constant-one product using the existing
isSeriesGenerator_translatedY.exists_mul_eq_one, then substitute the inverse
into each mu_j and take products. An arbitrary constant-one member of an
arbitrary subring need not be a unit; the series-generator theorem supplies
the required reason here.

The candidate implements the one-variable norm-product lemma and the
two-copy product/inverse from a hypothesis (delta,-1) in normPairClosure.
Its proof adapts the original prod_rescale_theta_mem, replacing delta_i^2
by the independent CM norm parameter s_i.

For later bounds it is convenient to bundle a cancellation package with:
  - a positive copy count n;
  - delta : Fin n -> C_p;
  - delta_0=1;
  - norm(delta_i)<=1 for every i;
  - the scaled product U belongs to C.multiSeriesRing mu;
  - an inverse of U in that ring;
  - constantCoeff U=1.

The norm-pair lemma itself does not need the norm bounds. The bounds are
essential when applying integral substitution and leastValLE_of_rescale.

Stage 2: formalize the local selectors
------------------------------------
A. Inert p

Use the existing
  Lib/CM/FormalGroup/FormalParameter.lean
    mem_normPairClosure_of_pow_eq_one
and primeNorm d p=p^2. For odd p choose zeta of order 2(p+1), prove

    zeta^(p+1)=-1,
    zeta^(p^2-1)=1,
    norm(zeta)=1.

The divisibility 2(p+1) | p^2-1 uses oddness of p. A primitive root of unity
exists in C_p by the same roots-of-unity infrastructure already used in
FormalParameter. The source lemma then yields (zeta,-1) in the closure.
Take the two scalars (1,zeta). No global field extension is required.

In the admissible-data application, every nonsplit prime is >=13 by
New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.thirteen_le_of_chiK_ne_one. Never apply
this inert construction at p=2.

B. Split p, including 2

Use
  Lib/CM/FormalGroup/PrimeNotation.lean
    QuadField.star_ne_of_chiK_eq_one.
Let w be the prime selected by the p-adic embedding. Then w and star(w)
are distinct maximal ideals. Their n-th powers are coprime. By CRT choose
alpha_n in O_K with

    alpha_n = 1 mod w^n,
    alpha_n = -1 mod star(w)^n.

Under the selected embedding, prove alpha_n -> 1 and star(alpha_n) -> -1.
Use continuity of multiplication and the closure definition to obtain
(1,-1) in normPairClosure d. Take the two scalars (1,1). This argument
covers p=2 without dividing by 2. It does not assert the existence of a
global unit of norm -1 in the imaginary quadratic field.

An optional elementary local-root implementation uses
beta=(1+sqrt(-d))/2, with polynomial X^2-X+(d+1)/4. At a split prime its
two residue roots are distinct; Hensel lift them. Solve the two local
linear equations a+b beta=1 and a+b(1-beta)=-1. The determinant
2 beta-1 is a unit even at p=2. The CRT route aligns more directly with
the cited source theorem, so the local-root proof is an alternative
obligation, not an additional assumption required by the proposal.

C. Ramified p=d

Here p is odd and p=7 mod 8. Prove a_0^2+b_0^2=-1 over F_p by the
intersection of the two sets of cardinality (p+1)/2 consisting of squares
and -1 minus squares. Choose a nonzero coordinate. Hensel lift that
coordinate, keeping the other fixed, to obtain a,b in Z_p with
a^2+b^2=-1. Use density of integers in Z_p to put the three rational norm
pairs (1,1), (a,a^2), (b,b^2) in normPairClosure. The scalars (1,a,b)
form the required three-copy package.

Do not force a two-copy norm-minus-one selector at this prime: modulo d,
norms are squares and -1 is not a square. The three-copy clause is needed.

Stage 3: generalize the two coefficient mechanisms
-------------------------------------------------
A. Unit-coefficient mechanism

Generalize leastValLE_of_unit_coefficients in
New/Derivatives/Sharpness/LeastValuation.lean to a cancellation package
with arbitrary positive n. The source proof uses its fixed six copies
only to obtain the package. It already permits C_p contraction scalars
in leastValLE_of_rescale.

Keep exactly the current single-curve input katz_divisibility_limit.
Set H_0=G/U, H_i=1 for i>0, with G(z)=g(mu_i0(z)). The substituted
product is G, so the same proof gives

    LeastValLE mu c n nu (gamma_p * |nu| - v_p(nu!)).

Instantiate n=2 for split and inert primes, n=3 at the ramified prime.

B. Coupling mechanism

IsCouplingFamily.norm_coeff_pairExponent in
New/Derivatives/Sharpness/Couplings.lean is already generic over n+1
scalars in C_p. Generalize exists_prod_rescale_rescale_thetaProd_eq and
the specialized coupling witnesses, which currently fix that number to
six. At inert primes instantiate the new two-copy package. Preserve the
existing leading-coefficient, derivative, factorial and scaling proofs.

C. Span residual

leastValLE_zero_of_span in LeastValuation already proves

    h_one_copy : LeastValLE mu c 1 nu 0

before its last line pads five zero-degree factors. Expose that inner
result as leastValLE_one_zero_of_span. The old lemma can remain as a
compatibility wrapper. This stronger one-copy result is used for the
large-inert-prime residual only. The small-inert-prime residual needs
the stronger gamma_p*|nu|-v_p(nu!) bound and therefore uses two copies.

Stage 4: prove the exact four-copy theorem
-----------------------------------------
Target the identical hypotheses and right side of
  New/Derivatives/Sharpness/Cases.lean
    Data.leastValLE_twelve,
but replace its copy count 12 by 4:

    LeastValLE mu c 4 l
      (S_p + 2 * sum_tau v_p(B_tau) - v_p(l!)).

Here S_p is localPrimeSumTotal p and B_tau is the unchanged lossProduct
at the relevant degree. Do not reoptimize the loss constants in this step.

Cases:
  Split p: generic two-copy bound, gamma_p=S_p=0; pad two copies.
  Inert p<=k: two-copy coupling plus two-copy residual; use the existing
    factorial comparison and partner-loss inequalities unchanged.
  Inert p>k: two-copy coupling plus the one-copy span residual; pad one.
  Ramified p=d: IsNearPair.ne_d in NearPairs makes the near-pair set
    empty. Apply the generic three-copy rule directly to the whole degree.
    In gammaPrime_mul_degree_sub_le_localPrimeSumTotal_add, put e=j=0;
    the near-pair hypotheses are vacuous. Drop the nonnegative
    3*f*(logB+2) term, and pad one copy. The scale inequality d<=k is
    the same one used by the existing proof to show p>k is inert.

The candidate supplies a generic LeastValLE.pad wrapper. It can implement
the zero-degree padding in all these clauses via LeastValLE.add and
leastValLE_zero.

Stage 5: preserve the existing global interface first
----------------------------------------------------
Reprove leastValLE_twelve by applying the four-copy result and padding
eight zero-degree copies. Since (4+8)=12, the bound and theorem type are
identical to the existing interface. Then build the project and inspect
the main theorem statements and axiom lists.

This route leaves BoundAttained, its global scalar t, the rank-one test
forms, all determinant statements and the analytic endpoint unchanged.
It is the recommended first integration. No downstream height claim
needs to be altered merely to test the local refactor.

Why the local selectors do not create a global-height problem:
LeastValLE.exists_monomial removes arbitrary integral-series coefficients
by a finite ultrametric maximum. It returns monomials in the fixed global
functions y(mu_i z), with natural exponents and coefficient 1. The local
multiplier does not become a coefficient of the eventual global test
form. BoundAttained chooses t before the universal quantifier over primes
and embeddings; only the coefficient/indicator witnesses depend on them.

Stage 6: optional reduction of the global derivative block
---------------------------------------------------------
After the compatibility route is checked, parameterize the definitions
now named twelveSpace, twelveTaylor and indicatorDatum, and their Fourier
coefficient bridge in BoundAttained, by a positive copy count h. With h=4:

    vacuumScale^12 -> vacuumScale^4,
    M^(12*f)      -> M^(4*f).

The loss product remains the same. The positive rank-dependent terms in
the conjugate bound decrease from
12*f*log M + 12*constantBound*q1^2
to
4*f*log M + 4*constantBound*q1^2.

In New/Determinant/Twist/FitTwists.RankOneIndex, replace the decomposition

    (Fin 12 + Fin 4 + Fin 4) + Fin (2*z-20)

with

    (Fin 4 + Fin 4 + Fin 4) + Fin (2*z-12).

The total ambient rank stays 2*z; all four blocks remain even. The last
empty-row character now has rank 2*(z-6). In the application z>m>=10000
and z<=r, so the required positivity and upper bound by t=5*r remain
available. Update all embedding maps, Fin cardinality calculations,
normalizing powers and dependent types in TwistedValueHeights and its
consumers. This is a broader migration, not an implemented result of
the supplied candidate. Do not blindly replace every numeric occurrence
of 12 or 20: many numbers elsewhere have unrelated mathematical roles.

This changes an auxiliary derivative block. It does not shorten the
main chain of m theta lifts, change its total target rank, improve the
one-half exponent, or bypass the deep arithmetic inputs.

Static review of the candidate and remaining risks
-------------------------------------------------
- The exponential product identity follows the exact source proof; its
  norm parameters are generalized from rational squares to the h_pair
  hypotheses. The source expSubst_sum interface was checked.
- The two-copy proof uses the exact expSubst_sub_rescale_mem and
  expSubst_sub_rescale_eq declarations from LogTheta.
- The inverse proof explicitly supplies constantCoeff_taylorVia_theta
  to simplification; this lemma is not marked simp in its declaration.
- The padding proof follows the source's existing h_one_copy.add padding.
- The candidate's proof terms still depend on Lean elaboration of the
  power-series rescaling and finite-product expressions, coercions between
  one-variable and multivariable series, simplifier orientation and
  namespace resolution. These have not been checked by a Lean executable.
- The Cp-valued multivariate wrappers, local selectors, generalized
  coefficient lemmas and four-copy case theorem are specifications in this
  guide, not hidden declarations in the candidate.
- A successful check of the four small candidate lemmas would verify only
  those lemmas. Full integration, downstream compilation and the theorem
  audit are separate remaining gates.

Finite exact checks in the archive support algebraic identities and
edge-case handling. They do not discharge any of those Lean obligations.
