\section{Answer, scope, and evidential status}

There are useful transfers, and this analysis produces two concrete advances.
The most direct one strengthens an internal theorem of the GRH argument:
its raw second-moment estimates already contain more quantitative spanning
information than the final packaged theorem records. The other is a small,
exact representation-theoretic identity that produces additional holomorphic
zeta comparisons in an $S_5$ extension. Both have complete written proofs and
reproducible finite checks where computation is used.

These advances do not complete the transfer to a new general nonabelian or
automorphic Riemann hypothesis. The main missing work is still arithmetic:
one must realize the appropriate nonabelian Frobenius statistic by integral
objects with the right divisibility, size, and denominator bounds. A sharper
rank lemma helps such a construction; it cannot supply that realization.

\subsection{What the source actually exports}

At the pinned revision,
\src{Statements.lean}{\texttt{Statements.lean}} imports Mathlib directly and
defines the endpoint assertions; \src{Main.lean}{\texttt{Main.lean}} proves
five named statements. They include Mathlib's Riemann hypothesis and
nonvanishing of every Dirichlet $L(s,\chi)$ for $\Re s>1/2$, with the
principal-character pole at $s=1$ excepted, together with critical-line
versions. All positive moduli and imprimitive characters are covered
\cite{repo,statements}.

Thus ``GRH'' in the repository title means Dirichlet GRH over $\Q$.
The exported statements do not quantify over arbitrary number fields,
nonabelian Artin representations, or all automorphic representations.
Those larger families are meaningful extension targets even when the
repository's endpoint is taken as established.

The source README reports builds, the axiom list
\code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice}, \code{Quot.sound}, independent export
checks, and Comparator checks. This report does not repeat those checks.
Lean and Lake were not installed in this workspace. Our conclusions about
new results rest on the written arguments and exact certificates supplied
here, while claims about upstream compilation are attributed to its README.
Static source inspection is not a kernel replay.

\subsection{Three different meanings of a transfer}

It is useful to keep the following distinctions explicit.
An \emph{endpoint consequence} uses RH or Dirichlet GRH as a black box; the
user's request largely excludes this category. A \emph{transfer of the
arithmetic mechanism} recreates the short-form, spanning, and determinant
comparison for a new statistic. This is the ambitious route to new
critical-line theorems. A \emph{transfer of an ingredient} develops a piece
of that comparison or its analytic and algebraic interfaces for another
problem. Such work can be worthwhile without depending on RH at all.

The spanning theorem in this report improves an ingredient inside the
arithmetic mechanism itself. The $S_5$ identity advances an algebraic
interface introduced by the earlier reflection reports. Neither is being
presented as a new invention of Cauchy--Schwarz, greedy spanning, or Artin
induction. These are classical tools. The concrete new content relative to
the inspected material is the retained two-error formula, its use at the
actual source constants, the exact finite selection procedure, and the
three-subgroup comparison identity with its arithmetic consequences.
