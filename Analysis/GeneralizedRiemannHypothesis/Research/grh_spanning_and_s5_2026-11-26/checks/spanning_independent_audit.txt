1. Necessary hypotheses and exact square-root estimate
-----------------------------------------------------
Let s=dim S>0, d=dim W>0, and let a finite group act unitarily on T.
Let P project orthogonally onto the invariant subspace W. Write
C_g=P rho(g) B. Assume, for every U<=S,

  dim U - u s <= ||PB P_U||_HS^2,
  d mean_g ||B* rho(g) B||_HS^2 <= (1+v)s^2,

where 0<=u<1 and v>=0. The condition s>0 must not be omitted: the
original abstract source lemma permits S=0, in which case an asserted
positive total-rank lower bound would be false.

Put M=mean_g rho(g)BB*rho(g)*. The group change of variables gives
tr(M^2)=mean_g ||B*rho(g)B||_HS^2. For F<=W of dimension k,

  mean_g ||P_F rho(g)B||_HS^2
    = tr(P_F M) <= sqrt(k tr(M^2))
    <= s sqrt((1+v)k/d).

Choose a translate at or below this average and set U=C_g^{-1}(F).
On U, C_g equals P_F rho(g) B. Unitarity, commutation of P with rho,
and contraction of the HS norm under right orthogonal projection give

  dim C_g^{-1}(F) <= s[u+sqrt((1+v)k/d)].

This is exactly the estimate available before the arithmetic-mean step
in the source. Applying near-isometry to ker(PB) also gives
(1-u)s <= rank(PB) <= d.

2. Sharp universal rank coefficient, with its precise scope
----------------------------------------------------------
Apply the estimate to F=sum_g im(C_g), when every preimage is all S.
The total span has rank at least alpha*d, where

  alpha=(1-u)^2/(1+v).

For rational u,v choose positive integers d,r with r/d=alpha. Take
S=C, T=W=C^d, P=I, a cyclic group of order r rotating the first r
coordinate axes and fixing the rest, and B(1)=sqrt(1-u)e_1.
Near-isometry is an equality on S; the mean squared overlap is
(1-u)^2/r; the moment hypothesis is an equality; the total span has
rank r. Thus the coefficient cannot be increased universally.
For arbitrary real u,v, r=ceil(alpha*d) in the same construction
gives moment <=1+v and rank fraction approaching alpha as d grows.

The claim is a sharp UNIVERSAL coefficient. It is not a formula for
the exact minimum rank at every prescribed integer d,s and error pair.
The independent lower bound ceil((1-u)s) may be stronger at particular
dimensions. The underlying trace/rank method is classical; the new
result here is the quantitative source interface and its application,
not a claim to have invented the second-moment rank inequality.

3. Finite selection, integer certificates, and source restriction
---------------------------------------------------------------
If the current rank is k<alpha*d, the selected translate adds at least

  s[(1-u)-sqrt((1+v)k/d)]
    >= h(alpha*d-k),
  h=s(1+v)/(2d(1-u)).

With q=max(0,1-h), after at most t translates the span has rank at least
alpha*d*(1-q^t). Once the rank crosses alpha*d one may stop selecting.
For a source subspace of codimension r0, the same selection loses at
most t*r0 dimensions. All appearances of the floor in the translate
budget must be retained; replacing floor(X) by X gives an incorrect
unrounded exponential estimate.

The exact algorithm in verify_spanning.py uses R=ceil(alpha*d) and

  m(k)=min(s, floor(su+s sqrt((1+v)k/d))),
  T(k)=min(R,k+s-m(k)),
  H(q)=min_{q<=k<=R} T(k),
  q_0=0, q_{j+1}=H(q_j).

The suffix minimum is necessary because the actual current rank can
exceed the available certified lower bound, and T need not be monotone.
It is the greatest monotone minorant of this one-step transition.
For k<R, m(k)<=s-1, so H(q)>=q+1 below R and termination takes at most R
steps. This is a certificate derived from the stated profile; it is not
a claim that the number of selected translates is optimal for every
actual group action. Each floor is checked by exact rational squared
comparisons and adjacent integer boundary checks, without numerical
square roots. Logarithmic budget floors use rigorous rational atanh
series bounds after range reduction.

4. Improved arithmetic-source constants: b>=6
--------------------------------------------
Keep the source's separate errors u=5/b^4 and v=9/b^4. Put
a=1-u, z=1+v, alpha=a^2/z, delta=1-alpha, and L=log(1+2b^2).
Let t=floor(4dL/s) and suppose r0/s<=b^-8.

Since s/d<=1/a and b>=6 (indeed b>=4 suffices for this step),

  h<=z/(2a^2)<=33920/63001<1.

The floor gives t>=4dL/s-1, hence the missing share is at most

  delta + alpha*exp(h)*(1+2b^2)^(-2z/a) + 4b^-8 L.

The three terms are bounded respectively by

  19/b^4, 3/(4b^4), 8/b^6.

Indeed delta=(19q-25q^2)/(1+9q)<=19q for q=b^-4; alpha<=1,
exp(h)<e<3, z/a>=1; and L<=2b^2. For every real b>=6,

  19 + 3/4 + 8/b^2 <= 719/36 < 20.

Therefore at most the count coefficient 4 suffices to span
(1-20/b^4)d dimensions, including the source restriction. More
precisely |A|s<=4dL, and hence |A|dim(S0)<=4dL. This improves both
the source theorem's count coefficient 5 and its loss 4/b^2. All
rounding and restriction costs have been included.

For b>=4 the original coefficient 5 yields the same 20/b^4 loss:
its tail is <=1/b^5 and restriction loss <=10/b^6, so the deficit
is at most b^-4[19+1/b+10/b^2]<=159/(8b^4)<20/b^4.
The B=4 computational example is explicitly this five-times variant.
