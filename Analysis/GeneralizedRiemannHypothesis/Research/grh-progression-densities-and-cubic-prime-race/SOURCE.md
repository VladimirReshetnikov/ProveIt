Progression-free exponent sets. Read grh/src/New/Reduction/Data/Existence.lean at the pinned commit, particularly lines 32–37 (authors credit Behrend and Shapira), 57–74 (strict convexity of a sphere), 76–114 (exists_three_term_free), 163–183 (Parameters.exists_large_isSparse), and 263 (application of the large sparse set lemma). With (LH)^n ≤ o and L>0, exists_three_term_free constructs at least H^n/(nH²) exponents below o, on which any modular weighted midpoint with two positive weights of sum ≤L is trivial. They use carry-free digit coordinates on an integer sphere. In exists_large_isSparse, these exponents give places indexed by −g^e. A forbidden pair of prime-power ratios through one place would produce the forbidden relation among exponents. Our polynomial transfer retains the conversion from multiplicative triples to exponent progressions. In the polynomial ring each monic irreducible gives its own exact integral exponent coordinate; a finite local exchange, or the construction in coprime fibers, replaces the sphere. The polynomial results use this exponent principle independently of the zero-location theorem.
