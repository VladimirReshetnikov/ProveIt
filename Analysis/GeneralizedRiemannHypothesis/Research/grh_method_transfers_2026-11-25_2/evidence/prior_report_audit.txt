## Critical finding for the current report

**The Mok–Zheng K-theory preprint is not a new discovery relative to the supplied reports.**

`grh_transfer_analysis_2026-11-25.zip`, manuscript `grh_transfer_report/report.tex`, Section 10.3 (source lines 1195–1213) already explicitly cites:

- L. Pintar, *Vanishing of K_(4k)(Z) through degree 104 from Alpöge's Step 17*, https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00829v3 (PDF labelled Version 5, 23 November 2026), as conditional on the original `corGK_of` arithmetic construction.
- C. P. Mok and H. Zheng, *Vanishing of K_(4m)(Z) through degree 120*, https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00998v1 (24 November 2026), as claiming unconditional vanishing from pointwise Selmer bounds and exact Hecke certificates, with an explicit caveat that their modified Lean entry point had not been fully rebuilt.

Section 10.4 already proves the products-before-gcds lemma and gives the branch-switching counterexample `(2,3)` and `(3,2)`. Section 10.5 explicitly asks for a standalone theorem taking an ordinary strict Selmer class to an admissible pointwise automorphic witness and then to integral determinant divisibility, with all local hypotheses and quantifiers specified. A new report can add value by auditing that bridge or reproducing exact certificates; merely describing these preprints or repeating the elementary gcd lemma is already-covered work.

## Exact results that should not be re-presented as new

### 1. Determinants, sparse twists, and sparse scales

The first analysis archive proves the comparison

`R <= [A+(B+2I)/theta]/m`

from `theta D<=n<=D`, `V<=n(M-mR+A)`, `delta<=DI`, and `D(M-B)<=V+2delta`, with the stated nonnegative parameters. Its completion-cost variant includes a loss for the missing D-n directions. A rank fraction and a subpower numerator with m>=c X^mu yield the corresponding X^(-mu+epsilon) remainder saving. These are already in a small Lean supplement; a reparameterized lemma is not additional research.

The dated report allows a large successful twist family, with exponent threshold determined by eta=(beta-1/2)/18. The later CM-prime report sharpens this into a combined sparse criterion: a family S with counting exponent at least `1-(beta-1/2)/18` and remainder upper bounds only along scales with gaps O(x^((1+beta)/2)) excludes zeros to the right of beta. It uses convexity in t=1/x and a chord-error bound. Fourth-power sampling suffices as beta approaches 1/2; the successful twist family can have convergent reciprocal sum. Mere sparsification is already done.

### 2. Nonabelian tests and congruence obstruction

The reports cover conductor-23 S3, Q(cuberoot(2)), arbitrary finite-Galois completely split primes, and a general number-field subtraction of its maximal abelian subfield.

For a finite Galois group G, D=[G,G], and conjugacy-invariant target C, the arithmetic-transfers report gives exact best fixed-congruence classification error density:

`(1/|G|) sum_(T in G/D) min(|T intersect C|, |T minus C|)`.

For K=L^H and F=L^(HD), the minimum prime-average squared error approximating the degree-one prime count a_K(p) by a periodic function is

`|H\G/H| - [F:Q]`,

attained by a_F. It follows that a nonabelian normal closure cannot have its zeta function represented by a finite product or quotient of Dirichlet L-functions up to finitely many Euler factors. This is an obstruction to that specific strategy, not an impossibility theorem for every method of relating zeros.

### 3. Sharp finite torsion test already proved

Let p be prime, v(p)=1, r>0 rational, and O=O_(C_p). For finitely many integral formal power series F_i in s variables and possibly different reference points x_i with v(x_ia)>=r and total B_0=sum_i v(F_i(x_i))<=B<infinity, define

`h*(r)=min{h>=1:1/(p^(h-1)(p-1))<=r}`.

Then one tuple of p-power roots of unity, with each coordinate at an order level between h*(r) and `H=h*(r)+floor B`, makes every F_i(zeta-1) nonzero. H is independent of s and of degrees and is sharp even in one variable for the stated radial constraint. The proof uses the integral ideal of a Cartesian grid cut out by a product of shifted cyclotomic polynomials; its weighted Gauss valuation is the number of levels, exceeding B.

For every j=1,...,m simultaneously, the report gives `H=h*(r/m)+floor(m B)` by substitution. The bound is not claimed optimal for m>1. This leaves a potentially real quantitative question, but it would require a proof stronger than the already supplied argument.

The same report extends the upstream near-valuation-preservation theorem from algebraic polynomials to integral formal series over completed C_p without changing H, by fixing the finite torsion grid first and uniformly truncating and approximating. A sufficient explicit (coarse) H grows exponentially in `(m B+1)(s_0+2)/min(epsilon,1)` via a harmonic-sum criterion.

It also controls minima of k-minor valuations for finitely many matrices at all first m powers. Those are Fitting-ideal valuations; they do not separately upper-bound each individual invariant factor, nor equal ordinary module lengths over the nondiscrete C_p valuation ring. This distinction is expressly addressed with examples.

### 4. Odd cocycle comparison already completed

For CM E contained in Q(mu_N), odd coefficient prime ell, finite O/F over Q_ell, and e>0, the latest report defines actual cocycles S_n into `(O/ell^n O)(-e)`, unramified away from ell, zero on `D_v intersect ker chi_ell` above ell, and satisfying `z(cgc)=-z(g)`.

It proves the canonical comparison

`colim_n S_n = Sha^1(E,(F/O)(-e))^(c=(-1)^(e+1))`,

where Sha^1 means the all-places locally trivial ordinary H^1 group. The source bound `New.exists_length_shaOdd_le` gives an integer

`1 <= X <= 2^(4(N+t+3)^27) h_E`

such that this group's O-length is at most v_O(X), for 0<e<t and the coefficient-ring hypotheses from the source. It is finite, killed by X, and zero if ell does not divide X. Integral odd cocycles vanish.

The proof distinguishes Tz(g)=z(cgc) from the natural complex-conjugation action: the latter includes coefficient action (-1)^e. Thus for even e the source's odd module corresponds to the natural MINUS eigenspace, while classes descended from Q are PLUS. This is the wrong parity for a direct route to K_(4m)(Z) from this particular module. Further, E,N,t are fixed before ell varies, so “almost all ell for a fixed E” cannot be substituted along E=Q(mu_ell). Finite-level local kernels are not naively identified with usual finite-coefficient Sha; the comparison passes to divisible coefficients.

The earlier dated report separately proves that a nonzero pure Tate-twist lattice cannot directly meet the generic twisting interface's inertia-scalar condition `zeta_*^2-1 in O^units`: away from ell, Tate twists are unramified, so zeta_*=1.

### 5. CM sparse equations and prime-race statements

The latest CM report's main theorem is `h(Delta)>=4 => s(Delta)>=5`; for h=4 equality follows. It treats rational coefficients and coefficients in the imaginary quadratic CM field. Its local determinant lemma says that k distinct numbers within a sufficiently small nonzero p-adic disk have a nonvanishing generalized power Vandermonde for every set of k distinct nonnegative exponents. At p>=k it uses strict radius `v(z_i/c-1)>1/(p-k+1)`.

At p=7 or13, nonsplit unramified-in-conductor CM reduction puts every conjugate in a single nonzero supersingular residue disk with depth at least 1/2. Split primes supply archimedean separation; a finite discriminant sieve and 79 exact disk certificates complete the claimed four-term proof. Those 79 disk checks were rerun here. The proof is not merely a corollary of GRH; the paper explicitly claims it can remove the analytic input.

For Delta=-131, the class polynomial reduces to `X(X-8)^4 mod17`, with one root of valuation3 and four units. A putative five-term relation with exponents 0=e0<e1<...<e4=D must satisfy, for some j>0,

`3 e_j <= sum_(k>0,k!=j) [v17(e_k-e_j)-v17(e_k)]`.

Hence D>=17^e1. A finite modular check excludes normalized degree D<=1000; larger-degree five-term relations remain open. Claiming the full five-term theorem from this finite check would be invalid.

The same report proves/claims a centered prime-power race vector D(x) has a fixed Euclidean ball in the convex hull of {D(n):X<=n<=C_q X} for every large X. The central zero multiplicities are explicitly subtracted. No linear independence or simplicity of zeros is used. This is convex balance, not a single threshold realizing every desired ranking. It also gives a short-interval multiplicity-rank theorem from Banks's formula and a vector Laplace averaging theorem. These are not available as untouched new topics.

## Potential additions beyond the current predecessors

1. **A source-exact strict-Selmer/Hecke bridge audit**, followed by replay of nontrivial Mok–Zheng certificate data, would materially deepen the dated report. It must use the original development's stronger pointwise theorem, not identify it with the shorter repository's odd-cocycle bound.
2. **An exact branching length certificate** has not been found in the eleven manuscripts. If arithmetic supplies `for every probe q, exists branch j: length(M)<=v_ell(A_qj)`, then the sharp universal integer is `gcd_q lcm_j |A_qj|`, with valuation `min_q max_j v_ell(A_qj)`. The already-known product/gcd certificate captures prime support, but overestimates higher valuations. This is elementary and should be presented as a useful certificate refinement, not a major conjecture result. Its applicability depends on a full length bound, not just a nonzero-witness implication.
3. **Improving the first-m-power torsion depth** below `h*(r/m)+floor(mB)` or proving a matching lower bound remains open in the supplied reports. The sharpness proof handles only m=1.
4. **The h>=5 six-term CM question**, particularly Delta=-131, is a concrete remaining target. It is outside a conventional RH implication, but the previous finite degree cutoff does not resolve it.
5. **A collective residue-separation theorem** could replace one zero-free auxiliary twist by a positive family whose residue vectors have no cancellation. Prior reports identify this as a possible further direction without a completed arithmetic application.
