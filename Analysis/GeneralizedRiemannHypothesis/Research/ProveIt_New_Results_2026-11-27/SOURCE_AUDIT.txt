https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/tree/41a284d6ef8eb16d783bfc685ab99cc6e1d28daa/Analysis/GeneralizedRiemannHypothesis/

The requested directory was analyzed at this fixed revision. The default branch
moved during the investigation; later changes were not silently incorporated.

The article README describes a merged collection of 209 research reports,
982 pages, and 1,321 theorem-like statements. Those are repository descriptions,
not an independently certified count of correct new theorems. The investigation
surveyed the article structure and result indices, searched the section text,
closely reviewed the positive-filter arguments and source-scope discussions,
and compared candidate extensions with the sixteen available incoming ZIPs.
The source manifest records the inspected article copies and incoming archives.

The incoming files matter: several initially promising symplectic and
finite-observation extensions were already covered there. Those candidates
were discarded rather than presented again as new.

FORMAL INPUT: OBSERVED SCOPE

The inspected vendored Statements.lean defines nonvanishing to the right of
Re(s)=1/2 and critical-strip zero localization for Dirichlet L-functions,
with the principal-character pole convention handled separately. Main.lean
exports the corresponding statements through New.grh. These are Dirichlet
statements; they do not by themselves assert nonabelian Artin GRH, general Hecke
GRH, simple zeros, central nonvanishing, or Elliott–Halberstam.

The vendor README pins lsernau/grh-lean at
72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207 and says its 818 files are byte-identical
to that upstream snapshot. This investigation read selected source files and
the stated interface; it did not rebuild or kernel-check that dependency tree.
The ordinary ProveIt build does not by itself validate those vendored files.

The source reduction was also inspected: its simultaneous upper/lower lattice
bounds and torsion substitution are substantial reusable mechanisms, but their
arithmetic carriers and conductor costs are specific. No arbitrary Frobenius
weight replacement or nonabelian transfer was assumed. The source article itself
identifies missing ingredients for such transfers.

DIRECT INPUTS USED BY THE PAPERS

Article/sections/fil.tex:

  fil:lem:strip — the integrated strip identity for positive exponential sums;
  fil:thm:meanbound — support diameter times mean Fourier frequency >= n*pi;
  fil:lem:tails — eventual positivity and tail control for theta kernels.

The first paper reproduces the strip proof and its finite moment reduction,
then proves the full complex-height prefix bound, hinge domination, sharp
reciprocal moments, equality, stability, support uncertainty and gap applications.
The second uses the eventually positive theta tail to dominate a possibly signed
compact core, with a polynomial extrapolation bound uniform in matrix size.
Neither argument invokes RH or GRH.

PUBLISHED QUESTIONS AND THEIR PRECISE TREATMENT

Stefan Steinerberger, Some Open Problems:
https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf

The author PDF's indexed primary text identifies Problem 41, page 41, in the
version last updated 31 March 2026. It asks whether nonnegative functions with
specified vanishing Fourier coefficients satisfy a support-radius lower bound
proportional to the square root of the sum of reciprocal frequency squares.
The main paper proves that inequality with the optimal constant. The broader
question of exact minimum support for every prescribed set remains open here.
Direct PDF retrieval returned HTTP 404; the mathematical problem was retrieved
from the indexed text of the author's PDF. The query

  site:faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf "probability measure centered"

retrieved the complete relevant question. An older 10 June 2024 copy numbers the
same question 42. The paper states the Fourier convention explicitly to avoid
ambiguity between a unit circle and a 2*pi angular circle.

Gady Kozma and Ferenc Oravecz, On the gaps between zeros of trigonometric
polynomials, Real Analysis Exchange 28(2) (2002/2003), 447–454:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0601021

Question 2, page 2 of the preprint, concerns square frequencies and asks whether
the O(1/N) bound is the correct order even when the band is large. The main paper
answers that order alternative, gives a uniform finite-band comparison, and an
exact short-band leading term. It does not determine the exact wide-band
extremum or its leading constant.

EARLIER WORK THAT IS CREDITED

Alexandre Eremenko and Alexander Fryntov, Remarks on the Obrechkoff inequality,
Proceedings of the AMS 144 (2016), 703–707:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.4849

This is the integrated angular positive-polynomial antecedent of the repository's
strip identity. The manuscript does not claim that integrated zero counting or
the general majorization principle was invented here.

Ian Wagner, The Jensen–Polya program for various L-functions, Forum Math. 32(2)
(2020), preprint 2019:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11269

Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 1.4 already give arbitrary-order central-derivative
asymptotics for real primitive Dirichlet characters. This rules out treating the
existence of such an asymptotic, or its qualitative eventual-increase consequence,
as a first result of this investigation. The companion credits it prominently.

Arshay Sheth and Wayne Voo, Super-positivity via Dirichlet theta functions,
arXiv:2611.12152v1 (9 November 2026):
https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2611.12152

Their Conjecture 1.5 asks for eventual increase and at most one turning point.
The companion gives a direct effective eventual-increase proof but does not
claim the global turning-point conclusion. Its main new quantitative focus is
the finite Hankel criterion and the growing range r <= alpha*m*log(m).

Stephane R. Louboutin and Marc Munsch, On positive real zeros of theta and
L-functions associated with real, even and primitive characters, Publ. Math.
Debrecen 83 (2013), 643–665:
https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/1837/download/10_5486_PMD_2013_5670.pdf

Section 1.1 records the negative theta value at conductor 53. This confirms that
allowing a signed compact core is a substantive enlargement beyond globally
positive theta kernels. The companion also proves that a negative interval
prevents positivity of all Hankel dimensions at a single fixed derivative index.

LIMITS OF THE NOVELTY CHECK

The search compared the pinned article, incoming archives, the cited primary
papers, and targeted searches for the precise support and square-gap questions.
No earlier matching resolution was found. This is not an exhaustive priority
determination. A result answering a precisely stated specialist question is not
being represented as a solution of a globally prominent conjecture.

The complete arguments and exact scope are in the two PDFs. Internal independent
proof review is recorded separately. No empirical computation is substituted
for a universal mathematical proof.
