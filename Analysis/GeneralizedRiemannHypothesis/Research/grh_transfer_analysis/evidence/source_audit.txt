# Independent endpoint, source, and analytic-interface audit

Audited snapshot: `lsernau/grh-lean`, commit
`72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207` (2026-11-25).

This audit reads source and reconstructs the final analytic reasoning. It does
**not** report a Lean build, kernel replay, or independent-checker execution by
this reviewer. The source scan is reproducible with `audit_artifacts/static_audit.py`;
its recorded output is `audit_artifacts/static_audit.json`.

## 1. Endpoint scope is genuine and unambiguous in source

`grh/src/Statements.lean` imports only `Mathlib`. It defines:

- `RiemannZetaZerosOnLine`: every zero `s` of the actual Mathlib `riemannZeta`
  other than `s = 1` or `s = -2(n+1)` has `s.re = 1/2`.
- `DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf`: for every positive modulus `N`, every
  `DirichletCharacter ℂ N`, and every complex `s` with `s.re > 1/2`, Mathlib's
  `DirichletCharacter.LFunction χ s` is nonzero, with the exception of the
  principal character at `s = 1` (the pole).
- `DirichletLZerosOnLine`: all zeros of those L-functions in `0 < Re(s) < 1`
  have real part `1/2`.
- `PrimitiveDirichletLZerosOnLine`: the same conclusion restricted to primitive
  characters; logically weaker than the preceding endpoint.

There are no project-specific L-function definitions, implicit arithmetic
hypotheses, a presumed GRH, or new logical axioms in these endpoint definitions.
`NeZero N` means modulus at least 1, not some exceptional restricted family.
The scope includes all primitive and imprimitive Dirichlet characters, with all
conductors; it does not include arbitrary number-field Hecke or automorphic
L-functions.

`grh/src/Main.lean` exports five **unparameterized theorem constants**:

```lean
theorem riemannHypothesis : RiemannHypothesis := New.zeta_zeros_on_line New.grh
theorem riemannZeta_eq_zero_re : RiemannZetaZerosOnLine := riemannHypothesis
theorem LFunction_ne_zero_of_half_lt_re : DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf := New.grh
theorem LFunction_eq_zero_re : DirichletLZerosOnLine := New.zeros_on_line New.grh
theorem LFunction_eq_zero_re_of_isPrimitive : PrimitiveDirichletLZerosOnLine :=
  fun N _ χ _ ↦ LFunction_eq_zero_re N χ
```

If checked, the second assignment in particular establishes definitional
compatibility between the project's expanded zeta statement and Mathlib's
`RiemannHypothesis`. There is no theorem-statement substitution apparent in
these files. `Statements.lean` is byte-identical across the two editions; their
`Main.lean` code is identical after removing comments and whitespace.

The final conversion in `grh/src/New/Main.lean` (lines 24-41 and 46-68) uses
functional-equation reflection. A zero in the open strip produces a reflected
zero for the inverse character. Both must have real part at most one half,
forcing equality. Imprimitive Euler factors have no zero with nonzero real
part (`Lib/Analytic/ZeroSymmetry.lean`), so they do not obstruct reflection of
zeros within the strip. At zeta's trivial zeros the gamma factor issue is
explicitly excluded; `s = 0` is excluded by the actual value of zeta at zero.

## 3. The narrow, reusable analytic interface

The principal analytic interface is
`New.LFunction_ne_zero_of_primeRemainder_le`, in
`grh/src/New/Reduction/Zeros/Criterion.lean`, lines 37-91.

Fix a positive modulus `q` divisible by 8, a reduced residue class `d0` modulo
`q` with `d0 ≡ 7 (mod 8)`, and a real `β > 1/2`. For each prime
`d ≡ d0 (mod q)`, put `χ_d(n) = (n/d)` and

\[
 R_{d,q}(x)=
 \sum_{\substack{n\le x\\n\equiv1\pmod q}}
 \Lambda(n)(1-\chi_d(n))\left(\frac1n-\frac1x\right)
 -\frac{\log x}{\varphi(q)}-c_{d,q}.
\]

The constant is defined as the real part of `logDerivMean d q 1`, and the
project separately proves that it is the constant making the remainder tend
to zero. The criterion says:

> If, for every such auxiliary prime `d`, one has
> `R_{d,q}(x) ≤ x^(β−1)` for all sufficiently large real `x`, then every
> Dirichlet L-function modulo `q` is nonzero at every `ρ ≠ 1` with
> `Re(ρ) > β`.

The threshold in “sufficiently large” may depend on `d`, `q`, and `β`. Uniformity
in the auxiliary discriminant is not needed for this qualitative conclusion.

This is not an assertion that a small numerical experiment controls all zeros.
The hypothesis is an asymptotic theorem, for every auxiliary prime of the
class. The construction producing the estimate uses very large scales.

### 3.1 Transform and residues

Write `Eχ(s)` for `L(s,χ)`, with the principal-character pole removed by its
holomorphic factor `(s−1)L(s,χ)`. Let

\[
 m(s)=\sum_{\chi\bmod q}\operatorname{ord}_s E_\chi,
 \qquad
 m_d(s)=\sum_{\chi\bmod q}\operatorname{ord}_s E_{\chi\chi_d}.
\]

`isPolar_primeRemainder`, in `PrimeError.lean` lines 405-441, proves that

\[
 \Phi_{d,q}(s)=\int_0^\infty R_{d,q}(e^v)e^{(1-s)v}\,dv
             =\int_1^\infty R_{d,q}(x)x^{-s}\,dx
\]

continues meromorphically and has residue

\[
 r_{d,q}(\rho)=\frac{m(\rho)-m_d(\rho)}
 {\varphi(q)\rho(1-\rho)}
\]

at `ρ ≠ 0,1`. The singularity at 1 is removable and its residue is zero.

The difference of multiplicities is essential: a zero of the target product
can cancel against a zero of the auxiliary product. Thus a bound on this
remainder is not automatically a bound for zeros of either factor separately.

### 3.2 How auxiliary twists prevent cancellation

`exists_entireL_quadTwist_ne_zero`, in
`Lib/Analytic/QuadraticTwist.lean` lines 789-835, chooses one prime `d` in the
specified class such that **all** its quadratic twists are zero-free in any
specified box

\[
 \operatorname{Re}(s)\ge\beta,\qquad |\operatorname{Im}(s)|\le H.
\]

The proof supplies a weak zero-density estimate
`# bad primes up to X = O(X^(1−δ/2))` for `β=1/2+9δ`, based on smoothed theta
sums and Pólya–Vinogradov. This is compared with the divergence of
`∑ log(p)/p` over the reduced residue class. This auxiliary result is explicitly
classed as known, with sources Jutila and Heath-Brown, although the formulation
and proof route are tailored to the repository.

For a putative zero `ρ`, take `H=|Im(ρ)|`. Then `m_d(ρ)=0`; also `m_d(x)=0` for
all real `x≥β`. In the real interval `(β,1)`, the transform residue is
nonnegative because `m(x)≥0` and `x(1−x)>0`. For `x≥1`, the relevant entire
L-functions are already nonzero. This is precisely the sign condition needed
by the next lemma.

### 3.3 The general Landau lemma

`New.IsPolar.landau`, in `Lib/Analytic/Landau.lean` lines 212-273, says:

> Suppose `E` is measurable, has at most exponential growth
> `|E(v)|≤C e^v` for `v≥0`, and its transform
> `Φ(s)=∫_0^∞ E(v)e^((1−s)v)dv` continues meromorphically with at most simple
> poles in `Re(s)>0`, of residues `r(s)`. If `β>0`,
> `E(v)≤e^((β−1)v)` eventually, and `Re(r(x))≥0` for every real `x>β`, then
> `r(ρ)=0` whenever `Re(ρ)>β`.

(The source's `IsPolar` asks for a global meromorphic function; only the stated
half-plane is needed in the argument.)

Proof idea with all crucial signs: enlarge a constant `K` so that
`A(v)=K e^(βv)−E(v)e^v≥0` on `v≥0`. Its Laplace transform equals
`K/(s−β)−Φ(s)` far to the right. A nonnegative measure's Laplace transform has
a singularity at its finite abscissa of convergence (Landau). If that abscissa
were a real number `a>β`, the meromorphic continuation could have at most a
simple pole there, of residue `−r(a)≤0`. Positivity as `s` approaches `a` from
the right forces that residue to be nonnegative too. It is therefore zero,
so the singularity is removable, contradicting Landau. The abscissa is at most
`β`; the transform, and hence `Φ`, is holomorphic to its right.

This is the independently reusable analytic component. The serious arithmetic
work is proving the bound and the required sign/noncancellation hypotheses in
a new setting.

## 4. A derived zero-divisor rigidity proposition

The following is a transparent deduction from the preceding interface. It is
not claimed as a new theorem in the literature.

**Proposition (one-sided remainder forces equality of zero divisors).**
Let `0<β<1` and `c>0`. Let `F_1,…,F_r` and `G_1,…,G_t` be holomorphic functions
on `Re(s)>0`, not identically zero, and nonzero on `Re(s)≥1`. Write `m_F(s)` and
`m_G(s)` for the respective sums of their zero multiplicities. Suppose a real,
measurable function `E(v)` has growth `|E(v)|≤C e^v` for `v≥0` and its transform
continues meromorphically to `Re(s)>0`, with at most simple poles, removable
singularity at 1, and residues

\[
 r(s)=\frac{m_F(s)-m_G(s)}{c s(1-s)}\quad(s\ne1).
\]

Assume `E(v)≤exp((β−1)v)` eventually and
`m_F(x)≥m_G(x)` for all real `β<x<1`. Then

\[
 m_F(s)=m_G(s)\qquad(\operatorname{Re}(s)>\beta).
\]

**Proof.** For real `β<x<1`, the displayed residue has nonnegative real part,
since `c x(1−x)>0`. For `x≥1`, both multiplicities vanish and the residue is
zero (including the removable point 1). The Landau lemma forces all residues
to vanish on `Re(s)>β`. At every such `s≠1`, the denominator `c s(1−s)` is
nonzero, since `Re(s)>β>0`; hence the two multiplicities agree. At `s=1` they
both vanish by hypothesis. This proves the assertion.

**Useful refinement of the repository interface.** To rule out one target
zero `ρ`, it is enough to find one auxiliary family with no real zero in
`(β,1)` and no zero at `ρ`, and for which the one-sided bound holds. There is no
need for this auxiliary family to be zero-free at every complex point in the
entire half-plane. The repository establishes the stronger finite-box witness
using a zero-density argument. This refines the design specification for a
transfer to number-field or automorphic L-functions.

**Why a noncancellation hypothesis remains necessary.** Take `F_1=G_1=s−ρ`
with `β<Re(ρ)<1`, and `E=0`. All residues vanish and the one-sided bound holds,
yet both functions have the zero `ρ`. The conclusion is equality of divisors,
not absence of either divisor.

**Why the real-residue sign remains necessary.** Fix `0<β<α<1` and set

\[
 E(v)=-e^{(\alpha-1)v},\qquad
 \Phi(s)=-\frac1{s-\alpha}.
\]

Then `|E(v)|≤e^v`, and `E(v)≤e^((β−1)v)` for every `v≥0`, but `Φ` has a pole at
`α>β` with residue `−1`. Thus an upper bound alone cannot force a zero-free
half-plane. This explicitly prevents an erroneous generalization in which the
arithmetic sign mechanism is discarded.

## 5. Where the difficult arithmetic estimate enters

`New/Reduction/MainInequality.lean` combines three ingredients:

- A: enough short integral source forms, and controlled index of their images.
- B: translates of the Poincare map fill a positive fraction of the target
  space, by a second-moment estimate.
- C: independent integral target forms cannot all be too short, from a
  covolume lower bound obtained by twisting locally and controlling determinant
  ratios globally.

A common main term cancels, leaving

\[
 m\,\mathrm{remainder}\le f a (\log b)^5.
\]

At lines 132-156 one can literally see the comparison, cancellation, and
division by a family of size at least half the target dimension. This is a
useful abstraction of the proof, but the cancellation is backed by the
specific theta-lift, congruence, integrality, and arithmetic-height lemmas.
It is not a free numerical inequality for arbitrary prime-counting functions.

In `OneSidedBound.lean`, lines 28-67 and 124-149, the chosen number of lifts is
`m ≍ sqrt(x)`, while `log b ≤ 2 x^ε`; this produces

\[
 R_{d,q}(x)\le(64\varphi(q)+3)x^{-1/2+6\varepsilon}
\]

for all large integer `x`, and then the desired real-variable bound with any
positive exponent slack. The two much larger scales `x1` and `b` let ordinary
prime-number-theorem estimates make the extra remainder terms negligible.
This is a scale-separation mechanism, not a direct numerical evaluation of the
zeta zeros.

Finally, `OneSidedBound.lean` lines 239-267 chooses `q0=18480q`, proves that the
auxiliary class `d0=9240q−1` is coprime to it, supplies the one-sided bound,
applies the analytic criterion, and descends along change of conductor. Thus
the concluding theorem is not conditional on an uninhabited `Data` structure:
`Data/Existence.lean` proves `eventually_exists_data` (line 898), and the final
proof explicitly uses it through the bounds.

## 6. Consequences for transfer assessment

1. A direct use of `New.grh` can discharge the Dirichlet-GRH hypothesis in an
   already known implication. This is a valid potential consequence of an
   accepted proof, but is not a new proof method or a newly discovered
   conditional implication.
2. Arithmetic methods for Hecke L-functions over imaginary quadratic fields
   are a plausible first genuine extension, because the construction already
   uses CM fields, theta lifts, and anticyclotomic twists. One must still supply
   the new remainder identity and the analogues of A, B, C with exactly the
   requisite main-term cancellation and losses.
3. Arbitrary number-field GRH or automorphic GRH does not follow merely by
   renaming Dirichlet characters. Euler products, zero-density witnesses,
   local integrality, determinant formulas, and denominator control all need
   new theorems in the new setting.
4. Universal positivity-of-measures and determinant-comparison components are
   reusable even if no new conjecture is resolved. The read-through can
   motivate a precise target theorem with a clear missing inequality, rather
   than an unsupported announcement.
