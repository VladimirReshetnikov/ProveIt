# Supplement: pinned Mathlib endpoints and independent transfer check

Inspected the actual local Mathlib checkout at
`derived_lean/.lake/packages/mathlib`; `git rev-parse HEAD` returns
`56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679`, the repository's pinned commit.
This is a source-level semantic audit, not a rebuild of the GRH proof.

## 1. Standard scalars, characters, and analytic continuations

The pinned Mathlib definitions are the intended conventional objects:

- `Mathlib/Basic/Real/Basic.lean:33-41` defines `Real` through the Cauchy
  completion of the rational numbers. Its ordinary field structure is supplied
  at lines 481-496; its order is constructed from the Cauchy order.
- `Mathlib/Basic/Complex/Basic.lean:33-41` defines `Complex` with real and
  imaginary components in `ℝ`. Real embedding is `(r,0)` at lines 81-89;
  complex multiplication and the usual ring/field structures are constructed
  in the same file, including `instField` at lines 712-720. These are not
  singleton or characteristic-two fields. Consequently `1/2` in the endpoint
  is the ordinary real half.
- `Mathlib/NumberTheory/DirichletCharacter/Basic.lean:37-39` defines
  `DirichletCharacter R N` as `MulChar (ZMod N) R`.
  `Mathlib/NumberTheory/MulChar/Basic.lean:70-74` makes this a multiplicative
  monoid homomorphism that maps nonunits to zero. In particular its value at
  1 is 1; the family is not defined to consist of zero functions.
- `Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/RiemannZeta.lean:119-121` defines
  `riemannZeta` as the even Hurwitz zeta function at parameter 0. The file
  proves analyticity away from 1 (lines 138-148), value `ζ(0)=-1/2`
  (lines 150-152), the usual negative-even trivial zeros (lines 172-174),
  and the identity with `Σ_(n≥0)(n+1)^(-s)` for `Re(s)>1`
  (`zeta_eq_tsum_one_div_nat_add_one_cpow`, following line 212).
- `RiemannZeta.lean:185-186` defines **exactly**

  ```lean
  def RiemannHypothesis : Prop :=
    ∀ (s : ℂ) (_ : riemannZeta s = 0)
      (_ : ¬∃ n : ℕ, s = -2 * (n + 1)) (_ : s ≠ 1), s.re = 1 / 2
  ```

  This agrees with the expanded source statement in `grh/src/Statements.lean`.
- `Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/DirichletContinuation.lean:50-60` defines
  `DirichletCharacter.LFunction χ s := ZMod.LFunction χ s` for `[NeZero N]`.
  `Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/ZMod.lean:85-86` gives the latter as

  ```lean
  N ^ (-s) * ∑ j : ZMod N, Φ j * hurwitzZeta (toAddCircle j) s
  ```

  This is the usual finite Hurwitz-zeta formula for meromorphic continuation.
  The character specialization agrees with its Dirichlet series when
  `Re(s)>1` (DirichletContinuation lines 74-76), is holomorphic away from the
  principal-character pole (lines 90-99), and specializes to zeta at modulus 1
  (lines 66-68).

**An important semantic distinction was checked:** the endpoint uses the
analytic continuation `LFunction`, not a naive infinite sum which defaults to
zero when an unordered series is nonsummable. The pinned Mathlib documentation
explicitly distinguishes `LFunction` from `LSeries` for this reason. Thus the
critical-strip assertion does not exploit the totalization convention for
infinite sums.

Formalized meromorphic functions are still total functions, so an arbitrary
finite value is assigned at a pole. The exclusion of `s=1` for the principal
character is correct and prevents that value from being mistaken for a zero.
The usual residue of the principal-character pole is separately proved in
DirichletContinuation lines 179-194. The zeta endpoint similarly excludes 1.

The fixed endpoint definitions import only Mathlib. Therefore later changes
to proof-search priorities in `Lib/Speed.lean` do not replace their fields,
arithmetic, or L-functions. No misleading degenerate definition was found.
This judgment does not claim to have audited every foundational Mathlib proof.

## 2. Independent check of the transfer analysis

I independently reviewed `transfer_methods.md`, especially its general
Landau criterion and the proposed principal-class target for `K=Q(sqrt(-23))`.
**The substantive statements and calculations are correct**, subject to the
classical background results named there. They remain reductions: the missing
one-sided estimate is not proved by the current source.

### General analytic criterion

Local boundedness plus the eventual upper bound allows a single finite `K`
with `K exp((β-1)v)-E(v)≥0` for every `v≥0`. Therefore

\[
 F(s)=K/(s-\beta)-H(s)
\]

is the Laplace transform of the nonnegative density
`K exp(βv)-E(v)exp(v)`. Exponential growth bounds the abscissa of convergence
above. If that abscissa is a finite real `a>β`, Landau's theorem forces a
singularity at `a`. The continuation is meromorphic with at most a simple pole,
so its residue must be nonzero. Positivity from the right makes it positive,
but it is minus the nonnegative residue of `H`; contradiction. The case of
abscissa minus infinity is harmless. Thus the proof does establish
holomorphy of `H` on `Re(s)>β`, not merely vanishing of some residues.

The quantitative determinant comparison and its completion-defect variant
also follow by the displayed algebra. One small qualification is advisable:
the generic sentence “amplification `x^θ` excludes zeros right of `1-θ`” should
assume `0<θ<1`, or instead state the conclusion in
`Re(s)>max(0,1-θ)`, since the analytic continuation used in that lemma is only
specified for `Re(s)>0`. The intended `θ=1/2` case is unaffected.

### The discriminant -23 reduction

The polynomial `x^3-x-1` is irreducible by the rational-root test and has
squarefree discriminant `-23`. Its splitting field `H` has group `S3`, with
quadratic subfield `K=Q(sqrt(-23))`. The three reduced forms listed in the
analysis exhaust the discriminant `-23` classes, giving class number 3.
The only ramified rational prime is 23; its tame inertia in the splitting
field is a transposition. For additional explicit verification, modulo 23

\[
 x^3-x-1=(x-10)^2(x-3).
\]

Since this inertia maps nontrivially into `Gal(K/Q)`, its intersection with
`Gal(H/K)=A3` is trivial. Thus the cyclic cubic extension `H/K` is unramified
at every finite prime; there are no real places of `K`. By class-field theory
it is the Hilbert class field. These arguments support the identification
used in the target, rather than merely inferring it from matching degrees.

Artin induction/Hecke formalism gives

\[
 \zeta_F=\zeta\,L_K(s,\psi),\qquad
 \zeta_H=\zeta\,L(s,\chi_{-23})L_K(s,\psi)^2.
\]

The equality `L_K(s,ψ)=L_K(s,ψ^{-1})` follows from conjugating ideals: norms
are unchanged and ideal classes invert. No general Artin-entireness
conjecture is needed for this dihedral factor. The claimed equivalences of
critical-strip RH statements are correct once the indicated Dirichlet factors
are supplied, because all factors are holomorphic in that strip, leaving no
poles with which zeros could cancel.

### Principal-class selector and the exact constant

For `a` a nonzero integral ideal of `K`, a nontrivial class character has order
3 and is faithful. Therefore

\[
 \mathbf1_{[a]=1}=(1+\psi(a)+\psi(a)^2)/3.
\]

Set

\[
 A(s)=\sum_a\Lambda_K(a)\mathbf1_{[a]=1}(Na)^{-s}.
\]

Then `A(s)=-(1/3)ζ'_H(s)/ζ_H(s)` is correct, with no omitted Euler factor:
`H/K` is everywhere unramified at finite primes. For a prime ideal `p` of
class order `f∈{1,3}`, the logarithmic derivative contributes `log(Np)` at
precisely the powers `p^k` with `f|k`, exactly the principal powers.

If `ζ_H(s)=κ_H/(s-1)+κ_H γ_H+O(s-1)`, then

\[
 A(s)=\frac1{3(s-1)}-\frac{\gamma_H}{3}+O(s-1).
\]

The proposed remainder is consequently normalized correctly:

\[
 R(X)=\sum_{Na\le X}\Lambda_K(a)\mathbf1_{[a]=1}
       (1/Na-1/X)-\frac{\log X}{3}+\frac{1+\gamma_H}{3}.
\]

Termwise integration on `Re(s)>1` gives exactly

\[
 \int_1^\infty R(X)X^{-s}\,dX
 =\frac{A(s)}{s(s-1)}-\frac1{3(s-1)^2}
   +\frac{1+\gamma_H}{3(s-1)}.
\]

Expanding `1/s` at 1 confirms cancellation of **both** the double pole and the
simple pole. At a zero `ρ` of `ζ_H` with multiplicity `m`, the residue is
`m/[3ρ(1-ρ)]`, so it has the required positive sign at real `0<ρ<1`.
Dedekind zeta has no zeros on `Re(s)≥1`; the cancellation handles 1 itself.
The claimed growth justification is sound: the coefficient of norm `p^k` in
`-ζ'_K/ζ_K` is at most `2 log p=2Λ(p^k)`, and the selector only decreases it.

It follows rigorously that the proposed family of one-sided bounds for `R`
would imply RH for `ζ_H`, hence GRH for the selected Hecke function. This
direct selector needs **no auxiliary-twist nonvanishing assumption**, because
its residues are nonnegative target multiplicities already. The optional
`(1-η)` variant correctly reintroduces differences of multiplicities and then
does require an auxiliary noncancellation argument. For clarity, qualify the
sentence in section 4 about conjugate-character summation as applying to that
*difference-of-orders* setting; it should not obscure the valid direct route.

The obstruction described in the analysis is also accurate: primes inert in
`K/Q` give principal ideals `(p)` of norm `p^2`, so a class character is blind
to new class information there. The current divisibility gains at nonsplit
primes do not automatically detect classes of split primes. The proposed
arithmetic extension has therefore been identified, not solved.
