## Thin-subfamily consequence of the proof

Fix a positive q divisible by 8 and an invertible residue class a congruent to 7 mod 8. For 1/2<beta<1 let kappa=(beta-1/2)/18 and let S be a subset of the primes d congruent to a modulo q. Write S(X) for its count to X. If limsup S(X)/X^(1-kappa) is infinite and the eventual estimate R_{d,q}(x)<=x^(beta-1) holds individually for every d in S, then no L-function of a character modulo q has a zero different from 1 to the right of beta. Indeed fix a candidate zero, hence its height. For its fixed box the zero-density lemma bounds the entire bad set by O(X^(1-kappa)). Therefore some d in S is good. Use that d in steps 3–4. This uses the quantitative exceptional set which the final Dirichlet GRH statement does not contain. In particular a single subfamily of upper counting exponent 1 suffices simultaneously for all beta>1/2. It may have zero relative density, for example by keeping admissible primes with increasing indices floor(j log(j+1)), j>=2; the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions gives count of order X/(log X)^2 for fixed q. The counting condition is sufficient, with no claim of optimality. The result weakens the hypotheses for proving a zero-free statement; it does not certify a new instance outside the Dirichlet class.
