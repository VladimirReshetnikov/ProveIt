## Nearby classical signed-approximation literature

Two relevant predecessors arose in the additional targeted search:

1. F. López-Blázquez and B. Salamanca Miño, “Binomial approximation to hypergeometric probabilities,” *Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference* 87(1) (2000), 21–29. DOI 10.1016/S0378-3758(99)00187-1. The publisher abstract describes an orthogonal Krawtchouk expansion, truncations giving binomial approximations, and their limiting behavior. This is clear precedent for scalar signed hypergeometric correction methods. Full text was not available in this check. Primary metadata: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378375899001871
2. J. N. Darroch, M. Jirina, and T. P. Speed, “Sampling Without Replacement: Approximation to the Probability Distribution,” *Journal of the Australian Mathematical Society* 44(2) (1988), 197–213. DOI 10.1017/S1446788700029785. The publisher abstract states signed approximations of the complete sample law with error of order (n²/N)^(r+1) when n²/N→0. This differs from the explicit rare-defect bound uniform in ht/m≤M and independent of the group, but establishes that signed corrections for sampling without replacement are classical. Primary source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-australian-mathematical-society/article/sampling-without-replacement-approximation-to-the-probability-distribution/683413169B1FEA59066EE689E9E91336

Also relevant is Bero Roos's Krawtchouk expansion for the Poisson-binomial law (independent Bernoulli variables), which treats finite signed measures and total variation. It is background rather than a matching theorem for the present dependent, group-valued sampling law. Primary author copy: https://www.math.uni-trier.de/~roos/bin57.pdf

The searches did not locate an exact predecessor for the complete arbitrary-retained-density arithmetic classification, its converse realization at reciprocal-density boundaries, and the bounded-order fibre iff criterion. The manuscript should nevertheless keep its current candidate-novelty qualification. The scalar correction idea and general compound-Poisson principles should not be presented as new.
