FEWNOMIAL LITERATURE CHECK AND SCOPE
Research date: 27 November 2026.

1. H. W. Lenstra, Jr., On the factorization of lacunary polynomials,
   in Number Theory in Progress, vol. 1, De Gruyter (1999), 277-292.
   Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110285581.277
   Author's scanned full text:
   https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1999b/art.pdf
   Author's publication list:
   https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/pub.html

   Primary-source inspection: pages 278, 279, 280, 281, and 282 were
   read from the author-hosted scan, with visual inspection of pp. 278
   and 282 to correct OCR errors. In particular, the disk inequality
   in Theorem 3 is GREATER THAN OR EQUAL TO, not strict greater than.

   Theorem 3 (p. 278) already proves a uniform degree-independent
   upper bound C(p,k,r), for every r>0 and polynomials with at most
   k+1 nonzero terms, on zeros with v(x-1)>=r, counting multiplicity.
   The field can be any characteristic-zero valued field with v(p)=1;
   the result is therefore applicable to geometric roots. Neither
   degree independence nor the existence of an effective local upper
   bound is new in the present work.

   On p. 280, d_k(m) denotes the least common multiple of products of
   at most k distinct members of {1,...,m}. Proposition 2.2 says that
   for a prescribed set E of k+1 integer exponents, d_k(m)*binom(T,m)
   has, on E, an interpolating polynomial with integer coefficients.
   Corollary 2.3 (p. 281) expresses binom(T,m), on E, in terms of
   binom(T,i), 0<=i<=k, with coefficient denominators dividing
   d_k(m)/i!. Proposition 2.4 bounds v_p(d_k(m)) by k floor(log_p m).

   Section 3, p. 282, gives the explicit finite cutoff

     C(p,k,r) = max { m>=0 :
       m*r - v_p(d_k(m)) <= max_{0<=i<=k}(i*r - v_p(i!)) }.

   The valuation of d_k(m) can equivalently be calculated as

     v_p(d_k(m)) = sum_{a>=1} min(k, floor(m/p^a)).

   Explanation of this equivalent expression: the k integers in
   {1,...,m} with the largest p-adic valuations maximize the valuation
   of their product, and at level a the number of such integers with
   valuation >=a is min(k,floor(m/p^a)). Only finitely many terms occur.
   C is a proved upper bound, not generally the exact optimum.

   The introduction (p. 279) asks whether the BEST GLOBAL value
   B(k,L) is computable from k and data defining a fixed finite
   extension L of Q_p. That B counts all nonzero roots belonging to
   L, with no one-disk restriction. The local geometric algorithm
   developed here is a different problem. It must not be presented
   as a solution of Lenstra's stated global B(k,L) question.

2. A. J. van der Poorten and R. S. Rumely, Zeros of p-adic
   exponential polynomials II, Journal of the London Mathematical
   Society (2) 36 (1987), 1-15.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s2-36.1.1
   Publisher abstract:
   https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1112/jlms/s2-36.1.1

   The publisher abstract states a bound (n-2)p for order-n
   exponential polynomials defined over Q_p when p>n>=3, and says
   analogous bounds hold in general. The full article was not
   accessible through the publisher during this review, so no claim
   about what it does not contain is based on its abstract alone.

3. J. Maurice Rojas, Arithmetic multivariate Descartes' rule,
   American Journal of Mathematics 126 (2004), 1-30.
   Primary paper mirror:
   https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=506b0ec662ed92d9fee412b5822b60c2de238564&repid=rep1&type=pdf
   Related author preprint:
   https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0010260

   This work concerns finiteness and explicit bounds for isolated
   roots of sparse systems over local and number fields. It supplies
   important arithmetic fewnomial background; it is not evidence
   that a specific exact local profile or the sharp staircase was
   already known. Its field and root conventions must be retained
   when making comparisons.

4. P. Buergisser, A. Kulkarni, A. Lerario, Nonarchimedean integral
   geometry, Selecta Mathematica (N.S.) 32, article 10 (2026).
   Published 5 January 2026; DOI:
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s00029-025-01120-y
   Open-access primary article:
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00029-025-01120-y

   Section 7 reviews deterministic sparse root bounds and develops
   expected zero counts for random fewnomials over nonarchimedean
   local fields. Its discussion reports Lenstra's O(t^2 q e log(et))
   bound in a finite extension with residue-field size q and
   ramification index e, and O(t^2 p log t) over Q_p. Its random
   result is a different question from optimizing geometric local
   root counts over all coefficients.

ASSESSMENT

The independently derived staircase extends the pinned repository's
proved range. The finite-basis and finite-residue results aim at exact
support-sensitive optimization, rather than merely at another
upper bound. The searched primary literature does not by itself
establish publication priority for these formulations. Priority
remains subject to a fuller literature review and external review.

The work does not resolve a further prominent general conjecture.
In particular it does not solve global B(k,L) computability,
multivariate fewnomial optimization, general automorphic GRH,
Artin's primitive-root conjecture, or Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer.
