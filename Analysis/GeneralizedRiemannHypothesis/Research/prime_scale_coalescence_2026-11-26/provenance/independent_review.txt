All statements below use fixed distinct real offsets u_i, the covariance
C(h)=2 sum_{gamma>0 distinct} m_gamma^2 cos(gamma h)/(1/4+gamma^2)^2,
and tau=epsilon^3 log(1/epsilon). Long-time covariance is taken first;
epsilon then tends to zero.

1. Fixed contrast classification: approved.
If s=sum c_i is nonzero, c^T G_epsilon c -> C(0)s^2.
If s=0 but v=sum u_i c_i is nonzero, the variance is asymptotic to
D v^2 epsilon^2, where D=2 sum m_gamma^2 gamma^2/(1/4+gamma^2)^2.
If s=v=0 and c is nonzero, the variance is Theta(tau).
Every fixed, UNNORMALIZED forward difference of order d>=2 lies in
the final class. Dividing the difference by epsilon^d changes its rate.

2. Linear prediction: approved, including epsilon-dependent coefficients.
For a target coordinate c_0=1 and at least two other distinct offsets,
the affine constraints sum c_i=sum u_i c_i=0 are feasible.
A fixed feasible contrast gives the upper bound O(tau). The smallest
eigenvalue lower bound gives c^T G c >= const*tau*||c||^2, hence both
a lower prediction bound and uniform boundedness of the unique optimal
coefficient vectors. Therefore the optimal error is Theta(tau).

Under M2(T)~kappa N(T), the exact scaled error tends to
kappa min{integral S_c(t)^2 dt : c in W, c_0=1},
where W annihilates constants and linear offsets and
S_c(t)=sum c_i(t-u_i)_+.
The minimizer is unique because the energy is positive definite on W.
Optimal coefficients converge to it. No independence of zeros is used.
The limiting coefficient vector is independent of kappa.

A direct lower-limit argument uses s=sum c_i, v=sum u_i c_i,
w=sum u_i^2 c_i and the completed square
C0(s-D epsilon^2 w/(2C0))^2 + D epsilon^2 v^2
-D^2 epsilon^4 w^2/(4C0) +(kappa/12)tau*c^T K c+o(tau)||c||^2.
For bounded minimizing vectors the negative epsilon^4 term is o(tau).
Energy O(tau) forces every limit point into W, and the c_0=1 constraint
persists. Recovery uses any fixed feasible W-vector. Strict convexity
then proves convergence of all optimal coefficients.

3. Finite deletion: approved with scope stated precisely.
Delete a fixed finite set of positive ordinates, together with their
negative conjugates, so that the retained covariance is real.
For fixed c in W, each deleted frequency contributes O(epsilon^4),
which is o(tau). Cubic contrast constants, small eigenvalue constants,
and the limiting optimal prediction energy are unchanged.
C(0) and D DO change. The deletion set must be fixed independently of
epsilon. No claim of uniformity for a growing cutoff or arbitrary
epsilon-dependent deletion follows.

K_ij=|u_i-u_j|^3. On W,

The exact covariance coefficient is kappa/12, not kappa/6.
For offsets 0,1,2 and contrast (1,-2,1), the limiting coefficient is

5. Stronger Montgomery local comparison: approved.
Fix 0<alpha<1 and define
K_T(v)=sinc^2(alpha log(T) v/2)*4/(4+v^2), with sinc(x)=sin(x)/x.
Let S_T(X) be the sum of K_T(gamma-gamma') over ordered pairs
0<gamma,gamma'<=X, repeating zeros according to multiplicity.
For X=aT or bT, rewrite the kernel with parameter
alpha_X=alpha log(T)/log(X), which tends to alpha.
Montgomery's equation (6) applies uniformly for alpha_X in a compact
subset of (0,1). This uniformity follows by integrating his uniform
form-factor theorem against triangular Fourier weights. Thus
S_T(X)=(1/alpha+alpha/3+o(1))*X log(T)/(2pi).
The SAME kernel is used at both cutoffs. Therefore
M2(bT)-M2(aT) <= S_T(bT)-S_T(aT)
<= (1/alpha+alpha/3+o(1))*(b-a)T log(T)/(2pi).
Every newly included off-diagonal term is nonnegative.
Let alpha tend to 1 AFTER taking the limit in T. Together with
M2 increments >= N increments, this proves limiting local densities
between 1/(2pi) and 4/(3*2pi).

Global M2(T)<<T log T controls small-frequency and tail contributions.
It follows, uniformly on the unit sphere of W, that
K|W/12-o(1)I <= (G_epsilon|W)/tau <= K|W/9+o(1)I.
Schur-complement errors from the constant and slope blocks are o(tau),
so small eigenvalue coefficients are bracketed by eta_j/12 and eta_j/9.
For r=3 equally spaced nodes these bounds are 1/9 and 4/27.
For contrast (1,-2,1) they are 2/3 and 8/9.

The same argument gives optimal prediction brackets
E_* <= liminf error/tau <= limsup error/tau <= (4/3)E_*,
where E_*=min_{c in W,c_0=1} integral S_c^2.

6. Sources and qualifications.
Montgomery (1973), author-hosted PDF:
https://public.websites.umich.edu/~hlm/paircor1.pdf
Equation (6), journal p.182; proof of Corollary 2, pp.191-192.
His main theorem is uniform on compact subintervals of 0<=alpha<1.
It is a theorem under RH, not the pair-correlation conjecture.
The exact kappa limit is an extra assumption; RH alone gives brackets.
All zeros simple, or all but finitely many simple, implies kappa=1.
A mere density-one simple-zero assertion does not by itself justify
the squared-multiplicity asymptotic with unbounded exceptions.

Existing flat-kernel eigenvalue theory:
Barthelme--Usevich, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 42 (2021),
arXiv:1910.14067. The potential contribution is the arithmetic
logarithmic scale, multiplicity-robust explicit brackets, and the
prime-error prediction specialization.

The epsilon^3 log scale is a sharp order. The numerical upper factor
4/3 is a convenient classical constant, not a best-possible constant;
optimized Montgomery--Taylor kernels slightly improve it.
