FORMALIZATION ROADMAP -- NOT AN EXECUTABLE LEAN PROOF
===================================================

This file describes a possible Mathlib formalization of the new theorem.
It does not assert that these interfaces already exist or that the new
theorem has been checked by Lean. No sorry/admit-based file is supplied as
if it were a proof.

1. Finite multisets and partial matching

Represent every occurrence separately by a finite index type so repeated
ordinates preserve multiplicity. Define a partial injection between the two
index types and its cost:
  unmatched_count_A + unmatched_count_B
  + sum (min 1 (abs (ordinate_A i - ordinate_B j))).

Prove attainment of the minimum by finiteness; definiteness; symmetry;
and the triangle inequality by composition of partial matchings. Prove the
test-function estimate for complex w with bounds S and L:
  norm (sum_A w - sum_B w) <= max (2*S) L * edit_distance A B.

These are finite algebra/order statements and can be developed independently
of the analytic number theory.

2. Character and spectrum interfaces

Fix the existing Mathlib definitions of Dirichlet characters, primitive
conductor, Dirichlet L-functions, and the precise critical-line theorem from
the upstream Statements.lean. Record exactly where the primitive principal
character corresponds to zeta. Construct finite absolute-height windows of
the nontrivial zero divisor with natural multiplicities.

Prove conjugation reflects signed ordinates. Do not identify the zeros of a
complex character with their negatives: that property belongs to the
conjugate character.

3. Classical analytic prerequisites

Formalize or locate:
  - completed Dirichlet L-functions and their functional equation;
  - entire order-one factorization with canonical products;
  - the real part of the centered logarithmic derivative;
  - gamma logarithmic derivative <= log on the positive real axis;
  - a Weil explicit formula for compact logarithmic-frequency support.

The last interface must include the conductor term, both oriented arithmetic
terms, gamma integral, and the two zeta pole terms. Removing the terms only
after verifying support conditions prevents a normalization/sign mismatch.

4. Angular prime separation

Use the centered logarithmic derivative to prove the coarse count under the
critical-line input. For the triangular logarithmic bump, apply the explicit
formula and bound the zero contribution by partial summation. Prove the
ramified Euler-factor bound and higher-prime-power bound. The numerical
contradiction at x = 10^6 (log(2M))^2 then gives a prime with Re psi(p)<=1/2.
Uniqueness of primitive inducing characters makes the quotient of two
distinct primitive characters nonprincipal after induction to the lcm.

5. Compact kernel and cutoff refinement

Define the m-fold uniform convolution as a probability density. For even m,
write it as g*g; derive f(0)>=1/h and f<=f(0) by Cauchy-Schwarz. Define
phi_H=f/f(0)*sinc(Hu/8)^2. Prove its support, value at zero, mass bounds,
transform-as-triangular-average, and explicit tail inequality.

The nontrivial integration steps are Tonelli/Fubini with nonnegative or
absolutely integrable integrands, differentiating a compact integral, and
Stieltjes partial summation for the zero counting measure. The paper gives
the required majorants and endpoint terms.

6. Quantitative assembly

Prove the scalar inequalities for m, H, gamma and pole corrections, and the
1/8 zero tail. Apply the finite test-function lemma at the separating prime.
Choose X_Q, m_Q, and H_Q as in Theorem 1.1 and simplify the constants.

The corruption and decoder corollaries are then short applications of the
metric triangle inequality and the angular prime gap.

Boundary conditions to preserve
-------------------------------
Q>=3; X>=2; even m>=8; H>=256mX; inclusive |gamma|<=H windows;
strict < half-separation for unique decoding; every zero multiplicity;
signed ordinates; zero-count envelope only needed above height 2;
zeta pole correction whenever conductor=1.

Verification boundary
---------------------
The Python files in this package check computations and examples. They are
not imported assumptions, proof terms, or substitutes for the analytic
formalization obligations listed above.
