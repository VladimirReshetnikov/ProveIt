## Points checked independently

1. **Complex character Hadamard constant.** The functional equation gives modulus symmetry on the real axis of the centered completed function even when the character is nonreal. Each genus-one factor belonging to a zero `i gamma` has modulus `sqrt(1+u^2/gamma^2)` at a real argument `u`; its exponential part has modulus one. The modulus of the canonical product is therefore even without any assumption that ordinates for one character occur in pairs. This forces `Re B=0` and justifies the positive Poisson-kernel sum, including central multiplicity `r/u`.
2. **Order-one convergence.** The completed function is entire of order one (or xi for the principal primitive character). Its canonical product has `sum |gamma|^{-2}<infinity`, so the modulus differentiation and subsequent real-part sum converge locally away from the zeros. No prior zero-count estimate is being used circularly.
3. **Euler derivative bound.** Monotonicity of `log(t)t^{-5/2}` for `t>=2` gives the stated sum bound by the first term plus the integral, exactly `( (7/12)log 2 + 2/9 )/sqrt 2 < 1/2`.
4. **Digamma bound and coarse count.** Jensen applied to a gamma variable gives `psi_Gamma(y)<=log y`. The factor `7e/(8pi)` in the nonprincipal estimate follows from `(T+3/2)/(2T)<=7/8` for `T>=2`. For xi, the rational terms are at most `16/15` and `(T+1/2)/(2pi T)<=5/(8pi)`. The proposed counts follow.
5. **Triangle explicit formula.** The Fourier transform of `(1-|v|)_+` is `sinc(t/2)^2`. At the zeta poles `t=+/-i/2`, the common factor is `(sinh(1/4)/(1/4))^2`. The conductor term is zero because the frequency support lies strictly to the right of zero. The negative-frequency prime sum is absent. The gamma correction has the stated negative sign.
6. **Zero-sum bound.** The small zeros contribute at most `4 log(4q)` and the large ones at most `8(log(4q)+1)`. Adding the gamma error is safely bounded by `B(q)=20log(2q)+21`.
7. **Ramified loss.** Since `1/(sqrt p-1)<=sqrt 2+1<5/2`, the omitted Euler factors cost at most `(5/2)log M`.
8. **Prime-power loss.** The factor `1/(1-1/sqrt 2)<4` yields the bound by `4 sum_{n<=sqrt(ex)}log n/n`. The integral estimate gives exactly `0.5(1+log x)^2+4`.
9. **Angular contradiction.** If each relevant prime has real part greater than `1/2`, then the first powers contribute at least half their principal sum and all higher powers cost at most `3/2 E_pp`. The constants combine as `47.5L+89+1.5(1+log x)^2`. With `x=10^6L^2`, this is at most `333L`, while the main term exceeds `1000L`.
10. **Distinct conductors.** The quotient on the least common multiple is nonprincipal when the two primitive characters differ, by uniqueness of the primitive inducing character. The inequality `log(2M)<=2log(2Q)` gives the stated universal prime bound `12*10^6 log^2(2Q)`.
