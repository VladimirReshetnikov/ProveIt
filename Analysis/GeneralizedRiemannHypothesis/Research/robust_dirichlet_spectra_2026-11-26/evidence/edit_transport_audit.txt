# Finite-window edit/transport separation: independent audit

This note gives a fully specified analytic interface. It assumes the separating-prime lemma and the zero-count envelope; it does not prove those inputs. All zeros below are nontrivial zeros, with multiplicities. For a complex character, use the full signed set of ordinates of that character. Do not replace it by twice the positive ordinates.

## Input and conclusion

Let `X >= 2`, `A > 0`, `B >= 1`. Assume primitive Dirichlet characters under consideration satisfy GRH and

`N_chi(t) := #{gamma : |gamma| <= t} <= A (t+1) log(B(t+2))` for all `t >= 1`.

Assume every distinct pair has a prime `p <= X`, unramified for both characters, such that

`|chi_1(p) - chi_2(p)| >= 1`.

The angle condition `Re(chi_1(p) conjugate(chi_2(p))) <= 1/2` implies this.

Define

* `h = 1/(64 X)`;
* `D = 1 + log(B(256 X + 2))`;
* `Z = 80 A sqrt(X) D`;
* `m` to be any even integer at least 8 and at least `(2/log 3) log Z`;
* `T_* = 128 m X = 2m/h`.

For every real `H >= 2 T_*`, let `Gamma_chi(H)` be the finite multiset of ordinates in `[-H,H]`. Then

`d_edit(Gamma_chi1(H), Gamma_chi2(H)) >= (5 log 2)/(128 pi) * H/sqrt(X)`.

Here a partial matching has cost equal to the number of unmatched points of both multisets plus `sum min(1, |gamma-gamma'|)` over its matched pairs; `d_edit` is the minimum cost. Multiplicities are individual points.

If `X = C (log(2Q))^2`, `A` is absolute, and `B = C' Q^c` for absolute constants, then `m=O(log log(2Q))`, `T_*=O((log(2Q))^2 log log(2Q))`, and separation is at least an absolute constant times `H/log(2Q)`. Every observed multiset within less than half this separation of one candidate has a unique such candidate. This is an information-theoretic identification statement; it does not by itself specify an efficient search over candidate characters.

## Filter

Let `f=f_{m,h}` be the probability density of the sum of `m` independent uniform variables on `[-h/m,h/m]`. It is nonnegative, even, supported in `[-h,h]`, and has Fourier transform

`F_f(t) = sinc(ht/m)^m`, where `sinc(x)=sin(x)/x` and `sinc(0)=1`.

For even `m`, write `f=g*g` where `g` is the density of the first `m/2` variables. Since `g` is even and supported in an interval of length `h`,

`f(0)=integral g^2 >= 1/h`.

Cauchy--Schwarz also gives `f(u) <= f(0)` for every `u`. These two facts require no separate unimodality theorem.

For the actual observation cutoff `H`, put

`phi(u) = f(u)/f(0) * sinc(Hu/8)^2`.

Then `phi >= 0`, `phi(0)=1`, `supp(phi) subset [-h,h]`, `phi <= 1`, and

`integral phi <= integral sinc(Hu/8)^2 = 8 pi/H`.

Put `K(t)=integral phi(u) exp(i t u) du` and `b_p(t)=exp(i t log p) K(t)`. With Fourier convention `hat b(v)=integral b(t) exp(-i t v) dt`,

`hat b_p(v)=2 pi phi(v-log p)`.

Because `h < log(1+1/X)` and `p<=X`, this transform isolates the single integer `p` in the arithmetic side of the explicit formula and has no negative-frequency arithmetic support. It also vanishes at zero, removing the conductor term.

## Explicit formula including the principal character

Let `a=0` or `1` be the parity of the primitive character. Write `delta_chi=1` only for the primitive principal character of conductor 1, and zero otherwise. Define the scaled corrections

`G_a(p) = sqrt(p)/log p * integral_{-h}^h phi(u) exp(-(a+1/2)(log p+u))/(1-exp(-2(log p+u))) du`,

`P(p) = 1/log p * integral_{-h}^h phi(u) [exp(-u/2)+p exp(u/2)] du`.

The Weil explicit formula gives

`chi(p) = delta_chi P(p) - G_a(p) - sqrt(p)/log p * sum_gamma b_p(gamma)`.

The principal term equals `sqrt(p)/log p * [b_p(i/2)+b_p(-i/2)]`. The sign of the gamma term is negative. The `log(q/pi)` term is zero because `hat b_p(0)=0`. Complex characters require signed ordinates; the gamma correction is still real because the two gamma integrals combine into `Re digamma`.

The test has sufficient decay for the explicit formula: `m>=8`, and the tail estimate below shows absolute convergence of the zero sum. Equivalently one may apply the formula first to smooth approximations to the compactly supported B-spline filter and pass to the limit.

## Correction bounds

Since `h<=1/128`, `log 2>1/2`, `exp(h/2)<4/3` and `exp(2h)<4/3`,

`0 <= G_a(p) <= (2h/log 2) exp(h/2)/(1-exp(2h)/4) <= 4h/log 2 <= 1/16`.

In particular the difference of two gamma corrections is at most `1/16`, not `1/8`, since each lies in the same interval.

For the pole correction, using the `L1` bound for `phi`, `p+1 <= (3/2)X`, and `H>=256mX`,

`0 <= P(p) <= (p+1)exp(h/2)/log p * 8pi/H <= 32pi X/H <= pi/(8m) <= pi/64 < 1/16`.

Thus for any pair, including a principal/nonprincipal pair,

`|delta_1 P - G_a1 - (delta_2 P - G_a2)| <= 1/8`.

The small pole term depends on using the filter at the actual observation cutoff `H`; it cannot simply be omitted for conductor 1.

## Uniform zero tail

Let `tau_H(v)=(4/H)(1-4|v|/H)_+`. It is a probability density on `[-H/4,H/4]`. Fourier convolution yields

`K(t) = 1/f(0) * integral tau_H(v) sinc(h(t-v)/m)^m dv`.

For `|t|>=H`,

`|K(t)| <= h [4m/(3h|t|)]^m`.

For a counting function `N(t)<=A(t+1)log(B(t+2))`, integration by parts gives

`sum_{|gamma|>H} |gamma|^{-m} <= A m (H+1) H^{-m} [log(B(H+2))/(m-1) + 1/(m-1)^2]`.

This follows by discarding the nonpositive boundary term and using, for `t>=H`,

`t+1 <= (H+1)t/H`, and `log(B(t+2)) <= log(B(H+2))+log(t/H)`.

Consequently the scaled zero tail is at most

`E(H) = (A sqrt(X)/log 2) h m (H+1) [4m/(3hH)]^m [log(B(H+2))/(m-1)+1/(m-1)^2]`.

For fixed `m>=4`, this bound decreases for `H>=1`. Indeed the relevant varying factor is `(H+1)H^{-m}(log(B(H+2))+1/(m-1))`; its logarithmic derivative is at most `(2-m)/H` because `log(B(H+2))>=log 3>1`.

At `H0=4m/h=256mX`, the bracketed power is `3^{-m}`. Since `m>=4`,

`E(H0) <= 10 A sqrt(X) m [1+log(B(H0+2))] 3^{-m}`.

Since `H0+2 <= m(256X+2)`, the logarithmic factor is at most `D+log m <= Dm`. Hence

`E(H) <= 10 A sqrt(X) D m^2 3^{-m}`.

For even `m>=8`, `m^2<=3^{m/2}` (base case `64<=81` and induction in steps of two), while the choice of `m` gives `3^{m/2}>=Z`. Therefore `E(H)<=1/8` uniformly for all `H>=H0`.

## Separation and edit metric

Set `w_p(t)=sqrt(p)/log p * b_p(t)`. Its sup norm and Lipschitz constant satisfy

`S_p := sup |w_p| <= 8pi sqrt(p)/(H log p)`,

`L_p := sup |w_p'| <= 8pi sqrt(p)(log p+h)/(H log p)`.

For a matched pair,

`|w_p(t)-w_p(s)| <= max(2S_p,L_p) min(1,|t-s|)`.

The factor `2S_p` is essential when clipped matching costs are used. For unmatched points the contribution is at most `S_p`. Thus every partial matching gives

`|sum_Gamma1 w_p - sum_Gamma2 w_p| <= C_p * matching_cost`,

where

`C_p=max(2S_p,L_p) <= 16pi sqrt(X)/(H log 2)`.

On the other hand the separating prime, the correction difference bound `1/8`, and the two tails `1/8` each give

`|sum_Gamma1 w_p - sum_Gamma2 w_p| >= 1-1/8-1/8-1/8=5/8`.

Taking the minimum over matchings proves the announced lower bound.

## Scope and cautions

* The matching metric automatically handles unequal zero counts and cutoff-crossing perturbations as insertions/deletions.
* An observed multiset with `r` erasures, `s` insertions and matched ordinate perturbations `epsilon_j` has edit distance at most `r+s+sum min(1,|epsilon_j|)`.
* If only nonprincipal characters are considered, the correction difference is at most `1/16`, and `5/8` may be replaced by `11/16` without other changes.
* No lower bound on distances between distinct zeros is required, and multiplicities are permitted.
* The proof establishes order-uniform robustness once the angular separating-prime lemma is proved. A lemma that only distinguishes roots of unity by a tiny angle is insufficient for the advertised order-uniform constant.
* Standard Dirichlet GRH is the upstream input; no Artin or general automorphic GRH is used.
