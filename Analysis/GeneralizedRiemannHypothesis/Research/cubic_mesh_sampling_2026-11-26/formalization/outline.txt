1. Data and definitions (proposed; names are not existing declarations)

Use real variable x>=1, parameter beta>0, a>=0, and E:R->R.
Set M(x)=a*x*Real.log x+c*x.
Assume ConvexOn R (Set.Ici 1) (fun x => E x+M x).

A mesh should be represented by:
  x : N -> R
  1<=x 0
  StrictMono x
  Tendsto x atTop atTop.

Endpoint bounds:
  F : N -> R
  forall j, 0<=F j
  forall j, E(x j)<=F j.

Define delta j=x(j+1)-x j and intervalEnergy:
  (F j+F(j+1))*delta j/(2*(x j)^(beta+1))
  + a*(delta j)^3/(12*(x j)^(beta+2)),
where real powers are Real.rpow and all bases are positive.

Define finite cumulative energy either with a Finset initial segment bounded
by x j<=X, or as a nonnegative discrete measure on left endpoints.
The left-endpoint convention handles the final straddling interval cleanly.

2. Convex interpolation and defect bounds

Prove generic chord notation and its affine properties on [A,B], A<B.

Main local lemmas:
  convex_upper_chord:
    ConvexOn R [A,B] (E+M) -> E(x)<=chord E(x)+chord M(x)-M(x).

  log_center_second_derivative:
    M''(x)=a/x for x>0.

  chord_defect_upper:
    0<=a -> 1<=A -> A<=x -> x<=B ->
    chord M(x)-M(x)<=a*(x-A)*(B-x)/(2*A).

  chord_defect_lower:
    ... >=a*(x-A)*(B-x)/(2*B).

One route avoids a specialized Green kernel: subtract the upper quadratic
a*(x-A)*(B-x)/(2A) from the defect, compute its second derivative, apply
convexity/concavity from second-derivative sign, and use zero boundary values.
This uses standard real differentiability and convexity infrastructure.

Then show:
  max(E(x),0)<=chord F(x)+defect(x),
since the right side is nonnegative.

Integrate against x^(-beta-1). Explicitly prove:
  integral_A^B (x-A)*(B-x) dx=(B-A)^3/6;
  integral_A^B (B-x)/(B-A) dx=(B-A)/2,
and the symmetric affine coefficient identity.
Use monotonicity of the weight for beta>-1 to derive intervalEnergy.

3. Subpolynomial budget and positive-part integrability

Assume:
  for every eps>0 there exist C,X0 such that for X>=X0,
  cumulativeEnergy(X)<=C*X^eps.

For sigma>beta, put delta=sigma-beta>0 and choose eps=delta/2.
Partition the left endpoints into dyadic blocks [2^k,2^(k+1)).
The energy after multiplication by (x j)^(-delta) is bounded by a convergent
geometric series O(2^(-k*delta/2)).

Conclude:
  IntegrableOn (fun x => max(E x,0)*x^(-sigma-1)) (Set.Ici 1).

Implementation choices:
- nonnegative Bochner integrals can be handled through ENNReal lintegrals;
- prove disjoint interval decomposition up to endpoint null sets;
- obtain complex-parameter analyticity by domination on compact substrips,
  always selecting a smaller real exponent still above beta.

4. Weighted-positive-part version of Landau's theorem

Work with Phi(s)=integral_1^infinity E(x)*x^(-s-1)dx.
Assume an initial half-plane of absolute convergence, meromorphic continuation,
at most simple poles in Re(s)>beta, and nonnegative real residues.

Set P(s)=Mellin(max E 0) and N(s)=Mellin(max(-E)0).
The positive-part integrability makes P holomorphic on Re(s)>beta.
Initially N=P-Phi. N is a positive transform.

Prove a reusable positive-transform singularity lemma:
  a finite real abscissa of convergence cannot be a regular point.
Use Taylor expansion at a real point to the right, nonnegative moments,
and monotone convergence/Tonelli to extend convergence to the left.

At a putative convergence abscissa alpha>beta for N, the meromorphic function
P-Phi has residue -Res Phi(alpha)<=0. Positivity rules out a negative residue;
zero residue plus the simple-pole hypothesis makes the singularity removable.
Contradict the singularity lemma. Conclude N is holomorphic on Re(s)>beta,
hence Phi=P-N has no poles there.

5. Arithmetic specialization

Existing repository declarations:
  grh/src/New/Reduction/Zeros/PrimeError.lean
    New.lambdaKResidueClass
    New.lambdaKResidueClass_nonneg
    New.primeRemainder
    New.isPolar_primeRemainder

Prove the normalization identity E(x)=x*primeRemainder(d,q,x), and its expression
as a locally finite sum of convex hinges minus a*x*log x+c*x, with a=1/phi(q).
The sign convention for c must match the report: E=C-a*x*log x-c*x.

The repository's IsPolar uses logarithmic variable v=log x:
  Phi(s)=integral_0^infinity R(exp v)*exp((1-s)v)dv.
Substitution x=exp v identifies this with integral_1^infinity R(x)x^(-s)dx,
and with integral_1^infinity E(x)x^(-s-1)dx. Check this shift explicitly.

For a candidate zero rho, use:
  grh/src/Lib/Analytic/QuadraticTwist.lean
    New.exists_entireL_quadTwist_ne_zero
to choose a d whose twisted L-functions have no zeros in
  beta<=Re(s), |Im(s)|<=|Im(rho)|.

Use the generalized Landau theorem, with the repository's residue formula:
  (sum untwisted orders-sum twisted orders)/(phi(q)*rho*(1-rho)).
The twisted orders vanish. The real residues above beta are nonnegative because
t*(1-t)>0 for beta<t<1; at t>=1 the normalized L-functions have no zeros.
Handle s=1 by removability, not substitution into the displayed fraction.
The residue at rho must vanish, so each untwisted nonnegative order is zero.

6. Sparse auxiliary-prime and sampling corollaries

Sparse d corollary:
  Use New.zero_density_quadTwist with delta=(beta-1/2)/9.
  The bad count is O(X^(1-(beta-1/2)/18)).
  A set S whose ratio to this power has unbounded limsup contains a good d.
  No uniformity in the endpoint-energy constants across d is required.
  Preserve the strict exponent inequality; the boundary case is unsupported.

Mesh scale corollaries:
  regular gaps O(x^theta) give subpolynomial cubic energy for
    theta<=(beta+1)/2;
  geometrically separated gaps O(x^eta) give it for
    eta<=(beta+2)/3.
  At beta=1/2 these exponents are 3/4 and 5/6.
