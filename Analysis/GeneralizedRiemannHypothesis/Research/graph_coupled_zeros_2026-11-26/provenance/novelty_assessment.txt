Candidate contribution
----------------------
For the explicitly defined rooted-forest differential polynomial P_G,
the complete finite theorem identifies a fixed symmetric matrix K_G,
proves that every ordered zero lies to the left of the corresponding
zero of (product p_i)', and derives the exact relation

 W_1(zero law of P_G, zero law of (product p_i)')
   = sum_{i<j} d_i d_j R_ij / [N(N-1)].

The equality is independent of all input root locations.  A single-edge
conductance change has an exact positive rank-one matrix realization,
giving interlacing and an exact root-independent transport budget.
This combined formulation and these electrical transport identities are
the candidate original contribution.  The proof is complete, but the
priority claim remains provisional.

What is classical, and should be credited
-----------------------------------------
1. Matrix-tree and matrix-forest determinant formulas.  Relevant primary
   reference: Pavel Chebotarev and Elena Shamis, "Matrix-Forest Theorems",
   arXiv:math/0602575, manuscript 1994–1997; arXiv 2006, v2 2023.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0602575
   This treats rooted-forest determinants and weighted multigraphs.
   Our vertex-variable identity follows directly by adjoining one root.

2. The fact that the roots of p' are eigenvalues of the compression of
   the diagonal root matrix to the all-ones orthogonal complement is
   classical differentiator theory, not a new theorem here.
   Rajesh Pereira, "Differentiators and the geometry of polynomials",
   J. Math. Anal. Appl. 285 (2003), 336–348.
   DOI: 10.1016/S0022-247X(03)00465-7.
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022247X03004657
   Pereira credits Davis (1959) for the differentiator concept.

3. Real-root preservation by stable differential operators has a general
   existing theory.  In our notation, P_G is obtained by applying
   det(L+diag(partial_{z_1},...,partial_{z_n})) to product_i p_i(z_i),
   then setting every z_i=z.  The differential symbol det(L-diag(w_i))
   is nonzero when every Im(w_i)>0.  Thus basic real-rootedness belongs
   within the Borcea–Brändén framework.
   Julius Borcea and Petter Brändén,
   "The Lee–Yang and Pólya–Schur Programs. I. Linear Operators Preserving
   Stability", Invent. Math. 177 (2009), 541–569.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.0401
   DOI: 10.1007/s00222-009-0189-3.
   Part II, "Theory of Stable Polynomials and Applications",
   Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 62 (2009), 1595–1631.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.3087
   DOI: 10.1002/cpa.20295.

4. Schur complements, the matrix determinant lemma, inverse rank-one
   updates, Hermitian eigenvalue interlacing, and min–max monotonicity
   are standard linear algebra.  The rank-one perturbation interpretation
   is not a new general perturbation theorem.

5. Weighted sums of effective resistances are standard versions of
   weighted Kirchhoff indices.  Here the weights d_i are polynomial
   degrees, not graph-theoretic vertex degrees.  Do not claim the
   resistance invariant itself is new.

6. Herglotz determinant interlacing and selfadjoint coupling have a large
   existing literature.  The entire-function extension and collision
   formulas should be presented as explicit extensions/application
   within that context, not as a new general theory of Herglotz functions.
   The prior-results agent identified Reiffenstein (arXiv:2108.10746)
   and Simonov–Woracek (2024) as nearby references.
