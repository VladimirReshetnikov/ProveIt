# Lean formalization roadmap (uncompiled)

The supplied manuscript is a conventional mathematical proof, not a kernel-verified extension. The following decomposition isolates the obligations. It avoids adding new RH axioms or assuming stronger L-function hypotheses. These are proposed interfaces, not claims about existing Mathlib declaration names.

## 1. Finite model

Represent `V = Fin r → ZMod p`, with `Fact (Nat.Prime p)` and finite dual given by the dot product. Represent signed packets by `V → ℤ` with zero sum. Define positive mass as a natural number and use integer histograms `n(v,j)` of the pushforward along the dot product. Prove local equality via the histogram and Fourier inversion. The group-ring version is convenient: use the quotient `ℤ[T]/(cyclotomic p ℤ)` and its complex embedding at a primitive root. Prove equality of local polynomials iff all histogram differences vanish. The trace lemma requires the degree `p-1` of the prime cyclotomic polynomial.

## 2. Integral cyclotomic algebra

Prove augmentation lattice isomorphism: the image of the zero-sum vectors is the principal ideal generated by `1-ζ`; adjacent differences give a basis. Prove `p = unit * (1-ζ)^(p-1)`, and identify the residue quotient with `ZMod p`. For a nonzero histogram, use the binomial expansion and Newton identities at sizes `< p` to get the valuation bound. An alternative is to define divisibility in the quotient ring first and avoid explicit local field machinery. The finite code checks use exactly this quotient-ring representation.

## 3. Polynomial support

Use finite differences to express the leading residue in the binomial basis. Prove the Galois scaling law under multiplication by `λ : (ZMod p)ˣ`. Uniqueness of reduced multivariate polynomials then gives a homogeneous representative. Prove the affine weight bound by induction and the projective weight bound by point-hyperplane incidence as in the paper. These parts are elementary and should not need analytic libraries.

## 4. Equality and constructions

The split construction uses the cyclic unit group of `ZMod p`, the nonsplit construction the norm-one subgroup of the finite field of order `p^2`. Fourier support/coset invariance is a finite lemma. For necessity of divisibility, there are two substantial external formalization tasks: Ghorpade–Ludhani Theorem 4.3 (classification of minimum-weight projective Reed–Muller codewords) and Blokhuis–Korchmáros–Mazzocca Theorem 5.1 (one collinear component of a dual 3-net), including their finite-field affine classification. They can be isolated as explicitly named theorem interfaces until formal proofs are developed. Do not represent the resulting full theorem as axiom-free until those obligations are discharged. The unconditional lower bound, all explicit constructions, the complete exact results at orders 3 and 5, and the saturation theorem do not require these classification results.

## 5. Analytic interface

The upstream readable package exports `LFunction_eq_zero_re : DirichletLZerosOnLine` and `LFunction_ne_zero_of_half_lt_re : DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf` in `Main.lean`. The exact types are in `Statements.lean` (they quantify over every modulus `N : ℕ` with `[NeZero N]`). To integrate, use that package's pinned toolchain and Mathlib rather than silently mixing it with the ProveIt root toolchain. Rebuild according to its README. The required analytic bridge is the uniform smoothed prime estimate for a single character; it uses the ordinary explicit formula, zero counting, primitive induction, and removal of prime powers. Prove a Fourier linear-combination estimate, then the finite detection bound follows immediately from Parseval and nonnegativity. The extremal size recovery additionally needs the equality support classification and elementary rounding.

## 6. Check boundary

The delivered Python suite checks finite examples, not a universal theorem. A future Lean package should report axiom dependencies for every exported result and avoid `sorry`, `admit`, or unproved assertions disguised as axioms. In particular the theorem on universal minima for p=3,5 is a good first target, before the full equality classification.
