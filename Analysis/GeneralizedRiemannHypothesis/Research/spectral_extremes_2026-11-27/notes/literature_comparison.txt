LITERATURE COMPARISON FOR CONDITIONAL SPECTRAL EXTREMES
Checked 27 November 2026. This is a targeted comparison, not a priority certificate.

Proposed theorem examined
------------------------
Let N(T) be regularly varying with positive index alpha<p and let G_j be
independent Gamma(theta,1) variables. Condition X=sum G_j/gamma_j^p on
X<=x as x decreases to zero. With exact saddle t=u^p and exact threshold
b_u determined by the tilted exceedance mass 1, the marked extremes
(t G_j/gamma_j^p-b_u, b_u(gamma_j/u)^p) converge to a Poisson process
with product intensity e^{-z} dz times Gamma(alpha/p,1). The conditioning
slack t(x-X) tends to an independent Exp(1) variable. The winner's spectral
height is u/b_u^(1/p), smaller than the bulk scale u.

1. Direct repository predecessors
---------------------------------
Pinned ProveIt commit: 00cadb961bbe77c74e5d082c4948f8884d58a970.
https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/00cadb961bbe77c74e5d082c4948f8884d58a970/Analysis/GeneralizedRiemannHypothesis/Article/sections/ana.tex

The source contains the same spectral gamma-sum model and its saddle
analysis. In particular ana:thm:condlaw gives finite-coordinate and
macroscopic bridge limits; ana:thm:block gives total-variation equivalence
of every submacroscopic block h=o(u) to independent tilted gamma
coordinates, jointly with the exponential slack; ana:thm:macro identifies
the macroscopic independence obstruction; ana:cor:fourfifths gives the
untilted-rate transition; ana:thm:medianheight treats the height carrying
half the mass.

The proposed theorem uses those tools to determine extreme individual
contributions and their spectral locations. Their threshold is logarithmic,
their height scale is u/(log N(u))^(1/p), and their limiting location mark
is Gamma(alpha/p,1), independent of gamma shape theta. Those are different
observables and scales from the source's mass quantile and bulk bridge.
Text searches across all current Article sections returned no occurrence
of Gumbel, marked Poisson, Poisson point process, or the tested phrases
for largest contribution / winning spectral height. This supports a
repository-level distinction, but search alone is not an exhaustive
mathematical nonduplication argument. The relevant conditional-law section
was also read directly.

2. Gamma and Dirichlet maxima
-----------------------------
Arup Bose, Amites Dasgupta and Krishanu Maulik,
Maxima of Dirichlet and triangular arrays of gamma variables,
Statistics & Probability Letters 78(16) (2008), 2811-2820.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spl.2008.03.032
Primary preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3518
Full primary PDF read: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.3518

Section 1 explicitly defines rows of iid Gamma(alpha_n,1) variables and
exchangeable Dirichlet vectors. Section 2 derives gamma maxima under
different shape regimes; Section 3 transfers to Dirichlet maxima through
the gamma representation. The fixed-shape centering is the familiar
log n+(theta-1)log log n-log Gamma(theta).

Distinction: the proposed model has infinitely many nonidentical spectral
rates after tilting, a rare small weighted-sum constraint, and a marked
limit describing where the largest contribution occurs. In the Dirichlet
zero case its centering has (theta-3/2)log log N(u), reflecting loss of a
factor sqrt(log N(u)) in the effective population. One should not describe
the Gumbel law for gamma maxima by itself as new.

3. Small Gaussian ellipsoids and conditional moments
----------------------------------------------------
Eddy Mayer-Wolf and Ofer Zeitouni,
The Probability of Small Gaussian Ellipsoids and Associated Conditional Moments,
Annals of Probability 21(1) (1993), 14-24.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176989391
Primary author PDF: https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~zeitouni/pdf/eddyelip.pdf
Author publication listing:
https://www.ee.technion.ac.il/~zeitouni/reprints1990-1994.html

The abstract and opening discussion concern the lower tail of a Gaussian
norm and conditional exponential moments, with Onsager-Machlup applications.
This is a direct antecedent for the conditioning framework. The accessible
PDF's text encoding is defective; the usable comparison here is limited
to the primary title, abstract and bibliographic record. Do not make a
claim about every technical theorem in that article solely from the abstract.

Distinction established at the level of stated scope: small-norm tail and
conditional moment bounds versus a marked maximum process and moving
spectral location. This comparison does not prove no implication of an
older general theorem covers the new statement.

4. Gibbs conditioning under extreme deviations
-----------------------------------------------
Zhansheng Cao, An Abelian theorem with application to the conditional Gibbs principle
(2013), https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.1337

The primary abstract concerns iid light-tailed variables, a tilted density,
Edgeworth expansions, and a local limit under a large-sum constraint with
sample mean tending to infinity. It is a methodological antecedent for
tilting and local limits. The proposed theorem concerns an infinite
weighted sum tending to zero and a location-marked extreme process.

5. Unconditioned maxima of spectral intensities
-----------------------------------------------
Geza Gyorgyi, Peter C. W. Holdsworth, Baptiste Portelli and Zoltan Racz,
Statistics of extremal intensities for Gaussian interfaces,
Physical Review E 68 (2003), 056116.
Primary preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0307645
Full primary PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0307645
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.68.056116

This is a close antecedent for spectral maxima: independent exponential
Fourier intensities have power-law rates. The paper studies their
unconditioned maximum and compares it with the roughness (their sum).
It obtains Gumbel limits in white-noise, high-dimensional and
short-wavelength regimes. It does not formulate the small-total
conditioned, location-marked point process proved in this manuscript.
The new statement should therefore be described as a conditional marked
spectral theorem, not as the first study of nonidentical spectral maxima.

6. Small deviations of a weighted maximum
-----------------------------------------
L. V. Rozovsky, Small deviations of the maximal element of a sequence
of independent variables with smooth weights, Zap. Nauchn. Sem. POMI 368
(2009), 190-200; English translation J. Math. Sci. 167(4) (2010), 543-549.
Official record: https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=znsl&option_lang=eng&paperid=3513&wshow=paper
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10958-010-9942-9

The official abstract studies the logarithmic probability that a weighted
maximum is small, for independent identically distributed nonnegative
variables and regular weights. This differs from the distribution of
the maximum conditioned on a small weighted sum. Only the primary abstract
and bibliographic record were audited for this item, not its full text.

Proof-review note
------------------
The source's growing-block theorem supplies a clean transfer route. Choose
h=u/b_u^(1/(2p)), so h=o(u) but h/(u/b_u^(1/p)) tends to infinity. Prove
the independent tilted point-process limit on this block, and transfer it
jointly with the slack in total variation. To exclude maxima outside the
block under the conditioned law, a uniform one-coordinate marginal
density-ratio bound suffices; deleting any one coordinate leaves a fixed
spectral band with order N(u) terms and hence a uniform Fourier density
bound. This extra argument is needed: a tilted-measure union bound alone
does not automatically survive rare-event conditioning.
