Main result: no substantive mathematical gap found
-------------------------------------------------
1. General regularly varying counting functions are treated at the correct
   moving scale. The proof first shows b~log N(u), and only then evaluates
   counting measures at v=u/b^(1/p), which tends to infinity. It never
   silently replaces N(v) by N(u)b^(-alpha/p). The reduced-count center
   m=N(u/(log N(u))^(1/p)) is valid under the stated assumptions: the
   arguments v and u/(log N(u))^(1/p) have ratio tending to one, so the
   uniform convergence theorem applies. Also log m/log N(u)->1. Thus
   log b may be replaced by log log m with additive error o(1).

2. The local limit proof supports uniform singleton deletion. Every
   singleton removes at most theta/sigma^2 of the normalized variance;
   the retained [u,2u] band still supplies a uniform Fourier envelope.
   The small-s expansion and integrable envelope are uniform in the
   deleted index. Consequently sup_{j,z}|g_{u,j}(z)-phi(z)|->0.
   In the exact marginal density, integration over the Exp(1) variable
   leaves an upper bound sup_z g_{u,j}(z)/(sigma D). This is 1+o(1)
   uniformly in j and the observed singleton value. It is a legitimate
   upper bound even for very rare individual exceedance events.

3. The gamma-tail asymptotic has a usable global envelope for every
   theta>0. If theta<1, the power factor stays bounded because the tail
   argument divided by b is bounded below by 1/2. If theta>=1, a
   polynomial times exp(-y^p/2) suffices. Normalized counting-measure
   convergence at v, dyadic regular-variation bounds at infinity, and
   N(epsilon v)/N(v)->epsilon^alpha at zero justify the integral limit.

4. The marked rare-event argument first works under the independent
   tilted law, then transfers each mark-truncated point process using
   the entire gamma<=R v block. This block is o(u), so its TV transfer
   is justified. The singleton upper bound controls the complement:
   probability of any exceedance above z0 with a mark outside [eps,R]
   is bounded by a constant times the corresponding tilted expected
   count. These tails vanish as eps decreases to zero and R increases.
   Upper-height tightness and lower-height Poisson counts justify the
   stated top-k ordering and the Gumbel limit.

5. The strengthened finite-band density formula is correct. In the
   final band, the random block shift cancels once w1 is defined as
   the full first macroscopic increment. Uniform convergence of the
   final density, L1 convergence of the other densities, translation
   continuity for the small block shift, and the Exp(1) integrability
   of the slack give the claimed TV factorization. Its Gaussian
   factor is exactly a Brownian bridge at the prescribed variance
   times. This finite-dimensional statement also supplies the joint
   distributions used by the separate functional theorem.

6. Constants in the Dirichlet specialization check out:
   C1=pi/2, C2=pi/4, N(u)~d*u*log(u)/(2*pi),
   b=log(u)+(theta-1/2)log log(u)+log[d/(4*sqrt(pi)*Gamma(theta))]+o(1),
   x~theta*d*log(u)/(4*u), and maximum/total~4/(theta*d*u).
   Multiplicity is allowed throughout, and the result concerns random
   gamma weights on a deterministic spectrum, not random zero locations.

Final functional bridge: previous concerns resolved
--------------------------------------------------
The final notes/functional_bridge.tex resolves both issues in the earlier
scratch route. No substantive mathematical gap was found in this version.

7. Equal ordinates are grouped explicitly. Uniform convergence of the
   variance clocks gives max_g q_g->0 for aggregate jump variances.
   Gamma fourth cumulants give the correct identity and bound

      sum_g E[J_g^4] <= 3*max_g q_g + 6/sigma^2 -> 0.

   This supplies Lindeberg for the actual simultaneous jumps, not
   merely for a finer ordering of individual equal-height coordinates.

8. The direct L4 modulus argument is valid. On a partition into m
   intervals, the increment variances q_i have sum one and maximum
   at most 1/m+2*eta_u. Therefore sum_i q_i^2 is bounded by that same
   maximum. The fourth cumulants sum to at most 6/sigma^2. Doob's L4
   inequality inside each interval, a union bound, and a fixed factor
   for two adjacent intervals yield the stated ordinary-modulus bound.
   Truncating the infinite tail and passing in L4 is legitimate since
   its variance and fourth cumulant both tend to zero as the truncation
   increases. Together with the independent-band central limits, this
   directly proves the tilted Brownian-motion limit; no unproved
   within-multiplicity-group interpolation step remains.

9. Conditional domination for every fixed prefix follows by retaining
   a positive-length band in its complement. For tails starting at
   r>=1/2, the complementary prefix contains one common fixed band,
   so the density-supremum bound is uniform in r. The finite reverse
   ordering used solely for Doob's L2 maximal inequality is valid and
   makes no assertion that a strict-inequality reversed process is
   cadlag. L2 truncation yields the infinite-tail supremum bound.
   Thus the earlier right-continuity concern is absent in this proof.

10. Prefix modulus control and the uniform endpoint estimate give
    C-tightness on the full interval. The endpoint is -Y_x/sigma and
    tends to zero. The already proved joint finite-dimensional laws,
    continuity of every subsequential limit, and marginal tightness
    identify the full bridge jointly and independently of the marked
    point process and exponential slack. The existing point-process
    tail estimates continue to justify the top-k mapping jointly.

Refined heat-trace center: no substantive gap found
-------------------------------------------------
11. For N0(T)=c*T*(log T+A-1), differentiation gives
    dN0=c*(log T+A)dT. Integration against exp(-(T/v)^2) therefore
    gives K*v*(log v+kappa), with K=c*sqrt(pi)/2 and
    kappa=A-EulerGamma/2-log 2. Stieltjes integration of the remainder
    has the stated positive-sign integral. Boundary terms vanish.

12. The finite remainder bound is correct. The density 2*y*exp(-y^2)
    integrates to one and has mean sqrt(pi)/2. The inequality
    log(v*y+2)<=log(v+2)+y yields the logarithmic-error bound; a global
    bounded remainder gives |R_heat(v)|<=C0 directly. At B, the exact
    intensity differs from one by at most epsilon. Its logarithmic
    derivative is at most -1, so the claimed enclosure follows in
    both cases Lambda(B)>=1 and Lambda(B)<=1. A negative lower endpoint
    causes no problem: it is simply a weaker lower bound on b>0.

13. The conductor constants are consistent with summing the standard
    two-term counts, with conjugate factors counted separately:
    c=d/(2*pi), A=(log Q)/d-log(2*pi), K=d/(4*sqrt(pi)), and
    kappa=(log Q)/d-log(4*pi)-EulerGamma/2. Finite central-zero removal
    changes only the bounded part of the count. The fixed-product
    restriction appropriately avoids a claim of conductor uniformity.

14. Taking logarithms in the root equation and setting L=log u gives
    B=L+(log L)/2+log K+d_L/L+O((log L)^2/L^2), where
    d_L=kappa-(3/4)*log L-(1/2)*log K. The stated second-order
    coefficient and remainder are correct. The implicit root should
    indeed be retained when claiming the sharper power-scale error;
    a finite logarithmic expansion has a much larger truncation error.

15. For the synthetic inverse-count spectrum in the diagnostics,
    |N-N0|<=1 holds globally, including the initial interval where
    N0 is negative. Below 2*pi*e, N=0 and -1<=N0<=0; above it,
    N=floor(N0). Hence the analytic finite bracket uses exp(-B).
    The separate refined_center.py reads existing theta=1 numerical
    roots and evaluates this bracket without rerunning simulations
    or spectral sums. All four stored roots lie inside the numerical
    brackets. This is floating-point consistency, not interval
    certification of the endpoints or the stored roots.

Overall verdict: no substantive gap found in the final main theorem,
its centering and Dirichlet corollaries, the full functional bridge
extension, or the refined heat-trace center, under their explicitly
stated assumptions.
