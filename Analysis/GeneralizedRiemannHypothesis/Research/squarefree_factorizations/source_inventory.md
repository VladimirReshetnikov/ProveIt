For an exhaustive partition into two parts, prescribing both products reduces to prescribing the product of the first part. With different moduli the Chinese remainder theorem gives the same reduction, subject to the total-product compatibility condition at their greatest common divisor. A joint allocation into three or more parts imposes additional counting constraints.

## Primary literature compared

- Booker and Pomerance obtain squarefree smooth representatives of classes modulo a prime and discuss the size of the largest necessary prime factor. Munsch and Shparlinski obtain sharper smoothness and representative-size bounds. [Booker and Pomerance](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.01557) · [Munsch and Shparlinski](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12297)
- Martin and Parvardi count products of subsets from a full interval of small positive integers for one prime modulus. [Martin and Parvardi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10198)
- Jao, Miller and Venkatesan use GRH to bound the spectrum of walks generated by small primes and their inverses. The walks permit repeated generators. [Jao, Miller and Venkatesan](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0647)
- Klurman, Shparlinski and Teräväinen obtain one small prime set whose unrestricted subset products cover the unit group for almost all moduli up to Q. Hughes attains the optimal first-order list size in this almost-all formulation. [Klurman, Shparlinski and Teräväinen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25393) · [Hughes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04678)
- The prescribed-block-size, prescribed-sum partition problem has a history in group theory. Cichacz and Suchan recall Tannenbaum's formulation for the set of all nonzero group elements. Müyesser and Pokrovskiy study existence of zero-sum partitions for large abelian groups. Eberhard studies Fourier coefficients of random bijections onto an entire finite abelian group. [Cichacz and Suchan, §1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09395) · [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, §6.3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01328-x) · [Eberhard](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02407)
- Roos's inequality for the characteristic function of a random assignment gives an upper bound using any disjoint pairing of matrix columns. Theorem 1.4, equation (28), applies directly to the matrix of character values in a uniformly permuted allocation. This supplies a general antecedent for the Fourier damping step. [Roos](https://doi.org/10.1080/03081087.2024.2436061)
