# Sources compared

The dated ProveIt snapshot is 5cc267af75a635199ac5d93a7d49cbce521daefc. The inventory contains the directory README and 46 archives. Manuscript sources were screened for McNew, Rankin and progression-free density. This screening identifies relevant overlap and does not validate every statement in those reports.

* grh_transfers_density_gaps_and_zero_tests.zip treats differences between natural and upper densities. Its integers.tex and integer_more.tex use smooth factors and squarefree rough parts. Its final questions explicitly retain McNew 7.2 and 7.4. Its jensen.tex, gaussian.tex and sector.tex treat conductor 23 and include a counterexample for a general cubic-kernel criterion. The present report compares the two natural densities directly.
* grh-progression-densities-and-cubic-prime-race.zip and grh-methods-polynomial-density.zip treat related polynomial-ring problems. The three exact natural-density optima in the present report concern subsets of positive integers.
* grh_relative_prime_races_2026-11-25.zip and grh_cyclic_generator_transfer_2026-11-25.zip give signed Frobenius tests and transfer conditions. The classification here imposes binary values and strict negativity on the full nonlinear spectrum. Hence its minimality and uniqueness statements concern a specified class of tests.
* grh_nonabelian_transfer_2026-11-25.zip works toward a geometric input for a nonabelian prime sum. The classification and Landau argument in this report do not prove that input.

McNew, Math. Comp. 84 (2015), 2893–2910, author's copy https://www.nathanmcnew.com/GPFsets.pdf, gives the original question numbers used here. Different manuscript versions can number the questions differently. Section 6 in particular observes the sufficient condition that the product of p/(p−1) over the allowed primes is at most 2. All three restricted comparisons in the present report fall outside this condition.
