FORMALIZATION PLAN AND STATUS
27 November 2026

STATUS

The new results in paper.tex have complete human-readable mathematical
proofs. Exact Python/SymPy certificates and exhaustive small-case checks
are provided. No new result has been compiled in Lean, Rocq, or another
proof assistant. This file is a concrete implementation plan, not a
machine-checked proof or a list of existing Mathlib theorem names.

Any upstream Lean sources included with the archive are preserved inputs
from the requested development. They do not formalize the new statements.
The upstream package uses Lean 4.35.0 and Mathlib revision
56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679. It is a separate Lake package;
the root ProveIt build does not build the vendored directory.

The finite-ring and graph theorems can be formalized independently of the
large upstream RH proof. Only the final application to Dirichlet-zero
packets needs its critical-line theorem.

PART A. FINITE-RING SYMPLECTIC PROJECTIONS

1. Algebraic setup.
   Work first over a finite local principal ideal ring R=O/(pi^h), with
   residue field cardinality q. Use a genuinely alternating bilinear
   form (zero diagonal), not merely a skew-symmetric matrix. This keeps
   residue characteristic two in scope. Define n=2m, the ambient form J
   of rank 2n, a fixed unimodular alternating n-by-n H, and the finite
   set of frames X satisfying transpose(X)*J*X=H. Define the projection
   A by the first n rows. Do not use the literal first n columns of a
   standard-ordered symplectic matrix as the input frame.

2. Scalar pair fibers.
   The first useful standalone lemma counts solutions to
      pi^a*x-pi^b*y=c
   in R^2 for 0<=a,b<=h, including a=h or b=h. The image is the ideal
   generated by pi^min(a,b), and every nonempty fiber has cardinality
   q^(h+min(a,b)). This requires no probability or spectral theory.

3. Smith-coordinate Gram fibers.
   Formalize the change of variables for PAT=D and
   B'=P^(-T)BT. Prove that A^T B-B^T A=H is equivalent to
   D B'-B'^T D=T^T H T. Multiply the independent pair-fiber counts,
   add the free diagonal coordinates, and obtain the exact cardinality.
   The capped exponents a_i=h must remain in the statement.

4. Frame orbit and normalization.
   Prove that Gram equality implies a split injection. Split the
   nondegenerate orthogonal complement into hyperbolic planes over the
   local ring. Deduce transitivity of the symplectic group and identify
   the pointwise stabilizer. Count the reduction kernels by the onto
   map Z -> Z^T J+JZ, using strictly upper-triangular matrices to solve
   the alternating correction equation. Combine the finite-field order
   and lifting to obtain the frame cardinality.

5. Orbit averages and the Smith likelihood.
   Formalize uniform group actions on a finite Smith orbit. Show that
   the transformed H is uniform among unimodular alternating forms,
   and count first-digit isotropic k-frames. Count independent later
   digit restrictions. This proves the exact cancellation between
   q^S in the pointwise density and q^(-S) in the compatible-orientation
   probability. Equality of conditional Smith laws is only stated for
   feasible k<=m.

6. Exact testing distances.
   Express finite total variation as half the sum of absolute mass
   differences. Establish the sign of R_(n,k)-C_n, reducing the Smith
   distance to k=0,1. For the complete matrix, use that every supported
   likelihood exceeds one, so total variation is the iid probability
   of incompatibility.

PART B. ARBITRARY PRECISION AND HAAR LIMITS

7. Smith-nullity transition.
   Prove the iid Schur-complement reduction: conditioned on first-digit
   rank, the remaining block divided by pi is a fresh iid smaller
   matrix. This proves the decreasing Markov chain for k_l and the
   exact finite precision recurrence. Keep the initialization f_0=1
   algebraic; the total-variation formula begins at positive precision.

8. Precision rate.
   On states k>=2, prove the substochastic row bound q^-5 by separating
   k=2, k=3, and k>=4. Solve the k=2 recurrence exactly. This yields the
   upper bound and matching positive lower bound without numerical
   approximation or spectral assumptions about an infinite operator.

9. Infinite precision.
   Construct the Haar/projective-limit distributions from their finite
   quotients. Show that an iid square Haar matrix has nonzero determinant
   almost surely. The finite densities stabilize almost surely after
   the largest Smith exponent. Orientation averaging shows that their
   pointwise limit has integral one. Scheffe's lemma then supplies L1
   convergence and excludes a singular component of the symplectic law.
   Alternatively, prove the infinite total-variation formula through
   increasing finite-cylinder sigma-algebras before introducing the
   pointwise density.

10. Dimension limit and certificates.
    Derive the exact iid cokernel law by counting surjections and
    lattice-generating column tuples. Prove the infinite product and
    series bounds with rational remainders. Prove the simultaneous
    dimension/precision estimate using test functions valued in [0,1]
    so the measure comparison uses total variation without a factor two.

PART C. OPTIMAL GRAPH ZERO TRANSPORT

11. Finite determinant identity.
    Define the graph Laplacian from nonnegative conductances and prove
    positivity on the perpendicular space to constants for connected
    support. Formalize the rooted-forest determinant identity, the
    derivative compression, and the nonzero-spectrum reduction from B
    to C^(1/2) L^(-1) C^(1/2) on that space. Retain multiplicities and
    the N-1 empirical normalization throughout.

12. Start with the infinity endpoint.
    Prove t>=tr(C)/(2W) from C<=tL. Equality forces L proportional to C.
    This endpoint avoids fractional powers and is a compact first
    formal target resolving the Article's explicit optimization problem.

13. Finite p and feasibility.
    Prove the convex displacement bound using Hermitian eigenvalue
    perturbations or an established majorization theorem. In a C
    eigenbasis apply spectral Jensen, inverse-diagonal Cauchy-Schwarz,
    and the scalar constrained minimum. Audit the equality conditions
    separately, especially p=1. For graph feasibility prove the positive
    resolvent integral for every off-diagonal entry of C^r; no abstract
    graph fractional-power theorem is required for this special matrix.

14. Stability and zero-packet application.
    Formalize the exact inverse resolvent identity and trace
    cancellation for the p=1 gap. The sharpness of exponent 1/2 follows
    by an admissible trace-preserving perturbation of two edge weights.
    Finally use the upstream critical-line theorem only to assert that
    finite selected Dirichlet ordinates are real inputs. Do not infer
    an infinite-spectrum minimax statement or optimality for a specific
    arithmetic packet from the universal worst-case theorem.
