INDEPENDENT PROOF AUDIT: OPTIMAL GRAPH COUPLINGS

Target inspected: the formula sent by /root/analytic_theorem, compared against Article/sections/zer.tex, labels zer:thm:electrical and the final Optimal couplings question.

Verdict: the finite-packet minimax theorem is correct with p>=1, continuous nonnegative edge conductances of total W>0, at least two vertices, and strictly positive integer degrees. No issue found in the constants, multiplicities, rank, or p=infinity endpoint.

Exact normalization. If n is the number of vertices and N=sum d_i, the input product has degree N and the derivative/coupled polynomial each has N-1 roots counted with multiplicity. Thus empirical Wasserstein p-distance is (sum displacement^p/(N-1))^(1/p). The perturbation B_G has n-1 positive eigenvalues and N-n zero eigenvalues on the N-1 dimensional compression space. Its nonzero spectrum equals that of C^(1/2) L^+ C^(1/2), restricted to 1-perp, where C=diag(d)-dd^T/N. The factor (N-1)^(-1/p) must be retained.

Sharpness. Lidskii majorization bounds the nonnegative ordered eigenvalue differences by the eigenvalues of B_G in convex p-power sums, p>=1. Taking every input p_i(z)=(z-c)^(d_i) makes the unperturbed compression scalar cI, so this bound is attained simultaneously for all p. Repeated roots and common roots are explicitly permitted by the Article. If simple mutually distinct inputs are desired, perturbing the common roots gives the same supremum by continuity of Hermitian eigenvalues; it need not attain the supremum. For p=1 the trace identity already makes the cost independent of root locations for every input.

Optimization. Work only on 1-perp. In a C eigenbasis let its eigenvalues be c_i>0 and the diagonal entries of L be l_i>0. For finite p>=1,
  tr[(C^(1/2)L^(-1)C^(1/2))^p]
    >= sum_i [c_i (L^(-1))_ii]^p
    >= sum_i (c_i/l_i)^p
    >= (sum_i c_i^(p/(p+1)))^(p+1)/(sum_i l_i)^p.
The first inequality is the spectral Jensen inequality for the convex scalar function x^p, the second is (L^(-1))_ii L_ii>=1, and the last is scalar Holder or strict convex minimization. The equality conditions are adequate even at p=1: because each c_i>0, equality in the second inequality holds for every coordinate, forcing L to be diagonal in this basis. The unique diagonal minimizer is l_i proportional c_i^(p/(p+1)). Thus the optimizer is basis-independent and unique, L_p=2W C^(p/(p+1))/tr C^(p/(p+1)).

For p=infinity, t=lambda_max(C^(1/2)L^(-1)C^(1/2)) implies C<=tL. Traces force t>=tr C/(2W), and equality forces C=tL because a positive semidefinite matrix of trace zero is zero. Thus L_infinity=2WC/tr C is unique, with weights w_ij=W d_i d_j/sum_{a<b}d_a d_b. This is the Article's exact requested largest-displacement optimum.

Feasibility. C is a connected Laplacian with strictly negative off-diagonals. For 0<r<1 its spectral power has zero row sums and strictly negative off-diagonals, as follows from
  -(C^r)_ij = (sin(pi r)/pi) integral_0^infinity
    t^(r-1) d_i d_j / [(t+d_i)(t+d_j) sum_l d_l/(t+d_l)] dt, i!=j.
The integrand is positive. Near zero it is asymptotic to t^(r-1)/n; near infinity it is asymptotic to (d_i d_j/N)t^(r-2), proving convergence. Hence every optimizer is an admissible complete weighted graph. At r=1 use C directly rather than substituting into the integral.

Scope. Finite packets of actual Dirichlet zeros are valid real-rooted inputs under the source theorem, with their zero multiplicities. The formula optimizes the universal worst-case guarantee for those packet sizes. The all-coincident extremizer need not be an actual Dirichlet zero packet, so this does not show that the same graph minimizes displacement for a particular known Dirichlet packet. It also does not solve an infinite-spectrum minimax problem: N and the degree covariance diverge as packets increase. The Article's real-entire-function coupling theorem remains separately applicable, but it is not a passage to the limit of a finite degree-dependent optimal transport bound.

Independent numerical diagnostics. check_graph_minimax.py tests 50 random degree profiles with 2-6 vertices, p in {1,1.5,2,3,infinity}, and 8 budget-normalized competitor graphs (a path plus random complete graphs) per case. All 2,000 objective/transport comparisons passed, with largest relative discrepancy at the predicted optimum 2.45e-15.
