1. Scope of the source theorem

Statements.lean imports Mathlib, and defines its predicates directly in terms of Mathlib's riemannZeta and DirichletCharacter.LFunction. Main.lean states the five public theorem names using those types. The half-plane statement quantifies over N : Nat with [NeZero N], every complex Dirichlet character modulo N, and every complex s, and concludes L(s,chi) != 0 from 1/2 < Re(s) and (chi != 1 or s != 1). The line statement covers all zeros with 0 < Re(s) < 1. The primitive-character theorem is explicitly only a special case. The Riemann zeta statement excludes 1 and the negative even integers.

Thus the appropriate input for a new theorem is RH and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions. This does not by itself furnish GRH for arbitrary Artin, automorphic, or nonabelian Dedekind L-functions. No simplicity or separation assertion about zeros, or linear independence of zero ordinates, appears in the public contract. Such assumptions must not be silently added to an application.

3. Arithmetic remainder and analytic engine

For a prime d, positive modulus q, and the quadratic symbol chi_d(n)=(n/d), define
  lambda_{d,q}(n) = Lambda(n) (1-chi_d(n)) 1_{n == 1 (mod q)}.
The weight is nonnegative, at most 2 Lambda(n). The remainder is
  R_{d,q}(x) = sum_{1<=n<=x} lambda_{d,q}(n) (1/n-1/x)
               - log(x)/phi(q) - c_{d,q},
where c is chosen so R tends to zero. In the source it is Re(logDerivMean d q 1).

For E(v)=R(exp(v)), the transform integral_0^infinity E(v) exp((1-s)v) dv has meromorphic continuation with at most simple poles in Re(s)>0 and no pole at 1. Away from 1 its residue is
  (n(s)-n_twist(s)) / (phi(q) s (1-s)),
where n and n_twist are sums of zero multiplicities of entire-normalized character L-functions and their quadratic twists.

Landau.lean proves the following usable principle: if E has the specified mild growth and meromorphic transform, beta>0, E(v)<=exp((beta-1)v) eventually, and the real-axis residues satisfy Re(r(x))>=0 for x>beta, then every residue with Re(s)>beta vanishes. The one-sided upper bound constructs a positive measure whose density is K exp(beta v)-E(v) exp(v). Positivity of that measure forces its rightmost singularity onto the real axis, where the assumed residue sign gives a contradiction. This is a positivity/analytic-continuation mechanism; it does not derive two-sided cancellation from an unsigned average.

The criterion in New/Reduction/Zeros/Criterion.lean chooses an auxiliary prime d in a prescribed progression so every twisted L-function has no zero in a box containing the candidate zero. The sum n_twist then vanishes throughout that box and the real-axis residues are nonnegative. The quadratic-twist existence lemma comes from an unconditional zero-density estimate: with beta=1/2+9 delta, only O(X^(1-delta/2)) primes d<=X are bad. The proof uses theta smoothing, a Mobius mollifier, three-lines, and Polya-Vinogradov. The choice of d may depend on beta and the target height; one fixed d is not claimed to work for all heights simultaneously.

4. Where the arithmetic estimate enters

For admissible CM data, MainInequality.lean proves
  m * totalRemainder <= f * a * (log b)^5.
The proof compares an upper and lower bound for the same covolume corrected by its lattice index. Short integral source forms, a sufficiently large span of their translated images, and a determinant lower bound supply the two inequalities; the main terms cancel. This is the difficult arithmetic input, not supplied by Landau's lemma alone.

OneSidedBound.lean then proves
  R_{d,q0}(x) <= phi(q0)*(log b)^6/m
                + |R_{d,q0}(x1)| + (log b)*|R_{d,1}(x1)|.
Here q0 is divisible by 18480, d==q0/2-1 modulo q0, m lies in [sqrt(x),2 sqrt(x)], and the scales can be chosen with log b <= 2 x^epsilon and log x1 >= x^(epsilon/12). An ordinary prime number theorem at the much larger x1 makes the latter errors <=x^-2. Interpolation from integer x to real x costs at most 26/x. Consequently, for every epsilon>0,
  R_{d,q0}(x) <= x^(-1/2+epsilon)
eventually. The source has no user-facing effective numerical threshold in this final statement.

Finally any original modulus q is promoted to q0=18480q, the analytic criterion applies, and the induced character's finite Euler factors transfer nonvanishing back to q. New/Main.lean uses the functional equation to move zeros left of 1/2 to zeros right of 1/2 and obtains the critical-line conclusions.

5. Productive source-derived directions

For a mathematically independent extension, the strongest reusable analytic pattern is positive prime weights -> a transform whose residues encode multiplicities -> positive-measure rigidity. The strongest arithmetic pattern is to compare two estimates of one determinant while tracking missing dimension and lattice-index loss. The Article already explores many versions of both, so a merely reparameterized one-sided criterion or a standard GRH prime-error bound should not be sold as novel.

A potentially distinct rigorous target is an exact robustness or identifiability threshold for a new family of nonnegative prime observations, derived by finite-dimensional convex duality and then transferred through the explicit formula. Its significance would require an explicit optimal constant or an exact impossibility theorem, not only applying a classical GRH estimate. This suggestion is a research direction, not a proved result in this audit.
