# Independent review of the odd-cocycle comparison

**Verdict: correct as a paper proof for an odd prime ell and a positive integer e.** I compared the proposed argument in `research/open_targets.md` directly with `grh/src/Lib/GaloisRep/Main/SelmerModules.lean` (definitions of `shaLocalSet` and `shaOdd`) and `grh/src/Lib/Cyclotomic/OddSha.lean` (`New.exists_length_shaOdd_le`). The statement is not part of the inspected Lean theorem; it must be labelled a separately proved, not yet Lean-formalized comparison.

## Exact statement reviewed

Let O be the integers of a finite extension F/Q_ell, let A=(F/O)(-e), and identify A_n=(O/ell^nO)(-e) with ell^{-n}O/O. For a CM field E and the source's chosen complex conjugation c, let S_n be its actual-cocycle module `shaOdd ell E O e n`. Then

\[
\varinjlim_n S_n\cong
\ker\!\left(H^1(G_E,A)\to\prod_{v\text{ finite}}H^1(D_v,A)\right)^{c=(-1)^{e+1}}.
\]

The transition A_n to A_{n+1} is multiplication by ell, hence injective. Infinite places add no condition because E is totally imaginary. All cohomology is continuous and A is discrete.

## 1. Source local conditions agree with the stated ones

For e>0 the source tests the entire decomposition subgroup's **cyclotomic kernel**, not merely inertia, at each place above ell. Away from ell it tests inertia. These tested subgroups act trivially on A and A_n.

One small exposition detail should be added to the main proof. The source quantifies over all closure places, whereas the comparison picks one D_v for every v. These formulations agree. If z is a cocycle for the character a(g)=chi_ell(g)^(-e), then

\[
z(ghg^{-1})=a(g)z(h)+(1-a(h))z(g).
\]

When h belongs to one of the tested subgroups, a(h)=1. Thus vanishing on the chosen subgroup implies vanishing on all its G_E-conjugates. No extra conjugacy-invariance hypothesis is needed.

## 2. Odd representatives are unique

Set Tz(g)=z(cgc). Since chi_ell(cgc)=chi_ell(g), T preserves cocycles and T^2=1. Every coboundary delta(b)(g)=(a(g)-1)b is T-fixed. Because 2 is invertible on the ell-primary coefficients, a T-odd coboundary is zero. Therefore the map from T-odd cocycles to H^1 is injective.

If a cohomology class satisfies T=-1, then (z-Tz)/2 is a continuous T-odd representative and represents the same class. Hence the injection is also onto the T-odd cohomology sector. The local conditions are preserved by T because c permutes places and preserves the cyclotomic character. In fact the tested subgroups act trivially, so every representative of a locally trivial class already vanishes there.

The natural action of c on cochains also acts on coefficients:

\[
(c\cdot z)(g)=(-1)^e z(cgc)=(-1)^e Tz(g).
\]

Consequently source oddness T=-1 corresponds exactly to natural c eigenvalue (-1)^{e+1}. Omitting the coefficient action would produce the wrong parity.

## 3. Local vanishing above ell

The image Gamma=chi_ell(D_v) is an open subgroup of Z_ell^times, because E_v/Q_ell is finite. For odd ell, its intersection Gamma_0 with 1+ell Z_ell is procyclic, isomorphic to Z_ell, and has prime-to-ell index in Gamma. If gamma is a topological generator and u=chi_ell(gamma), then u^{-e}-1 is nonzero because e>0 and u has infinite order.

The needed H^1(Gamma_0,A)=0 can be proved directly, without relying on an implicit continuous-cohomology convention. Given a continuous cocycle z, divisibility of F/O by each nonzero element of O supplies b with

\[
(u^{-e}-1)b=z(\gamma).
\]

Subtract delta(b). The resulting cocycle is zero at gamma, hence at every integral power of gamma by the cocycle identity, hence on all of Gamma_0 by continuity and density. Thus every cocycle is a coboundary. Inflation-restriction through the finite prime-to-ell quotient and the averaging operator then gives H^1(Gamma,A)=0.

With K_v=D_v intersect ker chi_ell, inflation-restriction gives an injection

\[
H^1(D_v,A)\hookrightarrow H^1(K_v,A).
\]

Since K_v acts trivially, a cocycle has zero class there exactly when it is the zero cocycle there. The source's local zero condition is therefore equivalent to full local triviality at v **for the divisible module A**. It is important not to assert this equivalence at every finite level A_n: multiplication by u^{-e}-1 need not be surjective on A_n.

## 4. Local vanishing away from ell

Inertia acts trivially on A. A cocycle vanishing there descends to D_v/I_v, a procyclic group topologically generated by arithmetic Frobenius. Frobenius acts by (Nv)^{-e}; this scalar minus 1 is nonzero. Divisibility again permits killing the value at Frobenius, and dense cyclic continuity kills the entire cocycle. Thus the unramified local H^1 is zero. Conversely a locally trivial class has zero restriction to inertia because inertia coboundaries are zero.

Whether arithmetic or geometric Frobenius is chosen changes the scalar to its inverse; nonzero-scalar surjectivity, and therefore the conclusion, are unchanged. This removes a possible convention issue.

## 5. Direct limit and continuity

A continuous map from compact G_E to the discrete module A has finite image. Any finite subset of F/O is contained in ell^{-n}O/O for some n. Consequently every continuous cocycle satisfying the conditions appears at a finite coefficient level. The inclusion of coefficient modules preserves the cocycle, parity and local equations. The map S_n to S_{n+1} is injective because multiplication by ell on O/ell^nO is injective into O/ell^{n+1}O.

No general assertion that cohomology commutes with arbitrary direct limits is required: the proof works directly with actual continuous cocycles and their finite images. This is preferable because it makes the local and odd conditions transparent.

## 6. Passing the source length bound

The source gives one nonzero X, independent of ell, O, e<t and n, with length_O S_n<=v_O(X), provided the field and coefficient-ring hypotheses are retained. The lengths are finite integers; injectivity makes them nondecreasing; boundedness makes them eventually constant. An injective map of finite-length modules of equal length is an isomorphism, so the union stabilizes and has length at most v_O(X).

A finite-length module of length r over a DVR is killed by the r-th power of its maximal ideal. Since r<=v_O(X), the limit is killed by X. Its cardinality is at most q^{v_O(X)}, with q the residue-field cardinality. It vanishes if ell does not divide X. There is no automatic explicit bound for the stabilization index n.

If z:G_E to O(-e) is a continuous integral cocycle with these conditions, each reduction modulo ell^n is locally constant, lies in S_n and is killed by X. Hence Xz(g) belongs to every ell^nO and is zero. O is torsion-free and X is nonzero, so z=0. This integral conclusion is valid and does not say that the torsion cohomology group vanishes at primes dividing X.

## 7. Essential restrictions

- ell=2 is excluded: odd projection and the assertion that an odd coboundary is zero use invertibility of 2.
- e=0 is excluded: both local arguments require a nonzero scalar chi^{-e}-1; the source also changes its local and parity definitions at e=0.
- The c eigenvalue is (-1)^{e+1}, not an unconditional natural minus eigenspace.
- The source's `HasEnoughRootsOfUnity` and cyclotomic containment E subset Q(mu_N) hypotheses remain necessary when importing the uniform length bound.
- X is uniform for fixed E,N,t. Taking E=Q(mu_ell) varies that data with ell and does not make the diagonal prime automatically good.
- The result identifies a standard full local H^1 kernel and derives a finite exceptional set. It supplies neither the required K-theory comparison nor vanishing at exceptional primes, and it does not prove Vandiver.
- The comparison relies on standard continuous cohomology and divisibility facts. It is new relative to the five inspected reports as an explicit completed bridge, not a claim of publication priority.
