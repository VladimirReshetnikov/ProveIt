# Bounded prior-art comparison for exact torsion specialization

Date of search: 25 November 2026. This is a targeted comparison, not an exhaustive novelty certification. It covers the primary sources below and searches for the combinations “Gauss norm / valuation”, “roots of unity / torsion”, “maximum modulus”, “integral power series”, and “effective / conductor”. No matching published statement with exactly the uniform bound below was located in the examined sources. No claim of worldwide priority or of solving a named open conjecture follows from that negative search.

## Statement being compared

For a nonzero integral polynomial g in O_Cp[T], normalize v(p)=1 and set

    A = G_rho(g) = min_j {v(a_j) + rho*j} <= B,
    r_h = 1 / phi(p^h),
    h0 >= 1 with a = r_h0 <= rho,
    c = 1 - 1/p.

The exact specialization theorem produces an h with h0<=h<=H and a primitive p^h-th root zeta satisfying v(g(zeta-1))<=A, with

    H = h0 + ceil((h0+B+2)*B/(c*a)).

When B<a, every primitive p^h0 root works. The salient feature is that H depends only on the radius and valuation budget, not the degree or coefficient field. The same budget supports finitely many variables via coefficient induction, and a product gives a bound for the sum of specialized valuations. The mathematical proof is supplied separately in `sections/exact_torsion.tex`; this note only compares its scope with literature.

## 1. Kriz–Nordentoft: exact maximum modulus is already present

Daniel Kriz and Asbjørn Christian Nordentoft, *Horizontal p-adic L-functions*, arXiv:2310.20678v3 (version record revised 16 November 2025).

Primary text: https://arxiv.org/html/2310.20678v3

Relevant results: Proposition 2.7, Theorem 2.8, Proposition 2.11, Theorems 2.12–2.13.

Proposition 2.7 gives an exact comparison with the trivial-character value, under an integrality/finite-group-size hypothesis. Proposition 2.11 gives a related comparison using a character outside the p-th powers. Theorem 2.8 gives an epsilon approximation to the infimum of Fourier valuations for arbitrary pro-p abelian groups after a twist from a finite exceptional set. Its valuation in equation (2.12) is a Fourier infimum, not the positive-radius weighted Gauss valuation used above.

Therefore the ideas “exact p-adic maximum modulus at torsion” and “simultaneous nonvanishing via products” must not be advertised as new. What is not visibly furnished by these statements is an explicit interval of p-power orders controlled only by B and rho, uniformly in polynomial degree over O_Cp. Their groups may also be horizontal products of finite cyclic groups, whereas the specialization statement concerns the usual formal multiplicative coordinates.

## 2. Serban: near-zero torsion loci and a discrete-coefficient lemma

Vlad Serban, *An infinitesimal p-adic multiplicative Manin–Mumford Conjecture*, Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux **30** (2018), no. 2, 393–408. DOI: 10.5802/jtnb.1030. arXiv:1607.01428.

Primary published PDF: https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2018__30_2_393_0.pdf

Publisher record: https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.1030/

Relevant results: Proposition 2.8 and Lemma 2.9; also the discussion immediately before Section 2.

Proposition 2.8 confines sufficiently small torsion values of an integral formal series to finitely many special formal subschemes along which it vanishes. Lemma 2.9 uses discrete coefficients: for a series with first unit coefficient in degree M, the proof uses the threshold v(pi)/M and obtains v(phi(x))=M v(x) sufficiently near the boundary. The article explicitly distinguishes this setting from unrestricted O_Cp coefficients.

The exact specialization result is an existence statement at a uniformly bounded selection of orders, not a classification of all torsion zeros or all near-zero points. Its coefficient-field independence and prescribed positive-radius budget differ from Serban's function-dependent thresholds. It neither proves nor strengthens Serban's full geometric classification.

## 3. Schefer: a close comparator for the proof method

Gerold Schefer, *p-adic equidistribution and an application to S-units*, arXiv:2410.24088v1 (31 October 2024); published Journal of the London Mathematical Society **113** (2026), no. 3, e70495. DOI: 10.1112/jlms.70495.

Primary preprint text examined: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24088v1

Relevant results: Lemmas 7, 9–14 and Theorem 34.

The one-variable argument factors the polynomial, selects a nearest conjugate torsion point for each root, and sums ultrametric root distances. Lemma 14 bounds the discrepancy of a torsion average from the Gauss norm by

    deg(F)/#M * (c(F) + k log p),

where c(F) is a polynomial-dependent lower-separation constant and the torsion order has p-part p^k. The multivariate argument also uses exact Gauss-norm specialization, employing prime-to-p torsion.

This is a genuine conceptual predecessor to capped shell averaging: nearest-root selection and cyclotomic distance summation are established ingredients. The new calculation should be described as using truncation/capping and a Gauss-budget accounting argument to obtain the stated uniform existence bound. Schefer's quantitative average, with its degree and c(F) dependence, does not directly give that bound as stated. Conversely, a single-point existence bound does not recover his equidistribution theorem.

## 4. What can safely be said

Suggested report wording:

> We prove an exact torsion-specialization estimate with an explicit order bound depending only on a positive-radius Gauss-valuation budget. It refines the epsilon-loss statement in the supplied previous report. Exact maximum-modulus principles at finite-order characters, formal torsion rigidity, and root-distance averaging all have substantial antecedents. We did not locate this particular degree-independent bound in the primary sources examined; its historical novelty remains unestablished.

The improvement relative to a specific previous report can be checked from that report's exact theorem. This is a much narrower and better-supported claim than saying a classical conjecture was resolved.

## 5. Limits that should remain attached to the theorem

The root has some order p^h in the stated interval. It need not have order exactly p^H. Indeed the integral polynomial Phi_{p^H}(1+T) vanishes at every primitive p^H-th root shifted by -1, while its positive-radius Gauss valuation stays at most one. Thus a universal single prescribed shell would be false for this class of inputs.

The comparison is with G_rho at rho>0. It is not a theorem that the unweighted coefficient infimum is attained on p-power torsion. For example, g(T)=T has coefficient minimum zero but v(g(zeta-1))>0 at every nontrivial p-power torsion point. The positive radius is the feature that permits an exact comparison.

For a packet, taking the product yields a sum bound. It does not by itself give each factor an individual valuation bound equal to its own Gauss valuation at the same point. The multivariable conclusion permits different coordinate orders within the common interval. These distinctions prevent the result from being confused with stronger simultaneous or fixed-conductor assertions.
