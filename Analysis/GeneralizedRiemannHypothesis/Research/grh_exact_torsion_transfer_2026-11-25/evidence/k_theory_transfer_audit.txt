## 1. Main finding

Algebraic K-theory is a serious transfer target, because the original proof contains a pointwise Selmer-length estimate that can be coupled to exact finite Hecke calculations. It is not a consequence of the truth of RH or Dirichlet GRH alone. However, the relevant stronger input belongs to the original proof archive. The shortened `grh-lean` route studied here deliberately removes the need to bound the even class groups in which Vandiver's conjecture lives. Its theorem `New.exists_length_shaOdd_le` cannot simply substitute for that input.

This is visible both in the source comments and the mathematical types. `DenominatorBound.lean` says its denominator estimate does not use the orders of even classes. `TrianglePrimes.lean` constructs auxiliary primes imposing triangular reciprocity conditions, and explicitly says a bound on the even classes is unnecessary. This describes avoidance of an obstruction in a deformation argument, not a theorem that the original even class group is zero. The distinction is essential when reading Alpöge's Step 17 and footnote 6 about possible K-group applications.

## 2. What the OddSha theorem actually says

Let E be a CM subfield of Q(mu_N), with N fixed and nonzero, and fix a nonnegative cutoff t. The theorem gives a nonzero integer X with

    X <= 2^[4(N+t+3)^27] h_E

such that, for every prime ell, every suitable ell-adic integer ring O containing enough roots of unity for the characters modulo N, every e<t, and every n>=0,

    length_O(shaOdd(ell,E,O,e,n)) <= v_O(X).

The code's `addVal O` is the valuation with its own normalization; retain that normalization if stating a Lean corollary. The elementary conclusions below only use whether it is zero.

The exact statement is `New.exists_length_shaOdd_le` in [OddSha.lean](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/Lib/Cyclotomic/OddSha.lean), lines 645–680. It proves a uniform finite-level bound for its defined module of cocycles. The file's introduction explicitly does not prove an identification of this module with a cohomological Sha group, nor an equality of Iwasawa characteristic ideals.

For e>0, `shaOdd` is a submodule of functions on G_E, rather than a quotient of cocycles by coboundaries. Its defining requirements are:

1. The function is a continuous one-cocycle for the inverse e-th cyclotomic twist, unramified outside ell.
2. It vanishes on elements of decomposition groups above ell whose cyclotomic character is 1.
3. Under conjugation of G_E by complex conjugation c, it satisfies z(cgc)=-z(g).

For e=0, it instead gives everywhere unramified homomorphisms; the class number is the controlling quantity. See [SelmerModules.lean](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/Lib/GaloisRep/Main/SelmerModules.lean), lines 174–189.

The construction is explicit in principle. For each e>0 the cyclotomic `Voronoi` argument supplies a nonzero Stickelberger bound X_e. The proof of the aggregate theorem takes

    Y_e = 16 phi(N) X_e^2,
    X = (product_{0<e<t} Y_e)^[(N+t+3)^20] h_E.

Thus X is a support bound, not a claimed unit. This gives useful finite exceptional sets for the particular modules, but it is not a solution to Vandiver.

### Immediate correct corollary

For fixed E,N,t, if ell does not divide the witness X, every `shaOdd(ell,E,O,e,n)` in the theorem is zero. Indeed its length is nonnegative and at most zero, so it has length zero and is the zero module. No new open-problem claim attaches to this elementary corollary.

### Four invalid inferences to avoid

- A bound by v_O(X)>0 does not imply vanishing. Even O/pi has length one.
- A length bound does not imply cyclicity. The module (O/pi) direct-sum (O/pi) has length two and is noncyclic.
- X is chosen after E,N,t. Taking E=Q(zeta_ell) and N=ell as ell varies does not produce a single exceptional integer independent of ell. Moreover its bound includes h_E itself.
- The label `shaOdd` does not supply a comparison with every group denoted Sha in arithmetic. A comparison must respect the actual cocycle quotient, local conditions, external conjugation sign, and coefficient conventions.

## 3. An elementary parity obstruction, with complete proof

**Proposition (restriction parity).** Let a group G act additively on an abelian group M. Let c in G satisfy c^2=1, let c act trivially on M, and assume multiplication by 2 is injective on M. Let H be a subgroup normalized by c. If z:G->M is a one-cocycle, then

    z(c)=0,   z(chc)=z(h)  for every h in H.

Consequently, if its restriction also satisfies z(chc)=-z(h), then z|_H=0.

**Proof.** The cocycle rule is z(ab)=z(a)+a z(b). It gives z(1)=0 and

    0=z(c^2)=z(c)+c z(c)=2z(c).

Injectivity of 2 gives z(c)=0. Applying the rule twice gives

    z(chc)=z(c)+c z(h)+ch z(c)=z(h).

If the restriction is also anti-invariant, then 2z(h)=0, hence z(h)=0. This proves every assertion.

For M=Q_ell/Z_ell(-e), or a finite-level ell-primary version, ell odd and e even imply c acts by (-1)^e=1 and multiplication by 2 is invertible. Rational strict Selmer classes relevant to K_{4k}(Z) use e=2k. Their restriction to a CM field therefore belongs to the **even** external-conjugation sector. The repository's `shaOdd` condition for e>0 selects the other sector. In particular, a natural restriction map does not identify the rational classes needed for K_{4k} with this `shaOdd` module.

The actual `evenClasses` definition is in [EvenClasses.lean](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Congruences/FirstOrder/EvenClasses.lean), lines 53–62. A duality or reflection theorem might relate different groups or different twists, but it would be a substantive extra bridge; it cannot be inferred from the word “odd.” This proposition is elementary and offered as an audit lemma, not as a historically novel theorem.

## 4. The actual K-theory bridge

For an odd prime ell, the cyclotomic comparison identifies the obstruction to vanishing of the ell-primary part of K_{4k}(Z) with the even Teichmuller eigenspace indexed by -2k in the class group of Q(zeta_ell). Equivalently, it is detected by

    H^2_et(Z[1/ell], mu_ell^{tensor(2k+1)}).

These relationships are standard K-theory, not deductions from RH. Original literature includes [Kurihara, Compositio Math. 81 (1992), 223–236](https://numdam.org/item/CM_1992__81_2_223_0.pdf); the modern formulation appears in [Weibel, K-book VI, Theorem 10.9](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~weibel/Kbook/Kbook.VI.pdf). Vandiver asserts vanishing of the relevant even cyclotomic class-group part for every ell.

The genuinely useful stronger source is the original archive's pointwise estimate, not the shortened proof's denominator theorem. [NumberU3US.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/ModularityArgument/ThresholdArguments/DenseThresholdCases/NumberU3US.lean), lines 49–59, defines `UpperTheory.V.Z.J.OneCharU_s`. Its quantifier order is

    for all admissible arithmetic data, for every test element sigma,
    if all admissible trace differences at sigma are nonzero,
    there exists a finite set X_sigma of admissible representations
    with Vn <= 2 sum_{rho in X_sigma} v_ell(tr rho(sigma)-t_sigma).

The representations in X_sigma are pairwise non-isomorphic. They lie in one of two admissible branches (`Adm3U_s` and `Adm2A`). The trace target is Psi(sigma)+1+Psi(sigma)^(-1). The estimate also asserts finiteness of the length `Vlen`.

In [CorGKOf.lean](https://github.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/blob/7cbf7a0c32fca1badf05931879a37c3f8a2b03b4/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/SmallClassNumbers/CorGKOf.lean), `corGK_of` constructs the required pointwise estimate from `HypothesesH'`; `corGK` supplies that hypothesis via `published_hypotheses_from_patching_alt litN SR.descWeak`. This source inspection confirms the stronger term is present in the original dependency path. We have not compiled an extracted wrapper or checked its full dependency closure.

The shortened theorem `exists_mFoldTwist_hasDivisibleSelmerLengthLe`, in [TwistingTheorem.lean](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean/blob/72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207/grh/src/New/Congruences/Selmer/TwistingTheorem.lean), changes the character in order to make certain Selmer groups short. That is excellent for the denominator argument but is not an estimate forcing the original untwisted even Selmer group to vanish.

## 5. Exact finite certificate theorem

The following elementary deduction clarifies how to use a pointwise estimate without incorrectly imposing a common witness at all Frobenius probes.

**Proposition (products before gcd).** Fix a prime ell, an arithmetic obstruction S, and a nonempty finite set Q of permitted probes. Suppose that for every q in Q the pointwise estimate has the following consequence: if S is nonzero, then some admissible branch j has a nonunit trace difference at q. Suppose exact integers D_{q,j} have been constructed such that every nonunit trace difference from branch j implies ell divides D_{q,j}. Then

    S nonzero implies ell divides gcd_{q in Q} product_j D_{q,j}.

**Proof.** For each q separately choose whichever branch contains its nonunit witness. Its integer factor is divisible by ell, so the product over branches is divisible by ell. This holds for every q; hence their gcd is divisible by ell. No common representation or common branch across probes is required.

The integers may be shifted Hecke determinants det(T_{q,j}-a_{q,j}I), or integral norms of such determinants when the coefficient field is nontrivial. To justify the implication to a determinant, prove coverage: the admissible trace difference, after multiplication by an ell-adic unit, is an eigenvalue of the corresponding shifted matrix, with compatible Frobenius convention. Reduction modulo a place over ell then gives a zero eigenvalue and a zero determinant. Coefficient norms can overinclude exceptional primes, which is harmless for exclusion but must not omit any branch.

For a finite abelian group A, suppose this implication holds for every ell outside an explicitly controlled set B, and its ell-primary part is already known zero for ell in B. If every prime divisor of the displayed gcd belongs to B, then A=0: otherwise an odd or even prime divisor of |A| would contradict the appropriate condition. This is the finite arithmetic endpoint of a valid K-group proof. The hard work lies in the Selmer comparison and coverage, rather than the gcd identity.

The pointwise estimate's “all differences nonzero” premise is automatic when all branch determinant tests are ell-adic units. If the conclusion is used contrapositively when some determinant is divisible by ell, maintain this premise explicitly; do not apply the estimate at a trace equality without checking the original statement.

## 6. Current literature and bounded novelty

Two very recent primary preprints already pursue this route. Pintar's [version 3 landing record](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00829v3), with [PDF labelled version 5](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00829v3), states vanishing through degree 104 conditional on the Step 17 arithmetic construction. Mok–Zheng's [24 November 2026 preprint](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00998v1) claims unconditional vanishing of K_{4m}(Z) for 1<=m<=30. Its provenance notes that the modified Lean entry point has not been fully rebuilt; its PDF distinguishes finite arithmetic verification from the still-awaited wrapper replay. These are reported claims, not independent verifications by this audit. No novelty claim through degree 120 is justified here.

Likewise, cyclicity of K_n(Z) for n=2 modulo 4 through degree roughly 100 was already far below the classical known range: Weibel VI, Examples 10.3 and 10.3.2, records cyclicity for these degrees below 20,000. Thus footnote 6 must not be repackaged as a new small-degree cyclicity discovery.

K_124(Z)=0 is a concrete next target beyond the range of the located preprint, with e=62. This is an example of a follow-up problem, not a claim that a literature search proves it is the first presently unknown case. An honest work package is:

1. Extract and compile the original `OneCharU_s` term with its exact arithmetic data and the strict rational Selmer comparison. Preserve all local conditions and admissible branches.
2. Validate the spectrum coverage, including exceptional local types, the chosen imaginary quadratic field, split versus inert Frobenius, integral lattices, and coefficient norms.
3. Reproduce an already claimed degree as an end-to-end check of the bridge.
4. At e=62 construct exact matrices, compute the products of branch determinants at several probes, and exclude every remaining prime via a second field or a separate cyclotomic calculation.
5. Only after both the bridge and arithmetic certificate close should one assert a new K-group vanishing theorem.

The finite certificate method offers a realistic incremental route. An all-weight theorem K_{4k}(Z)=0 would require uniform control of these determinants or a genuinely stronger Selmer argument and is equivalent to the Vandiver problem in the usual global formulation. The present audit does not supply that missing result.

## 7. Other transfers: short assessment

The analytic component in the shortened repository is broader than its final Dirichlet-L application. `Lib/Analytic/Landau.lean` abstracts a one-sided remainder estimate into elimination of poles with nonnegative real-axis residues. `New/Reduction/Zeros/Criterion.lean` combines it with quadratic twists chosen to have no zeros in a selected compact rectangle. A higher-degree Artin or automorphic application would still need two substantial inputs: an arithmetic construction yielding the corresponding one-sided remainder bound with square-root exponent, and an auxiliary twist family with the required compact-region nonvanishing. Neither follows merely by relabelling the present Dirichlet characters. Prior reports already discuss this broad direction, so the K-theory audit above is the more discriminating addition.
