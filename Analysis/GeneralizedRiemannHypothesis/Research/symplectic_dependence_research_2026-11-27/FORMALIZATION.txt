FORMALIZATION STATUS AND PROPOSED LEAN DEVELOPMENT

Status
------
This archive supplies complete written proofs and exact computational
checks. The finite identities were also checked by independent vector
enumeration, including characteristic two and a nonprime field. These
checks are evidence about the formulas, not a formal proof of the general
theorems. No compiled Lean formalization of the new results is included,
and external peer review has not been completed. Parallel mathematical
review during preparation was an internal check. Nothing here asserts a
new audit of the repository's complete RH/GRH formalization.

The proposed interface names below describe future contracts. They are
not claims that declarations with these names already exist in Mathlib.
No placeholder Lean file, admitted theorem, or untested proof script is
being presented as a completed formalization. The new finite-field
results are unconditional and require no RH or GRH theorem as an axiom.

1. Common finite foundation
---------------------------
Use a finite field F with cardinality q, and a finite-dimensional
F-vector space V of dimension 2*n equipped with a nondegenerate
alternating bilinear form. Keep the cardinality M=q^(2*n) distinct from
the ambient dimension. Assume n>=1. Define the subtype of nonzero vectors,
its uniform probability mass function, and independent vertex sampling
on a finite simple graph with a chosen orientation for every edge.

Proposed interfaces: nonzeroUniform, edgeObservation, graphObservationLaw,
edgeMarginal, independentEdgeLaw, finiteTV, finiteChiSquare.

First prove the one-edge counts and strict positivity of the comparison
law. Use finite sums for exact probability identities; convert to real
probability measures only when needed for the limiting results. Separate
the characteristic-free alternating-form arguments from coordinates.

2. Exact cycle laws
------------------
Dependencies: additive-character orthogonality, matrix multiplication and
trace, the constant subspace and its complement, and multiplicative
characters of F's nonzero elements.

Proposed interfaces: characterMatrix, characterMatrix_rowSum,
characterMatrix_mul, cycleCharacteristicFunction, evenCycleResonance,
cyclePointMass, cycleGroupedLaw, cycleTV_closed, cycleChiSquare_closed.

Prove F_s F_t = M*P_(s/t)-J on nonzero vectors. Derive the cyclic trace
formulas before Fourier inversion. Establish the elementary Gauss-sum
identity and the even-cycle resonance sign (-1)^(length/2) in every
characteristic. Prove the grouped multiplicities, normalization,
nonnegativity, and divergence formulas by finite sums. The sufficient
statistic records the number of zero labels, plus the resonance bit for
an even cycle without zero labels. It is sufficient; minimality is not
claimed. Cycle lengths are at least three and orientations are cyclic.

3. Forests and cactus factorization
----------------------------------
Dependencies: the symplectic group's transitivity on nonzero vectors,
finite product measures, leaf removal, and a block decomposition of a
finite cactus graph.

Proposed interfaces: forestEdgeIndependence, blockLaw_givenArticulation,
cactusBlockFactorization, graphEdgesIndependent_iff_forest,
cactusLikelihoodFactorization, cactusChiSquare_product.

Prove first that a block's conditional law does not depend on its shared
nonzero articulation vector. Induct over leaf blocks to obtain exact
independence of whole cycle blocks and bridges. Do not replace the cactus
hypothesis by an assumption about merely counting cycles: overlapping
cycles generally do not provide independent information.

4. A reusable product-experiment limit theorem
---------------------------------------------
Formulate this independently of finite fields. Let independent factors
have likelihoods 1+u, each with reference mean zero, maximum absolute
increment tending to zero, and total variance I equal to the sum of
their chi-square divergences.

Proposed interfaces: productLogLikelihood_normalLimit,
productExperimentTV_profile, productExperiment_smallInformation,
productExperiment_largeInformation.

Formalize explicit Taylor remainder bounds, a triangular-array central
limit argument, and the bounded continuous function (1-exp(x))_+.
Use the exact chi-square product identity when I tends to zero and
Hellinger affinity when I tends to infinity. This isolates the analytic
work from the exact finite computations. Apply it to cacti with fixed q,
n tending to infinity, and uniformly bounded cycle lengths. The number
of cycles may grow arbitrarily. The profile is
2*Phi(sqrt(tau)/2)-1 when I tends to tau, including the endpoints.

5. Fixed anchors and overlapping-cycle examples
-----------------------------------------------
Proposed interfaces: independentAnchorProbability,
anchorConditionalColumnLaw, anchorMixtureTV_bound,
anchorColumnChiSquare_closed, anchorCriticalProfile,
overlappingCyclesCounterexample, rareAnchorChiSquare_lowerBound.

For finite statements require 2<=a<=2*n. Count independent nonzero
anchors, prove surjectivity of their pairing map, and count its fibres.
Conditional on anchor independence, all observed columns have the same
explicit law and are independent. Prove the total-variation mixture
error uniformly in the number of columns. Distinguish the original
mixture from this conditional product experiment: their chi-square
divergences can behave very differently. For the limiting profile fix
q and a, let n grow, and apply the product-experiment theorem. Derive
the K_(2,m) overlapping-cycle and rare-event bounds separately.

6. Companion: many prescribed small frames
-----------------------------------------
Proposed interfaces: alternatingBorderNullity,
prescribedBlockKernelBadEvent_bound, nullityChain_orderedCoupling,
nullityChain_hittingBound, prescribedBlockNullityTV_bound,
unrestrictedGramLikelihood, conditionalBlockInjection_bound,
smallFrameCriticalProfile.

Start with exact alternating-form embedding counts, Gaussian binomial
coefficients, and the unrestricted Gram likelihood by radical dimension.
Next formalize the nullity chain with transition probabilities q^(-k)
and 1-q^(-k). The prescribed-block coupling uses an unconditional bad-
event bound, same-parity order preservation, and a killed exponential
moment; it does not require a uniformly small conditional bad-event
probability for every history. Establish the quantitative estimate for
D>=8 and maximum block size b<=n.

Condition independent matrix blocks on their prescribed Grams and then
on injectivity. Transfer nullity universality through the bounded
function (1-f)_+, not through an unbounded likelihood. Finally prove
convergence of the finite products and nullity masses. The companion
limit assumes fixed q, fixed integer c, D=2*n+c, and b=o(D). Prescribed
alternating forms may have arbitrary radicals. Any extension to growing
c or growing field order needs a separately checked error estimate.

Completion criterion
--------------------
A future formalization should pin Lean and Mathlib versions, compile
all declarations without sorry or new mathematical axioms, and record
the actual dependency and axiom reports. Small finite examples should
then be connected to the definitions as regression checks. The present
Python outputs remain reproducible verification artifacts, not substitutes
for these future kernel-checked proofs.
