3. MATHEMATICAL INPUT ACTUALLY USED

Article/sections/spn.tex develops finite-field Fourier estimates,
positive-type stabilizer bounds, prescribed-Gram embedding counts, exact
two-frame laws, and rank/residual-form comparisons. Two particularly
useful formulas are:

  spn:lem:alt-gram:
    A rank-2t alternating Fourier variable has coefficient q^(-2nt)
    for the Gram matrix of a uniform 2n-by-D matrix.

  spn:lem:gram-frames:
    The number of isometric injections of a d-dimensional alternating
    space of rank 2s into a symplectic space of dimension 2n is
    q^(2nd-binomial(d,2)) product_{j=n-d+s+1}^n (1-q^(-2j)),
    if d-s<=n, and zero otherwise.

Article/sections/src.tex explains the transfer of mechanisms from the
larger proof program. The new papers use the finite-field mechanism itself;
they do not invoke RH or Dirichlet GRH.

6. RELATED PRIMARY LITERATURE

Z. Tang and Z.-X. Wan, “Symplectic graphs and their automorphisms,”
European Journal of Combinatorics 27 (2006), 38–50.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2004.08.002
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0411452
Their projective symplectic graph spectra already provide classical
cycle-count information; over F_2 their vertices are nonzero vectors.
The new claims concern full field-labelled laws and the resulting
statistical experiments, not the invention of symplectic graph spectra.

A. Iosevich, G. Jardine and B. McDonald, “Cycles of arbitrary length
in distance graphs on F_q^d” (2021).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00748
This supplies cycle-count estimates for distance and dot-product graphs
on large subsets, a direct antecedent for the counting subject.

P. Bright et al., “Generalized point configurations in F_q^d” (2023).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10853
This treats graph configurations for general nondegenerate bilinear and
quadratic forms over odd finite fields. Those subset-counting estimates
are distinct from the exact full-label experiment used here.

M. Brennan, G. Bresler and B. Huang, “De Finetti-Style Results for
Wishart Matrices: Combinatorial Structure and Phase Transitions” (2021).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14011
This is a close conceptual predecessor: a graph specifies observed entries
of a real Gram/Wishart matrix, and graph structure governs total variation
thresholds. Topology-dependent detectability is an established theme.

S. Bubeck, J. Ding, R. Eldan and M. Z. Racz, “Testing for high-dimensional
geometry in random graphs,” Random Structures & Algorithms 49 (2016).
https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20633
https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5713
This gives an earlier geometric-versus-independent-edge testing setting
and signed triangle statistics. The broad testing idea is not new here.

J. Koenig and H. Nguyen, “Rank of Near Uniform Matrices” (2021).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00107
https://people.math.osu.edu/nguyen.1261/cikk/nearuniform-arxiv.pdf
Rank universality with deterministic entries is a substantial predecessor
to the companion. Its stated alternating result assumes odd q. The
companion credits this background and claims the uniform full conditioned
experiment and profile, rather than the general idea of rank universality.

A. Goebel, M. Pappik and L. Schiller, “Information-Theoretic Thresholds
for Bipartite Latent-Space Graphs Under Noisy Observations” (2026).
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/gobel26b.html
https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11129
The conference record describes Gaussian latent vectors, noisy observed
edges, and information-theoretic thresholds. It provides another related
continuous setting rather than these exact finite-field formulas.

The search covered symplectic graphs/association schemes, finite-field
cycle counts and bilinear forms, finite-field geometric testing, masked
Wishart laws, and alternating-rank universality. It was not exhaustive.

7. VERIFICATION AND ITS LIMITS

The main enumerator generates actual nonzero-vector assignments, then
compares their empirical exact counts with separately derived formulas.
It does not generate its “observations” from the proposed probability law.
GF(4) uses F_2[x]/(x^2+x+1), not arithmetic modulo four.

The anchor checks test conditional laws, mixture bounds, information
constants, and rare-event bounds. The companion independently generates
Gram matrices and conditioned frames and checks the stated density,
normalizer, bordering chain, and coupling bound in finite cases.

All recorded checks passed. The PDFs were rendered and visually inspected;
the recorded PDF checks found no clipping or unresolved references.
Finite checks do not establish the universal theorems: the written proofs
do. FORMALIZATION.txt gives a precise future formalization boundary.
