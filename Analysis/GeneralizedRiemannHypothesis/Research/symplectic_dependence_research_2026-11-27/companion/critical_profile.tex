\title{\textbf{Universal critical profiles\\for many prescribed symplectic frames}\\[6pt]
\large A continuation of the finite-field spanning and frame method}
\author{Research note prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov}
\date{27 November 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Consider many independently placed ordered frames in a finite symplectic
space. Prescribe every frame's internal alternating form, including
arbitrary degeneracy, and observe all pairings between different frames.
We prove that their total variation distance from independent uniform
cross-pairings has a universal limiting profile when the total frame
dimension differs from the ambient dimension by a fixed integer. The
profile is independent of the number, sizes, and internal forms of the
frames, provided the largest frame has size negligible compared with the
total. It includes both parities of the total dimension and every finite
field. Global nullity or rank tests attain the limiting distance. A
quantitative proof combines a bordering coupling for block-constrained
alternating matrices, exact Gram counts, and a bounded-likelihood transfer
that handles conditioning on degenerate forms. The scalar even profile
was previously known in the source for two nondegenerate frames; the
extension is the full experiment for many arbitrary small frames. All
results are unconditional finite algebra and probability.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Source, contribution, and verification status}
The starting point is the finite-field frame method in the ProveIt GRH
research collection, inspected at commit
\texttt{00cadb961bbe77c74e5d082c4948f8884d58a970} \cite{SourceArticle}.
This proof has no dependence on a statement about zeros of $L$-functions.
The source already supplies exact two-frame symplectic laws and an even
critical profile for two nondegenerate frames \cite{SourceProfile}.
Here the collection can contain arbitrarily many prescribed frames, and
every frame can have an arbitrary radical. The output concerns their
entire joint cross-pairing law, not merely a rank marginal.

Finite-field rank universality with some deterministic entries has substantial
antecedents. In particular, Koenig and Nguyen allow a small linear number
of arbitrary entries per column; their stated alternating theorem assumes
odd $q$ \cite{KoenigNguyen}. We give a direct, explicit argument for the
block setting in every characteristic, then transfer it to the conditioned
Gram experiment. No priority claim is made for rank universality itself,
for standard alternating rank counts, or for the even two-frame scalar
profile. The many-frame experiment and its uniform arbitrary-form critical
profile are the extension relative to the inspected repository. The
bibliographical comparison does not establish worldwide priority.

The manuscript contains a complete mathematical proof. The accompanying
program uses exact rational arithmetic and actual finite-field enumeration
to check its finite ingredients. A separate mathematical review checked
the conditioning and coupling arguments. The new theorem is not
Lean-formalized or externally peer-reviewed.

\section{Model and main theorem}

Fix a prime power $q$. Set
\[
 \eta_j=\prod_{i=1}^j(1-q^{-2i}),\qquad
 \gamma_j=\prod_{i=1}^j(1-q^{-i}),\qquad
 \theta_\infty=\gamma_\infty/\eta_\infty.
\]
Let $V_n$ be a symplectic space of dimension $2n$ over $\mathbb F_q$.
For each $n$, let $E_i$ have dimension $d_i\ge1$ and prescribed alternating
Gram matrix $H_i$, possibly degenerate. Independently choose uniform
isometric injections $X_i:E_i\to V_n$. Put $D=\sum_i d_i$ and
$b=\max_i d_i$. The observation consists of every cross-pairing matrix
$X_i^{\mathsf T}JX_j$, $i<j$. Its law is $P$. Let $Q$ be the law in which
all these entries are independent and uniform. In both experiments form
the complete alternating $D\times D$ matrix $\Gamma$ by inserting the
fixed diagonal blocks $H_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Universal critical profile]\label{thm:main}
If $D=2n+c$ for a fixed integer $c$ and $b=o(D)$, then, uniformly over all
partitions and all prescribed forms,
\[
 d_{\rm TV}(P,Q)\longrightarrow\delta_q(c).
\]
Writing $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$ for the parity of $c$,
\[
 \delta_q(c)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{\theta_\infty}{\gamma_\varepsilon}
     \left(1-\frac{\eta_\infty}{\eta_{(\varepsilon-c)/2}}\right),
       &c\le1,\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle\sum_{\substack{0\le k<c\\k\equiv c\pmod2}}
       \frac{\theta_\infty q^{-k(k-1)/2}}{\gamma_k},&c\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
For $c\le1$ the test that selects $P$ when $\dim\ker\Gamma>\varepsilon$
is asymptotically optimal. For $c\ge2$, the test that selects $P$ when
$\operatorname{rank}\Gamma\le2n$ is asymptotically optimal.
No hypothesis on the ranks or radicals of the individual $H_i$ is needed.
Since $b=o(D)$, all embeddings exist once $n$ is large.
\end{theorem}


\begin{corollary}[Dependence invisible to bounded collections]\label{cor:local}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, fix any positive integer $\ell$. The joint
cross-pairings of any chosen collection of at most $\ell$ frames converge
in total variation to independent uniform entries, uniformly over the
chosen collection and prescribed forms. The distance for the complete
collection instead tends to the strictly positive constant $\delta_q(c)$.
\end{corollary}

Thus any bounded collection can look independent while the full collection
retains a fixed statistical signal. The optimal global test only needs the
rank or nullity of the assembled Gram matrix.

The even part of the scalar profile is already present for two
nondegenerate frames in the inspected source \cite{SourceProfile}.
The extension here is its universality for arbitrarily many small frames
with arbitrary prescribed alternating forms, including degenerate ones;
the odd total-dimension profile is also included. This is not a novelty
claim for the even scalar profile itself.

\section{A block-nullity universality lemma}

Let $A_D$ be uniform over alternating matrices with arbitrary prescribed
diagonal blocks of sizes at most $b$, and let $T_D$ be an unrestricted
uniform alternating matrix. If $D\ge8$, then
\begin{equation}\label{cw:eq:nullity-bound}
 d_{\rm TV}(\dim\ker A_D,\dim\ker T_D)
 \le \frac{q^{2b-\lfloor D/4\rfloor}}{1-q^{-1}}+\beta^D,
 \qquad
 \beta=\left(\frac{151959}{160000}\right)^{1/4}<1.
\end{equation}
The right side is permitted to exceed one. In particular this bound tends
to zero exponentially for $b=o(D)$, or for $b\le\rho D$ with any fixed
$\rho<1/8$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Expose the rows and columns in any fixed order. At size $m$, let $S$ be
the previously exposed coordinates belonging to the same prescribed block
as the next coordinate. Put $s=|S|\le b$. Conditional on the exposed
matrix $A_m$, the new column is fixed on $S$ and independent uniform on
its complement. If
\[
 \ker A_m\cap\mathbb F_q^S=0,
\]
restriction of this random column to $(\ker A_m)^*$ is uniform. Indeed
the annihilator of the image of the random coordinate subspace in this
dual space is precisely the displayed intersection. Thus, writing
$k=\dim\ker A_m$, alternating bordering changes nullity to $k+1$ with
probability $q^{-k}$ and to $k-1$ with probability $1-q^{-k}$.
This statement uses no division by two.

For each nonzero $v$ supported on $S$, the equations $A_m v=0$ on the
$m-s$ coordinates outside its block are independent uniform equations.
Consequently
\[
 \Pr(\ker A_m\cap\mathbb F_q^S\ne0)
 \le(q^s-1)q^{-(m-s)}\le q^{2b-m}.
\]
Coupling from a starting size $m_0$ onwards with the standard alternating
nullity chain therefore costs at most
$q^{2b-m_0}/(1-q^{-1})$.

For completeness, the standard chain can be mixed from every initial
nullity at most $m_0$ with the correct parity. Couple all such chains with
one sequence of independent uniform variables. The upward probability
$q^{-k}$ decreases in $k$, so the coupling preserves order; two states
of equal parity can never cross. All chains coalesce by the first time
the upper chain, started at $m_0$, reaches 1 (or 0 if initially at 0).
Let $\tau$ be this time. Until $\tau$, the state is at least 2, so for
$r=6/5$,
\[
 \mathbb E[r^{Z_{t+1}}\mid Z_t=k]
 =r^k\{q^{-k}r+(1-q^{-k})r^{-1}\}
 \le r^k\frac{37}{40}.
\]
Applying this to the chain killed on reaching 1 gives
\[
 \Pr(\tau>L)\le r^{m_0-2}(37/40)^L.
\]
Couple with the standard nullity process of an unrestricted uniform
alternating matrix, whose size-$m_0$ nullity is at most $m_0$ and has
the same parity. Take $m_0=\lfloor D/4\rfloor$ and $L=D-m_0$.
The last bound is at most $\beta^D$, since
$(6/5)(37/40)^3=151959/160000$. This proves
\eqref{cw:eq:nullity-bound}.

\section{Unrestricted Gram laws and the scalar profile}

Let $X$ be a uniform $2n\times D$ matrix and let
$G=X^{\mathsf T}JX$. Its density relative to $T_D$ depends only on
$k=\dim\ker G$. Put $c=D-2n$. For $k\equiv c\pmod2$,
\begin{equation}\label{cw:eq:likelihood}
 f_{n,D}(k)=\eta_n
  \sum_{t=\max(0,(c+k)/2)}^k
    \qbinom{k}{t}
    \frac{q^{ct-t(t+1)/2}}{\eta_{t-(c+k)/2}}.
\end{equation}
An empty sum means zero. To prove it, choose the kernel $K$ of the map
$X$. It is a $t$-subspace of the radical of $G$. The quotient has
dimension $D-t$ and rank $D-k$, and its number of isometric injections
into $V_n$ is
\[
 q^{2n(D-t)-\binom{D-t}{2}}
 \frac{\eta_n}{\eta_{t-(c+k)/2}}.
\]
The quotient embeds precisely when $t\ge(c+k)/2$. Multiply by the
number $\qbinom{k}{t}$ of kernels and divide by $q^{2nD-\binom D2}$.

The following sign fact works for even and odd $D$:
every feasible $k\ge2$ satisfies $f_{n,D}(k)>1$.
Here feasibility means $k\ge c$ and the correct parity.
Write $F=f_{n,D}/\eta_n$. If $(c+k)/2\ge2$, its first summand is at least
$q^{t(t-1)/2}\ge q$, using $\qbinom{k}{t}\ge q^{t(k-t)}$.
If $(c+k)/2=1$, that summand is
$\qbinom{k}{1} q^{1-k}\ge1+q^{-1}$.
Both exceed $\eta_\infty^{-1}$, since
$q\eta_\infty>1$ and $(1+q^{-1})\eta_\infty>1$.
For the latter inequalities one can use
$\eta_\infty>1-(q^2-1)^{-1}$; at $q=2$ the strictness of the product
bound supplies the strict inequality.

In the remaining case write $h=-(c+k)/2\ge0$ and $Q=q^{-2h-2}$.
The terms $t=0,1$ give
\[
 F\ge\frac{1+aQ}{\eta_{h+1}},\qquad
 a=\frac{1-q^{1-k}}{q-1}\ge q^{-1}.
\]
Because $\eta_\infty/\eta_{h+1}\ge1-Q/(q^2-1)$,
\[
 \eta_\infty F
 \ge(1+aQ)(1-Q/(q^2-1))>1.
\]
Indeed $a(q^2-1-Q)>1$ follows from $a\ge q^{-1}$,
$Q\le q^{-2}$, and $q\ge2$.

Write $D=2d+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$.
If $d\le n$, the only likelihood deficit is at the minimum possible
nullity $\varepsilon$, where
\[
 f_{n,D}(\varepsilon)=\eta_n/\eta_{n-d}.
\]
For $\varepsilon=1$ the last identity follows either by choosing the
radical vector after the nondegenerate part, or from the two terms in
\eqref{cw:eq:likelihood}. Therefore
\[
 d_{\rm TV}(G,T_D)
  =\Pr(\dim\ker T_D=\varepsilon)
      (1-\eta_n/\eta_{n-d}).
\]
For $D\ge2n+2$ the likelihood is zero off its rank-$\le2n$ support
and greater than one everywhere on it. Hence
\[
 d_{\rm TV}(G,T_D)=\Pr(\operatorname{rank}T_D>2n).
\]

Choosing the radical and a nonsingular form on the quotient gives
\[
 \Pr(\dim\ker T_D=k)
 =q^{-k(k-1)/2}\frac{\gamma_D}
  {\gamma_k\eta_{(D-k)/2}},\qquad k\equiv D\pmod2.
\]
These classical rank laws are also treated in \cite{FulmanGoldstein}.
For each fixed parity this tends to
$\lambda_\varepsilon(k)=\theta_\infty q^{-k(k-1)/2}/\gamma_k$.
The tails are bounded by a constant times $q^{-k(k-1)/2}$, so these
limits sum to one. For fixed $c=D-2n$, the displayed exact total
variation formulas converge to $\delta_q(c)$, with error
$O_{q,c}(q^{-2n})$.

\section{Conditioning on arbitrary prescribed blocks}

For a uniform $2n\times d$ matrix, let $F_{n,d}(H)$ be its Gram density
at the alternating form $H$. If $d\le n$, finite Fourier inversion gives
\begin{equation}\label{cw:eq:small-block}
 |F_{n,d}(H)-1|\le C_1(q)q^{2d-2n},\qquad
 C_1(q)=\frac{q^{-3}}{\eta_\infty(1-q^{-3})}.
\end{equation}
Indeed a Fourier character indexed by an alternating matrix of rank $2t$
has coefficient $q^{-2nt}$, and the number of such matrices is
\[
 q^{t(2d-2t-1)}\frac{\gamma_d}{\gamma_{d-2t}\eta_t}
 \le\eta_\infty^{-1}q^{t(2d-2t-1)}.
\]
Sum the resulting geometric majorant. The character calculation uses
independent row pairs and $\mathbb E\psi(u^{\mathsf T}Wv)=q^{-\operatorname{rank}W}$,
so it is valid in characteristic two.

If $\operatorname{rank}H=2s$, the density contributed by injective maps
alone is
\[
 \Phi_{n,d,s}=\frac{\eta_n}{\eta_{n-d+s}},\qquad
 1-\Phi_{n,d,s}\le\frac{q^{2d-2n}}{q^2-1}.
\]
Since $F_{n,d}(H)\ge\Phi_{n,d,s}\ge\eta_\infty$, the conditional
probability that a map with Gram $H$ is not injective is at most
\begin{equation}\label{cw:eq:inj-cost}
 C_2(q)q^{2d-2n},\qquad
 C_2(q)=\eta_\infty^{-1}\{C_1(q)+(q^2-1)^{-1}\}.
\end{equation}

Now condition $X$ only on its diagonal-block Grams $H_i$, and call the
cross-block law $P'$. Its density relative to $Q$ is exactly
\[
 \frac{dP'}{dQ}(\Gamma)=\frac{f_{n,D}(\dim\ker\Gamma)}{Z},
 \qquad Z=\prod_i F_{n,d_i}(H_i).
\]
Independence of the matrix blocks proves the product normalizer. Write
$S=\sum_i q^{2d_i-2n}\le Dq^{2b-2n}$. The bounds above imply
\[
 |Z-1|\le e^{C_1(q)S}-1,\qquad
 d_{\rm TV}(P,P')\le C_2(q)S.
\]
The latter is simply conditioning the independent blocks further on
injectivity, then applying the observation map.

There is no need to control an unbounded likelihood by a nullity-TV
estimate. Let $g(k)=(1-f_{n,D}(k))_+\in[0,1]$. Since
$\mathbb E_Q f_{n,D}=Z$, the Lipschitz property of $x\mapsto(1-x)_+$
gives
\[
 \left|d_{\rm TV}(P',Q)-\mathbb E_Q g(\dim\ker\Gamma)\right|
 \le |Z-1|.
\]
Changing the nullity law from $Q$ to $T_D$ costs at most
\eqref{cw:eq:nullity-bound}, and
$\mathbb E g(\dim\ker T_D)=d_{\rm TV}(G,T_D)$.
Thus, whenever $D\ge8$ and $b\le n$, the fully quantitative comparison is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 |d_{\rm TV}(P,Q)-d_{\rm TV}(G,T_D)|
 \le \frac{q^{2b-\lfloor D/4\rfloor}}{1-q^{-1}}+\beta^D
       +e^{C_1(q)S}-1+C_2(q)S .\quad}
\]
This proves the main limit, uniformly over the forms and partitions.

For the testing assertion when $c\le1$, the likelihood is constant at
nullity $\varepsilon$, so
\[
 P'(\dim\ker\Gamma>\varepsilon)-Q(\dim\ker\Gamma>\varepsilon)
 =Q(\dim\ker\Gamma=\varepsilon)
   \left(1-\frac{f_{n,D}(\varepsilon)}Z\right)
 \longrightarrow\delta_q(c).
\]
Replacing $P'$ by $P$ costs $o(1)$. For $c\ge2$, every true Gram matrix
has rank at most $2n$, and the uniform model's probability of violating
this bound tends to $\delta_q(c)$. In both cases the test attains the
limiting total variation distance.


\paragraph{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:local}.}
For a collection of at most $\ell$ frames, its total dimension $D'$ is at
most $\ell b=o(n)$ and eventually is at most $n$. Apply
\eqref{cw:eq:small-block} to the entire $D'$-column Gram matrix, as well
as to its prescribed blocks. Its unnormalized density differs uniformly
from one by $O_q(q^{2D'-2n})$. The product normalizer differs from one by
$O_{q,\ell}(q^{2b-2n})$, and the further conditioning on injectivity costs
$O_{q,\ell}(q^{2b-2n})$. These bounds tend to zero uniformly over the
chosen collection. The full-collection assertion is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\paragraph{Tail behavior of the profile.}
As $c\to-\infty$ along parity $\varepsilon$,
\[
 \delta_q(c)\sim
 \frac{\theta_\infty}{\gamma_\varepsilon(q^2-1)}q^{c-\varepsilon}.
\]
As $c\to+\infty$,
\[
 1-\delta_q(c)\sim\eta_\infty^{-1}q^{-c(c-1)/2}.
\]
These statements concern the explicit limiting profile. Passing to offsets
$c=c_n$ growing with $n$ needs checking the quantitative error against the
profile tail; no relative-error assertion for arbitrary growing offsets
is implicit in the fixed-$c$ theorem.

\section{Exact verification and reproduction}
The program \texttt{code/verify.py} uses only Python's standard library.
It generates actual vectors and alternating matrices independently of the
formulas used for comparison. Arithmetic over $\mathbb F_4$ is implemented
as $\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^2+x+1)$, with no integer arithmetic modulo four.

The first check enumerates 1,607,096 vector arrays in 16 cases over
$\mathbb F_2$, $\mathbb F_3$, $\mathbb F_4$, and $\mathbb F_5$. For all
55,631 possible Gram fibers in these cases, it compares the exact count
with \eqref{cw:eq:likelihood}. It also checks the alternating nullity
formula, every relevant likelihood sign, and the exact total variation
identity. Both parities and all three regimes (below, at, and above
ambient dimension) occur.

The second check addresses the new block argument directly. Eight
prescribed-block configurations include isotropic, nondegenerate, and
mixed forms. The program enumerates 473 prefixes satisfying the required
kernel condition and all 16,274 random bordering columns for those
prefixes. In every case the upward nullity probability is exactly
$q^{-k}$. It separately checks the bad-kernel union bound. A further 5,612
exact rational comparisons verify the killed-chain bound across four
field sizes and a range of starting states and times.

Finally, four small conditioned-frame experiments enumerate another
262,144 arrays and 53,550 actual frame tuples to check the product
normalizer $Z$, the density $f/Z$, and the injectivity-conditioning
comparison by direct enumeration. These distinguish the law conditioned
only on Gram matrices from the law of actual frames, a distinction needed
for degenerate forms. All checks pass. Their exact counts and rational
probabilities are in \texttt{results/verification.json}; a compact count
summary is in \texttt{results/summary.json}. The finite checks support the
formulas but do not replace the proof of the asymptotic statement.

To reproduce the tests and rebuild the document, run from this directory:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 code/verify.py
pdflatex critical_profile.tex
pdflatex critical_profile.tex
\end{verbatim}
The script writes the exact results to \texttt{results/verification.json}
and \texttt{results/summary.json}. The delivered PDF was compiled with pdfLaTeX.

\section{Scope}
The theorem fixes the finite field and the offset $c$. The largest frame
must have dimension $o(D)$; the quantitative bound also covers a sufficiently
small fixed proportion of $D$. It does not cover an unrestricted collection
containing a frame of size comparable with the entire ambient space.
The quantitative comparison assumes $D\ge8$ and $\max_i d_i\le n$;
both conditions hold eventually under the theorem's assumptions.
The asymptotic optimality is for one complete observation of all
cross-pairings. No assertion of exact optimality for finite degenerate
frame experiments is made.
