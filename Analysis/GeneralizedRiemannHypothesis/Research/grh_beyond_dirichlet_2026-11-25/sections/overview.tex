\section{Answer and attribution}

\textbf{Yes: there is a concrete transfer beyond standard consequences of RH
and GRH.} The latest incoming work contains a counterexample to
Asada--Fourakis--Manski--McNew--Miller--Moreland Conjecture~6.3 on the upper
density of geometric-progression-free subsets of \(\F_q[x]\)
\cite{asada,priorDensity}. Our inspection revalidates that argument and gives
a sharper explicit certificate. Its relation to the Lean project is a
combinatorial ingredient used inside the proof, rather than the conclusion
that certain zeros lie on a line.

The distinction in attribution matters. The polynomial-ring disproof was
already in the user's repository. We do not present it as discovered in
this session. The underlying carry-free sphere construction is classical
Behrend technology, used by the Lean development for an arithmetic purpose
\cite{behrend,repo}. Our additional results are the variance-based cutoff
and the stabilizer-controlled kernel-moment formulation. Neither receives
a claim of worldwide priority. Searching for prior work can uncover
precedents; failing to find one cannot establish novelty.

\subsection{The actual arithmetic mechanism}

The readable edition organizes the proof around three assertions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Short forms.} A normalized Poincare construction produces
integral source forms whose lifted norms are small, while controlling the
local index cost.
\item \textbf{Enough forms.} A second-moment argument finds few translates
whose images span almost the entire target space.
\item \textbf{Long integral forms.} A product formula, Gram determinants,
twists chosen at finite places, and denominator estimates give a lower
bound for the same covolume.
\end{enumerate}

Write \(d\) for target dimension, \(h\) for the principal height contribution,
\(V\) for logarithmic squared covolume, and \(\delta\) for index cost.
Schematically the same quantity is trapped between
\[
 h-\text{lower error}
 \ \le\ \frac{V+2\delta}{d}
 \ \le\ h-\frac{n}{d}m\,\mathrm{rem}+\text{upper error}.
\]
The common \(h\) cancels. With the actual admissible data,
\path{star_inequality} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star}
 m\,\mathrm{rem}\le f\,a(\log b)^5.
\end{equation}
The number of theta lifts satisfies
\(\sqrt{x}\le m\le2\sqrt{x}\). Thus the saving \(1/m\) has the
square-root scale \cite{mainInequality,guide}.

In the readable endgame the normalized rational-prime remainder obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remainder}
\begin{split}
R_{q_0}(x)\le {}&
\frac{\varphi(q_0)(\log b)^6}{m}
{}+|R_{q_0}(x_1)|+(\log b)|R_1(x_1)|.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The much larger second cutoff makes the latter terms negligible. The
result is a one-sided bound of size \(x^{-1/2+\varepsilon}\).
A suitable quadratic twist prevents cancellation at a hypothetical
off-line zero, and a Landau argument excludes it
\cite{oneSided,suzuki}.

This architecture explains both the promise and the difficulty of transfer.
The reusable features are divisibility versus size, nearly spanning
families, local twists, and one-sided analytic criteria. A new \(L\)-function
requires the correct arithmetic objects, local prime weights, and matching
principal terms; a functional equation and an Euler product do not supply
these objects.

\subsection{What the earlier reports already cover}

The incoming corpus already explores many directions that would otherwise
look like new proposals. The following distinctions guide this report.

\begin{itemize}
\item The polynomial-density archive already disproves Conjecture~6.3.
The contribution here is review, a self-contained reconstruction, and an
improved quantitative margin.
\item The symplectic-block archive already passes from single-vector
positive-type Fourier estimates to ordered frames, bounds the full
rectangular compression law in total variation, and analyzes an isotropic
symplectic boundary law. We use this as prior work, rather than claiming
the frame construction as new \cite{priorSymplectic}.
\item The \(K\)-theory reports already investigate Bernoulli screening and
comparison spaces for \(K_{124}(\Z)\) and \(K_{128}(\Z)\). They retain missing
large certificates and Selmer-to-Hecke bridges. The shortened proof's odd
Selmer statement cannot be applied to an even Tate exponent merely by
renaming it.
\item The fixed-field report already emphasizes the near-full-rank
hypothesis and expensive completion vectors. Knowing only that half the
target has been spanned is not an acceptable substitute for
\(n\ge(1-4/b^2)d\) where the lower-bound theorem requires it.
\item The nonabelian synthesis already constructs local \(S_3\) frames.
The conductor-\(23\) target below supplies a particularly explicit analytic
test case and an exact benchmark, while retaining the unresolved global
arithmetic steps.
\end{itemize}

These observations are documented in
\path{sources/prior_work_review.txt}. They prevent an apparent advance
from being only a rephrasing of an existing report.
