FORMALIZATION ROADMAP
Sharp quantitative Hermite--Sylvester certificates

Status
------
The human-readable mathematical proof in the accompanying report is complete.
A full Lean formalization has not been performed. This file is a mathematical
implementation plan, not Lean source, and no part of it is represented as a
compiled proof. No placeholder axioms or admitted Lean declarations are
included. Mathlib declaration names should be resolved against the chosen
version during implementation; none are guessed here.

1. Exact core objects and target
-------------------------------
Fix an integer N>=2 and complex numbers z_j indexed by j in {0,...,N-1}.
Assume |z_j|<=1 and that the multiset of the z_j is invariant under complex
conjugation, including multiplicities. An implementation can express the latter
by a permutation pi of the index set satisfying z_(pi(j))=conj(z_j).

Define, for natural k:
  s_k = sum_j z_j^k,
  H_(r,c) = Re(s_(r+c)),  0<=r,c<N,
  Q_H(v) = sum_r sum_c v_r H_(r,c) v_c, for real vectors v,
  ||v||_2^2 = sum_r v_r^2,
  b = max_j |Im(z_j)|,
  K_N = 4 floor(N/2),
  p_N = 2N-2.

First prove Im(s_k)=0 from conjugation symmetry. For epsilon>=0, define the
quadratic-form hypothesis
  LowerBound(H,epsilon) :
    for every real vector v, Q_H(v)>=-epsilon ||v||_2^2.

The preferred exact target avoids real roots and the eigenvalue API:

  LowerBound(H,epsilon)
    implies 2 b^(p_N) <= epsilon K_N^(p_N).

Equivalently, the same polynomial inequality holds with |Im(z_j)| in place of
b for every j. The displayed width bound in the paper follows by taking the
positive p_N-th root. Keep the integer-power statement as the main formal
endpoint and derive the real-power presentation separately.

A second exact target is

  for every real vector v,
  Q_H(v) >= -C_N b^2 ||v||_2^2,
  C_N=N^2(N-1)(7N-11)/6.

A defect defined through the smallest eigenvalue is unnecessary for these two
core theorems. Afterward, finite-dimensional spectral theory can identify the
least nonnegative epsilon satisfying LowerBound with
max(0,-lambda_min(H)), if that presentation is desired.

2. Finite algebra and interpolation lemmas
-----------------------------------------
(a) Power-sum quadratic identity.
For a real polynomial q(x)=sum_(r=0)^(N-1) v_r x^r, prove
  Q_H(v)=sum_j q(z_j)^2,
where the complex sum on the right is real. It is essential that this is the
square q(z_j)^2, not the squared absolute value.

(b) Distinct support and multiplicities.
Replace repeated root locations by d distinct nodes with positive integer
weights. Preserve d<=N and the original power sums. Only ordinary polynomial
interpolation at these distinct nodes is needed.

(c) Imaginary-height gap.
When b>0, list the k distinct positive imaginary heights
  0=y_0<y_1<...<y_k=b,
with 1<=k<=floor(d/2). A telescoping sum gives a consecutive gap delta>=b/k.
Partition the nodes into lower, middle, and upper groups using that gap.

(d) Conjugation-respecting interpolation.
Prescribe -i, 0, i on those three groups. Construct the unique interpolation
polynomial q of degree at most d-1. Prove its coefficients are real by conjugating
the interpolation data and applying uniqueness.

(e) Cluster divided differences.
Order the nodes by the three groups. Prove that every positive-order divided
difference wholly within one group vanishes. The first-order divided
differences are at most 1/delta in modulus: adjacent group values differ by 1
with distance at least delta; lower/upper values differ by 2 with distance at
least 2delta. Induction in the divided-difference recurrence then gives
  |divided_difference_of_order_r| <= 2^(r-1)/delta^r, r>=1.
No lower bound on distances between two nodes within one group is assumed.

(f) Coefficient bounds.
Prove that the coefficient l1 norm of product_(a<r)(x-z_a) is at most 2^r for
nodes in the unit disk. Set T=4/delta. Newton interpolation and the improved
first-order bound give
  ||coeff(q)||_2 <= ||coeff(q)||_1
  <= 1+(1/2) sum_(r=1)^(d-1) T^r <= T^(d-1),
using T>=2. Extend the coefficient vector by zero to dimension N.

(g) Negative witness and elimination of auxiliary parameters.
The quadratic identity gives Q_H(coeff(q))<=-2 because at least one conjugate
pair receives values +/-i. LowerBound therefore yields
  2<=epsilon ||coeff(q)||_2^2.
Combine the coefficient bound with delta>=b/k, k<=floor(d/2), d<=N, and b<=1
to prove the polynomial target in Section 1. Handle b=0 separately.

3. Quadratic upper bound
------------------------
(a) Let a_j=Re(z_j). The real-projection moment matrix H_R is PSD, since
Q_(H_R)(v)=sum_j q(a_j)^2>=0.

(b) Taylor's integral remainder on the vertical segment from a_j to z_j gives
  |Re(z_j^k)-a_j^k| <= k(k-1) b^2/2, k>=2.
The segment lies in the unit disk and the first-order term is purely imaginary.
For k=0,1 the difference is zero.

(c) Sum over the N roots and prove the symmetric row-sum quadratic estimate.
This can be done directly from 2|v_r v_c|<=v_r^2+v_c^2, avoiding an operator-norm
API. The maximal row is r=N-1, and its combinatorial sum is
  sum_(c=0)^(N-1)(N-1+c)(N-2+c)=N(N-1)(7N-11)/3.
This gives LowerBound(H,C_N b^2).

4. Perturbed data corollary
--------------------------
Let Hhat be assembled from real approximate moments with certified errors
|shat_k-s_k|<=e_k. A direct quadratic estimate gives
  |Q_(Hhat-H)(v)|<=E ||v||_2^2,
  E=max_r sum_c e_(r+c).
Uniform errors e_k<=e imply E<=Ne. Consequently
  LowerBound(Hhat,eta) implies LowerBound(H,eta+E).
Feed this into the polynomial target. Eigenvalue interval certificates, when
available, can be connected afterward; they are not needed for the abstract
error-budget theorem.

5. Sharpness
------------
Upper exponent: formalize the exact parity-block calculation for the root
multiset {ib,-ib,0,...,0}. The even block is PSD and the odd block is rank one
negative, with defect
  2b^2 sum_(r=0)^(floor(N/2)-1)b^(4r).

Lower exponent: choose N-2 distinct nonzero real base nodes and a pair +/-ih,
with fixed sufficiently small h>0. Establish positive definiteness of the
leading (N-1)-dimensional moment block A by continuity from h=0. The full
Vandermonde determinant is negative, so its Schur complement sigma is negative.
After scaling all nodes by t, completing the square gives
  Q_(H(t))(v)>=sigma t^(2N-2)||v||_2^2,
while the largest imaginary part is ht. This already rules out any larger
uniform inverse exponent. A matching Rayleigh vector gives the asymptotic
statement about the smallest eigenvalue if desired.

The cubic rational example in the report is a useful first milestone: every
matrix entry, Schur complement, and quadratic identity can be checked by exact
algebra before formalizing the all-N continuity argument.

The sharpness claim concerns bounds based only on the scalar semidefinite
defect. Do not formulate it as an impossibility theorem for every reconstruction
method using the full moment list or additional constraints.

6. Contour and L-function application
------------------------------------
Treat this as a separate analytic layer. For a holomorphic function F satisfying
F(conj z)=conj(F(z)), a conjugation-invariant domain contained in the unit disk,
and a zero-free positively oriented boundary, the residue theorem identifies
  (1/(2pi i)) integral z^k F'(z)/F(z) dz
with the power sum of the enclosed zeros, counted with multiplicities. Establish
finiteness and the exact count N before applying the matrix theorem. Meromorphic
functions with uncancelled poles give signed weights and require a different
statement.

For Xi(w)=xi(1/2+iw), take F(z)=Xi(T+Az), with real T and A>0. The standard
functional equation and conjugation symmetry show that F is real entire. A
zero corresponds to rho=1/2+i(T+Az), so
  |Re(rho)-1/2|=A|Im(z)|.

For a primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet character, write its root number as
exp(i theta) and use
  F(z)=exp(-i theta/2) Lambda_chi(1/2+i(T+Az)).
The functional equation makes this real entire. The constant phase cancels
from F'/F. Handle the primitive principal case through xi and reduce
imprimitive characters to their primitive completed L-functions.

These finite-window certificates do not require RH or GRH. A formalization
should preserve that independence rather than importing the repository's RH
theorem unnecessarily. The contour and L-function layers may require substantial
analytic library work beyond the finite algebraic theorem.
