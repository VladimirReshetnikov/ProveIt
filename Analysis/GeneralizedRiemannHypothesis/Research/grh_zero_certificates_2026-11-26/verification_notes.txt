MATHEMATICAL VERIFICATION NOTES
Sharp quantitative Hermite--Sylvester certificates
26 November 2026

Proof status
------------
The accompanying paper contains a complete human-readable proof. A full Lean
formalization has not been performed. The formalization roadmap describes the
remaining implementation work and is not a compiled proof. Numerical examples
illustrate the estimates; a numerical assertion is a certificate only when its
moment and eigenvalue error bounds have themselves been established.

Checked statement and constants
-------------------------------
For a conjugation-invariant multiset of N>=2 roots in the closed unit disk,
let H=(s_(r+c))_(0<=r,c<N), b=max_j|Im(z_j)|, and
D=max(0,-lambda_min(H)). The estimates are

  2 [b/(4 floor(N/2))]^(2N-2) <= D
  <= [N^2(N-1)(7N-11)/6] b^2.

The constants are explicit but are not claimed optimal. The exponents are
optimal in the sense specified below. The matrices are real symmetric because
the root multiset is invariant under conjugation with multiplicities retained.

Interpolation and repeated roots
--------------------------------
The negative quadratic-form witness interpolates the values -i, 0, i on three
imaginary-height clusters. Ordinary interpolation uses only distinct root
locations; repeated roots contribute their positive integer multiplicities to
the quadratic form. Thus repeated roots require no division by zero and no
confluent interpolation.

No within-cluster root separation is assumed. Every positive-order divided
difference within one cluster vanishes. A mixed consecutive segment has its
endpoints in different clusters, separated by at least the chosen height gap.
The first-order bound is 1/delta, and the higher-order bound is
2^(r-1)/delta^r. This gives the coefficient bound used in the stated constant.

The central quadratic identity involves sum_j q(z_j)^2. Replacing these squares
by squared absolute values would destroy the argument. Conjugation symmetry
and real polynomial coefficients make the displayed sum real.

Upper quadratic bound
---------------------
Replacing each root by its real part gives a PSD moment matrix. Taylor's
remainder on the vertical segment to the original root is quadratic in its
imaginary part because the linear term is purely imaginary. Summing the
entrywise bounds and using a symmetric row-sum estimate gives

  sum_(c=0)^(N-1)(N-1+c)(N-2+c)
  =N(N-1)(7N-11)/3.

The additional factor N/2 gives the verified coefficient
N^2(N-1)(7N-11)/6.

Sharpness checks
----------------
For the upper exponent, the roots {ib,-ib,0,...,0} give the exact defect

  D=2b^2 sum_(r=0)^(floor(N/2)-1)b^(4r).

The even-index matrix block is PSD; the odd-index block has one negative
eigenvalue. Hence no uniform replacement of b^2 by b^p with p>2 is possible.

For the lower exponent, take N-2 distinct nonzero real base nodes and a fixed
small conjugate pair, then scale every node by t. The leading moment block is
positive definite and the full matrix has a negative Schur complement sigma.
Completion of the square and a matching Rayleigh vector give

  lambda_min(H(t))/t^(2N-2) -> sigma<0,

while the largest imaginary part is proportional to t. This rules out a
larger uniform exponent than 1/(2N-2) in the inverse width bound.

For the explicit cubic family {t,it/2,-it/2},

  H(t)=[[3,t,t^2/2],[t,t^2/2,t^3],[t^2/2,t^3,9t^4/8]],
  sigma=-25/8,

and the exact bounds are

  -25t^4/8 <= lambda_min(H(t))
  <= -25t^4/[8(1+25t^2+9t^4/4)].

These optimality statements concern the relation between root displacement
and the scalar semidefinite defect. They do not establish minimax limits for
all algorithms using the complete moment list, coefficient information,
integral multiplicities, or additional zero data.

Noisy data and normalization
----------------------------
Moment errors |shat_k-s_k|<=e_k yield the operator-norm bound
E<=max_r sum_c e_(r+c); uniform errors at most e give E<=Ne. If a certified
interval [ell,u] contains lambda_min(Hhat), then

  max(0,-u-E) <= D <= max(0,-ell+E).

Substitution into the two-sided theorem gives valid displacement bounds. A
floating-point eigenvalue without an error enclosure does not suffice.

The certificate depends on the chosen polynomial coordinate and monomial
basis. For a physical window centered at height T with scale A, normalized
roots in the unit disk correspond to actual zeros
rho=1/2+i(T+Az). The physical horizontal displacement is A|Im(z)|. The scale
factor A must therefore be restored in the final width estimate.

Contour applications
--------------------
A contour logarithmic derivative supplies power sums only after the enclosed
zero count and the absence of boundary zeros have been established. The
function must be holomorphic inside the contour: uncancelled poles introduce
negative weights and invalidate the positive-multiplicity argument.

The Riemann function Xi(w)=xi(1/2+iw) is real entire. For a primitive
nonprincipal Dirichlet character with root number exp(i theta),
exp(-i theta/2) Lambda_chi(1/2+iw) is likewise real entire. The constant phase
cancels in its logarithmic derivative. The primitive principal case is handled
through xi. Imprimitive Euler-factor zeros must not be silently treated as
critical-line zeros.

The finite-window theorem is independent of RH and GRH. It supplies a
quantitative certificate from finite data, and by itself does not certify all
infinitely many zeros or turn a positive error tolerance into exact reality.

Provenance and originality
--------------------------
Classical Hermite--Sylvester real-rootedness and contour power-moment formulas
are established ingredients. The proposed contribution is the explicit
separation-free estimate, its sharp exponents, and the error-budget corollary.
The paper's targeted literature comparison supports only its stated qualified
originality claim; it is not an exhaustive proof of priority.

Repository source inspection and externally reported kernel checks are distinct
from a full repository rebuild in this session. Likewise, the new estimates
must not be described as Lean-verified without a separate completed build.
