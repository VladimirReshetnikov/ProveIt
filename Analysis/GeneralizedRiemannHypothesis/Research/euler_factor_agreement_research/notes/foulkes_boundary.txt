FOULKES-CHARACTER REDUCTION: A USEFUL IDENTITY, NOT A RESOLUTION

Let phi_{n,i}, 0<=i<=n-1, be the usual Foulkes/descent characters indexed so phi_{n,0}=1 and sum_i phi_{n,i}=Reg_{S_n}. For g with c(g) cycles, define their generating polynomial by
P_g(z)=sum_i phi_{n,i}(g) z^i
      =(1-z)^(n+1) sum_{k>=0}(k+1)^{c(g)}z^k
      =(1-z)^{n-c(g)} A_{c(g)}(z),
where A_c is the Eulerian polynomial with A_c(1)=c! and A_c'(1)=(c-1)c!/2.
Then P'_g(1) is:
(n-1)n!/2 if g=1;
-(n-1)! if g is a transposition;
0 otherwise.
These are precisely the values of (n-1)Ind_{<t>}^{S_n}(sign).
Therefore
U_n=(n-1)Ind(sign)-Reg+1
   =sum_{i=2}^{n-1}(i-1)phi_{n,i}.
This proves nonnegative decomposition in the Foulkes/descent characters, and so another proof that U_n is an actual character.

It does NOT prove that U_n is a positive rational sum of induced LINEAR characters. Foulkes/descent characters are sums of ribbon Specht characters; they should not be confused with Thrall's higher Lie characters or with the induced-cyclic representations also sometimes called Foulkes representations.

A second exact induction identity is
U_n - Ind_{S_{n-1}}^{S_n} U_{n-1}
 = Ind_{<t>}^{S_n}(sign)-std_n.
A uniform positive monomial decomposition of the right side would solve nab:q:un by induction, but none was found.
