REFEREE REVIEW: THE SHARP 7/9 BOUND FOR THREE CHARACTER FACTORS
2026-11-27

Assessment: the compact-group theorem, its equality classification, and the
extension to arbitrary Hecke quasi-character triples over number fields are
correct. The latter follows directly from Rajan's Theorem 1 and finite-order
twist classes; a separate unitary Hecke second-moment argument is unnecessary.

1. Precise compact-group statement

Let A and B be distinct multisets of three continuous one-dimensional unitary
characters of a compact group G with normalized Haar measure. Write D for the
set where their complete characteristic polynomials differ. Then m(D)>=2/9.
Equality holds exactly when, after possibly exchanging the packets, there are
a continuous unitary character a and independent order-three characters chi,
psi such that

  A = a {1,chi,chi^2},    B = a psi {1,chi,chi^2}.

Independence means <chi,psi> is isomorphic to C3 x C3. No finite-image or
abelian hypothesis on G is necessary; all the characters factor through its
abelianization. The common character a can have infinite order.

2. Bound, with cancellation handled explicitly

Cancel common characters with multiplicity; let r be the number of remaining
characters on each side. The disagreement set is unchanged because common
polynomial factors cancel. For the resulting trace difference f, orthogonality
gives N=int|f|^2=sum_c m_c^2 >= sum_c |m_c| = 2r, as positive and negative
multiplicities now have disjoint support. If r=1 or r=2, the pointwise bound
|f|<=2r gives m(D)>=1/(2r), hence at least 1/4. For r=3, different determinant
characters force disagreement on the complement of the kernel of their
quotient, a set of measure at least 1/2. If determinants coincide, the SU(3)
trace triangle gives |f|^2<=27. Therefore 6<=N<=27 m(D), proving 2/9.

3. Equality classification

At m(D)=2/9 we must have r=3, identical determinants, N=6, and |f|^2=27
throughout D. The last conclusion first holds almost everywhere and then
pointwise: D is open, the nonnegative deficit 27-|f|^2 is continuous there,
and Haar measure is positive on every nonempty open set. N=6 means all six
characters are distinct. Equality in the SU(3) trace diameter forces both
diagonal matrices to be scalar on D, with distinct scalar values whose ratio
is a primitive cube root of unity.

The two scalar kernels coincide: on D both matrices are scalar; outside D,
equal characteristic polynomials imply that if one matrix is scalar, so is
the other, with the same scalar value. Call this common closed subgroup K,
and the two scalar characters on K lambda and mu. The quotient lambda/mu
has nontrivial image in mu_3, and

  D = {g in K : lambda(g) != mu(g)}.

Consequently m(D)=(2/3)m(K), so m(K)=1/3 and [G:K]=3. The ratios among
characters within either packet are trivial on K. Since G/K=C3 and each
packet consists of three distinct characters, for a generator chi of the
annihilator of K we have A=a{1,chi,chi^2}, B=b{1,chi,chi^2}. Their determinants
are a^3 and b^3, hence psi=b/a satisfies psi^3=1. The packets are distinct,
so psi is not in <chi>; chi and psi are independent. Conversely this form
has disagreement exactly chi=1, psi!=1, of Haar measure 2/9.

4. Arbitrary Hecke quasi-character triples: unconditional reduction

Let K be a number field. Let A and B be distinct multisets of three idele
class quasi-characters. Omit the finitely many primes ramified for any of
them. Partition the six characters into equivalence classes under

  alpha ~ beta  iff  alpha/beta has finite order.

Rajan 2000, Theorem 1, p. 692, says that equality of the local components of
two quasi-characters on a positive-upper-density set forces their quotient
to have finite order. Therefore, for any characters in different classes,
the primes where their local components agree have upper natural density
zero. The union E of these finitely many exceptional sets also has density
zero. For an unramified character, equality of its value on a uniformizer
is equivalent to equality of its local character.

At every prime outside E, equality of the complete degree-three polynomials
is equivalent to equality of the two multisets inside every equivalence
class separately. If some class has unequal multiplicities in A and B,
there is no agreement outside E, so the total agreement density is zero.

Otherwise the multiplicities balance in every class. Within each class,
divide all characters by one fixed member. All resulting characters have
finite order. They factor through one common finite abelian Galois extension,
and Chebotarev computes every Boolean combination of their local equality
conditions. In particular the total agreement set has a natural density;
up to E it is a Chebotarev set.

If at least two classes occur and the full packets are unequal, some
nonidentical class has size at most two on each side. Its local disagreement
density is at least 1/4 by the cancellation bound above. Total disagreement
therefore has density at least 1/4. If just one class occurs, divide all six
characters by a common quasi-character and apply the finite compact-group
theorem: total disagreement density is at least 2/9.

Equality 2/9 can only occur in the single-class case. Hence all equality
pairs are exactly

  A = a {1,chi,chi^2},    B = a psi {1,chi,chi^2},

where a is now an ARBITRARY idele class quasi-character and chi,psi are
independent cubic finite-order Hecke characters. Conversely every such pair
has agreement density 7/9 by class field theory and Chebotarev. This proves
the optimal replacement of the n=3 threshold for arbitrary inducing triples,
including the triples appearing in Rajan's A_B. No RH or GRH is used.

5. Wording and scope

State the strongest result directly for distinct inducing character
multisets. At unramified primes the spherical principal-series classification
identifies these local multisets with the local representation. Thus the
result applies exactly to the character conclusion of Rajan's Conjecture 3.
If asserting a further global-isomorphism conclusion for arbitrary A_B
subquotients, follow Rajan's stated convention for pi(mu_1,...,mu_n); do not
silently strengthen to a uniqueness theorem for arbitrary subquotients of
one fixed induced representation. The optimal 7/9 bound and classification
of unequal inducing data do not need that additional issue resolved.

The finite-order cubic equality construction already appears in the supplied
repository. The contribution here is the sharp extension to all compact
one-dimensional character triples and all Hecke quasi-character triples,
its equality classification, and the connection to the published conjecture.
