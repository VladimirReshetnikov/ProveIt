FINAL INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF THE WRITTEN COMPANION
Date: 2026-11-27
Reviewed: rajan_counterexample.tex

Verdict: accepted mathematically. No concrete proof error or unmet automorphic
hypothesis was found in the written manuscript. This is an internal independent
review, not external peer review or formal proof verification.

The printed Conjecture 3 is accurately scoped: A_B permits Borel-induced
irreducible automorphic representations and does not require cuspidality.
The examples use legitimate finite-order Hecke inducing data. The text correctly
distinguishes irreducibility of an automorphic representation from reducibility
of its diagonal Galois parameter. The new theorem about arbitrary inducing
quasi-characters does not make an unwarranted uniqueness claim about arbitrary
global subquotients sharing the same data.

The moduli 7 and 13 construction, density 7/9, sixteen disagreement classes,
and first distinguishing prime 29 are correct. The mixed-order proof compares
complete eigenvalue multisets by explicit bijections; it is not merely a
trace-agreement argument. Its exact disagreement set, packet cardinalities,
arithmetic realization, and common-packet padding across the stated degree
intervals are all correct.

The compact-group sharp threshold proof handles determinant mismatch, common
character cancellation, repeated characters, and arbitrary compact groups
correctly. The equality proof upgrades almost-everywhere saturation using
continuity, identifies the common scalar subgroup of index three, and forces
precisely the claimed common twists of independent cubic packets. It has no
hidden assumption that the ambient compact group is abelian.

I independently rechecked Rajan's Theorem 1 on p. 692 in the primary PDF's
indexed text. It explicitly covers idele class quasi-characters, and the
preceding definition makes its upper density upper natural density. Its
contrapositive therefore justifies the density-zero cross-class coincidence
sets in the manuscript. Balanced finite-order equivalence classes reduce to
one finite abelian extension; imbalanced classes give density zero. When more
than one balanced class occurs, a differing class has reduced size at most
two and forces disagreement at least 1/4. A single class reduces exactly to
the finite-order cubic theorem. These steps prove existence of natural
density, the 7/9 upper bound, and the stated all-quasi-character equality
classification. The extension is valid.

One minor convention should preferably be explicit: "Dirichlet density" in
the new all-Hecke statement means the usual logarithmic-limit density.
Natural density implies that convention by partial summation. Rajan's paper
uses a stronger analytic-remainder convention in its introductory definition;
the written argument need not and does not establish that stronger convention
for the exceptional density-zero sets. Adding the usual limit definition
removes the potential ambiguity without changing any theorem or proof.

Attribution is appropriately limited: the original cubic packet is credited
to the ProveIt source, the application to the named conjecture and further
results are distinguished, and publication priority is not asserted as
certified. The manuscript does not overstate the specialist conjecture as a
disproof of Ramakrishnan's cuspidal conjecture or as a consequence requiring RH.

Integration note: The usual logarithmic-limit definition of Dirichlet density was added explicitly before final compilation.
