Scope
I read independent_extension.txt in full and checked every displayed mathematical step independently. The argument proves a sharp finite-group theorem, and extends to continuous finite-dimensional representations of compact groups with normalized Haar measure. It does not establish untempered automorphic strong multiplicity one or a new general RH.

Core verification
1. SU(3) triangle lemma is correct: for unit complex a,b,
   |1+a+ab|^2=3+2 Re(a+b+(ab)^(-1)).
   Applying this to omega^j U gives the three supporting half-planes for the triangle with vertices 3,3omega,3omega^2. Its diameter is 3sqrt(3); equality occurs only at two distinct vertices. Equality |tr U|=3 in the triangle inequality for eigenvalues forces U scalar.
2. If determinants differ, their quotient is a nontrivial one-dimensional character; at least half the group has different determinant, so the same fraction has different full characteristic polynomial.
3. If determinants agree, pointwise normalization by a cube root of the common determinant puts both matrices in SU(3). No global cube-root character is assumed. Thus squared trace difference is at most27.
4. Orthogonality gives integral |chi-rho minus chi-sigma|^2=2 for inequivalent irreducibles. Hence delta>=2/27.
5. In equality every disagreement element is scalar for both reps, with distinct scalar values. Every scalar element in agreement is scalar in both with the same value. Therefore scalar kernels coincide. On K the ratio of scalar characters has order3, so D=K minus its kernel and Haar mass(D)=2 Haar mass(K)/3. It follows [G:K]=9. A cyclic projective image would preserve an eigenline, so quotient C3 x C3. Conversely norm1 forces traces to vanish outside K; in dimension3 trace and determinant determine a unitary characteristic polynomial, giving exact agreement there.
6. The stability inequality in the note with constant18 is correct, and the later improvement to12 is also verified. Put a=|z|+|w|, 0<=a<=6. If a<=9/2, then |z-w|^2<=a^2<=(9/2)a. This controls disagreement elements only, as correctly stated.

Broader representation theorem
Irreducibility is unnecessary for the lower bound. Decompose two inequivalent dimension3 representations into irreducibles. The squared L2 norm M of their character difference is a sum of squares of integer multiplicity differences. Equal total dimension forces both positive and negative differences, so M>=2. Hence delta>=M/27>=2/27 when determinants agree. At equality M=2, the difference consists of replacing one irreducible by another of the same degree. If that degree is less than3, a common nonzero summand remains; distinct scalar matrices on D cannot act differently on a common summand. Thus equality forces irreducibility, and the existing equality classification applies without change.

Exact reducibility gap: delta>=1/8
The stronger argument proposed by the original deriving agent is sound. If some one-dimensional character lambda occurs with different multiplicity, then the integer inner product <chi_rho-chi_sigma,lambda> has absolute value>=1. When determinants agree,

Their trace difference has squared norm2 and pointwise modulus<=4, yielding delta>=1/8. Compact groups
All preceding proofs carry over with Haar integration. A nontrivial determinant-ratio character has image either a finite cyclic group of size at least2 or the full circle; its kernel has mass at most1/2. Character orthogonality and integral multiplicities hold for finite-dimensional continuous representations of compact groups. At equality K is closed with mass1/9 and therefore open of index9. The converse norm argument works unchanged. No subtle measure-zero issue invalidates the proof: equality of nonnegative integrals initially gives scalarity almost everywhere on D, but D is open (unequal continuous characteristic-polynomial coefficients), and the scalar locus is closed. The intersection of D with its complement scalar locus would be a nonempty open set, which has positive Haar measure. Hence the almost-everywhere statement upgrades to every element of D. This upgrade should be written explicitly in the compact proof.

Additional equality structure
Every equality pair is induced from the same index3 subgroup. Choose a line of G/K=C3^2 and let A be its preimage. The image rho(A) is generated by scalars and one matrix, hence is simultaneously diagonalizable. A lift of a nonzero line element has trace0 and cube scalar, so has3 distinct eigenlines. Irreducibility makes G permute these lines transitively, their stabilizer is A, and rho=Ind_A^G theta for a one-dimensional character theta. The same argument applies to sigma using the same A. Thus extremizers are monomial and cannot be primitive.

Explicit arithmetic realization over F=Q(omega)
This avoids needing an abstract solvable inverse-Galois theorem.
Let omega be a primitive cube root of1, set
  x^3=6, y^3=7, alpha=1+x,
  beta=(1+x)^2(1+omega x), z^3=beta,
  E=F(x), K=E(y), L=K(z).
Then [L:F]=27 and Gal(L/F) is the exponent3 Heisenberg group.

Degree proof:

The rational prime7 splits in F, and at either prime above7 the polynomial X^3-6 has3 distinct roots in F7. The discriminant -27*6^2 has no factor7, so that prime splits into3 unramified primes of E. The factors alpha, sigma(alpha), sigma^2(alpha), whose product is7, have respective valuation vectors (1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1) at those primes. Therefore7 is not a cube in E and [K:E]=3. The valuation vector of beta is(2,1,0).

If beta became a cube in K, choose u in K with u^3=beta. The generator tau of K/E satisfies tau(u)/u in mu3. Since K=E+Ey+Ey^2, its eigenspaces are exactly Ey^k, k=0,1,2. Therefore beta=7^k c^3 for some c in E. Its valuations would give (2-k,1-k,-k) divisible by3 simultaneously, impossible. Hence [L:K]=3.

Automorphisms:
  sigma(x)=omega x, sigma(y)=y, sigma(z)=y z/(1+x);
  tau(x)=x, tau(y)=omega y, tau(z)=z;
