INDEPENDENT CHECKS

1. Compact separation and equality classification.
   The SU(3) trace identity gives the stated equilateral triangle; its squared
   diameter is 27, with equality only at distinct scalar vertices. The common
   determinant is normalized pointwise, without requiring a cube-root character.
   Character orthogonality gives an integer energy M >= 2 even for reducible
   representations. At delta = 2/27, M = 2; equal total dimension makes the
   character difference a difference of same-degree irreducibles. Degrees 1 and
   2 are excluded by the energy bound with pointwise squared maximum at most 16.

   The passage from almost-everywhere to pointwise scalar behavior on D is
   justified because D is open and Haar measure has full support. Equal scalar
   kernels follow from unitary diagonalizability and equality of characteristic
   polynomials outside D. The order-three scalar-character ratio yields
   delta = (2/3)m(K), hence index nine. A cyclic projective quotient would give
   an invariant eigenline, so the quotient is C3 x C3. Conversely, scalar kernel
   index nine exhausts the irreducible character norm on K, forcing both traces
   to vanish outside K; the stated equality characterization follows.

2. Reducible gap and its witness.
   Different one-dimensional multiplicities give delta >= 1/(3 sqrt(3)), which
   exceeds 1/8. When those multiplicities all coincide, a reducible cubic pair
   necessarily has a common one-dimensional summand and inequivalent irreducible
   two-dimensional remaining summands. Their squared energy is two and their
   squared pointwise trace difference is at most 16. The D8 x C2 construction
   really has equal determinants and exactly two full-factor mismatches among
   sixteen elements. Thus 1/8 is a sharp universal bound in the stated class.

3. Strengthened stability constant.
   I checked the independent scalar inequality used for the constant 12.
   For z,w in the triangle, put a = |z| + |w| <= 6. If a <= 9/2, the triangle
   inequality gives |z-w|^2 <= (9/2)a. Otherwise the bilinear minimum
   Re(z conjugate(w)) >= -9/2 and
   |z|^2 + |w|^2 <= 9 + (a-3)^2 give the same conclusion, since
   (a-9/2)(a-6) <= 0. This is precisely the displayed pointwise inequality
   with factor 4/9. Integration gives (4/9)(27 delta - M) <= 12 epsilon.
   The assertion is correctly restricted to the disagreement set.

4. Explicit radical extension.
   At the prime above 2, v(6)=1, proving [F(cuberoot(6)):F]=3.
   At either prime above 7, X^3-6 has the three distinct roots 3,5,6 and
   discriminant prime to 7. The valuation vector of alpha=1+x is a coordinate
   vector, because its norm is 7. Consequently the vectors of 7 and beta are
   (1,1,1) and (2,1,0), respectively. The elementary eigenvector/Kummer argument
   proves beta is not a cube after adjoining cuberoot(7), giving total degree 27.
   The identity s(beta)/beta=7/alpha^3 validates sigma(z)=yz/alpha.
   The displayed maps have order three, with tau sigma = kappa sigma tau.
   Their 27 distinct actions exhaust the degree, proving the claimed Galois
   group and the arithmetic sharp example. The argument is complete over
   Q(omega); the separate existence assertion over Q is explicitly attributed
   to the solvable inverse-Galois theorem.

5. Arithmetic density statements.
   A continuous complex representation of a profinite group has finite image
   by the no-small-subgroups property of GL3(C). A common finite Galois quotient
   and Chebotarev therefore give actual natural and Dirichlet densities.
   The upper-agreement-density formulations and the error/erasure transfers
   follow from complements and subadditivity. RH, GRH and Artin holomorphy are
   not needed for these finite-image conclusions.

6. Tempered automorphic theorem.
   The stated standard Rankin--Selberg inputs give a double pole in the quotient
   R at one, and hence log R = 2 log(1/(s-1)) + O(1). The two mixed factors are
   conjugate on the real axis; imaginary norm twists introduce no pole at one
   unless the representations are equivalent. Temperedness outside the finite
   excluded set makes all higher prime-power terms uniformly summable, yielding
   the displayed prime-level energy identity. Equal central characters impose
   equal local determinants, and the 27 bound therefore gives lower Dirichlet
   disagreement density 2/27. Different central characters instead give the
   GL1 energy identity and the valid stronger lower bound 1/2.

   The H27 representations are induced from noninvariant characters of an
   abelian normal subgroup of index three. Cyclic automorphic induction gives
   cuspidal unitary representations with the required finite-order Satake
   eigenvalues, so sharpness lies within the stated tempered class. The proof
   uses neither unproved Artin holomorphy for arbitrary groups nor generalized
   Ramanujan. The remaining general non-tempered problem is explicitly left open.

MINOR CORRECTION

The definition of squared Frobenius distance contains the TeX expression
  \\norm{U-zI}_{F}^{,2}
with a stray comma in the exponent. It should be
  \\norm{U-zI}_{F}^{2}.
This is a notation/typography issue only; the accompanying formula 6-2|tr U|
and all later mathematical arguments use the correct squared norm.
