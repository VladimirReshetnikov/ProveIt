\section{Comparison with all twenty-six incoming archives}
\label{app:prior-reports}

The comparison uses the incoming directory of \texttt{VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt}
at commit \nolinkurl{6fe2c1bd450aa7f19eab17fb9a6e76170b4fce00}.
Its twenty-six ZIP files were retrieved and matched to their Git blob hashes.
We read the principal \TeX{} statements and relevant sections across all
twenty-six archives and searched the extracted \TeX{} and Markdown for the
specific proposed extensions. The accompanying
\nolinkurl{prior_inventory.json} and \nolinkurl{prior_inventory.csv}
record exact archive identities, principal source paths, claimed results,
and remaining obligations. This is a comparison of their mathematical
content, not an independent certification of every prior proof or large
finite computation. In the compact labels below, \texttt{[date]} means
\texttt{2026-11-25}; every listed item is a ZIP archive.

\paragraph{Foundational source audits and the cubic benchmark (three).}
\nolinkurl{grh_transfer_analysis.zip},
\nolinkurl{grh_transfer_analysis_[date].zip}, and
\nolinkurl{grh_lean_transfer_analysis.zip}
map the public Dirichlet-GRH statements to the determinant and one-sided
analytic arguments. They give explicit covolume loss inequalities,
weaken the required auxiliary-prime family, and identify the cubic field
of discriminant $-23$ as a first nonabelian target. Its class-sensitive
prime estimate is left unproved. Their small Lean lemmas or audit drivers
must be distinguished from a new verification of the entire upstream
development.

\paragraph{Arithmetic and analytic refinements of the internal method (five).}
\nolinkurl{grh_method_transfers_[date].zip} proves an optimal fixed-congruence
approximation theorem, a sparse-scale criterion, and a compulsory
ramification cost that prevents rounding small normalized Selmer lengths
to zero. Its variants
\nolinkurl{grh_method_transfers_[date](1).zip} and
\nolinkurl{grh_method_transfers_[date] (2).zip}
respectively develop bounded-family torsion selection, an $A_5$ detector
and genus-field exclusions; and positive-part average criteria, exact
$K$-theory screening tests and small Hecke computations.
\nolinkurl{grh_beyond_dirichlet_grh_[date].zip} adds formal-series and
Fitting-ideal extensions and a comparison between the source's odd
cocycles and a standard locally trivial cohomology group.
\nolinkurl{grh_quantitative_twists_[date].zip} gives polynomial
order-exponent bounds and packets of favorable twists. These reports
retain the source's arithmetic representation hypotheses; their
numerical screens do not supply a missing Selmer--Hecke comparison or
prove new $K$-group vanishing.

\paragraph{Finite-group detectors and compressed prime races (six).}
\nolinkurl{psl2_grh_transfer.zip},
\nolinkurl{grh_reflection_transfer_[date].zip},
\nolinkurl{grh_sylow_transfer_[date].zip},
\nolinkurl{grh_cyclic_generator_transfer_[date].zip},
\nolinkurl{grh_a6_transfer_[date].zip}, and
\nolinkurl{grh_relative_prime_races_[date].zip}
develop character identities, multiplicity inequalities, support or degree
optimality, and equivalent one-sided prime tests. Their examples include
even-field $\mathrm{PSL}_2$, $\mathrm{PSL}_2(7)$, reflection groups,
symmetric and alternating groups, $A_6$, and normal towers.
The cyclic-generator report also sharpens auxiliary logarithmic windows.
These are proved reductions under their stated analytic inputs; none
proves the new nonabelian prime inequality.

\paragraph{Positive deficits, modular targets and Kummer blocks (two).}
\nolinkurl{grh_deficit_transfer_[date].zip} gives nonnegative
Frobenius-deficit criteria, finite differences and averaged variants,
including analytic targets for modular forms and symmetric powers.
\nolinkurl{grh_kummer_window_transfer_[date].zip} develops linear
auxiliary windows and prime-index Kummer block criteria, with an
Artin--Hooley consequence if the required inequalities hold.
Neither archive establishes the additional arithmetic-geometric bound.
In particular, naming symmetric-power RH as a target is already present
in this corpus.

\paragraph{Completed arithmetic results claimed in the predecessors (five).}
\nolinkurl{grh-transfer.zip} claims a classification of 229 abelian
fields satisfying Zhao's sufficient spectral condition, along with
representation and splitting-prime results.
\nolinkurl{grh-lean-transfers.zip} claims a classification of 26 quintic
CM coefficient fields and related odd-prime-degree classifications.
\nolinkurl{grh-arithmetic-transfers.zip} claims an exact prime-plus-prime-cube
threshold, the $t\ge2$ range of a geometric-interval conjecture, and
explicit even-degree isogeny restrictions.
\nolinkurl{grh_transfers.zip} gives partial exponent-six class-group
classifications and prime-race criteria; the later
\nolinkurl{grh-exponent-six-and-transfers.zip} claims to complete the
432-field and 1,236-order exponent-six censuses using a large exact
finite certificate. Thus the older reports' statement that this census
remains unfinished is not the current claim of the collected corpus.
These arithmetic deductions include applications of the exported GRH
theorem and classical inputs, rather than only transfers of its internal
theta-lift machinery.

\paragraph{Sparse equations for singular moduli (four).}
\nolinkurl{grh_cm_prime_applications.zip},
\nolinkurl{grh_cm_four_terms_and_prime_bias.zip},
\nolinkurl{grh_cm_sparse_equations.zip}, and
\nolinkurl{grh_sparse_singular_moduli.zip}
successively address sparse annihilating polynomials, support growth,
finite certificates, prime-generating constants and prime-error biases.
The last archive claims the full five-term classification
$s(\Delta)\le5\iff h(\Delta)\le4$, including arbitrary exponent widths
at the residual discriminants of its predecessors. It also claims
infinite full-support families and quantitative density results.
We preserve these claims as prior work; we do not recertify their entire
computational and arithmetic arguments here.

\paragraph{Heavenly curves and critical-line products (one).}
\nolinkurl{dirichlet-grh-research.zip} contains four papers. They claim
answers to O'Hara's cubic finiteness questions, uniform
Rasmussen--Tamagawa bounds for $A_4$- and $A_5$-sextic fields, paired
moving-height Euler-product limits, and periodic geometric-progression
constructions. The ordinary all-cutoff, fixed-center deep RH is still
left open. Some of these deductions are independent of RH.

\paragraph{What the present report adds to this corpus.}
No predecessor proof was found of the conjugate-duality obstruction
forcing the source's \texttt{congruenceTerm} to be infinite, or of its
preservation by anticyclotomic twisting. Nor did we find the exact integral
spectral-projector denominator analysis or the proposal to retain an
entire Galois orbit to avoid isolating one eigenform. The rank-two
scalar-inertia obstruction \emph{is} already proved in the
\texttt{(1)} method-transfer archive, including Tate twists and duals;
the symmetric-power treatment here is an extension of that observation.
These distinctions concern the actual admissibility and cost of an
arithmetic transfer. They do not certify worldwide priority, and they
do not replace the missing arithmetic estimate by another zero criterion.
