# Source audit of the vendored GRH development

## Pin and scope

The inspected repository is VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt at commit
`3d8689ceae76501a5c50f67da37e85369aac4fb6`. Its directory
`Analysis/GeneralizedRiemannHypothesis/vendor/grh-lean/grh` is the readable
edition of lsernau/grh-lean, pinned to upstream commit
`72fead102f663d5c7a407964a13dc0cf9d977207`. The vendor README says that all
818 imported files are unmodified. The fetched recursive tree contains
those files. This audit fetched the endpoint definitions and the principal
analytic, determinant, and second-moment sources at the pinned ProveIt
commit. Fifteen reviewed files were downloaded for the audit. The accompanying
`reviewed_lean_hashes.json` records their Git blob SHA-1 values, all independently
recomputed from the saved bytes and matched to the API results. The upstream
source itself is not redistributed in this research archive.

This session performed source analysis and byte verification of that
subset. It did not compile or independently replay the complete upstream
development. The vendor package is deliberately separate from ProveIt's
root build. The former pins Lean 4.35.0 and Mathlib
`56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679`; the latter uses another
toolchain. A successful build at ProveIt's root would not check this
vendored package.

## Exact endpoint statements

All source paths below are relative to the vendored `grh/` directory.

`src/Statements.lean` imports only Mathlib at line 1. Lines 3--16 give the
four named propositions, in Mathlib's own complex numbers, Dirichlet
characters and L-functions. In particular:

1. `DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf` quantifies over all nonzero natural
   moduli and all Dirichlet characters. It asserts nonvanishing for real
   part greater than one half, with the principal character's pole at 1
   excluded.
2. `DirichletLZerosOnLine` puts every zero with real part strictly between
   zero and one on the critical line, including imprimitive characters.
3. `PrimitiveDirichletLZerosOnLine` is the corresponding primitive case.
4. `RiemannZetaZerosOnLine` excludes the negative even integers and the
   point 1, and puts the remaining zeta zeros on the critical line.

`src/Main.lean`, lines 5--22, exports the five theorems. The first has the
literal Mathlib type `RiemannHypothesis`; the other four have the
propositions just described. Its half-plane endpoint is `New.grh`.
`scripts/PrintAxioms.lean` asks Lean for the dependencies of all five
endpoints. There is no source-level weakening of the target statement in
these endpoint files.

This is Dirichlet GRH. These endpoint types do not assert RH for arbitrary
Dedekind zeta functions, nonabelian Artin L-functions or higher-degree
automorphic L-functions, nor do they assert simple zeros or independence
of zero ordinates.

## The proof architecture actually available for transfer

### 1. One covolume, two bounds, and cancellation

`src/New/Reduction/MainInequality.lean`, lines 5--79, documents the
comparison. Theorem `New.star_inequality`, starting at line 87, proves

    D.m * D.totalRemainder <= D.f * D.a * D.logB ^ 5.

The upper bound comes from independent images of short integral forms,
Hadamard's determinant bound, and a bound on their lattice index. The
lower bound is for the same family and integral structure. The common main
amount cancels. The proof needs enough independent images, so the rank
deficit is quantitatively relevant. It does not extract an estimate for
each individual form from a bound on their determinant.

The actual ingredients are more specialized than a generic positivity
argument: theta-lift chains, arithmetic heights of ratios of determinants,
and twists whose local denominators fit a common budget.

### 2. Separate twists at separate primes

`src/New/Reduction/Lattices/TwistedLattice.lean`, lines 466--518, states and
proves `covolume_add_index_nonneg`. A family of short independent vectors is
completed to a basis. An allowed twist is chosen for each prime-adic
embedding; it need not be the same twist at every prime. The conclusion is

    0 <= c + 2*delta + n'*(b_short + b_denom) + b_volume.

Here `n'` is the number of completing vectors. The completion cost cannot
be dropped merely because the original family occupies a positive
fraction of the target. `Heights.lean`, line 191, is the underlying
product-formula nonnegativity statement for the constant family one.

### 3. Second moments turn into rank

`src/New/Spanning/SecondMoment/FewTranslates.lean`, lines 149--152, defines
`IsNearIsometry A epsilon` by an inequality on **every** source subspace,
not only on the total Hilbert--Schmidt norm. Lines 314--389 prove
`isSpread_of_second_moment`: if the projected source is nearly isometric
in that sense and the averaged squared Hilbert--Schmidt correlation is
small, one translate has small preimage of any prescribed target
subspace. Lines 475--519 implement a greedy selection with a logarithmic
number of translates. This is an especially reusable finite-dimensional
part of the source.

The article already sharpens the universal rank fraction and gives a
better greedy schedule (`spn.tex`, labels `spn:lem:sqrt`,
`spn:thm:greedy`, `spn:prop:sharp`). Its explicit open question
`spn:q:selection` asks for the exact minimum total orbit rank at fixed
source dimension as well. That finite-parameter question is resolved in
the companion work; it should not be confused with the article's
universal-fraction sharpness, which varies the dimensions.

### 4. Positive-type weights and finite-field matrix statistics

`src/New/Spanning/SecondMoment/PositiveType.lean`, lines 106--151, bounds
an additive-character matrix coefficient using the positive-type Gram
representation and the masses of two stabilizers. Lines 162--222 turn
that bound into a mass estimate for singular compressions. The proof is
Fourier orthogonality followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, not a conclusion from
GRH alone.

The article already substantially extends this mechanism: general
rectangular compression laws, higher factorial kernel moments, complete
symplectic compression laws, and fixed-frame asymptotic corrections all
appear in `spn.tex`. Reproving just the first moment or a rank-tail bound
would repeat existing material. The characteristic-two alternating-form
condition must retain a zero diagonal; skew-symmetry alone is insufficient.

### 5. The one-sided analytic conclusion

`src/New/Reduction/OneSidedBound.lean`, lines 200--235, proves an eventual
bound of the form `primeRemainder d q0 y <= y^(-1/2 + epsilon)` for fixed
admissible parameters. `New.grh`, lines 239--271, applies the zero-free
criterion and removes an induced level.

`src/New/Reduction/Zeros/Criterion.lean`, lines 37--91, uses the family of
quadratic twists to choose one with no competing zero in a prescribed
rectangle. The residue of the remainder's transform then has a
nonnegative zero-multiplicity numerator. `Lib/Analytic/Landau.lean`
provides the one-sided Laplace-transform argument, as described in its
lines 4--30. The selection of a suitable twist prevents cancellation
between twisted and untwisted zeros; it is not dispensable in that
argument.

## Consequences for choosing an extension

The source supports two different kinds of follow-up: use Dirichlet GRH
as an established input, or extract a separate finite-dimensional,
combinatorial, or local lemma. Those should be labeled distinctly.
The finite polynomial network theorem in `network_optima.tex` is of the
second kind, via the article's graph-coupled zero construction; its proof
requires no RH assumption. Its source-specific contribution is an exact
answer to the article's optimal-coupling question, supplemented by the
distinct mean optimizer, explicit edges and stability estimates. General
conductance optimization and fractional Laplacians are prior work, not
new terminology or newly discovered tools.

None of the reviewed transfer arguments establishes an arbitrary
nonabelian RH, simplicity of Dirichlet zeros, pair correlation, twin
primes, or a fixed-support graph-design formula. Such claims would need
additional input beyond the inspected statements.
