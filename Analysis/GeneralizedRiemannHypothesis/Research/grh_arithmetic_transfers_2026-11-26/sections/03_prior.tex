\section{The prior reports: completed work and remaining gaps}
\label{sec:prior-landscape}

The pinned ProveIt snapshot contains 46 GRH-related ZIP packages in
\path{docs/incoming}. Their exact archive identities, descriptive titles,
main manuscript members and review scope are recorded in the accompanying
CSV and JSON ledger. All 46 downloaded archives match their Git blob hashes.
This establishes the identity of the material compared; it does not verify
its mathematics. We inspected the overviews and selected relevant arguments,
with a deeper review of the integer density-gap proof and the earlier
conjugate-duality analysis. We did not independently audit every proof or
rerun every computation in these packages.\cite{priorinventory}

The corpus has moved substantially beyond an initial list of consequences
of Dirichlet GRH. It contains both complete downstream paper arguments and
many conditional extensions of the analytic method. In the table,
\emph{reviewed} means that the stated argument was examined in this
investigation; \emph{reported} means that a package supplies a proof and
supporting artifacts whose full validation is outside this review;
\emph{open} identifies the missing conclusion or arithmetic estimate.
None of these flags transfers the upstream Lean verification to a new
paper theorem.

\subsection{A completed inherited result that survives a fresh check}

The package
\path{grh_transfers_density_gaps_and_zero_tests.zip}
contains a proof answering the strict comparisons posed in McNew's
Questions~7.1 and~7.3. Let $\alpha$ and $\overline\alpha$ be the supremal
natural and upper densities of sets of positive integers avoiding
three-term geometric progressions with rational ratio greater than one.
Let $\beta$ and $\overline\beta$ denote the corresponding extrema when
only integer ratios are forbidden. Its claimed inequalities are
\[
 \overline\alpha-\alpha>0.002836,
 \qquad
 \overline\beta-\beta>0.069218.
\]
These are inherited results, not new discoveries of the present report.
Their proof uses no hypothesis about zeros of an $L$-function.
\cite{priordensity,mcnew2015}

We read the two integer proof sections and their exact checkers, and reran
both checkers successfully. A separate implementation also verified the
simpler core certificate: all admissible subsets through 12 have a loss
of at least $1/156$ against the integrated sequence of optimal prefix
counts. Together with smooth--rough factorization and an exact prime-tail
bound, this gives both gaps greater than $0.000404$.
The stronger displayed constants use the larger supplied computations;
the integer bound additionally uses McNew's published lower bound
$\overline\beta>0.818410$. The appendix gives the complete elementary
proof of the common gap and distinguishes the independent implementation
from the two larger replays.\cite{priordensity,mcnew2015}

The conceptual connection to the source proof is the use of additive
restrictions on multiplicative exponents, developed in the earlier
transfer program from its progression-free construction. The gap argument
also uses classical factorization and reciprocal weighting. Calling this
a consequence of the statement of RH would misdescribe its dependencies.
Conversely, using an idea appearing inside the GRH development does not
make that classical idea itself new. The new content of the inherited
argument lies in comparing compatible infinite choices with separately
optimal finite cutoffs.\cite{priordensity}

\subsection{Other completed claims should retain their exact status}

The singular-modulus reports progress from residual finite cases to the
reported classification $s\le5$ if and only if the class number is at most
four, where $s$ is the fewest nonzero coefficients of a nonzero rational polynomial
vanishing at the modulus. Later reports construct maximal-support families
and substantially improve the density certified for prime conductors in
$\mathbb Q(i)$. These are stronger than the original trinomial application,
but a global six-term classification and a density-one theorem are not
claimed. The main reference is
\path{grh_sparse_singular_moduli.zip}.\cite{priorsparse,priorinventory}

The local specialization literature within the corpus is similarly mature.
Sharp shell bounds, positive-tolerance regimes, formal-series extensions,
fixed coefficient fields and formal-group variants already occur, including
in \path{grh_sharp_torsion_and_symplectic_blocks.zip}.
A generic torsion-selection lemma would therefore repeat prior work.
The graph package
\path{grh_method_transfers_graph_towers_and_finite_rings.zip}
reports complete formulas for specified graph towers and a reduced-ring
density criterion. Its deductions use cyclotomic valuations and additive
combinatorics, independently of the truth of RH.
These additional claims were compared at the stated overview level,
not independently certified in this investigation.\cite{priortorsion,priorgraphs}

The class-group censuses require a different qualification. Their reported
global completions combine new exclusion arguments and finite calculations
with published enumerations and quadratic Dirichlet GRH. They are neither
automatic corollaries of a zero-location statement nor wholly independent
of that statement. The ledger preserves those dependencies and prevents
an earlier partial census from being mistaken for the latest claimed
frontier.\cite{priorinventory}

\subsection{What the present congruence-depth analysis adds}

The closest predecessor is
\path{grh_structural_transfers_2026-11-25.zip}.
It already gives a finite matrix of intertwining equations and the Smith
length formula
\[
 \operatorname{length}H_n=hn+\sum_j\min(n,a_j),
\]
where $h$ is the dimension of the rational intertwiner space and $a_j$
are the nonzero Smith valuations. It also proves anticyclotomic invariance,
the symmetric-image refinement of the mirror argument, and the obstruction
created by a surviving rational conjugate Tate-dual pairing.
Under integral matrix spanning and a nonzero rational intertwiner, it
identifies every finite-level Hom with a scalar line.\cite{priorstruct}

The present refinement concerns the complementary situation in which
rational Hom vanishes but congruences can survive at finite depth.
Residual absolute irreducibility reduces the finite-level Hom to one
cyclic module, turning the general Smith sum into a single depth.
The trace formulation and finite trace certificates make that depth
arithmetically accessible. This is the specific addition; the general
Smith formula, the rational self-duality obstruction and the sign-sensitive
mirror observation are credited to the earlier report. No global
bibliographic priority is inferred from this scoped comparison.
\cite{priorstruct,priorinventory}

Finally, the extensive nonabelian zero detectors do not supply the required
new arithmetic bound. Even the more concrete constructions in
\path{grh_transfer_bridges_2026-11-25.zip} separate local-to-global twist
compatibility, finite certificates and rank costs from an asymptotic
covolume estimate for the desired Frobenius weights. The same distinction
applies to symmetric-power RH and to Selmer or higher $K$-group vanishing.
The present exact congruence analysis sharpens one necessary input; it
does not silently discharge the remaining hypotheses of a global
twisting or zero-exclusion theorem.\cite{priorbridges,priorstruct}
