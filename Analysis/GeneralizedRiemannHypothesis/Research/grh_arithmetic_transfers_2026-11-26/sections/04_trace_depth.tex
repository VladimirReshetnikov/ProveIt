\section{Exact trace depth and a smaller Selmer congruence budget}
\label{sec:trace-depth}

The source contains an internal quantity which is useful independently of
the RH endpoint.  If $T$ is a lattice over a discrete valuation ring
$\mathcal O$, it measures congruences between $T$ and its conjugate Tate
dual $D(T)$:
\[
 C(T)=\sup_{n\geq1}\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}
 \operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathcal O/\pi^n)[G]}
       (T/\pi^nT,D(T)/\pi^nD(T)).
\]
Here $\pi$ is a uniformizer.  This is the source's
\texttt{New.Selmer.congruenceTerm}; it is a term in a Selmer bound,
not the Selmer group itself.  The present section identifies this term
exactly under the integral irreducibility hypothesis already imposed
by \texttt{SelmerDatum}.\cite{selmerdata}

\begin{theorem}[Exact trace-congruence depth]
\label{thm:trace-depth}
Let $\rho,\sigma:G\to\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathcal O)$, $d\geq1$, be
representations such that
\[
 \operatorname{span}_{\mathcal O}\rho(G)
  =M_d(\mathcal O)
  =\operatorname{span}_{\mathcal O}\sigma(G).
\]
Set
\[
 a=\min_{g\in G}v_\pi\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\rho(g)
                       -\operatorname{tr}\sigma(g)\bigr)
       \in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}.
\]
For every $n\geq1$, the module
\[
 H_n=\operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathcal O/\pi^n)[G]}
           (\rho\bmod\pi^n,\sigma\bmod\pi^n)
\]
is cyclic and isomorphic to
$\mathcal O/\pi^{\min(n,a)}$.  In particular,
\[
 \operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}H_n=\min(n,a),
 \qquad \sup_n\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}H_n=a.
\]
No restriction on the residue characteristic relative to $d$ is needed. The trace-rigidity ingredient is classical.\cite{carayol}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first recall the trace-rigidity argument, a classical theorem of
Carayol.  Put $A=\mathcal O/\pi^r$, and suppose the two traces agree
modulo $\pi^r$ at every group element.  Sending $\rho(g)$ to $\sigma(g)$
defines an $A$-algebra automorphism of $M_d(A)$.  Indeed, if
$\sum_i b_i\rho(g_i)=0$, then for every $h\in G$,
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\left(\left(\sum_i b_i\sigma(g_i)\right)\sigma(h)\right)
 =\sum_i b_i\operatorname{tr}\sigma(g_i h)
 =\sum_i b_i\operatorname{tr}\rho(g_i h)=0.
\]
The $\sigma(h)$ span $M_d(A)$, and the trace pairing on that algebra
is perfect: each matrix unit pairs with its transpose.  Thus
$\sum_i b_i\sigma(g_i)=0$.  The same argument in reverse supplies the
inverse, and the group law gives multiplicativity.

An automorphism of $M_d(A)$ is inner because $A$ is local.  One elementary
proof takes the images $F_{ij}$ of the matrix units, chooses a generator
$v_1$ of the rank-one direct summand $F_{11}A^d$, and uses
$v_i=F_{i1}v_1$ as a basis.  In this basis the $F_{ij}$ are the usual
matrix units.  Consequently $\rho$ and $\sigma$ are equivalent modulo
$\pi^r$.  The converse follows from invariance of trace.

Next, the $\pi$-torsion submodule of $H_n$ is naturally
\[
 H_n[\pi]\simeq
 \operatorname{Hom}_{k[G]}(\bar\rho,\bar\sigma),
 \qquad k=\mathcal O/\pi,
\]
by multiplying an entrywise lift by $\pi^{n-1}$.  The matrix-span
hypotheses imply absolute irreducibility of the residual representations.
Schur's lemma bounds the displayed $k$-dimension by one.  The structure
theorem over a DVR therefore makes $H_n$ cyclic or zero; write its
length as $r\leq n$.

If $r>0$, choose a generator $X$ of exact order $\pi^r$ and write
$X=\pi^{n-r}Y$, with $Y$ a matrix modulo $\pi^r$ having a unit entry.
The intertwining equation descends to $Y$.  Its residual reduction is
a nonzero map between irreducible representations of equal dimension,
hence invertible; therefore $Y$ is invertible modulo $\pi^r$.
The representations are equivalent there, which gives $r\leq a$.

Conversely, for $s=\min(n,a)>0$, trace rigidity supplies an invertible
intertwiner modulo $\pi^s$.  Multiplying any lift by $\pi^{n-s}$
gives an element of $H_n$ of exact order $\pi^s$.  Thus $r\geq s$,
proving the formula; the case $s=0$ follows from the first inequality.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Classical status and comparison with the earlier report.}
The trace-rigidity input is Carayol's classical theorem, and the length
formula is a standard consequence of Schur's lemma and DVR module
structure.  We do not claim these as new representation theory.
The earlier structural-transfer report established an arbitrary Smith
formula and treated the exact rationally self-dual case.  The result
above treats the finite non-self-dual congruence case and identifies
its length with a trace ideal.  Its purpose here is to sharpen and
make computationally accessible a particular internal source estimate.

\subsection{Removing the rank factor in the source}

For the conjugate Tate dual,
\[
 \sigma(g)=\chi(g^c)\rho(g^c)^{-\mathsf t},
\]
where $\chi$ is the cyclotomic character.  The full matrix-span
condition for $\rho$ automatically gives the same condition for
$\sigma$: conjugation and inversion permute the group elements,
transpose preserves matrix span, and the extra factors are units.
Identifying the finite character module of $\mathcal O/\pi^n$ with
itself by a generating additive character does not affect length.
Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-depth} therefore applies to the source quantity.

\begin{corollary}[Sharp scalar-discrepancy bound]
\label{cor:sharp-scalar}
Suppose $\rho(g)=\alpha I$ and
$\rho((g^c)^{-1})=\alpha'I$.  Then
\[
 C(T)\leq v_\pi\bigl(\alpha-\chi(g^c)\alpha'\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The intertwining equation says that
$\Delta=\alpha-\chi(g^c)\alpha'$ annihilates $H_n$.
Since $H_n$ is cyclic, its length is at most $v_\pi(\Delta)$.
Take the supremum over $n$.  If $\Delta=0$, the bound is vacuous.
\end{proof}

The source's \texttt{congruenceTerm\_le} gives instead
$d\,v_\pi(\Delta)$.  Its proof embeds the Hom module into a rank-$d$
character module; cyclicity removes this unnecessary factor.
In \texttt{MainSummands.lean}, the proof of
\texttt{IsMainSummandLike.normLength\_congruenceTerm\_le}
already establishes the necessary scalar discrepancy.  With the same
hypotheses its conclusion improves to
\[
 \texttt{O.normLength(congruenceTerm }\ell\texttt{ u)}
 \ \leq\ v_\ell(\texttt{D.chosenGroupOrder}),
\]
instead of $d$ times the right side.  This changes only the global
congruence term.  The local terms in the full Selmer budget still have
their original estimates; no factor-$d$ improvement of the entire
RH proof is asserted.\cite{selmerdata,mainsummands}

\subsection{Finite-depth symmetry, including the dyadic caveat}

The source's mirror map has a symmetric image, as established in the
earlier structural report.  The finite-depth theorem gives an exact
version of that refinement.  Assume $0<a<\infty$ for the conjugate-dual
pair, and choose an invertible intertwiner $P$ modulo $\pi^a$.
Transposition preserves the intertwining equation, because $c^2=1$
and $\chi(g^c)=\chi(g)$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-depth} makes the
module at level $a$ a free line, so
\[
 P^{\mathsf t}=\epsilon P,\qquad
 \epsilon\in(\mathcal O/\pi^a)^\times,\qquad \epsilon^2=1.
\]
Put $s=\min(a,v_\pi(\epsilon-1))$, interpreting the valuation through
any lift and truncating at $a$.  Then the symmetric finite-level Hom
module has exact length
\[
 \operatorname{length}H_n^+=\min(n,a,s)=\min(n,s),
 \qquad C^+(T)=s.
\]
Indeed, for $r=\min(n,a)$ every intertwiner is a scalar multiple of
$\pi^{n-r}P\bmod\pi^r$; symmetry imposes exactly
$(\epsilon-1)b=0\pmod{\pi^r}$.
When the residue characteristic is odd, $\epsilon=1$ or $-1$, and
$C^+(T)$ equals $a$ or $0$ respectively.

At residue characteristic two one must retain the measured value of
$s$.  A scalar whose square is one modulo $8$ need not be $\pm1$:
$3$ and $5$ also occur.  The invertible matrix
\[
 P_\epsilon=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\\epsilon&0\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{over }\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z
\]
satisfies $P_\epsilon^{\mathsf t}=\epsilon P_\epsilon$ for each of
$\epsilon=1,3,5,7$.  This is a finite algebra illustration, not a
Galois realization.  The original rational self-dual sign dichotomy
remains as in the prior report; the present statement concerns
congruences that disappear beyond a finite depth.

\subsection{Exact model and verification}

Let $G$ be free on $x,y,z$, and over $\mathbb Z_p$ put
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad q=1+p^b,\quad b\geq1.
\]
Set $\rho(x)=\sigma(x)=A$, $\rho(y)=\sigma(y)=B$,
$\rho(z)=I$, and $\sigma(z)=qI$.
The matrices $I,A,B,AB$ form an integral basis of $M_2(\mathbb Z_p)$.
Commuting with $A,B$ forces an intertwiner to be scalar, and its last
relation is $p^bt=0$.  Hence $\operatorname{length}H_n=\min(n,b)$.
The sharper bound is $b$, where the source estimate is $2b$.

For $p=2,b=3$, the discrepancy at $z$ is $-16$, of valuation $4$;
the discrepancy at $zxy$ is $-24$, of valuation $3$.  Thus tracing
only the scalar element can lose information when $p\mid d$, while
the complete trace criterion remains exact.  The relation matrix has
Smith factors $1,1,1,p^b$.
The accompanying Python certificate checks these Smith factors,
the unit basis and trace-Gram determinants, exhaustive small-ring
kernels, and the dyadic transpose examples.  It is a finite algebra
check; no Lean build or arithmetic realization is claimed.
