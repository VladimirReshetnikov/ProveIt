## Already in the incoming five-node paper

Its Theorem 1, **All shapes**, proves:
1. The same four-term symbol with lags 1,2,3,4.
2. The same quadratic equation in Re(2^(i xi)).
3. The same exact phase membership in Z+(log3/log2)Z.
4. At most two positive blind frequencies when the lag-three coefficient is nonzero.
5. The zero-or-infinite dichotomy when it vanishes.

Its Theorem 2, **Attainment of the two-frequency bound**, proves:
1. Genuine five-nonzero-weight attainment of two positive missed frequencies.
2. Countable density of two-missed-mode shapes in an explicit local family near Q(z)=1+8z+14z^2.
3. Density of one-missed-mode shapes there and local almost-everywhere zero-blindness.

The discussion following Theorem 2 constructs positive densities with two adjustable invisible cosine amplitudes. It does not classify every possible tangent or give the two-mode count averaging repair.

Therefore the complete finite classification and the existence/sharpness of two missed modes must be credited as prior repository results. Our specific small-frequency exact root/certificate is a new explicit independent verification example, not a new existence theorem. Our global feasible domain and global meagre/null theorem go beyond its local parameter discussion but should remain secondary.

The incoming Theorem 1 does NOT identify the unique normalized squared-response shape in its C=0/infinite case. Its final paragraph recalls the known example but does not prove uniqueness. Our exact (s,t)=(2/7,1/14) uniqueness is a new refinement.

## Already in the merged source

`zer:thm:stencilbank` gives the general common-real-zero criterion and sufficient relative mesh condition. `zer:rem:stencilexist` gives proportionality of all finite variance limits.

The paragraph immediately following `zer:thm:fourpoint` proves that any two distinct FOUR-node response shapes jointly recover the second-difference response. This is a one-dimensional shape family. Our theorem for any two distinct FIVE-node response shapes is a substantive extension to a two-dimensional shape domain; it should be described exactly that way.

`zer:thm:missingfreq` already proves the complete one-frequency positive tangent form [d+Re(C x^(i omega))]dx and a(T)+a(exp(pi/omega)T)->2d. Our finite tangent statement at k=1 and its two-dilation averaging formula are prior. The new finite extension is k=2, including the full nonnegative two-frequency tangent profiles and at most four dilation averaging.

The merged five-node example gives the specific infinite-blind kernel and one cosine counterexample. It does NOT give all positive tangent profiles for that kernel, their anti-periodicity classification, or the universal count repair a(T)+a(2T)->2d.

## Results not found in these source/incoming texts

1. **Universal pair rigidity for five nodes**: every two distinct normalized response shapes have disjoint real symbol zero sets, including two individually blind shapes.
2. **Exact three-line uniform pair-gap classification**: failure of uniform joint gap occurs precisely when both shapes share one of L1=4-52s+216t=0, L2=-12+66s-96t=0, L3=-12+12s+120t=0.
3. **Unique infinitely blind normalized response**, s=2/7,t=1/14.
4. **Full positive tangent classification in the infinite case**: [d+f(log x)]dx with f(y+log2)=-f(y) and |f|<=d, and converse realization of every such profile.
5. **Two-mode finite tangent classification and averaging repair**: complete profile rather than a family of constructed examples, and at most four multiplicative count samples recover d.
6. **Universal dyadic count repair for the infinite case**: [a(T)+a(2T)]/2->d.
7. **Global normalized shape domain/global meagre-null theorem** and exact elementary single-symbol-gap formula.

The first, second, fourth, and sixth are the cleanest new main theorems. The third/fifth support the full structural picture. The numerical certificate is a useful artifact and independent example, with prior sharpness explicitly acknowledged.

## Other incoming papers

`coupled_stencil_inversion.tex` proves sharp necessity of the relative mesh condition for elliptic kernels and counterexamples for multiple filters on finitely many geometric grids. Its included `coupled_variance_rate.tex` gives a quantitative inverse rate for three-point contrasts. Neither contains the five-point pair rigidity, three exceptional lines, complete tangent classification, or new averaging repair. Our mesh sufficiency is established prior background, not a novelty claim.

`cayley_cluster_filters.tex` studies minimum support diameter of positive measures cancelling specified Fourier frequencies, the equality/reflection criterion, and sixth-order asymmetric cluster-splitting costs. It has no overlap with the above five-point pair/tangent/averaging contributions.

## Proof status and cautions

I independently audited the current full manuscript. The analytic proofs and rational certificate are sound. The source comparison changes attribution and the leading novelty claim, not mathematical correctness.

The joint gap is for the finite Mellin symbols, not a finite-height noisy inversion rate. The phase membership condition is not claimed to be a terminating numerical decision procedure. Abstract countermodels/tangent realizations are not claimed to be actual Euler-product zero spectra. These qualifications should remain.

## Classical scope and publication priority

Mellin nonvanishing as a cancellation/Tauberian criterion, finite-family systems of kernels, log-periodic obstructions, and spectral ambiguities of variances are established prior literature. Relevant references include Wiener (1932), Bingham--Inoue on systems of kernels, Jacobsen--Mikosch--Rosinski--Samorodnitsky (2009), Greenhall on spectral ambiguity of Allan variance (1998), and Kevei on regularly log-periodic functions (2020). They are context and machinery, not new claims here. The inspected sources do not establish an earlier statement of the exact five-node universal-pair and three-line theorems or the complete anti-periodic tangent classification, but this limited comparison does not establish publication priority. Describe the contribution as new relative to the inspected merged article and relevant unmerged incoming texts, with publication novelty still requiring broader expert review.
