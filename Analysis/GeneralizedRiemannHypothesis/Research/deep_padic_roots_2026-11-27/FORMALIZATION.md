# Mathematical dependencies and verification boundary

The archive contains rigorous written arguments and exact finite computations. It does not contain a Lean formalization and does not claim kernel verification. This document separates the mathematical proof from the executable evidence.

## 1. Algebraic transport

Inputs: prime p; t >= 2; two distinct t-element supports E,F of nonnegative integers; a common coefficient field finite over Q_p; nonzero input polynomial.

Obligations:

1. Prove the Vandermonde evaluation maps from coefficients to the first t moments are isomorphisms.
2. Prove the moment sequence satisfies the monic recurrence with characteristic polynomial product(T-n).
3. Prove that congruent characteristic polynomials and identical initial moments give congruent later moments, after normalizing the minimum initial valuation to zero.
4. Prove Legendre's factorial valuation formula and the strict digit-weight inequality displayed in the main paper.

These steps are finite algebra and valuation estimates. They prove coefficientwise agreement below the possible Newton minimum and equality of normalized initial forms. The zero polynomial must remain excluded.

## 2. Analytic bridge

Inputs: a rational radius r > 1/(p-1), and b of valuation r in a finite extension.

Obligations:

1. The exponential and logarithm are inverse analytic isometries between the indicated disks, with nonzero derivative.
2. The transformed moment series is restricted: coefficient valuations tend to infinity.
3. For a restricted nonzero series, the last minimizing coefficient index counts roots in the closed unit disk, with multiplicity.
4. Normalized reduction records leading residues of the roots, with multiplicity.
5. Equality of counts at every rational depth implies equality of finite geometric root-depth multisets. The root at one has multiplicity equal to the first nonzero moment index.

These are standard nonarchimedean analytic facts used explicitly in the written proof. The deterministic examples do not replace these obligations.

## 3. Finite lattice rules

Construct the full lattice generated by scaled Taylor columns over Q_p(pi), pi^e=p. The closed rule maximizes a last minimizing index; the open rule maximizes a first minimizing index.

For arbitrary coefficient extensions, reduce an integral functional modulo the maximal ideal of that extension, not modulo pi times its integers. This distinction is necessary under further ramification.

Prove dual-lattice attainment over Q_p(pi). For descent, expand coefficient vectors in the basis 1,pi,...,pi^(e-1): at each Taylor coefficient the components have different valuations modulo Z, so there is no cancellation between components. Select a component realizing the decisive minimum. Rational approximation and CRT perturbations then give integer, full-support witnesses with simple roots in the disk. Remove any forced monomial factor before claiming global separability or irreducibility.

The determinant-envelope formulation is a finite alternative: minimal determinant valuation identifies exactly the generating-column bases of the lattice.

## 4. Eight-term classification

The general transport theorem reduces every support to one of 165 residue profiles modulo four. The upper bound and complete support classification are computer-assisted finite theorems.

Two algorithms independently calculate the exact open-disk lattice invariant:

- integer Hermite normal form, with p-adic localization of the resulting lattice;
- globally minimal valuation pivots over Z_(2), tracking invertible row operations and reducing genuine lattice coordinates modulo two.

The comparison checks the complete list of prefix-rank increases for each profile, not only the largest index. A future kernel proof could certify each matrix reduction or reimplement the finite classification with verified arithmetic.

The explicit lower bound is short enough to inspect directly: binomial translation, lower Newton polygon with vertices (0,11),(9,1),(10,0), and a Bezout identity modulo seven. These do not depend on numerical approximations.

## 5. Positive-filter companion

Dependencies on the inspected article: reflection-paired positive certificate, the analytic optimizer setup, and the horizontal-strip identity. The companion spells out the invertible-Jacobian certificate continuation and the domination needed to differentiate the strip integral.

New deductions: the explicit node-velocity polynomial; the weighted-variance Hessian; its exact mirror tangent kernel; uniform transverse quadratic bounds; and the three-frequency curvature formula.

The symbolic checker verifies rational identities and series constants. Nine high-precision optimizer computations provide numerical consistency checks. They are not interval certificates, and are not used to prove local or global optimality.
