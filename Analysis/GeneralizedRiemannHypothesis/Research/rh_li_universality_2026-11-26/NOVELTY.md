# Novelty assessment — 26 November 2026

## Proposed original statements

**A. All-order normalized local law for the transformed-zero process.**
The report proves an explicit expansion at every inverse-logarithmic order,
uniform over the real line with every fixed spatial derivative. It gives
the exact Lambert-W scale, the full coefficient formula, an error smaller
than all inverse-logarithmic orders when comparing equal counting main
terms, and the associated circle trace formula.

**B. Global bounded positive-weight example with an invisible Mellin pole.**
One explicit sequence has all prescribed power-grid samples equal to zero,
uniformly bounded strictly positive coefficients, and a Mellin transform
whose pole location and residue are calculated exactly. For fifth powers
the pole is at 3/5 with residue 1/4; for pth powers it is at 1-2/p.

No matching statement of A or B was located in the sources examined.
This is a limited literature assessment. It does not certify worldwide
priority, nor claim that a previously named open conjecture was resolved.

## Established ingredients credited

- Li (1997), Bombieri–Lagarias (1999): Li criterion and generalized zero sums.
- Lagarias (2007): Li asymptotics and entire interpolation with transformed
  zero-angle frequency support. This is particularly close prior work.
- Lévy–Khintchine/Schoenberg theory: a nonnegative cosine sum gives a
  symmetric convolution semigroup. Semigroup existence alone is not new.
- Nakamura–Suzuki (2023): RH and infinitely divisible probability laws.
  Their construction uses zero ordinates with inverse-square weights.
- Figueroa-López (2008) and related Lévy literature: small-time moment
  asymptotics. The report's transition is an explicit specialization,
  including its calculated critical coefficient.
- Bennett–Martin–O'Bryant–Rechnitzer (2021): Dirichlet zero counting.
- Setiawan (2026), version 2: fourth-power sampling sufficiency and
  single-interval sharpness of convex interpolation. These are not new here.
- Specht's ratio: the exact logarithmic convex-chord maximum is classical;
  the report does not claim it as a new inequality.
