3. Precise new contribution: symplectic compressions

Inherited source results, explicitly attributed:
- spn:thm:balanced: exact finite residual likelihood and optimal singularity test.
- spn:thm:square-block: square marginal correction and full-distance expansion.
- spn:thm:defect-universality: square defect-nullity correction.
- spn:thm:nondeg-blind: the increasingly tall limit, with a weaker rank bound.

New results in this packet:
- Uniform signed rank and defect-nullity laws for every even a >= b, with
  errors O_q(q^(-a-b)) independent of the gap d=a-b.
- The sharp rank-testing distance gamma_infinity/((q-1)gamma_d) q^(-a),
  including fixed positive gaps that the source explicitly left open.
- The full-matrix distance tends to theta_infinity(1-eta_infinity) for every
  growth regime in which both dimensions tend to infinity.
- A sharper finite-b baseline correction, useful when a is much larger than b.
- A first-order tensor formula for dependence between ordinary nullity and
  the quadratic defect's nullity, including its sharp total variation rate.

The relevant explicit source question is spn:q:symplectic. Other parts of that
question (arbitrary growing radicals, general local-ring multi-pair densities,
other classical groups) are not claimed to be resolved.

4. Precise new contribution: dyadic filters

Inherited source results:
- ana:thm:dyadiceven: the sharp constant and minimizers for all 2m filters.
- ana:prop:dyadicstab: stability for the full set of observations.
- ana:thm:dyadic: the multiplier/residue interpretation.

New result: retain only indices {0,1,3,5,...,2m-1}. These m+1 filters have the
same sharp constant and the same two minimizers as all 2m filters. Every
intermediate family has that property. The proof uses the retained
fourth-power angular recurrence and the original concavity mechanism.
The note also gives stability under these weaker observations, the sharp
uniform error bound for residue recovery, and an explicit angular sublevel law.

This resolves an infinite family within ana:q:dyadic. It does not claim the
same deletion rule for odd lengths or a classification of arbitrary deletions.
The source's odd-length warning in ana:rem:dyadicactive is consistent with it.
