TARGETED PRIMARY-LITERATURE COMPARISON — 26 NOVEMBER 2026 (America/Los_Angeles)

Question: has the cumulative displacement between real zeros of F'+aF and
F'+(a+b)F, or a matrix/graph counterpart with Dirichlet completions, already
been evaluated with leading order T/log T?

Conclusion of this limited search: no exact predecessor for the proposed
arithmetic cumulative-transport formula was located. There are close and
substantial precedents. Publication priority has not been established.
The theorem should be presented as a result derived and proved here, with
an explicit limited-search novelty statement, not as an assured first.

1. CLASSICAL MATRIX AND SPECTRAL-SHIFT BACKGROUND

Jakob Reiffenstein, Higher-order interlacing for matrix-valued meromorphic
Herglotz functions, J. Math. Anal. Appl. 514 (2022), 126260.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10746
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10746
Theorem 4.3 treats generalized interlacing for matrix Herglotz functions,
principal minors, and factorization of determinants into scalar Herglotz
functions. Theorem 5.6 gives a matrix Hermite–Biehler theorem. Therefore
basic real-zero location and interlacing for the graph determinant are
part of an established matrix theory, not independently new phenomena.

Jussi Behrndt, Fritz Gesztesy, Shu Nakamura, Spectral shift functions and
Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps, Math. Ann., DOI 10.1007/s00208-017-1593-4.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.08292
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.08292
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6407858/
This develops spectral-shift trace identities and logarithms of
operator-valued boundary/Weyl functions. Perturbation-determinant and
resolvent-trace formulas are classical machinery. Our arithmetic
application should cite that background; the generic Poisson identity
alone is not an adequate novelty claim.

Matthias Langer and Harald Woracek, Karamata's theorem for regularised
Cauchy transforms, Proc. Roy. Soc. Edinburgh A 155 (2025), 1431–1491.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/prm.2023.128
https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12249
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.12249
Theorem 4.7 directly links regular variation of symmetric measure mass
to its Cauchy-transform imaginary part. Appendix Theorem A.7 and Remark
A.8 give the Stieltjes version with exact constants. For index one,
mu((-y,y)) ~ (2/pi)y Im q(iy). In the present normalization Im q(iy)
~2 tr(B)/log y yields 4 tr(B)y/(pi log y). No evenness of each input
Dirichlet function is needed: the theorem concerns symmetric mass.

3. ESSENTIAL CAUCHY-LAW PRECEDENTS AND THE EXACT DISTINCTION

Michael Aizenman and Simone Warzel, On the ubiquity of the Cauchy
distribution in spectral problems, Probab. Theory Related Fields 163
(2015), 61–87.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.7769
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.7769
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-014-0587-3
Theorem 2.3 and Lemma 2.5 give the Cauchy boundary-value distribution of
shift-amenable Herglotz functions with real boundary values; the proof
uses the Poisson formula. Proposition 2.6 extends Boole's identity. The
scalar mechanism behind the proposed transport result is therefore
closely related to classical Herglotz harmonic measure and Cauchy laws.

Sasha Sodin, On the critical points of random matrix characteristic
polynomials and of the Riemann xi-function, Q. J. Math. 69 (2018), 183–210.
https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/hax033
https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.10037
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.10037
Section 3.4.5, equation (55), proves under RH that, for uniform t in [0,T],
2i xi'(1/2+it)/(xi(1/2+it)log T) converges in distribution to standard
Cauchy. The paper also treats one-parameter level sets Phi'=a Phi for
the limiting random entire function. This is a genuine scalar precedent.

Joseph Najnudel and Ashkan Nikeghbali, Cauchy laws for zeta logarithmic
derivatives and stationary Stieltjes transforms, arXiv:2609.15862v1,
submitted 14 September 2026.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15862
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15862v1
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15862
Theorem 1.1 gives an unconditional projected-ordinate Cauchy limit and
the logarithmic-derivative limit under horizontal collapse, which includes
RH. It credits Sodin's RH result. Equation (1.4) controls comparison with
the projected transform, but the following paragraph expressly says a
rate to the Cauchy law would need a rate for that transform. This is weak
convergence, without the shrinking-window local asymptotic at issue here.

Mathematical distinction (our reasoning, not a quoted literature claim):
For g=Xi'/Xi and fixed b>0,
xi_{a,b}(t)=1_{-a-b<g(t)<-a}.
The proposed law on [-T,T] is
measure{-T<t<T: -a-b<g(t)<-a} ~ 4bT/(pi log T).
After normalizing g by 2/log T, this is a value interval of width
2b/log T shrinking to zero. Weak convergence of 2g/log T to Cauchy does
not imply its relative asymptotic probability. A counterexample in
general is a weakly convergent distribution with an arbitrarily small
hole of width 1/log T near zero. Thus the precise fixed-window law is a
refinement of the cited Cauchy convergence, though the proof uses closely
related Herglotz machinery. Matrix-valued positive perturbations and the
electrical coefficients are further extensions.

Proof sanity check: h(z)=log[(g(z)+a+b)/(g(z)+a)] has an analytic branch
with 0<=Im h<=pi in the upper half-plane. Its boundary imaginary part
is pi xi_{a,b}; bounded harmonicity gives the exact Poisson integral.
Gamma-factor asymptotics give h(iy)~2ib/log y. Karamata gives the symmetric
T/log T mass. For arbitrary macroscopic windows [alpha T,beta T], one
also needs the asymptotic h(Tz)~2ib/log T for each fixed z in the upper
half-plane; radial information alone does not identify asymmetric mass.
Sector Stirling supplies this stronger input. One must justify weighted
compactness/Poisson-transform uniqueness to pass to nonsymmetric windows.

4. REGULARIZED ZEROS BACKGROUND

Andre Voros, Zeta functions for the Riemann zeros, Ann. Inst. Fourier 53
(2003), 665–699.
https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_2003__53_3_665_0.pdf
This constructs secondary zeta functions and regularized determinants
from squared Riemann zeros and shifts. It is relevant background for
squared-zero regularization, not evidence of a prior network-dependent
Kirchhoff-index displacement formula. Only the introduction and source
description were inspected, so this is not a negative full-paper audit.
