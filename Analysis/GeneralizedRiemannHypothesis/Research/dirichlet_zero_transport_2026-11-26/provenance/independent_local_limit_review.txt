ADVERSARIAL MATHEMATICAL REVIEW
File reviewed: output/dirichlet_zero_transport/sections/local_cauchy.tex
Dependencies checked: sections/transport.tex, especially Lemma ut:radial
and the compactness/tail proof of Theorem ut:main.

Conclusion: the stated arbitrary shrinking-band theorem and the
epsilon(T)=exp(-T) corollary are mathematically sound. No substantive
correction is needed. The proof is self-contained relative to the cited
radial lemma and positive-Poisson scaling argument in transport.tex.

1. Analytic branch and boundary indicator
For each T and Im z>0, Im g(z)<0. Thus
 f_T(z) = integral_[a_T,b_T] 1/(g(z)-s) ds
is analytic and has imaginary part strictly between 0 and pi, because
its imaginary part is the integral of a positive Cauchy kernel over a
finite real interval. It exponentiates to (g-a_T)/(g-b_T). At almost
every real point its imaginary part is exactly pi times the indicator
of a_T<g<b_T. The excluded level-set endpoints and poles are discrete.
There is therefore an exact bounded-harmonic Poisson representation,
with no additional singular boundary measure or linear y term.

2. Arbitrarily small widths cause no error amplification
The potentially dangerous operation is division by delta_T=b_T-a_T.
The proof handles this correctly by keeping the integral representation:
 (log T/delta_T) f_T(Tz)
   = average_[a_T,b_T] 1/(g(Tz)/log T - s/log T).
For every fixed upper-half-plane compact set, all denominators tend
uniformly to -d-i/2, which is nonzero. The integrand error is uniform
over the entire shrinking s-interval. Its integral is delta_T times
that error. Consequently division by delta_T does not amplify it.
No lower bound on delta_T, numerical resolution assumption, or rate of
convergence is concealed here. delta_T=exp(-T), or smaller, is allowed.

3. Positive Poisson transforms really imply the required local masses
The normalized measures are positive, although their densities may be
very large. The transform at iR bounds their masses on [-R,R], producing
vague compactness. The kernel comparison for |x|>R bounds the tail of
any fixed Poisson transform by O(1/R) times its transform at iR. The
latter converges to a constant independent of R. Thus mass escaping
to infinity cannot create an extra linear harmonic term. Every vague
subsequential limit has the same constant Poisson transform and hence
is the constant Lebesgue measure. Because that measure gives endpoints
zero mass, interval masses converge. This argument is valid for an
arbitrary T-dependent family of positive measures, not just a dilation
of one fixed density. It therefore covers the moving value bands.

Counterexample mechanisms considered and excluded:
- Tiny intervals magnifying an absolute approximation error: excluded
  by taking a normalized integral average before any approximation.
- Escaping positive mass with an unseen harmonic contribution: excluded
  by the iR tail estimate, which tends to zero as R tends to infinity.
- Narrow resonant peaks or endpoint effects: any macroscopic limiting
  concentration would contradict uniqueness of the constant Poisson
  transform; interval boundaries are null for the limiting measure.
- Arbitrary signed cancellation: unavailable, since all normalized
  measures here are nonnegative. The positivity assumption is essential.

4. Constants and normalization
Im 1/(-d-i/2) = 2/(1+4d^2). The Poisson kernel integrates to pi,
so the limiting spatial density is 2/[pi(1+4d^2)]. This gives
 |{t in [uT,vT]: a_T<g(t)<b_T}|
   ~ 2(v-u) delta_T T/[pi(1+4d^2) log T].
For Z_T=2g(U_T)/log T, set a_T=alpha_T log T/2 and
b_T=beta_T log T/2. Then d=c/2 and delta_T=(beta_T-alpha_T)log T/2.
Division by (v-u)T gives exactly
 P(alpha_T<Z_T<beta_T) ~ (beta_T-alpha_T)/[pi(1+c^2)].
For a_T=-epsilon(T), b_T=epsilon(T), and epsilon=o(log T),
d=0 and delta_T=2epsilon(T), giving
 |{t in [T,2T]: |g(t)|<epsilon(T)}|
    ~ 4epsilon(T)T/[pi log T].
All factors of two and pi in the reviewed file are correct.

5. Scope
The statement concerns Lebesgue measure under continuous sampling,
not counts on a discrete grid. The manuscript says this explicitly.
It relies on the real-zero/strict half-plane sign supplied by RH/GRH
for the specified fixed completed functions. It does not follow from
weak Cauchy convergence alone, and the manuscript correctly distinguishes
the local relative asymptotic from that earlier result. This review
validates the argument; it does not independently establish worldwide
historical priority.
