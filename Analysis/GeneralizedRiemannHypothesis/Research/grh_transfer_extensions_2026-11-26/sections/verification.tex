\section{Verification, reproducibility, and formalization scope}
\label{sec:verification}

\subsection{What was actually checked}
The archive contains the LaTeX source, this PDF, three executable mathematical
checkers, their data and transcripts, the pinned repository inventories, and
the selected upstream source files with their license and attribution notices.
No numerical floating-point solver is part of the proof of any stated theorem.

The general local-field and graph theorems are proved in the manuscript.
The finite examples test their formulas through independent arithmetic operations;
they do not replace the all-parameter proofs. The density theorem is explicitly
computer-assisted at the finite inequality, with a proof certificate and two
independent checking strategies.

\subsection{Reproducing the package}
The two density and graph checkers use Python's standard library. The
local-field checker additionally uses SymPy 1.14.0 for exact polynomial resultants.
From the archive's top-level directory run
\begin{verbatim}
python3 code/certify_density.py
python3 code/check_false_tate.py
python3 code/verify_moment_extremum.py
latexmk -pdf -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error report.tex
\end{verbatim}
The scripts recreate the numerical JSON files and the density proof tree.
The package's checksum file describes the delivered files before any local
regeneration. The source excerpts are evidence for the analysis, not a complete
standalone Lean build with every transitive dependency.

\subsection{A concrete formalization path}
The companion formalization plan separates the three proofs at their natural
interfaces. For the density result, one can check the finite hypergraph certificate
in a small verified kernel and then prove the smooth-weight and natural-density
bridge. For the graph result, the first nonzero moment lemma is finite-field
linear algebra, followed by a polynomial root count and a matrix-tree computation.
For the local result, the essential additions are the ramification calculation,
the triangular valuation basis, and the Gauss-valuation reduction for packets.

None of the new source files in this package is represented as a completed Lean
formalization. A future formal proof should preserve all strict inequalities:
the stopping condition below a torsion exponent, the strict loss threshold
in Theorem~\ref{thm:false-tate-cost}, and the prime-size hypotheses in the
graph theorem. Changing an endpoint can change the conclusion.

\subsection{Limits of the conclusions}
We establish three definite results and a source-based arithmetic assessment.
The natural-density separation answers the mathematical question as formulated
in McNew's paper, while giving explicit credit to the earlier incoming proof.
The local-field and graph results resolve extensions posed in the supplied
research corpus. A targeted search of primary literature supplied context,
but did not establish worldwide publication priority for the exact formulations.

There is no claimed new proof of Artin holomorphy, nonabelian GRH, BSD,
Vandiver, Bloch--Kato, or an additional algebraic $K$-group vanishing theorem.
For those directions the report identifies an actual missing construction
or estimate, rather than treating a conditional criterion as a solution.
