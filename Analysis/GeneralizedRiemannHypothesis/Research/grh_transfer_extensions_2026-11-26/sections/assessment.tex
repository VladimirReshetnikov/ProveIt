\section{Assessment and comparison with the earlier work}

\subsection{What the repository establishes and what this report adds}
The public statements quantify over Dirichlet characters, including imprimitive
characters of every positive modulus. The five exports include RH, its explicit
zeta-zero formulation, and three formulations of Dirichlet GRH. General Hecke,
Artin, and automorphic $L$-functions are outside those exported statements
\cite{upstream}. This distinction identifies the genuinely new work required by
a nonabelian transfer, independently of accepting the user's RH/GRH premise.

The reusable content has several levels. The central arithmetic construction
compares short divisible forms with lower bounds for their determinant; its
quantitative torsion lemma is independently reusable. The proof also contains
classical combinatorial ingredients, including a Behrend-type progression-free
construction. Reusing such an ingredient can lead to a separate theorem without
using either RH or the entire arithmetic proof. The three completed contributions
below make those relationships explicit.

All three results are independent of RH and GRH. The torsion calculation is a
direct refinement of an arithmetic tool used by the repository. The graph
argument extends a previous transfer of torsion valuations to Laplacian
determinants. The density argument is a combinatorial continuation prompted by
the coordinate progression-avoidance ingredient. It would be inaccurate to
attribute the density theorem to the final RH theorem, or to call Behrend's
classical idea an invention of this repository.

\subsection{Pinned evidence and depth of verification}
The two repository snapshots are

The current incoming directory contains 52 ZIP archives. We retrieved or reused
their exact bytes, checked every Git blob hash against the pinned directory
listing, and extracted the text and computational material for comparison.
This is an inventory and source-comparison claim, not a claim to have independently
proved every statement in those 52 manuscripts. The complete inventory and hashes
are in the accompanying evidence files.

For the source audit we verified a clean checkout at the upstream revision,
examined the public endpoints and the pertinent determinant, Selmer, torsion, and
cyclotomic modules, and recorded 21 file hashes and 22 declaration references.
The readable edition contains 803 tracked Lean files, counting 495 in
\texttt{Lib}, 306 in \texttt{New}, and the two endpoint files. The toolchain is
Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib revision
\texttt{56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679}.
The upstream README reports compilation and independent machine checks; those
checks are not reruns performed in this report. Its statement that no
mathematician has read the entire proof is likewise part of the upstream status
description \cite{upstream}.

\subsection{What must be credited to the incoming corpus}
The newest material changes several conclusions that would follow from reading
only the older summaries.
\begin{itemize}
\item The latest \incoming{mcnew_density_and_grh_transfers.zip}{McNew report}
already claims $\beta-\alpha>0.0001306$. We replay its two finite methods,
check its passage to natural density, and improve the bound. The initial
strict-separation idea and the 102-element replacement belong to that report.
\item The \incoming{grh_method_transfers_graph_towers_and_finite_rings.zip}{graph-tower report}
already supplies the finite-field moment formula, an extremal calculation for
a nonzero quadratic moment, and the wild cubic torsion example. We retain those
as inputs and resolve the stated extensions, with the necessary proofs reproduced.
\item Prior reports already develop many nonabelian Frobenius detectors,
Mellin--Landau zero tests, and exact torsion selection bounds. Producing one more
formal implication from an unproved prime estimate would not by itself resolve
nonabelian RH.
\item The current exponent-seven report claims a completed census of 33
imaginary quadratic fields and 40 orders. We did not replay its extensive
certificates, and do not describe that census as either a new result here or
an unresolved task to be rediscovered.
\item Earlier discussions sometimes treated the Selmer construction as controlling
only a chosen finite torsion level. The source contains the full divisible-module
endpoint and an explicit stopping argument. Section~\ref{sec:source-architecture}
states the correction and the restrictions that actually remain.
\end{itemize}
The comparison notes in the archive also identify the inherited signed-cycle,
singular-modulus, density-gap, and class-group work. We do not repackage those
results as newly proved conclusions of this investigation.
