# Formalization of the GRH article

Build these Lean results from the repository root with `lake build GRHArticle`.
The priorities come from [remarkable-results.tex](../Article/remarkable-results.tex).
Each entry below identifies the part of the article covered by the Lean statements.

## Integer geometric progressions

[Rankin.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/Rankin.lean) proves that Rankin's
valuation rule excludes all nonconstant three-term geometric progressions
with rational ratios. It uses the equivalence between `a * c = b * b` and the
arithmetic midpoint relation at every prime exponent. It also proves
progression-freeness for the positive-integer instance of the one-digit
exchange in `gpi:thm:onedigit`. The same file proves the exact finite
reciprocal-mass difference and uses divergence of prime reciprocals to find
a finite set of odd-prime satellites giving a strict gain with marker two.
[Density.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/Density.lean) proves the exact natural density for fixed prime valuations, finite correlated families of valuation tuples, and finite independent exponent rules. [RankinTruncation.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/RankinTruncation.lean) specializes this formula to finitely many Rankin primes and exponents, and bounds which integers the truncation can misclassify.
[DensityBounds.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/DensityBounds.lean) bounds the contribution of omitted prime squares by the reciprocal of the cutoff, uniformly in the sample size. [RankinApproximation.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/RankinApproximation.lean) uses this bound to prove that Rankin’s set has a natural density. [EulerLowerBound.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/EulerLowerBound.lean) bounds every finite product of the squarefree factors from below by one half.

[ExchangeDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/ExchangeDensity.lean) proves the integer counterexample to McNew’s conjecture from `gpi:thm:onedigit(d)`. The exchanged set excludes rational-ratio progressions, has a natural density, and strictly exceeds Rankin’s natural density. [DigitPatternDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/DigitPatternDensity.lean) factors the finite weights, and [ExchangeApproximation.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/ExchangeApproximation.lean) transfers the comparison to the full sets. [ExplicitExchange.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/ExplicitExchange.lean) proves that using all odd primes up to ⌈exp(exp 7)⌉ gives strict gain. [PrimeReciprocalBound.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PrimeReciprocalBound.lean) supplies the finite reciprocal-sum estimate. The number-field extension remains open in Lean.

For the distinct four-marker construction in `gpi:thm:explicit`, [ExplicitArithmetic.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/ExplicitArithmetic.lean) verifies the count of 225 primes through 1427, the odd-prime product bound, the scaled mass inequality and the finite factor used for the rough density bound. [FourMarkerExchange.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/FourMarkerExchange.lean) proves that this exact exchange excludes all nonconstant rational-ratio progressions. [ExplicitExponentBound.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/ExplicitExponentBound.lean) bounds every finite truncation of the greedy exponent mass at two, and [SatelliteProfiles.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/SatelliteProfiles.lean) computes the weights of the distinguished satellite profiles. [FourMarkerDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/FourMarkerDensity.lean) proves the full quantitative theorem. The natural densities of the two specified integer sets exist and the gain is strictly greater than 10⁻⁵⁹⁶. [Article section](../Article/sections/gpi.tex)

[IntegerPrefix.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerPrefix.lean) proves that the loss from using a single admissible subset through 12 is exactly $1/156$ under either ratio convention. [IntegerPrefixEighty.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerPrefixEighty.lean) proves the exact reciprocal-weight optimum through 80. [IntegerPrefixEightyExact.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerPrefixEightyExact.lean) proves the exact maximum admissible cardinality at every cutoff from 1 through 80. The exact finite losses at 80 are $1553/45360$ for rational ratios and $16567/132300$ for integer ratios. [IntegerPrefixTransfer.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerPrefixTransfer.lean) transfers each finite loss to the gap between the two full smooth optimization problems for both ratio conventions. It proves reciprocal-weight summability from the finite Euler product and bounds the upper smooth optimization by one. [IntegerDensityExtrema.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerDensityExtrema.lean) defines the two actual extremal densities. [IntegerSmoothNaturalBound.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerSmoothNaturalBound.lean) proves $b_{\mathbb Q}\le B$ for both ratio conventions. It bounds reciprocal mass in the actual rough-part classes and uses finite summation by parts to transfer that bound to natural density. [IntegerSquarefreeRoughDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerSquarefreeRoughDensity.lean) proves the squarefree rough integers have density $c_Yv_Y$ and gives $v_Y U_Y\le u$ for both ratio conventions. The proof glues arbitrarily large finite prefixes into one admissible set. [IntegerRationalDensityGap.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerRationalDensityGap.lean) proves the rational-ratio conclusion of McNew’s Question 7.1, $\overline\alpha-\alpha>0.002836$. For integral ratios, [IntegerFordDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerFordDensity.lean) proves Ford’s lower bound $271529/331776\le\overline\beta$ from the exact cardinality and admissibility of two separated blocks.

[IntegerPrimeSquareTail.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerPrimeSquareTail.lean) bounds the discarded square multiples by the actual prime-square tail at every cutoff. [IntegerPrimeGapCertificate.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerPrimeGapCertificate.lean) proves that the finite loss times the rough density factor, after subtracting this infinite tail, exceeds $0.002836$ at $Y=293$ for rational ratios and $0.013336$ at $Y=83$ for integral ratios. [IntegerDyadicArithmetic.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerDyadicArithmetic.lean) evaluates sums and downward-rounded products in balanced blocks. The integer computation for the 80-term prefix rounds the contributions upwards through 16383. [IntegerWheelTail.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerWheelTail.lean) bounds the remaining series using the 48 possible prime residues modulo 210.

[IntegerMcNewGaps.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerMcNewGaps.lean) proves both assertions of `gpi:thm:integer-gap`. With integral ratios it gives $\overline\beta-\beta>0.070515$, strengthening the article’s $0.069218$. [IntegerSevenSmoothPacking.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerSevenSmoothPacking.lean) verifies a packing of 268 forbidden triples on 321 smooth integers. Their total weight and independently checked vertex capacities give $\beta\le327204259/437500000<0.747896$.

[IntegerRoughTemplateDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerRoughTemplateDensity.lean) computes the natural density of all products of a chosen rough part and any fixed smooth template, including infinite templates. [IntegerNaturalDensityApproximation.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerNaturalDensityApproximation.lean) proves $v_Y B_Y\le b\le B_Y$ for either ratio convention. The error is at most the actual prime-square tail, hence at most $1/Y$ for positive $Y$.

For the exchange used in McNew’s Question 7.4, [IntegerBoundaryExchangeReplacement.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerBoundaryExchangeReplacement.lean) proves that the specified 102-element set is 5-smooth, is admissible for integral ratios, and has reciprocal weight $1928068463873/680244480000$. [IntegerBoundaryExchangePacking.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerBoundaryExchangePacking.lean) proves soundness of the weighted certificate checker used to bound the alternative rational-ratio sets.

[IntegerFiniteTemplateOptimization.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerFiniteTemplateOptimization.lean) bounds the smooth optimum using a finite weighted search and the remaining reciprocal mass. [IntegerNaturalDensityComputability.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerNaturalDensityComputability.lean) proves the computability assertion in `gpi:thm:attain`. For each ratio convention its rational program approximates the actual natural-density supremum on input $k$ with absolute error at most $2^{-(k+1)}$. [IntegerNaturalDensityAttainment.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerNaturalDensityAttainment.lean) completes the attainment assertion of `gpi:thm:attain`. For each ratio convention, a single admissible set attains the natural-density supremum and its logarithmic density attains the upper logarithmic supremum. [IntegerTemplateSmoothIndependence.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerTemplateSmoothIndependence.lean) controls the finite rules uniformly on large smoothness cutoffs. [IntegerLargestPrimeBandSelection.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerLargestPrimeBandSelection.lean) chooses successive cutoffs with summable errors. [IntegerNearOptimalTemplates.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerNearOptimalTemplates.lean) constructs finite smooth templates whose product sets have natural densities tending to the supremum. [IntegerLargestPrimePastingDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerLargestPrimePastingDensity.lean) proves that summable errors on successive largest-prime bands suffice to attain the limiting density. [IntegerLargestPrimeDilations.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerLargestPrimeDilations.lean) proves that the density of smooth numbers changes by a quantity tending to zero under any fixed dilation of the counting cutoff, uniformly in the smoothness bound.

[IntegerBoundaryExchangeVertices.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerBoundaryExchangeVertices.lean) identifies the 507-vertex finite domain with all 5-smooth integers through one million and checks the exact total capacity. [IntegerBoundaryExchangeTail.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerBoundaryExchangeTail.lean) evaluates the inverse-square sum over integers outside the 5-smooth numbers as $\pi^2/6-25/16$ and proves it lies below the exchange charge.

[IntegerUpperDensityUpperBound.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerUpperDensityUpperBound.lean) proves $u\le U_Y$ by assigning one smooth integer to every increase in the optimal prefix cardinality. With the squarefree lower bound, [IntegerUpperDensityComputability.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerUpperDensityComputability.lean) computes the upper-density supremum for either ratio convention with dyadic error. [IntegerLogarithmicDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerLogarithmicDensity.lean) proves that a set with natural density has the same logarithmic density, and that the supremum of upper logarithmic densities equals the natural-density supremum in both conventions, as stated in `gpi:thm:attain`.

[IntegerBoundaryPatch.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerBoundaryPatch.lean) proves that the 5-smooth exchange can be extended through any larger prime cutoff. It removes the final terms of cross-boundary integral progressions and bounds the cost by the actual inverse-square sum. [IntegerBoundaryDensityBridge.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerBoundaryDensityBridge.lean) transfers a certified local gain $g$ to the bound $\beta-\alpha\ge c_Yg-\sum_{p>Y}p^{-2}$. For natural-density attainment, [IntegerLargestPrimeBands.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerLargestPrimeBands.lean) proves that pasting squarefree-rough templates by their largest prime factors preserves admissibility under either ratio convention.

[IntegerBoundaryExchangeMargin.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerBoundaryExchangeMargin.lean) proves that the proposed local gain at cutoff 6619 would yield a natural-density gap greater than $0.000135206$. Balanced integer computations certify the smooth density factor and all sieve blocks through 524287. The wheel estimate accounts for the entire remaining prime tail.

[GreedyLengthExponents.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/GreedyLengthExponents.lean) defines the executable greedy exponent set for each progression length and proves that every rejected exponent completes a forbidden progression. Its integer specialization verifies admissibility of the edited greedy set. [SharedDeletionProgressions.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/SharedDeletionProgressions.lean) proves the shared-deletion lemma `gpi:lem:kavoid` for signed arithmetic progressions in exponent vectors. [IntegerSharedDeletion.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/IntegerSharedDeletion.lean) applies it to positive integers and excludes geometric progressions of the specified length with rational ratio.

## Geometric progressions of ideals in number fields

[Density.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/NumberFieldIdeals/Density.lean) counts nonzero integral ideals in norm order. Multiplying an arbitrary set with natural density by a fixed ideal divides that density by the ideal norm. [LocalFactors.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/NumberFieldIdeals/LocalFactors.lean) computes the density of ideals coprime to finitely many primes. [ValuationCylinders.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/NumberFieldIdeals/ValuationCylinders.lean) proves that the exponents at any finite set of prime ideals have the independent geometric densities in `gpi:lem:product`, for every number field.

## Polynomial geometric progressions

[PolynomialDeficitCertificate.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialDeficitCertificate.lean) verifies the 74 distinct degree deficits between 0 and 480 used in `gpp:thm:counterexample` and proves that they contain no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. [PolynomialDeficitWeight.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialDeficitWeight.lean) bounds the gain over the full greedy series strictly above 3/200 at 127/128. [PolynomialDeficitSet.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialDeficitSet.lean) proves the deficit rule excludes progressions with a positive-degree polynomial ratio in each degree ball. [PolynomialReplacementSeries.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialReplacementSeries.lean) finds a positive integer coefficient in the product of the signed deficit series and the finite smooth-factor series. [PolynomialSmoothSeries.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSmoothSeries.lean) specializes this to all monic irreducibles of degree at most 480 over a finite field. [PolynomialRoughReplacement.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialRoughReplacement.lean) proves the actual squarefree rough-class replacement is progression-free in every degree ball.

[PolynomialSmoothCount.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSmoothCount.lean) proves that each coefficient of the finite geometric product counts the actual monic smooth polynomials of that degree. At some cutoff the signed sum of these counts is at least one. [PolynomialPrimeExclusion.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialPrimeExclusion.lean) proves that excluding one squarefree prime divides the limiting normalized count by one plus its weight whenever the two counts satisfy the degree recurrence.

[PolynomialBlocks.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialBlocks.lean) proves the loss from taking the top j + 1 degree layers is at most q⁻⁽ʲ⁺¹⁾. [PolynomialReplacementUnion.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialReplacementUnion.lean) constructs a single infinite progression-free set and proves its upper density dominates the limiting replacement proportion whenever that limit is established. [PolynomialDegreeDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialDegreeDensity.lean) counts the unmodified degree layers and computes their limiting ball proportions for every deficit rule. [PolynomialRoughParts.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialRoughParts.lean) factors each nonzero polynomial into its leading scalar, monic rough part and smooth part, and characterizes the changed classes by squarefreeness and degree. [PolynomialReplacementCount.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialReplacementCount.lean) gives the exact change in each modified ball by counting scalars, squarefree rough parts and monic smooth parts. [PolynomialRoughDensity.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialRoughDensity.lean) proves the normalized rough count tends to the positive Euler factor in the article.

[PolynomialCounterexample.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialCounterexample.lean) proves the counterexample to Asada–Fourakis–Manski–McNew–Miller–Moreland's Conjecture 6.3. For every finite field, a single infinite set has no three-term progression with a polynomial ratio of positive degree and has upper density strictly larger than the greedy degree benchmark. Its quantitative lower bound implies the strict inequality $m_q<\beta_q$ for the [supremum over all admissible sets](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialDensitySupremum.lean). [PolynomialSupremum.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSupremum.lean) proves the attainment assertion in `gpp:thm:supremum`. There is a single zero-free progression-free set over each finite field whose upper density equals $\beta_q$. [Article section](../Article/sections/gpp.tex)

For the explicit margins, [PolynomialIrreducibleDegrees.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialIrreducibleDegrees.lean) proves the exact divisor recurrence for the counts of monic irreducibles by factoring the Frobenius polynomial. [PolynomialRoughDensityBound.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialRoughDensityBound.lean) bounds the prime-weight sum by a harmonic number and proves $c_2>2^{-12}$. [PolynomialSearchCutoff.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSearchCutoff.lean) defines the article's upward-rounded budgets and proves all five displayed numerical bounds on $R_q^*$, including $R_2^*+13<2^{476}$. [PolynomialCoefficientConcentration.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialCoefficientConcentration.lean) proves the concentration estimate for signed generating functions. [PolynomialSearchMoments.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSearchMoments.lean) computes the actual tilted mean and variance of the smooth counts, and [PolynomialMomentBudgetBounds.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialMomentBudgetBounds.lean) bounds them by the integer budgets. [PolynomialSearchMargin.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSearchMargin.lean) proves the full explicit cutoff and margin theorem. Some coefficient through $R_q^*$ is at least one, and $\beta_q\ge m_q+(q-1)c_q/q^{R_q^*+1}$. In particular, $\beta_2>m_2+2^{-(R_2^*+13)}>m_2+2^{-2^{476}}$.

[PolynomialSmoothOptimization.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSmoothOptimization.lean) computes the maximum size of a progression-free subset of the monic smooth degree ball. [PolynomialSmoothApproximation.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSmoothApproximation.lean) gives rational truncations of the article's upper approximation $U_T$, proves that they converge, and bounds the error explicitly in the number of enumerated primes, the field size and the truncation degree.

[PolynomialRoughCount.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialRoughCount.lean) shows the normalized count of monic polynomials avoiding the smooth primes equals its Euler factor once the degree reaches the sum of the prime degrees. [PolynomialEulerTail.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialEulerTail.lean) proves $0\le 1-v_T\le q^{-(T+1)}$ by evaluating the smooth series at $q^{-2}$. Its first $T+1$ coefficients count every monic polynomial. [PolynomialOptimizationBounds.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialOptimizationBounds.lean) partitions each smooth ball by degree and proves $0\le U_T\le1$.

[PolynomialSmoothUpperBound.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialSmoothUpperBound.lean) bounds each scalar and rough-part class in an arbitrary admissible set by its smooth-ball optimum. [PolynomialOptimizationLower.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialOptimizationLower.lean) realizes the lower bound using optimal subsets in squarefree rough classes. Together they prove $v_TU_T\le\beta_q\le U_T$ for the polynomial-ratio convention. [PolynomialDensityComputability.lean](GRHArticle/ProgressionFree/PolynomialDensityComputability.lean) proves $U_T\to\beta_q$ and supplies an executable rational approximation to $\beta_q$ with error at most $2^{-(k+1)}$ on input $k$. This completes the polynomial-ratio computability and attainment assertions of `gpp:thm:supremum`. Its rational-ratio extension remains open.
