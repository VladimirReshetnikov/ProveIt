# Scope of the research and comparison

## The explicit repository question

The companion source's "Further research directions" asks for an arithmetic
transseries algebra for divisor sums that retains divisibility indicators,
permits accumulation at log q, and specifies summable products and inverse
operations. Its benchmark is `t2:prop:necklace-cutoff`, in chapter
`ct:ch:necklaces`.

The exact open-question passage was fetched at the pinned commit, source
lines 4955–4978. The broader necklace chapter and synthesis were also read.
The question is answered here for a specified arithmetic Hahn ring, a weighted
absolute completion, scalar analytic operations, and fixed-label reversion.
No maximality or unrestricted moving-label compositional closure is claimed.

## Existing material not claimed as new

The companion already contains the divisor formulas, finite cutoffs,
fixed-radical analytic sheets, and threshold/range-inverse methods. The catalogue
of 29 September arrivals describes an analytic action-measure inversion theory
and explicitly states that it does not retain the divisibility indicators.
Only that catalogue comparison, not a line-by-line correctness review of the
entire action-measure article, is relied upon here.

LCM convolution and its divisor-zeta interpretation are classical and are
credited to the literature. The elementary Hahn-support and Lagrange-inversion
arguments are supplied for completeness, not promoted as new mathematical
principles. The 2026 necklace-polynomial preprint was checked as related
literature; its topic is distinct from this construction. No exhaustive
priority search has been completed.

## Proposed contributions

1. Joint action–label Banach completion and its faithful analytic sheet family.
2. Exact elementary-block admission criterion and explicit uniform majorants.
3. Absolutely summable inverse transport with all arithmetic action tuples and
   a quantitative geometric homogeneous tail.
4. Exact coefficient coordinate change and fixed-order curvature lifting of
   pure-exponential resonances, including the first post-accumulation sector.

The idempotent and profinite obstructions explain why the sheet convention is
needed. They are not assertions that every alternative generalized summation
or arithmetic difference theory is impossible.
