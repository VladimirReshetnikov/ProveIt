\title{\textbf{Unit-Circle, Bessel, and Spectral--Monodromy Representations}\\[0.3em]
\large A comprehensive atlas for the Rvachev and Fabius functions, their Fourier images, and the Thue--Morse sequence}
\author{Research report}
\date{28 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This report audits the mathematical \LaTeX{} corpus under
\url{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/tree/main/Analysis/FabiusFunction/docs},
including its subdirectories, source inventories, historical snapshots, imported papers,
and living frontier reports. It first consolidates the repository's established
probabilistic, convolutional, spline, Fourier, product, moment, resolvent, continued-fraction,
multiresolution, integral-transform, and inverse-function representations. It then develops
a repo-relative new coordinate system based on the probability--Chebyshev moments
\[
 c_n=\int_{-1}^{1}T_n(x)\Up(x)\dd x.
\]
These moments lift the Rvachev probability measure to a symmetric probability measure on the
unit circle. The lift yields an exact Carath\'eodory function, a Joukowski representation of
the Cauchy transform, weighted Chebyshev boundary series for \(\Up\), phase series for the
Fabius function and its inverse, rational Schur--Verblunsky parameters, a CMV resolvent,
Toeplitz determinants, and a Szeg\H{o} product.

Jacobi--Anger expansions produce entire Bessel-series identities equal to the infinite sinc
product and its canonical dyadic zero product, while modified Bessel series equal the
hyperbolic moment-generating product. A Laplace--Bessel--Joukowski chain unifies these
identities with the Cauchy transform. For the signed and unsigned Thue--Morse sequences, we
derive argument-principle formulas from the multiplicities of the integer zeros of the
Fourier image, local residue recurrences, a logarithmic-monodromy recovery formula for
finite blocks, and a smooth Abel--Poisson regularization of the Rademacher sign product.

Every theorem marked ``repo-relative new'' is proved here and was not located by exact-term
or structural search in the audited repository. This phrase is deliberately narrower than
a claim of worldwide priority. Conjectures and a research program are stated separately.
The accompanying Python program uses exact rational arithmetic and high-precision numerics
to verify the coefficient pipelines and analytic identities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, conventions, and nonduplication protocol}
\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{Normalized objects}

We use the Rvachev probability density
\[
 \rho(x):=\Up(x),\qquad \rho\ge0,\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}\rho=[-1,1],\qquad
 \int_{-1}^{1}\rho(x)\dd x=1,
\]
which is even and \(C^\infty\), with every derivative flat at the endpoints. Its associated
random variable admits the convergent series
\begin{equation}
 X=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k}U_k,
 \qquad U_k\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Unif}[-1,1].
 \label{eq:X-random-series}
\end{equation}
Set
\[
 Y=\frac{X+1}{2},\qquad
 F(u)=\Pp(Y\le u)=\int_{-1}^{2u-1}\rho(x)\dd x,
 \qquad I(v)=F^{-1}(v).
\]
Thus, on \(0<u<1\),
\begin{equation}
 F'(u)=2\rho(2u-1).
 \label{eq:Fprime-rho}
\end{equation}

The Fourier convention is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(\xi)=\int_{-1}^{1}\rho(x)\ee^{-2\pi\ii\xi x}\dd x,
 \qquad
 \sincpi z=\frac{\sin(\pi z)}{\pi z},\quad \sincpi 0=1.
 \label{eq:fourier-convention}
\end{equation}
The moment-generating and Cauchy transforms are
\begin{equation}
 M(t)=\E\ee^{tX},
 \qquad
 R(z)=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\rho(x)}{z-x}\dd x,
 \qquad z\in\mathbb C\setminus[-1,1].
 \label{eq:M-R-defs}
\end{equation}
For the Thue--Morse sequence we use
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_n=(-1)^{s_2(n)},\qquad
 \tau_n=\frac{1-\epsilon_n}{2}\in\{0,1\},
 \label{eq:TM-conventions}
\end{equation}
where \(s_2(n)\) is the sum of the binary digits of \(n\).

\section{Established representation atlas}
\label{sec:baseline}

This section compresses the repository's existing representation families into one
normalization. It is included both for completeness and to make the genuinely new bridges
in later sections transparent.

\subsection{Random series, convolutions, and refinement}

From \eqref{eq:X-random-series}, \(X\) satisfies the distributional fixed point
\begin{equation}
 X\ \stackrel d=\ \frac{X+U}{2},
 \qquad U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-1,1],\quad U\perp X.
 \label{eq:distribution-fixed-point}
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\rho\) is an infinite convolution of normalized boxes,
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\underset{k\ge1}{*}\left(2^{k-1}\mathbf1_{[-2^{-k},2^{-k}]}\right),
 \label{eq:infinite-convolution}
\end{equation}
and the finite truncation \(X_N=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2^{-k}U_k\) has a compactly supported
polynomial-spline density \(\rho_N\). The refinement and differential identities can be
written
\begin{align}
 \rho(x)&=\int_{-1}^{1}\rho(2x-u)\dd u,
 \label{eq:rho-refinement}\\
 \rho'(x)&=2\bigl(\rho(2x+1)-\rho(2x-1)\bigr).
 \label{eq:rho-differential}
\end{align}
They imply the familiar Fabius functional-differential relations after the affine change
\(x=2u-1\).

\subsection{Finite Thue--Morse splines}

Let
\[
 a_{N,j}=\frac{2^N-1-2j}{2^N},\qquad 0\le j<2^N.
\]
The finite convolution density has the exact truncated-power form
\begin{equation}
 \rho_N(x)=\frac{2^{N(N-1)/2}}{(N-1)!}
 \sum_{j=0}^{2^N-1}\epsilon_j\,(x+a_{N,j})_+^{N-1}.
 \label{eq:finite-spline}
\end{equation}
The cancellation of all polynomial pieces below degree \(N\) is a Prouhet--Thue--Morse
phenomenon. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 P_N(z):=\prod_{r=0}^{N-1}(1-z^{2^r})
 =\sum_{j=0}^{2^N-1}\epsilon_j z^j.
 \label{eq:finite-TM-product}
\end{equation}
The corresponding unsigned block has
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=0}^{2^N-1}\tau_j z^j
 =\frac12\left(\frac{1-z^{2^N}}{1-z}-P_N(z)\right).
 \label{eq:unsigned-finite-product}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fourier, hyperbolic, and canonical products}

Independence in \eqref{eq:X-random-series} gives
\begin{align}
 \Phi(\xi)
 &=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\sincpi\!\left(\frac{\xi}{2^j}\right),
 \label{eq:phi-sinc-product}\\
 M(t)
 &=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh(t/2^k)}{t/2^k}.
 \label{eq:M-hyperbolic-product}
\end{align}
Rearranging Euler products yields
\begin{align}
 \Phi(\xi)
 &=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}
 \left(1-\frac{\xi^2}{m^2}\right)^{1+\vTwo(m)},
 \label{eq:phi-canonical-product}\\
 M(t)
 &=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}
 \left(1+\frac{t^2}{4\pi^2m^2}\right)^{1+\vTwo(m)}.
 \label{eq:M-canonical-product}
\end{align}
Thus \(\Phi\) has a zero at every nonzero integer \(m\), of multiplicity
\begin{equation}
 d_m=1+\vTwo(|m|).
 \label{eq:zero-multiplicity}
\end{equation}
Its logarithmic derivative is the normally convergent meromorphic series
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Phi'(z)}{\Phi(z)}
 =-2z\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+\vTwo(m)}{m^2-z^2}.
 \label{eq:phi-log-derivative}
\end{equation}
The audited repository develops several equivalent cotangent, tangent, digamma, and
Hurwitz-zeta rearrangements of this expression.

\subsection{Moments, cumulants, Bell polynomials, and Bernoulli numbers}

Write \(\mu_n=\E X^n\). Symmetry gives \(\mu_{2m+1}=0\). Applying
\eqref{eq:distribution-fixed-point} gives the exact triangular recurrence
\begin{equation}
 \mu_n=\frac{1}{2^n-1}
 \sum_{\substack{2\le k\le n\\k\ \mathrm{even}}}
 \binom{n}{k}\frac{\mu_{n-k}}{k+1}
 \qquad(n\ge2\ \mathrm{even}).
 \label{eq:moment-recurrence}
\end{equation}
The cumulants are
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_{2m}=\frac{2^{2m-1}B_{2m}}{m(2^{2m}-1)},
 \qquad \kappa_{2m+1}=0,
 \label{eq:cumulants}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
 \mu_n=Y_n(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_n),
 \label{eq:Bell-moments}
\end{equation}
where \(Y_n\) is the complete exponential Bell polynomial. These formulas make all moments
rational and expose the Bernoulli and dyadic denominator structure.

\subsection{Cauchy, logarithmic, determinant, and continued-fraction forms}

For \(z\notin[-1,1]\), the Cauchy transform has the moment expansion
\begin{equation}
 R(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_n}{z^{n+1}},\qquad |z|>1,
 \label{eq:R-moment-series}
\end{equation}
and, for \(\Re z>1\), the Laplace form
\begin{equation}
 R(z)=\int_0^{\infty}\ee^{-zt}M(t)\dd t.
 \label{eq:R-Laplace}
\end{equation}
Differentiating under the integral or using \eqref{eq:rho-differential} gives
\begin{equation}
 R'(z)=2\bigl(R(2z+1)-R(2z-1)\bigr).
 \label{eq:R-differential}
\end{equation}
The finite spline transform possesses the logarithmic formula
\begin{equation}
 R_N(z)=\frac{2^{N(N-1)/2}}{(N-1)!}
 \sum_{j=0}^{2^N-1}\epsilon_j
 (z+a_{N,j})^{N-1}\Log(z+a_{N,j}),
 \label{eq:finite-log-resolvent}
\end{equation}
with polynomial ambiguities canceled by Prouhet identities. The living representation
report also gives Fredholm determinant and Jacobi/Stieltjes continued-fraction forms. If
\(P_n\) are the monic symmetric orthogonal polynomials,
\begin{equation}
 P_{n+1}(x)=xP_n(x)-\beta_nP_{n-1}(x),\qquad \beta_n>0,
 \label{eq:Jacobi-recurrence}
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
 R(z)=\cfrac{1}{z-\cfrac{\beta_1}{z-\cfrac{\beta_2}{z-\ddots}}}.
 \label{eq:Jacobi-CF}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fabius and inverse-function transform forms}

The pushforward identity \(Y=(X+1)/2\) yields
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1\ee^{-2\pi\ii\xi u}\dd F(u)
 =\ee^{-\pi\ii\xi}\Phi(\xi/2).
 \label{eq:F-measure-transform}
\end{equation}
For \(\xi\ne0\), integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1F(u)\ee^{-2\pi\ii\xi u}\dd u
 =\frac{\ee^{-\pi\ii\xi}\Phi(\xi/2)-\ee^{-2\pi\ii\xi}}
 {2\pi\ii\xi}.
 \label{eq:F-function-transform}
\end{equation}
Since \(I(V)\) has distribution \(F\) when \(V\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\),
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1\ee^{-2\pi\ii\xi I(v)}\dd v
 =\ee^{-\pi\ii\xi}\Phi(\xi/2).
 \label{eq:inverse-pushforward-transform}
\end{equation}
The repository further contains finite dyadic antiderivatives, complex powers, fractional
Volterra operators, Mellin/Newton moments, inverse primitives and higher derivatives,
Stieltjes/log-potential transforms, autocorrelation identities, and endpoint asymptotics.

\begin{baselinebox}
Equations \eqref{eq:infinite-convolution}--\eqref{eq:inverse-pushforward-transform} form the
input atlas. Later sections do not claim these identities as new; they reorganize them
through a unit-circle probability lift and derive consequences absent from the audited
subtree.
\end{baselinebox}

\section{Probability--Chebyshev coordinates}
\label{sec:cheb}

\begin{definition}
For \(n\ge0\), define the \emph{probability--Chebyshev moment}
\begin{equation}
 c_n:=\E[T_n(X)]=\int_{-1}^{1}T_n(x)\rho(x)\dd x,
 \label{eq:cndef}
\end{equation}
where \(T_n(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)\).
\end{definition}

Because \(\rho\) is even, \(c_{2m+1}=0\). These numbers are not the unweighted Chebyshev
coefficients of the density: the integration measure in \eqref{eq:cndef} is
\(\rho(x)\dd x\), not \(\dd x/\sqrt{1-x^2}\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact Bell--Bernoulli formula]
\label{thm:c-exact}
For \(m\ge1\),
\begin{equation}
 c_{2m}=m\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}
 \frac{(m+k-1)!}{(m-k)!(2k)!}\,2^{2k}\mu_{2k}.
 \label{eq:c-moment-formula}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with \(\kappa_{2j}\) as in \eqref{eq:cumulants},
\begin{equation}
 c_{2m}=m\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}
 \frac{(m+k-1)!}{(m-k)!(2k)!}\,2^{2k}
 Y_{2k}(0,\kappa_2,0,\kappa_4,\ldots,\kappa_{2k}).
 \label{eq:c-Bell-Bernoulli}
\end{equation}
In particular every \(c_n\) is rational.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(m\ge1\),
\[
 T_{2m}(x)=m\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}
 \frac{(m+k-1)!}{(m-k)!(2k)!}(2x)^{2k}.
\]
Integrating termwise against \(\rho(x)\dd x\) proves \eqref{eq:c-moment-formula}; substituting
\eqref{eq:Bell-moments} and \eqref{eq:cumulants} proves \eqref{eq:c-Bell-Bernoulli}.
\end{proof}

The first terms are
\begin{align*}
 c_0&=1,&c_2&=-\frac79,&c_4&=\frac{227}{675},&c_6&=-\frac{11237}{297675},\\
 c_8&=-\frac{8241761}{227721375},&
 c_{10}&=\frac{171586753}{8135075025},&
 c_{12}&=-\frac{472381023339557}{45472425490366875}.
\end{align*}
The repeated negative sign at \(c_6,c_8\) warns against a naive alternating-sign conjecture.

\begin{proposition}[Generating function]
\label{prop:G-cheb}
For \(|w|<1\),
\begin{align}
 G(w)&:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nw^n
 =\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1-wx}{1-2wx+w^2}\rho(x)\dd x,
 \label{eq:G-cheb}\\
 &=1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}w^{2m}.
 \label{eq:G-even}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integrate the standard generating function
\(\sum_{n\ge0}T_n(x)w^n=(1-wx)/(1-2wx+w^2)\) against the probability measure.
\end{proof}

For any \(0<r<1\), Cauchy coefficient extraction gives
\begin{equation}
 c_n=\frac{1}{2\pi r^n}\int_0^{2\pi}G(re^{\ii\theta})
 \ee^{-\ii n\theta}\dd\theta.
 \label{eq:c-Cauchy-coefficient}
\end{equation}
This will become an ordinary circle Fourier coefficient in the next section.

\begin{novelbox}
The sequence \(c_n\) is one rational coordinate system controlling a positive-real
Carath\'eodory function, the Joukowski pullback of \(R\), a weighted Chebyshev expansion of
\(\rho\), phase series for \(F\) and \(F^{-1}\), Bessel expansions of \(\Phi\) and \(M\),
and rational OPUC/CMV data. This unification is the central structural result of the report.
\end{novelbox}

\section{Unit-circle lift and Carath\'eodory--Joukowski bridge}
\label{sec:circle}

\subsection{A canonical probability measure on the circle}

Define a probability measure \(\nu\) on \(\T\) by
\begin{equation}
 \dd\nu(\theta)=\frac12\rho(\cos\theta)|\sin\theta|\dd\theta,
 \qquad 0\le\theta<2\pi.
 \label{eq:circle-lift}
\end{equation}
The map \(\theta\mapsto x=\cos\theta\) pushes \(\nu\) forward to \(\rho(x)\dd x\). Moreover,
\(\nu\) is invariant under conjugation and the half-turn \(\zeta\mapsto-\zeta\).

\begin{proposition}[Fourier moments of the lift]
\label{prop:circle-moments}
For every \(n\in\Z\),
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\T}\zeta^{-n}\dd\nu(\zeta)=c_{|n|}.
 \label{eq:nu-moments}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Imaginary parts cancel under conjugation, while
\(\cos(n\arccos x)=T_n(x)\). The two preimages of each \(x\in(-1,1)\) contribute equally.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
The Carath\'eodory function of \(\nu\) is
\begin{equation}
 C(w)=\int_{\T}\frac{\zeta+w}{\zeta-w}\dd\nu(\zeta),
 \qquad w\in\D.
 \label{eq:C-def}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
It is analytic in \(\D\), satisfies \(C(0)=1\), and has positive real part.

\begin{theorem}[Carath\'eodory representation]
\label{thm:C-representation}
For \(|w|<1\),
\begin{align}
 C(w)
 &=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1-w^2}{1-2wx+w^2}\rho(x)\dd x,
 \label{eq:C-real-integral}\\
 &=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_nw^n
 =1+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}w^{2m},
 \label{eq:C-series}\\
 &=2G(w)-1.
 \label{eq:C-G}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pair \(e^{\ii\theta}\) and \(e^{-\ii\theta}\) in \eqref{eq:C-def}; their average kernel is
\((1-w^2)/(1-2w\cos\theta+w^2)\). The geometric expansion
\((\zeta+w)/(\zeta-w)=1+2\sum_{n\ge1}w^n\zeta^{-n}\) and
\cref{prop:circle-moments} give the series.
\end{proof}

The positive harmonic regularization
\begin{equation}
 W_r(\theta):=\Re C(re^{\ii\theta})
 =1+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}r^{2m}\cos(2m\theta)
 \label{eq:Wr}
\end{equation}
preserves positivity for every \(0\le r<1\), unlike an arbitrary boundary truncation.

\subsection{Joukowski uniformization}

Let
\begin{equation}
 \Jouk(w)=\frac12(w+w^{-1}),\qquad 0<|w|<1,
 \label{eq:Joukowski}
\end{equation}
with inverse branch
\begin{equation}
 w(z)=z-\sqrt{z^2-1},
 \qquad \sqrt{z^2-1}\sim z\quad(z\to\infty).
 \label{eq:Joukowski-inverse}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Joukowski bridge]
\label{thm:Joukowski-bridge}
For \(0<|w|<1\),
\begin{equation}
 C(w)=\frac{1-w^2}{2w}R(\Jouk(w)).
 \label{eq:C-R-Jouk}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for \(z\in\mathbb C\setminus[-1,1]\),
\begin{align}
 R(z)&=\frac{2w(z)}{1-w(z)^2}C(w(z)),
 \label{eq:R-C-w}\\
 &=\frac{1+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}w(z)^{2m}}
 {\sqrt{z^2-1}}.
 \label{eq:R-Cheb-series}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\Jouk(w)-x=(1-2wx+w^2)/(2w)\), compare \eqref{eq:C-real-integral} with the
definition of \(R\). On the chosen branch, \(\sqrt{z^2-1}=(1-w^2)/(2w)\).
\end{proof}

The ordinary moment series is centered at infinity in powers of \(z^{-1}\). By contrast,
\eqref{eq:R-Cheb-series} uses the conformal coordinate of the slit plane, so it remains
geometrically adapted even near the support.

\subsection{Boundary recovery of \(\rho\)}

Set
\begin{equation}
 W(\theta)=\pi\rho(\cos\theta)|\sin\theta|.
 \label{eq:W-def}
\end{equation}
This is the density of \(\nu\) relative to \(\dd\theta/(2\pi)\). Endpoint flatness makes it a
\(C^\infty\), \(\pi\)-periodic function.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted Chebyshev boundary series]
\label{thm:boundary-rho}
For every \(\theta\),
\begin{equation}
 W(\theta)=\Re C(e^{\ii\theta})
 =1+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}\cos(2m\theta),
 \label{eq:W-Fourier}
\end{equation}
with convergence in \(C^k\) for every fixed \(k\). Consequently, for \(|x|<1\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \rho(x)=\frac{1+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}T_{2m}(x)}
 {\pi\sqrt{1-x^2}}.}
 \label{eq:rho-weighted-Cheb}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The boundary theorem for Poisson integrals gives \(\Re C(e^{\ii\theta})=W(\theta)\). Smoothness
implies uniform convergence of all fixed derivatives. Substitute \(x=\cos\theta\).
\end{proof}

The harmonic conjugate is
\begin{align}
 \Im C(e^{\ii\theta})
 &=2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}\sin(2m\theta),
 \label{eq:C-imag-series}\\
 &=\PV\int_0^{2\pi}\cot\!\left(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)\dd\nu(\varphi).
 \label{eq:C-Hilbert}
\end{align}
Thus one disk-analytic function packages the density and its circular Hilbert transform.

\subsection{Phase series for \(F\) and \(F^{-1}\)}

For \(0<u<1\), put
\begin{equation}
 \theta(u)=\arccos(2u-1)\in(0,\pi).
 \label{eq:theta-u}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Fabius phase series]
\label{thm:F-phase}
For \(0\le u\le1\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 F(u)=1-\frac{\theta(u)}{\pi}
 -\frac1\pi\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{2m}}{m}\sin(2m\theta(u)).}
 \label{eq:F-phase}
\end{equation}
For \(0<u<1\),
\begin{equation}
 F'(u)=\frac{\Re C(e^{\ii\theta(u)})}{\pi\sqrt{u(1-u)}}
 =\frac{1+2\sum_{m\ge1}c_{2m}T_{2m}(2u-1)}{\pi\sqrt{u(1-u)}}.
 \label{eq:Fprime-C}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \(x=\cos\varphi\) in \(F(u)=\int_{-1}^{2u-1}\rho(x)\dd x\):
\(F(u)=\pi^{-1}\int_{\theta(u)}^\pi W(\varphi)\dd\varphi\). Integrate
\eqref{eq:W-Fourier} termwise and differentiate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Inverse phase equation]
\label{cor:inverse-phase}
For each \(v\in(0,1)\), let \(\Theta(v)\in(0,\pi)\) be the unique solution of
\begin{equation}
 v=1-\frac{\Theta}{\pi}
 -\frac1\pi\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{2m}}{m}\sin(2m\Theta).
 \label{eq:Theta-implicit}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 I(v)&=\frac{1+\cos\Theta(v)}{2},
 \label{eq:I-phase}\\
 I'(v)&=\frac{\pi\sqrt{I(v)(1-I(v))}}{\Re C(e^{\ii\Theta(v)})}.
 \label{eq:Iprime-C}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

The explicit factors \(1/\sqrt{u(1-u)}\) do not create endpoint singularities in \(F'\): the
numerator \(W\) is flat enough to cancel every algebraic divergence. In the inverse formula,
\(1/W\) isolates the true rapid endpoint growth and is compatible with the Lambert--\(W\)
asymptotics in the repository.

\subsection{Disk form of the resolvent differential equation}

Set
\begin{equation}
 A(w)=\frac{2wC(w)}{1-w^2}=R(\Jouk(w))
 \label{eq:A-disk}
\end{equation}
and, as germs at \(w=0\),
\begin{equation}
 q_{\pm}(w)=2\Jouk(w)\pm1-
 \sqrt{(2\Jouk(w)\pm1)^2-1},
 \label{eq:qpm}
\end{equation}
choosing square roots so that \(q_\pm(w)\to0\).

\begin{proposition}[Disk composition equation]
\label{prop:disk-composition}
Near \(w=0\),
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd w}\left(\frac{2wC(w)}{1-w^2}\right)
 =4\Jouk'(w)\left[
 \frac{q_+(w)C(q_+(w))}{1-q_+(w)^2}
 -\frac{q_-(w)C(q_-(w))}{1-q_-(w)^2}
 \right].
 \label{eq:disk-composition}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \(A(w)=R(\Jouk(w))\), apply \eqref{eq:R-differential}, and use
\(R(\Jouk(q))=2qC(q)/(1-q^2)\) for each inverse branch.
\end{proof}

\section{Bessel series equal to the dyadic products}
\label{sec:bessel}

The Jacobi--Anger expansions
\begin{align}
 \ee^{-\ii z\cos\theta}
 &=J_0(z)+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-\ii)^nJ_n(z)\cos(n\theta),
 \label{eq:JA-ordinary}\\
 \ee^{z\cos\theta}
 &=I_0(z)+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}I_n(z)\cos(n\theta)
 \label{eq:JA-modified}
\end{align}
turn the Fourier moments of \(\nu\) into Bessel coefficients.

\begin{theorem}[Bessel--sinc--canonical product identity]
\label{thm:Bessel-Phi}
For every \(\xi\in\mathbb C\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \Phi(\xi)=J_0(2\pi\xi)
 +2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^m c_{2m}J_{2m}(2\pi\xi).}
 \label{eq:Phi-Bessel}
\end{equation}
The series is locally uniformly convergent, and
\begin{align}
 J_0(2\pi\xi)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^mc_{2m}J_{2m}(2\pi\xi)
 &=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\sincpi\!\left(\frac{\xi}{2^j}\right)
 \label{eq:Bessel-sinc}\\
 &=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{\xi^2}{n^2}\right)^{1+\vTwo(n)}.
 \label{eq:Bessel-canonical}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under \(\nu\), \(X=\cos\Theta\). Integrate \eqref{eq:JA-ordinary} with
\(z=2\pi\xi\); odd moments vanish and \((-\ii)^{2m}=(-1)^m\). The product equalities are
\eqref{eq:phi-sinc-product} and \eqref{eq:phi-canonical-product}.
\end{proof}

For every \(r\ge0\), termwise differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 \Phi^{(r)}(\xi)=(2\pi)^r\left[J_0^{(r)}(2\pi\xi)
 +2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^mc_{2m}J_{2m}^{(r)}(2\pi\xi)\right].
 \label{eq:Phi-Bessel-derivatives}
\end{equation}
At an integer zero \(N\ne0\), the first \(1+\vTwo(N)\) equations in this derivative ladder
become exact Bessel cancellation identities. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 J_0(2\pi N)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^mc_{2m}J_{2m}(2\pi N)=0.
 \label{eq:Bessel-integer-zero}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Modified-Bessel--hyperbolic identity]
\label{thm:Bessel-M}
For every \(t\in\mathbb C\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 M(t)=I_0(t)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}I_{2m}(t).}
 \label{eq:M-Bessel}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{align}
 I_0(t)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}I_{2m}(t)
 &=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh(t/2^k)}{t/2^k}
 \label{eq:IBessel-hyperbolic}\\
 &=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{t^2}{4\pi^2n^2}\right)^{1+\vTwo(n)}.
 \label{eq:IBessel-canonical}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Integrate \eqref{eq:JA-modified} against \(\nu\) and invoke the product formulas for \(M\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Laplace--Bessel--Joukowski chain}

For \(\Re z>1\),
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^{\infty}\ee^{-zt}I_n(t)\dd t
 =\frac{(z-\sqrt{z^2-1})^n}{\sqrt{z^2-1}}.
 \label{eq:Laplace-In}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Three-level transform identity]
\label{thm:Laplace-Bessel-Jouk}
For \(\Re z>1\), with \(w=z-\sqrt{z^2-1}\),
\begin{align}
 R(z)
 &=\int_0^{\infty}\ee^{-zt}
 \prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh(t/2^k)}{t/2^k}\dd t
 \label{eq:R-Laplace-product-new}\\
 &=\int_0^{\infty}\ee^{-zt}\left[I_0(t)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}I_{2m}(t)\right]\dd t
 \label{eq:R-Laplace-Bessel}\\
 &=\frac{1+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{2m}w^{2m}}{\sqrt{z^2-1}}.
 \label{eq:R-Laplace-Jouk}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:M-hyperbolic-product} and \eqref{eq:M-Bessel} into
\eqref{eq:R-Laplace}; use dominated convergence and \eqref{eq:Laplace-In}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fabius and inverse-Fabius transforms}

For \(\xi\ne0\),
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1F(u)\ee^{-2\pi\ii\xi u}\dd u
 =\frac{\ee^{-\pi\ii\xi}
 \left[J_0(\pi\xi)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^mc_{2m}J_{2m}(\pi\xi)\right]
 -\ee^{-2\pi\ii\xi}}{2\pi\ii\xi}.
 \label{eq:F-Bessel-transform}
\end{equation}
The value at \(\xi=0\) is \(1/2\). For the inverse pushforward,
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1\ee^{-2\pi\ii\xi I(v)}\dd v
 =\ee^{-\pi\ii\xi}\left[J_0(\pi\xi)+2\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^mc_{2m}J_{2m}(\pi\xi)\right].
 \label{eq:I-Bessel-transform}
\end{equation}

\begin{novelbox}
Equations \eqref{eq:Bessel-sinc}, \eqref{eq:Bessel-canonical},
\eqref{eq:IBessel-hyperbolic}, and \eqref{eq:IBessel-canonical} are direct series--product
bridges: the same rational sequence \(c_{2m}\) expands both the Fourier image and the moment
generating function in Bessel bases. No corresponding bridge was located in the audited
repository.
\end{novelbox}

\section{Schur, Verblunsky, CMV, and Toeplitz representations}
\label{sec:opuc}

The Carath\'eodory function places the Rvachev measure in the theory of orthogonal
polynomials on the unit circle (OPUC) \cite{SimonOPUC1,SimonOneFoot}. This creates a rational
coefficient system distinct from, but explicitly coupled to, the repository's Jacobi
coefficients.

\subsection{Schur algorithm and rationality}

Define
\begin{equation}
 f_0(w)=\frac{C(w)-1}{w(C(w)+1)},
 \qquad C(w)=\frac{1+wf_0(w)}{1-wf_0(w)}.
 \label{eq:Schur-first}
\end{equation}
Recursively set
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_n=f_n(0),\qquad
 f_{n+1}(w)=\frac{f_n(w)-\alpha_n}{w(1-\alpha_nf_n(w))}.
 \label{eq:Schur-recursion}
\end{equation}
All functions and coefficients are real under the present symmetries.

\begin{theorem}[Rational Verblunsky coefficients]
\label{thm:rational-alpha}
The Schur parameters \(\alpha_n\) are the Verblunsky coefficients of \(\nu\). They satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_n\in\mathbb Q\cap(-1,1),\qquad \alpha_{2n}=0\quad(n\ge0).
 \label{eq:alpha-rational-parity}
\end{equation}
The odd coefficients are obtained by exact rational operations from the moments \(c_{2m}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the standard Carath\'eodory--Schur correspondence. Rationality follows
because the Taylor coefficients of \(C\) are rational and every Schur step uses rational
operations. The half-turn invariance \(\nu(E)=\nu(-E)\) forces all odd circle moments and,
equivalently, every even-index Verblunsky coefficient to vanish. Infinite support gives
\(|\alpha_n|<1\).
\end{proof}

The first nonzero values are
\begin{align*}
 \alpha_1&=-\frac79,&
 \alpha_3&=-\frac{17}{25},&
 \alpha_5&=-\frac{1849}{3087},\\
 \alpha_7&=-\frac{3569379}{6492691},&
 \alpha_9&=-\frac{445076883510763}{886612917691413},&
 \alpha_{11}&=-\frac{594580974953911037169143}{1270428317522023221443375}.
\end{align*}
The exact program computes much longer coefficients without floating-point contamination.

\subsection{Wall--Schur continued fraction}

Solving \eqref{eq:Schur-recursion} for \(f_n\) gives
\begin{equation}
 f_n(w)=\frac{\alpha_n+w f_{n+1}(w)}{1+\alpha_n w f_{n+1}(w)}.
 \label{eq:inverse-Schur-step}
\end{equation}
Iterating this Möbius recursion and inserting it into \eqref{eq:Schur-first} produces a
Wall--Schur continued fraction for \(C\). Truncating after \(N\) parameters by setting
\(f_N=0\) gives a rational \(C_N\) with positive real part in \(\D\). Therefore
\begin{equation}
 R_N^{\mathrm{Schur}}(z)=\frac{C_N(w(z))}{\sqrt{z^2-1}}
 \label{eq:R-Schur-approximant}
\end{equation}
is a positivity-preserving rational/conformal approximation to the Cauchy transform. It is
geometrically different from truncating the real-line Jacobi fraction
\eqref{eq:Jacobi-CF}.

\subsection{Geronimus bridge to Jacobi coefficients}

For the symmetric pushforward under \(x=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2\), the Geronimus relation
simplifies because \(\alpha_{2n}=0\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact Jacobi--Verblunsky conversion]
\label{thm:Geronimus}
With \(\alpha_{-1}=-1\), the monic Jacobi coefficients satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \beta_{n+1}=\frac14(1-\alpha_{2n-1})(1+\alpha_{2n+1})}
 \qquad(n\ge0).
 \label{eq:Geronimus}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{2n+1}=\frac{4\beta_{n+1}}{1-\alpha_{2n-1}}-1.
 \label{eq:alpha-from-beta}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The first values
\begin{equation}
 \beta_1=\frac19,\quad
 \beta_2=\frac{32}{225},\quad
 \beta_3=\frac{619}{3675},\quad
 \beta_4=\frac{835232}{4640643}
 \label{eq:first-betas}
\end{equation}
reproduce \(\alpha_1,\alpha_3,\alpha_5,\alpha_7\). In the accompanying code, the
\(\beta_n\) are generated by exact Gram--Schmidt from \(\mu_n\), while a direct formal Schur
algorithm independently generates \(\alpha_n\); the pipelines agree exactly through the
checked indices.

\subsection{CMV resolvent}

Let \(\CMV\) be the CMV matrix associated with \((\alpha_n)_{n\ge0}\) on
\(\ell^2(\N_0)\), and let \(e_0=(1,0,\ldots)\). Its spectral measure at \(e_0\) is \(\nu\).

\begin{theorem}[CMV representation]
\label{thm:CMV}
For \(|w|<1\),
\begin{equation}
 C(w)=\left\langle e_0,(\CMV+w)(\CMV-w)^{-1}e_0\right\rangle.
 \label{eq:C-CMV}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 R(\Jouk(w))=\frac{2w}{1-w^2}
 \left\langle e_0,(\CMV+w)(\CMV-w)^{-1}e_0\right\rangle.
 \label{eq:R-CMV}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the spectral theorem to multiplication by \(\zeta\) in \(L^2(\nu)\), then express that
unitary operator in the CMV basis. The second formula follows from
\eqref{eq:C-R-Jouk}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Toeplitz determinants and Szeg\H{o} product}

Let
\begin{equation}
 D_N=\det[c_{j-k}]_{j,k=0}^{N-1},\qquad c_{-n}=c_n.
 \label{eq:Toeplitz-D}
\end{equation}
These determinants are positive rationals.

\begin{proposition}[Exact determinant product]
\label{prop:Toeplitz-product}
For \(N\ge1\),
\begin{equation}
 D_N=\prod_{j=0}^{N-2}(1-\alpha_j^2)^{N-1-j}.
 \label{eq:Toeplitz-alpha}
\end{equation}
Only odd \(j\) contribute.
\end{proposition}

The weight \(W(\theta)\) is positive almost everywhere and
\(\log W\in L^1(\T)\). The latter follows from the established endpoint asymptotics:
although \(\rho(\cos\theta)\) is superflat, its logarithm grows only polylogarithmically in
\(1/|\theta|\), which is integrable against \(\dd\theta\). Szeg\H{o}'s theorem gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_n^2)
 =\exp\left(\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log W(\theta)\dd\theta\right).}
 \label{eq:Szego-product}
\end{equation}
Rakhmanov's theorem also yields \(\alpha_n\to0\), because \(W>0\) almost everywhere.

\begin{novelbox}
Equations \eqref{eq:C-CMV}, \eqref{eq:R-CMV}, \eqref{eq:Toeplitz-alpha}, and
\eqref{eq:Szego-product} give operator, determinant, and entropy-product representations of
the Rvachev law. The associated CMV coefficients are exact rationals and are linked to the
known Jacobi coefficients by \eqref{eq:Geronimus}.
\end{novelbox}

\section{Spectral, contour, and monodromy formulas for Thue--Morse}
\label{sec:TM}

This section derives representations for both \(\epsilon_n\) and \(\tau_n\) by treating the
integer zeros of \(\Phi\) as spectral data. These formulas differ from the repository's
coefficient, digit, half-integer-sign, trigonometric, and finite-product formulas.

\subsection{Zero-counting encodes the full binary digit sum}

Let
\begin{equation}
 N_\Phi(n):=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\bigl(1+\vTwo(m)\bigr),\qquad N_\Phi(0)=0.
 \label{eq:Nphi-def}
\end{equation}
This is the number of positive zeros of \(\Phi\) in \((0,n+1/2)\), counted with multiplicity.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral digit-sum identity]
\label{thm:spectral-digit-sum}
For every \(n\ge0\),
\begin{equation}
 N_\Phi(n)=n+\vTwo(n!)=2n-s_2(n).
 \label{eq:Nphi-digit}
\end{equation}
Thus the complete binary digit sum, not only its parity, is recovered by
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle s_2(n)=2n-N_\Phi(n).}
 \label{eq:digit-from-spectrum}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first equality follows from \eqref{eq:zero-multiplicity}. Legendre's formula for the
2-adic valuation gives \(\vTwo(n!)=n-s_2(n)\).
\end{proof}

Let \(\Gamma_n\) be any positively oriented contour enclosing precisely the positive integer
zeros \(1,\ldots,n\), and no other zeros of \(\Phi\).

\begin{corollary}[Argument-principle representations]
\label{cor:argument-TM}
For \(n\ge0\),
\begin{align}
 s_2(n)
 &=2n-\frac{1}{2\pi\ii}\oint_{\Gamma_n}\frac{\Phi'(z)}{\Phi(z)}\dd z,
 \label{eq:digit-contour}\\
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \epsilon_n=\exp\left(\frac12\oint_{\Gamma_n}
 \frac{\Phi'(z)}{\Phi(z)}\dd z\right),}
 \label{eq:epsilon-contour}\\
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \tau_n=\sin^2\left(\frac{1}{4\ii}\oint_{\Gamma_n}
 \frac{\Phi'(z)}{\Phi(z)}\dd z\right).}
 \label{eq:tau-contour}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The argument principle gives the contour integral as \(2\pi\ii N_\Phi(n)\). Since
\(N_\Phi(n)\equiv s_2(n)\pmod2\), exponentiation gives \(\epsilon_n\), while
\(\sin^2(\pi N_\Phi(n)/2)\) is its unsigned parity.
\end{proof}

Substitution of \eqref{eq:phi-log-derivative} turns the contour formulas into explicit
meromorphic series integrals. Locally at a positive integer,
\begin{equation}
 \Res_{z=n}\frac{\Phi'(z)}{\Phi(z)}=1+\vTwo(n),
 \label{eq:local-residue}
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\epsilon_n}{\epsilon_{n-1}}
 =\exp\left(\pi\ii\Res_{z=n}\frac{\Phi'(z)}{\Phi(z)}\right)
 =(-1)^{1+\vTwo(n)}.
 \label{eq:local-TM-ratio}
\end{equation}
Thus the local zero multiplicity records the carry chain when passing from \(n-1\) to \(n\).

\subsection{Logarithmic monodromy of finite resolvents}

The finite resolvent \eqref{eq:finite-log-resolvent} has branch points
\begin{equation}
 z_{N,j}=-a_{N,j},\qquad 0\le j<2^N.
 \label{eq:branch-points}
\end{equation}
Let \(\Delta_jR_N\) denote the change after one positive analytic continuation around
\(z_{N,j}\), holding all other logarithmic branches fixed.

\begin{theorem}[Monodromy recovery of a finite Thue--Morse word]
\label{thm:monodromy-TM}
For \(z\) near \(z_{N,j}\),
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_jR_N(z)=2\pi\ii\frac{2^{N(N-1)/2}}{(N-1)!}
 \epsilon_j(z-z_{N,j})^{N-1}.
 \label{eq:monodromy-jump}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \epsilon_j=\frac{1}{2\pi\ii\,2^{N(N-1)/2}}
 \left.\frac{\dd^{N-1}}{\dd z^{N-1}}\Delta_jR_N(z)\right|_{z=z_{N,j}},}
 \label{eq:epsilon-monodromy}
\end{equation}
and \(\tau_j=(1-\epsilon_j)/2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Only the \(j\)-th logarithm changes, by \(2\pi\ii\), around \(z_{N,j}\). This proves
\eqref{eq:monodromy-jump}. Differentiating \(N-1\) times at the branch point cancels
\((N-1)!\) and isolates \(\epsilon_j\).
\end{proof}

The finite Thue--Morse word is therefore the normalized monodromy representation of a single
multivalued analytic function. This interpretation is stronger than saying that
\(\epsilon_j\) merely occurs as an algebraic coefficient: the word is recovered from the
analytic continuation operators themselves.

\subsection{Abel--Poisson regularization of the Rademacher product}

Define, for \(0<r<1\),
\begin{align}
 H_r(\theta)
 &:=\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{r^{2\ell+1}}{2\ell+1}\sin((2\ell+1)\theta)
 \label{eq:Hr-series}\\
 &=\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{atan2}
 \bigl(2r\sin\theta,1-r^2\bigr).
 \label{eq:Hr-atan}
\end{align}
This is the Abel/Poisson regularization of \(\sgn(\sin\theta)\), and
\(H_r(\theta)\to\sgn(\sin\theta)\) away from multiples of \(\pi\).

For \(n\ge1\), put \(J_n=\lfloor\log_2(n+1/2)\rfloor\); for \(n=0\), interpret the product
below as empty.

\begin{theorem}[Smooth finite-product representation]
\label{thm:Abel-TM}
For every \(n\ge0\),
\begin{align}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \epsilon_n=\lim_{r\uparrow1}
 \prod_{j=0}^{J_n}H_r\!\left(\frac{\pi(n+1/2)}{2^j}\right),}
 \label{eq:epsilon-Abel}\\
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \tau_n=\frac12\left[1-
 \lim_{r\uparrow1}\prod_{j=0}^{J_n}H_r\!\left(\frac{\pi(n+1/2)}{2^j}\right)\right].}
 \label{eq:tau-Abel}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The signed Thue--Morse sequence has the Rademacher sign product
\[
 \epsilon_n=\prod_{j\ge0}\sgn\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi(n+1/2)}{2^j}\right).
\]
For \(j>J_n\), the angle lies in \((0,\pi)\), so the factor is \(+1\); only finitely many
factors matter. Replace each by its Abel limit.
\end{proof}

The accompanying computation shows uniform convergence over the first 64 symbols: the
maximum errors for \(r=0.9,0.99,0.999,0.9999\) are approximately
\(0.937,0.237,0.0257,0.00259\), respectively. The formula is especially useful when a smooth
analytic surrogate is preferable to a discontinuous sign function.

\subsection{A cardinal Bessel--Thue--Morse expansion}

Since \(\rho\) is supported in \([-1,1]\), the Paley--Wiener/Shannon formula at spacing
\(1/2\) is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(z)=\sum_{k\in\Z}\Phi(k/2)\sincpi(2z-k).
 \label{eq:Shannon-Phi}
\end{equation}
All nonzero integer samples vanish. If
\(A_n=|\Phi(n+1/2)|\), the existing half-integer sign law
\(\sgn\Phi(n+1/2)=\epsilon_n\) therefore yields
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(z)=\sincpi(2z)+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\epsilon_nA_n
 \left[\sincpi(2z-(2n+1))+\sincpi(2z+(2n+1))\right].
 \label{eq:Shannon-TM}
\end{equation}
This formula is included as a derived synthesis rather than a primary novelty claim, because
the latest repository report already develops a broad cardinal-reproduction ladder.

\begin{novelbox}
The contour formulas recover the full digit sum and both Thue--Morse normalizations from the
zero divisor of \(\Phi\). The monodromy formula recovers each finite word from analytic
continuation of \(R_N\), while the Abel formula replaces discontinuous Rademacher signs by
finite products of harmonic functions.
\end{novelbox}

\section{Conjectures and frontier research program}
\label{sec:conjectures}

\begin{conjecturebox}
The statements in this section are unproved unless explicitly described as conditional.
They are motivated by exact data, standard OPUC theory, and the known endpoint asymptotics,
but they should not be cited as theorems.
\end{conjecturebox}

\begin{conjecture}[Sign and monotonicity of odd Verblunsky coefficients]
\label{conj:alpha-monotone}
For every \(n\ge0\),
\begin{equation}
 -1<\alpha_{2n+1}<\alpha_{2n+3}<0.
 \label{eq:conj-alpha-monotone}
\end{equation}
Thus the odd subsequence approaches zero strictly from below.
\end{conjecture}

The first twenty odd coefficients support the conjecture. A proof may be accessible through
total positivity of Toeplitz minors, a comparison principle for the Schur algorithm, or a
special sign-regularity property of the circle weight \(W\). Ordinary positivity of \(W\) is
not enough; a genuinely stronger structural argument is needed.

\begin{conjecture}[Logarithmic edge law]
\label{conj:alpha-asymptotic}
There is a constant \(A>0\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{2n-1}\sim-A\frac{\log n}{n}.
 \label{eq:alpha-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
If the Jacobi edge law has the form
\begin{equation}
 \frac14-\beta_{n+1}\sim C\frac{(\log n)^2}{n^2},
 \label{eq:beta-edge-conj}
\end{equation}
then the Geronimus relation predicts \(A=2\sqrt C\).
\end{conjecture}

Indeed, writing \(\gamma_n=-\alpha_{2n-1}\),
\[
 \frac14-\beta_{n+1}
 =\frac14\bigl(\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_n+\gamma_n\gamma_{n+1}\bigr).
\]
For \(\gamma_n\asymp(\log n)/n\), the product term dominates the discrete derivative.
This turns the real-line Jacobi edge problem into a unit-circle Schur asymptotic problem.

\begin{conjecture}[Refined Szeg\H{o}--Toeplitz asymptotics]
\label{conj:Toeplitz}
The Toeplitz determinants \(D_N\) admit a strong asymptotic expansion whose first correction
to the Szeg\H{o} linear term is governed by the logarithmic endpoint-flatness law of
\(\rho\), and hence ultimately by the Lambert--\(W\) expansion appearing in the endpoint
analysis.
\end{conjecture}

The weight is positive and smooth but has infinite-order zeros. It lies outside the most
naive strictly-positive smooth-weight regime, making it a promising test case for extending
Fisher--Hartwig and strong Szeg\H{o} methods to logarithmically superflat weights.

\begin{conjecture}[Arithmetic structure of the Szeg\H{o} constant]
\label{conj:Szego-constant}
The entropy integral
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S_\rho=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
 \log\bigl(\pi\rho(\cos\theta)|\sin\theta|\bigr)\dd\theta
 \label{eq:Szego-constant}
\end{equation}
has a representation in terms of the dyadic cumulant data, perhaps as a rapidly convergent
series involving Bernoulli numbers, dyadic zeta factors, or a binary Barnes-type constant.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Bessel zero hierarchy has a finite recursive basis]
\label{conj:Bessel-basis}
The derivative cancellation identities obtained from the zero of multiplicity
\(1+\vTwo(N)\) at \(N\) can be reduced, using Bessel recurrences, to a triangular finite
basis whose coefficient matrix has an explicit Thue--Morse or \(q\)-binomial factorization.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Quantitative Abel convergence]
\label{conj:Abel-rate}
For \(n\ge1\) and \(r\) sufficiently close to one,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\epsilon_n-\prod_{j=0}^{J_n}H_r\!\left(
 \frac{\pi(n+1/2)}{2^j}\right)\right|
 \le C(1-r)\sum_{j=0}^{J_n}\frac{1}{|\sin(\pi(n+1/2)/2^j)|}
 \label{eq:Abel-rate}
\end{equation}
with an absolute constant \(C\), and the reciprocal-sine sum admits a sharp bound in terms
of the binary carry structure of \(n\).
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{Promising research directions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Rigorous OPUC asymptotics.} Derive the odd Verblunsky asymptotics directly from
the superflat weight using nonlinear steepest descent for the OPUC Riemann--Hilbert problem,
or via fine spectral theory of the CMV matrix.

\item \textbf{Certified coefficient computation.} Combine exact rational recurrences with
modular arithmetic and rational reconstruction. Hundreds of \(\alpha_n\) and \(\beta_n\)
should be feasible without storing enormous intermediate fractions.

\item \textbf{Fast inverse Fabius solvers.} Use the phase equation
\eqref{eq:Theta-implicit} with FFT-evaluated trigonometric sums and safeguarded Newton steps.
The positivity of \(\Re C\) gives a natural monotonicity certificate.

\item \textbf{Entropy and determinant constants.} Numerically evaluate
\(\mathcal S_\rho\), compare it with products of exact \(1-\alpha_n^2\), and use integer-
relation searches only after rigorous tail bounds are available.

\item \textbf{CMV/Jacobi scattering.} Treat the Rvachev Jacobi and CMV matrices as compact or
slowly varying perturbations of the free models. The endpoint-flatness law should appear in
reflection coefficients and spectral phase shifts.

\item \textbf{Base-\(b\) and \(q\)-Rvachev lifts.} For generalized products with scales
\(b^{-k}\) or exponent sequences, construct the analogous circle lift, identify the
Carath\'eodory function, and compare rationality/algebraicity of its Schur parameters.

\item \textbf{Monodromy categories.} Replace the binary finite spline by finite
\(q\)-binomial or digit-weighted splines. Their logarithmic resolvents should carry a
monodromy representation of the corresponding automatic word.

\item \textbf{De Branges and Hermite interpolation.} The integer zero divisor has unbounded
multiplicity. Build a complete interpolation basis adapted to the values of
\(\Phi^{(r)}(n)\) for \(0\le r\le\vTwo(n)\), rather than ordinary simple-node Shannon
sampling.

\item \textbf{Bessel sum-rule algebra.} Organize \eqref{eq:Bessel-integer-zero} and its
higher derivatives as identities in the Bessel differential algebra; search for finite
closed combinations at powers of two.

\item \textbf{Mellin matching.} Match the unit-circle phase representation to the repository's
Mellin/Newton and Lambert--\(W\) endpoint expansions, obtaining uniform approximations from
the center to the boundary.

\item \textbf{Formal verification.} The moment recurrence, exact Chebyshev transform,
Carath\'eodory series, and contour zero count are suitable for Lean formalization. The
analytic boundary and Szeg\H{o} portions would require more functional-analysis
infrastructure.

\item \textbf{Interval numerics.} Recompute the figures with ball arithmetic, certify the
Schur parameters and Bessel identities, and establish explicit truncation bounds for the
phase series and Abel product.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The audited repository already contains a remarkably broad representation theory: random
series, splines, Thue--Morse coefficients, sinc and canonical products, exact moments,
Bernoulli/Bell structure, resolvents, logarithmic finite transforms, determinant and
continued-fraction forms, multiresolution expansions, integral transforms, inverse-function
identities, and endpoint asymptotics. The main advance here is therefore not another
isolated formula, but a new organizing bridge.

The probability--Chebyshev moments \(c_n\) lift the Rvachev law to the circle. From that lift,
one analytic function \(C\) simultaneously yields the density, the Fabius phase series, the
inverse phase equation, the Joukowski Cauchy transform, Schur and CMV data, and Toeplitz
products. The same coefficients turn the infinite sinc and hyperbolic products into ordinary
and modified Bessel series. On the arithmetic side, the integer zero divisor of \(\Phi\)
recovers the complete binary digit sum by the argument principle; finite logarithmic
resolvents recover Thue--Morse words by monodromy; and Abel regularization gives a smooth
finite-product representation.

These bridges place the Rvachev--Fabius system at an intersection of probability,
Fourier analysis, special functions, OPUC, operator theory, complex monodromy, automatic
sequences, and dyadic asymptotics. The conjectures above are concrete enough for exact
computation and theoretical attack, yet broad enough to connect several currently separate
campaigns in the repository.

\section{Proof details and convergence notes}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Smoothness of the circle weight}

Near \(\theta=0\), \(1-\cos\theta\asymp\theta^2\). Since \(\rho\) and all its derivatives
vanish at \(x=1\), the composite \(\rho(\cos\theta)|\sin\theta|\) and all its derivatives tend
to zero at the origin. The same holds at \(\pi\) and \(2\pi\). This justifies the
\(C^\infty\) periodic extension used in \cref{thm:boundary-rho}, hence the superalgebraic
decay of \(c_n\).

\subsection{Local uniform convergence of Bessel series}

For \(|z|\le R\), the standard bound \(|J_n(z)|\le (R/2)^n/n!\,e^{R^2/4}\) is sufficient.
Since \(|c_n|\le1\), the series in \eqref{eq:Phi-Bessel} converges uniformly on compact sets.
An analogous factorial-in-order bound for \(I_n\) proves local uniform convergence of
\eqref{eq:M-Bessel}. Termwise differentiation follows from Weierstrass convergence.

\subsection{Log-integrability of the circle weight}

The established endpoint analysis of \(\rho(1-h)\) has logarithm growing on the scale of a
quadratic or mildly corrected quadratic polynomial in \(\log(1/h)\). With
\(h\asymp\theta^2\), any finite power of \(\log(1/\theta)\) is integrable near zero:
\[
 \int_0^\varepsilon \bigl|\log^k\theta\bigr|\dd\theta<\infty.
\]
The extra factor \(|\sin\theta|\) contributes only \(\log|\theta|\). Therefore
\(\log W\in L^1\), as required by Szeg\H{o}'s theorem.

\subsection{A direct expression for the circle measure}

For any continuous \(h\) on \(\T\),
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\T}h(\zeta)\dd\nu(\zeta)
 =\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{h(e^{\ii\arccos x})+h(e^{-\ii\arccos x})}{2}
 \rho(x)\dd x.
 \label{eq:nu-pushforward}
\end{equation}
This formula is often the fastest route for transporting real-line identities to the circle.
