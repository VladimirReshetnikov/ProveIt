\begin{abstract}
The current \repo\ corpus already contains a very broad formal and semi-formal theory of the Fabius function, its inverse, Rvachev's compactly supported up-function, Thue--Morse identities, dyadic arithmetic, geometric-
\(q\) laws, infinite sinc products, Lambert-
\(W\) endpoint asymptotics, Legendre expansions, and Bell--Bernoulli moment algebra.  This report deliberately starts from a narrow piece of that established theory---the exact integer-zero divisor of the Rvachev Fourier product---and follows consequences that are absent from the current primary and frontier volumes.

The main new results are as follows.  First, the Rvachev Fourier transform is placed in the Laguerre--P\'olya class, while a square-root moment transform belongs to the type-I subclass.  Its normalized even moments therefore form a P\'olya-frequency sequence of infinite order, all shifted Jensen polynomials are hyperbolic, every derivative of the Fourier transform has only real zeros, and the moments satisfy a new sharp two-sided Tur\'an hierarchy.  Second, the unbounded dyadic zero multiplicities prove that the Fourier transform and its moment transform are not D-finite; consequently the exact even-moment sequence is not P-recursive.  Third, reciprocal-integer bases are treated uniformly: the products are Cartwright-class entire functions whose exact zero counts contain base-
\(b\) digit sums.

A second chain of results gives a probabilistic spectral dual.  The logarithmic derivative of the moment transform is a Stieltjes transform, its logarithm is a complete Bernstein function, and every negative power is the Laplace transform of an explicit generalized gamma convolution with a purely atomic Thorin measure.  Its L\'evy density is controlled by an arithmetic heat trace.  Mellin inversion gives the heat trace's complete algebraic and log-periodic skeleton.  Jacobi inversion then supplies an \emph{exact} beyond-all-orders remainder, not merely an asymptotic estimate.  Laplace transformation of this identity yields an exact modular-dual formula for the infinite sinc moment product; the exponentially small factor is the generating product for partitions into powers of \(b\).  The leading heat defect has an explicit lower-Lambert-
\(W\) inverse, creating a new bridge to the inverse-Fabius asymptotic machinery.

All claims marked ``Theorem'' or ``Corollary'' are proved in the report.  Conjectures are isolated in a separate section.  A commented Python program reproduces exact moment tables, Jensen-root checks, periodic Fourier coefficients, and the heat/modular-dual numerical validations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Normalization and reciprocal-integer-base family}

\subsection{The dyadic law and its \texorpdfstring{base-\(b\)}{base-b} analogue}

For an integer \(b\ge2\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Phi-b}
  \Phi_b(\xi):=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\SincRad\!\left(\frac{\pi\xi}{b^j}\right).
\end{equation}
This is the Fourier transform of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Xb}
  X_b:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{U_j}{2b^j},
  \qquad
  \SupportOperator (X_b)=
  \left[-\frac{b}{2(b-1)},\frac{b}{2(b-1)}\right].
\end{equation}
The dyadic case \(b=2\) is exactly \eqref{eq:Phi-definition} and \eqref{eq:random-X}.

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:db}
  \nu_b(m):=\max\{k\ge0:b^k\mid m\},
  \qquad d_b(m):=1+\nu_b(m),\qquad m\ge1.
\end{equation}
For composite \(b\), \(\nu_b\) is a whole-base divisibility exponent, not a prime-adic valuation.

\begin{proposition}[Arithmetic canonical product]\label{prop:canonical}
For every integer \(b\ge2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-Phi-b}
  \Phi_b(z)=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}
  \left(1-\frac{z^2}{m^2}\right)^{d_b(m)},
\end{equation}
locally uniformly on \(\ComplexNumbers\).  Hence the nonzero zeros are precisely the signed integers and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order-db}
  \OrderOperator_{z=\pm m}\Phi_b=d_b(m).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Euler's product
\(
\SincRad(\pi w)=\prod_{n\ge1}(1-w^2/n^2)
\)
gives
\[
  \Phi_b(z)=\prod_{j\ge0}\prod_{n\ge1}
  \left(1-\frac{z^2}{b^{2j}n^2}\right).
\]
A positive integer \(m\) occurs as \(b^jn\) exactly for
\(j=0,1,\ldots,\nu_b(m)\).  Regrouping therefore gives \eqref{eq:canonical-Phi-b}.  The regrouping is legitimate because
\[
 \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{d_b(m)}{m^2}
 =\frac{\zeta(2)}{1-b^{-2}}<\infty.
\]
The multiplicity statement follows immediately.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The square-root moment transform}

Because \(\Phi_b\) is even, the expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-b}
  A_b(z):=\Phi_b\!\left(\frac{i\sqrt z}{\pi}\right)
\end{equation}
is single-valued and entire.  Equivalently,
\begin{align}
  A_b(z)
  &=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}
    \frac{\sinh(\sqrt z/b^j)}{\sqrt z/b^j}
    \label{eq:A-scale-product}\\
  &=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}
    \left(1+\frac{z}{\pi^2m^2}\right)^{d_b(m)}.
    \label{eq:A-spectral-product}
\end{align}
For \(b=2\), if \(\mu_{2n}:=\Expectation[X^{2n}]\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a-moment}
  A_2(z)=\Expectation e^{2\sqrt z X}
  =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^n,
  \qquad a_n=\frac{4^n\mu_{2n}}{(2n)!}.
\end{equation}
The normalization removes all powers of \(\pi\): every \(a_n\) is rational.

\section{Laguerre--P\'olya structure, total positivity, and Jensen hyperbolicity}

\subsection{Real-rooted entire functions}

\begin{theorem}[Laguerre--P\'olya classification]\label{thm:LP}
For every integer \(b\ge2\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\roman*)}]
  \item \(\Phi_b\) belongs to the Laguerre--P\'olya class \(\LP\).
  \item \(A_b\) belongs to the type-I Laguerre--P\'olya class \(\LPI\).
  \item Every derivative \(\Phi_b^{(r)}\) and \(A_b^{(r)}\) has only real zeros.
  \item The zeros of successive derivatives interlace in the usual weak sense; after common multiplicity factors are removed, the interlacing is strict.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Truncate \eqref{eq:canonical-Phi-b} at \(m\le M\).  The resulting polynomial has only real zeros, and the products converge locally uniformly because
\(\sum d_b(m)m^{-2}<\infty\).  Thus \(\Phi_b\) is a locally uniform limit of real-rooted polynomials and belongs to \(\LP\); see \cite{Levin1996} for the standard Laguerre--P\'olya framework.

Likewise, truncations of \eqref{eq:A-spectral-product} have only negative zeros.  The summability of
\(d_b(m)/(\pi^2m^2)\) puts \(A_b\) in the canonical type-I form.  Closure of \(\LP\) under differentiation proves the real-zero statement.  Interlacing follows by applying Rolle's theorem to finite truncations and passing to the locally uniform limit, with repeated zeros carried through their common factors.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The conclusion is stronger than the already known location and multiplicity of the zeros of \(\Phi\).  It controls the zeros of \emph{every} Fourier derivative.  In particular, no derivative creates a nonreal zero pair.
\end{remark}

\subsection{P\'olya-frequency moments}

Recall that a sequence \((c_n)_{n\ge0}\) is a P\'olya-frequency sequence of infinite order if every minor of the Toeplitz matrix
\((c_{j-i})_{i,j\ge0}\), with \(c_k=0\) for \(k<0\), is nonnegative.

\begin{theorem}[Total nonnegativity of normalized moments]\label{thm:PF}
For every integer \(b\ge2\), the coefficient sequence
\begin{equation}
  A_b(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n^{(b)}z^n
\end{equation}
belongs to \(\PF\).  In particular, all Toeplitz minors are nonnegative and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary-turan}
  \bigl(a_n^{(b)}\bigr)^2\ge a_{n-1}^{(b)}a_{n+1}^{(b)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Aissen--Edrei--Schoenberg--Whitney characterization \cite{Aissen1951,Aissen1952} says that a nonnegative sequence is \(\PF\) precisely when its generating function has the form
\[
 Cz^me^{\gamma z}
 \frac{\prod_i(1+\alpha_i z)}{\prod_j(1-\beta_jz)},
 \qquad \alpha_i,\beta_j,\gamma\ge0,
 \quad \sum_i\alpha_i+\sum_j\beta_j<\infty.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:A-spectral-product} has this form with \(C=1\), \(m=0\), \(\gamma=0\), no \(\beta_j\), and with
\(d_b(m)\) copies of \(\alpha=(\pi^2m^2)^{-1}\).  The required sum is finite.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Variation-diminishing moment transform]\label{cor:variation}
Convolution by the sequence \((a_n^{(b)})\) cannot increase the number of sign changes of a finitely supported real sequence.  Thus the normalized Rvachev moment kernel is a discrete variation-diminishing operator.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{All shifted Jensen polynomials}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-n}
  \gamma_n^{(b)}:=n!a_n^{(b)}=A_b^{(n)}(0)
\end{equation}
and define the shifted Jensen polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Jensen}
  J_{d,n}^{(b)}(x):=
  \sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom dj\gamma_{n+j}^{(b)}x^j.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Jensen hyperbolicity hierarchy]\label{thm:Jensen}
For all integers \(b\ge2\), \(d\ge1\), and \(n\ge0\), the polynomial
\(J_{d,n}^{(b)}\) has only real negative zeros.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The derivative \(A_b^{(n)}\) is in \(\LP\) by \cref{thm:LP}.  Jensen's characterization of the Laguerre--P\'olya class (in the modern Jensen-polynomial formulation, see \cite{GORZ2019}) therefore makes its degree-
\(d\) Jensen polynomial hyperbolic.  Formula \eqref{eq:Jensen} has strictly positive coefficients, so a real zero cannot be nonnegative.  Hence every zero is negative.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strong Tur\'an inequality]\label{cor:strong-turan}
For \(n\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-turan-a}
  \bigl(a_n^{(b)}\bigr)^2>
  \frac{n+1}{n}a_{n-1}^{(b)}a_{n+1}^{(b)}.
\end{equation}
In the dyadic case this becomes the new two-sided moment inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-two-sided}
  \frac{2n-1}{2n+1}
  <\frac{\mu_{2n}^2}{\mu_{2n-2}\mu_{2n+2}}<1,
  \qquad n\ge1.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the strict Laguerre inequality
\[
  \bigl(f'(0)\bigr)^2-f(0)f''(0)>0
\]
to \(f=A_b^{(n-1)}\).  Strictness holds because \(A_b^{(n-1)}\) has real zeros and is not an exponential.  In terms of \(\gamma_n=n!a_n\), this is
\(\gamma_n^2>\gamma_{n-1}\gamma_{n+1}\), which rearranges to \eqref{eq:strong-turan-a}.

Using \eqref{eq:a-moment}, the lower inequality is exactly
\[
 \frac{\mu_{2n}^2}{\mu_{2n-2}\mu_{2n+2}}
 >\frac{n+1}{n}
   \frac{(2n)!^2}{(2n-2)!(2n+2)!}
 =\frac{2n-1}{2n+1}.
\]
The upper inequality is the strict log-convexity of the moment sequence,
obtained from Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(|X|^{n-1}\) and \(|X|^{n+1}\).  Equality would force \(|X|\) to be almost surely constant, which is false for the up-density.
\end{proof}

\section{Bernoulli, Bell, and symmetric-function formulas}

\subsection{Spectral power sums}

\begin{proposition}[Valuation Dirichlet series]\label{prop:Dirichlet}
For \(\Re s>1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dirichlet-db}
  \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_b(m)}{m^s}
  =\frac{\zeta(s)}{1-b^{-s}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prb}
  p_r^{(b)}:=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{d_b(m)}{\pi^{2r}m^{2r}},
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pr-zeta}
  p_r^{(b)}=
  \frac{\zeta(2r)}{\pi^{2r}(1-b^{-2r})}.
\end{equation}
For \(b=2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pr-Bernoulli}
  p_r^{(2)}=
  \frac{(-1)^{r+1}2^{4r-1}B_{2r}}
       {(2r)!(2^{2r}-1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since
\(
\nu_b(m)=\sum_{j\ge1}\IndicatorOf{b^j\mid m}
\),
absolute convergence gives
\[
 \sum_{m\ge1}\frac{\nu_b(m)}{m^s}
 =\sum_{j\ge1}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{(b^jn)^s}
 =\zeta(s)\frac{b^{-s}}{1-b^{-s}}.
\]
Adding \(\zeta(s)\) proves \eqref{eq:Dirichlet-db}; Euler's evaluation of \(\zeta(2r)\) gives \eqref{eq:pr-Bernoulli}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Newton recurrence and Bell polynomial}

Taking logarithms in \eqref{eq:A-spectral-product} gives, for \(|z|<\pi^2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logA-series}
  \log A_b(z)=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{(-1)^{r-1}}{r}p_r^{(b)}z^r.
\end{equation}
Differentiation yields the exact Newton recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Newton-recurrence}
  n a_n^{(b)}=
  \sum_{r=1}^{n}(-1)^{r-1}p_r^{(b)}a_{n-r}^{(b)},
  \qquad a_0^{(b)}=1.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with \(B_n\) the complete exponential Bell polynomial,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bell-form}
 a_n^{(b)}=\frac1{n!}
 B_n\!\left(p_1^{(b)},-1!p_2^{(b)},2!p_3^{(b)},\ldots,
 (-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!p_n^{(b)}\right).
\end{equation}
The elementary-symmetric determinant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Newton-determinant}
  a_n^{(b)}=\frac1{n!}
  \det\begin{pmatrix}
  p_1&1&0&\cdots&0\\
  p_2&p_1&2&\cdots&0\\
  p_3&p_2&p_1&\ddots&\vdots\\
  \vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&n-1\\
  p_n&p_{n-1}&\cdots&p_2&p_1
  \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where \(p_r=p_r^{(b)}\).

\begin{remark}
The repository already contains extensive Bell--Bernoulli moment algebra.  The new feature here is the interpretation of exactly the same coefficients as elementary symmetric functions of the arithmetic spectrum
\[
 \left\{\frac1{\pi^2m^2}\text{ repeated }d_b(m)\text{ times}\right\},
\]
which is what makes total positivity and Jensen hyperbolicity automatic.
\end{remark}

\section{Non-holonomicity and non-P-recursive moments}

\subsection{A zero-multiplicity obstruction}

\begin{lemma}[Multiplicity bound for D-finite entire functions]\label{lem:Dfinite-multiplicity}
Let \(f\not\equiv0\) be an entire solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dfinite-ode}
  P_r(z)f^{(r)}(z)+P_{r-1}(z)f^{(r-1)}(z)+\cdots+P_0(z)f(z)=0,
\end{equation}
where \(P_r\not\equiv0\) is a polynomial.  At every point
\(z_0\) with \(P_r(z_0)\ne0\), the multiplicity of a zero of \(f\) is at most \(r-1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(f\) vanished to order at least \(r\) at an ordinary point \(z_0\), then
\(f(z_0)=\cdots=f^{(r-1)}(z_0)=0\).  Equation \eqref{eq:Dfinite-ode} gives
\(f^{(r)}(z_0)=0\).  Differentiating the equation repeatedly then forces every derivative of \(f\) at \(z_0\) to vanish, contradicting \(f\not\equiv0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Non-D-finiteness]\label{thm:nonDfinite}
For every integer \(b\ge2\), neither \(\Phi_b\) nor \(A_b\) is D-finite over \(\ComplexNumbers(z)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At \(m=b^k\), \cref{prop:canonical} gives
\[
  \OrderOperator_{z=m}\Phi_b=1+\nu_b(b^k)=k+1.
\]
The multiplicities are unbounded.  If \(\Phi_b\) satisfied an equation of order \(r\), all but finitely many of these zeros would be ordinary points of that equation, contradicting \cref{lem:Dfinite-multiplicity}.  The zeros of \(A_b\) at
\(-\pi^2b^{2k}\) have the same unbounded multiplicities, so the identical argument applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The moments are not P-recursive]\label{cor:notPrecursive}
The coefficient sequence \((a_n^{(b)})_{n\ge0}\) is not P-recursive.  In particular, the Rvachev even moments \((\mu_{2n})_{n\ge0}\) are not P-recursive.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A power series is D-finite if and only if its coefficient sequence is P-recursive \cite{Stanley1980}.  Thus the first assertion follows from \cref{thm:nonDfinite}.  In the dyadic case
\(a_n=4^n\mu_{2n}/(2n)!\).  Multiplication or division by this nonzero hypergeometric factor converts a polynomial-coefficient recurrence into another one after denominators are cleared.  Hence P-recursiveness of one sequence would imply that of the other.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Non-D-finite Thorin transforms]\label{cor:negative-power-nondfinite}
For every \(t>0\), the germ \(A_b(z)^{-t}\) is not D-finite.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For integral \(t\), the continuation has poles at every
\(-\pi^2m^2\); for nonintegral \(t\), it has branch singularities there.  A D-finite germ has only finitely many finite singular points, all among the roots of a leading polynomial.  The infinite singular set is therefore impossible.
\end{proof}

\section{Cartwright class and digital zero counts}

\begin{theorem}[Cartwright classification]\label{thm:Cartwright}
For every integer \(b\ge2\), \(\Phi_b\) is a Cartwright-class entire function of exact exponential type
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type}
  \tau_b=\frac{\pi b}{b-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The random variable \(X_b\) in \eqref{eq:Xb} has support
\([-R_b,R_b]\) with \(R_b=b/[2(b-1)]\).  Its Fourier--Laplace transform therefore has exponential type at most
\(2\pi R_b=\pi b/(b-1)\).  The support reaches arbitrarily close to both endpoints, so the type is exact; equivalently, one can evaluate
\(\Phi_b(iy)=\Expectation e^{2\pi yX_b}\) and use a fixed positive mass in
\((R_b-\varepsilon,R_b]\).  On the real axis every sinc factor has modulus at most one, hence
\(|\Phi_b(x)|\le1\) and
\[
 \int_{\RealNumbers}\frac{\log^+|\Phi_b(x)|}{1+x^2}\,\Differential x=0.
\]
This is the Cartwright condition; standard entire-function background is in \cite{Levin1996}.
\end{proof}

Let \(s_b(N)\) denote the sum of the base-\(b\) digits of \(N\).

\begin{proposition}[Exact digital zero count]\label{prop:zero-count}
The total multiplicity of the positive zeros \(1,2,\ldots,N\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-count}
  M_b(N):=\sum_{m=1}^{N}d_b(m)
  =\frac{bN-s_b(N)}{b-1}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the number of nonzero zeros in \(|z|\le R\), counted with multiplicity, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total-count}
  n_b(R)=
  \frac{2\bigl(b\Floor{R}-s_b(\Floor{R})\bigr)}{b-1}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-asym}
  n_b(R)=\frac{2b}{b-1}R+O(\log R),
\end{equation}
whose leading density equals \(2\tau_b/\pi\), as predicted by Cartwright theory.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using \(\nu_b(m)=\sum_{j\ge1}\IndicatorOf{b^j\mid m}\),
\[
 M_b(N)=N+\sum_{j\ge1}\Floor{\frac{N}{b^j}}.
\]
The standard base-
\(b\) digit identity
\(
\sum_{j\ge1}\Floor{N/b^j}=(N-s_b(N))/(b-1)
\)
gives \eqref{eq:positive-count}; reflection gives \eqref{eq:total-count}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Thue--Morse consistency]
For \(b=2\),
\(
M_2(N)=2N-\BinaryDigitSum(N)
\), so
\[
 (-1)^{M_2(N)}=\ThueMorseSign{N},
 \qquad
 \ThueMorseSign{N}=(-1)^{\BinaryDigitSum(N)}.
\]
Since \(\Phi(0)>0\), the sign of \(\Phi\) on the lobe
\((N,N+1)\) is therefore the Thue--Morse sign.  This recovers, but does not duplicate as a novelty claim, the repository's formalized lobe-sign theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Complete Bernstein structure and a Thorin gamma dual}

\subsection{Stieltjes logarithmic derivative}

\begin{theorem}[Stieltjes and complete Bernstein structure]\label{thm:Bernstein}
For \(z>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Stieltjes-logderivative}
  \frac{A_b'(z)}{A_b(z)}
  =\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_b(m)}{z+\pi^2m^2}.
\end{equation}
Thus \(A_b'/A_b\) is a Stieltjes function and \(\log A_b\) is a complete Bernstein function on \((0,\infty)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Logarithmic differentiation of \eqref{eq:A-spectral-product} is justified locally uniformly away from the negative zeros and gives \eqref{eq:Stieltjes-logderivative}.  It is the Stieltjes transform of the positive discrete measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-measure}
  \rho_b:=\sum_{m\ge1}d_b(m)\,\delta_{\pi^2m^2}.
\end{equation}
A nonnegative function vanishing at zero whose derivative is Stieltjes is a complete Bernstein function; alternatively, sum the elementary complete Bernstein functions
\(d_b(m)\log(1+z/(\pi^2m^2))\); see \cite{Schilling2012}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Generalized gamma convolution}

\begin{theorem}[Thorin spectral law]\label{thm:GGC}
For every \(t>0\), \(A_b(z)^{-t}\) is the Laplace transform of the almost surely finite random series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ybt}
  Y_{b,t}:=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}G_{m,t},
  \qquad
  G_{m,t}\sim\operatorname{Gamma}
  \bigl(td_b(m),\text{ rate }\pi^2m^2\bigr),
\end{equation}
with independent summands.  Hence \(Y_{b,t}\) is a generalized gamma convolution with Thorin measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Thorin-measure}
  U_{b,t}=t\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}d_b(m)\,\delta_{\pi^2m^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Laplace transform of a gamma variable of shape \(\alpha\) and rate \(\lambda\) is
\((1+z/\lambda)^{-\alpha}\).  Independence and \eqref{eq:A-spectral-product} give
\[
 \Expectation e^{-zY_{b,t}}
 =\prod_{m\ge1}\left(1+\frac{z}{\pi^2m^2}\right)^{-td_b(m)}
 =A_b(z)^{-t}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \Expectation Y_{b,t}=t\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{d_b(m)}{\pi^2m^2}
 =t\,p_1^{(b)}<\infty,
\]
so the nonnegative series is finite almost surely.  The product is exactly the canonical Thorin representation in the theory of generalized gamma convolutions \cite{Bondesson1992,James2008}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cumulants]\label{cor:GGC-cumulants}
The cumulants of \(Y_{b,t}\) are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GGC-cumulants}
  \kappa_r(Y_{b,t})=t(r-1)!p_r^{(b)}.
\end{equation}
For \(b=2\),
\begin{equation}
  \Expectation Y_{2,t}=\frac{2t}{9},
  \qquad
  \Variance(Y_{2,t})=\frac{8t}{675}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use \eqref{eq:logA-series} in
\(
\log\Expectation e^{sY_{b,t}}=-t\log A_b(-s)
\)
and compare with the cumulant series.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scale self-decomposition]\label{cor:selfdecomp}
Let
\begin{equation}
  S(z):=\frac{\sinh\sqrt z}{\sqrt z}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-renewal}
  A_b(z)=S(z)A_b(z/b^2).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Y-selfdecomp}
  Y_{b,t}\stackrel{d}{=}Z_t+b^{-2}Y_{b,t}',
\end{equation}
where \(Y_{b,t}'\) is an independent copy and
\begin{equation}
  \Expectation e^{-zZ_t}=S(z)^{-t}
  =\prod_{n\ge1}\left(1+\frac{z}{\pi^2n^2}\right)^{-t}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Separate the \(j=0\) factor in \eqref{eq:A-scale-product}; then interpret the factors as Laplace transforms.
\end{proof}

\section{The arithmetic heat trace}

\subsection{Definition, q-renewal, and Malmsten representation}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Theta-definition}
  \Theta_b(x):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}d_b(m)e^{-\pi^2m^2x},
  \qquad x>0,
\end{equation}
and the ordinary positive theta tail
\begin{equation}
  \vartheta_+(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi^2n^2x}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact theta renewal]\label{prop:theta-renewal}
For every \(x>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Theta-theta-sum}
  \Theta_b(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\vartheta_+(b^{2j}x),
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Theta-q-difference}
  \Theta_b(x)-\Theta_b(b^2x)=\vartheta_+(x).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use
\(d_b(m)=\sum_{j\ge0}\IndicatorOf{b^j\mid m}\), then set \(m=b^jn\).  All terms
are nonnegative, so Tonelli's theorem applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Malmsten--L\'evy integral]\label{prop:Malmsten}
For \(z\ge0\),
\begin{align}
  \frac{A_b'(z)}{A_b(z)}
  &=\int_0^{\infty}e^{-zx}\Theta_b(x)\,\Differential x,
  \label{eq:logderivative-heat}\\
  \log A_b(z)
  &=\int_0^{\infty}\bigl(1-e^{-zx}\bigr)
    \frac{\Theta_b(x)}{x}\,\Differential x.
  \label{eq:Malmsten}
\end{align}
Thus the L\'evy density of \(Y_{b,t}\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Levy-density}
  \ell_{b,t}(x)=\frac{t}{x}\Theta_b(x).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Insert
\(
(z+\lambda)^{-1}=\int_0^\infty e^{-(z+\lambda)x}\Differential x
\)
in \eqref{eq:Stieltjes-logderivative}.  Integrating from \(0\) to \(z\), or using
\(
\log(1+z/\lambda)=\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-zx})e^{-\lambda x}\Differential x/x
\), gives \eqref{eq:Malmsten}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mellin transform and log-periodic skeleton}

\begin{theorem}[Mellin transform]\label{thm:Mellin-Theta}
For \(\Re s>1/2\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mellin-Theta}
  \int_0^{\infty}x^{s-1}\Theta_b(x)\,\Differential x
  =\Gamma(s)\pi^{-2s}\frac{\zeta(2s)}{1-b^{-2s}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Termwise Mellin transformation of \eqref{eq:Theta-definition} gives
\[
 \Gamma(s)\pi^{-2s}\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{d_b(m)}{m^{2s}},
\]
and \cref{prop:Dirichlet} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

Put \(L=\log b\) and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sk}
  s_k:=\frac{\pi ik}{L},\qquad k\in\IntegerNumbers\setminus\{0\}.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ck}
  c_{b,k}:=
  \frac{\Gamma(s_k)\pi^{-2s_k}\zeta(2s_k)}{2L},
\end{equation}
and the absolutely convergent one-periodic Fourier series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P-b}
  P_b(u):=2\Re\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{b,k}e^{2\pi iku}.
\end{equation}
Absolute convergence follows from the exponential decay of \(\Gamma(i y)\).

\begin{theorem}[Complete algebraic/log-periodic skeleton]\label{thm:heat-skeleton}
As \(x\downarrow0\), for every \(N>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat-asymptotic}
 \Theta_b(x)=\mathcal H_b(x)+O_N(x^N),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hb}
\boxed{
 \mathcal H_b(x)=
 \frac{b}{2(b-1)\sqrt{\pi x}}
 -\frac{\log(1/x)}{4\log b}
 -\frac14+\frac{\gamma-2\log2}{4\log b}
 +P_b\!\left(\frac{\log(1/x)}{2\log b}\right).
}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Mellin inversion of \eqref{eq:Mellin-Theta} starts on a vertical line; the harmonic-sum methodology is standard \cite{Flajolet1995}.
\(\Re s>1/2\).  The integrand has:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a simple pole at \(s=1/2\), with residue
  \(b/[2(b-1)\sqrt\pi]\);
  \item a double pole at \(s=0\);
  \item simple poles at \(s=s_k\), \(k\ne0\), from
  \(1-b^{-2s}=0\).
\end{itemize}
The poles of \(\Gamma(s)\) at negative integers are canceled by the trivial zeros of \(\zeta(2s)\).  Near zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mellin-zero-expansion}
 \Gamma(s)\pi^{-2s}\frac{\zeta(2s)}{1-b^{-2s}}
 =-\frac1{4L}\frac1{s^2}
 +\left[-\frac14+\frac{\gamma-2\log2}{4L}\right]\frac1s+O(1).
\end{equation}
Multiplication by \(x^{-s}\) supplies the logarithmic term in \eqref{eq:Hb}.  The nonzero imaginary poles supply \eqref{eq:P-b}.  Shifting the contour arbitrarily far left gives \(O_N(x^N)\), since no further poles remain and \(\Gamma(s)\) decays exponentially on vertical lines.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact beyond-all-orders remainder}

The all-orders estimate in \cref{thm:heat-skeleton} conceals a simpler exact identity.

\begin{theorem}[Jacobi-dual transseries]\label{thm:exact-heat-remainder}
For every \(x>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-heat-remainder}
\boxed{
 \Theta_b(x)=\mathcal H_b(x)
 -\frac1{\sqrt{\pi x}}
  \sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b^j
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
  \exp\!\left(-\frac{b^{2j}n^2}{x}\right).
}
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\Theta_b(x)<\mathcal H_b(x)\) for every \(x>0\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leading-flat}
 \mathcal H_b(x)-\Theta_b(x)
 \sim\frac{b}{\sqrt{\pi x}}e^{-b^2/x}
 \qquad(x\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
More precisely, the relative error in \eqref{eq:leading-flat} is
\(O(e^{-c_b/x})\), where
\begin{equation}
  c_b=\min\{3b^2,b^4-b^2\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Jacobi inversion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Jacobi-positive}
 \vartheta_+(x)=
 \frac1{2\sqrt{\pi x}}-\frac12
 +\frac1{\sqrt{\pi x}}
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-n^2/x}.
\end{equation}
A direct calculation from \eqref{eq:Hb}, using the one-periodicity of \(P_b\), shows
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_b(x)-\mathcal H_b(b^2x)
 =\frac1{2\sqrt{\pi x}}-\frac12.
\end{equation}
Let \(R_b=\Theta_b-\mathcal H_b\).  Combining this identity with
\eqref{eq:Theta-q-difference} and \eqref{eq:Jacobi-positive} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-renewal}
 R_b(x)-R_b(b^2x)=
 \frac1{\sqrt{\pi x}}\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-n^2/x}.
\end{equation}
Replace \(x\) by \(x/b^2\) and iterate toward zero:
\[
 R_b(x)=R_b(x/b^{2J})-
 \frac1{\sqrt{\pi x}}
 \sum_{j=1}^{J}b^j\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-b^{2j}n^2/x}.
\]
By \cref{thm:heat-skeleton}, \(R_b(y)\to0\) faster than every power as
\(y\downarrow0\).  Letting \(J\to\infty\) proves \eqref{eq:exact-heat-remainder}.  The leading term is \((j,n)=(1,1)\); the next exponential action is
\(\min(4b^2,b^4)\), giving the stated relative error.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Lower-Lambert inversion of the heat defect]\label{cor:Lambert-heat}
Let
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_b(x):=\mathcal H_b(x)-\Theta_b(x).
\end{equation}
For any asymptotic inverse branch as \(r\downarrow0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambert-heat-inverse}
  \Delta_b^{-1}(r)
  \sim
  \frac{2b^2}{-W_{-1}(-2\pi r^2)}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Square \eqref{eq:leading-flat} and put \(y=2b^2/x\):
\[
 r^2\sim\frac{b^2}{\pi x}e^{-2b^2/x}
 =\frac{y}{2\pi}e^{-y}.
\]
The relevant large positive solution is
\(y\sim-W_{-1}(-2\pi r^2)\), using the lower real branch described in \cite{Corless1996}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The same lower Lambert branch governs the inverse-Fabius endpoint theory, but it arises here from a different mechanism: Jacobi-dual exponential action rather than the original Fabius saddle.  This provides a concrete route for transferring the repository's Lambert phase and phase-locked extrapolation machinery to spectral heat defects.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact modular duality for the infinite sinc moment product}

\subsection{Laplace transform of the heat identity}

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-constant}
 B_b:=-\frac14+\frac{\gamma-2\log2}{4\log b}.
\end{equation}
The Laplace transform of \eqref{eq:exact-heat-remainder} can be evaluated term by term.  The elementary integrals are
\begin{align}
 \int_0^\infty e^{-zx}x^{-1/2}\,\Differential x&=\sqrt\pi z^{-1/2},\\
 \int_0^\infty e^{-zx}\log(1/x)\,\Differential x&=\frac{\log z+\gamma}{z},\\
 \int_0^\infty x^{-1/2}e^{-zx-a^2/x}\,\Differential x
 &=\sqrt{\frac\pi z}\,e^{-2a\sqrt z}.
\end{align}
For \(s_k\) from \eqref{eq:sk}, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qbk}
  q_{b,k}:=-c_{b,k}\Gamma(-s_k)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qb}
  Q_b(v):=2\Re\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_{b,k}e^{2\pi ikv}.
\end{equation}
This Fourier series is absolutely convergent.

\begin{theorem}[Exact modular-dual formula]\label{thm:modular-dual}
There exists a real constant \(C_b\) such that, for every \(z>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modular-dual}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \log A_b(z)
 &=\frac{b}{b-1}\sqrt z
 -\frac{(\log z)^2}{8\log b}
 -\left(\frac14+\frac{\log2}{2\log b}\right)\log z\\
 &\quad+C_b
 +Q_b\!\left(\frac{\log z}{2\log b}\right)
 -\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\log\!\left(1-e^{-2b^j\sqrt z}\right).
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
One rapidly convergent definition of the constant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cb-definition}
 C_b=\log A_b(1)-\frac{b}{b-1}-Q_b(0)
 +\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\log(1-e^{-2b^j}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:logderivative-heat}, Laplace transformation of the skeleton in
\eqref{eq:Hb} gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac{\Differential}{\Differential z}\log A_b(z)
 &=\frac{b}{2(b-1)}z^{-1/2}
 -\frac{\log z+\gamma}{4(\log b)z}
 +\frac{B_b}{z}\\
 &\quad+2\Re\sum_{k\ge1}c_{b,k}\Gamma(1-s_k)z^{s_k-1}\\
 &\quad-z^{-1/2}\sum_{j\ge1}
 \frac{b^j}{e^{2b^j\sqrt z}-1}.
\end{align*}
Integrating in \(z\) gives the first line of \eqref{eq:modular-dual}; the logarithmic coefficient simplifies because
\[
 B_b-\frac{\gamma}{4\log b}
 =-\frac14-\frac{\log2}{2\log b}.
\]
Furthermore,
\(
\Gamma(1-s_k)/s_k=-\Gamma(-s_k)
\), producing \eqref{eq:qbk}.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{\Differential}{\Differential z}
 \left[-\log(1-e^{-2a\sqrt z})\right]
 =-\frac{a}{\sqrt z\,(e^{2a\sqrt z}-1)}.
\]
Thus the last derivative integrates to the lacunary logarithmic product.  The remaining integration constant is fixed at \(z=1\), giving \eqref{eq:Cb-definition}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A partition product hidden in the sinc product}

Let \(q=e^{-2\sqrt z}\).  The final factor in \eqref{eq:modular-dual} exponentiates to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lacunary-partition}
  \prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{b^j})^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This is the ordinary generating function for partitions into parts from
\(\{b,b^2,b^3,\ldots\}\).  In the dyadic case it is the binary-partition product with the part \(1\) omitted.  Hence the Rvachev moment product has an exact modular dual in which an infinite sinc convolution is paired with a lacunary partition product.

\begin{corollary}[Large-\(z\) transseries]\label{cor:large-z}
Uniformly for \(z\to+\infty\),
\begin{align}
 \log A_b(z)
 &=\frac{b}{b-1}\sqrt z
 -\frac{(\log z)^2}{8\log b}
 -\left(\frac14+\frac{\log2}{2\log b}\right)\log z
 +C_b\\
 &\quad+Q_b\!\left(\frac{\log z}{2\log b}\right)
 +e^{-2b\sqrt z}+O(e^{-4b\sqrt z}+e^{-2b^2\sqrt z}).
 \label{eq:large-z-transseries}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Expand the last logarithm in \eqref{eq:modular-dual} as
\(-\log(1-w)=w+O(w^2)\).  The first action is \(j=1\), with
\(w=e^{-2b\sqrt z}\).
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences for computation and approximation}

\subsection{Certified Stieltjes--Pad\'e control}

Because \(A_b'/A_b\) is a Stieltjes function, its diagonal and subdiagonal Pad\'e approximants have negative real poles and positive residues, and they form the classical alternating upper/lower bounds on the positive axis.  This yields a new certified route to the saddle equations that occur in the Fabius endpoint theory:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item compute the exact rational coefficients \(p_r^{(b)}\) from Bernoulli numbers;
  \item form Stieltjes Pad\'e approximants to \(A_b'/A_b\);
  \item integrate the rational bounds to bracket \(\log A_b\);
  \item transfer the bounds through the monotone saddle map and, when needed, through the lower Lambert branch.
\end{enumerate}
Unlike an unconstrained power-series Pad\'e computation, every approximant respects the correct negative spectral axis.

\subsection{Moment cones and Legendre expansions}

Every even Legendre coefficient of \(\RvachevUp\) is a finite rational linear combination of
\(\mu_0,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{2n}\).  The \(\PF\) constraints and the Jensen hyperbolicity hierarchy therefore place the admissible moment vectors in a nested family of explicit determinantal cones.  This does not by itself determine the sign of each Legendre coefficient, but it supplies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item rigorous conditioning inequalities for the moment-to-Legendre triangular transform;
  \item determinant certificates that can accompany the repository's exact Legendre-energy calculations;
  \item hyperbolic-polynomial barriers for moment truncation and rational reconstruction.
\end{itemize}
A promising next step is to combine these cones with the existing shifted-up synthesis of Legendre polynomials.

\subsection{Inverse-Fabius transfer}

The new formulas enter inverse-Fabius analysis in three ways.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \cref{cor:Lambert-heat} gives a second, independent lower-
  \(W\) inversion problem with the same logarithmic anatomy as the endpoint inverse of \(F\).
  \item The exact periodic functions \(P_b\) and \(Q_b\) have period
  \(2\log b\) in the raw logarithmic variable, reflecting the quadratic spectral lift
  \(m\mapsto\pi^2m^2\).  They can be treated by the repository's phase-locked Richardson filters after a period rescaling.
  \item The Stieltjes bounds above can replace floating-point evaluation of the sinc product in saddle iterations by certified rational inequalities.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conjectures and frontier problems}

\begin{conjecture}[Differential transcendence]\label{conj:difftrans}
For every integer \(b\ge2\), the functions \(\Phi_b\) and \(A_b\) are differentially transcendental over \(\ComplexNumbers(z)\).
\end{conjecture}

\noindent
\emph{Evidence.}  \cref{thm:nonDfinite} excludes linear differential equations.  The exact divisor has unbounded arithmetic multiplicities, while the scale relation
\(A_b(z)=S(z)A_b(z/b^2)\) couples differential and Mahler-type structures.  Non-D-finiteness alone is not enough---for example, some familiar meromorphic functions are differentially algebraic---so a proof will require a genuinely nonlinear obstruction, perhaps through Nevanlinna theory for differential equations with a prescribed divisor.

\begin{conjecture}[Strict total positivity after forced zeros]\label{conj:strictPF}
Every Toeplitz minor of \((a_n^{(b)})\) that is not forced to vanish by the zero convention \(a_n=0\) for \(n<0\) is strictly positive.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent
Finite truncations have strictly positive spectral parameters, but repeated zeros in the generating polynomial make a direct limiting argument delicate.  A proof would strengthen variation diminution to strict sign-regularity.

\begin{conjecture}[Hermite universality of Jensen polynomials]\label{conj:Hermite}
For each fixed degree \(d\), an affine normalization of
\(J_{d,n}^{(2)}\) converges coefficientwise to the degree-
\(d\) Hermite polynomial as \(n\to\infty\).
\end{conjecture}

\noindent
The repository's all-order endpoint asymptotics should give the required regular expansion of
\(\log\gamma_n\).  Establishing the Griffin--Ono--Rolen--Zagier hypotheses \cite{GORZ2019} would convert the exact hyperbolicity theorem into a universal limiting shape theorem.

\begin{conjecture}[Closed form for \(C_b\)]\label{conj:Cb}
The constant \(C_b\) in \eqref{eq:modular-dual} can be expressed using a base-
\(b\) Glaisher--Kinkelin/Barnes-type constant and elementary combinations of
\(\log b\), \(\log(2\pi)\), and zeta derivatives at zero.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent
The double Mellin pole explains the quadratic logarithm, while \(C_b\) is the finite part left by the same spectral determinant.  A zeta-regularized determinant of the spectral multiset
\[
 \bigl\{\pi^2m^2:m\ge1,\ \text{with multiplicity }d_b(m)\bigr\}
\]
is the natural candidate.

\begin{problem}[Nonintegral geometric bases]
For real \(\beta>1\), the same summable real-zero product and compact-support argument already show that
\[
 \Phi_\beta(z)=\prod_{j\ge0}\SincRad(\pi z/\beta^j)
\]
belongs to the Laguerre--P\'olya and Cartwright classes.  Classify instead its D-finiteness, zero-collision multiplicities, exact counting fluctuations, and heat-trace periodicity.  The zero set is the union \(\{\pm n\beta^j\}\), and collisions depend on multiplicative relations among powers of \(\beta\).  The integer-base proof by unbounded multiplicity no longer applies generically.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Orthogonal-polynomial asymptotics]
Use the known endpoint flatness of \(\RvachevUp\) to place it precisely in the Szeg\H{o} class and determine the first correction to the Jacobi recurrence coefficients.  The log-squared endpoint profile suggests a slowly varying, non-Fisher--Hartwig correction rather than a pure power law.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Lean formalization]
A realistic formalization sequence is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item derive \eqref{eq:canonical-Phi-b} from the existing sinc canonical product and weighted-scale multiplicity API;
  \item prove the D-finite multiplicity lemma in an abstract analytic ODE setting;
  \item formalize \eqref{eq:Dirichlet-db} and the exact digital count;
  \item define \(\Theta_b\), prove \eqref{eq:Theta-theta-sum}, and import a Jacobi theta transformation;
  \item prove the exact remainder \eqref{eq:exact-heat-remainder};
  \item formalize the Stieltjes/Laplace identities and the gamma-series probability construction.
\end{enumerate}
The LP/PF/Jensen portion will require more library infrastructure than the heat-trace and non-D-finite portions.
\end{problem}

\section{Summary of the new theorem network}

The report's deductions can be read as two interacting chains.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Entire-function chain:}
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{integer divisor}
\Longrightarrow \LP/\LPI
\Longrightarrow \PF\text{ moments}\\
\Longrightarrow \text{Jensen hyperbolicity}
\Longrightarrow \text{Tur\'an inequalities}.
\end{gathered}
\]
while the same unbounded multiplicities imply
\[
\text{integer divisor}
\Longrightarrow \text{non-D-finite}
\Longrightarrow \text{non-P-recursive moments}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Spectral-probability chain:}
\[
\begin{gathered}
\frac{A_b'}{A_b}\text{ Stieltjes}
\Longrightarrow \log A_b\text{ complete Bernstein}\\
\Longrightarrow A_b^{-t}\text{ GGC}
\Longrightarrow \Theta_b\text{ L\'evy heat trace}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Then
\[
\begin{gathered}
\Theta_b\text{ Mellin poles}
\Longrightarrow \text{log-periodic skeleton}\\
\overset{\text{Jacobi}}{\Longrightarrow}
\text{exact flat remainder}
\overset{\text{Laplace}}{\Longrightarrow}
\text{lacunary partition duality}.
\end{gathered}
\]
These results are structurally compatible with the repository's q-series, Bell--Bernoulli, Thue--Morse, Lambert--\(W\), inverse-Fabius, and Legendre programs, but they are not restatements of the current documents.
