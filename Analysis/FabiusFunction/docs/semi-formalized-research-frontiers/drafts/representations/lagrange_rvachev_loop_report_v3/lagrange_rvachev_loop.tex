\begin{abstract}
Let $u=\RvachevUp$ denote Rvachev's even $C^\infty$ probability density supported on
$[-1,1]$.  The repository already contains two ingredients that at first look
like opposite transformations: exact finite synthesis of every polynomial on a
compact interval from shifted or shifted-and-dilated copies of $u$, and an
absolutely and uniformly convergent Fourier--Legendre series
$u=\sum_{n\ge0}u_nP_{2n}$.  The present report makes the missing basis change
explicit and closes the loop.

For arbitrary distinct interpolation nodes $\Xi=\{\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_d\}\subset
[-1,1]$, with Lagrange cardinal polynomials $\ell_j$, put $M=2^d$ and let
$\Dscr=\MM_u(D)^{-1}$ be moment deconvolution.  We prove the literal-shift
formula
\[
 \ell_j(x)=\frac1M\sum_{|k|<2M}(\Dscr\ell_j)(k/M)\,u(x-k/M),
 \qquad -1\le x\le1.
\]
The coefficient matrix $B$ is an exact right inverse of the up-shift
collocation matrix $U$: $UB=I$.  This produces an oblique projector $BU$, a
Vandermonde--Appell transmutation, and a basis-independent factorization
$B=DV^{-1}$.  At Gauss--Legendre nodes it becomes a deconvolved
Christoffel--Darboux formula
\[
 B_{kj}=\frac{\omega_j}{M}K_d^\sharp(k/M,\xi_j),
 \qquad
 K_d^\sharp(t,y)=\sum_{n=0}^d\frac{2n+1}{2}Q_n(t)P_n(y),
\]
where $Q_n=\Dscr P_n$.  A weighted singular-value identity isolates all
conditioning in the deconvolved Legendre block.  In the repository's minimal
$d+2$-atom endpoint dictionary, the complementary projector is proved to be
rank one with an explicit cofactor factorization; functionally, every local
up-spline is its nodal Lagrange interpolant plus one scalar multiple of a unique
nodal-defect spline.

Substitution into the Legendre expansion closes the loop in blockwise form:
\[
 u(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{u_n}{4^n}
 \sum_{|k|<2\cdot4^n}Q_{2n}(k/4^n)u(x-k/4^n),
\]
with absolute and uniform convergence when each finite inner sum is kept as a
block.  The report then proves that this block structure is essential.  If
$C=\MM_u(\pi\ImaginaryUnit)$, then
\[
 Q_{2n}(0)=\frac{2(-1)^n}{C}\frac{(4n)!}{(2n)!(4\pi)^{2n}}
 \left(1+\frac{2\pi^2}{4n-1}+\BigO(n^{-2})\right),
\]
so $|Q_{2n}(0)|^{1/(2n)}\sim2n/(\pi\EulerE)$.  Since $u$ is flat and nonanalytic at
its endpoints, the central-atom coefficients $u_nQ_{2n}(0)/4^n$ are unbounded
in absolute value.  Thus grouping the repeated central copies $u(x)$ is
impossible, and the flattened atom series is not absolute or unconditional;
small Legendre blocks arise from increasingly violent internal cancellation.
Exact symbolic computations, FFT checks through degree $10$, and an 80-digit
minimal-dictionary certificate accompany the report.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Lagrange is not Legendre}

The prompt deliberately puts two easily confused names in one sentence.
Throughout this report:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Lagrange polynomials} are the cardinal interpolation polynomials
  $\ell_j$ associated with arbitrary distinct nodes $\xi_j\in[-1,1]$;
  \item \emph{Legendre polynomials} are the orthogonal polynomials $P_n$ with
  $P_n(1)=1$.
\end{itemize}
The repository's existing convergent series for $u$ is a Fourier--Legendre
series, not a series in Lagrange cardinal functions.  The loop therefore has
three legs:
\[
 \text{nodal data}
 \longleftrightarrow \text{Lagrange cardinals}
 \longleftrightarrow \text{Legendre coordinates}
 \longleftrightarrow \text{finite up-atom coefficients}.
\]
Gauss--Legendre nodes are the natural point at which all three coordinate
systems meet without approximation.

\section{Imported foundations and notation}
\label{sec:foundations}

\subsection{The up density, Fourier product, and moment generating function}

Let $u=\RvachevUp$ be the even nonnegative $C^\infty$ function supported on $[-1,1]$
and normalized by $\int_{-1}^1u(x)\Differential x=1$.  We use the repository's Fourier
convention
\[
 \widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\RealNumbers}f(x)\EulerE^{-2\pi\ImaginaryUnit\xi x}\Differential x,
 \qquad
 \widehat u(\xi)=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\SincPi(\xi/2^j),
 \quad
 \SincPi(t)=\frac{\sin(\pi t)}{\pi t}.
\]
At every nonzero integer $m$,
\[
 \ord_{\xi=m}\widehat u(\xi)=1+\TwoAdicValuation(m).
\]
Equivalently, if $V_j$ are independent uniform random variables on $[-1,1]$,
then
\[
 Z=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}V_j
\]
has density $u$.  Its moment generating function is
\begin{equation}
 \MM_u(z)=\Expectation\EulerE^{zZ}
 =\int_{-1}^1\EulerE^{zx}u(x)\Differential x
 =\prod_{j\ge1}\frac{\sinh(z/2^j)}{z/2^j}.
 \label{eq:mgf}
\end{equation}
Write
\begin{equation}
 \log\MM_u(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\kappa_r\frac{z^r}{r!},
 \qquad
 \kappa_{2r}=\frac{B_{2r}}{2r(1-4^{-r})},
 \qquad \kappa_{2r+1}=0.
 \label{eq:cumulants}
\end{equation}
The Bernoulli numbers therefore control every polynomial coefficient appearing
below.

\subsection{Reciprocal MGF and the Rvachev--Appell family}

Define reciprocal coefficients $\gamma_r$ by
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{\MM_u(z)}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\gamma_r\frac{z^r}{r!}.
 \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
All odd $\gamma_r$ vanish.  On polynomials the formal differential operator
\begin{equation}
 \Dscr:=\MM_u(D)^{-1}
 =\sum_{r\ge0}\gamma_r\frac{D^r}{r!}
 \label{eq:deconv}
\end{equation}
terminates exactly.  Its Appell sequence is
\begin{equation}
 A_n(x):=\Dscr x^n,
 \qquad
 \frac{\EulerE^{xz}}{\MM_u(z)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_n(x)\frac{z^n}{n!}.
 \label{eq:appell}
\end{equation}
In complete Bell-polynomial notation,
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_n=Y_n(0,-\kappa_2,0,-\kappa_4,\ldots,-\kappa_n),
 \qquad
 A_n(x)=Y_n(x,-\kappa_2,0,-\kappa_4,\ldots,-\kappa_n).
 \label{eq:bell-form}
\end{equation}
Thus every coefficient in this report is a finite Bell polynomial in Bernoulli
numbers.

\subsection{Exact polynomial synthesis already available in the corpus}

The basic imported theorem is the following.

\begin{theorem}[Global exact synthesis; imported]
\label{thm:imported-global}
Let $P$ have degree at most $d$, let $M=2^d$, and put
\[
 Q=\MM_u(MD)^{-1}P.
\]
Then, globally on $\RealNumbers$,
\begin{equation}
 P(t)=\frac1M\sum_{k\in\IntegerNumbers}Q(k)
 u\!\left(\frac{t-k}{M}\right),
 \label{eq:global-synthesis}
\end{equation}
where the sum is locally finite.
\end{theorem}

The physical-scale form will be more convenient for literal, unscaled
translates on $[-1,1]$.

\begin{theorem}[Physical-scale interval synthesis; imported]
\label{thm:physical}
Let $p\in\PiD$, let $I=[A,B]$, choose a spacing $h>0$, an origin $x_0$, and an
integer ratio $m$ satisfying $\TwoAdicValuation(m)\ge d$.  Put $\rho=mh$ and
$p_\rho^\sharp=\MM_u(\rho D)^{-1}p$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 p(x)=\frac1m
 \sum_{k=\Ceiling{(A-x_0)/h-m}}^{\Floor{(B-x_0)/h+m}}
 p_\rho^\sharp(x_0+kh)
 u\!\left(\frac{x-x_0-kh}{\rho}\right),
 \qquad x\in[A,B].
 \label{eq:physical}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof in the corpus is a Poisson-summation collapse: the exact integer-zero
multiplicities annihilate all aliases through degree $d$, while
$\MM_u(\rho D)^{-1}$ reverses the remaining zero-frequency smoothing.

\section{Finite literal-shift synthesis of Lagrange cardinal polynomials}
\label{sec:lagrange-synthesis}

\subsection{The main cardinal formula}

Fix pairwise distinct nodes
\[
 \Xi=\{\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_d\}\subset[-1,1]
\]
and define the Lagrange cardinal polynomials
\begin{equation}
 \ell_j(x)=\prod_{\substack{0\le r\le d\\r\ne j}}
 \frac{x-\xi_r}{\xi_j-\xi_r},
 \qquad
 \ell_j(\xi_i)=\delta_{ij}.
 \label{eq:lagrange-cardinal}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Literal shifted-up representation of Lagrange cardinals]
\label{thm:literal-lagrange}
Let $M=2^d$.  For every $0\le j\le d$ and every $x\in[-1,1]$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \ell_j(x)=\frac1M\sum_{|k|<2M}
 (\Dscr\ell_j)(k/M)\,u(x-k/M).}
 \label{eq:literal-lagrange}
\end{equation}
All atoms are literal translates of the same unscaled function $u$; only their
centers $k/M$ and scalar coefficients vary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:physical} with $A=-1$, $B=1$, $x_0=0$, $m=M$, $h=M^{-1}$,
and hence $\rho=1$.  The deconvolved polynomial is $\Dscr\ell_j$.  The formal
limits include $k=\pm2M$, but for $x\in[-1,1]$ those terms evaluate $u$ only at
points outside $(-1,1)$, including the zero endpoints, so they vanish
identically.  Removing them gives \eqref{eq:literal-lagrange}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Atom count versus data complexity]
The formula displays $4M-1=2^{d+2}-1$ atoms, but the coefficient vector is not
independent data: it is obtained by sampling one degree-$d$ polynomial
$\Dscr\ell_j$.  Thus its exact description still requires only $\BigO(d)$
field elements.  The later endpoint compression reduces the displayed atom
count to $d+2$ at the cost of a very large common dilation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Closed coefficients through symmetric functions and Appell polynomials}

Put
\[
 \lambda_j=\left(\prod_{r\ne j}(\xi_j-\xi_r)\right)^{-1},
\]
and let $e_s^{(j)}$ be the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree $s$ in
the $d$ nodes $\{\xi_r:r\ne j\}$.  Then
\[
 \ell_j(x)=\lambda_j\sum_{n=0}^{d}(-1)^{d-n}e_{d-n}^{(j)}x^n.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Appell form of every cardinal coefficient]
\label{prop:appell-cardinal}
For all $t\in\RealNumbers$,
\begin{align}
 (\Dscr\ell_j)(t)
 &=\sum_{r=0}^{\Floor{d/2}}
   \gamma_{2r}\frac{\ell_j^{(2r)}(t)}{(2r)!}
 \label{eq:derivative-cardinal}\\
 &=\lambda_j\sum_{n=0}^{d}(-1)^{d-n}e_{d-n}^{(j)}A_n(t).
 \label{eq:appell-cardinal}
\end{align}
Consequently, every coefficient in \eqref{eq:literal-lagrange} is an explicit
rational combination of complete Bell polynomials in the Bernoulli cumulants
whenever the nodes are rational.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:derivative-cardinal} is the terminating operator
\eqref{eq:deconv}; odd terms vanish.  Apply $\Dscr x^n=A_n(x)$ to the power
expansion of $\ell_j$ to obtain \eqref{eq:appell-cardinal}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A complete degree-two example}

For nodes $(-1,0,1)$,
\[
 \ell_-(x)=\frac{x^2-x}{2},\qquad
 \ell_0(x)=1-x^2,\qquad
 \ell_+(x)=\frac{x^2+x}{2}.
\]
Because $\gamma_2=-1/9$,
\begin{align*}
 \Dscr\ell_-(x)&=\frac{x^2-x-1/9}{2},\\
 \Dscr\ell_0(x)&=\frac{10}{9}-x^2,\\
 \Dscr\ell_+(x)&=\frac{x^2+x-1/9}{2}.
\end{align*}
With $M=4$, each cardinal is therefore a sum over the fifteen centers
$k/4$, $-7\le k\le7$.  No numerical linear system is being solved: all
coefficients are exact rational values of the displayed quadratics.

\section{The exact matrix loop: right inverses, projectors, and moments}
\label{sec:matrix}

\subsection{Collocation and synthesis matrices}

Let
\[
 \mathcal K_d=\{k\in\IntegerNumbers:|k|<2M\},
 \qquad q_d=|\mathcal K_d|=4M-1.
\]
Define
\begin{equation}
 U_{ik}=u(\xi_i-k/M),
 \qquad
 B_{kj}=\frac1M(\Dscr\ell_j)(k/M),
 \label{eq:UB-def}
\end{equation}
where rows of $U$ are indexed by nodes and columns by $k\in\mathcal K_d$.
Thus $U\in\RealNumbers^{(d+1)\times q_d}$ and $B\in\RealNumbers^{q_d\times(d+1)}$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact nodal closure and oblique projector; new here]
\label{thm:UB}
The matrices in \eqref{eq:UB-def} satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{UB=I_{d+1}.}
 \label{eq:UB}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P:=BU,
 \qquad
 \mathcal P^2=\mathcal P,
 \qquad
 \RankOperator\mathcal P=\tr\mathcal P=d+1,
 \label{eq:oblique-projector}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{range}\mathcal P=\operatorname{range}B,
 \qquad
 \ker\mathcal P=\ker U.
 \label{eq:projector-subspaces}
\end{equation}
In general $\mathcal P$ is oblique rather than orthogonal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Evaluate \eqref{eq:literal-lagrange} at $x=\xi_i$.  The result is
$(UB)_{ij}=\ell_j(\xi_i)=\delta_{ij}$.  All remaining identities follow from
$UB=I$: $\mathcal P^2=B(UB)U=BU$, the range and kernel assertions are standard,
and the nonzero eigenvalues of an idempotent are all $1$.
\end{proof}

This matrix identity gives the precise finite-dimensional meaning of
``closing the Lagrange loop'':
\[
 \boxed{
 \text{nodal values}
 \xrightarrow{\ B\ }
 \text{up-shift coefficients}
 \xrightarrow{\ U\ }
 \text{the same nodal values}.}
\]

\subsection{Partition, Appell moments, and Cauchy--Binet identities}

Let $\bm 1$ denote an all-ones vector and write
$\bm\xi^{\circ r}=(\xi_0^r,\ldots,\xi_d^r)^T$.

\begin{proposition}[Vandermonde--Appell transmutation; new here]
\label{prop:moment-transmutation}
For $0\le r\le d$,
\begin{equation}
 B\bm 1=\frac1M\bm 1,
 \qquad
 B\bm\xi^{\circ r}
 =\frac1M\bigl(A_r(k/M)\bigr)_{k\in\mathcal K_d}.
 \label{eq:moment-transmutation}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 1=\det(UB)=
 \sum_{\substack{S\subset\mathcal K_d\\|S|=d+1}}
 \det U_{:,S}\,\det B_{S,:}.
 \label{eq:cauchy-binet}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The nodal vector $\bm\xi^{\circ r}$ interpolates the polynomial $x^r$ exactly.
Its deconvolution is $A_r$, so \eqref{eq:moment-transmutation} follows from
\cref{thm:literal-lagrange}.  The first identity is the case $r=0$.
Equation \eqref{eq:cauchy-binet} is the Cauchy--Binet formula applied to
$UB=I$.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{eq:cauchy-binet} is a nontrivial determinant partition of
unity over all square subdictionaries.  It can be used to compare sparse
choices of centers without recomputing the interpolation problem itself.

\subsection{Any polynomial basis fits the same triangle}

Let $p_0,\ldots,p_d$ be any basis of $\PiD$ and define
\begin{equation}
 V_{in}=p_n(\xi_i),
 \qquad
 D_{kn}=\frac1M(\Dscr p_n)(k/M).
 \label{eq:VD-def}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Basis-independent factorization; new here]
\label{thm:basis-factorization}
With \eqref{eq:VD-def},
\begin{equation}
 UD=V,
 \qquad
 B=DV^{-1},
 \qquad
 D=BV.
 \label{eq:basis-factorization}
\end{equation}
Thus the Lagrange-to-up transform is exactly the composition of a standard
basis change $V^{-1}$ and basis-polynomial deconvolution/sampling $D$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The $n$th column of $UD$ is the vector obtained by evaluating the exact up
synthesis of $p_n$ at all nodes, hence it equals the $n$th column of $V$.
The columns of $V^{-1}$ are the coordinates of the cardinal polynomials in the
basis $p_n$, so applying $D$ gives $B=DV^{-1}$.  Multiplication by $V$ gives
the last identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{algorithm}[Exact cardinal synthesis without expanding every cardinal]
\label{alg:cardinal}
Given nodes $\xi_i$ and a numerically or symbolically convenient polynomial
basis $p_n$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item form the small $(d+1)\times(d+1)$ Vandermonde-type matrix $V$;
  \item compute the $q_d\times(d+1)$ table
  $D_{kn}=M^{-1}(\Dscr p_n)(k/M)$;
  \item output $B=DV^{-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
For Gauss--Legendre nodes, \cref{sec:gauss} removes the general matrix inverse
entirely.
\end{algorithm}

\section{Gauss--Legendre nodes and a deconvolved Christoffel--Darboux kernel}
\label{sec:gauss}

\subsection{Deconvolved Legendre polynomials}

Set
\begin{equation}
 Q_n(x):=\Dscr P_n(x).
 \label{eq:Q-def}
\end{equation}
Using the standard power expansion of $P_n$ gives an explicit Appell formula.

\begin{proposition}[Appell expansion and parity]
\label{prop:Q-expansion}
For every $n\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 Q_n(x)=2^{-n}\sum_{r=0}^{\Floor{n/2}}(-1)^r
 \frac{(2n-2r)!}{r!(n-r)!(n-2r)!}\,A_{n-2r}(x).
 \label{eq:Q-expansion}
\end{equation}
In particular, $Q_n(-x)=(-1)^nQ_n(x)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply $\Dscr x^m=A_m(x)$ termwise to
\[
 P_n(x)=2^{-n}\sum_{r=0}^{\Floor{n/2}}(-1)^r
 \frac{(2n-2r)!}{r!(n-r)!(n-2r)!}x^{n-2r}.
\]
Parity follows because $\Dscr$ contains only even derivatives.
\end{proof}

The first few $Q_n$ were displayed in \cref{sec:foundations}.  Unlike the
Legendre family, they are not orthogonal; they are the exact coefficient
polynomials that undo convolution by the up law.

\subsection{A nonlocal three-term recurrence}

Define the odd differential symbol
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda(z):=-\frac{\MM_u'(z)}{\MM_u(z)}
 =-\sum_{r\ge1}\kappa_{2r}\frac{z^{2r-1}}{(2r-1)!}.
 \label{eq:Lambda}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Deconvolved Legendre recurrence; new here]
\label{prop:Q-recurrence}
For $n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 (n+1)Q_{n+1}
 =(2n+1)\bigl[xQ_n+\Lambda(D)Q_n\bigr]-nQ_{n-1}.}
 \label{eq:Q-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 (n+1)Q_{n+1}
 =(2n+1)\left[xQ_n-
 \sum_{r\ge1}\kappa_{2r}\frac{Q_n^{(2r-1)}}{(2r-1)!}\right]-nQ_{n-1},
 \label{eq:Q-recurrence-expanded}
\end{equation}
where the derivative sum terminates.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any formal power series $g$, the operator commutator is
$g(D)x=xg(D)+g'(D)$.  Taking $g=1/\MM_u$ gives
\[
 \Dscr(xf)=x\Dscr f+\frac{g'(D)}{g(D)}\Dscr f
 =x\Dscr f+\Lambda(D)\Dscr f.
\]
Apply $\Dscr$ to the classical Legendre recurrence
$(n+1)P_{n+1}=(2n+1)xP_n-nP_{n-1}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The kernel theorem}

Let $\xi_0,\ldots,\xi_d$ now be the zeros of $P_{d+1}$ and let
$\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_d$ be the Gauss--Legendre weights.  Write
\begin{equation}
 h_n=\int_{-1}^{1}P_n(x)^2\Differential x=\frac{2}{2n+1},
 \qquad
 K_d(x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{d}\frac{P_n(x)P_n(y)}{h_n}.
 \label{eq:CD-kernel}
\end{equation}
Define its deconvolution in the first variable by
\begin{equation}
 K_d^\sharp(t,y)
 :=\mathscr D_{u,t}K_d(t,y)
 =\sum_{n=0}^{d}\frac{Q_n(t)P_n(y)}{h_n}.
 \label{eq:deconv-CD}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Deconvolved Christoffel--Darboux synthesis; new here]
\label{thm:CD-synthesis}
Let $M=2^d$.  For $x,y\in[-1,1]$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 K_d(x,y)=\frac1M\sum_{|k|<2M}
 K_d^\sharp(k/M,y)u(x-k/M).}
 \label{eq:CD-synthesis}
\end{equation}
The Gauss cardinal polynomials satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \ell_j(x)=\omega_jK_d(x,\xi_j),
 \label{eq:gauss-cardinal-kernel}
\end{equation}
and therefore their up coefficients are
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 B_{kj}=\frac{\omega_j}{M}K_d^\sharp(k/M,\xi_j).}
 \label{eq:B-kernel}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the following discrete biorthogonality holds:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\omega_j}{M}\sum_{|k|<2M}
 K_d^\sharp(k/M,\xi_j)u(\xi_i-k/M)=\delta_{ij}.
 \label{eq:kernel-biorthogonality}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $y$, the function $x\mapsto K_d(x,y)$ is a polynomial of degree at
most $d$.  Its deconvolution is exactly $K_d^\sharp(\,\cdot\,,y)$, so
\cref{thm:literal-lagrange} applied to this polynomial gives
\eqref{eq:CD-synthesis}.  Gauss quadrature is exact on products of two
polynomials of degree at most $d$, hence for $p\in\PiD$,
\[
 p(x)=\int_{-1}^1K_d(x,y)p(y)\Differential y
 =\sum_{j=0}^{d}\omega_jK_d(x,\xi_j)p(\xi_j).
\]
Uniqueness of cardinal interpolation proves \eqref{eq:gauss-cardinal-kernel}.
Substitute \eqref{eq:CD-synthesis} and compare with \eqref{eq:UB-def}; then
evaluate at $x=\xi_i$ to obtain \eqref{eq:kernel-biorthogonality}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A weighted singular-value identity}

Let
\[
 V_{in}=P_n(\xi_i),
 \qquad
 W=\DiagonalOperator(\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_d),
 \qquad
 H=\DiagonalOperator(h_0,\ldots,h_d),
\]
and let $D_{kn}=M^{-1}Q_n(k/M)$ as in \cref{thm:basis-factorization}.
Discrete orthogonality gives $V^TWV=H$.

\begin{theorem}[Gauss transform is condition-neutral in natural weights; new here]
\label{thm:weighted-svd}
The matrix
\[
 O=W^{1/2}VH^{-1/2}
\]
is orthogonal, and
\begin{equation}
 BW^{-1/2}=DH^{-1/2}O^T.
 \label{eq:weighted-factorization}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \sigma_j(BW^{-1/2})=\sigma_j(DH^{-1/2})
 \quad(0\le j\le d),}
 \label{eq:weighted-svd}
\end{equation}
including equality of spectral norms and condition numbers on the nonzero
singular spectrum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity $V^TWV=H$ gives $O^TO=I$; since $O$ is square, it is orthogonal.
Solving $V=W^{-1/2}OH^{1/2}$ gives
$V^{-1}=H^{-1/2}O^TW^{1/2}$.  Insert this into $B=DV^{-1}$ and right-multiply
by $W^{-1/2}$.  Right multiplication by an orthogonal matrix preserves all
singular values.
\end{proof}

This is a useful diagnostic: at Gauss nodes, poor conditioning is not created
by the Lagrange/Legendre transform itself.  It is already present in the
sampled deconvolution polynomials $Q_n$.

\section{The minimal \texorpdfstring{$d+2$}{d+2}-atom dictionary and its rank-one complement}
\label{sec:minimal}

\subsection{Imported endpoint compression, specialized to interpolation}

Map $x\in[-1,1]$ to $t=(x+1)/2\in[0,1]$ and put $M=2^d$.  The imported
endpoint theorem retains the $d+2$ integer centers
\begin{equation}
 k_r=M-d-1+r,
 \qquad 0\le r\le d+1,
 \label{eq:retained-centers}
\end{equation}
and the normalized atoms
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_r(x)=\frac1M
 u\!\left(\frac{x+1-2k_r}{2M}\right).
 \label{eq:minimal-atoms}
\end{equation}
For each cardinal polynomial there is a unique coefficient vector
$\widehat B_{:,j}\in\RealNumbers^{d+2}$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \ell_j(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{d+1}\widehat B_{rj}\Psi_r(x),
 \qquad -1\le x\le1.
 \label{eq:minimal-cardinal}
\end{equation}
The coefficients are produced exactly by the corpus's dyadic annihilator
\begin{equation}
 A_d(z)=\prod_{m=1}^{d}[2^m]_z,
 \qquad
 [N]_z=1+z+\cdots+z^{N-1},
 \label{eq:annihilator}
\end{equation}
whose degree is $2M-d-2$.  This is where the $q$-integer and Thue--Morse
structures enter the compression.

Let
\begin{equation}
 \widehat U_{ir}=\Psi_r(\xi_i).
 \label{eq:Uhat}
\end{equation}
Then evaluating \eqref{eq:minimal-cardinal} at the nodes gives
\begin{equation}
 \widehat U\widehat B=I_{d+1}.
 \label{eq:UhatBhat}
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat U$ is $(d+1)\times(d+2)$ and $\widehat B$ is
$(d+2)\times(d+1)$, so the coefficient space has exactly one redundant
direction.

\subsection{Cofactors identify the unique redundant direction}

For $0\le r\le d+1$, let $\widehat U_{\widehat r}$ denote the square matrix
obtained by deleting column $r$ of $\widehat U$, and let
$\widehat B_{\widehat r}$ denote the square matrix obtained by deleting row
$r$ of $\widehat B$.  Define cofactor vectors
\begin{equation}
 v_r=(-1)^r\det(\widehat U_{\widehat r}),
 \qquad
 \lambda_r=(-1)^r\det(\widehat B_{\widehat r}).
 \label{eq:cofactor-vectors}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Rank-one complement of the minimal interpolation loop; new here]
\label{thm:rank-one}
The vectors in \eqref{eq:cofactor-vectors} satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \widehat Uv=0,
 \qquad
 \lambda^T\widehat B=0,
 \qquad
 \lambda^Tv=1.
 \label{eq:cofactor-null}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 I_{d+2}-\widehat B\widehat U=v\lambda^T.}
 \label{eq:rank-one-factorization}
\end{equation}
Thus $\lambda^Ta=0$ is the unique scalar polynomiality certificate for a
minimal-dictionary coefficient vector $a$, and $v$ is the unique coefficient
direction whose up-spline vanishes at every interpolation node.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The alternating maximal minors of a full-row-rank matrix give a right-null
vector, so $\widehat Uv=0$.  The same cofactor argument applied to
$\widehat B^T$ gives $\lambda^T\widehat B=0$.  Cauchy--Binet and
$\widehat U\widehat B=I$ yield
\[
 1=\det(\widehat U\widehat B)
 =\sum_{r=0}^{d+1}
 \det(\widehat U_{\widehat r})\det(\widehat B_{\widehat r})
 =\lambda^Tv.
\]
The idempotent $\widehat B\widehat U$ has rank $d+1$, so its complementary
idempotent has rank one, range $\ker\widehat U=\SpanOperator\{v\}$, and left range
$\ker\widehat B^T=\SpanOperator\{\lambda\}$.  The normalization
$\lambda^Tv=1$ determines the factorization uniquely.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Every local up-spline is a polynomial plus one nodal defect]
\label{cor:functional-defect}
For $a\in\RealNumbers^{d+2}$ define
\[
 F_a(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{d+1}a_r\Psi_r(x),
 \qquad
 G_\Xi(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{d+1}v_r\Psi_r(x).
\]
Then $G_\Xi(\xi_i)=0$ for all $i$, and
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 F_a(x)=I_\Xi F_a(x)+(\lambda^Ta)G_\Xi(x),}
 \label{eq:functional-defect}
\end{equation}
where $I_\Xi F_a$ is the degree-$d$ Lagrange interpolant of the nodal values of
$F_a$.  In particular, $F_a\in\PiD$ on $[-1,1]$ if and only if
$\lambda^Ta=0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient vector of $I_\Xi F_a$ in the minimal dictionary is
$\widehat B\widehat Ua$.  Subtract this from $a$ and use
\eqref{eq:rank-one-factorization}.  The endpoint dictionary spans a space of
dimension $d+2$ whose polynomial subspace has dimension $d+1$, so the final
criterion is exact.
\end{proof}

This result is the nodal-coordinate form of the one-dimensional quotient found
in the repository's local dimension theorem.  Any previously chosen
Thue--Morse defect functional on that quotient must be proportional to
$\lambda^T$; \cref{thm:rank-one} fixes the proportionality by cofactors.

\section{The literal Lagrange interpolation loop}
\label{sec:interpolation-loop}

\subsection{Arbitrary nodal data}

For a function $f$ known at the nodes, let
\begin{equation}
 I_\Xi f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}f(\xi_j)\ell_j(x)
 \label{eq:lagrange-interpolant}
\end{equation}
be its degree-$d$ interpolant.

\begin{theorem}[Finite up-shift realization of any interpolant]
\label{thm:interpolant-loop}
With $M=2^d$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 I_\Xi f(x)=\frac1M\sum_{|k|<2M}
 c_{d,k}(f)u(x-k/M),}
 \label{eq:interpolant-up}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 c_{d,k}(f)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{d}f(\xi_j)(\Dscr\ell_j)(k/M)
 =(\Dscr I_\Xi f)(k/M).
 \label{eq:interpolant-coeff}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiply \eqref{eq:literal-lagrange} by $f(\xi_j)$, sum over $j$, and use
linearity of $\Dscr$.
\end{proof}

For $f=u$, \eqref{eq:interpolant-up} is already a finite self-representation:
it writes a polynomial approximation of $u$ as a finite sum of literal
translates of $u$ itself.

\subsection{Superalgebraic convergence at Chebyshev--Lobatto nodes}

Let $I_d^{\mathrm{CL}}u$ use the Chebyshev--Lobatto nodes
$\cos(j\pi/d)$.  Since $u\in C^\infty[-1,1]$, Jackson estimates give, for
every integer $r\ge1$,
\[
 E_d(u):=\inf_{p\in\Pi_d}\Norm{u-p}_\infty=\BigO_r(d^{-r}).
\]
The Chebyshev--Lobatto Lebesgue constants satisfy $\Lambda_d=\BigO(\log d)$,
so Lebesgue's lemma yields
\begin{equation}
 \Norm{u-I_d^{\mathrm{CL}}u}_\infty
 \le(1+\Lambda_d)E_d(u)
 =\BigO_N(d^{-N})
 \quad\text{for every }N.
 \label{eq:CL-superalg}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:CL-superalg} with \cref{thm:interpolant-loop} produces a
sequence of finite literal-shift sums converging uniformly and faster than any
fixed power of $d^{-1}$ to $u$.

There is no geometric conclusion: $u$ is flat but nonanalytic at $\pm1$, so a
uniform geometric polynomial rate would contradict classical Bernstein
ellipse theory.

\subsection{Gauss data factor through a discrete Legendre transform}

At Gauss--Legendre nodes, define the discrete Legendre coefficients
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde f_n=\frac{2n+1}{2}
 \sum_{j=0}^{d}\omega_jf(\xi_j)P_n(\xi_j).
 \label{eq:discrete-Legendre}
\end{equation}
Then $I_\Xi f=\sum_{n=0}^d\widetilde f_nP_n$, and
\begin{equation}
 I_\Xi f(x)=\frac1M\sum_{|k|<2M}
 \left(\sum_{n=0}^d\widetilde f_nQ_n(k/M)\right)u(x-k/M).
 \label{eq:gauss-interpolant-loop}
\end{equation}
Thus nodal interpolation, orthogonal coordinates, and up synthesis form an
exact commutative diagram.

\subsection{The corresponding projection-kernel loop}

For any integrable $f$, let $\Pi_df$ be its degree-$d$ Legendre projection.
By \cref{thm:CD-synthesis},
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \Pi_df(x)=\frac1M\sum_{|k|<2M}
 \left[\int_{-1}^1K_d^\sharp(k/M,y)f(y)\Differential y\right]u(x-k/M).}
 \label{eq:projection-loop}
\end{equation}
This is the continuous analogue of \eqref{eq:gauss-interpolant-loop}.  The
bracketed coefficients are not obtained by fitting an up basis; they are exact
Legendre moments passed through $\Dscr$.

\section{Closing the canonical Fourier--Legendre loop}
\label{sec:legendre-loop}

\subsection{The imported series}

The canonical repository expansion is
\begin{equation}
 u(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_nP_{2n}(x),
 \qquad
 u_n=\frac{4n+1}{2}\int_{-1}^1u(x)P_{2n}(x)\Differential x,
 \label{eq:canonical-Legendre}
\end{equation}
with absolute and uniform convergence on $[-1,1]$.  Its coefficients have an
exact finite formula in dyadic Fabius values; equivalently, they are rational
because all moments of the up law are rational.

\begin{center}
\input{legendre_coefficients.tex}
\end{center}

\subsection{Termwise finite closure}

For the polynomial $P_{2n}$ use degree $d=2n$, hence
$M_n=2^{2n}=4^n$.  \Cref{thm:literal-lagrange} applied to this single
polynomial gives
\begin{equation}
 P_{2n}(x)=\frac1{4^n}
 \sum_{|k|<2\cdot4^n}Q_{2n}(k/4^n)u(x-k/4^n).
 \label{eq:Legendre-atomization}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Blockwise Lagrange--Legendre--up closure; new here]
\label{thm:block-loop}
For every $x\in[-1,1]$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 u(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_n}{4^n}
 \sum_{|k|<2\cdot4^n}
 Q_{2n}(k/4^n)u(x-k/4^n).}
 \label{eq:block-loop}
\end{equation}
The outer series converges absolutely and uniformly when each finite inner sum
is retained as one block.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \eqref{eq:Legendre-atomization} into
\eqref{eq:canonical-Legendre}.  The $n$th finite block is exactly
$u_nP_{2n}(x)$.  Since $\Norm{P_m}_\infty\le1$ on $[-1,1]$ and the repository
proves $\sum_n|u_n|<\infty$,
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}\Norm{u_nP_{2n}}_\infty\le\sum_{n\ge0}|u_n|<\infty.
\]
\end{proof}

The theorem is an exact self-representation of $u$, but it is a series of
\emph{finite cancellation blocks}, not yet a single unconditionally convergent
series of individual atoms.  \Cref{sec:nonflattening} proves that this
distinction cannot be removed.

\subsection{One fixed lattice for each partial sum}

Let
\begin{equation}
 S_N(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}u_nP_{2n}(x),
 \qquad
 C_N(t)=\Dscr S_N(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}u_nQ_{2n}(t).
 \label{eq:SN-CN}
\end{equation}
The degree of $S_N$ is $2N$, so a single lattice with $M_N=4^N$ synthesizes the
entire partial sum.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-scale finite self-approximants]
\label{cor:fixed-scale}
For $x\in[-1,1]$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 S_N(x)=\frac1{4^N}
 \sum_{|k|<2\cdot4^N}C_N(k/4^N)u(x-k/4^N).}
 \label{eq:fixed-scale}
\end{equation}
Every displayed coefficient is rational, and the right side converges
uniformly to $u$ as $N\to\infty$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the physical-scale synthesis theorem to $S_N$.  Rationality follows from
rationality of $u_n$, of $\gamma_r$, and of evaluation of rational
polynomials at dyadic rationals.  Uniform convergence is exactly the imported
uniform convergence $S_N\to u$.
\end{proof}

Taking $d=2N$ in the endpoint compression gives an even more economical but
more ill-conditioned version.

\begin{corollary}[Only \texorpdfstring{$2N+2$}{2N+2} atoms per Legendre partial sum]
\label{cor:linear-atoms}
Let $M=4^N$ and put $P_N(t)=S_N(2t-1)$.  There are unique rational coefficients
$b_{N,k}$, $M-2N-1\le k\le M$, such that
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 S_N(x)=\frac1M\sum_{k=M-2N-1}^{M}b_{N,k}
 u\!\left(\frac{x+1-2k}{2M}\right),
 \qquad -1\le x\le1.}
 \label{eq:linear-atoms}
\end{equation}
The sums in \eqref{eq:linear-atoms} have only $2N+2$ atoms and converge
uniformly to $u$.
\end{corollary}

This gives a striking scale--cardinality tradeoff: literal unit-scale shifts
need exponentially many displayed centers, while a common dilation of order
$4^N$ permits a linear number of atoms.  The latter coefficients exhibit
severe cancellation, as \cref{sec:minimal,sec:nonflattening} explain.

\subsection{Superalgebraic convergence rate of the closed partial loop}

Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Lf=-\bigl((1-x^2)f'(x)\bigr)'.
 \label{eq:Legendre-operator}
\end{equation}
Then $\mathcal LP_m=m(m+1)P_m$.  Boundary terms vanish because of the factor
$1-x^2$, so repeated self-adjointness gives, for every $r\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \int_{-1}^{1}fP_m
 =\frac1{[m(m+1)]^r}\int_{-1}^{1}(\mathcal L^rf)P_m.
 \label{eq:SL-integration}
\end{equation}
By Cauchy--Schwarz and $\Norm{P_m}_2^2=2/(2m+1)$, the normalized Legendre
coefficient $a_m=(2m+1)\int fP_m/2$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |a_m|\le
 \sqrt{\frac{2m+1}{2}}\,
 \frac{\Norm{\mathcal L^rf}_2}{[m(m+1)]^r}
 =\BigO_r(m^{1/2-2r}).
 \label{eq:Legendre-rate}
\end{equation}
For $f=u$, $r$ is arbitrary.  Summing the tail and using
$\Norm{P_m}_\infty\le1$ shows
\begin{equation}
 \Norm{u-S_N}_\infty=\BigO_R(N^{-R})
 \quad\text{for every }R>0.
 \label{eq:SN-superalg}
\end{equation}
Therefore both exact atomizations \eqref{eq:fixed-scale} and
\eqref{eq:linear-atoms} converge superalgebraically as functions, even though
their coefficient maps become rapidly unstable.

\section{Factorial deconvolution and the non-flattening theorem}
\label{sec:nonflattening}

This section analyzes the closed loop rather than merely writing it down.  The
main phenomenon is that $\Dscr$ is harmless on each fixed polynomial but is a
factorially growing transform as the degree increases.

\subsection{Reciprocal-MGF pole input}

Let
\begin{equation}
 C=\MM_u(\pi\ImaginaryUnit)
 =\prod_{j\ge1}\frac{\sin(\pi/2^j)}{\pi/2^j}
 \approx0.553771275888\ldots.
 \label{eq:C-constant}
\end{equation}
The nearest poles of $1/\MM_u(z)$ are the simple conjugate pair
$\pm2\pi\ImaginaryUnit$.  The repository proves the sharper three-shell formula; in the
present notation its leading consequence is
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_{2m}=\frac{2(-1)^m(2m)!}{C(2\pi)^{2m}}
 \left(1+\BigO(m4^{-m})\right).
 \label{eq:gamma-asymptotic}
\end{equation}

\subsection{A new asymptotic for the deconvolved Legendre constants}

Write the even Legendre polynomial as
\begin{equation}
 P_{2n}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{n,j}x^{2n-2j},
 \qquad
 c_{n,j}=2^{-2n}(-1)^j
 \frac{(4n-2j)!}{j!(2n-j)!(2n-2j)!}.
 \label{eq:Legendre-coeffs-even}
\end{equation}
Since $\Dscr x^r$ has constant term $\gamma_r$,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{2n}(0)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{n,j}\gamma_{2n-2j}.
 \label{eq:Q0-finite-sum}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Factorial asymptotic of \texorpdfstring{$Q_{2n}(0)$}{Q2n(0)}; new here]
\label{thm:Q0-asymptotic}
As $n\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 Q_{2n}(0)=
 \frac{2(-1)^n}{C}\frac{(4n)!}{(2n)!(4\pi)^{2n}}
 \left(1+\frac{2\pi^2}{4n-1}+\BigO(n^{-2})\right).}
 \label{eq:Q0-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{2n}(0)=\frac{2\sqrt2}{C}(-1)^n
 \left(\frac{2n}{\pi\EulerE}\right)^{2n}
 \left(1+\BigO(n^{-1})\right),
 \label{eq:Q0-Stirling}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \AbsoluteValue{Q_{2n}(0)}^{1/(2n)}\sim\frac{2n}{\pi\EulerE}.
 \label{eq:Q0-root}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $T_{n,j}=c_{n,j}\gamma_{2n-2j}$ and first isolate $T_{n,0}$.  The
coefficient ratio for $j=1$ is exact:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{c_{n,1}}{c_{n,0}}=-\frac{n(2n-1)}{4n-1}.
 \label{eq:c-ratio-one}
\end{equation}
From \eqref{eq:gamma-asymptotic},
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\gamma_{2n-2}}{\gamma_{2n}}
 =-\frac{(2\pi)^2}{(2n)(2n-1)}
 \left(1+\BigO(n4^{-n})\right).
 \label{eq:gamma-ratio-one}
\end{equation}
Multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{T_{n,1}}{T_{n,0}}
 =\frac{2\pi^2}{4n-1}+\BigO(4^{-n}).
 \label{eq:first-correction}
\end{equation}

It remains to control $j\ge2$ uniformly.  The meromorphic asymptotic implies
constants $0<c<C_1$ such that, for all sufficiently large indices,
\[
 c\frac{(2m)!}{(2\pi)^{2m}}
 \le|\gamma_{2m}|\le
 C_1\frac{(2m)!}{(2\pi)^{2m}},
\]
with finitely many small indices absorbed into $C_1$.  Hence
\begin{align*}
 \left|\frac{T_{n,j}}{T_{n,0}}\right|
 &\le C_2
 \frac{(4n-2j)!(2n)!}{j!(2n-j)!(4n)!}(2\pi)^{2j}\\
 &\le \frac{C_2}{j!}\left(\frac{2\pi^2}{n}\right)^j,
 \qquad 0\le j\le n.
\end{align*}
Indeed, $(2n)!/(2n-j)!\le(2n)^j$, whereas every factor in
$(4n)!/(4n-2j)!$ is at least $2n$.  Summing over $j\ge2$ gives
$\BigO(n^{-2})$.  Therefore
\[
 Q_{2n}(0)=c_{n,0}\gamma_{2n}
 \left(1+\frac{2\pi^2}{4n-1}+\BigO(n^{-2})\right).
\]
Now $c_{n,0}=2^{-2n}\binom{4n}{2n}$, and substitution of
\eqref{eq:gamma-asymptotic} gives \eqref{eq:Q0-asymptotic}.  Stirling's
formula gives \eqref{eq:Q0-Stirling} and \eqref{eq:Q0-root}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Formal degree generating functions have zero radius]
Applying $\Dscr$ to the classical generating function gives the formal identity
\begin{align}
 \sum_{n\ge0}Q_n(x)t^n
 &=(1-2xt+t^2)^{-1/2}
 \sum_{r\ge0}\gamma_r\frac{(1/2)_r}{r!}
 \left(\frac{2t}{1-2xt+t^2}\right)^r.
 \label{eq:formal-Q-generating}
\end{align}
Theorem \ref{thm:Q0-asymptotic} proves that at $x=0$ this is a purely formal,
Gevrey-type expansion with zero ordinary radius of convergence.  This is not a
contradiction: $\Dscr$ was defined as a terminating operator on each fixed
polynomial, not as a bounded operator on an analytic function space.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A new Lambert--\texorpdfstring{$W$}{W} threshold}

Let
\[
 n_Q(X)=\min\{n\ge1:|Q_{2n}(0)|\ge X\}.
\]
From \eqref{eq:Q0-Stirling}, the leading logarithmic equation is
\[
 2n\log\frac{2n}{\pi\EulerE}=\log X.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Legendre-deconvolution threshold; new here]
\label{cor:Q-Lambert}
As $X\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 n_Q(X)=
 \frac{\log X}{2W\!\left(\log X/(\pi\EulerE)\right)}+\BigO(1).}
 \label{eq:Q-Lambert}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Set $H=\log X$ and $w=W(H/(\pi\EulerE))$.  Then
$n_0=H/(2w)$ satisfies
$2n_0\log(2n_0/(\pi\EulerE))=H$.  The omitted logarithmic prefactor in
\eqref{eq:Q0-Stirling} changes the continuous solution by $\LittleO(1)$, and
rounding to the least integer contributes $\BigO(1)$.
\end{proof}

The repository's reciprocal-MGF threshold has
$W(\log X/(2\pi\EulerE))$ instead.  The changed argument records the extra central
binomial amplification contributed by the leading coefficient of $P_{2n}$.

\subsection{The central atom cannot be grouped}

In the $n$th block of \eqref{eq:block-loop}, the index $k=0$ multiplies the
same atom $u(x)$ for every $n$.  Its scalar coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 \beta_n=\frac{u_nQ_{2n}(0)}{4^n}.
 \label{eq:beta-central}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Supergeometric Legendre coefficients force analyticity]
\label{lem:supergeom-analytic}
If the even Legendre coefficients of a continuous function $f$ satisfy
$|a_{2n}|^{1/n}\to0$, then its Legendre series converges locally uniformly in
$\ComplexNumbers$ and defines an entire function agreeing with $f$ on $[-1,1]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every compact $K\subset\ComplexNumbers$ there are constants $C_K,R_K$ with
$|P_m(z)|\le C_KR_K^m$ for $z\in K$.  Supergeometric decay makes
$\sum a_{2n}P_{2n}(z)$ uniformly convergent on $K$.  The limit is entire and
agrees with $f$ on the interval by uniqueness of the Legendre expansion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rigorous non-flattening of the closed loop; new here]
\label{thm:nonflattening}
The central coefficients \eqref{eq:beta-central} are unbounded in absolute
value:
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\beta_n|=\infty.}
 \label{eq:beta-unbounded}
\end{equation}
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the repeated central copies $u(x)$ in \eqref{eq:block-loop} cannot be
  grouped into a scalar coefficient series, because its terms do not tend to
  zero;
  \item the atomwise coefficient variation
  \begin{equation}
   \Lambda_n=\frac{|u_n|}{4^n}
   \sum_{|k|<2\cdot4^n}|Q_{2n}(k/4^n)|
   \label{eq:block-variation}
  \end{equation}
  is unbounded;
  \item the double atom expansion is neither absolutely nor unconditionally
  convergent when flattened term by term, even though its finite blocks form
  an absolutely and uniformly convergent outer series.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $|\beta_n|$ were bounded.  By \eqref{eq:Q0-root},
\begin{align*}
 |u_n|^{1/n}
 &\le \left(\frac{C_0 4^n}{|Q_{2n}(0)|}\right)^{1/n}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
The even Legendre coefficients of $u$ would therefore be supergeometric.
\Cref{lem:supergeom-analytic} would extend $u$ analytically across the
endpoints.  But $u$ is $C^\infty$ with compact support, so every derivative at
$\pm1$ vanishes; an analytic extension with a zero Taylor series at an endpoint
would be identically zero, contradicting $u(0)=1$.  Hence $\beta_n$ is
unbounded.  Since $\Lambda_n\ge|\beta_n|$, the remaining conclusions follow.
\end{proof}

The theorem gives a precise answer to ``what happens after closing the loop?''
The loop is mathematically exact, but its natural mode of convergence is
\emph{blockwise}.  Deconvolution creates huge signed coefficients; the
Strang--Fix identities then cancel them inside each block to leave the small,
smooth function $u_nP_{2n}$.

\section{Connections to \texorpdfstring{$q$}{q}-binomials, Bell polynomials, Thue--Morse, and Lambert \texorpdfstring{$W$}{W}}
\label{sec:connections}

\subsection{A \texorpdfstring{$q$}{q}-geometric row lift of Lagrange weights}

The primary corpus proves a reversed Gaussian-binomial formula for Lagrange
weights on geometric nodes.  The present synthesis lifts that single
functional row to an entire up-coefficient matrix.

Take distinct nodes $1,q,\ldots,q^d$ over a field and set
\begin{equation}
 G_{d+1}(x)=\prod_{r=0}^{d}(x-q^r)
 =\sum_{s=0}^{d+1}g_sx^s,
 \qquad
 g_s=(-1)^{d+1-s}q^{\binom{d+1-s}{2}}
 \GaussianBinomial{d+1}{s}{q}.
 \label{eq:q-node-product}
\end{equation}
Synthetic division gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{G_{d+1}(x)}{x-q^j}
 =\sum_{n=0}^{d}h_{n,j}x^n,
 \qquad
 h_{n,j}=\sum_{s=n+1}^{d+1}g_sq^{j(s-n-1)}.
 \label{eq:q-synthetic}
\end{equation}
The denominator is
\begin{equation}
 G_{d+1}'(q^j)=
 (-1)^j q^{jd-j(j+1)/2}(q;q)_j(q;q)_{d-j}.
 \label{eq:q-denominator}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian-binomial/Appell synthesis matrix; new here]
\label{prop:q-row-lift}
For geometric nodes $q^j$ and $M=2^d$, the literal-shift coefficient matrix is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 B_{kj}^{(q)}=
 \frac{1}{M G_{d+1}'(q^j)}
 \sum_{n=0}^{d}h_{n,j}A_n(k/M),}
 \label{eq:q-row-lift}
\end{equation}
with $h_{n,j}$ given by \eqref{eq:q-synthetic}.  Thus every row is a finite
convolution of a Gaussian-binomial coefficient row with the Rvachev--Appell
family.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cardinal polynomial is
$\ell_j(x)=G_{d+1}(x)/[(x-q^j)G_{d+1}'(q^j)]$.  Insert
\eqref{eq:q-synthetic}, apply $\Dscr x^n=A_n(x)$, and sample as in
\eqref{eq:UB-def}.
\end{proof}

Affine images of geometric nodes can be placed inside $[-1,1]$.  At evaluation
$x=0$, the proposition collapses to the type of q-binomial Lagrange weights
already formalized in the corpus; the new content is the simultaneous lift to
all dyadic up-shift rows.

\subsection{The two different \texorpdfstring{$q$}{q}-products in the loop}

Two $q$-structures coexist but play different roles:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item geometric interpolation nodes produce Gaussian coefficients
  $\GaussianBinomial{d+1}{s}{q}$ in \eqref{eq:q-node-product};
  \item dyadic Fourier zero multiplicities produce the annihilator
  $A_d(z)=\prod_{m=1}^{d}[2^m]_z$ in \eqref{eq:annihilator}.
\end{enumerate}
The first is a coordinate choice; the second is intrinsic to the binary sinc
product.  Their interaction in \eqref{eq:q-row-lift} suggests a two-parameter
calculus in which node geometry and dyadic alias geometry can be varied
independently.

\subsection{Bell--Bernoulli algebra is the coefficient engine}

Equations \eqref{eq:cumulants}, \eqref{eq:bell-form}, and
\eqref{eq:appell-cardinal} show that all exact Lagrange-to-up coefficients are
finite combinations of:
\[
 \text{node symmetric functions}
 \quad\times\quad
 \text{complete Bell polynomials}
 \quad\times\quad
 \text{Bernoulli cumulants}.
\]
The same cumulants appear in the nonlocal recurrence
\eqref{eq:Q-recurrence-expanded}.  Thus Bell and Bernoulli polynomials are not
an ornamental reformulation: they are the exact algebraic engine that undoes
the smoothing by the random binary uniform series.

\subsection{Thue--Morse controls compression, not the literal coefficients}

The literal formula \eqref{eq:literal-lagrange} follows from Fourier zeros and
moment deconvolution.  Thue--Morse becomes visible when one asks to remove the
exponentially many redundant active atoms on a cell.  The corpus identity
relating $A_d(z)(z-1)^{d+1}$ to the length-$2^d$ Thue--Morse polynomial gives
the polynomiality certificate and the $d+2$ local dimension.  In the present
matrix language, the cofactor functional $\lambda^T$ of
\cref{thm:rank-one} is a normalized coordinate on exactly that same
one-dimensional defect quotient.

\subsection{Two Lambert geometries}

The repository's $W_{-1}$ endpoint asymptotics invert a small spatial relation
of the form $x\asymp\lambda2^{-\lambda}$.  Its reciprocal-MGF coefficient
threshold uses the principal branch $W$ to invert factorial growth.  The new
threshold \eqref{eq:Q-Lambert} is a third occurrence: the central binomial
coefficient of $P_{2n}$ shifts the scale from $2\pi\EulerE$ to $\pi\EulerE$ in the
argument of $W$.  The common mechanism is inversion of a quantity
$n\log n$, but the constants encode different geometry.

\section{Frontier conjectures and research directions}
\label{sec:frontiers}

The exact loop and the non-flattening theorem shift the main questions from
existence to geometry, conditioning, and summability.

\begin{conjecture}[Asymptotic singular-vector localization]
\label{conj:localization}
For even degree $d=2n$, after Gauss weighting, the dominant right singular
vector of $D_dH^{-1/2}$ concentrates on the highest few even Legendre modes,
and its dominant row mass eventually concentrates on a shrinking neighborhood
of the central lattice point $k=0$.  More precisely, there should be
$\delta_n\downarrow0$ such that the fraction of the top left singular vector's
$\ell^2$ mass on $|k|/4^n\le\delta_n$ tends to one.
\end{conjecture}

The factorial lower bound from $Q_{2n}(0)$ supports the conjecture, but the
large finite-degree values near $|k|/M\approx2$ compete for a long
pre-asymptotic range.

\begin{problem}[Optimal oversampling versus coefficient growth]
\label{prob:optimal-ratio}
The exact reproduction order only requires $\TwoAdicValuation(m)\ge d$, so one may replace
$M=2^d$ by $m=2^dq$ with odd $q$, or alter the physical radius and spacing
while preserving their ratio.  Determine the choice minimizing a natural
stability functional such as
\[
 \Norm{B_mW^{-1/2}}_2,
 \qquad
 \max_j\sum_k|B_{m,kj}|,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 \sup_x\sum_k|B_{m,kj}u((x-kh)/\rho)|.
\]
The atom-minimal dictionary is not expected to be condition-minimal.
\end{problem}

\begin{conjecture}[Interlacing of the unique nodal-defect spline]
\label{conj:interlacing}
For Gauss--Legendre or Chebyshev--Lobatto nodes, the unique defect function
$G_\Xi$ of \cref{cor:functional-defect} has no zeros other than the prescribed
$d+1$ simple nodal zeros in $(-1,1)$, with alternating sign on consecutive
node intervals.
\end{conjecture}

A proof would amount to an extended-Chebyshev property for the minimal
endpoint up-dictionary and would make the rank-one complement geometrically
canonical, not merely algebraically canonical.

\begin{conjecture}[Sign regularity for geometric-node synthesis]
\label{conj:q-total-positivity}
For $0<q<1$, after the natural checkerboard row and column sign changes, the
matrix $B^{(q)}$ in \eqref{eq:q-row-lift} is sign regular of order $d+1$ on the
active dyadic lattice.  Its maximal minors should admit product formulae
combining Gaussian coefficients with the dyadic annihilator.
\end{conjecture}

This would connect total positivity of interpolation systems to the
q-binomial/Appell row lift and could lead to stable barycentric variants.

\begin{problem}[Borel and resurgent interpretation of the formal \texorpdfstring{$Q$}{Q} generating function]
\label{prob:Borel}
Develop a Borel--Laplace summation theory for
\eqref{eq:formal-Q-generating}.  The reciprocal-MGF pole shells at
$\pm2\pi\ImaginaryUnit n$, with multiplicity $1+\TwoAdicValuation(n)$, should reappear as a
2-adically stratified singularity set in the Borel plane.  Determine whether a
canonical lateral sum exists away from the corresponding rays and whether its
Stokes jumps have a direct Thue--Morse interpretation.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Preconditioned exact loops]
\label{prob:preconditioned-loop}
Find a polynomial basis or a biorthogonal up-atom dictionary in which the
Legendre partial loop remains exact but the coefficient blocks have controlled
variation.  The weighted identity \eqref{eq:weighted-svd} shows that merely
switching between Gauss Lagrange and Legendre coordinates cannot solve the
problem; a successful preconditioner must alter the deconvolution stage or the
atom dictionary itself.
\end{problem}

\subsection{Promising computational directions}

Several concrete experiments appear particularly informative:
\begin{itemize}
  \item compute the full singular spectrum of $D_dH^{-1/2}$ in arbitrary
  precision for $d$ up to at least $40$, comparing central and edge lattice
  localization;
  \item track zeros of $Q_n$ and of the nodal-defect spline $G_\Xi$ as $d$
  grows;
  \item optimize odd overscaling factors $q$ for stability, not just exact
  order;
  \item search for closed q-hypergeometric simplifications of
  \eqref{eq:q-row-lift};
  \item compare Gauss, Chebyshev, Leja, and geometric nodes through the exact
  projector norm $\Norm{BU}$ and the certificate vector $\lambda$;
  \item use interval arithmetic to certify the high-degree asymptotic and
  singular-value experiments.
\end{itemize}

\section{Formalization roadmap}
\label{sec:formalization}

The results are well suited to a modular Lean development because the analytic
input has largely already been formalized in the repository.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Polynomial deconvolution API.}  Define $\Dscr$ on
  \texttt{Polynomial} as the terminating reciprocal-MGF differential
  operator; prove linearity, degree preservation, parity, and
  $\MM_u(D)\Dscr p=p$.
  \item \textbf{Lagrange specialization.}  Instantiate the existing interval
  synthesis theorem with $[-1,1]$, $h=2^{-d}$, and $\rho=1$; prove the two
  endpoint atoms vanish and obtain \eqref{eq:literal-lagrange}.
  \item \textbf{Finite matrices.}  Use \texttt{Matrix} indexed by
  \texttt{Fin (d+1)} and the finite integer interval $|k|<2^{d+1}$; prove
  $U\,B=1$, then the projector and moment identities.
  \item \textbf{Basis triangle.}  Package $UD=V$, $B=DV^{-1}$, and specialize
  to Legendre polynomials.
  \item \textbf{Gauss kernel.}  Import or prove finite Gauss quadrature
  orthogonality, $V^TWV=H$, the cardinal kernel identity, and the weighted
  singular-value factorization.
  \item \textbf{Minimal cofactor theorem.}  Formalize alternating maximal
  minors for a rectangular matrix of corank one and derive
  $I-\widehat B\widehat U=v\lambda^T$ from Cauchy--Binet.
  \item \textbf{Closed loop.}  Combine the formal Fourier--Legendre series
  with finite block identities.  Keep the outer series in a block-valued
  Banach space to make the legal grouping explicit.
  \item \textbf{Asymptotic and non-flattening.}  Reuse the formalized or
  semi-formalized reciprocal coefficient asymptotic, prove the finite
  Legendre-coefficient estimate, and formalize the analytic-extension
  contradiction for supergeometric Legendre coefficients.
\end{enumerate}

The matrix and cofactor parts are finite algebra.  The only genuinely new
analytic bridge needed for the final theorem is a convenient formal criterion
that supergeometric Legendre coefficients imply analytic extension.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The requested finite representation exists in a particularly explicit form:
for any nodes on $[-1,1]$, every Lagrange cardinal polynomial is obtained by
sampling one deconvolved polynomial on the dyadic lattice and attaching those
samples to literal translates of $u$.  This turns interpolation into an exact
right-inverse problem, not an approximate radial-basis fit.

The Gauss--Legendre specialization closes the conceptual gap between Lagrange
and Legendre.  Its deconvolved Christoffel--Darboux kernel gives a compact
formula for the entire coefficient matrix, while the weighted singular-value
identity shows precisely where instability enters.  The endpoint compression
reveals the one-dimensional quotient beyond polynomials, and cofactors provide
a canonical rank-one defect decomposition.

Substituting the finite Legendre synthesis into the repository's canonical
series does close the loop, both termwise and at each fixed partial-sum scale.
But the loop is not a benign self-expansion.  The new asymptotic for
$Q_{2n}(0)$ and the nonanalyticity of $u$ force the repeated central-atom
coefficients to be unbounded.  The correct mathematical object is therefore an
absolutely convergent series of exact finite cancellation blocks.  Any future
stable algorithm must respect that block geometry or redesign the dictionary;
it cannot simply flatten the atoms and hope that ordinary absolute convergence
survives.

code.  Let $P(t)=p(2t-1)$, $M=2^d$, and
\[
 Q(t)=\MM_u(MD_t)^{-1}P(t).
\]
The active integer centers on $[0,1]$ are
$-M+1,-M+2,\ldots,M$.  Write
\[
 A_d(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{R_d}a_rz^r,
 \qquad R_d=2M-d-2,
 \qquad a_{R_d}=1.
\]
Initialize a null sequence by
\[
 n_k=Q(k),
 \qquad -M+1\le k\le -M+R_d,
\]
and extend it by the monic recurrence
\begin{equation}
 n_{s+R_d}=-\sum_{r=0}^{R_d-1}a_rn_{s+r}.
 \label{eq:null-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 b_k=Q(k)-n_k,
 \qquad M-d-1\le k\le M,
\]
are the unique endpoint coefficients and give
\[
 p(x)=\frac1M\sum_{k=M-d-1}^{M}b_k
 u\!\left(\frac{x+1-2k}{2M}\right).
\]
Applying this columnwise to the cardinals produces $\widehat B$.
