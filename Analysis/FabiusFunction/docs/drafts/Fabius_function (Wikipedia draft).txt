In [[mathematics]], the '''Fabius function''' is a [[smooth function|smooth]] [[cumulative distribution function]] <math>F\colon\mathbb R\to[0,1]</math> that is strictly increasing on <math>[0,1]</math> but [[real analytic function|nowhere real-analytic]]. It is the distribution function of the random series
<math display="block">
 X=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}U_n,
</math>
where the <math>U_n</math> are independent random variables uniformly distributed on <math>[0,1]</math>. Equivalently, it is characterized by
<math display="block">
 F(0)=0,\qquad F(1-x)=1-F(x),\qquad
 F'(x)=2F(2x)\quad\left(0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\right).
</math>
Although every positive-order derivative of <math>F</math> vanishes at both endpoints, <math>F</math> is nonconstant on every subinterval of <math>[0,1]</math>.<ref name="Fabius1966"/><ref name="Arias1982"/>

The same object occurs, after a change of variables, as '''[[Volodymyr Rvachov|Rvachev]]'s up function''' <math>u</math>, an even <math>C^\infty</math> [[bump function]] supported on <math>[-1,1]</math>. Its Fourier transform is an infinite product of [[sinc function]]s, or equivalently a product of powers of cosines. The function also has an unusual arithmetic side: every value <math>F(a/2^n)</math> at a [[dyadic rational]] is rational, and the numerators, denominators and [[p-adic valuation|2-adic valuations]] of these values obey strong divisibility laws.<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>

The underlying distribution already appeared in work of [[Børge Jessen]] and [[Aurel Wintner]] in 1935. Jaap Fabius independently introduced it probabilistically in 1966 as an example of a nowhere-analytic smooth function, and V. A. Rvachev independently introduced the associated compactly supported solution of a functional–differential equation.<ref name="JessenWintner1935"/><ref name="Fabius1966"/><ref name="Arias2018"/> Near zero, <math>F</math> is smaller than every power of its argument; its exact rate of decay is governed by the lower branch of the [[Lambert W function]] together with a small correction that repeats each time the argument is halved. That correction alters <math>F</math> by only about two parts in a million, but it does not fade away as the argument tends to zero, so no asymptotic formula that omits it can be exact.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

== Notation ==

The literature uses several incompatible conventions. In this article, <math>F</math> denotes the bounded cumulative distribution function, extended by constants outside <math>[0,1]</math>; <math>u</math> denotes Rvachev's compactly supported up function; and <math>\mathcal F</math> denotes the signed global extension satisfying <math>\mathcal F'(x)=2\mathcal F(2x)</math> on the whole real line. Logarithms are natural unless a base is written explicitly, <math>s_2(n)</math> is the sum of the binary digits of <math>n</math>, and <math>\nu_2(n)</math> is the exponent of 2 in <math>n</math>.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

== Definition and elementary properties ==

There is a unique <math>C^\infty</math> function <math>F\colon\mathbb R\to[0,1]</math> such that
<math display="block">
 F(x)=0\quad(x\leq0),\qquad F(x)=1\quad(x\geq1),
</math>
and, for <math>0\leq x\leq1</math>,
<math display="block">
 F(1-x)=1-F(x),
</math>
while
<math display="block">
 F'(x)=2F(2x)\qquad\left(0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\right).
</math>
The constant continuation outside the unit interval is smooth because all positive-order derivatives vanish at the endpoints.<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>

Using the symmetry, the differential equation can be written on the whole unit interval as
<math display="block">
 F'(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  2F(2x),&0\leq x\leq\tfrac12,\\[3pt]
  2F(2-2x),&\tfrac12\leq x\leq1.
 \end{cases}
</math>
The derivative is positive on <math>(0,1)</math>, so <math>F</math> is strictly increasing there. In particular,
<math display="block">
 F\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=\tfrac12,\qquad
 F'\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=2,
</math>
and
<math display="block">
 F'(1-x)=F'(x),\qquad
 F'(x)+F'\!\left(\tfrac12-x\right)=2
 \quad\left(0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\right).
</math>
The endpoint flatness is
<math display="block">
 F^{(m)}(0)=F^{(m)}(1)=0\qquad(m\geq1).
</math>

Integrating the differential equation gives a self-integral equation, which together with the symmetry and the normalization characterizes <math>F</math>:<ref name="Arias1982"/>
<math display="block">
 F(x)=\int_0^{2x}F(t)\,dt
 \qquad\left(0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\right).
</math>

== Probabilistic and convolution constructions ==

Let <math>U_1,U_2,\ldots</math> be independent random variables, each uniformly distributed on <math>[0,1]</math>. The series
<math display="block">
 X=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}U_n
</math>
converges almost surely and takes values in <math>[0,1]</math>. The Fabius function is
<math display="block">
 F(x)=\Pr(X\leq x).
</math>
The decomposition
<math display="block">
 X\ \stackrel{d}{=}\ \frac{U+X'}2,
</math>
where <math>U</math> is uniform on <math>[0,1]</math> and <math>X'</math> is an independent copy of <math>X</math>, gives
<math display="block">
 F(x)=\int_0^1 F(2x-t)\,dt.
</math>
For <math>0\leq x\leq\tfrac12</math>, the constant continuation <math>F(t)=0</math> for <math>t\leq0</math> reduces this to the self-integral equation above.<ref name="Fabius1966"/><ref name="Arias1982"/>

The elementary moments of <math>X</math> are<ref name="Fabius1966"/><ref name="Arias1982"/>
<math display="block">
 \operatorname E X=\frac12,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac1{36}.
</math>
Set
<math display="block">
 Y=2X-1=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}V_n,
 \qquad V_n=2U_n-1.
</math>
The variables <math>V_n</math> are independent and uniform on <math>[-1,1]</math>. The random variable <math>Y</math> has density <math>u</math>, and
<math display="block">
 F'(x)=2u(2x-1)\qquad(0\leq x\leq1).
</math>
Thus <math>u</math> is the infinite convolution<ref name="JessenWintner1935"/><ref name="Arias1982"/>
<math display="block">
 u=h_1*h_2*h_3*\cdots,\qquad
 h_n(t)=2^{n-1}\mathbf 1_{[-2^{-n},\,2^{-n}]}(t),
</math>
where <math>\mathbf 1_A</math> denotes the indicator of <math>A</math>. This construction accounts for both the compact support and the product formula for the Fourier transform.

== Rvachev's up function ==

The up function associated with <math>F</math> is
<math display="block">
 u(t)=
 \begin{cases}
  F(t+1),&-1\leq t\leq0,\\
  F(1-t),&0\leq t\leq1,\\
  0,&|t|>1.
 \end{cases}
</math>
Equivalently,
<math display="block">
 u(t)=F(1-|t|)\qquad(|t|\leq1).
</math>
It is even, nonnegative, positive on <math>(-1,1)</math>, satisfies <math>u(0)=1</math>, has integral one, and has topological support exactly <math>[-1,1]</math>.<ref name="OEIS288163"/> Up to a constant factor it is the unique smooth compactly supported solution of the [[delay differential equation]]
<math display="block">
 u'(t)=2\bigl(u(2t+1)-u(2t-1)\bigr),
</math>
and the normalization <math>\int_{\mathbb R}u(t)\,dt=1</math> fixes that factor. Every derivative of <math>u</math> vanishes at <math>t=\pm1</math>.<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="Rvachev1990"/>

=== Fourier transform ===

Use the Fourier-transform convention
<math display="block">
 \widehat u(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}u(t)e^{-2\pi izt}\,dt.
</math>
Then <math>\widehat u</math> is an entire even function with <math>\widehat u(0)=1</math>, and has the equivalent product representations<ref name="JessenWintner1935"/><ref name="Arias1982"/>
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat u(z)
 &=\prod_{h=0}^{\infty}
   \frac{\sin(\pi z/2^h)}{\pi z/2^h}\\
 &=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}
   \left(\cos\frac{\pi z}{2^m}\right)^m\\
 &=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}
   \left(1-\frac{z^2}{m^2}\right)^{1+\nu_2(m)} .
 \end{aligned}
</math>
Consequently, the zeros of <math>\widehat u</math> are precisely the nonzero integers, and the zero at <math>m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}</math> has multiplicity <math>1+\nu_2(|m|)</math>.

Fourier inversion gives<ref name="Arias1982"/>
<math display="block">
 u(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat u(t)e^{2\pi itx}\,dt.
</math>
Because <math>u</math> is smooth and compactly supported, its Fourier transform decreases faster than every inverse power on the real axis.

=== Partitions of unity and Fourier series ===

The integer translates of <math>u</math> form a partition of unity:
<math display="block">
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}u(x+k)=1.
</math>
More generally, for every positive integer <math>n</math>,
<math display="block">
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}u\!\left(x+\frac{k}{n}\right)=n.
</math>
Each sum is locally finite because <math>u</math> is compactly supported. These identities also follow from the zeros of <math>\widehat u</math> and the [[Poisson summation formula]].<ref name="Arias1982"/>

The 2-periodic extension of <math>u|_{[-1,1]}</math> has the absolutely and uniformly convergent cosine series<ref name="Arias1982"/>
<math display="block">
 u(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
 \widehat u\!\left(\frac{2j+1}{2}\right)
 \cos\bigl((2j+1)\pi x\bigr),
 \qquad -1\leq x\leq1.
</math>
Only odd harmonics occur because <math>\widehat u(k)=0</math> for every nonzero integer <math>k</math>.

=== Fourier–Legendre expansion ===

Because <math>u</math> is even, only even [[Legendre polynomials]] occur in its Fourier–Legendre series:
<math display="block">
 u(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nP_{2n}(x),
 \qquad -1\leq x\leq1,
</math>
where
<math display="block">
 a_n=\frac{4n+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}u(x)P_{2n}(x)\,dx.
</math>

<!-- REVIEW NOTE: unsourced-to-published-literature. See submission notes. -->
A formula for the coefficients in terms of reciprocal-power values of <math>F</math> is<ref name="ReshetnikovFormalization"/>
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 a_n={}&4^{-n}(4n+1)
 \sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n+k}
 \binom{2n}{n+k}\binom{2n+2k}{2n}(2k)!\\
 &\qquad{}\times
 2^{\binom{2k+1}{2}}F\!\left(2^{-2k-1}\right).
 \end{aligned}
</math>
The series converges absolutely and uniformly on <math>[-1,1]</math>, including at the endpoints.

== Signed global extension and non-analyticity ==

[[File:Graph of the Fabius function.png|thumb|upright=1.7|The signed global extension on the nonnegative real axis. The signs of successive bumps follow the Thue–Morse sequence.]]

There is a unique extension <math>\mathcal F</math> of <math>F</math> to the whole real line that satisfies the self-differential equation everywhere:
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F'(x)=2\mathcal F(2x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).
</math>
It is given by <math>\mathcal F(x)=0</math> for <math>x\leq0</math>, by <math>\mathcal F(x+1)=1-\mathcal F(x)</math> for <math>0\leq x\leq1</math>, and by
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F(x+2^r)=-\mathcal F(x)\qquad(0\leq x\leq2^r)
</math>
for every positive integer <math>r</math>. Equivalently, it is the locally finite sum
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F(x)=
 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{s_2(n)}
 u(x-2n-1).
</math>
It agrees with the bounded Fabius function on <math>[0,1]</math>, and on the interval <math>[2n,2n+2]</math> is a translated copy of <math>u</math> with sign <math>(-1)^{s_2(n)}</math>. Its sequence of signs is therefore the [[Thue–Morse sequence]].<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="Alkauskas2001"/>

Repeated differentiation gives
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F^{(k)}(x)
 =2^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\mathcal F(2^kx)
 \qquad(k\geq0).
</math>
In particular, every positive-order derivative vanishes at every integer.

If <math>q=a/2^n>0</math> is a positive dyadic number, with <math>a</math> a positive integer, then
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F^{(k)}(q)
 =2^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\mathcal F(2^kq).
</math>
For <math>k>n</math>, the argument <math>2^kq</math> is an even integer, so these derivatives vanish. Hence the Taylor series of <math>\mathcal F</math> at a dyadic point is a polynomial of degree at most <math>n</math>; when <math>a</math> is odd, the degree is exactly <math>n</math>. The function does not agree locally with this polynomial, and the dyadic rationals are dense, so <math>F</math> is nowhere real-analytic on <math>[0,1]</math>.<ref name="Arias1982"/>

== Moments and generating functions ==

Let
<math display="block">
 c_n=\int_{-1}^{1}t^{2n}u(t)\,dt
     =\operatorname E(Y^{2n})
 \qquad(n\geq0).
</math>
All odd moments of <math>u</math> vanish. The even moments are rational and satisfy<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>
<math display="block">
 c_0=1,\qquad
 (2n+1)(2^{2n}-1)c_n
 =\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{2n+1}{2k}c_k
 \quad(n\geq1).
</math>
The moment expansion of the Fourier transform is
<math display="block">
 \widehat u(z)=
 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
 (-1)^n\frac{c_n}{(2n)!}(2\pi z)^{2n}.
</math>
The moments are also related to Fabius values by<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>
<math display="block">
 c_n=
 2(2n)!\,2^{\binom{2n+1}{2}}
 F\!\left(2^{-2n-1}\right).
</math>

The moment-generating function of <math>X</math> is
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 D(z)
 &=\operatorname E(e^{zX})
  =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_n\frac{z^n}{n!}\\
 &=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}
   \frac{e^{z/2^j}-1}{z/2^j}\\
 &=e^{z/2}\widehat u\!\left(\frac{iz}{4\pi}\right)\\
 &=1+z\int_0^1e^{zt}u(t)\,dt .
 \end{aligned}
</math>
It obeys the dilation equation
<math display="block">
 D(2z)=\frac{e^z-1}{z}D(z),
</math>
and consequently
<math display="block">
 d_0=1,\qquad
 (n+1)(2^n-1)d_n
 =\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n+1}{k}d_k
 \quad(n\geq1).
</math>
The coefficients are the raw moments <math>d_n=\operatorname E(X^n)</math> and satisfy the identity
<math display="block">
 d_n=n!\,2^{\binom n2}F(2^{-n}),
</math>
which yields the values <math>F(2^{-n})</math> in exact rational arithmetic.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

== Dyadic values and exact computation ==

Every value of <math>F</math> at a dyadic rational is rational. A closed formula can be stated using the even moments <math>c_k</math>. For integers <math>n\geq0</math> and <math>0\leq a\leq2^n</math>,
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 F\!\left(\frac{a}{2^n}\right)
 ={}&\frac{2^{-\binom{n+1}{2}}}{n!}
 \sum_{h=0}^{a-1}(-1)^{s_2(h)}
 \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
 \binom n{2k}\\
 &\qquad{}\times
 (2a-2h-1)^{n-2k}c_k .
 \end{aligned}
</math>
The appearance of the binary digit sum <math>s_2(h)</math> reflects the Thue–Morse signs of the global extension.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

There is also an efficient recursive evaluator. Arias de Reyna attributes the method to V. A. Rvachev and reports having reconstructed it from a Mathematica program that V. Reshetnikov posted on Mathematica Stack Exchange.<ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="MSE120331"/> Given <math>0<x\leq1</math>, choose the unique integer <math>n\geq0</math> such that
<math display="block">
 2^{-n}\leq x<2^{-n+1},
</math>
and put <math>y=x-2^{-n}</math>. Then
<math display="block">
 F(x)=-F(y)+
 \sum_{k=0}^{n}
 \frac{2^{\binom{k+1}{2}}F(2^{k-n})}{k!}\,y^k.
</math>
For a dyadic input, replacing <math>x</math> by <math>y</math> removes its leading nonzero binary digit, so the iteration terminates after as many steps as there are 1s in the binary numerator, and every step is exact rational arithmetic.<ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="Haugland2016"/>

=== Arithmetic of reciprocal-power values ===

For <math>n\geq1</math>, introduce the normalization
<math display="block">
 R_n=
 2^{\binom{n-1}{2}}(2n)!\,
 F(2^{-n})
 \prod_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2^{2m}-1).
</math>
Answering a question of V. Reshetnikov, Arias de Reyna proved that <math>R_n</math> is a positive odd integer.<ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="MO261649"/> Its first values are
<math display="block">
 1,\ 5,\ 15,\ 1001,\ 5985,\ 2853675,\ldots
</math>
The exact 2-adic valuation is<ref name="Arias2018"/>
<math display="block">
 \nu_2\!\left(F(2^{-n})\right)
 =-\binom n2-\nu_2(n!)-1
 \qquad(n\geq1).
</math>

Let <math>D_n</math> be the least common multiple of the denominators, in lowest terms, of <math>u(a/2^n)</math> for odd <math>a</math> with <math>1\leq a<2^n</math>. Then
<math display="block">
 D_n\ \bigm|\
 n!\,2^{\binom{n+1}{2}}
 \bigl(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\bigr)!!
 \prod_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2^{2m}-1).
</math>
Thus the denominators are controlled by a factorial, a power of two, an odd double factorial, and even-indexed Mersenne factors.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

== Asymptotic behaviour near zero ==

The flatness at zero implies
<math display="block">
 F(x)=o(x^N)\qquad(x\to0^+)
</math>
for every fixed <math>N>0</math>, and more precisely
<math display="block">
 \log F(x)\sim-\frac{\log^2x}{2\log2}.
</math>
A sharp description requires both the [[Lambert W function]] and a logarithmically periodic correction.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

<!-- REVIEW NOTE: unsourced-to-published-literature. See submission notes. -->
=== Sharp Lambert-W form ===

Put
<math display="block">
 L=\log2,\qquad
 C_0=\frac{6\gamma^2+12\gamma_1-\pi^2}{12},
</math>
where <math>\gamma</math> is the [[Euler–Mascheroni constant]] and <math>\gamma_1</math> is the first [[Stieltjes constants|Stieltjes constant]], normalized by <math>\zeta(s)=\tfrac1{s-1}+\gamma-\gamma_1(s-1)+\cdots</math>. For sufficiently small positive <math>x</math>, put
<math display="block">
 W=W_{-1}(-Lx),\qquad
 \lambda(x)=-\frac{W}{L},
</math>
where <math>W_{-1}</math> is the lower real branch of the Lambert <math>W</math> function; equivalently, <math>\lambda(x)</math> is the large positive solution of <math>\lambda\,2^{-\lambda}=x</math>. The '''sharp main term''' is<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>
<math display="block">
 M(x)=
 -\frac{7L}{12}-\frac12\log(\pi x)
 +\frac{C_0-W-\tfrac12W^2}{L}
 +\Psi\!\bigl(\lambda(x)\bigr),
</math>
where <math>\Psi</math> is the one-periodic function described below, and, as <math>x\to0^+</math>,
<math display="block">
 \log F(x)=M(x)+O\!\left(\frac1{-\log x}\right).
</math>

<!-- REVIEW NOTE: unsourced-to-published-literature. See submission notes. -->
=== The periodic correction ===

The function <math>\Psi</math> is real-valued, smooth and one-periodic, and is normalized to have mean zero over a period. With <math>\chi_k=2\pi ik/L</math>, its absolutely convergent Fourier series is<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>
<math display="block">
 \Psi(t)=
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}}
 \left(
 -\frac{\Gamma(-\chi_k)\zeta(1-\chi_k)}{L}
 \right)e^{2\pi ikt},
</math>
where <math>\Gamma</math> is the [[gamma function]] and <math>\zeta</math> is the [[Riemann zeta function]]. The coefficient of index <math>k</math> is nonzero for every <math>k\neq0</math>, because the gamma function has no zeros and the zeta function has no zeros on the line <math>\operatorname{Re}s=1</math>, on which <math>1-\chi_k</math> lies. Hence <math>\Psi</math> is nonconstant, and deleting <math>\Psi(\lambda(x))</math> from <math>M(x)</math> leaves a bounded oscillation in <math>\log F(x)</math> that the <math>O(1/(-\log x))</math> remainder cannot absorb. The oscillation is small: <math>\Psi</math> has amplitude about <math>2.1\times10^{-6}</math>.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

=== Expanded elementary form ===

The Lambert phase can itself be expanded in powers of <math>-\log x</math> and <math>\log(-\log x)</math>. Writing
<math display="block">
 \ell=\log x,\qquad
 a=\log(-\ell),\qquad
 b=\log L,
</math>
the sharp form becomes<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 \log F(x)={}&
 -\frac{\ell^2}{2L}
 +\frac{\ell a}{L}
 -\left(\frac12+\frac{1+b}{L}\right)\ell
 -\frac{a^2}{2L}
 +\frac{ba}{L}\\
 &+\left(
 \frac{6\gamma^2+12\gamma_1-\pi^2-6b^2}{12L}
 -\frac{7L}{12}-\frac{\log\pi}{2}
 \right)\\
 &+\frac{a^2}{2L\ell}
 -\frac{ba}{L\ell}
 +\Psi\!\bigl(\lambda(x)\bigr)
 +O\!\left(\frac1{-\ell}\right).
 \end{aligned}
</math>
At this precision the periodic term is evaluated at the exact phase <math>\lambda(x)</math>.

For reciprocal powers of two, let <math>\lambda_n=\lambda(2^{-n})</math>, so that <math>\lambda_n-\log_2\lambda_n=n</math>, and put
<math display="block">
 C_{\mathrm{sharp}}=
 \frac{6\gamma^2+12\gamma_1-\pi^2}{12L}
 -\frac{7L}{12}-\frac{\log\pi}{2}.
</math>
Then<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 \log F(2^{-n})={}&
 -\frac{L}{2}n^2-n\log n
 +\left(1+\frac L2\right)n
 -\frac{\log^2n}{2L}
 +C_{\mathrm{sharp}}\\
 &-\frac{\log^2n}{2L^2n}
 +\Psi(\lambda_n)
 +O\!\left(\frac1n\right).
 \end{aligned}
</math>
The oscillation is periodic in the exact Lambert phase, not in the integer <math>n</math> itself.

=== Origin of the logarithmic oscillation ===

For <math>s>0</math> the moment-generating function has the product
<math display="block">
 D(-s)=
 \prod_{j=1}^{\infty}
 \frac{1-e^{-s/2^j}}{s/2^j},
</math>
whose logarithm has an exact dyadic-scale decomposition<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>
<math display="block">
 \log D(-s)=
 -\frac{\log^2s}{2L}
 +\frac12\log s
 +R(\log_2s)+E(s),
</math>
with <math>R</math> one-periodic and <math>|E(s)|\leq e^{-s}/(1-e^{-s})^2</math>. Rescaling by a factor of 2 therefore contributes an additive period-one term in the logarithmic variable, and saddle-point inversion carries the centred form of that term to <math>\Psi(\lambda(x))</math> in the asymptotic of <math>F(x)</math>.

<!-- REVIEW NOTE: unsourced-to-published-literature. See submission notes. -->
=== Full saddle-point expansion ===

There are continuous one-periodic functions <math>P_1,P_2,\ldots</math> such that, for every integer <math>N\geq1</math>,<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>
<math display="block">
 \log F(x)=
 M(x)+
 \sum_{j=1}^{N-1}
 \frac{P_j(\lambda(x))}{\lambda(x)^j}
 +O\!\left(\lambda(x)^{-N}\right),
</math>
where <math>M</math> is the sharp main term. The first correction is
<math display="block">
 P_1(t)=
 \frac1{24}+\frac1{2L}
 -\frac{\Psi''(t)+(\Psi'(t))^2}{2L^2}.
</math>

== History ==

Arias de Reyna's historical survey records at least six independent appearances of the function or an equivalent one.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

* In 1935, Jessen and Wintner considered the associated distribution and obtained its smoothness through the infinite Fourier product.<ref name="JessenWintner1935"/>
* In 1966, Fabius gave the probabilistic construction as an example of a smooth nowhere-analytic function.<ref name="Fabius1966"/>
* In 1971, V. A. Rvachev introduced the compactly supported solution now called the up function through its functional–differential equation; the work is recorded in a monograph by V. L. Rvachev and V. A. Rvachev, and a later survey by V. A. Rvachev developed a broader theory of compactly supported solutions of such equations.<ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="Rvachev1990"/>
* G. Kh. Kirov and G. A. Totkov gave another independent construction in 1981.<ref name="Arias2018"/>
* Arias de Reyna developed the up function systematically in 1982, proving uniqueness, the probability interpretation, partition-of-unity identities, derivative formulas, rationality at dyadic points and exact computation formulas. An English translation appeared in 2017.<ref name="Arias1982"/>
* R. Schnabl gave a further independent construction in 1985.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

Arias de Reyna's 2018 paper developed the arithmetic of the dyadic values, including the exact 2-adic valuation, the integrality of the numbers <math>R_n</math> conjectured by V. Reshetnikov, denominator bounds and a terminating exact evaluator.<ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="MO261649"/> Haugland gave another explicit treatment of exact evaluation at arbitrary dyadic arguments.<ref name="Haugland2016"/>

<!-- REVIEW NOTE: unsourced-to-published-literature. See submission notes. -->
== Formal verification ==

A Lean 4 development in the ''ProveIt'' repository formalizes the existence and uniqueness of the bounded Fabius function, its probabilistic construction, the up function, Fourier products and inversion, moment recurrences, exact dyadic arithmetic, denominator and valuation theorems, the signed global extension, nowhere analyticity, partition-of-unity formulas and orthogonal expansions. It deliberately keeps the bounded cumulative distribution function separate from the signed global extension, which are both denoted <math>F</math> in parts of the literature.<ref name="ReshetnikovFormalization"/> The same development derives the sharp asymptotic with the nonconstant gamma–zeta periodic term, proves its absolute Fourier reconstruction, and supplies a full exact-Lambert saddle expansion to arbitrary order.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>
