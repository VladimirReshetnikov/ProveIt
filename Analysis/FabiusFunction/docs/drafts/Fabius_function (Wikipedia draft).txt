[[File:Graph of the Fabius function between 0 and 1.png|thumb|upright=1.25|The Fabius function on the unit interval.]]

In [[mathematics]], the '''Fabius function''' is a [[smooth function|smooth]] [[cumulative distribution function]] <math>F\colon[0,1]\to[0,1]</math> that is strictly increasing but [[real analytic function|nowhere real-analytic]]. It is the distribution function of the random series
<math display="block">
 X=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}U_n,
</math>
where the <math>U_n</math> are independent random variables uniformly distributed on <math>[0,1]</math>. Equivalently, it is characterized by
<math display="block">
 F(0)=0,\qquad F(1-x)=1-F(x),\qquad
 F'(x)=2F(2x)\quad\left(0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\right).
</math>
Although every positive-order derivative of <math>F</math> vanishes at both endpoints, <math>F</math> is nonconstant on every subinterval of <math>[0,1]</math>.<ref name="Fabius1966"/><ref name="Arias1982"/>

The same object occurs, after a change of variables, as '''Rvachev's up function''' <math>u</math>, an even <math>C^\infty</math> [[bump function]] supported on <math>[-1,1]</math>. Its Fourier transform is an infinite product of [[sinc function]]s, or equivalently a product of powers of cosines. The function also has an unusual arithmetic side: every value <math>F(a/2^n)</math> at a [[dyadic rational]] is rational, and the numerators, denominators and [[p-adic valuation|2-adic valuations]] of these values obey strong divisibility laws.<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>

The underlying distribution already appeared in work of [[Børge Jessen]] and [[Aurel Wintner]] in 1935. Jaap Fabius independently introduced it probabilistically in 1966 as an example of a nowhere-analytic smooth function, and V. L. Rvachev and V. A. Rvachev independently introduced the associated compactly supported solution of a functional–differential equation.<ref name="JessenWintner1935"/><ref name="Fabius1966"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>

Near zero, <math>F</math> is smaller than every power of its argument. Its sharp asymptotic behaviour is governed by the lower branch of the [[Lambert W function]] and contains a bounded, nonconstant oscillation periodic in an exact logarithmic saddle-point phase. This periodic term is essential: omitting it leaves an order-one error in <math>\log F(x)</math>, rather than a remainder tending to zero.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

The literature uses several incompatible conventions. In this article, <math>F</math> denotes the bounded cumulative distribution function (extended by constants outside <math>[0,1]</math>), <math>u</math> denotes Rvachev's compactly supported up function, and <math>\mathcal F</math> denotes the signed global extension satisfying <math>\mathcal F'(x)=2\mathcal F(2x)</math> on the whole real line.

== Definition and elementary properties ==

There is a unique <math>C^\infty</math> function <math>F\colon\mathbb R\to[0,1]</math> such that
<math display="block">
 F(x)=0\quad(x\leq0),\qquad F(x)=1\quad(x\geq1),
</math>
and, for <math>0\leq x\leq1</math>,
<math display="block">
 F(1-x)=1-F(x),
</math>
while
<math display="block">
 F'(x)=2F(2x)\qquad\left(0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\right).
</math>
The constant continuation outside the unit interval is smooth because all positive-order derivatives vanish at the endpoints.<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>

Using the symmetry, the differential equation can be written on the whole unit interval as
<math display="block">
 F'(x)=
 \begin{cases}
  2F(2x),&0\leq x\leq\tfrac12,\\[3pt]
  2F(2-2x),&\tfrac12\leq x\leq1.
 \end{cases}
</math>
It follows that <math>F'(x)>0</math> for <math>0<x<1</math>, so <math>F</math> is strictly increasing there. In particular,
<math display="block">
 F\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=\tfrac12,\qquad
 F'\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=2,
</math>
and
<math display="block">
 F'(1-x)=F'(x),\qquad
 F'(x)+F'\!\left(\tfrac12-x\right)=2
 \quad\left(0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\right).
</math>
The endpoint flatness is
<math display="block">
 F^{(m)}(0)=F^{(m)}(1)=0\qquad(m\geq1).
</math>

Integrating the differential equation gives a self-integral equation:
<math display="block">
 F(x)=\int_0^{2x}F(t)\,dt
 \qquad\left(0\leq x\leq\tfrac12\right).
</math>
Conversely, this integral equation together with the symmetry and normalization determines <math>F</math>.

== Probabilistic and convolution constructions ==

Let <math>U_1,U_2,\ldots</math> be independent random variables, each uniformly distributed on <math>[0,1]</math>. The series
<math display="block">
 X=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}U_n
</math>
converges almost surely and takes values in <math>[0,1]</math>. The Fabius function is
<math display="block">
 F(x)=\Pr(X\leq x).
</math>
The decomposition
<math display="block">
 X\ \stackrel{d}{=}\ \frac{U+X'}2,
</math>
where <math>U</math> is uniform on <math>[0,1]</math> and <math>X'</math> is an independent copy of <math>X</math>, gives
<math display="block">
 F(x)=\int_0^1 F(2x-t)\,dt.
</math>
For <math>0\leq x\leq\tfrac12</math>, the constant continuation <math>F(t)=0</math> for <math>t\leq0</math> reduces this to the self-integral equation above.<ref name="Fabius1966"/><ref name="Arias1982"/>

The elementary moments of <math>X</math> include
<math display="block">
 \operatorname E X=\frac12,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac1{36}.
</math>
Set
<math display="block">
 Y=2X-1=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}V_n,
 \qquad V_n=2U_n-1.
</math>
The variables <math>V_n</math> are independent and uniform on <math>[-1,1]</math>. The random variable <math>Y</math> has density <math>u</math>, and
<math display="block">
 F'(x)=2u(2x-1)\qquad(0\leq x\leq1).
</math>
Thus <math>u</math> is the infinite convolution
<math display="block">
 u=h_1*h_2*h_3*\cdots,\qquad
 h_n(t)=2^{n-1}\mathbf 1_{[-2^{-n},\,2^{-n}]}(t),
</math>
where <math>\mathbf 1_A</math> denotes the indicator of <math>A</math>. This construction explains both the compact support and the product formula for the Fourier transform.

== Rvachev's up function ==

The up function associated with <math>F</math> is
<math display="block">
 u(t)=
 \begin{cases}
  F(t+1),&-1\leq t\leq0,\\
  F(1-t),&0\leq t\leq1,\\
  0,&|t|>1.
 \end{cases}
</math>
Equivalently,
<math display="block">
 u(t)=F(1-|t|)\qquad(|t|\leq1).
</math>
It is even, nonnegative, positive on <math>(-1,1)</math>, satisfies <math>u(0)=1</math>, has integral one, and has topological support exactly <math>[-1,1]</math>. It is the unique normalized smooth compactly supported solution of the functional–differential equation
<math display="block">
 u'(t)=2\bigl(u(2t+1)-u(2t-1)\bigr).
</math>
Every derivative of <math>u</math> vanishes at <math>t=\pm1</math>.<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Rvachev1990"/>

=== Fourier transform ===

Use the Fourier-transform convention
<math display="block">
 \widehat u(z)=\int_{\mathbb R}u(t)e^{-2\pi izt}\,dt.
</math>
Then <math>\widehat u</math> is an entire even function and has the equivalent product representations
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat u(z)
 &=\prod_{h=0}^{\infty}
   \frac{\sin(\pi z/2^h)}{\pi z/2^h}\\
 &=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}
   \left(\cos\frac{\pi z}{2^m}\right)^m\\
 &=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}
   \left(1-\frac{z^2}{m^2}\right)^{1+\nu_2(m)} .
 \end{aligned}
</math>
where <math>\nu_2(m)</math> is the exponent of 2 in <math>m</math>.<ref name="JessenWintner1935"/><ref name="Arias1982"/> Consequently, the nonzero zeros of <math>\widehat u</math> are precisely the nonzero integers, and the zero at <math>m\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}</math> has multiplicity <math>1+\nu_2(|m|)</math>.

Fourier inversion gives
<math display="block">
 u(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}\widehat u(t)e^{2\pi itx}\,dt.
</math>
Because <math>u</math> is smooth and compactly supported, its Fourier transform decreases faster than every inverse power on the real axis.

=== Partitions of unity and Fourier series ===

The integer translates of <math>u</math> form a partition of unity:
<math display="block">
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}u(x+k)=1.
</math>
More generally, for every positive integer <math>n</math>,
<math display="block">
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}u\!\left(x+\frac{k}{n}\right)=n.
</math>
Each sum is locally finite because <math>u</math> is compactly supported. These identities also follow from the zeros of <math>\widehat u</math> and the [[Poisson summation formula]].<ref name="Arias1982"/>

The 2-periodic extension of <math>u|_{[-1,1]}</math> has the absolutely and uniformly convergent cosine series
<math display="block">
 u(x)=\frac12+
 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
 \widehat u\!\left(\frac{2j+1}{2}\right)
 \cos\bigl((2j+1)\pi x\bigr),
 \qquad -1\leq x\leq1.
</math>
Only odd harmonics occur because <math>\widehat u(k)=0</math> for every nonzero integer <math>k</math>.

=== Fourier–Legendre expansion ===

Because <math>u</math> is even, only even [[Legendre polynomials]] occur in its Fourier–Legendre series:
<math display="block">
 u(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nP_{2n}(x),
 \qquad -1\leq x\leq1,
</math>
where
<math display="block">
 a_n=\frac{4n+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}u(x)P_{2n}(x)\,dx.
</math>
A formula in terms of reciprocal-power values of <math>F</math> is
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 a_n={}&4^{-n}(4n+1)
 \sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n+k}
 \binom{2n}{n+k}\binom{2n+2k}{2n}(2k)!\\
 &\qquad{}\times
 2^{\binom{2k+1}{2}}F\!\left(2^{-2k-1}\right).
 \end{aligned}
</math>
The series converges absolutely and uniformly on <math>[-1,1]</math>, including at the endpoints.<ref name="ReshetnikovFormalization"/>

== Signed global extension and non-analyticity ==

[[File:Graph of the Fabius function.png|thumb|upright=1.7|The signed global extension on the nonnegative real axis. The signs of successive bumps follow the Thue–Morse sequence.]]

Let <math>s_2(n)</math> denote the sum of the binary digits of <math>n</math>. The signed global extension is the locally finite sum
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F(x)=
 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{s_2(n)}
 u(x-2n-1).
</math>
It vanishes for <math>x\leq0</math>, agrees with the bounded Fabius function on <math>[0,1]</math>, and on the interval <math>[2n,2n+2]</math> is a translated copy of <math>u</math> with sign <math>(-1)^{s_2(n)}</math>. Thus its sequence of signs is the [[Thue–Morse sequence]].<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>

Unlike the clamped cumulative distribution function, <math>\mathcal F</math> satisfies the self-differential equation globally:
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F'(x)=2\mathcal F(2x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb R).
</math>
Repeated differentiation gives
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F^{(k)}(x)
 =2^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\mathcal F(2^kx)
 \qquad(k\geq0).
</math>
In particular, every positive-order derivative vanishes at every integer.

If <math>q=a/2^n>0</math> is a positive dyadic number, with <math>a</math> a positive integer, then
<math display="block">
 \mathcal F^{(k)}(q)
 =2^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\mathcal F(2^kq).
</math>
For <math>k>n</math>, the argument <math>2^kq</math> is an even integer, so these derivatives vanish. Hence the Taylor series of <math>\mathcal F</math> at a dyadic point is a polynomial of degree at most <math>n</math>; when <math>a</math> is odd, the degree is exactly <math>n</math>. The function does not agree locally with this polynomial. Since dyadic rationals are dense, no nonempty interval in the nonconstant region can be a domain of analyticity. This gives a direct proof that <math>F</math> is nowhere real-analytic on <math>[0,1]</math>.<ref name="Arias1982"/>

== Moments and generating functions ==

Let
<math display="block">
 c_n=\int_{-1}^{1}t^{2n}u(t)\,dt
     =\operatorname E(Y^{2n})
 \qquad(n\geq0).
</math>
All odd moments of <math>u</math> vanish. The even moments are rational and satisfy
<math display="block">
 c_0=1,\qquad
 (2n+1)(2^{2n}-1)c_n
 =\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{2n+1}{2k}c_k
 \quad(n\geq1).
</math>
The moment expansion of the Fourier transform is
<math display="block">
 \widehat u(z)=
 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
 (-1)^n\frac{c_n}{(2n)!}(2\pi z)^{2n}.
</math>
The moments are also related to Fabius values by
<math display="block">
 c_n=
 2(2n)!\,2^{\binom{2n+1}{2}}
 F\!\left(2^{-2n-1}\right).
</math>
<ref name="Arias1982"/><ref name="Arias2018"/>

The moment-generating function of <math>X</math> is
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 D(z)
 &=\operatorname E(e^{zX})
  =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_n\frac{z^n}{n!}\\
 &=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}
   \frac{e^{z/2^j}-1}{z/2^j}\\
 &=e^{z/2}\widehat u\!\left(\frac{iz}{4\pi}\right)\\
 &=1+z\int_0^1e^{zt}u(t)\,dt .
 \end{aligned}
</math>
It obeys the dilation equation
<math display="block">
 D(2z)=\frac{e^z-1}{z}D(z).
</math>
Consequently,
<math display="block">
 d_0=1,\qquad
 (n+1)(2^n-1)d_n
 =\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n+1}{k}d_k
 \quad(n\geq1).
</math>
The coefficients are the raw moments <math>d_n=\operatorname E(X^n)</math> and satisfy the particularly useful identity
<math display="block">
 d_n=n!\,2^{\binom n2}F(2^{-n}).
</math>
This recurrence is one route to exact computation of the values <math>F(2^{-n})</math>.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

== Dyadic values and exact computation ==

Every value of <math>F</math> at a dyadic rational is rational. A closed formula can be stated using the even moments <math>c_k</math>. For integers <math>n\geq0</math> and <math>0\leq a\leq2^n</math>,
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 F\!\left(\frac{a}{2^n}\right)
 ={}&\frac{2^{-\binom{n+1}{2}}}{n!}
 \sum_{h=0}^{a-1}(-1)^{s_2(h)}
 \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
 \binom n{2k}\\
 &\qquad{}\times
 (2a-2h-1)^{n-2k}c_k .
 \end{aligned}
</math>
The appearance of the binary digit sum <math>s_2(h)</math> reflects the Thue–Morse signs of the global extension.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

There is also an efficient recursive evaluator. Given <math>0<x\leq1</math>, choose the unique integer <math>n\geq0</math> such that
<math display="block">
 2^{-n}\leq x<2^{-n+1},
</math>
and put <math>y=x-2^{-n}</math>. Then
<math display="block">
 F(x)=-F(y)+
 \sum_{k=0}^{n}
 \frac{2^{\binom{k+1}{2}}F(2^{k-n})}{k!}\,y^k.
</math>
For a dyadic input, replacing <math>x</math> by <math>y</math> removes its leading nonzero binary digit, so iteration terminates after the number of 1s in the binary numerator. This gives exact rational arithmetic rather than numerical quadrature.<ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="Haugland2020"/>

=== Arithmetic of reciprocal-power values ===

For <math>n\geq1</math>, introduce the normalization
<math display="block">
 R_n=
 2^{\binom{n-1}{2}}(2n)!\,
 F(2^{-n})
 \prod_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2^{2m}-1).
</math>
Then <math>R_n</math> is a positive odd integer. Its first values are
<math display="block">
 1,\ 5,\ 15,\ 1001,\ 5985,\ 2853675,\ldots
</math>
The exact 2-adic valuation is
<math display="block">
 \nu_2\!\left(F(2^{-n})\right)
 =-\binom n2-\nu_2(n!)-1.
</math>
<ref name="Arias2018"/>

Let <math>D_n</math> be the least common multiple of the denominators, in lowest terms, of <math>u(a/2^n)</math> for odd <math>a</math> with <math>1\leq a<2^n</math>. Then
<math display="block">
 D_n\ \bigm|\ 
 n!\,2^{\binom{n+1}{2}}
 \bigl(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\bigr)!!
 \prod_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(2^{2m}-1).
</math>
Thus the denominators are controlled by a factorial, a power of two, an odd double factorial, and even-indexed Mersenne factors.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

== Asymptotic behaviour near zero ==

All logarithms in this section are natural unless a base is written explicitly. The flatness at zero implies
<math display="block">
 F(x)=o(x^N)\qquad(x\to0^+)
</math>
for every fixed <math>N>0</math>. More precisely,
<math display="block">
 \log F(x)\sim-\frac{\log^2x}{2\log2}.
</math>
A sharp description requires both the Lambert <math>W</math> function and a logarithmically periodic correction.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

=== Sharp Lambert-W form ===

Put
<math display="block">
 L=\log2,\qquad
 C_0=\frac{6\gamma^2+12\gamma_1-\pi^2}{12},
</math>
where <math>\gamma</math> is the [[Euler–Mascheroni constant]] and <math>\gamma_1</math> is the first [[Stieltjes constants|Stieltjes constant]], with convention
<math display="block">
 \zeta(s)=\frac1{s-1}+\gamma-\gamma_1(s-1)+\cdots.
</math>
For sufficiently small positive <math>x</math>, define
<math display="block">
 W=W_{-1}(-Lx),\qquad
 \lambda(x)=-\frac{W}{L},
</math>
where <math>W_{-1}</math> is the lower real branch of the Lambert <math>W</math> function. Equivalently, <math>\lambda(x)</math> is the large positive solution of
<math display="block">
 \lambda\,2^{-\lambda}=x.
</math>
Then, as <math>x\to0^+</math>,
<math display="block">
 \boxed{\;
 \log F(x)=
 -\frac{7L}{12}-\frac12\log(\pi x)
 +\frac{C_0-W-\tfrac12W^2}{L}
 +\Psi\!\bigl(\lambda(x)\bigr)
 +O\!\left(\frac1{-\log x}\right)
 \;}
</math>
and hence
<math display="block">
 F(x)\sim
 \frac{2^{-7/12}}{\sqrt{\pi x}}\,
 \exp\!\left(
 \frac{C_0-W-\tfrac12W^2}{L}
 +\Psi\!\bigl(\lambda(x)\bigr)
 \right).
</math>
<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

The function <math>\Psi</math> is real-valued, smooth, one-periodic and normalized to have mean zero:
<math display="block">
 \Psi(t+1)=\Psi(t),\qquad
 \int_0^1\Psi(t)\,dt=0.
</math>
Let
<math display="block">
 \chi_k=\frac{2\pi i k}{L}.
</math>
Its absolutely convergent Fourier series is
<math display="block">
 \boxed{\;
 \Psi(t)=
 \sum_{k\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}}
 \left(
 -\frac{\Gamma(-\chi_k)\zeta(1-\chi_k)}{L}
 \right)e^{2\pi ikt}.
 \;}
</math>
Here <math>\Gamma</math> is the [[gamma function]] and <math>\zeta</math> is the [[Riemann zeta function]]. Every nonzero Fourier coefficient in this series is nonzero, so <math>\Psi</math> is genuinely nonconstant. Therefore deleting <math>\Psi(\lambda(x))</math> from the sharp formula leaves a bounded nonvanishing oscillation in <math>\log F(x)</math>; it cannot be absorbed into an <math>O(1/(-\log x))</math> remainder.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

=== Expanded elementary form ===

The Lambert phase can itself be expanded in powers of <math>-\log x</math> and <math>\log(-\log x)</math>. To display the result compactly, write
<math display="block">
 \ell=\log x,\qquad
 a=\log(-\ell),\qquad
 b=\log L.
</math>
Then the preceding formula gives
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 \log F(x)={}&
 -\frac{\ell^2}{2L}
 +\frac{\ell a}{L}
 -\left(\frac12+\frac{1+b}{L}\right)\ell
 -\frac{a^2}{2L}
 +\frac{ba}{L}\\
 &+\left(
 \frac{6\gamma^2+12\gamma_1-\pi^2-6b^2}{12L}
 -\frac{7L}{12}-\frac{\log\pi}{2}
 \right)\\
 &+\frac{a^2}{2L\ell}
 -\frac{ba}{L\ell}
 +\Psi\!\bigl(\lambda(x)\bigr)
 +O\!\left(\frac1{-\ell}\right).
 \end{aligned}
</math>
The periodic term must remain evaluated at the exact phase <math>\lambda(x)</math> at this precision; replacing its argument by an insufficiently accurate truncated phase can produce an error larger than the displayed remainder.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

For reciprocal powers of two, let
<math display="block">
 \lambda_n=\lambda(2^{-n}),
 \qquad\text{so that}\qquad
 \lambda_n-\log_2\lambda_n=n.
</math>
With
<math display="block">
 C_{\mathrm{sharp}}=
 \frac{6\gamma^2+12\gamma_1-\pi^2}{12L}
 -\frac{7L}{12}-\frac{\log\pi}{2},
</math>
one convenient dyadic expansion is
<math display="block">
 \begin{aligned}
 \log F(2^{-n})={}&
 -\frac{L}{2}n^2-n\log n
 +\left(1+\frac L2\right)n
 -\frac{\log^2n}{2L}
 +C_{\mathrm{sharp}}\\
 &-\frac{\log^2n}{2L^2n}
 +\Psi(\lambda_n)
 +O\!\left(\frac1n\right).
 \end{aligned}
</math>
The oscillation is periodic in the exact Lambert phase, not in the integer <math>n</math> itself.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

=== Origin of the logarithmic oscillation ===

The moment-generating function at a negative real argument has the product
<math display="block">
 D(-s)=
 \prod_{j=1}^{\infty}
 \frac{1-e^{-s/2^j}}{s/2^j}
 \qquad(s>0).
</math>
Its logarithm has an exact dyadic-scale decomposition
<math display="block">
 \log D(-s)=
 -\frac{\log^2s}{2L}
 +\frac12\log s
 +R(\log_2s)+E(s),
</math>
where <math>R(t+1)=R(t)</math> and
<math display="block">
 |E(s)|\leq
 \frac{e^{-s}}{(1-e^{-s})^2}.
</math>
Thus multiplicative rescaling by 2 naturally produces an additive period-one term in the logarithmic variable. Saddle-point inversion transfers the centered form of this term to <math>\Psi(\lambda(x))</math> in the asymptotic of <math>F(x)</math>.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

=== Full saddle-point expansion ===

Let <math>M(x)</math> denote the boxed sharp main term above, including <math>\Psi(\lambda(x))</math> but excluding the error term. There are continuous one-periodic functions <math>P_1,P_2,\ldots</math> such that, for every integer <math>N\geq1</math>,
<math display="block">
 \log F(x)=
 M(x)+
 \sum_{j=1}^{N-1}
 \frac{P_j(\lambda(x))}{\lambda(x)^j}
 +O\!\left(\lambda(x)^{-N}\right).
</math>
The first correction is
<math display="block">
 P_1(t)=
 \frac1{24}+\frac1{2L}
 -\frac{\Psi''(t)+(\Psi'(t))^2}{2L^2}.
</math>
In particular,
<math display="block">
 \log F(x)=M(x)+
 \frac{P_1(\lambda(x))}{\lambda(x)}
 +O\!\left(\lambda(x)^{-2}\right).
</math>
This expansion separates the nonoscillatory Lambert geometry from a hierarchy of bounded periodic saddle coefficients.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>

== History ==

Arias de Reyna's historical survey records at least six independent appearances of the function or an equivalent one.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

* In 1935, Jessen and Wintner considered the associated distribution and obtained its smoothness through the infinite Fourier product.<ref name="JessenWintner1935"/>
* In 1966, Fabius gave the probabilistic construction as an example of a smooth nowhere-analytic function.<ref name="Fabius1966"/>
* In 1971, V. L. Rvachev and V. A. Rvachev introduced the compactly supported solution now called the up function through its functional–differential equation. A later survey by V. A. Rvachev developed a broader theory of compactly supported solutions of such equations.<ref name="Arias2018"/><ref name="Rvachev1990"/>
* G. Kh. Kirov and G. A. Totkov gave another independent construction in 1981.<ref name="Arias2018"/>
* Arias de Reyna developed the up function systematically in 1982, proving uniqueness, the probability interpretation, partition-of-unity identities, derivative formulas, rationality at dyadic points and exact computation formulas. An English translation appeared in 2017.<ref name="Arias1982"/>
* R. Schnabl gave a further independent construction in 1985.<ref name="Arias2018"/>

Arias de Reyna's 2018 paper developed the arithmetic of the dyadic values, including the exact 2-adic valuation, the integrality of the numbers <math>R_n</math>, denominator bounds and a terminating exact evaluator.<ref name="Arias2018"/> Haugland later gave another explicit treatment of exact evaluation at arbitrary dyadic arguments.<ref name="Haugland2020"/>

== Formal verification ==

A Lean 4 development in the ''ProveIt'' repository formalizes the existence and uniqueness of the bounded Fabius function, its probabilistic construction, the up function, Fourier products and inversion, moment recurrences, exact dyadic arithmetic, denominator and valuation theorems, the signed global extension, nowhere analyticity, partition-of-unity formulas and orthogonal expansions. It deliberately keeps the bounded cumulative distribution function separate from the signed global extension, which are both denoted <math>F</math> in parts of the literature.<ref name="ReshetnikovFormalization"/>

The same development derives the corrected sharp asymptotic with the nonconstant gamma–zeta periodic term, proves its absolute Fourier reconstruction, and supplies a full exact-Lambert saddle expansion to arbitrary order.<ref name="ReshetnikovAsymptotic"/>
