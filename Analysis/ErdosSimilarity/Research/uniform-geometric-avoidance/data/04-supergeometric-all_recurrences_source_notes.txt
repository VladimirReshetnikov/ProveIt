COMPACT ALL-RECURRENCE EXTENSION: SOURCE AND AUDIT NOTES
Prepared 8 October 2026.

Primary background source:
Stefan Gerhold, The Shape of the Value Sets of Linear Recurrence
Sequences, Journal of Integer Sequences 12 (2009), Article 09.3.6.
Published 27 April 2009.
https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL12/Gerhold/gerhold5.pdf
https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.4043

The published primary article and its arXiv counterpart were inspected.

What is used:
The bounded/unit-modulus portion of Gerhold's argument: after a stable
remainder tends to zero, a finite sum of unit-root exponentials can be
split into finitely many residue classes whose closures are continuous
real torus images, hence compact intervals. See PDF pages 2-3, equations
(2)-(4), and the final Kronecker remark on PDF page 4.

The extension proves this independently. It isolates individual active
roots by shift operators, uses a Q-basis of their arguments followed by
one denominator q, and gives the elementary Fourier-average proof of
the required density. No closed-subgroup or compact Lie group theorem
is invoked. The classical structural lemma is not claimed novel.

Source interpretation:
Gerhold's published proof, PDF page 2, states that when the dominant
root modulus differs from one, or the dominant polynomial degree is
positive, |a_n| tends to zero or infinity. As stated this inference is
not valid for general oscillatory recurrences: e.g. a_n=n(1+(-1)^n)
has zeros on every odd index and diverges on even indices. We neither
quote nor rely on that inference or on the general unbounded closure
theorem. Our all-recurrence avoidance proof reduces unbounded patterns
directly by compactness; only bounded recurrence structure is needed.
This observation does not establish that the whole published theorem
is false, and no such claim is made.

Scope checks:
- The stronger theorem applies to compact K=F intersect [0,1].
- It is false for the nonempty 1-periodic F itself: x+Z lies in F.
- Infinite range is necessary for universal avoidance; finite patterns
  occur at sufficiently small scales in any positive-measure set.
- Infinite range of a bounded sequence persists after finite truncation.
- At least one residue subsequence then has infinite range.
- Arithmetic subsequences satisfy a constant-coefficient recurrence by
  applying Cayley-Hamilton to T^q.
- Subtracting the limit adjoins at most the root 1 to an annihilator.
- The previous null-recurrence theorem excludes infinitely many
  distinct points, not just infinitely many repeated occurrences.


Integration: the extension is included in Section 5 of the delivered article.
