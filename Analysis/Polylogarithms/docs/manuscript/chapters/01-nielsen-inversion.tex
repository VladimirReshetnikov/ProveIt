\subsubsection*{A finite inversion formula}
The earlier draft inferred failure of closure from a search in the basket
$\{S_{1,2}(z),\zeta(3),\pi^2\log z,\log^3z\}$. That basket omits
polylogarithms and mixed log products needed by the actual transformation.
Inversion does close in the Nielsen family after those products and endpoint
constants are included. The following direct coefficient construction makes
the distinction precise.

\begin{proposition}[Nielsen inversion with explicit endpoint data]
For integers $n,p\ge1$, let $u=\log(-z)$ on
$\mathbb C\setminus[0,\infty)$ and define
\begin{align*}
\mathcal N_{n,p}(z)={}&\frac{u^{n+p}}{(n+p)!}\\
&+\sum_{r=1}^p\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-r}(-1)^\ell
 \binom{n+\ell-1}{\ell}
 \frac{u^{p-r-\ell}}{(p-r-\ell)!}S_{n+\ell,r}(z).
\end{align*}
Then
\[
S_{n,p}(1/z)=(-1)^n\mathcal N_{n,p}(z)+C_{n,p}(u),
\]
where $C_{n,p}$ is a polynomial of degree at most $n-1$. Its coefficients
are determined entirely by Nielsen values at $-1$: for $0\le j<n$,
\begin{align*}
C_{n,p}^{(j)}(0)={}&(-1)^j S_{n-j,p}(-1)\\
&-(-1)^n\sum_{r=1}^p(-1)^{p-r}
 \binom{n-j+p-r-1}{p-r}S_{n-j+p-r,r}(-1).
\end{align*}
All logarithms and polylogarithms use the common principal continuation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First take $z<0$. Extend the notation by
$S_{0,r}(z)=(-1)^r\log^r(1-z)/r!$ and $S_{0,0}=1$.
Since $\log(1-1/z)=\log(1-z)-u$, the binomial theorem gives
\[
S_{0,p}(1/z)=\sum_{r=0}^p\frac{u^{p-r}}{(p-r)!}S_{0,r}(z).
\]
The ladder $dS_{m,r}/du=S_{m-1,r}$ implies, by Pascal's identity,
that $d\mathcal N_{n,p}/du=\mathcal N_{n-1,p}$, with
$\mathcal N_{0,p}=S_{0,p}(1/z)$. On the other hand
$dS_{n,p}(1/z)/du=-S_{n-1,p}(1/z)$.
Consequently the difference has vanishing $n$th derivative in $u$
and is a polynomial of degree at most $n-1$. Evaluate its first
$n$ derivatives at $u=0$, namely $z=-1$, to obtain the displayed
endpoint coefficients. Both sides are holomorphic on the stated slit
plane, so the identity theorem supplies the continuation.
\end{proof}
At weight three this gives the particularly useful exact identity
\[
S_{1,2}(1/z)=-S_{1,2}(z)+\Li_3(z)-\log(-z)\Li_2(z)
-\frac16\log^3(-z)+\zeta(3).
\]
Closure of a function representation does not establish independence of
its values. Other argument changes should likewise be derived by transporting
the kernels in the appropriate alphabet, rather than by an unsuccessful
search in an underspecified basket.
