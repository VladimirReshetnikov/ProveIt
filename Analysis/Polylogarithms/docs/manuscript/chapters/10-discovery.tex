\chapter{Experimental discovery and the route to proof}\label{ch:10-discovery}
The mathematical progression of the manuscript suggests a practical method.
One begins with a value that is easy to compute but hard to recognize, finds
an exact transformation that organizes its arguments, and then asks which
coordinates remain after known laws are imposed. Numerical experiments are
especially effective at proposing those coordinates. Their usefulness
increases when the proposed identity is subsequently derived, and when a
negative result is recorded with enough detail to explain its limits.

\section{Constructing a meaningful integer-relation experiment}
An experiment consists of a target, a finite ordered basket of constants,
their coefficient field, a working precision, a coefficient bound and an
acceptance rule. Weight and character parity give strong guidance for the
basket. For instance a rational-angle Fourier transform can require
$\sqrt q$-weighted atoms; searching only with rational coefficients in the
unweighted values misses that structure. The
$\pi^3\psi^{(-3)}(1/4)$ coordinate in Chapter~\ref{ch:08-differentiation}
is another example: omitting the $\pi^3$ factor changes the question.

Before searching, remove known basket dependencies. A search containing
both $\log\Gamma(1/2)$ and $\frac12\log\pi$ may return their tautology
instead of a relation involving the target. Reflecting arguments,
distributing roots of unity and replacing elementary odd beta values
reduce this problem before numerical linear algebra starts.

Working precision must exceed the cancellation loss as well as the digits
needed to discriminate the proposed coefficient height. The heuristic
$10^{P/d}$, for $P$ available digits and $d$ basket coordinates, estimates
a scale at which accidental short numerical relations can appear. It is
neither a theorem about a specific basket nor a rigorous exclusion bound.
Coefficient sizes, conditioning and the number of searches all matter.
The earlier false negative for $S_5$ illustrates this: a $51$-coordinate
basket at $150$ digits obscured the height-$3840$ relation that a smaller,
properly structured basket exposed.

\section{Controls and independent recomputation}
A planted positive control checks that the search can recover a known
relation of comparable scale. A perturbation of one coefficient tests the
resolution of the residual check. A negative control and an auxiliary
``canary'' constant can expose numerical coincidences. These controls are
diagnostics. In particular no independence theorem is known between the
Champernowne constant and the periods in these baskets, and a zero canary
coefficient cannot certify an identity.

A successful candidate should be frozen as exact rational or algebraic
coefficients, then reevaluated at higher precision using an independent
representation. For a nested sum that may be an iterated integral; for a
CM row sum it may be a modular Fourier series; for Herglotz values it may
be a defining integral compared with the finite dilogarithm sum.
Agreement between two engines using the same incorrect formula is less
informative than agreement between different mathematical representations.
Error-controlled interval evaluation can establish a finite residual
bound, but even an arbitrarily small nonzero numerical bound alone does
not establish equality of general transcendental constants.

\section{What exact linear algebra proves}
An exact relation matrix proves an upper bound on the number of necessary
coordinates in its presented quotient. Its row-combination matrix is a
replayable finite certificate relative to the input identities. A free
column is independent in that formal quotient, not automatically among
the evaluated numerical periods. Additional functional equations or
regularization can reduce the quotient further.

Likewise a motivic dimension theorem concerns motivic objects. To infer
numerical independence one needs a period-injectivity statement, generally
conjectural. A dimension for a chosen word-length grading need not be the
dimension of the classical depth filtration. The Gaussian triples are a
natural test case for this distinction.

Certificates require both exact atom cancellation and sound law
instantiation. Substitution strings must be parsed within a restricted
grammar; branches and domains must be established mathematically. A
numerical branch guard is useful for rejecting errors but leaves a
separate domain obligation. These issues motivate a future formal
development rather than a current claim of proof-assistant verification.

\section{Computational conventions}
The manuscript uses decreasing nested indices. In the tested Wolfram
Language~15 interface,
\[
\Li_{a,b}(z_1,z_2)=
\texttt{MultiplePolyLog[\{a,b\},\{z1,z2\}]},\qquad
\zeta(a,b)=\texttt{MultipleZeta[\{a,b\}]}.
\]
Finite checks such as $\zeta(2,1,1)=\zeta(4)$ and
$H(0,1;z)=\Li_2(z)$ calibrate the interface. A divergent call does not
resolve ordering ambiguities on its own. The same calibration is needed
for signed harmonic indices and generalized-polylogarithm letter order.

Negative-order polygamma means the integral based at zero:
$\psi^{(-1)}=\log\Gamma$ and
$\psi^{(-n)}(z)=\int_0^z\psi^{(-(n-1))}(t)\,dt$ for $n\ge2$.
Balanced conventions can differ by explicit polynomials. In Hurwitz jets,
$\zeta^{(j)}$ always differentiates the spectral variable; the superscript
$(k)$ on $\gamma_n$ differentiates its parameter.
The CM hexagonal point $e^{i\pi/3}$ and the Eisenstein cube root
$e^{2\pi i/3}$ are different arguments even when a local section denotes
each by $\rho$.

\section{The surviving research programme}
Several concrete proof problems now fit into one framework. The odd-weight
$S_p$ directions need a precise choice of comparison algebra and a
rigorous reduction or obstruction; the even-weight direction has the
generating-integral proof of Chapter~\ref{ch:04-depth}. The Gaussian and
Eisenstein inverse-color points now have an all-weight partial-fraction
reduction, and the distribution ranks have a complete character normal
form. A full regularized relation computation remains useful for the other
colored points. Numerical independence of character derivative coordinates
remains a separate arithmetic problem.

The gamma programme can transport its exact row certificates across the
DFT and prove branch validity for each law instance. Its finite-matrix
termination should remain separate from Rohrlich's conjecture. The CM
programme can replace fitted coefficients by modular equations and
class-field computations, while taking account of periods under Galois
conjugation. The Herglotz programme needs complete explicit class-character
and unit definitions for the regulator candidates before they become
locally replayable, followed by the relevant Stark argument.

The cubic log-gamma moment now has the explicit Tornheim representation
and certified enclosure of Section~\ref{gaussian:sec:gamma}. Reduction of
those derivative coordinates into a stated smaller algebra remains a precise
question, alongside the opaque individual CM components. The Gaussian
weight-five relation, weight-six doubles, weight-four triples and cubic/quartic
ladders likewise now have exact proofs. The $S_4$ relation now has the exact certificate of
Theorem~\ref{gaussian:thm:S4}. Higher golden ladder certificates and
numerical period independence remain open; the $S_6$ candidate supplies
a concrete next mixed-reduction problem. A richer representation is often more productive than a
larger unstructured search. The scientific value of the experiments is
that they tell us which representation to seek next.

\section{Questions sharpened by the exact continuations}
\subsection{Zero geometry beyond a fixed Laurent index}
The complete classical counts are now known through Laurent index seven.
The fifth-index transition has threshold three, and
Theorem~\ref{higherzeros:thm:thresholds} proves sharp thresholds four and
five at indices six and seven. Critical-point descent determines the
deficits below saturation with exact signs, rather than extrapolating from
a root scan. Small-order classification for higher indices remains open.
Effective thresholds uniform in the positive Lerch
parameter require quantitative separation of the Appell roots and explicit
concentration remainders. The spectral coefficient expansion for
$n=O(\log k)$ does not by itself supply moving-zero asymptotics throughout
that range, because the extreme root slopes can leave a compact scale.
For higher zero branches, monotonicity in the deformation parameter is
suggested by the first correction but is proved globally only at $n=1$.

\subsection{Certificates, integral structure and arithmetic evaluation}
The complex distribution module is free even at resonant weights. What
changes there is the usefulness of selected coordinates, not its rank.
Integral Smith forms, torsion, cyclotomic coefficient arithmetic and short
certificate generation remain separate questions. The same distinction
applies to the exterior-symbol calculus: a nonzero formal obstruction rules
out a certificate in that calculus, while the kernel of numerical period
evaluation is a deeper arithmetic problem. Constructive reduction can now
be benchmarked against an exact admissibility criterion rather than a
denominator heuristic.

\subsection{Further positive kernels and approximation regimes}
The harmonic-index transition and Herglotz optimal cutoff illustrate two
ways that positive kernels convert experiments into uniform asymptotics.
Other roots of unity introduce complex weights and can destroy positivity;
their extensions need a replacement argument rather than a formal reuse of
the signed bounds. The general $J(p/q)$ symbol problem extends the proved
$J(2/q)$ classification. At the optimal approximation scale, algebraic
corrections and exponentially smaller arithmetic shifts require separately
defined reference transitions. Their numerical diagnostics should retain
that separation of scales.

\subsection{A focused path toward formal verification}
The finite algebraic interfaces are suitable first targets: character
projectors, prime distribution edges, polynomial normal forms, exact
partial fractions and the rational interval operations. Analytical
formalization can then isolate the root-preserving operator, Laplace
variation argument, positive-kernel remainder theorems and local normalized
functional equation. These are explicit future tasks, not claims that the
present ordinary proofs have already been checked by a proof assistant.


\section{From decimal fits to short executable proofs}
The Gaussian continuation illustrates a complete experimental cycle. A
weight-five relation first found with large coefficients is the combination
of two simple shuffle rows; the parity theorem then explains all five
weight-six double reductions. The one-two integral supplies the three
weight-four triples, while algebraic argument substitutions and rational
row operations certify the cubic and quartic ladders. The cubic log-gamma
moment requires a Tornheim derivative coordinate rather than a larger
unstructured single-value basket.

The accompanying fresh replay re-expands all 511 ending-in-one binary
words through weight nine and checks 45 bigraded sectors. It verifies 35
exact algebraic ladder substitutions and reproduces the integer cubic
moment certificate, whose endpoints share 112 decimal places. Independent
defining-integral comparisons check 64 double and one-two formulas. The
Chebyshev implementation supplies 89 interval residual enclosures, including
one for $S_4$, which was conjectural at that checkpoint. That enclosure
containing zero did not prove equality; the later octahedral certificate
provides the exact argument. Six separate midpoint rectangles enclose the
Gaussian and Eisenstein inverse-color values to widths below $10^{-70}$.
These finite replays test the implementations. The all-weight and asymptotic
statements rest on the analytic and algebraic arguments in the chapters.


\section{What the new reports change in the research programme}
The exact $S_4$ proof adds a convergent octahedral word transformation and
higher-depth lifted rows to a vocabulary that the finite obstructions had
shown insufficient. The weight-three companion in Theorem~\ref{s2proof:thm:S2} supplies a
second exact mixed reduction. The product-matrix rank conjecture is now
proved by Theorem~\ref{l4rank:thm:rank}. The next task is to compress the
larger certificate and
investigate the frozen $S_6$ relation with the same analytic control.
Pure complementary seeds now have a complete collision classification and
an algebraic-degree cutoff; arbitrary multiplicative unit relations and
maximal ladder weights remain broader questions.

Primitive-grid determinants and Smith factors quantify coordinate failures
without changing the free distribution quotient. Explicit finite descent
and pole-cancelled Fourier jets give constructive all-index identities and
trace zeros; integral lattices and certified high-order implementations
remain distinct developments. The new sharp and reflected moment theories
separate fixed-exponent asymptotics, optimal truncation and simultaneous
large-exponent limits. Likewise, the Herglotz oscillation is proved for fixed
positive argument and large derivative order; joint scaling requires further
uniform estimates.

\section{A certified rejection beyond the discovery precision}
\label{research:sec:S8-rejected}
A new Cayley continuation supplies a useful counterexample to premature
promotion of integer-relation output. At weight nine, define the ordered
basket
\[
 \mathbf b_8=(S_8,g_{8,1},g_{7,2},g_{6,3},g_{5,4},\pi^9,
 \beta(8)\log2,\beta(2)\zeta(7),\beta(4)\zeta(5),\beta(6)\zeta(3)).
\]
A search with 110 working digits, tolerance $10^{-90}$ and coefficient
ceiling $10^{12}$ returned the integer vector
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathbf c_8=(&-6451250972,-4072849461,-710107844,-10984374763,\\
 &-6393592639,31875,-1955138863,3459066102,-1034180101,-1477786143).
\end{split}
\]
\begin{proposition}[The frozen weight-nine vector is false]
\label{research:prop:S8-rejected}
With $R_8=\mathbf c_8\cdot\mathbf b_8$,
\[
 -1.3274179020580459\times10^{-86}<R_8
 <-1.3274179020580457\times10^{-86}<0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The delivered rational evaluator encloses each harmonic double and each
single atom with 1000 Euler terms, using the explicit signed tail budgets.
It forms all products and the frozen linear combination in exact interval
arithmetic. The resulting rational residual interval has width less than
$2.080\times10^{-290}$ and lies strictly inside the displayed negative
interval. The fresh isolated replay recomputes every rational atom endpoint
and the residual, comparing exact fractions with the preserved record
\texttt{reports/cayley-s4-continuation/data/S8\_exclusion\_N1000.json}.
Both the negative sign and the containment are exact rational comparisons.
This computation proves the displayed exclusion; it uses no floating-point
sign or integer-relation search as an acceptance criterion.
\end{proof}
This rejects precisely the frozen vector, not every possible relation in
the basket. A later unsuccessful higher-precision search likewise proves
no arithmetic independence. In contrast the new alternative $S_6$ vector
still has an enclosure containing zero; it remains conjectural, and Proposition~\ref{s6change:prop:coordinates} proves by two convergent
shuffles that it is the earlier $S_6$ conjecture in a different basket.
