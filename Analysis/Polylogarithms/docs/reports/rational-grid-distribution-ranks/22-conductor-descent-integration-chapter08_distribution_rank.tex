\begin{theorem}[Uniform rank of the complete distribution presentation]
\label{tower:thm:rank}
For $q\ge2$, let $M_q(\mathbf t)$ have columns $e_1,\ldots,e_q$,
where $e_a$ represents the reduced fraction $a/q$ and $e_q$ is the endpoint.
Its rows, for every prime $p\mid q$ and $1\le b\le q/p$, are
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{p-1}e_{b+jq/p}-t_p e_{pb}.
\]
For every complex specialization of the prime weights,
\[
 \operatorname{rank}M_q(\mathbf t)
 =\operatorname{rank}\widehat M_q(\mathbf t)=q-\varphi(q),
\]
where $\widehat M_q$ is obtained by deleting the endpoint column, not
by omitting rows that involve the endpoint. Thus the endpoint-fixed
formal quotient has dimension $\varphi(q)-1$.
In particular this holds for the Stieltjes top-index distribution rows
$t_p=p^{k+1}$ for all $k\ge1$ and all Stieltjes indices $n\ge0$,
with lower indices treated as known.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work first over a characteristic-zero splitting field $K$, with
independent variables $t_p$ and coefficient ring $R=K[t_p:p\mid q]$.
Organize the $q$ fraction symbols by exact denominator $d\mid q$.
For each primitive character $\chi^*$ of conductor $f\mid d$, set
\[
 Z_{d,\chi^*}=\sum_{a\in(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z)^\times}
       \chi^*(a)E(a/d),
\]
using $Z_{1,1}=E(1)$ at conductor one. Character orthogonality is an
invertible change of coordinates at each denominator.

The prime row with reduced parent $a/d$ is
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{p-1}E\bigl((a+jd)/(pd)\bigr)-t_pE(a/d),
 \qquad pd\mid q.
\]
Summing over units $a$ with weight $\chi^*(a)$ gives
\[
 Z_{pd,\chi^*}=\begin{cases}
 t_pZ_{d,\chi^*},&p\mid d,\\
 (t_p-\chi^*(p))Z_{d,\chi^*},&p\nmid d.
 \end{cases}
\]
In the second case exactly one lift is divisible by $p$; after reducing
it contributes $\chi^*(p)Z_{d,\chi^*}$. Fourier inversion on the parent
units recovers every original row, so these are all relations in each
character component.

For fixed $\chi^*$, the available denominators are $f\mid d\mid q$.
The edge relations express all coordinates in terms of $Z_{f,\chi^*}$:
\[
 Z_{d,\chi^*}=\left(
 \prod_{p\mid f}t_p^{v_p(d)-v_p(f)}
 \prod_{\substack{p\mid d\\p\nmid f}}
 t_p^{v_p(d)-1}(t_p-\chi^*(p))\right)Z_{f,\chi^*}.
\]
Different primes commute. Mapping $Z_{d,\chi^*}$ to the displayed
polynomial respects every row and maps $Z_{f,\chi^*}$ to $1$.
Thus each character component is free of rank one, not merely generated
by one element. The number of primitive characters with conductor dividing
$q$ is $\varphi(q)$. The quotient is therefore free of rank $\varphi(q)$
over $R$, and this normal form survives every specialization without division.

The endpoint is the free basis vector $Z_{1,1}$. Fixing it removes exactly
one quotient dimension, proving the same rank after endpoint-column deletion.
Finally,
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(-1)^n\gamma_n^{(k)}(a)}{n!}\varepsilon^n
 =(-1)^k(1+\varepsilon)_k\zeta(k+1+\varepsilon,a)
\]
has a common invertible factor for $k\ge1$. At top index $n$ its
coefficient rows have $t_p=p^{k+1}$; lower-index terms are inhomogeneous
known quantities. This gives the Stieltjes assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope and higher jets]
This theorem specifies the complete presentation rather than identifying
it with an unavailable historical script. Composite divisor rows follow
from prime rows by repeated distribution, with multiplicative weights.
For jets through order $N$, substitute
$t_p=p^{s_0}\exp(\varepsilon\log p)$ in the polynomial normal form,
modulo $\varepsilon^{N+1}$. The full rank is
$(N+1)(q-\varphi(q))$, and fixing all endpoint jets leaves
$(N+1)(\varphi(q)-1)$ formal coordinates. These are dimensions of a
presented quotient, not numerical independence results for the evaluated
constants. The character method is compatible with classical
universal-distribution theory; the accompanying research note gives
attribution, constructive descent formulas and exact certificates.
\end{remark}
