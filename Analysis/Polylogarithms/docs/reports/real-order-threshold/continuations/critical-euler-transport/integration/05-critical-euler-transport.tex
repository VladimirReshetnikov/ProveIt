\subsection{The sharp critical Euler constant and its finite-depth limit}
\label{criticaleuler:section}
For $0<a<1$, write
\[
 F_{a,1-a}(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{H_{n-1}^{(1-a)}}{n^a}z^n,
 \qquad g_a=\operatorname{Im}F_{a,1-a}(i).
\]
At unit radius the value is analytic/Abel, not an ordinary convergent sum.
Let $E_N$ be the usual $N$-term Euler transform and
$R_N(a)=2^N(E_N-g_a)$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp uniform critical constant]
\label{criticaleuler:constant}
The map $a\mapsto-2g_a$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,1)$, and
\[
 0<E_N-g_a<\left(\frac\pi4+\frac{\log2}{2}\right)2^{-N}
 \qquad(N\ge1).
\]
The constant is optimal uniformly over the critical line and all $N$.
\end{theorem}
This resolves Conjecture 12.3 of the incoming real-order-threshold report.
The proof is Theorem 3.2 of \emph{Critical Euler Errors for Harmonic
Polylogarithms}. Its beta--gamma comparison applies more generally at
fixed total order and imaginary radii at most $\sqrt3$.

The critical negative density satisfies $p_a(T)>1$. Consequently
\[
 \frac1a-\frac{H_{n-1}^{(1-a)}}{n^a}-\frac1n
 =\int_0^1u^{n-1}\bigl(p_a(-\log u)-1\bigr)\,du
\]
is a strict positive Hausdorff moment sequence; all its decreasing
finite differences and its finite Hankel determinants are positive.
See Theorems 4.2--4.3 of the same article for the balanced positive
integral establishing the density inequality.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite-depth asymptotics]
\label{criticaleuler:drift}
Put $L=\tfrac12\log N$, $\ell=\log L$, and
$d=\gamma_1+(\gamma^2+\zeta(2))/2$. For every choice of maximizing
parameter $a_N$ on the continuously extended critical line,
\[
 1-a_N=\ell^{-1}-2d\ell^{-3}+O(\ell^{-4}),
\]
and
\[
 \max_{0\le a\le1}R_N(a)
 =\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt N}
 \left[1+\frac1{eL\ell}
 \left(1-\frac d{\ell^2}+O(\ell^{-3})\right)\right].
\]
These assertions hold as $N\to\infty$ and do not assume uniqueness
of the finite-depth maximizer.
\end{theorem}
The full article's Theorems 7.2, 7.4, and 7.5 supply endpoint-uniform
remainders and the optimization proof. The monotonicity of $-2g_a$
\emph{does not} extend to each $R_N$: exact rational intervals prove
$R_{128}(1/2)>R_{128}(1/4)$. The numeric optimizer proposals and the
conjecture of eventual unimodality must retain their separate status.
