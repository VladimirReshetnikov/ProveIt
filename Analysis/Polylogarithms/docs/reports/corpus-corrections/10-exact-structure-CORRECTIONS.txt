Scope
-----
This is a focused mathematical review of the eight articles in
Analysis/Polylogarithms/docs/articles and selected supporting reports. It is
not an exhaustive audit of all 31 reports. Source filenames below are relative
to Analysis/Polylogarithms/docs, except where the README is named. Line numbers
refer to the exact source files at the pinned commit, not to PDF pages.

The accompanying article contains the proofs of the principal corrections and
extensions; the code and data directories contain independent verification
artifacts. The provenance/source-manifest.json file records source URLs, Git
blob identifiers, and SHA-256 hashes. The original drafts are not reproduced
in this package, and no source draft was silently overwritten.

Status and severity
-------------------
"Established error" means an explicit contradiction, counterexample, or
corrected formula is supplied. "Unproved claim" means the supplied argument
does not establish the assertion; it does NOT mean the assertion is false.
"Existing warning" means the repository's intake README already identified
the issue. "Gap resolved here" means the accompanying article now supplies
the missing proof, for the stated formal relation system.

High severity affects a central theorem, identity, convergence claim, or
mathematical definition. Medium severity affects a condition, interpretation,
or complexity statement. Low severity is a localized wording or notation fix.


1. THE FOUR ALLEGED NEW MIXED CONSTANTS HAVE EXPLICIT REDUCTIONS
=============================================================

Status: Established error, newly resolved by explicit identities.
Severity: High.
Source: reports/eisenstein-gaussian-mixed-cuberoot-doubles__abd2e7116aa9.tex
Locators: abstract lines 51-54; table and interpretation lines 170-184;
Gaussian dimension claim lines 211-216; thm:parallel, lines 219-226.

The assertion that each mixed tower contributes exactly two new constants is
false relative to the base family specified in that report. The nearby
numerical-evidence caveat at lines 186-195 does not repair thm:parallel.

Use the source's descending-index convention

  Li_{a,b}(u,v) = sum_{n>m>=1} u^n v^m/(n^a m^b).

Put rho=exp(2*pi*i/3), G=beta(2), and C_{2j}=Im Li_{2j}(rho). The four
replacement evaluations are

  Re Li_{4,1}(i,-i)
   = -587 zeta(5)/1024 + 3 zeta(2)zeta(3)/32
     +135 zeta(4)log(2)/256;

  Im Li_{5,1}(i,-i)
   = 3 beta(6)-zeta(2)beta(4)-zeta(4)G
     -5*pi^5*log(2)/3072;

  Re Li_{4,1}(rho^2,rho)
   = -281 zeta(5)/162 + 4 zeta(2)zeta(3)/9
     +20 zeta(4)log(3)/27;

  Im Li_{5,1}(rho^2,rho)
   = -3 C_6+zeta(2)C_4+zeta(4)C_2+pi^5*log(3)/729.

All four belong to the report's single-polylogarithmic data. They do not
require extra mixed generators. They were independently checked by integral
quadrature at 100-digit working precision; their proofs are exact identities,
not integer-relation searches.

The correction holds at every positive integral weight, not merely for these
four examples. For M_{a,b}(z)=Li_{a,b}(z,z^{-1}), w=a+b, and |z|=1, z!=1,

 M_{a,b}(z)
  = (-1)^(b+1) sum_{j=1}^a binom(w-j-1,b-1) Li_{w-j,j}(z,1)
    +(-1)^(b+1) binom(w-1,b) Li_w(z)
    +sum_{j=2}^b (-1)^(b-j) binom(w-j-1,a-1) zeta(j)Li_{w-j}(z)
    +(-1)^b sum_{j=2}^a binom(w-j-1,b-1) zeta(j)Li_{w-j}(z).

Empty sums are zero. This follows by partial fractions after writing n=m+k.
The article also proves that the rearrangement agrees with the original
outer-index boundary sum, including a=1; it is not merely an Abel-value
identification. In particular,

 Li_{a,1}(z,z^{-1})
  = a Li_{a+1}(z)+sum_{j=1}^a Li_{a+1-j,j}(z,1)
    -sum_{j=2}^a zeta(j)Li_{a+1-j}(z).

Suggested replacement for thm:parallel:

 "For every positive integral weight, the inverse-color points (i,-i) and
 (rho^2,rho) contribute zero to the quotient by the corresponding (z,1)
 double family, single polylogarithms, and single-polylogarithm products.
 The four previously named candidate survivors have the explicit
 single-data evaluations displayed above. This statement does not classify
 all other colored double points or assert arithmetic independence of a
 proposed remaining basis."

The report's cited evaluator and value stores are not present in the supplied
source snapshot. The exact cause of its unsuccessful relation searches has
therefore not been identified; attributing the failure to one particular
implementation or precision bug would exceed the evidence.


2. ABSOLUTE CONVERGENCE IS CONFUSED WITH CONDITIONAL CONVERGENCE
=============================================================

Status: Established error.
Severity: High.
Source: articles/multiple-polylogarithms-introduction.tex
Locators: unlabelled theorem "Domain of absolute convergence", lines 231-235.

The claimed condition already fails in depth one: Li_1(i) satisfies the
printed exclusion (s_1,z_1)!=(1,1), but sum |i^n/n| diverges. A nontrivial
phase can provide conditional convergence; it cannot establish absolute
convergence by itself.

Suggested replacement:

 "For Re(s_i)>=1 and |z_i|<=1, a sufficient condition for absolute
 convergence is Re(s_1)>1, or |z_1|<1. On the unit-modulus boundary,
 nontrivial phases may instead give conditional convergence. Each boundary
 rearrangement must be justified for its particular summation convention.
 For positive integral indices with all arguments equal to 1, convergence
 is equivalent to s_1>=2."

The last sentence is restricted to positive integral indices, as in the
surrounding multiple-zeta discussion. It should not be read as a convergence
classification for arbitrary complex indices and arbitrary argument tuples.


3. THE GONCHAROV WORD ORIENTATION AND ARGUMENT DICTIONARY CONFLICT
================================================================

Status: Established definition/formula incompatibility.
Severity: High.
Sources and locators:
  articles/gaussian-eisenstein-double-polylogs.tex,
    eq:G-def and eq:goncharov-dbl, lines 141-146;
  articles/multiple-polylogarithms-introduction.tex,
    lines 278-282, and the differentiation statement at line 287.

The first source attaches a_1 to the smallest simplex variable but then uses
the outermost-first word dictionary. With its printed simplex,
G(0,z^{-1};1) contains an inner integral dt/t and diverges, whereas the
following statement identifies its negative with Li_2(z).

Use one convention consistently. An explicit replacement is

  G(a_1,...,a_w;z)
    = integral_0^z dt/(t-a_1) G(a_2,...,a_w;t),
  G(;z)=1,

with the usual separate convention for all-zero words. At endpoint 1, this
corresponds to the simplex 0<t_w<...<t_1<1 with a_j attached to t_j.
Under this convention,

 Li_{s_1,...,s_k}(z_1,...,z_k)
  = (-1)^k G(0^(s_1-1),z_1^(-1),
             0^(s_2-1),(z_1 z_2)^(-1),...,
             0^(s_k-1),(z_1...z_k)^(-1);1).

The singularities are inverse PREFIX products. The introduction instead
states inverse suffix products while retaining an outermost-first word.
Also replace "the differential strips the last letter" at line 287 by
"the differential strips the first, outermost letter". Paths, branches and
any endpoint regularization remain part of the definition when required.


4. LEADING ZEROES DO NOT GENERALLY MAKE HPL RECURSION DIVERGE
==========================================================

Status: Established error.
Severity: High for the definition; medium for the associated sign example.
Source: articles/multiple-polylogarithms-introduction.tex
Locators: eq:hpl-rec and eq:hpl-zeros, lines 491-499;
alternating-value example, lines 552-555.

The recursion is valid with leading letter 0 whenever the tail makes the
integral convergent. The source's own example H(0,1;z)=Li_2(z) disproves
the claim that any leading 0 forces divergence.

Suggested replacement:

 "For a nonempty word that is not all zero, use
 H(a_1,...,a_w;z)=integral_0^z f_{a_1}(t)H(a_2,...,a_w;t)dt,
 including a_1=0 when the integral converges. Define the all-zero words
 separately by H(0,...,0;z)=log^w(z)/w!."

At line 552, with the printed convention
zeta(bar s)=sum_{n>=1}(-1)^n/n^s, the correct value is

  H(0,-1;1)=-Li_2(-1)=-zeta(bar 2)=pi^2/12.

The positive pi^2/12 is correct; its identification with +zeta(bar 2) is not.


5. THE FULL HPL ALPHABET IS NOT CLOSED UNDER ALL FIVE SUBSTITUTIONS
=================================================================

Status: Established error, with weight-one counterexamples.
Severity: High for the claimed general transformation algorithm.
Source: articles/multiple-polylogarithms-introduction.tex
Locators: eq:hpl-transforms and accompanying assertions, lines 559-579.

The transformations z->1-z and z->z^2 do not preserve the full singular
alphabet {0,1,-1,infinity}. For example,

  H(-1;1-z)=log(2-z),
  H(-1;z^2)=log(1+z^2).

The first has a logarithmic singularity at z=2; the second has logarithmic
singularities at z=+i,-i. These are not the singularities of the original
alphabet. The dilogarithm examples in the source are valid special cases;
they do not imply closure for all HPL words.

Suggested replacement:

 "The substitutions -z, 1/z, and (1-z)/(1+z) permute the singular set
 {0,1,-1,infinity}, with branch constants tracked. Reflection 1-z preserves
 the {0,1} subalphabet, but introduces the letter 2 when applied to the
 full {0,1,-1} class. Squaring pulls back the full alphabet to
 {0,1,-1,i,-i}. Implement these operations using the enlarged alphabet
 whenever the input word requires it."

10. GAMMA-RELATION COMPLETENESS IS NOT A KOBLITZ-OGUS THEOREM
==========================================================

Status: Existing intake warning, confirmed; no new arithmetic theorem claimed.
Severity: High.
Source: articles/gamma-lattice-and-certificates.tex
Locators: abstract lines 45-49; epistemic status lines 91-98;
basis interpretation lines 129-130; law:ko, lines 133-137;
benchmark interpretation lines 158-160.

The Koblitz-Ogus criterion supplies a sufficient algebraicity condition.
The claim that all such arithmetic relations are generated by reflection
and multiplication requires an appropriate form of Rohrlich's conjecture.
It is not an available completeness theorem for Euler's Gamma function.

Suggested replacement for law:ko:

 "Exact row reduction decides membership in the rational span of the
 encoded reflection and multiplication rows and supplies a certificate
 for every accepted relation. The reducer is sound, terminating, and
 complete relative to this specified finite relation system. The free
 columns form a basis of that formal quotient. Completeness for all
 multiplicative relations among the actual Gamma values, and arithmetic
 independence of the retained values, are not asserted."

For N>=3 the formal anchored reflection/distribution quotient has dimension
phi(N)/2, also supplied by the character argument in the accompanying
article at weight zero. This gives a precise unconditional interpretation
of the reported 16 formal basis columns at N=120. Valid emitted identities
remain valid if their explicit law certificates check.

Useful primary context: Anderson, Brownawell and Papanikolas, Annals of
Mathematics 160 (2004), section 1.4.2. That paper's positive-characteristic
Gamma theorem must not be transferred to Euler's Gamma function.


11. FORMAL CORANK, MOTIVIC DEPTH, AND ARITHMETIC INDEPENDENCE ARE DIFFERENT
=======================================================================

Status: Unproved claims and incompatible filtration interpretations.
Severity: High.
Source: articles/gaussian-eisenstein-double-polylogs.tex
Locators: thm:gauss-5, lines 275-285; thm:eis-1, lines 425-429;
product interpretation lines 315-318; thm:deligne and eq:binomial-dim,
lines 345-352; cor:depth3, lines 361-367.

A matrix of valid relations gives an upper bound on the dimension of actual
special values. It gives an exact dimension only for the formal quotient by
those particular rows. It cannot establish arithmetic lower bounds simply
because no additional relations were found numerically.

Replace "exactly five/single new constants" in the unproved arithmetic
dimension interpretations by "the proposed generators span modulo the
displayed reductions", or by an explicitly stated formal matrix-corank
claim if that matrix and its exact certificate are supplied. Do not label
these numerical constants false; the issue is the unsupported lower bound.

At line 317, a product of depth-one and depth-two values is not automatically
in the usual linear depth filtration D^2. The general product law is

  D^a D^b is contained in D^(a+b).

One may separately define the algebra generated by depth<=2 values, but
that is a different object and cannot be substituted for the ordinary
depth filtration in a motivic graded-dimension argument.

The binomial formula in eq:binomial-dim does not follow from freeness of a
Lie algebra with one generator per weight. Word length in free generators
and MPL depth are different gradings. Further, a positive dimension of an
ambient motivic space would not identify the images of the three selected
periods as nonzero or independent. The missing motivic calculation must be
provided before even a conditional period-conjecture conclusion follows.

The intake README already records a period-conjecture caveat for the triple
claim. The additional clarification here is that merely appending that
conjecture does not supply the absent depth-grading and selected-period
arguments. Retain proven shuffle reductions; label the remaining depth
classification open.


12. NUMERICAL SEARCHES DO NOT PROVE NONMEMBERSHIP OR INDEPENDENCE
==============================================================

Status: Unproved assertions; some were already flagged at intake.
Severity: High for conclusions advertised as theorems.

Apply the following replacement principle only where the evidence is a
finite numerical search:

 "No relation was found in the stated basis, precision, and coefficient
 range. The constants are retained as formal generators in this reduction
 scheme. Their arithmetic independence is not established."

A successful high-precision relation is a candidate identity until proved
or certified by exact reasoning. A failed search is not automatically a
rigorous bounded-height exclusion either: that requires certified input
enclosures and a certified exhaustive exclusion argument. This correction
does not declare the underlying candidate identities false.

14. THE STATED ALL-DIVISOR GAMMA MATRIX DOES NOT HAVE O(N) RAW ROWS
================================================================

Status: Established complexity-count error.
Severity: Medium.
Source: articles/gamma-lattice-and-certificates.tex
Locators: row construction lines 105-119; O(N) assertion at line 121.

Before eliminating duplicates or dependent rows, the explicitly stated
construction emits exactly

  floor((N-1)/2)+sum_{d|N,d>=2}N/d
   =floor((N-1)/2)+sigma_1(N)-N

rows. Since sigma_1(N)/N is unbounded, this is not uniformly O(N).
Use the exact count, or the standard O(N log log N) general upper bound
for N>=3. This does not impair finite termination or the soundness of
an exact relation certificate.
