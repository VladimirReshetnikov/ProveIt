## C1. Distribution rank: supply the missing general proof

**Source:** `docs/articles/stieltjes-parameter-derivative-tower.tex`, `thm:rank` and the following paragraph; the companion report's rank discussion.

**Classification:** Previously logged theorem-level proof gap, repaired by the companion article. The source theorem already specifies the relations generated by distribution rows. Its formal interpretation is not itself an arithmetic-independence assertion.

The displayed formula is stated for every denominator and derivative order, while the text supports it by finite exact row reduction. Computations through a finite denominator/order range do not prove an all-parameter statement. Nor does the dimension of the relation presentation exclude additional numerical identities coming from other sources.

The companion article proves that, for `Re(s)>0`, the vector space with symbols `[a]`, `a mod q`, modulo

`sum_(j=0)^(p-1) [b+jq/p] - p^s [pb]`, for primes `p|q`,

has dimension `phi(q)`; making `[0]` background leaves `phi(q)-1`. Prime rows generate composite rows. The prime-step inverse and explicit character extensions prove both uniqueness and existence of the normal form. The same construction over `C[t]/(t^(N+1))` proves the all-jet statement. For the derivative tower, take `s=k+1`.

**Proposed wording:** “Modulo the specified distribution relations, and with the integral coordinate and lower-order jet terms treated as background, the formal residual dimension is `phi(q)-1` for every `q>=2` and `k>=1`. The all-parameter proof is the explicit character-normal-form proof in the companion article. This does not assert arithmetic independence of the residual constants or completeness of all numerical relations.”

The original exact-computation scripts were not imported into ProveIt. The bundled implementation independently verifies the mathematical presentation; it does not claim to reproduce an unavailable internal representation. The source's phrase about bitwise-identical residual vectors is not treated as a separate mathematical error here.

## C2. Negative PSLQ does not prove independence

**Source:** the same article, `sec:atoms`, headed “The new atoms are genuine”; the companion report's six-atom discussion.

**Classification:** Additional unsupported strength of wording in this article. The README already warns of analogous numerical-versus-proof issues elsewhere.

The source calls six second-order `s`-derivative constants “genuine, mutually independent” on the basis of a negative PSLQ sweep. This does not prove rational linear independence, algebraic independence, irrationality, or non-elementarity. A failed search depends on the chosen basis, precision, coefficient-height limit, and algorithmic budget. Planted positive controls are useful diagnostics, not a converse theorem.

**Proposed section title:** “Numerical evidence for unresolved second-derivative constants.”

**Proposed replacement:** “The reported 200-digit search found no relation in the tested basis and search range. These computations motivate treating the six constants as separate candidate generators for further work. No linear or algebraic independence, irrationality, or non-elementarity conclusion is proved. Reproducibility requires the exact basis, precision, coefficient-height limit, and iteration budget.”

The new bundle does not rerun that historical PSLQ sweep, whose scripts and detailed basis are absent. It supplies new algebraic and analytic tests instead.

## C3. Write the trivial-zero regularization explicitly

**Source:** `rem:parity`, following `thm:bridge` in the derivative-tower article.

**Classification:** Completion of an underspecified statement. The source does not explicitly print an incorrect factor.

For a primitive character `chi`, `k>=1`, and a trivial zero at `-k`, define

`V(t)=L(-k+t, conjugate(chi))/t`.

Then `V'(0)/V(0)=L''(-k,conjugate(chi))/(2L'(-k,conjugate(chi)))`. The factor `1/2` is imposed by Taylor normalization. Thus the actual first-order bridge is

`L'(k+1,chi)/L(k+1,chi) + conjugate(L''(-k,chi)/(2 L'(-k,chi)))`
`= EulerGamma + log(2*pi/q) - H_k`.

The article gives the complete normalized functional equation and all logarithmic derivatives. At second order the regularized negative expression is

`L'''(-k)/(3 L'(-k)) - (L''(-k)/(2 L'(-k)))^2`,

not a naively substituted unregularized logarithmic derivative. For an imprimitive character, use its conductor and include the missing Euler factors.

## C4. Correct “elementary” residual vocabulary

**Source:** the quarter-argument paragraph before `eq:k2duality`, and the subsequent odd-`k` discussion.

**Classification:** Additional unsupported wording; no change proposed to the displayed `k=2` identity itself.

The text promises a finite combination of a negative-order polygamma value and elementary constants, but its displayed formula still contains `zeta'(3)`, `zeta(3)`, and `log(A)`, among other named constants. Eliminating the nonprincipal `beta'(3)` coordinate does not eliminate the principal zeta-jet coordinates. There is no justification here for calling all those residual constants elementary.

**Proposed replacement:** “At `k=2`, the identity eliminates the nonprincipal beta-derivative coordinate in favor of the negative-order polygamma value. The remaining terms include principal zeta jets and the explicitly displayed named constants.”

Likewise, describe `beta'(-1)=2G/pi` as reduction to a lower value (Catalan) coordinate, rather than as an established elementary reduction. This review does not assert a theorem of non-elementarity for Catalan's constant.

## C5. Character-coordinate scope versus functional-equation scope

**Source:** `prop:char` in the derivative-tower article.

**Classification:** Generalization of a correct statement, not a counterexample to it.

For `q>=2`, the finite identity

`sum_(a=1)^q chi(a) zeta(s,a/q) = q^s L(s,chi)`

holds for every Dirichlet character modulo `q`, not only primitive characters. Differentiating therefore extends the character-coordinate formula to all characters at all jet orders. The term with `a=q` vanishes because `chi(q)=0`. Keep the primitive hypothesis when applying the primitive functional equation in its unmodified form, or explicitly remove Euler factors and use the conductor.

## C6. Clausen parity alternates with weight

**Source:** `docs/articles/polylog-polygamma-bridge.tex`, `sec:dft`, consequence 2, concerning the parity split.

**Classification:** Algebraic parity correction.

The source convention is `Cl_(2m)(theta)=Im Li_(2m)(exp(i theta))` and `Cl_(2m+1)(theta)=Re Li_(2m+1)(exp(i theta))`. Therefore

`Cl_w(-theta)=(-1)^(w+1) Cl_w(theta)`.

The Clausen component is odd at even weight and even at odd weight. It is not always the odd part of the polygamma grid. In particular, the cosine/weight-three component belongs to the symmetric tetragamma combination. The Catalan/weight-two example remains correct. The correction fragment states this without changing the displayed Fourier transform.
