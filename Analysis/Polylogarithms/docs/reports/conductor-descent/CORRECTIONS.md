## 2. State the weight-one endpoint separately

**Location:** `chapters/04-depth.tex`, Gaussian single-value prose following `double:eq:g-diag`, and the Eisenstein formula `double:eq:eis-single`.

The rational-multiple-of-zeta language is literal for integer n >= 2. At n = 1 it needs an explicit removable-limit convention. Suggested additional text:

> For n = 1 use Li_1(i) = -log(2)/2 + i*pi/4 and Li_1(exp(2*pi*i/3)) = -log(3)/2 + i*pi/6. Zeta-factor formulas at n = 1 are interpreted as limits in the order variable, not as literal products of zero with zeta(1).

For instance, `(3^(1-s)-1)*zeta(s)/2` tends to `-log(3)/2`. This is a domain clarification, not a counterexample to the higher-weight identities.

## 3. Keep the S4 candidate conjectural

**Location:** `chapters/04-depth.tex`, label `gauss:eq:S4-closed`.

No correction to its coefficients is proposed. Independent quadrature supports them. The new exact obstruction concerns only a specified presentation generated by products of two depth-one polylogarithms, after passing to imaginary conjugation coordinates and treating single-value products as known.

Add a methodological note: the 96-row, 24-column formal law matrix has rank 20; the S4 target raises it to 21. A supplied integer vector annihilates every row and has target pairing 24. Thus these rows alone do not derive the formula. Higher-depth laws or another analytic transformation may still prove it. Do not describe the obstruction as evidence that the identity is false.

Earlier weight-five sporadic and weight-six parity results are not re-proved or advertised as new in this package; reconcile their status with previously supplied research packages separately.

## 4. Add guardrails when extending formulas

The following are preventative qualifications, not assertions that the existing manuscript already makes the corresponding mistake.

**Conductor inversion:** avoid dividing by `A_(d,f,chi)(s)` or `B_(d,f,chi)(s)` unless it is nonzero. Minimal-conductor coordinates give a polynomial normal form valid even when a top-level trace vanishes. Such vanishing is not a rank jump of the complete distribution system.

**Undifferentiated Stieltjes layer:** an extension to parameter-derivative order k = 0 needs the principal Laurent-pole contribution. The correction is

`(-1)^n * A_d^(n+1)(1) / (n+1)`.

Omitting it already fails for n = 0. Article Proposition 5.2 supplies the full formula.

**Order derivatives:** use distinct notation for partial_s Li_s(z) at s = 1 and for differentiation with respect to z. The new identities concern the former.
