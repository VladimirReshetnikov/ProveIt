1. Fixed-index elimination at arbitrary endpoints.

The strict-gap rule uses B_1(0) = -1/2 and B_1(1) = +1/2 correctly.
At m = 0 it gives
  H(0,s) = (a-1) h(s) - h(s-1),
  H(s,0) = h(s-1) - b h(s),
  H(s,0,t) = H(s-1,t) - H(s,t-1) - H(s,t).
These agree with direct finite strict-gap counts.

The passage to arbitrary a,b is algebraic in the meromorphic ordered
Hurwitz functions, followed by the existing entire H continuation. It
does not rely on an invalid identity-theorem argument from a=b+N.

An equivalent independent proof is the following insertion lemma.
For P0(y)=B_q(y)/q and P1(y)=B_q(y+1)/q, a formal series with endpoints
(U,V) has insertion value equal to the P0-shift of the left neighbor
(or U times the suffix if the left word is empty), minus the P1-shift
of the right neighbor (or V times the prefix if the right word is
empty). Under concatenation of endpoint pairs (U,V) and (V,W), cuts
on the two sides of the distinguished inserted letter produce the
extra terms +V F(L)G(R) and -V F(L)G(R); these cancel. The remaining
cuts regroup into the same insertion property with endpoints (U,W).
Inversion swaps endpoints. Since Z_c has endpoints (0,P0(c)),
Z_a^{-1}Z_b has endpoints (P0(a),P0(b)), exactly as stated.

2. Finite normal form.

Deleting an index -m consumes budget m+1; a neighbor shift transfers
part of that budget, while an endpoint factor contributes polynomial
degree at most the consumed budget. This proves the stated total
degree bound.

The uniqueness proof first establishes the analytic reduction, and
only then uses finite exponential independence and polynomial
Zariski density to compare universal coefficient polynomials. This
is valid. The integer configurations are dense for polynomial
identities despite their strict inequalities.

The (-1,s,-1) example was checked by direct expansion:
  (A-y^2-y)(y^2-y-B)
   = -y^4 + (A+B+1)y^2 + (B-A)y - AB.
The missing y^3 coefficient and every sign in its derivative at s=2
are correct.

3. Lerch generator.

For Re a,Re b>0 and |t|<2 pi, K(x-t) has no nonremovable singularity
on x>=0. A nonzero lattice pole would force t=x-2 pi i k and hence
|t|>=2 pi; y=0 is removable. Exponential upper-endpoint decay and
the local Taylor subtractions are uniform on compact parameter
sets. Multiplication by 1/Gamma(s) cancels all resulting Mellin poles,
proving the claimed joint holomorphy and entire spectral dependence.

The identity d_t L(s;t)=L(s-1;t) can independently be proved by
integration by parts first in Re s>1; the boundary term vanishes,
and entire continuation finishes the proof. The Lerch series yields
the same derivative directly when Re t<0.

The zero-block prefactors and logarithmic signs are correct. At s=0,
their right side simplifies exactly to
  ((1+u)^(a-b) - (1+v)^(a-b))/(u-v),
whose coefficient is binom(a-b,p+q+1), as required for a word
consisting of p+q+1 zeros. The stated |u|,|v|<1/2 domain gives
|log(1+v)-log(1+u)|<2 log 2<2 pi.
