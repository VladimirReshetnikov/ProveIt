\section{Ordered harmonic jets: orientation normal forms and Gamma primitives}
\label{ojnf:sec:normal-forms}

For a word of letters $(k,m)$, $k\ge1$, $m\ge0$, let
$\mathcal R_a(w)$ denote the constant coefficient in the polynomial
asymptotic, in $\log(N+a)$, of its decreasing-index finite sum with
letter function $x^{-k}\log^m x$. Write $Z_m=(1,m)$ and $X=Z_0$.
The finite quasi-shuffle merge is $(k,m)\circ(\ell,n)=(k+\ell,m+n)$.
The regularization preserves this product, and its one-letter values are
\[
 \ell_{k,m}(a)=\mathcal R_a((k,m))=
 \begin{cases}\gamma_m(a),&k=1,\\(-1)^m\zeta^{(m)}(k,a),&k\ge2.\end{cases}
\]
Derivatives on $\zeta$ in this section are spectral derivatives.

\begin{theorem}[Formal orientation count]\label{ojnf:thm:rank}
Let $\mathcal S$ be the single-letter subalgebra of the universal rational
quasi-shuffle algebra. Let $V_{j,r}$ be spanned by words
$Z_{m_1}\cdots Z_{m_j}$ with $\sum m_i=r$.
Then $V_{j,r}\cap\mathcal S$ is exactly the span of permutation-orbit sums,
and
\[
 \dim_{\mathbb Q}\frac{V_{j,r}}{V_{j,r}\cap\mathcal S}
 =\binom{j+r-1}{r}-p_{\le j}(r).
\]
For all the regular coefficients entering a transverse depth-$d$ finite
part, the total quotient dimension is $2^{d-1}-p(d)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A single-letter product of first weight $j$ has length at most $j$;
its length-$j$ part is a shuffle orbit sum of first-exponent-one letters.
Conversely the partition-lattice symmetric-sum formula puts every orbit
sum in $\mathcal S$. Count weak compositions and their permutation
orbits. Across all $j+r=d$, the raw words are compositions of $d$ and
the orbit classes are partitions of $d$.
\end{proof}

This is a rational linear quotient statement, not arithmetic independence,
transcendence degree, or a module rank after scalar extension. At depth four,
the quotient is the direct sum of a standard $S_3$ component and a sign
$S_2$ component on their respective word layers. A basis is
\begin{align*}
 D&=\mathcal R_a(Z_1XX)-\mathcal R_a(XXZ_1),\\
 \Theta&=\mathcal R_a(Z_1XX)-2\mathcal R_a(XZ_1X)+\mathcal R_a(XXZ_1),\\
 \Omega_2&=\mathcal R_a(XZ_2)-\mathcal R_a(Z_2X).
\end{align*}
The full report gives the complete quartic finite part, and reconstructs
these three coordinates from the positive rays $(1,1,1,2)$, $(1,1,2,3)$,
$(1,2,3,4)$ using a rational matrix of determinant $-1/144$.
It also proves that an individual transverse ray is single-letter-closed
in the stipulated formal algebra exactly when its inner slopes are equal.

\begin{theorem}[All-index Stieltjes commutators]\label{ojnf:thm:commutators}
For $p,q\ge0$, set
$\mathcal C_{p,q}(a)=\mathcal R_a(Z_pZ_q)-\mathcal R_a(Z_qZ_p)$ and
$\kappa_{p,q}=1/(q+1)-1/(p+1)$. For $a>0$,
\[
 \mathcal C_{p,q}(a)=\kappa_{p,q}\gamma_{p+q+1}(a)+
 \sum_{\nu\ge0}\frac{\log^q x\,\gamma_p(x)-\log^p x\,\gamma_q(x)
                -\kappa_{p,q}\log^{p+q+1}x}{x},\quad x=\nu+a.
\]
The series is ordinarily absolutely convergent. It has an ordinary Mellin
representation in Lerch functions, or polylogarithm order derivatives at
$a=1$, with kernel $(e^t-1)^{-1}-t^{-1}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the depth-two outer-tail decomposition with
$B(u,x)=\zeta(1+u,x+1)-x^{-u}/u$. Its divided difference contributes
exactly $\kappa_{p,q}\gamma_{p+q+1}(a)$. Antisymmetric Taylor extraction
in the normally convergent tail gives the series. The leading nonsummable
terms and the terms of order $x^{-2}$ cancel; the summand begins with
$(q-p)\log^{p+q-1}x/(12x^3)$ for $p\ne q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gamma-weighted Stieltjes primitives]\label{ojnf:thm:Gamma}
For $q\ge1$, put
\[
 D_q(a)=\mathcal R_a(Z_qXX)-\mathcal R_a(XXZ_q),\qquad
 H_m(a)=\sum_{\nu\ge0}\frac{\log^m(\nu+a)}{(\nu+a)^2}
                       \sum_{\mu=0}^{\nu-1}\frac1{\mu+a}.
\]
Then
\[
 \Gamma(a)\frac{d}{da}\left(\frac{\mathcal C_{0,q}(a)}{\Gamma(a)}\right)
 =q\{\gamma_0(a)\ell_{2,q-1}(a)-\ell_{3,q-1}(a)-2H_{q-1}(a)\}-2D_q(a).
\]
Consequently the ordinary integral satisfies
\[
 \int_1^2\frac{q\{\gamma_0(a)\ell_{2,q-1}(a)-\ell_{3,q-1}(a)
                         -2H_{q-1}(a)\}-2D_q(a)}{\Gamma(a)}\,da
 =\gamma_q(1).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Parameter differentiation is the word derivation
$(k,m)\mapsto-k(k+1,m)+m(k+1,m-1)$. Apply it to the commutator and
use three finite stuffle rows to eliminate the merged-letter terms.
The integrating factor follows from $\gamma_0=-\psi$.
Finally $\Gamma(1)=\Gamma(2)=1$ and the exact word shift gives
$\mathcal C_{0,q}(2)-\mathcal C_{0,q}(1)=\gamma_q(1)$.
\end{proof}

The formal depth-four organization target is therefore resolved in the
specified law vocabulary. Reduction of the resulting ordered values to a
smaller arithmetic constant algebra remains a separate question. No
numerical-independence or proof-assistant claim is attached to these results.
