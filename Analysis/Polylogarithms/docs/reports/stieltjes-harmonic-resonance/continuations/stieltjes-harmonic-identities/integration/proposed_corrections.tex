Two periodic factors with positive integer affine frequencies retain a
one-dimensional frequency constraint.  If $p=dP$, $q=dQ$, and
$\gcd(P,Q)=1$, then $pn+qm=0$ has exactly the parametrization
$(n,m)=(Qr,-Pr)$.  For disjoint singular sets, the shifted Hurwitz
integral therefore reduces to the unit-frequency kernel with the
factor $Q^{s-1}P^{t-1}$ and shift $\{Pb-Qa\}$.  Its Stieltjes
coefficients require the explicit local-scale contact terms when
finite parts are taken in the fixed coordinate $x-x_0$.
Products of three or more independent factors generally leave a
higher-dimensional frequency lattice and lead to genuinely coupled
multiple-sum kernels.
