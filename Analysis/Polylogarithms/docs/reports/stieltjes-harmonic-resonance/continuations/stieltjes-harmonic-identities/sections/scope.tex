\section{Scope, results, and relation to the source material}
\label{sec:scope}

This article continues two explicitly identified sources: the ProveIt
polylogarithm manuscript and its incoming research directory
\cite{proveit-manuscript,proveit-incoming}.  The repository was examined at
commit \nolinkurl{fc4d3bf80534ad7c901d3b8c9e71baf2df0064ed}.  The five incoming
ZIP reports dated 10 October 2026 are distinguished individually in the
source manifest and in Section~\ref{sec:audit}.  Representative manuscript
chapters and the relevant incoming sections were read for definitions,
proof dependencies, and open questions.  This is a targeted mathematical
audit, not a certification of every statement in the 417-page source
manuscript.

The source material already contains extensive one-factor differentiation
and antiderivative formulas, unit-frequency bilinear Stieltjes closure,
and log-Gamma correlations.  Repeating those as new discoveries would
misstate the problem.  The most useful next steps are the exact behavior
under unequal integer dilations, the first genuinely double-index
correlation problem with three translated factors, and independent
coefficient constructions joining harmonic series to Stieltjes constants.

\subsection{Main outcomes}

The following list describes what is proved, with the hypotheses that
matter for integration into the repository.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two affine frequencies.}
For $p=dP$, $q=dQ$, $\gcd(P,Q)=1$, and
$c=\{Pb-Qa\}\ne0$, the entire ordinary two-factor Hurwitz family reduces
to $Q^{s-1}P^{t-1}C(s,t;c)$.  All Stieltjes coefficients then reduce
to finitely many $\gamma_j(c)$ and $\gamma_j(1-c)$.  The universal
polynomial $n![z^{n+1}]e^{Lz}\prod_{j=1}^k(1-z/j)$ supplies every local
scale and differentiation correction.  See Theorems~\ref{dil:master},
\ref{dil:contact}, and~\ref{dil:allindices}.
\item \textbf{Three translated singular factors.}
When the three shifts are pairwise distinct, the spectral integral
is the sum of six explicitly colored Tornheim kernels.  A holomorphic
coefficient formula gives every finite-part triple Stieltjes product,
and specialization gives three digammas and correlations of arbitrary
normalized Stieltjes primitives.  See Theorems~\ref{thm:sixcone}
and~\ref{thm:triple-jets}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:triple-primitives}.
The coordinate class is proved; a reduction of every Tornheim jet to
single-variable Stieltjes constants is not claimed.
\item \textbf{Harmonic sums and their endpoint constants.}
If $E_{n,r}$ is the elementary harmonic coefficient of depth $r$, then
every convergent sum $\sum_nE_{n,r}R(n)$ with rational $R$ and permitted
poles has an explicit finite Gamma-coefficient reduction.  A jointly
holomorphic remainder at the moving pole determines the principal part
and constant at every depth.  See Theorem~\ref{harm:thm:gamma-generating},
Corollary~\ref{harm:cor:rational}, and
Theorem~\ref{harm:thm:moving-pole}.
\item \textbf{Gauss summation and all Stieltjes orders.}
Subtracting the matching Hurwitz term from a gamma ratio gives a normally
convergent series through zero balance.  Its arbitrary mixed parameter
jets are finite Bell-polynomial expressions with the correct generalized
Stieltjes term.  Further asymptotic subtractions give exact continuation
and residue identities at each negative integer.  See
Theorems~\ref{gs:master}, \ref{gs:spectral-jets}, and~\ref{gs:mixed-jets}.
\item \textbf{Corrected harmonic conjectures.}
A single two-parameter Gamma quotient proves all orders of the harmonic
identities underlying Conjectures~4 and~6 in Choi's inspected 2014
paper \cite{gs:Choi}.  It also corrects Corollary~5: the displayed
right sides (41)--(46) have an extra factor two, and two harmonic-order
misprints require repair.  See Theorem~\ref{gs:choi-master}.
These are statements about the printed formulas, without any assertion
that the corrected identities remained open in 2026.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is classical, what is extended, and what remains open}

The organizing tools are classical: the Hurwitz Fourier formula,
Gamma reflection and Gauss summation, elementary harmonic generating
functions, and Mellin continuation by Taylor subtraction
\cite{dlmf-hurwitz,gs:DLMF}.  Mikol\'as's dilation formula, as recorded
by Espinosa and Moll, is an antecedent to the frequency calculation
\cite{espinosa-hurwitz}.  Their Tornheim work and the colored reductions
of Zhao and Zhao--Zhou are relevant antecedents to the triple-product
coordinate class \cite{espinosa-tornheim,zhao-colored,zhao-zhou}.
The unshifted height-one Gamma generating function and harmonic
Stieltjes constants likewise have a substantial existing literature
\cite{harm:KanekoSakata,harm:Young2024,harm:Kargin2023}.

The additions established here combine those tools with explicit shifts,
all parameter orders, and stated finite-part coordinates, and prove the
resulting formulas directly.  Such an exact extension can answer a
repository research question even when its underlying method is
classical.  No global novelty claim follows merely from not finding
an identical formula in the reviewed sources.

The surviving manuscript problems called S6 and S8 are not declared
proved by this article.  Their narrower coefficient-algebra and
function-class requirements are separate from an exact representation
in a larger special-function class.  The same distinction applies to
coincident singularities, unrestricted higher harmonic Stieltjes data,
and arithmetic independence.  Section~\ref{sec:further} records these
limits and concrete next questions.

\subsection{Reading and integration order}

Section~\ref{sec:conventions} fixes signs, Fourier phases, and the meaning
of a finite part.  Sections~\ref{dil:section} and~\ref{sec:triple} form
the periodic-correlation continuation.  The harmonic and Gauss sections
can be read independently after the Stieltjes convention is fixed.
The proofs use generating identities before coefficient extraction;
this makes arbitrary orders reproducible without listing hundreds of
special cases.  The accompanying Python programs implement exact
coefficient arithmetic and independent numerical comparisons.  None of
the analytic conclusions rests on floating-point agreement.
