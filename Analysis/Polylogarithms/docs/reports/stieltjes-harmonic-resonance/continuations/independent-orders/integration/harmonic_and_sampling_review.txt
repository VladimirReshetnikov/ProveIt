Harmonic identification and claim status
--------------------------------------
The one-endpoint function is identified exactly as
H_{a,1}(s_1,...,s_r) = Psi(s_1,...,s_r; a-1) of Ihara, Nakamura, and
Yamamoto, "Interpolant of truncated multiple zeta functions" (2025).
Their Proposition 2.1 gives the reversed-star formula, and their
Corollary 4.2 supplies the entire spectral continuation. The one-endpoint
interpolant and its harmonic product law therefore have established
attribution. The two-endpoint ordered quotient and the explicit residue,
endpoint, Gamma, and Stieltjes calculations are presented as consequences
and an organization of this foundation, without a literature-wide
priority claim.

Independent sampling review
---------------------------
The finite power-logarithm germ theorem is valid with arbitrary complex
exponents and finite logarithmic degrees. Combining integer translates
of exponents keeps the coefficient germs holomorphic. A surviving
leading term is a nonzero finite trigonometric polynomial in log(x).
Vanishing logarithmic mesh gaps, bounded derivative, and the positive
limiting mean square rule out its disappearance on all the samples.
The identity theorem then gives equality along coherent analytic
continuation of the resulting germs.

The multicentre necessity proof is valid for arbitrary complex orders
and finite logarithmic jets. The monodromy difference at a nonintegral
centre reduces the proposed expression to a finite Euler-operator
module. A nonzero polynomial P(E) implies P(alpha_i+m) h_m = 0; hence
the remaining local holomorphic factor is polynomial. Continuation to
each other distinct centre forces its exponent to be a nonnegative
integer. Rational partial fractions or polynomial expansion supplies
the converse and works on both Fourier tails with the stated branches.

The requested terminology fix has been applied in sections/05_sampling.tex:
the operator is now explicitly the monodromy difference M_i-I around a
small loop. The loop action itself would not annihilate holomorphic
terms. This was a wording correction; the subsequent factor
exp(2*pi*i*alpha_i)-1 already specified the intended operator.

The incoming sampled-identity problem is the seventh question under
"Further questions with concrete first targets" in the Mixed Spectral
report's sections/06_audit_research.tex. It is distinct from Exact
Identities Question 8. The package resolves it in the stated finite
power-logarithm class and makes no arithmetic-independence assertion.
