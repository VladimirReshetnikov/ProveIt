MATHEMATICAL REVIEW RECEIPT: CUBIC DIRECTIONS AND CONTINUATION
11 October 2026 (UTC)

This is an independent mathematical review summary, not a formal proof
certificate or a guarantee of literature-wide novelty.

Cubic Tornheim section
  The elementary polar remainder is holomorphic because
  Gamma(1-A)/Gamma(1+C)-1 vanishes identically on A+C=0, and likewise
  for B+C=0. The quotient by that linear factor is therefore holomorphic.
  The exact C axis and the Euler relation yield the displayed quadratic
  jet constraints. The cubic directional formula follows by coefficient
  extraction and the explicit definition of the diagonal coordinate.
  The Gamma-series boundary identity follows from the absolutely
  convergent differentiated boundary sum and its Stirling subtraction.
  Conic and cyclic cancellation polynomials were checked independently.

  The two residual coordinates are a spanning collection. Neither is
  proved arithmetically independent or reduced to a finite algebra of
  ordinary zeta/Gamma constants. Restriction to the ray precedes the
  derivative, and the polar directions a+c=0 or b+c=0 are excluded.

Mixed-order continuation review
  Root filtering, positive-index shuffle words, repeated rational poles,
  the prefactor coefficient transform, and negative-inner elimination
  preserve the cumulative alphabet and terminate by decreasing depth.
  Every resonant nested output term is continued separately: its positive
  first-index base word has a finite power–logarithm expansion at the
  endpoint, so Mellin subtraction supplies its meromorphic first-index
  continuation. Without a letter 1 the kernel is analytic, and reciprocal
  Gamma zeros cancel all local poles, yielding entire continuation.

  The local Laurent-constant theorem correctly uses the simple zero of
  1/Gamma(C) at each nonpositive integer. The global regular remainder
  contributes no constant there, but generally contributes to higher
  regular coefficients. The stated distinction is essential.

Independent specializations
  Both printed triple kernels, their nonpositive Laurent constants,
  the z=-1 derivative values, and the normalized Euler primitive were
  checked independently. The polygamma–Hurwitz formula explicitly
  requires derivative order r>=1. No false formula was identified.
