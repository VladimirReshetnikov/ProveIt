LITERATURE RECONCILIATION AND VERSION SCOPE
11 October 2026 (UTC)

This record identifies the primary literature used to check attribution
and conventions. The bibliography in the article gives full citations.
The search was targeted; it does not establish literature-wide priority
for every derived specialization.

Ihara, Nakamura, Yamamoto (2025)
  Interpolant of truncated multiple zeta functions,
  Ramanujan Journal 67, article 1.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-025-01045-2
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20509
  Primary publisher text inspected. Proposition 2.1 is exactly the
  reversed-star formula identifying H_(a,1)(s)=Psi(s;a-1). Corollary 4.2
  already supplies entire dependence on independent spectral indices;
  Section 5 supplies the harmonic product. Its discussion credits earlier
  overlapping interpolation work by Komori. These are established results,
  not a discovery claimed here. The article's relative endpoint organization
  and explicit consequences preserve this attribution.

Mehta and Viswanadham (2017)
  Analytic continuation of multiple Hurwitz zeta functions,
  Journal of the Mathematical Society of Japan 69, 1431–1442.
  https://doi.org/10.2969/jmsj/06941431
  Primary paper inspected for continuation and prefix-divisor structure.
  The article supplies an explicit common-shift Euler–Maclaurin residue
  derivation and proves cancellation at divisor intersections.

Borwein and Dilcher (2018)
  Derivatives and fast evaluation of the Tornheim zeta function,
  Ramanujan Journal 45, 413–432.
  Author version: https://carmamaths.org/resources/jon/omega3.pdf
  The author PDF has the title “Derivatives and fast evaluation of the
  Witten zeta function.” Its elementary local-expansion corrections are
  already recorded in the incoming directional-zeta package. The present
  article carries them forward with that attribution rather than claiming
  a second discovery.

Sathyanarayana and Sharan (2026)
  Mordell–Tornheim zeta function: Kronecker limit type formulas and
  special values, Advances in Applied Mathematics 177, 103075.
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2026.103075
  Correct inspected preprint: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10093v1
  This is the later preprint matching the published title and including
  diagonal special values. The earlier arXiv:2501.01380v1 is a different
  source version and must not be silently identified with the inspected
  later one. Section 5 proposes further simultaneous-order approaches
  at points of indeterminacy; no all-direction cubic-origin classification
  was located in the inspected text.
  The printed local estimates in (2.3), (2.7), and the exponent quantifier
  in (2.8) require the corrections proved in article Section 6. The two
  elementary counterexamples are Li_0(e^-y)~1/y and
  Li_1(e^-y)=-log(y)+O(y). No claim is made that an uninspected final
  journal version repeats the errors, or that those errors refute its
  continuation formulas.

Dobrowolski (2026)
  Evaluation of symmetrized Mordell–Tornheim zeta function,
  arXiv:2603.20550v1.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20550v1
  Inspected for overlap: positive-integer symmetrized weights are a
  different setting from the cubic local jet at the joint origin.

Panzer (2015)
  Algorithms for the symbolic integration of hyperlogarithms with
  applications to Feynman integrals, CPC 188, 148–166.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.3385
  Hyperlogarithmic shuffle and rational manipulation are classical tools.
  The present mixed-order theorem specifies its one-free-order output
  class, confluent implementation, cumulative alphabet, and depth bound.

Kaneko and Ohno (2010)
  On a kind of duality of multiple zeta-star values, IJNT 6, 1927–1932.
  https://www2.math.kyushu-u.ac.jp/~mkaneko/papers/45zeta-star.pdf
  Equation (5) explicitly attributes the height-one Gamma generating
  function to Aomoto and Drinfel'd. The article gives a self-contained
  coefficient-integral and beta-integral proof before using it in the
  convergent triple-sum specialization.

Additional standard references
  Hoffman, Quasi-shuffle products (2000), for the word-algebra structure.
  Pila, Note on Carlson's theorem (2005), for uniqueness context; the
  finite power–logarithm sampling lemma here is proved elementarily and
  is not advertised as a new general Carlson theorem.
  NIST DLMF Sections 5.9, 5.11, 25.11, and 25.12, for the Gamma/Binet,
  Stirling, Hurwitz, and Jonquière normalizations used throughout.
