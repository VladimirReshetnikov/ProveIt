# Primary literature and novelty boundary

The following primary sources were checked for the ingredients used in the
article. They establish the classical framework; the article identifies the
specific new endpoint and determinant applications separately.

1. Su Hu and Min-Soo Kim, *On the Stieltjes constants and gamma functions
   with respect to alternating Hurwitz zeta functions*, Journal of
   Mathematical Analysis and Applications 509 (2022), article 125930.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2106.14674v4

   This supplies the alternating-Hurwitz recurrence, order derivatives,
   modified Stieltjes constants, and alternating Gamma framework. Those
   ingredients are not claimed as new here.

2. Wanyi Wang, Su Hu, and Min-Soo Kim, *On gamma functions with respect to
   the alternating Hurwitz zeta functions*, arXiv:2405.02854v4 (2024).
   https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02854v4

   Their alternating Gamma quotient is the reciprocal of h up to sqrt(pi/2).
   The article uses this classical quotient and its recurrence in new
   bilinear endpoint applications. The cited version is v4, not an assertion
   that v4 is the latest version.

3. Su Hu and Min-Soo Kim, *Asymptotic expansions for the alternating
   Hurwitz zeta function and its derivatives*, arXiv:2103.15528v6 (2023).
   https://arxiv.org/html/2103.15528v6

   The alternating-Hurwitz expansions, Euler specializations, and spectral
   derivative machinery are prior work. The present contribution is the
   explicit quadratic endpoint contact calculus, including cutoff translation.

4. Michael E. Hoffman, *Sums of products of Riemann zeta tails*,
   Mediterranean Journal of Mathematics 13 (2016), 2771–2781.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03399
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s00009-015-0653-9

   Products of tails and their multiple-zeta expressions are established
   prior art. The article does not claim that general theme as new.

5. S. A. Abramov, *The rational component of the solution of a first-order
   linear recurrence relation with rational right-hand side*, USSR
   Computational Mathematics and Mathematical Physics 15(4) (1975),216–221.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/0041-5553(75)90181-0

   The divisor-orbit criterion in the article is a specialized elementary
   rational difference-equation argument, not a new general summation algorithm.

6. Jonathan M. Borwein and David M. Bradley, *On Two Fundamental
   Identities for Euler Sums*, preprint, January 2005, especially Eq.(1.3).
   https://carmamaths.org/resources/jon/Preprints/Papers/ArXiv/Zeta21/z21.pdf

   This source documents Euler’s height-one multiple-zeta formula. The
   article credits it in reducing the new determinant endpoint and writes
   out the weight-six shuffle/stuffle arithmetic.

7. NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, Gamma functional
   relations/asymptotics and Hurwitz zeta.
   https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.5
   https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11
   https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.11

## Search scope

Targeted searches included alternating Hurwitz tails, Nielsen beta bilinear
products, Gamma-ratio tails, and rational adjacent-tail certificates, using
both available search engines and checking primary sources. No exact match
for the displayed bilinear or Gamma-increment laws was located. This is a
limited prior-art search, not a proof that every specialization is previously
unpublished. The defensible novelty claim is a proved continuation of the
inspected ProveIt corpus, with the classical inputs explicitly credited.

## Important exclusions

The Gamma weights depend on the two tail orders. An arbitrary independent
outer power and the ordinary-Hurwitz moving-pole problem are not solved.
The first off-diagonal contact does not determine all further hyperplanes.
The rational classification concerns its stated adjacent-product ansatz,
not every possible finite special-function reduction.

The project source map distinguishes the new critical rank-two polylogarithm
generator from the predecessor’s second-difference repeated-pole generator.
The cyclotomic coefficient is explicitly an already implied manuscript
consequence. No numerical or functional independence claim is made.
