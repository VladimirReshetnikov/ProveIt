# Proof and priority distinctions

## Classical inputs

Gauss summation, gamma-ratio Stirling expansions, Hurwitz-zeta parameter differentiation, the Laurent definition of generalized Stieltjes constants, and hypergeometric parameter differentiation are classical. Bühring's partial-sum work treats zero and negative integer excess. The article explicitly avoids presenting the zeroth-order constant-term phenomenon as historically new.

## What is supplied in this continuation

The article provides a complete fixed-shift subtraction construction; a finite Bernoulli coefficient differential law; a short proof of the all-resonance digamma evaluation; an all-order coefficient formula valid across vanishing residues; an exact account of revived reciprocal-Gamma-zero terms; an all-weight balanced harmonic-tail identity; and exact primitive and polylogarithmic endpoint calculi.

Each statement is proved in ordinary mathematics. The reusable exact coefficient engine, independently constructed numerical diagnostics, theorem-status map, and integration notes make this a reproducible repository contribution. These features are not assertions of global mathematical priority.

## Specializations

- The N=0 binomial-square constant is classical zero-balanced hypergeometric territory.
- The x=1 harmonic-tail theorem recovers `zeta-star({2}^k,1)=2*zeta(2k+1)`, a known multiple-zeta-star specialization.
- The half-parameter harmonic-tail and higher Stieltjes identities are derived uniformly here, but individual special cases may already occur in harmonic-sum literature.
- An exhaustive comparison with all incoming repository archives and all published hypergeometric harmonic identities was not completed.

## Research questions

Section 9 contains eight concrete proposed directions. They are research targets, not claims that every target is a previously recognized open problem. In particular, reductions into a specified smaller algebra must not be confused with proofs that the remaining constants are independent.
