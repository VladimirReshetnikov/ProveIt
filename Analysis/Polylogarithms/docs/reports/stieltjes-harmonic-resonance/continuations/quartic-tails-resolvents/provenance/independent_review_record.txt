1. Complex-power completion and its noninteger corollary
  Reviewed the source sections/complex_powers.tex, including the new
  power:noninteger formula. No mandatory mathematical correction found.

  The local families at zero are
    A_p(u) v_j(lambda) x^(u+lambda-p-1+j),
    b_j(u,lambda) x^(lambda+j).
  The second endpoint contributes
    exp(epsilon*pi*i*lambda)*(-1)^j*b_j(u,lambda)*y^(lambda+j).
  At u=0 a resonant integrated exponent can therefore occur only at
  lambda=p-j or lambda=-1-j, both integers. For any fixed noninteger
  lambda a sufficiently small parameter neighborhood misses all these
  hyperplanes. Finite Taylor subtraction leaves a uniform integrable
  remainder, and each subtracted denominator stays away from zero.
  Hence generalized power-log constant extraction commutes locally with
  lambda derivatives, exactly as power:noninteger states.

  The integer-power completion must retain the complete moving numerator
  A_p(u)*v_(p-L)(L+v)/(u+v). At negative L, the full two-endpoint term
    [1+(-1)^(-L-1)*exp(epsilon*pi*i*(L+v))]*b_(-L-1)(u,L+v)/v
  is removable but has value -epsilon*pi*i*b_(-L-1)(u,L), not zero.
  Subtracting only residues would discard this finite contact.

  contact_verification.tex gives an independent all-M residue proof of
  the negative-power mean correction. check_contacts.py verifies it from
  the formal cotangent Taylor series through M=10, both half-plane signs,
  and rejects all twenty deliberately phase-omitted variants. The script
  also verifies the z-primitive telescoping with symbolic L != 1 through
  logarithmic degree 5, the L=1 branch, and the boundary resonance canary.
  The machine-readable record is contact_checks.json.

2. Quartic bridge independent proof audit
  Reviewed quartic_bridge/quartic_bridge.tex and the critical constants
  and arithmetic in quartic_bridge/verify_quartic_bridge.py.
  No mandatory mathematical correction or unclosed proof gap found.

  Global Mittag-Leffler expansion:
    The local principal parts of psi(z)^4 and its regular coefficient
    kappa_4 agree with the fourth power of the digamma Laurent series.
    For f=psi^4-P_0, f is regular at zero. Integrating
    z*f(w)/(w*(w-z)) on |w|=N+1/2 is legitimate: reflection and the
    asymptotic digamma formula give a uniform O(log^4 N) bound while
    cot(pi*w) remains bounded on these circles. The integral is
    O(log^4 N/N), hence tends to zero. The residue at each -n is
    -(P_n(z)-P_n(0)). The normal convergence estimate
    O(log^3(n)/n^2) closes the argument and excludes an unspecified
    entire remainder.

  Integration and arithmetic reduction:
    Finite-part integration of P_0 gives the displayed endpoint constants.
    The finite-difference identity for a_n, Abel summation of its logarithm
    terms, and the symmetric-sum reductions all check. In particular the
    +4*zeta'(3) sign in A_4 is correct. The remaining infinite series uses
    the kernel (psi(n)^2+psi'(n)-log(n)^2)*log(n)/n, which is absolutely
    summable. The printed C_4 constant and the reindexing to this kernel
    are consistent.

  Theta identification:
    D_4(s)=2*Z_3(s,1,1)-2*gamma*Z_2(s,1)
           +(gamma^2+zeta(2))*zeta(s)-zeta''(s)
    agrees with its Dirichlet series on Re(s)>1 and continues regularly
    to Re(s)>0. Its derivative at 1 is
    2*theta-2*gamma*eta-(gamma^2+zeta(2))*gamma_1+gamma_3.
    Substitution gives the displayed quartic bridge with all signs.
    In the independent Mellin argument for theta, the regularized cubic
    kernel has R_3(0)=0 because it is one sixth of the derivative of
    log^3(1-exp(-t)). The subtraction at zero has vanishing difference
    in its cubic logarithmic primitive. Therefore the inverse-Gamma
    coefficient of degree four and R_3'(0) yield theta as claimed.

  Numerical remainder treatment:
    The Binet remainder bounds for psi and psi' have the correct powers
    and constants. The exact zeta derivative signs in the logarithmic
    tail compiler are consistent. The verifier's temporary use of
    gamma^2-zeta(2), followed by a separately added 2*zeta(2) term,
    is algebraically equivalent to the gamma^2+zeta(2) coefficient in
    the mathematical formula. Its reported agreement is a high-precision
    independent diagnostic, not an interval certificate, as documented.

3. Ordinary moments and log-resolvent checks
  moments.tex supplies a regularized Gauss exponential generating
  function for all nonreal z and Re(lambda)>-1, its Bernoulli moments,
  a general-q digamma-Lerch first moment, and the Gamma/harmonic formula
  at q=0. The logarithmic Gamma generating series has its smaller
  radius min(1,abs(1+lambda)) explicitly distinguished from the quotient's
  Taylor radius. Inverse-square-root endpoint examples are ordinary
  absolutely convergent integrals.

  check_moments.py completed nine independent exact coefficient checks,
  a deliberate prefactor corruption control, and nineteen numerical
  comparisons at 55-digit working precision. The largest absolute
  discrepancy is 2.824e-31, in an inverse-square-root endpoint quadrature.
  The two nonzero complex-q finite-part log-resolvent checks agree within
  4.3e-56; their stable integrands use exact endpoint subtraction.
  Numerical results are diagnostics, not rigorous interval enclosures.
