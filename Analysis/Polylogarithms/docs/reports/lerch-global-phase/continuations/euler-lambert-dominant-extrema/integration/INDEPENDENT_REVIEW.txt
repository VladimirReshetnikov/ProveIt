# Independent mathematical review and attribution notes

This review concerns the proposed research article and its supporting scripts,
against ProveIt commit `4c173c06cc32c9cea554b39be1837ad2ae897fc1`.
It records checks of the proofs and the limits of the claims. Numerical plots
and high precision computations were not used as substitutes for proof.

## Selected inverse sheet and harmonic velocities

The rectangle argument for the germ
`exp(U_A(t)) = A + t U_A(t)`, `U_A(0) = log A`, was checked directly for
every real `A > 1`. The horizontal factorization, strict vertical barrier,
right-edge estimate, root count, and choice of the accessible square-root
branch are correct. The proof identifies the singularities of the selected
germ, rather than merely listing critical values of the entire inverse map.
The compact boundary cover and the two local slit neighborhoods suffice for
coefficient transfer; no unproved exclusion of other inverse sheets or of
global asymptotic values is needed. In the cover argument, compactness applies
to the underlying uncut neighborhoods; the two outward rays are removed
afterward.

The first two coefficient terms, the all-order Puiseux formula, the finite-part
identity at a dominant critical parameter, bounded gaps between both signs,
and the rational/irrational sign-density distinction were independently
checked. The coefficient error must be stated additively: a relative error
would not be valid near cancellation of the two dominant contributions.
Compact-parameter uniformity is enough for the limiting zero distribution;
the proof does not require a uniform estimate at either endpoint of the
parameter range. The CDF proof uses a lower zero count on compact angle
intervals and the exact total number of positive roots, which avoids an
unjustified global bounded-discrepancy claim.

The exact convergence radius is already present, after a change of variables,
in G. A. Kalugin and D. J. Jeffrey, *Convergence in C of series for the Lambert
W function*, arXiv:1208.0754, Theorem 1 and equation (2.9). The correspondence
is `lambda = log A`, `sigma = -t/A`, and the series variable called `u` in that
paper is the present `V_A = log A - U_A`. Thus the radius should be attributed
as known. The self-contained selected-sheet proof and the stated subsequent
consequences should be distinguished from a priority claim for the radius.

The coefficient polynomials are also known: in Henri Cohen, *Lambert
W-Function Branch Identities*, arXiv:2012.11698, Proposition 4.8, the Lambert
polynomials satisfy `L_n(X) = (-1)^n p_n(X)`. Cohen's Conjecture 7.1 should be
discussed with its individual parts separated. In particular, real-rootedness,
interlacing, extreme-root expansions, and their generalized-parameter
analogues are different assertions. The elementary differential-operator
proof of real-rootedness already appears in the incoming harmonic baseline;
the bulk zero law should not be presented as the discovery of the underlying
polynomials. The literature comparisons are not an exhaustive priority search.

The all-orders fixed-index extreme-root theorem was independently checked
as well. The exact exponential extraction changes the reciprocal coordinate
to `v = N/(X-log n)` and leaves the bounded amplitude
`h_n(v)=n^v f_n(v)`. Its fixed derivatives are uniformly bounded on compact
sets. Taylor expansion through degree `2M+1` on the central circle arc has
an absolute Gaussian-integral remainder `O(N^(-M-1))`; the complementary arc
is exponentially negligible. The exact coefficient-moment generating
function gives all displayed saddle operators and their signs.

The Bernoulli-polynomial gamma expansion and saddle expansion give a full
two-parameter asymptotic series in `epsilon=1/n` and `eta=1/N`. The formal
simple root equals exactly `j` when `eta=0`, so its displacement is divisible
by `eta`. This divisibility makes the formal height series regular. The
finite-order residual argument, with `N/n` in a fixed compact positive
interval, and the limiting nonzero derivative justify the formal-to-actual
passage. The proof correctly accounts for the factor `N` in recovering `X`
and does not claim convergence of these formal asymptotic series.

Substitution `eta=epsilon/(1+kappa epsilon)` makes the coefficient of
`n^(-m)` a polynomial in `kappa=k-1` of exact degree `m-1`. For `m>=2`, the
highest-degree coefficient comes solely from the term linear in `eta` and
equals `(-1)^(m-1)(j^2 s3-j s2)`, where
`s2=H_(j-1)^(2)+zeta(2)>0` and `s3=H_(j-1)^(3)-zeta(3)<0`.
The first correction is `C_j=j^2 s3-j s2-j-1/2`. This proves the specified
fixed-parameter, all-orders assertion in Cohen's Conjecture 7.1(4).
The real-rooted pencil argument and the separate common-root contradiction
also correctly prove strict interlacing of the nonzero roots in part (3).
The theorem remains a fixed-index, fixed-parameter result, with no
uniformity claimed when either index grows with the degree.

Two explicit largest-root coefficients were checked independently:

    C0(k) = -3/2-zeta(2)-zeta(3),
    C1(k) = (k-1)(zeta(2)+zeta(3))+zeta(2)^2-zeta(2)zeta(3)
               +2zeta(3)+3zeta(5)-1/12.

The standard-library exact certificate `verify_cohen_c1.py` checks the
coefficient moments, finite perturbation algebra, and cancellation of
Euler's constant; its output is `verify_cohen_c1.json`. The local exponential
extraction with `c=H_(j-1)-gamma` also verifies the arithmetic corollary:
the coefficient of `n^(-m)` belongs to
`Q[zeta(2),...,zeta(2m+1)][kappa]`. Euler's constant appears only in the
leading additive shift. These formulas are derived symbolically, not by
integer-relation fitting.

The complementary smallest-root Bessel limit was checked directly from its
Stirling coefficients. The repeated-index bound proves the fixed-codimension
limit, and the positive elementary-symmetric bound dominates the entire
series on compact sets. The normalization yields precisely
`-(2x)^(k/2) J_k(sqrt(2x))`. Its fixed zero multiplicity at zero, local
uniform convergence, and real-rootedness justify the ordered smallest-root
limits, including the corresponding small lattice spacings.

## Euler kernel optimization

The exact pure-power envelope, its monotone decrease to a continuous limiting
profile, and the application of Dini's theorem are correct, including both
endpoints. All-maximizer localization, negative definiteness of the limiting
Hessian, and the analytic implicit-function argument justify eventual global
uniqueness and a convergent expansion in `1/N`. Eventual strict decrease follows
from analyticity and a positive first derivative at `1/N = 0`; it should not be
deduced solely from a two-term expansion with an uncontrolled remainder.
These are eventual statements, and do not prove uniqueness or monotonicity
for every finite `N`.

The exact `N = 2` elimination and interval calculations were independently
verified. The script `verify_kernel_algebra.py` uses only Python's standard
library and exact integers and rational numbers. It checks the bivariate
derivative numerators, the Sylvester resultant, the stationary-point
substitution, a multiplication-matrix norm, irreducibility modulo 5, the
unique root of the value polynomial in `(1,2)`, and rational enclosures for
the maximizing coordinates and value. In particular, the algebraic degree
seven is certified, rather than inferred merely from a degree-seven
annihilating polynomial. Its machine-readable output is
`verify_kernel_algebra.json`.

## Global Euler constant

The four-range localization argument was checked without relying on numerical
inequalities. The middle-range large-deviation exponent is uniformly positive;
the Taylor-remainder bounds and the monotonicity of the remainder in the first
gamma shape parameter have the required direction. They imply the strict
comparison with the boundary throughout the only competitive range. The
uniform twice-differentiated expansion gives the unique boundary optimizer and
the stated leading asymptotic behavior. The positive-quadrant result concerns
a supremum approached at the boundary; it does not assert an interior
maximizer.

The exclusion of `a >= 1` also has a short self-contained proof. Write
`T_a = T_1 + T_{a-1}` and `exp(-T_a) = Z U`, where `U` is uniform on `(0,1)`.
For fixed positive `Z,v <= 1`, the conditional kernel average is

    [v^(-1) integral_0^(Zv) W_N(t^2) dt
       - integral_0^Z W_N(t^2) dt] / [Z(1-v)]

and is at most the average of `W_N(t^2)` on `(0,Zv)`, strictly smaller than
one. This removes the need to treat the corresponding canonical theorem as
an external hypothesis.

The sharper gamma-transition calculation was also checked. Its Mellin
integral is `Gamma(1/2-lambda)/2`, with the positive sign required for the
second contribution. The density rescaling, uniform domination of the first
two shape derivatives, Stirling amplitude, strict ordering of the exponents,
negative optimizing shift, and the displayed inverse-logarithmic coefficient
are correct. For the inverse-logarithmic error estimate, a central interval
of size `M log log N` and the two uniform exponential tails give the asserted
remainder: `M` is fixed after selecting the desired logarithmic error power,
and powers of `N` dominate all resulting fixed powers of `log N`. The
strict log-convexity assertion for the optimized axis envelope uses an
attained finite maximizer at the intermediate argument; this attainment is
essential to the proof as written. Pointwise strict decrease and attainment
also show that the axis envelope decreases strictly for every real index at
least one.

The sharp joint boundary profile was independently checked. Uniformly for
outer order at most a fixed multiple of
`N^(-(p-1/2)) log N`, the exact loss is
`J_N(a) ~ sqrt(pi) a N^(-1/2)/log N`, including uniform control as `a`
decreases to zero. The integral on the transition region gives
`Gamma(1/2)/2`; conversion from `L=(log N)/2` supplies the factor two.
The separate small-`t` bounds are negligible even before division by
`Gamma(a)`. The exact error decomposition then gives the uniform joint
profile and, together with the prior necessity theorem, the complete
if-and-only-if criterion for near-optimality.

## Corrections to the existing material

No substantive mathematical error was established in the canonical
real-order chapters audited here. Two notation corrections are proposed in
`05-real-positive-kernel.tex`, in the proof of `rw:thm:transport`:

* The instruction to use the Hurwitz formula “with `q=n`” should read
  “with `x=n`”.
* The reference to “boundedness of `h`” should read “boundedness of `q`”.

The simultaneous radial degeneracy, its sextic behavior, and examples with
two radial extrema were already treated in later incoming research. They
should not be counted again as new results of this article. Claims that are
retained as questions or conjectures should remain visibly distinct from the
theorems proved in the article.

## Reproducibility and limitations

The exact certificate supplies a reproducible check of the finite algebra;
the analytic theorems rest on their written proofs. Research diagnostics and
plots are illustrative. In particular, the compact-parameter bulk law and the
fixed-index extreme-root expansion use separate uniformity arguments; neither
alone supplies an estimate for their intermediate transition regime.
