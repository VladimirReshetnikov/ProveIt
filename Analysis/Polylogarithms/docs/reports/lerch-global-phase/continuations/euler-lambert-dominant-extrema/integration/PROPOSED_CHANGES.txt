5. TWO REQUIRED LITERATURE IDENTIFICATIONS

5.1 Kalugin--Jeffrey antecedent

G. A. Kalugin and D. J. Jeffrey, "Convergence in C of series for the
Lambert W Function", arXiv:1208.0754v1 (2012).
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0754
Relevant locations: Theorem 1 and equations (1.3), (1.4), (2.2), (2.9).

Use the explicit change of variables
  lambda=log A, sigma=-t/A, v=V_A(t)=log A-U_A(t).
Their equation becomes
  1-exp(-v)+sigma*v-sigma*lambda=0,
equivalent to exp(U_A)=A+t*U_A. The fixed-lambda inverse series and
radius criterion are antecedents of the corresponding formulas here.
Credit them beside the generating function and radius discussion. The
new article gives a self-contained selected-sheet barrier proof and
additional consequences; it does not claim the radius formula first
appears here.

5.2 Cohen's Lambert polynomials and precise conjecture scope

Henri Cohen, "Lambert W-Function Branch Identities",
arXiv:2012.11698v2, version submitted 2021-01-18.
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11698
Relevant locations: Proposition 4.8 and Conjecture 7.1.

Make the identification L_n(X)=(-1)^n*p_n(X) explicit. The generalized
operator normalization is given in eq:cohen-operator of the current
article. The polynomial family and coefficient array are not new.

Current package support:
  sections/07_cohen_extremes.tex
    prop:cohen-interlacing
    thm:cohen-extreme-roots
    cor:cohen-two-constants
    cor:cohen-coefficient-algebra
  sections/06_zero_distribution.tex
    thm:velocity-zero-law
  sections/08_bessel_edge.tex
    thm:bessel-edge

Comparison with the cited version of Conjecture 7.1:
  (1) Real-rootedness and the stated asymptotic largest-root bound follow.
      The elementary real-rootedness method already occurs in the
      pinned harmonic continuation and should receive that credit.
  (2) The extreme-root statement is proved for each FIXED positive
      root index j, with a full expansion. Do not imply uniformity
      for j growing with n.
  (3) Successive L_n interlace; their common zero is only 0, and
      the interlacing inequalities for nonzero roots are strict.
  (4) For each FIXED POSITIVE INTEGER k, the all-orders largest-root
      expansion and the exact polynomial degree of each coefficient
      in k are proved. The current article does not claim the separate
      derivative interpretation for k<=0. Do not describe the scope
      as all real k, growing k, or an unrestricted joint n,j,k limit.

The all-orders root expansions are ASYMPTOTIC to every finite order;
their formal series are not asserted convergent. By contrast, the
kernel-maximizer expansion in 1/N is genuinely convergent near 0.

For the largest root in Cohen's indexing:
  X_(n,k,1)=n+k-1+log n+gamma+C0/n+C1(k)/n^2+O_k(n^(-3)),
  C0=-3/2-zeta(2)-zeta(3),
  C1(k)=(k-1)*(zeta(2)+zeta(3))+zeta(2)^2-zeta(2)*zeta(3)
          +2*zeta(3)+3*zeta(5)-1/12.
For the article's fixed-j coefficient at order n^(-m), the coefficient
ring is Q[zeta(2),...,zeta(2m+1)][k-1].

Cohen's earlier primary question used a different leading normalization:
  https://mathoverflow.net/questions/378900/stirling-number-bounds-and-polynomials-and-the-lambert-w-function
It asks for a in n+H_n+a/n+... . That constant is
  a=-2-zeta(2)-zeta(3)=C0-1/2.
The discrepancy of 1/2 is exactly the 1/(2n) term in H_n; keep the
normalizations separate. These are comparisons with the cited sources,
not an assertion of an exhaustive bibliographic priority search.
