Conclusion: no mathematical correction is required by this review.
The following points were checked directly, rather than accepted from
the numerical diagnostics or the previous source status.

1. Local model and derivatives
The Stieltjes shift relation is gamma_m(x)=log(x)^m/x+gamma_m(1+x).
Its r-th ordinary derivative has polynomial prod_{j=1}^r(D_X-j)X^m.
This verifies the one-sided local decomposition, the harmonic-polynomial
formula, and the sign (-1)^(m+k) in the analytic-tail coefficient.
No distributional differentiation is silently used.

2. The product's support and why there is one lower endpoint per subset
For a selected subset S, all its singular indicators multiply to
1_{x > -min_{i in S}s_i}. The other nodes lie to the left of this lower
endpoint, not in the integration interval. The expansion into subsets
therefore accounts for every original singular point with the stated
independent right-hand cutoffs.

3. Arbitrary hierarchical gaps
The contour formulas for P_S and A_S are correct Cauchy/Hermite formulas.
On their fixed contour, every |z+s_i| is bounded below uniformly, even
when some real gaps are vastly smaller than all the others. Thus A_S
is uniformly bounded without estimating individual Lagrange terms.
After y=x+b_S, every a_i=s_i-b_S is nonnegative. In a sufficiently
small fixed neighborhood, 0<y+a_i<1 and
|log(y+a_i)| <= |log y|. The residual product is dominated by the
integrable function C(1+|log y|)^M, independently of all gap ratios.
The changing upper endpoint tends to delta and causes no difficulty.
This proves the stated limit along paths such as gaps exp(-1/epsilon^2)
as well as along fixed-ratio paths. Continuity is proved; analyticity
for nonzero logarithmic indices is appropriately not asserted.

4. Full infinite interval and endpoint normalization
P_S/D_S=p_S/x+O(x^-2) at infinity. Multiplying logarithmic polynomials
leaves exactly p_S lambda_S(log x)/x as the divergent derivative; the
remainder is O(x^-2(1+log x)^M), locally uniformly with shift derivatives.
The sign in B_S=-p_S Psi_S(log delta)+integral(remainder) is correct.
Translation x=y-b_S leaves the lower ambient cutoff equal to eta and
changes log(R+b_S)^j to log(R)^j+o(1), so no constant is lost at infinity.
Pure monomials y^-k(log y)^j, k>=1, have total two-endpoint finite part
zero. This checks both the singleton convention and -B_S(0) as the
merged local principal contribution. Subtracting only a short local
integral would miss these constants; the actual definition avoids that.

5. The GPL reduction
The partial-fraction coefficients use q_S rather than P_S legitimately:
their difference is divisible by D_S. Simple poles prepend one letter
to a log product of weight at most M. Integration by parts for repeated
poles lowers total logarithmic degree; subsequent partial fractions can
move pole multiplicity but cannot reverse that decrease. The asserted
weight bound M+1 and termination are correct. All letters are on the
nonpositive real axis after translation, so there is no interior branch
crossing. Tangential regularization at zero is explicitly stated.

6. Direct check of the binary logarithmic kernels
For a>b, differentiation verifies
log(a-b) log(y+b) - Li_2(-(y+b)/(a-b)).
For b>a, differentiation verifies
log(y+a) log(1+(y+a)/(b-a)) + Li_2(-(y+a)/(b-a)).
Using Li_2(-t)+Li_2(-1/t)=-zeta(2)-log(t)^2/2 reproduces all six rows
of the printed U table, including the nonzero upper constants.
In particular U(0,a)=zeta(2)+log(a)^2/2 and
U(a,0)=-zeta(2)-log(a)^2/2; their signs are not interchangeable.

7. Direct check of the cubic pair contributions
For a pair of shifts a<b and its omitted analytic tail q:
 - logarithm at the first node: q(-b) B(log(b-a))/(b-a);
 - logarithm at the second node:
   [q(-a)B(log(b-a))+q(-b)log(b-a)^2/2]/(b-a);
 - no logarithm in the pair: q(-b)log(b-a)/(b-a).
Applying these rules to all three subsets independently reproduces
every term of T0, T1, and T2.
The triple partial fractions reproduce
A0=L^2/4-(log2)L/2+zeta(2)/2-log(2)^2/4,
A1=L^2/2-(log2)L/2+zeta(2)/4, and A2=L^2/2.

8. Finite anomalies and the merged ordinary integral
With h'(0)=-zeta(2) and k'(0)=beta=zeta(2)+zeta'(2), the three constant
terms in T_j are respectively
 -zeta(2)^2/2+zeta(2)log(2)^2/4,
 -beta log(2)/2,
 +zeta(2)^2/2.
The printed C0 power-log expansion has the same coefficients.
For Q, expanding (log x/x+k(x))(1/x+h(x))^2 gives pure singular terms
log x/x^3, 2gamma log x/x^2, and gamma_1/x^2 with finite integrals
-1/4, -2gamma, and -gamma_1. All other removed terms are proportional
to 1/x or log x/x and have zero finite constant. The stated remaining
integrand is exactly the difference and is absolutely integrable.
