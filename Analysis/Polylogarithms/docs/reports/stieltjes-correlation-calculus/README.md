# Stieltjes convolution and correlation calculus

This thematic report reconciles four independently delivered continuations
of the single-function Hurwitz-jet, Stieltjes and log-Gamma calculus. Their
complete original articles, verification programs, receipts and scope notes
are preserved under `continuations/`; the collective manuscript develops
accepted results through shared definitions rather than duplicating them.

The common dependency order is:

1. Fourier conventions and the normalized periodic Hurwitz family.
2. Scale-one finite parts at separated endpoints and their distributional residue.
3. Gamma-quotient coefficients, Bell normal forms and linear Stieltjes closure.
4. Contact terms for pointwise versus distributional differentiation.
5. Zero-mean primitive lifts, circular convolution powers and ordinary shifted log-Gamma correlations.
6. Coincident-point collision conversion and rational spectral specializations.

| Delivered continuation | Distinct audit contribution |
|---|---|
| convolution-algebra | Free one-generator mean-zero convolution algebra, explicit subtractions, differentiated rules and all log-Gamma convolution powers. |
| shifted-hurwitz-jets | Translated correlations, every-index primitive lifts and contact terms, with a complete exact coefficient engine. |
| bilinear-correlation-closure | Canonical coincident-point conversion, collision polynomials, rational traces and a numerical derivative canary. |
| convolution-calculus | Both convolution orientations, negative-integer jet collisions, Gamma cusp identities and scoped source corrections. |

These are preserved research contributions under active analytic review.
Successful finite replay is recorded separately from canonical acceptance.
Convolution powers are not ordinary pointwise powers; singular finite parts
are not bare digamma integrals. The mean-zero convolution unit is delta_0
minus the constant function one. Contact terms and cutoff scales are part
of the identities. The remaining S6/S8 period identities retain their status.
The resonance/nonseparable-jet continuation on the uniform-transition spine
provides a further Gamma-correlation comparison without being substituted
for these finite-part conventions.
