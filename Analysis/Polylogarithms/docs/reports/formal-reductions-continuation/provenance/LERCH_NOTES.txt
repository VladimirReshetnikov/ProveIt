LERCH DEFORMATION: ZERO GEOMETRY AT BOTH ENDPOINTS

Analytic contributions
----------------------
article/sections/lerch_background.tex gives a compact standalone account of the Stieltjes
and Lerch definitions, Appell--Laplace representation, strict real roots
of the reciprocal-gamma polynomials, global upper bound with multiplicity,
and persistence/rightward interlacing. These are attributed existing inputs.

article/sections/lerch_zeros.tex contains the complete proofs and integration notes:

1. Uniform compact containment of all positive zeros for rho in [0,1].
2. Complete small-positive-rho zero count for every fixed n > k >= 1,
   and convergent Puiseux expansions from the multiple zero at a=1.
3. For k=1, exactly one positive zero near rho=0 if n is odd, and
   exactly two if n is even; all of these positive zeros are simple.
4. Interior multiple-zero events forced for n=3,4 by the source endpoint
   certificates. No unproved saddle-node nondegeneracy is asserted.
5. Exact counterexample to decreasing-rho monotonicity: the smallest
   n=4,k=3 zero has a positive derivative at rho=0.
6. At n=2, exactly two simple positive zeros for every k>=1 and every
   rho in [0,1], so the optimal uniform saturation threshold is K_2=1.
7. At n=2,k=1, the first zero branch is not monotone on [0,1].

article/sections/lerch_abel.tex is an optional, independently usable continuation:

8. The critical Abel coefficient at rho=1 is
   (-1)^(n+k) tau^k log(1/tau)^(n+1)/(k! (n+1)), tau=-log(rho),
   after subtracting the Taylor polynomial through degree k-1.
9. A simple endpoint zero is C^(k-1), but not C^k, in the deformation
   parameter. The complete leading displacement and kth-derivative
   coefficients are proved directly.
10. The two n=2,k=1 branches have slopes tending to +infinity and
    -infinity, respectively, as rho increases to 1.

Reproduction
------------
From the archive root, run Python 3.10 or later; only the standard library is required:

    python3 code/verify_lerch_boundary.py --output data/lerch_boundary_certificates.json

The program regenerates data/lerch_boundary_certificates.json and checks:

- 152 polynomial identities by independent coefficient constructions;
- 120 exact rational sign intervals for Delta_(n,k), through n=16;
- the counterexample derivative interval
  (0.0122109650329620, 0.0122109650329621);
- 11 outward-integer Euler--Maclaurin endpoint certificates: precisely
  the two n=2 signs and the nine n=3,4 signs used in the analytic results.

No floating-point operation enters these certificates. The proof, not a
numerical root census, establishes the complete zero counts. The package
does not supply an explicit numerical small-rho threshold, an interior
multiple-zero location, or a nondegeneracy certificate for such an event.

Numerical figure
----------------
Run with mpmath and matplotlib installed:

    python3 code/plot_lerch_n2_branches.py

This generates the PDF and PNG in article/figures/, and
lerch_n2_branches.csv and lerch_n2_branches.json in data/. The two-panel figure
uses separate vertical scales to display both n=2,k=1 positive zero
branches at 51 parameter values, with dense samples near rho=1.
Safeguarded Newton uses the Appell--Laplace integral after u=log(x).
Fourteen selected root residuals are checked by an independent
Lerch/Hurwitz spectral representation at higher precision. The plot and
these residuals are numerical diagnostics, not interval certificates.
