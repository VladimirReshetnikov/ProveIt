Preasymptotic behavior is material:
  The theorem is uniform for each fixed L once N is sufficiently large
  depending on L. It does NOT promise a practical universal starting N.
  The fixtures deliberately include m about 3*sqrt(N) at N=24,48, where
  normalized half-term ratios 2.172646325... and 0.678747154... show that
  the asymptotic regime has not yet settled. At N=96,m=29 it has approached
  0.5 closely. Likewise m about 6*sqrt(k) can show large negative normalized
  coefficients at k=60,120 before approaching the stated positive regime.
  The proof explains this through outer-arc bounds q^k H^m: sufficiently
  large k depends on L. No finite-index global alternation is claimed.
