The result below extends the *specified formal weighted-distribution theory*.
It does not assert independence of evaluated periods, and it is not a claim that
these arbitrary binary deformation weights arise from complex spectral jets.
No global priority claim should be made: the elementary commutative-algebra
argument is supplied in full, and novelty is limited to its application and
explicit boundary within the inspected manuscript.

## Setup

Let `S` be a finite-dimensional commutative local Frobenius algebra over a field
`k`. Thus there is a linear functional `tau:S -> k` for which `(a,b) -> tau(ab)`
is a nondegenerate pairing. Write `T=dim_k S`. The rectangular jet algebras

    S = k[t_1,...,t_d]/(t_1^{L_1},...,t_d^{L_d})

have this property: take the coefficient of the socle monomial
`t_1^{L_1-1}...t_d^{L_d-1}`. Complementary monomials pair to one.

Let `f=(f_1,...,f_r)`, `r>=1`, and let `I=(f_1,...,f_r)`. Put
`ell=dim_k(S/I)`. In the distribution application `char k=2`, `r=r(q)` is the
active-prime count, and the entries are

    a_p-1 for odd p | q, and a_2(a_2-1) if 4 | q.

There is no active entry at 2 if `v_2(q)=1`.

## Theorem: the two-generator colength law

If the ideal `I` can be generated by at most two elements, then

    dim_k H_j(K_S(f)) = binom(r,j) ell,       0<=j<=r.

In particular the total homology length is `2^r ell`. No separability,
coordinate-power, or one-variable hypothesis is needed.

### Proof

If `I=S`, one entry of the list is a unit because `S` is local. The Koszul
complex is contractible, while `ell=0`. If `I=0`, all differentials vanish and
the assertion is immediate. Suppose henceforth `0<I<S`.

Choose a minimal subset of the given entries that generates `I`. Such a subset
exists: choose a basis of `I/mI` among their residue classes and apply Nakayama's
lemma. Its cardinality is at most two. Permute it to the beginning. Every
remaining entry is a linear combination of those first entries, and elementary
invertible row operations turn the sequence into `(f,g,0,...,0)`, allowing
`g=0` if there is just one generator. An invertible change of the free module
underlying the Koszul complex induces an isomorphism on its exterior algebra
and hence on the Koszul complex. If `r=1`, the kernel and cokernel of
multiplication by `f` have the same dimension `ell`, proving the claim directly.

For `r>=2`, the two-entry complex is

    0 -> S -> S^2 -> S -> 0.

Its zeroth homology is `S/I`. Its second homology is `Ann_S(I)`.
The Frobenius pairing identifies `Ann_S(I)` with `Hom_k(S/I,k)`.
Indeed the orthogonal complement of `I` is exactly `Ann_S(I)`: if
`tau(zi)=0` for every `i in I`, then for every `i in I` and `a in S`,
`tau((zi)a)=0` because `ia in I`; nondegeneracy implies `zi=0`.
Thus both end homology groups have dimension `ell`. The Euler characteristic
is `T-2T+T=0`, so `dim H_1=2ell`. Tensoring with the zero-differential exterior
algebra on `r-2` generators gives

    dim H_j = ell [binom(r-2,j)+2binom(r-2,j-1)+binom(r-2,j-2)]
            = ell binom(r,j).

This proves the theorem. The argument holds in arbitrary characteristic;
characteristic two enters only through the reflection application.

## Corollary: nonseparable reflection dimensions and integral Smith factors

Assume `q>2`, `n=phi(q)/2`, and `S` is as above of characteristic two. If the
active ideal needs at most two generators, then

    dim_k D_q(S,a)/(c-1)D_q(S,a) = n T + 2^{r(q)-1} ell.

Proof: use the existing characteristic-two Koszul model and
`dim coker(c-1) = [phi(q)T + dim ker(c-1)/im(c-1)]/2`.

For `B=Z[t_1,...,t_d]/(t_1^{L_1},...,t_d^{L_d})`, take `S=B/2B`.
If its reduced active ideal needs at most two generators, both signs have
the *abelian group* Smith decomposition

    C_+(D_B) ~= C_-(D_B) ~= Z^{n T} direct_sum (Z/2Z)^{2^{r(q)-1} ell}.

This uses the existing finite-free Tate theorem and the even and odd binomial
sums. It does not assert isomorphism of the two sign quotients as `B`-modules.

## Closed mixed-monomial family

Take `S=k[x,y]/(x^L,y^M)` and `I=(x^a,x^b y^c)`, where
`1<=b<a<=L` and `1<=c<=M`. The quotient basis consists of monomials with
`i<a` and either `i<b` or `j<c`. Therefore

    ell = b M + (a-b)c.

If these are the two active parameters, the reflected dimension is

    n L M + 2 [b M + (a-b)c].

For example, at `q=15`, take integral weights `a_3=1+x^2`, `a_5=1+xy` over
`B=Z[x,y]/(x^3,y^3)`. Then `T=9`, `ell=4`, `r=2`, `n=4`, so

    C_+ ~= C_- ~= Z^36 direct_sum (Z/2Z)^8

as abelian groups, and the binary reflected dimension is `44`.

The same ideal can occur with three active parameters: at `q=105`, add
`a_7=1+x^2 y+xy^2`. The third parameter is redundant, the Koszul dimensions
are `(4,12,12,4)`, the free integral rank is `216`, torsion multiplicity is
`16`, and binary reflected dimension is `232`.

## A module-level obstruction, even for two active parameters

For the level-15 mixed example let `Q=S/(x^2,xy)`. Then

    Q has basis {1,x,y,y^2};
    Ann_S(I) = (x^2,xy^2) has basis {x^2,x^2y,x^2y^2,xy^2}.

`Q` is cyclic. The annihilator needs two generators: modulo `m Ann(I)`
the classes `x^2` and `xy^2` are linearly independent. Therefore
`Ann(I)` is not isomorphic to `Q` as an `S`-module, although both have length
four. The negative-sign integer torsion is exactly

    Tor_Z C_- ~= H_0 direct_sum H_2 ~= Q direct_sum Ann(I).

It needs three `S`-generators, whereas `Q^2` needs two. Thus extending the
separable theorem's *module* formula by simply replacing its complete
intersection quotient with `S/I` would be false already for two active
parameters. The dimension theorem survives because Frobenius duality preserves
length, not because it identifies every module with its dual.

## Sharp failure of the colength formula for a three-generated active ideal

Let

    S=F_2[x,y,z]/(x^2,y^2,z^2),
    (f_1,f_2,f_3)=(xy,xz,yz).

Then `T=8`, `I=(xy,xz,yz)` has quotient basis `{1,x,y,z}`, so `ell=4`.
The Koszul homology dimensions are

    (h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3) = (4,14,14,4).

Here is a full pencil-and-paper verification, independent of software.
The first differential has image spanned by `xy,xz,yz,xyz`, and hence rank
four. The third differential has image spanned by

    yz e_12 + xz e_13 + xy e_23,
    xyz e_12, xyz e_13, xyz e_23,

and hence rank four. The image of the middle differential is spanned by

    xz e_1 + xy e_2,
    yz e_1 + xy e_3,
    yz e_2 + xz e_3,
    xyz e_1, xyz e_2, xyz e_3.

The first three vectors are independent homogeneous degree-two vectors;
the final three are independent degree-three vectors. All products of the
first three by ring monomials lie in their six-dimensional span. Thus the
middle differential has rank six. Taking kernel/image dimensions in the
complex of vector-space dimensions `8,24,24,8` proves the stated homology.

At `q=105`, over the integral algebra
`B=Z[x,y,z]/(x^2,y^2,z^2)`, use weights

    a_3=1+xy, a_5=1+xz, a_7=1+yz.

Here `n=24`, so the *correct* binary reflected dimension is

    24*8 + (4+14+14+4)/2 = 210.

The unsupported extrapolation `nT+2^{r-1}ell` would give `208`.
The integral Tate parity sums both equal `18`, proving

    C_+ ~= C_- ~= Z^192 direct_sum (Z/2Z)^18

as abelian groups. The failed colength extrapolation would predict only
sixteen torsion factors. This is a counterexample to a possible extension,
not a correction to the current manuscript, which carefully restricts its
separable product law.

## Independent verification artifacts

`verify_nonseparable_jets.py` is a standard-library Python verifier. It has
two independent routes:

1. Build the Koszul matrices by wedge bases and monomial multiplication.
2. Build the *original* raw prime-distribution rows and reflection rows in
   point/monomial coordinates, then compute their binary ranks. This routine
   imports no normal form, Frobenius calculation, or Koszul routine.

Exact outputs are in `nonseparable_jets_checks.json`:

| Example | Raw generators | Raw reflected relation rank | Reflected dimension |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Two mixed parameters, level 15 | 135 | 91 | 44 |
| Three-generator obstruction, level 105 | 840 | 630 | 210 |
| Redundant third parameter, level 105 | 945 | 713 | 232 |

The unreflected dimensions are respectively `72,384,432`, agreeing with the
all-ring basis theorem. Omitting reflection rows is an explicit corruption
control and produces different dimensions in each case. These computations
check the stated examples; the proofs establish the theorem's full scope.

## Further research questions

* Find a useful classification of active ideals for which every Koszul
  homology length is `binom(r,j) length(S/I)`; the theorem proves the
  two-generated case and separability is a distinct sufficient condition.
* For three active generators, Frobenius duality gives
  `(h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3)=(ell,h_1,h_1,ell)`. Determine `h_1` effectively in terms
  of the syzygies of `I`; the reflection defect is `ell+h_1`.
* Determine when `H_1(f,g;S)` splits as `S/I direct_sum Hom_k(S/I,k)` and
  when the two integral sign torsion modules are isomorphic. Equal lengths
  alone do not settle these module questions.
* Develop a Hilbert-series version of the nonseparable theorem for weighted
  homogeneous active parameters, retaining degree shifts rather than only
  total dimensions.
* For arithmetic deformations that genuinely come from spectral or parameter
  derivatives, determine which of these formal active ideals are realizable
  after choosing an integral coefficient lattice. Keep this separate from
  analytic period independence.
