\section*{Scope}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Scope}

This document catalogues the thirty-six research packages stored under
\dir{docs}.  Each entry names the problem the report attacks, the result it
claims, and the delivery it arrived in.

The collection divides in five, and so does the directory tree.  Eleven
reports, under \dir{docs/surreal}, are about the surreal field $\No$ itself and
its real Hahn subfields: birthdays and their behaviour under multiplication,
the option graphs of surreal forms, genetic functions and gaps, two questions
of Lipparini about evaluating formal sums, the rigidity of exponential
automorphisms, Gamma functions, differential transcendence of explicit
surreals, Hahn-valued measures and probability, and Markov generators.
Twenty, under \dir{docs/surcomplex}, are about the algebraic closure $\SC$:
the theory of holomorphic functions over it, its differential algebra, its
rank-one analytic geometry, its finite and infinite-dimensional spectral
theory, its local, quasi-periodic and expanding dynamics, its nonabelian
support obstructions, its entire functions at arbitrary valuation rank and
their holonomic rigidity, Tate curves and multiscale theta series, Herglotz
positivity, moment reconstruction, and the summability of Wick expansions.  One, under \dir{docs/surquaternions}, leaves
commutativity behind for Hamilton's division algebra $\mathbb{H}_{\No}$ over
$\No$.  One, under \dir{docs/physics}, is the only report here whose subject is
not mathematics: it asks what surreal scalars do and do not buy in general
relativity, and answers negatively.  The last three, under
\dir{docs/foundations-and-computation}, stand outside all of these: they are
about the subject rather than inside it, asking in what foundation this
mathematics legally lives, how one computes with it exactly, and which surreal
numbers are computable at all.

These reports were previously part of a larger collection, in a repository
whose subject was elsewhere; they were moved here with their git history.
That is why an entry may refer to a merge, a duplicate sweep or a delivery
number: those describe how the report reached its present form, and the
record is kept rather than rewritten.

Each directory holds a typeset article with its \LaTeX{} source, a
\texttt{README.md}, and in most cases verification code together with the
recorded output of running it.  The source manuscripts a merged report was
assembled from are not distributed here.  What is kept is the provenance
record: each merged report states how many manuscripts it was merged from,
what each contributed, and where the merge had to choose between them, and the
disposition of every source result is recorded in that material.  These are
AI-assisted research drafts. Some claims and prerequisites have checked Lean
proofs, mapped precisely in \texttt{FORMALIZATION.md}; no complete verification
of the collection is claimed. This catalogue describes the reports rather than
certifying them. The cross-report \texttt{NOTATION.md} explains shared names
and distinctions between local conventions.

\section{The surreal field}

Eleven reports about $\No$ and its real Hahn subfields.  Two questions of
Lipparini on evaluating formal sums; two independent halves of Gonshor's
product-birthday conjecture, on domains neither of which contains the other;
the subgraph question from Roughan's paper on birthday arithmetic; a genetic
counterexample separating pointwise derivatives from differences of
constants; a rigidity theorem for exponential automorphisms; a sharp
convexity classification of surreal Gamma functions; differential
transcendence of explicit surreals; Hahn-valued measures and probability;
and Markov generators at every valuation scale.  The first and the fourth
each arrived as a pair of archives and were merged; the exponential-automorphism
report later absorbed two independent re-derivations of its own theorem, and
the measures report merges three manuscripts; the rest are single
manuscripts.

\entry{A square-root obstruction to evaluating increasing Hahn series at $\omega$}
{surreal/hahn-evaluation-at-omega}
{article.pdf}{hahn\_evaluation\_research.zip + reversed\_hahn\_series.zip}
{Answers Problem~7.7 (p.~38) of Lipparini (arXiv:2505.00424v2) negatively: there
is no ring homomorphism from the field of set-supported real-coefficient Hahn
series with strictly increasing surreal exponents into the surreals sending
$x^a$ to $\omega^a$.  The certificate is $B\in\Q[[x]]$ with $B^2=1-x$, built by
the recursion $b_n=-\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}b_kb_{n-k}$: any such map would force
a square to equal $1-\omega<0$.  The homomorphism is applied only to the finite
identity, so no continuity, valuation, order preservation or preservation of
infinite sums is assumed.  Adds the exact classification of admissible images of
the variable as the infinitesimals, the monomial classification over an
arbitrary ordered abelian group, and order-uniqueness on the source field.
\emph{Merged; the core certificate was identical in both originals and is proved
once, but the two toolkits are kept --- in particular a direct
nonnegative-coefficient semiring certificate. Restriction and extension by
differences relate the ring and semiring maps; the two witnesses give distinct
elementary proofs.}}

\entry{Gonshor's Product-Birthday Conjecture for ordinal-support series}
{surreal/gonshor-product-birthdays}
{surreal\_product\_birthdays.pdf}{gonshor\_product\_birthdays\_research.zip}
{Proves Gonshor's bound $b(xy)\le b(x)\otimes b(y)$ on the ring of Conway normal
forms $\sum_{\alpha\in S}c_\alpha\omega^{-\alpha}$ with $S$ an arbitrary set of
ordinals, so transfinite and uncountable supports are allowed.  Supplies an
exact birthday formula by transfinite induction over the support with maintained
prefix bounds and an explicit limit stage, and a complete classification of the
equality cases.  The route is a leading-Cantor-degree gap, which also gives that
the degree of the birthday never rises under multiplication.  The conjecture for
arbitrary surreal numbers is not solved.  The domain is incomparable with that of the
Laurent-series report \nolinkurl{surreal/gonshor-laurent-birthdays}: it
excludes $\omega$ and every positive power.}

\entry{A Laurent-series case of Gonshor's birthday conjecture}
{surreal/gonshor-laurent-birthdays}
{article.pdf}{Gonshor\_Laurent\_Birthday\_Research.zip}
{Proves the same bound $b(xy)\le b(x)\otimes b(y)$ on the canonically embedded
field $\R((\omega^{-1}))$, which unlike the ring of
\nolinkurl{surreal/gonshor-product-birthdays} contains $\omega$
and its positive powers but restricts supports to order type at most $\omega$ on
the integer lattice.  The route is a bilinear budget: an exact splitting
$b(x)=b(P)\oplus b(u)$ into polynomial and infinitesimal parts, three separate
product estimates for the resulting cross terms, and a finite-jet stabilisation
carrying the finite bound to infinite series without any continuity claim.  Also
treats finite normal forms with arbitrary ordinal exponents, derives exact
birthdays for finite and infinite Laurent series, and proves a reciprocal
corollary.  Neither this theorem nor that one contains the other; on
their common corner $\R[[\omega^{-1}]]$ the two agree and are proved twice.}

\entry{Canonical forms need not be subgraphs}
{surreal/canonical-forms-need-not-be-subgraphs}
{surreal\_graphs.pdf}{surreal\_graphs\_research.zip + surreal\_sparse\_graphs.zip}
{Answers negatively the question in the Figure~3 caption of Roughan,
\emph{Surreal Birthdays and Their Arithmetic} (arXiv:1810.10373v2, p.~7):
the canonical form of a surreal value need not be a subgraph of every other form
of that value.  Five vertices and rank three are both least possible, and there
are exactly sixteen five-vertex counterexamples, eight triangle-free, four of
value $1/2$.  Adds cycle representations of $1/2$ at every admissible length,
the exact extremal function, a planar construction of maximum degree three and
arbitrarily large girth, classifications of which canonical graphs are
unavoidable, and transfinite witnesses.  \emph{Merged; the two originals
exhibited genuinely different five-vertex witnesses --- one passing through a
vertex of value $-1$, one with no negative value at all --- and both are printed
in full, as are two odd-cycle families, two high-girth constructions and two
proofs of the Fibonacci bound.}}

\entry{A countable genetic cut across the finite--infinite gap}
{surreal/genetic-gaps-and-primitives}
{article.pdf}{genetic\_gap\_counterexamples.zip}
{With $q(t)=t/(1+t^2)$ and $B(x)=1-\{q(n-x):n\in\N\mid\;\}$ as a Conway cut,
proves that $B$ is the indicator of the positive infinite surreals, is genetic
under the set-sized option constructors of the cited sources, and has pointwise
derivative zero everywhere.  Consequently $x$ and $x-B(x)$ are genetic
primitives of $1$ agreeing at every real input that do not differ by a constant,
the latter also an increasing bijection.  The sublevel class
$\{x:0<x<\omega,\;B(x)\le\tfrac12\}$ is nonempty and bounded yet has no surreal
supremum, so neither end gap supplies one.}

\entry{A Number-Valued Broadcast Sum of Surreal Sequences}
{surreal/broadcast-sum-of-surreal-sequences}
{article.pdf}{surreal\_broadcast\_sum.zip}
{Analyses the sign-truncation game of Lipparini (arXiv:2505.00424v2,
Remark~7.6(3), in connection with Problem~7.5).  Proves the game always has a
surreal-number value, that it agrees with finite surreal sums and with
Lipparini's ordinal operation on their respective domains, and that it fails
weak coordinatewise monotonicity, with an explicit family
$s[n]=3\cdot2^{-n-d-1}$ on a subset $E$ and $2^{-n-d}$ off it realising the
failure.  The underlying rule is Lipparini's; the name and the notation are
local to the report.}

\entry{Cofinal displacement and the rigidity of exponential automorphisms}
{surreal/exponential-automorphism-rigidity}
{article.pdf}{three manuscripts, delivered 21 and 22~September 2026}
{Let $F$ be an ordered field with a total increasing exponential
$E:(F,+)\to(F_{>0},\cdot)$ that is \emph{onto}, and $w$ a nontrivial convex
valuation --- no Hahn workspace, completeness, saturation or set-size
hypothesis.  The engine is one finite lemma: the displacement
$D(x)=\sigma(x)-x$ of a unital field endomorphism is \emph{not} a derivation
but obeys $D(ab)=\sigma(a)D(b)+bD(a)$, and from $D(a)=d\ne0$ the two probes
$b=2B(1+|\sigma(a)|)/|d|$ and $ab$ force $\max\{|D(b)|,|D(ab)|\}>B$, with
neither surjectivity nor order preservation of $\sigma$.  Through the
surjective, additive, order-reversing map $\nu=w\circ E$, a nonidentity
exponential automorphism stabilizing $\mathcal{O}_w$ displaces value-group
elements cofinally and coinitially, so
$\rho_w:\mathrm{Aut}_{\exp}(F;\mathcal{O}_w)\to\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma,+,<)$ is
injective; coarsening rigidity takes stabilization of $\Delta$ as an explicit
hypothesis.  The kernel of $\nu$ is a proper convex subgroup, bounded by a
\emph{field} element (the finite surreals by $\omega$), so on $\No$, with
$B=\omega$, an exponential automorphism fixing every natural-valuation value
is the identity (Theorem~5.1; the hypothesis is not to be weakened to
preserving comparisons), hence so is every exponential 1-automorphism, with
no strong-additivity or coefficient-fixing hypothesis, offered as a negative
answer to Question~5.4 of Kaplan--Krapp--Serra, arXiv:2509.22374v3.
\emph{Keep that version pinning prominent:} the reading was checked against
that PDF on 22~September 2026, which verifies the interpretation, not
priority, and Gonshor's exponential and the natural valuation are cited, not
reproved.  Section~8 gives two disjoint families of value-group
automorphisms, bounded-layer reweightings and positive rational dilations,
that lift as ordered field automorphisms but not exponentially, so cofinal
displacement is necessary and not sufficient; Section~9, for real closed $F$,
gives $\mathrm{Aut}_L(F(i))\cong\mathrm{Aut}_{\exp}(F)\times C_2$ for
$L(z)=\log\sqrt{z\bar z}$, with no complex-logarithm branch.  Two later
manuscripts re-derived the engine and the answer by the same proof, printed
once; merging them added the theorem that no nonzero twisted derivation of a
nontrivially valued field has all output valuations bounded below by one
element, at any rank and with no ordering (Theorem~3.5), rigidity from one
visible exponential scale without surjectivity of $E$ (Theorem~4.4), an
order-free detector (Corollary~4.5), profile rigidity on a final interval
(Theorem~6.1), cofinal multiplicative motion and cofinal commutation defects
of value-fixing maps, the latter not asserting that such a map exists
(Section~7), no global bound $v(\partial y)\ge v(y)+\gamma$ for a nonzero
$E$-compatible derivation of a nontrivially valued field, applied to the
Berarducci--Mantova derivation (Theorem~10.3), and $\R((t))$ sharpness
examples that are not counterexamples (Section~11).  Rigidity here means
faithfulness, not triviality; the image of $\rho_v$ on $\No$ is not
classified (Question~13.1); the derivation theorems exclude only global
bounds.  The Berarducci--Mantova derivation is an object, not a theorem,
shared with \nolinkurl{surreal/gamma-functions} and
\nolinkurl{surreal/tail-spans-and-differential-transcendence}, and this third
``rigidity'' is neither the all-scale polynomial rigidity of
\nolinkurl{surcomplex/analysis} nor the scalar rigidity of
\nolinkurl{surreal/gamma-functions}.  The staged source audit's conclusion
that the collection lacked this report is false, as its appended correction
says.  Not refereed; no Lean proof; no exhaustive priority claim.}

\entry{Gamma functions over the surreals: a sharp convexity classification}
{surreal/gamma-functions}
{article.pdf}{one manuscript, delivered 21~September 2026}
{Write $M(x)$ for the leading Conway monomial of a positive infinite surreal
with its real coefficient stripped and $\mathrm{pp}(x)$ for the purely infinite
part; for an arbitrary class assignment $a$ on positive infinite monomials, no
regularity assumed, set $h_a(x)=a(M(x))\,\mathrm{pp}(x)$ and
$L_a=L_0+h_a$ over the Taylor--Stirling log-Gamma baseline.  The main theorem
is a two-sided sharp threshold: $L_a$ is convex on all of $\No_{>0}$
\emph{iff} $m\,a(m)$ is finite for every positive infinite monomial $m$, and in
that case it is \emph{strictly} convex --- pointwise in $m$, no uniform real
bound across monomials, both signs allowed, and no merely-convex member exists.
Every member of the family, convex or not, preserves $\Gamma_a(1)=1$, the
recurrence $\Gamma_a(x+1)=x\Gamma_a(x)$, every finite Gauss multiplication
formula, every normalized finite-translation germ and all positive-order fine
derivatives of log-Gamma, so the literal surreal Bohr--Mollerup conditions
do not give uniqueness: $a_\lambda(m)=\lambda e^{-m}$, $\lambda\in\R$, gives pairwise
distinct strictly log-convex extensions whose logarithmic and relative differences are smaller than
\emph{every} ordinary inverse power at \emph{every} infinite argument, the
log-difference at $\omega$ being exactly $\lambda\,\omega e^{-\omega}$, and
adding all ordinary-order Stirling envelopes does not restore uniqueness.
Absolute Gamma differences need not be small: at $\omega$ the difference
$\Gamma_1-\Gamma_0$ exceeds every ordinary power of $\omega$. The
convexity proof uses no second derivatives and no mean value theorem: it works
with the tangent gap $D_F(y,x)=F(y)-F(x)-F'(x)(y-x)$ through four exhaustive
relative-scale regimes, the margin $(y-x)^2/(2x)$ being exactly what generates
the $O(m^{-1})$ threshold, and the mechanism is then isolated as a reusable
scale-margin gauge principle.  A positive second derivative does \emph{not}
imply convexity in this calculus --- the gauge with value one on one monomial
and zero elsewhere has all fine
derivatives equal to the baseline's and a negative tangent gap --- because such
gauges are finely locally constant and convexity cannot be decided locally.
Compatibility with the Berarducci--Mantova derivation forces $h_a$ into
$\ker\der=\R$ and kills every member but $a=0$, yet a different real-valued
non-flat gauge survives, so \emph{scalar compatibility alone is not a
uniqueness characterization}.  Closes with an exact finite-phase domain on the
tube $\mathrm{Re}(z)>0$, $\mathrm{Im}(z)$ finite, using the finite-angle
exponential of the imaginary log-Gamma value, distinct from choosing a
primitive in \nolinkurl{surcomplex/differential-equations}.
\emph{Not offered as a resolution of a named published problem}, and
the classification is exact only for the named monomialwise family.  Its own
audit read an earlier state of this catalogue; its eighteen-package count
is a historical description, not the current inventory.}

\entry{Tail-spans and differential transcendence in surreal and surcomplex Hahn fields}
{surreal/tail-spans-and-differential-transcendence}
{article.pdf}{one manuscript, delivered 22~September 2026}
{Let $M$ have characteristic zero, $\Gamma$ be a set-sized ordered abelian
group, $(a_i)\subset M^\times$ have independent square classes with
$r_i^2=a_i$, the exponents $\gamma_i$ be distinct with well-ordered image,
and $v_i\in M^r$; put $Y=\sum_i t^{\gamma_i}r_iv_i$ over $B=M((t^\Gamma))$.
The invariant is the \emph{cofinite span} $W$, the intersection of the spans
surviving every \emph{finite} deletion, not deletion of a transfinite initial
segment.  Theorem~5.1: $E=\{i:v_i\notin W\}$ is finite and
$B[Y_1,\ldots,Y_r]=B_E[s_1,\ldots,s_d]$, where $d=\dim_MW$,
$B_E=M(r_i:i\in E)((t^\Gamma))$ has degree $2^{|E|}$ over $B$ and the $s_j$
are algebraically independent over $B_E$ --- an equality of subrings, not of
dimensions; Theorem~5.2 splits the relation ideal of $Y$ over $B$, after base
change to $B_E$, into $2^{|E|}$ pairwise comaximal affine planes of
dimension~$d$.  The proof is finitary: the alternating sum of finitely many
coefficient sign changes of a degree-$D$ relation is a mixed difference equal
to $D!$ times its top polarization, which cofinite spanning forces to vanish
identically.  With $t=\omega^{-1}$,
$B_0=\Q((t^\Q))\subset H_0=\mathbb{A}_{\R}((t^\Q))\subset\No$ (real-algebraic
coefficients) and $\xi_A=\sum_{n\in A}\sqrt{p_n}\,\omega^{-n}$, $p_n$ the
$n$th prime: for \emph{every} infinite $A$ the Berarducci--Mantova jets
$\der^k\xi_A$ are algebraically independent over $B_0$ (Theorem~7.1), and
those of the explicit binary-prefix almost disjoint family
$(\xi_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\{0,1\}^\N}$ jointly (Theorem~7.3); with a separate
count $|H_0|\le2^{\aleph_0}$ for the upper bound,
$\mathrm{dtrdeg}_{B_0}H_0=\mathrm{trdeg}_{B_0}H_0=2^{\aleph_0}$.  For every
infinite $A$, all mixed jets $\partial_z^j\delta^kF_A$, $\delta=t\,d/dt$, of
$F_A=\sum_{n\in A}t^n\sqrt{1-z/2^n}$ are independent over $\C(z)((t^\Q))$
(Theorem~9.3), and continuum many such functions jointly on the one disk
$|z|<1$ (Theorem~10.1).  Also a value-group-relative valuation-closure
criterion (Theorem~12.1) and undecidability of algebraicity and differential
algebraicity over $B_0$ for total computable coefficient streams
(Proposition~13.1).  Cautions: the sign changes are not order-preserving and
are not extended to $\No$; $\C(z)((t^\Q))$ is not a common-domain ring; point
values are not claimed transcendental; $F=F_{\N_{>0}}$ satisfies
$F(2z,t)-tF(z,t)=t\sqrt{1-z}$, so functional equations are not excluded; the
family indexed by \emph{all} subsets is dependent,
$\xi_{A\cup B}+\xi_{A\cap B}=\xi_A+\xi_B$ (Remark~7.4, added on fitting).
The formula for $\der$ on $\C((t^\Q))$ is cited from
\nolinkurl{surcomplex/differential-equations}; only the restriction to
real-algebraic coefficients is checked here.
\nolinkurl{surcomplex/dynamics-and-normal-forms} keeps, unweakened, its
warning that its $H_\varepsilon$, with
$H_\varepsilon'=\Omega_\varepsilon H_\varepsilon$, is not differentially
transcendental; Theorem~9.3 concerns different functions.  Its 404 exact
finite checks prove no infinite statement; priority not certified, not
refereed. The Lean ledger records proofs of cofinite-span stabilization
and multilinear detection; the classification and differential-independence
theorems remain pending.}

\entry{Hahn-valued measures and probability: strong atomicity, coefficientwise extension, and hidden negative mass}
{surreal/hahn-valued-measures-and-probability}
{article.pdf}{three manuscripts, delivered 22~September 2026}
{Measures with values in $\R((t^\Gamma))$ and $\C((t^\Gamma))$, and through
$t^\gamma=\omega^{-\gamma}$ in $\No$ and $\SC$, with $\Gamma$ a set-sized ordered
abelian group \emph{not} assumed divisible except in one Gaussian quadrature
theorem, under two meanings of countable
additivity: a \emph{coefficientwise} Hahn measure is a Hahn series of ordinary
finite signed or complex measures on one well-ordered exponent set, added
exponent by exponent; a \emph{strong} one requires the masses of every
disjoint sequence of events to be strongly Hahn summable.
\textbf{Strong class.}  On every countably separated space a strong measure is
$\mu(A)=\sum^{\mathrm H}_{x\in A}w_x$ for one strongly summable family
$(w_x)$, common support and atomicity being conclusions; it extends uniquely
to all subsets, and a strong probability has finitely supported standard part.
Exact criteria are proved for coherent cylinder data over finite alphabets,
for independent finite-state coordinates (finitely many random at the residue
level, and the individual rare entries, not their aggregates, strongly
summable), and for moments on an ordinary real sample set.  One weight pattern
of support order type $\omega+1$ gives nonnegative Cantor-space cylinder
masses, or a functional strictly positive on nonzero Hahn polynomials
nonnegative on $[0,1]$, yet a negative singleton; $\omega+1$ is least.
\textbf{Coefficientwise class.}  A transfinite null-ideal criterion decides
positivity; two exponents already hide a negative singleton; independent
Bernoulli perturbations on a common well-ordered exponent set extend exactly
under weighted $\ell^2$ rows at baselines uniformly separated from $0$ and
$1$, and $\ell^1$ rows at deterministic ones.  \emph{The threshold $\omega+1$
and the finitely supported standard part hold in the strong class only.}  Sources 10 and
11 proved the classification independently, and the threshold for cylinder
and polynomial tests respectively; each is printed once, 10 as base; source 15's
classification on compact metric spaces, with a common support
\emph{assumed}, is recorded as a special case.  Not a first non-Archimedean
Kolmogorov theorem (Ludkovsky--Khrennikov proved one under other axioms);
priority not certified and not refereed.  The Lean ledger records checked
strong atomicity and product prerequisites; the complete report is not
formalized.
\nolinkurl{physics/surreal-scalars-and-spacetime} asked for a summation theory,
and this report works out two; it shows that the object refused by non-claim N75 of
\nolinkurl{surcomplex/dynamics-and-normal-forms} cannot exist in the strong class
for an ordinary group action with only infinite orbits,
and states canonically the null-ideal criterion proved independently in
\nolinkurl{surcomplex/hahn-herglotz-positivity}.}

\entry{Surreal Markov generators at every valuation scale}
{surreal/markov-generators-at-every-scale}
{article.pdf}{one manuscript, delivered 22~September 2026}
{Finite Markov chains with positive Hahn rates $q_e\in F_{>0}$,
$F=\R((t^\Gamma))$, on a strongly connected digraph with $n\ge2$ states;
$\Gamma$ is nonzero, set-sized and \emph{divisible} throughout, read in $\No$
through $t^\gamma\mapsto\omega^{-\gamma}$.  The object is the normalized
resolvent $R_L(s)=s(sI+L)^{-1}$ of the row Laplacian $L$ (generator $-L$),
defined rational-algebraically, and its residue matrix
$K_\alpha(c)=\mathrm{res}\,R_L(ct^\alpha)$ at each scale $\alpha\in\Gamma$,
the report's \emph{shadow}, unrelated to the shadow topology.  In-forests give
the valuation and leading coefficient of \emph{every} resolvent entry,
zero-residue entries included (Theorem~4.1).  There are at most $n-1$ crossover
scales, and the plateaux form a flag of distinct stochastic idempotents
$I=P_0,\dots,P_m=\mathbf{1}\pi_0$, $P_iP_j=P_jP_i=P_{\max(i,j)}$, of strictly
decreasing rank (Theorem~5.2).  On the canonical stochastic retract $P=UV$,
$VU=I$, each crossover is $U\,c(cI+G_\alpha)^{-1}V$ for a real Markov row
Laplacian $G_\alpha=(VK_\alpha(1)U)^{-1}-I$ with semisimple zero eigenvalue,
with no reversibility, simple-eigenvalue or unique-minimizing-forest assumption
(Theorem~6.2).  Conversely (Theorem~7.4, Corollary~7.5), every such flag ending
in rank one, with any compatible real effective generators, occurs at any
prescribed scales $\alpha_1<\dots<\alpha_m$ in an irreducible Hahn row
Laplacian, whose intermediate-scale positivity corrections add no crossover.
Also: relative rate errors of valuation $\ge\delta$ on one fixed graph give the
same bound for every resolvent entry, uniformly in $s>0$ (Theorem~8.1; sharp
by Example~8.3); a real one-parameter monomial model reproduces the whole
\emph{finite} marked-forest leading diagram, so such data cannot certify higher
rank (Theorem~9.2); and $\det(cI_p+G_\alpha)=cD_\alpha(c)/b_{k_-}$ yields the
leading surcomplex eigenvalue amplitudes of $L$, including tied scales and
nonreal eigenvalues, while the forest profile is convex (Theorems~10.2--10.3).

\section{The surcomplex plane}

Twenty reports about $\SC=\No[i]$.  Seven are about holomorphic function
theory, rest on one framework, and are all merges: twenty-seven manuscripts
reduced to seven documents.  The other thirteen came later and are listed
after them.  Eight are merges: differential algebra (ten manuscripts),
dynamics and normal forms (nine), nonabelian support obstructions (three),
entire functions at arbitrary rank (five), finite spectral theory (three),
Hahn--Tate uniformization (three), holonomic rigidity (two) and
infinite-dimensional spectral theory (two).  Five are single manuscripts: on
rank-one analytic geometry, expanding polynomial dynamics, Herglotz
positivity, moment reconstruction and Wick summability.  Each of the thirteen
was written against this repository and names the gap it fills.

\medskip
\noindent
\emph{Read \dir{analysis} first.}  It is the foundation the other six
continue. In the \emph{full fine topology}, every set-sized subset is closed
and discrete and every convergent set-indexed net is eventually constant.
Universe-relative formulations require smaller-universe subsets and indices.
These conclusions do not apply to the intrinsic valuation topology of a fixed
Hahn field: $t^n\to0$ in $\C((t^\Q))$. Strong Hahn summability is a separate
support condition; even in rank one it need not give convergence of finite
subsums, as the foundations report's $\sum t^{n/(n+1)}$ demonstrates.
Contour integration in the analytic reports is defined coefficientwise on
ordinary complex paths.

\medskip
\noindent
\emph{Then read the ring table in \dir{analytic-geometry}, before anything
else.}  Four coefficient rings circulate through this material under nearly
identical notation --- the radius-free $\C\{z\}((t^\Gamma))$, the
fixed-polydisk $\mathcal{O}(D)((t^\Gamma))$, the common-domain germ ring, and
the formal $\C[[z]]((t^\Gamma))$. These are not an interchangeable chain.
For $\eta>0$, the family $F(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}t^{r\eta}/(1-rz)$ lies in the
radius-free germ ring but not the common-domain germ ring: its coefficient
poles $1/r$ approach zero. This witnesses that particular strict inclusion.
The report gives separate fixed-domain obstructions and explains which local
proofs require shrinking. The remaining five reports can be read in any order.
