# Logical and computational audit

## Provenance of this package

This report merges two independently produced research reports on the same
question, `hahn-evaluation-at-omega` and `reversed-hahn-series`. Both proved
the decisive argument below by the *same* argument: the same quadratic
recursion for b_n, the same coefficient verification of B^2 = 1-x, the same
strong induction for b_n < 0, and the same application of the hypothetical map
to the single finite identity. The audit chain below is therefore audited
once, and the merged article proves it once. The supporting machinery of the
two reports genuinely diverged; the merged article is their union, and the
sections below flag which items are new.

## The decisive argument

**Claim.** Problem 7.7, arXiv:2505.00424v2, has a negative answer.

The audit chain is:

1. Its increasing surreal-exponent supports include the support N of B(x).
2. Define b_0=1, b_1=-1/2, and
   b_n=-(1/2) sum_{k=1}^{n-1} b_k b_{n-k} for n >= 2.
3. This defines rational coefficients at every natural index. All coefficients
   after b_0 are strictly negative and dyadic; no support entry is illegal.
4. Hahn convolution on this support is ordinary formal power-series convolution.
5. The recursion proves B^2=1-x at every coefficient, not only at tested degrees.
6. The prescribed image of x^0 forces Phi(1)=1; the image of x^1 forces Phi(x)=omega.
7. Ring operations alone give Phi(B)^2 = 1-omega.
8. The right-hand side is negative in the surreal ordered field. A square cannot
   be negative. This is a contradiction.

## Assumptions not used

- Preservation of infinite sums.
- Continuity in a topology or valuation.
- An assumption of order preservation.
- Fixation of arbitrary real coefficients.
- Injectivity of the hypothetical map.
- Real closedness of the target; any ordered field with the specified image > 1 fails.
- A class-sized witness, global choice for a proper class, or a large-cardinal axiom.
- The analytic convergence of the binomial series at any real or surreal point.

The recursion and the identity are internal to a set-sized ring. More strongly,
the obstruction lies in the countable finitely generated Q-algebra Q[x,B]=Q[B].
The larger ring Q[[x]] is not countable.

## The second, independent obstruction

**Claim.** No unital semiring homomorphism from Q_{>=0}[[x]] to an ordered
field K sends x to any u >= 1.

The audit chain is:

1. h_n = binom(2n,n)/4^n and the all-ones sequence are nonnegative rationals,
   so H(x) and G(x) lie in the semiring, which is closed under coefficientwise
   addition and the finite Cauchy product.
2. The coefficient ratio 2(n+1)h_{n+1} = (2n+1)h_n says 2(1-x)H' = H.
3. Computing in the containing ring Q[[x]], the derivative of (1-x)H^2 is
   -H^2 + 2(1-x)HH' = 0, and in characteristic zero a series with zero
   derivative is constant. Its constant coefficient is 1, so (1-x)H^2 = 1.
4. G = 1 + xG is immediate coefficientwise, so G inverts 1-x and H^2 = G.
   Steps 2–4 used subtraction, but the conclusion H^2 = G and G = 1 + xG
   mentions no negative number; both sides lie in the semiring.
5. Applying psi gives g = z^2 >= 0 and g = 1 + ug, hence (1-u)g = 1 in K.
6. For u >= 1 the left side is nonpositive and the right side is 1 > 0.

**This is not a corollary of the main theorem, and the main theorem is not a
corollary of it.** The hypotheses are weaker on two axes at once — a semiring
source with no additive inverses, and u >= 1 rather than u > 1 — while the
domain is smaller, so a ring homomorphism on Q[[x]] is not a semiring
homomorphism claim about Q_{>=0}[[x]]. Both statements are proved and both are
kept. Neither makes the other redundant.

**What is *not* assumed in the semiring argument.** No order is preserved and
no infinite sum is preserved. The images of arbitrary positive-coefficient
series are not assumed positive. The single value forced to be nonnegative is
g, and it is forced only because it is a square in the target. Only the
subsemiring generated by x, H, G and the nonnegative rational constants is
used.

## Care needed in the extensions

**Two square-root lemmas, both retained.** The merged article keeps both the
substitution proof that 1+qx is a unit square (obtained by substituting -qx
into B) and the general recursion giving a unique square root in 1 + x k[[x]]
for every unit over a characteristic-zero field k. Neither subsumes the other
in practice: the substitution proof needs only the already-constructed B and
is the one reproduced in the standalone `short-proof.tex`; the general lemma
supplies uniqueness, an arbitrary unit and an arbitrary base field, and is
what the Laurent-expansion and injectivity arguments later use.

**Two proofs of the two-support fact, both retained.** That A+B is well
ordered with finite fibres is derived twice: once as a corollary of Higman's
lemma via the positive-support monoid, and once directly from the
finite-product nondecreasing-subsequence principle, with no well-quasi-order
machinery at all. The second keeps the *definition* of the Hahn product
independent of Higman's lemma; the first is what the infinite-substitution
results need, since those require control of arbitrarily many factors.

**Two derivations of the closed form, both retained.** One forms E = D^2 and
reads off a first-order coefficient recurrence; the other divides by 1-x and
uses that a formal series with zero derivative is constant. The second
technique is the one reused for H, so it must be present in any case.

**Strict positivity in the positive-support lemma.** Allowing 0 into A would
destroy part (b): appending zeros gives infinitely many representations of the
same element. Part (a) would survive, but (b) is what the regrouping arguments
use.

**Summability is not convergence.** The merged article includes an explicit
counterexample: sum omega^{-n} = 1/(1-omega^{-1}) is a legitimate Hahn sum,
yet s - s_N > omega^{-omega} for every N, so no partial sum enters that
neighbourhood of s. Statements about infinite sums in this package are always
about Hahn summability, never about a topological limit.

**Infinitesimal bounds.** The roots of 1±nx are units. Their images are nonzero,
so their squares are strictly positive and yield strict inequalities.

**Sign in a nondivisible exponent group.** Q[[x]] can send x to a negative
infinitesimal. A prescribed pure monomial omega^{f(a)} is always positive.
The full surreal exponent group is divisible, so its positive monomials are
also squares. No unjustified positivity is assumed for arbitrary power-series maps.

**Strong versus ordinary homomorphisms.** The monomial existence criterion is
necessary for every unital homomorphism, with no strong-additivity hypothesis.
The sufficient construction is coefficient-fixing and strongly additive.
Uniqueness is proved only among maps with those two properties, not among all
abstract homomorphisms with the same monomial images.

**Coefficient embeddings.** An arbitrary embedding of the real field into No
is not assumed to fix each real merely because it fixes Q. The obstruction
avoids that issue, and the positive constructions explicitly fix coefficients.

**Conway omega-map.** All omega^a in the monomial classification refer to Conway's
omega-map. The article does not substitute the expression exp(a log omega)
as its definition for arbitrary surreal a.

**Classical repair.** The exponent-reversing construction is already explicit
in van den Dries–Ehrlich (2001), p. 176, for set-sized exponent groups. It is
background, not an originality claim. It reverses exponent order but preserves
field order.

**Geometric series.** Sending 1+x+x^2+... to 1/(1-omega) is consistent on the
rational subfield. Its negative value alone is not a contradiction to an
ordinary ring homomorphism. The square-root relation supplies the contradiction.

**Positive-support lemma.** The support is a well-ordered set of strictly
positive group elements. Higman's finite-word lemma gives both well-ordering
of the generated monoid and finiteness of every word-representation fiber.
The latter, not merely the former, is needed for summability and regrouping.
The special case of Higman's lemma used here is proved in the appendix.

**Square cone and order.** All positive elements being squares establishes
uniqueness of ordering in the 2-divisible Hahn case. It is not asserted that
this property alone characterizes real-closed fields.

## Computational evidence

Executed command:

    python code/verify.py --degree 256 --trials 1000 --output results

The run passed. It uses exact fractions and integers, with explicit runtime
checks rather than removable `assert` statements: `require` raises a dedicated
`VerificationFailure`, which `main` catches alongside `ValueError`,
`AssertionError` and `OSError`, so a failed check prints a `FAIL` line and
exits nonzero instead of producing a traceback. The following are checked:

- 257 coefficients of B, generated by five independent routes, agree exactly:
  the quadratic recursion, the general unit square-root recursion applied to
  1-x, the first-order binomial recursion, the Catalan closed formula, and
  Catalan numbers regenerated by the convolution recurrence
  C_m = sum C_i C_{m-1-i}.
- B^2 = 1-x modulo x^257.
- H^2 = G, BH = 1, and (1-x)G = 1, all modulo x^257.
- All tested nonconstant coefficients of B are negative and all are dyadic;
  all 257 tested coefficients of H are strictly positive.
- Both differential recurrences, 2(n+1)b_{n+1} = (2n-1)b_n and
  2(n+1)h_{n+1} = (2n+1)h_n, at every checked degree.
- 32 identities for 1±nx (1 <= n <= 16) through degree 64, obtained by scaling
  the coefficients of B, and 12 square roots of 1+cx at signed rational
  c in {±1, ±2, ±3, ±1/2, ±2/3, ±7/5} through degree 64, obtained from the
  general routine. The rational values exercise code the integer scalings do
  not reach.
- 1000 deterministic random finite sparse rational-exponent examples at
  randomly drawn positive rational scales, seed 20260920; and separately 200
  Laurent-polynomial pairs at each of the three fixed scales 1, 2, 3/2, that is
  600 scale/pair cases, seed 1729. Each case tests addition, multiplication,
  and strict reversal of the support order under a -> -c a, c > 0.

Both exponent-reversal harnesses are run because they are not the same test:
one samples scales at random, the other pins three scales including a
non-integer one, and the two generators draw exponents and coefficients from
different ranges.

`results/coefficients.csv` now carries seven columns — n, numerator,
denominator and exact_fraction for b_n, then h_n, coefficient_B_squared and
coefficient_H_squared — for degrees 0 to 256.

Not checked by this program: infinite summability, the class No, the field
axioms of No, arbitrary group supports, proof-assistant kernels, or novelty.
Those are not silently inferred from the finite checks.

## Priority and status

The application resolves the precise stated homomorphism question. It does
not settle the neighboring questions on infinitary operations for games or
all possible surreal series-summation schemes; in particular, Lipparini's
broader Problem 7.5 is not refuted by the failure of this ring-homomorphism
approach, since a partial operation, an operation on a smaller domain, or a
nonmultiplicative operation evades the obstruction. The latest inspected arXiv
revision explicitly poses the question. The focused search found no resolution,
but the article makes no guarantee of bibliographic priority. There has been
no independent referee or proof-assistant verification.

Two references carried over from the merged manuscript, Kaplan–Krapp–Serra
(arXiv:2509.22374v1) and Bournez–Guilmant (arXiv:2201.08199), were not
independently re-verified in this session. They are cited for standard
background only, nothing proved here depends on them, and `sources.md` records
that status separately from the version-audited sources.
