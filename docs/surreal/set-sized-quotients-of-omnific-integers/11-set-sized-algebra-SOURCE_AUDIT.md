## Published and posted sources actually checked

1. Sonia L'Innocente and Vincenzo Mantova, *A factorisation theory for
   generalised power series and omnific integers*, arXiv:1710.07304v5,
   22 January 2024, 64 pages.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07304
   https://arxiv.org/html/1710.07304v5
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.07304v5
   Published-version DOI: 10.1016/j.aim.2024.109513.

   The abstract and introduction, normal-form background, relevant portions
   of Section 8, and reference entries were checked. PDF pages 1-2 and 45-46
   were also viewed as images. Section 8 treats the same general kind of
   coefficient subring plus bounded-support series. Its Fact 8.0.1 attributes
   a lower-Archimedean-scale factorization observation to Gonshor; the
   geometric factorization displayed on page 46 is a clear precedent for
   the local division mechanism used here. Their existing primality theorem
   for omega^(sqrt(2)) + omega + 1 is explicitly imported, not claimed anew.

2. Jesse Elliott, *Transcendence degree of the surreals over the subfield
   generated by the ordinals*, MathOverflow question 188430,
   posted 30 November 2014.
   https://mathoverflow.net/questions/188430/transcendence-degree-of-the-surreals-over-the-subfield-generated-by-the-ordinals

   The full available page was read. Its final addition asks whether the
   natural ordinal Grothendieck ring is a quotient of Oz. The displayed
   answer by Eric Wofsey concerns transcendence degree, a different question.
   The present manuscript gives a negative answer to the quotient question,
   including a short additive proof. This is not a certification that no
   specialist or other source had previously supplied that answer.

3. Conway's *On Numbers and Games* and Gonshor's *An Introduction to the
   Theory of Surreal Numbers* are cited as classical background. Those books
   were not independently read in full in this task. Gonshor's series number
   110 and the background reference entries were cross-checked against the
   bibliography of the primary factorization paper.

## Contribution assessment

- Classical or established inputs: normal forms, monomial arithmetic, Hahn
  convolution, set cuts, the coefficient-subring construction, and the
  underlying subordinate-scale geometric division method.
- Main proposed contribution: the arbitrarily-large pairwise-difference
  divisibility construction and its use to prove that *every* homomorphism
  into a set-sized ring factors through constant term.
- Proposed refinements: the countable-support threshold and exact extension
  criterion, a target-cardinality theorem for set-sized exponent groups,
  and the full Gaussian version.
- Consequences proved explicitly: quotient and module classifications,
  set-sized derivations (including Gaussian 2-torsion), localization and
  polynomial-presentation reduction, and residually set-sized targets.
- The posted ordinal quotient question: answered negatively with two proofs;
  the elementary additive proof makes its actual difficulty transparent.

No identical universal ring-representation formulation was located in the
material inspected. Historical originality remains unverified.

## Verification boundary

The written proof checks reverse well-ordering, finiteness of coefficient
contributions, strict positivity of quotient exponents, and cardinality of
all index sets used. It distinguishes Hahn identities from topological
convergence. It also explains set-sized coding for quotient and module
language, without forming a class whose elements are arbitrary classes.

The exact finite script passed 1858 checks. It has no network dependencies
and no third-party Python dependencies. These checks validate only finite
algebra and boundary examples, not infinite support or class-size arguments.
No Lean proof or Lean build was performed, and no independent referee
review is claimed.

The PDF was compiled with pdfLaTeX, checked for unresolved references and
layout warnings, rendered, and visually inspected. Font binaries, build
intermediates, and page-render images are excluded from the ZIP.
