\begin{abstract}
Let $G$ be a nonzero set-sized ordered abelian group, let $K$ have characteristic
zero, and let $\mathcal B_G(K)$ be the ring of Hahn series whose support is
bounded above in $G$. Put $\mathcal F_G(K)=\Frac\mathcal B_G(K)$ and
$\kappa=\cof(G)$. We construct $2^\kappa$ elements of $K((t^G))$, all with
positive integer coefficients and one common support of order type $\kappa$,
that are algebraically independent over $\mathcal F_G(K)$. No smaller support
cardinality can support even one transcendental element over this base.
Every valuation ball contains such an independent family. In particular, the
transcendence degree is the continuum for real-exponent Hahn fields over
$\R$ or $\C$.

A complementary descent theorem proves that, whenever $H\subseteq G$ is
cofinal and $K\subseteq L$, the fields $K((t^H))$ and $\mathcal F_G(L)$ are
linearly disjoint over $\mathcal F_H(K)$. Polynomial relation ideals and finite
algebraic degrees therefore survive simultaneous enlargement of coefficients
and cofinal exponent groups. We also classify when $\mathcal B_G(K)$ is a
field, reduce its unit theory to the highest Archimedean quotient, and prove
a conditional GCD-descent theorem relevant to a published pre-Schreier
question. Concrete Conway-normal-form specializations give independent
families of actual surreal numbers, including maximal families of size
$2^\kappa$ at every infinite regular cardinal.

A second part, from a second manuscript, develops the Galois theory of
$\FF{\C}G$ and $\FF{\R}G$, which are also the fraction fields of the
canonical omnific subrings at the scale $G$. For divisible $G$ there is an
exact rank dichotomy: without an order unit $\FF{\C}G$ is algebraically
closed and $\FF{\R}G$ real closed; with one, every product of at most
continuum many finite groups is a Galois group over $\FF{\C}G$ inside the
ambient Hahn field, with explicit independent radical towers and an
explicit $S_m$ family. Cofinal inclusions of exponent groups preserve every
finite Galois extension, while noncofinal ones split every finite
extension after scalar extension; an explicit countable tower inside
$\No[i]$ repeats this.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem, the results, and their provenance}\label{bst:sec:intro}

A Hahn series can contain infinitely many terms while its exponents remain in
one bounded interval. Such a series is not a polynomial, and adjoining all
such series produces a much larger base than a rational function field.
The question pursued here is what remains genuinely transcendental after
\emph{every} bounded-support series has been admitted as a coefficient, and
how that answer changes when the ambient exponent group changes.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:base}
 \BB KG=\{f\in\HH KG:\supp(f)\text{ is bounded above in }G\},
 \qquad \FF KG=\Frac\BB KG.
\end{equation}
The phrase ``bounded support'' always refers to the order on the specified
exponent group. It does not mean finite support, bounded coefficients, or a
bound on the cardinality of the support. In particular it is not the
cardinal restriction sometimes called a $\lambda$-bounded Hahn field.

\subsection{The principal theorems}

Here is the theorem package in a form that can be read before its proofs.
All fields and groups in these statements are sets. Cardinal assertions are
made in set theory with choice.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal-support independence; proved in Section~\ref{bst:sec:independence}]
\label{bst:thm:mainintro}
Let $K$ have characteristic zero and $G\ne0$ be an ordered abelian group.
Set $\kappa=\cof(G)$. There are a strictly increasing cofinal sequence
$(a_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa}$ of positive elements of $G$ and positive integers
$c_A(\alpha)$, for $A\subseteq\kappa$, such that
\[
 Y_A=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}c_A(\alpha)t^{a_\alpha}
 \quad(A\subseteq\kappa)
\]
are algebraically independent over $\FF KG$. Their common support has order
type $\kappa$. The minimum possible cardinality, and the minimum possible
order type, of the support of a series transcendental over $\FF KG$ are both
$\kappa$. Every nonempty valuation ball contains a family of $2^\kappa$
elements algebraically independent over $\FF KG$.
\end{theorem}

The strength is not a cardinality comparison between the base and the larger
field: they can have the same cardinality. It is the construction of a free
polynomial algebra over the entire bounded-support fraction field, with
integer-coefficient witnesses and an optimal support requirement.

\begin{theorem}[Cofinal coefficient--exponent descent; proved in Section~\ref{bst:sec:descent}]
\label{bst:thm:descentintro}
Let $K\subseteq L$ be fields and let $0\ne H\subseteq G$ be ordered abelian
groups. If $H$ is cofinal in $G$, multiplication induces an injection
\[
 \FF LG\otimes_{\FF KH}\HH KH\ \longrightarrow\ \HH LG.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \FF LG\cap\HH KH=\FF KH.
\]
For every finite tuple from $\HH KH$, its polynomial relation ideal over
$\FF LG$ is the ideal generated by its relations over $\FF KH$.
Finite algebraic degrees are preserved. In characteristic zero the cofinality hypothesis is
also necessary for the displayed multiplication map to be injective.
\end{theorem}

For example, with $H=\Z$, $G=\R$, $K=\Q$, and $L=\C$, every algebraic
relation among ordinary rational-coefficient Laurent series over
$\FF{\C}{\R}$ already comes from relations over $\Q(t)$. This is a
simultaneous coefficient and exponent extension statement, not merely an
intersection statement.

The structural companion is an exact distinction between the existence and
absence of a highest scale. The ring $\BB KG$ is a field precisely when $G$
has no order unit. In that case it is the union of the Hahn fields over
proper convex subgroups. For divisible $G$ and real closed $K$, that union is real closed; for
algebraically closed $K$ of characteristic zero, it is algebraically closed.
When an order unit exists, the units are controlled by the highest
Archimedean quotient; see Section~\ref{bst:sec:rank}.

\subsection{What is already known, and what is proposed here}

L'Innocente and Mantova study bounded-support localization and its arithmetic
in \cite{LM24}. Their Proposition~2.4.5 already gives a cofinality obstruction
to representing all Hahn series by fractions of one-sided series. That
nonrationality obstruction is \emph{not} claimed here as new. The proposed
strengthening is the $2^{\cof(G)}$-sized algebraically independent family with
one optimal common support, together with the coefficient--exponent descent
and its exact converse. Their Proposition~3.5.1 supplies the upper-support
multiplicativity used only in the unit and GCD discussion.

The repository audit used the documentation catalogue and the closest
transcendence report at revision
\begin{center}\small\auditcommit.\end{center}
At that revision the catalogue listed 36 reports. Raw placement in commit
\placecommit\ added this report and three others, bringing that historical
count to 40; their editorial assembly followed in commit \assemblycommit. Its tail-span report \cite{RepoTail}
constructs differential independence using independent square classes in
coefficient extensions. Here the coefficients of the witnesses can all be
positive integers, the base is a bounded-support fraction field, and the
mechanism is support separation. These are different base fields and
different assertions; neither report should be described as automatically
subsuming the other. The catalogue also contains entire-function and
cofinality results \cite{RepoEntire}, but a ring of entire functions is not
the ring in \eqref{bst:eq:base}. Section~\ref{bst:sec:collection} states
precisely which results of these reports overlap with those here and which
differ.

The proposed priority claims are deliberately limited. Targeted searches did
not identify the combined descent theorem or the sharp independent-family
theorem in these formulations. This is not an exhaustive MathSciNet,
Zentralblatt, thesis, or citation-network audit. The elementary coding
lemma, standard linear-disjointness algebra, Hahn field construction,
real/algebraic closedness criteria, and Conway normal form are background,
not new discoveries. A later repository revision, \texttt{6e34651fe640},
was observed during the audit; its recorded change concerned Hahn--Hilbert
spectral theory. No claim of a full source-by-source audit of that later
revision is made.

\subsection{Dependencies and the boundary of the claim}

The main independence proof uses only Hahn multiplication, finite polynomial
algebra, cardinal cofinality, and a fully proved finite-pattern coding
lemma. It does not use a GCD theorem, the upper-support multiplicativity
result, any surreal derivation, or analytic convergence. The descent proof
is coefficientwise and works in arbitrary characteristic. Standard
Hahn-field closedness and Conway normal form are invoked only where named.
Appendix~\ref{bst:sec:gcd} proves an implication toward a published GCD/pre-Schreier
problem; it does not supply the missing real-exponent GCD input.

\section{Hahn series and bounded-support algebra}\label{bst:sec:setup}

\subsection{Conventions}

An ordered abelian group is written additively. A Hahn series over $K$ is a
formal expression
\[
 f=\sum_{g\in G} f_g t^g
\]
whose support $\{g:f_g\ne0\}$ is well ordered. Addition is coefficientwise
and multiplication is the Hahn convolution. At each exponent the relevant
sum is finite. Finite unions and finite sumsets of well-ordered supports
are well ordered. These facts give its ring operations. For a nonzero
series, factor out its leading monomial to write $f=a t^g(1+h)$ with
$a\in K^\times$ and $h$ of strictly positive support (or $h=0$).
The Hahn--Neumann positive-support lemma makes $\sum_{n\ge0}(-h)^n$
strongly summable and permits coefficientwise telescoping, giving the
inverse $a^{-1}t^{-g}\sum_{n\ge0}(-h)^n$. This standard inverse
construction completes the field $\HH KG$; see \cite[Section~2.1]{LM24}.
It uses no valuation convergence of the finite geometric sums.
We use the conventional valuation
\[
 v(f)=\min\supp(f)\quad(f\ne0),\qquad v(0)=+\infty.
\]
Thus $v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)$ and $v(f+g)\ge\min(v(f),v(g))$.
The convention at zero is stated explicitly because some treatments of
one-sided series use a different special-symbol convention.

A family of Hahn series is \emph{strongly summable} when its combined support
is well ordered and only finitely many summands contribute at any exponent.
A sum along a strictly increasing sequence of exponents is strongly
summable, independently of whether its finite partial sums converge in a
chosen topology. All transfinite sums below are Hahn sums in this sense.

For $f\in\HH KG$ and $\delta\in G$, write
\[
 f_{\le\delta}=\sum_{g\le\delta} f_g t^g.
\]
A nonzero Hahn support has a least element. Therefore ``bounded above'' in
\eqref{bst:eq:base} is equivalent to containment in some order interval
$[\ell,u]\subseteq G$.

\begin{lemma}[Elementary support bounds]\label{bst:lem:bounds}
The set $\BB KG$ is a subring of $\HH KG$, contains $K$ and every monomial,
and is closed under taking arbitrary subseries. If
$\supp(f)\subseteq[\ell,u]$ and
$\supp(g)\subseteq[\ell',u']$, then
\[
 \supp(fg)\subseteq[\ell+\ell',u+u'].
\]
Every finite family of bounded-support series has common lower and upper
support bounds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every exponent of a product is a sum of one exponent from each factor.
Cancellation only removes terms and cannot create an exponent outside the
indicated interval. A finite union is bounded by the minimum of its lower
bounds and the maximum of its upper bounds. Negation leaves the support
unchanged. Every subset of a well-ordered set is well ordered, proving the
subseries assertion. These observations also cover zero by using arbitrary
bounds for its empty support.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Localization and density]\label{bst:prop:localdensity}
For any nonzero $G$,
\[
 \BB KG=t^G K((t^{G^{\le0}})).
\]
Both $\BB KG$ and $\FF KG$ are dense in $\HH KG$ for the valuation topology.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\supp(f)\le u$, then $t^{-u}f$ has nonpositive support. Conversely a
monomial translate of a nonpositive support is bounded above. For density,
$f_{\le\delta}\in\BB KG$ and either $f=f_{\le\delta}$ or
$v(f-f_{\le\delta})>\delta$. Hence every basic valuation ball contains a
bounded-support element. The assertion for the fraction field follows.
Here $t^G K((t^{G^{\le0}}))$ denotes the set of monomial translates of
the nonpositive-support subring. It is its localization at the monomials
$t^{-g}$ for $g\ge0$: choose the upper bound $u\ge0$ above, so
$f=(t^{-u}f)/t^{-u}$. This specifies the localization used in Appendix~\ref{bst:sec:gcd}.
\end{proof}

There is no assertion here that bounded-support series are finite sums.
For example
\[
 \sum_{n\ge2} t^{1-1/n}\in\BB{\R}{\Q}
\]
has infinitely many exponents in $[1/2,1)$. Nor is density an algebraicity
statement. The main theorem will show that an enormous transcendental
extension can remain after adjoining this dense subring's entire fraction
field.

\subsection{Cofinality}

\begin{definition}
For a nonzero ordered abelian group $G$, its cofinality $\cof(G)$ is the least
cardinality of a subset unbounded above in $G$. An ordered subgroup $H$ is
\emph{cofinal} if for every $g\in G$ some $h\in H$ satisfies $h>g$.
An \emph{order unit} is an element $u>0$ such that for every $g\in G$ some
positive integer $n$ satisfies $\abs g\le nu$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{bst:lem:cofinality}
The cardinal $\kappa=\cof(G)$ is infinite and regular. There is a strictly
increasing positive cofinal sequence $(g_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa}$.
Every subset of $G$ of cardinality less than $\kappa$ is bounded above.
An ordered subgroup is either cofinal or bounded above.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
No finite subset is cofinal, since it has a maximum and $G$ has positive
elements. The last two assertions follow from the definitions. Start with
a cofinal family of size $\kappa$ and recursively choose a value exceeding
its next member and all previously chosen values. At stage $\alpha<\kappa$
the latter set has size less than $\kappa$, so a bound exists. This gives
the increasing sequence. If $\kappa$ were singular, a cofinal subsequence
of its indices of cardinality $\cof(\kappa)<\kappa$ would give a smaller
cofinal subset of $G$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

For a nonzero subgroup of $\R$, every positive element is an order unit and
the cofinality is $\aleph_0$. The converse implication from countable
cofinality to the existence of an order unit is false: a countable ascending
union of strictly increasing Archimedean scales has no highest scale.
Section~\ref{bst:sec:surreal} gives concrete examples inside $\No$.

\subsection{Terminology shared with other reports}\label{bst:subsec:terms}

The following conventions fix one meaning for each word and symbol that is
used differently elsewhere in the collection.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Cofinality.} $\cof(G)$ is the least cardinality of a subset of
the ordered set $G$ that is unbounded above. This is exactly the invariant
$\cof(\Gamma)$ of the entire-functions report \cite{RepoEntire}, whose
definition uses ``every element is at most some member''; for a nonzero
ordered group, which has no greatest element, the two definitions agree.
\item \emph{Cofinal.} The adjective is used for a subset or sequence of $G$
that is unbounded above, and for an ordered subgroup $H\subseteq G$ with the
same property. It is used once for a set of \emph{indices}: in
Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:coding} a set of ordinals is cofinal in the initial
ordinal $\kappa$. The entire-functions report expressly excludes that
ordinal-index meaning from its own word (its remark \texttt{ent:rem:one-name});
here it is confined to that lemma and the proofs that cite it.
\item \emph{Order unit.} The definition above agrees with
\texttt{ent:def:order-unit}: $\abs g\le nu$ for all $g$ is equivalent to
$\{mu:m\ge1\}$ being unbounded above. An order unit forces $\cof(G)=\aleph_0$,
and the converse fails, in both reports.
\item \emph{Cofinite} is not \emph{cofinal}. The tail-span report's
cofinite span is an intersection over finite deletions of coefficient
indices; nothing in this report deletes finitely many indices.
\item \emph{Bounded.} ``Bounded support'' always means bounded above in the
named exponent group, as in \eqref{bst:eq:base}, and never bounded above in
all of $\No$ (Section~\ref{bst:sec:surreal}). It is not a bound on
coefficients or on valuations, and not the cardinal restriction of a
$\lambda$-bounded Hahn field.
\item \emph{Symbols.} $\BB KG$ and $\FF KG$ always carry both the exponent
group and the coefficient field. The collection's notation guide
\cite{RepoNotation} writes $F_\Gamma$ for the full real Hahn field
$\R((t^\Gamma))$, and the tail-span report writes $B$ and $B_0$ for full
Hahn fields $M((t^\Gamma))$ and $\Q((t^\Q))$. Neither is meant by the
calligraphic letters here: $\FF KG$ is the fraction field of the
bounded-support ring, a proper subfield of $\HH KG$ in characteristic zero.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Cofinal coefficient--exponent descent}\label{bst:sec:descent}

The following result is useful independently of the lacunary constructions.
It explains why a bounded-support coefficient cannot conceal a new relation
among series supported on a cofinal subgroup.

\subsection{Coset projections and coefficient functionals}

Let $K\subseteq L$ be fields, $H\subseteq G$ an ordered subgroup, and
$r\in G$. Define the shifted coset projection
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:projection}
 \pi_r(f)=\sum_{h\in H}f_{r+h}t^h\in\HH LH.
\end{equation}
It is defined for every $f\in\HH LG$: a subset of its support remains well
ordered, and translation preserves that property. This is not asserted to
be a ring homomorphism: for $H=\Z\subset G=\Q$, one has
$\pi_0(t^{1/2})=0$ but $\pi_0((t^{1/2})^2)=t$. Its useful property
is the module identity below.

\begin{lemma}[Projection identity]\label{bst:lem:projection}
For $z\in\HH LH$ and $b\in\HH LG$,
\[
 \pi_r(bz)=\pi_r(b)z.
\]
If $H$ is cofinal and $b\in\BB LG$, then $\pi_r(b)\in\BB LH$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A term of $bz$ at an exponent in $r+H$ can only use a term of $b$ in that
same coset, because all exponents of $z$ lie in $H$. At every exponent the
convolution is finite, so selecting the coset commutes with it. If the
support of $b$ is bounded above by $u\in G$, then the shifted support is
bounded above in $G$ by $u-r$. Cofinality supplies an $H$-valued bound.
\end{proof}

Let $\lambda:L\to K$ be any $K$-linear functional. Apply it to each
coefficient to obtain
\[
 \lambda_*:\HH LH\longrightarrow\HH KH.
\]
This map can kill support terms but introduces none. In particular it
preserves boundedness of support. For $z\in\HH KH$ it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:lambda}
 \lambda_*(bz)=\lambda_*(b)z.
\end{equation}
The reason is again the finiteness of each convolution coefficient, now
combined with $K$-linearity. No multiplicativity of $\lambda$ is needed.
For any nonzero $a\in L$, a $K$-linear functional with $\lambda(a)\ne0$
exists: extend $a$ to a basis and prescribe its value to be $1$.

\subsection{The descent theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous descent]\label{bst:thm:descent}
Let $K\subseteq L$ be fields and let $0\ne H\subseteq G$ be cofinal.
Every finite family of elements of $\HH KH$ linearly independent over
$\FF KH$ remains linearly independent over $\FF LG$. Equivalently, the
natural multiplication map
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:tensor}
 \FF LG\otimes_{\FF KH}\HH KH\longrightarrow\HH LG
\end{equation}
is injective.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $z_1,\ldots,z_m\in\HH KH$ be linearly independent over $\FF KH$.
Suppose there is a nonzero linear relation over $\FF LG$. After multiplying
by a common nonzero denominator, it becomes
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:linearrel}
 \sum_{j=1}^m b_jz_j=0,\qquad b_j\in\BB LG,
\end{equation}
with some $b_{j_0}\ne0$. Choose an exponent $r\in\supp(b_{j_0})$ and set
$a=(b_{j_0})_r\ne0$. Applying $\pi_r$ to \eqref{bst:eq:linearrel} gives
\[
 \sum_j\pi_r(b_j)z_j=0,
\]
where all projected coefficients belong to $\BB LH$. Choose a $K$-linear
functional $\lambda:L\to K$ with $\lambda(a)=1$. Applying $\lambda_*$ and
using \eqref{bst:eq:lambda} yields
\[
 \sum_j\lambda_*(\pi_r(b_j))z_j=0.
\]
Its coefficients belong to $\BB KH\subseteq\FF KH$. The coefficient of
$t^0$ in $\lambda_*(\pi_r(b_{j_0}))$ is $1$, so the relation is nonzero.
This contradicts the assumed independence.

To verify the tensor formulation directly, write a tensor using a finite
$\FF KH$-linearly independent family of second factors. If its image is
zero, the first part of the proof forces every first-factor coefficient to
vanish. The converse equivalence follows by the same representation.
This agrees with the standard definition of linear disjointness
\cite{StacksLD}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Intersection, relations, and algebraic degree]\label{bst:cor:relations}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\FF LG\cap\HH KH=\FF KH$ inside $\HH LG$.
\item For a finite tuple $z$ in $\HH KH$, let $I_K(z)$ and $I_L(z)$ be its
relation ideals over $\FF KH$ and $\FF LG$, respectively. Then
\[
 I_L(z)=I_K(z)\,\FF LG[X_1,\ldots,X_m].
\]
\item Algebraic independence of any family in $\HH KH$ is preserved in both
directions by this base enlargement.
\item If $z\in\HH KH$ is algebraic of degree $d$ over $\FF KH$, it has degree
$d$ over $\FF LG$, with the same monic minimal polynomial.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the intersection statement, $z\notin\FF KH$ makes $1,z$ linearly
independent over $\FF KH$, hence over $\FF LG$; it cannot then belong to
$\FF LG$. The reverse inclusion is immediate.

Let $E=\FF KH$ and $F=\FF LG$. The quotient $E[X]/I_K(z)$ embeds in
$\HH KH$. Tensoring that inclusion with the $E$-vector space $F$ preserves
injectivity. By Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent}, the resulting algebra still embeds
in $\HH LG$. Its presentation is $F[X]/I_K(z)F[X]$, so its evaluation
kernel is exactly the asserted ideal. This proves the second assertion and
then the third. Finally $1,z,\ldots,z^{d-1}$ remain linearly independent,
while the old degree-$d$ equation still holds, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

For a finite Galois subextension of $\HH KH/\FF KH$, the same argument
preserves the degree and Galois group after compositum with $\FF LG$.
Indeed, the tensor product embeds in the Hahn field by descent, so it
is a domain. It is finite-dimensional over $\FF LG$ of the original
degree, hence a field: multiplication by a nonzero element is an injective
linear map and therefore surjective. Its distinct
base-field automorphisms extend by the identity on $\FF LG$, and their
number already equals that degree. This observation does not assert that
every algebraic germ extension is Galois.
(Added in batch 32.) Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport} adds that the whole
subgroup--intermediate-field correspondence is transported, and
Sections~\ref{bst:gr:sec:covers}--\ref{bst:gr:sec:real} use this transport to
realize every finite group, and products of at most $\cc$ of them, as Galois
groups over $\FF{\C}G$ when $G$ has an order unit.

\subsection{The ordinary Laurent-series case}

\begin{corollary}[One cofinal scale]\label{bst:cor:onescale}
If $u>0$ is an order unit of $G$ and $K\subseteq L$, then
$K((t^u))$ and $\FF LG$ are linearly disjoint over $K(t^u)$. In particular
\[
 K((t^u))\cap\FF LG=K(t^u).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The subgroup $\Z u$ is cofinal. Its bounded well-ordered subsets are finite,
so $\BB K{\Z u}=K[t^u,t^{-u}]$ and its fraction field is $K(t^u)$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[An algebraic germ does not become a bounded fraction]
Take $s=t^u$ and characteristic zero. The binomial series
\[
 z=(1+s)^{1/2}=1+\tfrac12s-\tfrac18s^2+\tfrac1{16}s^3-\cdots
 \in\Q[[s]]
\]
has degree two over $\FF LG$ for every characteristic-zero extension
$\Q\subseteq L$ and every $G$ in which $u$ is an order unit.
To check its degree over $\Q(s)$, observe that $1+s$ is not a rational
square: in a coprime quotient of polynomials, the multiplicity of the
factor $s+1$ would be even on a square and odd on $1+s$. Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:relations}
then preserves degree two. Thus it is algebraic but not itself in the
bounded-support fraction field. In contrast, $(1-s)^{-1}$ is in that
fraction field, although its support is unbounded.
\end{example}

(Added in batch 32.) In the notation of Section~\ref{bst:gr:sec:radicals},
$z=r_{-1,2}$. Proposition~\ref{bst:gr:prop:single} and
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:multirad} extend the example to all roots
$r_{a,n}$ of $1-as$ and to their joint degrees, and
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:closedness} uses the case $a=1$, $n=2$ to show that
the closedness of Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:closedbase} fails whenever $G$ has an
order unit.

\subsection{Why noncofinal enlargement behaves oppositely}

If $H$ is not cofinal, the entire group $H$ is bounded above in $G$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:collapse}
 \HH KH\subseteq\BB LG\subseteq\FF LG.
\end{equation}
Every old Hahn series becomes a bounded-support coefficient in the enlarged
workspace. Once Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent} has established
$\FF KH\ne\HH KH$ in characteristic zero, this proves the converse in
Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descentintro}: choose $z$ in that difference. The pair
$1,z$ is independent over the smaller base but dependent over the larger
one. Explicitly, $1\otimes z-z\otimes1$ is a nonzero tensor sent to zero
by multiplication. To see that it is nonzero, extend $1,z$ to a basis of
$\HH KH$ over $\FF KH$ and use the linear functional taking $1$ to zero
and $z$ to one; applying it to the second tensor factor gives $1$.
The exceptional subgroup $H=0$ is excluded because its Hahn field and
fraction field are both just $K$.

This dichotomy is about the actual embedding of exponent groups. Merely
saying that both groups are divisible or both have countable cofinality does
not imply that the embedding is cofinal.
(Added in batch 32.) Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:transition} states the Galois
form of this dichotomy for $K=L=\C$ and divisible $H$: a cofinal inclusion
keeps every finite extension of $\FF{\C}H$ a field of the same degree, and a
noncofinal one splits every finite extension completely.

\section{The support-separation mechanism}\label{bst:sec:gaps}

This section supplies the estimate that upgrades the earlier nonrationality
obstruction to algebraic independence. It is entirely algebraic. Even when
a truncation has infinitely many terms, its support has the bound used in
the estimate.

\subsection{Separated exponent systems}

\begin{definition}\label{bst:def:skeleton}
Let $\kappa$ be an infinite cardinal. A \emph{separated exponent system} in
$G$ consists of positive elements $a_\alpha,b_\alpha$ for $\alpha<\kappa$
such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $a_\alpha$ is strictly increasing and cofinal, and
$a_\beta\le b_\alpha$ whenever $\beta<\alpha$;
\item for every positive integer $d$ and every $w\in G$, eventually in
$\alpha$ one has
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:gaps}
 a_\alpha>d b_\alpha+w,
 \qquad a_{\alpha+1}>d a_\alpha+w.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Every infinite cardinal is a limit ordinal, so $\alpha+1<\kappa$ for all
$\alpha<\kappa$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Existence at the exact cofinality]\label{bst:lem:skeleton}
Every nonzero ordered abelian group admits a separated exponent system of
length $\kappa=\cof(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a strictly increasing positive cofinal sequence $g_\alpha$ from
Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:cofinality}.

First suppose $\kappa=\aleph_0$, and index by $n\ge0$. Choose $b_n>0$
strictly exceeding $g_n$ and, when $n>0$, $a_{n-1}$. Set
$a_n=(n+2)b_n$. These elements are increasing and cofinal, and earlier
$a_j$ are at most $b_n$. Given $d,w$, eventually $b_n>w$ and $n+2\ge d+1$,
so $a_n-d b_n\ge b_n>w$. At the next index the same estimate gives
$a_{n+1}>d b_{n+1}+w\ge d a_n+w$. Both inequalities hold eventually.

Now suppose $\kappa$ is uncountable. At stage $\alpha$, the earlier
$a_\beta$, together with $g_\alpha$, form a set of size less than $\kappa$.
Choose a positive strict upper bound $b_\alpha$ for them. The countable set
$\{n b_\alpha:n\ge1\}$ also has size less than $\kappa$, so choose
$a_\alpha$ strictly above every one of its elements. This gives a strictly
increasing cofinal sequence. For fixed $d,w$, eventually $b_\alpha>w$;
then
$a_\alpha>(d+1)b_\alpha>d b_\alpha+w$.
The estimate at $\alpha+1$, and $b_{\alpha+1}>a_\alpha$, give the second
inequality of \eqref{bst:eq:gaps}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Factorial spacing]
If $u$ is an order unit, the concrete choices
\[
 a_n=n!u,\qquad b_n=(n-1)!u\qquad(n\ge2)
\]
form a separated exponent system. For fixed $d$,
$a_n-d b_n=(n-d)(n-1)!u$ eventually exceeds every $w\in G$.
The next-step inequality is checked in the same way.
\end{example}

\subsection{A polynomial cannot bridge two separated support bands}

Fix a separated exponent system $(a_\alpha,b_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa}$.
Let
\[
 P(X)=\sum_{\nu}p_\nu X^\nu\in\BB KG[X_1,\ldots,X_m]
\]
be nonzero of total degree $d\ge1$. Its top homogeneous part is denoted
$P_d$. Choose $\ell,u\in G$ such that every $p_\nu$ is supported in
$[\ell,u]$. For coefficient vectors
$c(\alpha)=(c_1(\alpha),\ldots,c_m(\alpha))\in K^m$, put
\[
 Y_j=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}c_j(\alpha)t^{a_\alpha}.
\]
Zero entries cause no problem: each support is a subset of the well-ordered
set of $a_\alpha$.

\begin{lemma}[Support-band detection]\label{bst:lem:detection}
Suppose an index $\alpha$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:twogaps}
 a_\alpha>d b_\alpha+(u-\ell),\qquad
 a_{\alpha+1}>d a_\alpha+(u-\ell),
\end{equation}
and $P_d(c(\alpha))\ne0$ in $\HH KG$. Then $P(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m)\ne0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define the earlier part and the truncation through $\alpha$ by
\[
 A_j=\sum_{\beta<\alpha}c_j(\beta)t^{a_\beta},\qquad
 T_j=A_j+c_j(\alpha)t^{a_\alpha}.
\]
Every $A_j$ is supported in $[0,b_\alpha]$ and every $T_j$ in
$[0,a_\alpha]$. Expanding in an auxiliary \emph{polynomial} variable $Z$,
write
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:auxpoly}
 P(A_1+c_1(\alpha)Z,\ldots,A_m+c_m(\alpha)Z)
   =\sum_{k=0}^d Q_k Z^k.
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $Z^d$ is exactly $P_d(c(\alpha))$, so $Q_d\ne0$.
In a monomial of degree $|\nu|$, a contribution to $Q_k$ uses $k$
copies of $Z$ and $|\nu|-k$ factors from the $A_j$. Reaching degree
$d$ in $Z$ therefore uses only top-degree monomials and no $A_j$ factors.
Every coefficient $Q_k$ is a finite sum of products, each consisting of
a $p_\nu$, a scalar, and at most $d$ factors chosen from the $A_j$. Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:bounds} therefore gives
\[
 \supp(Q_k)\subseteq[\ell,u+d b_\alpha]\qquad(0\le k\le d).
\]
After substituting $Z=t^{a_\alpha}$, these supports lie in the bands
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:bands}
 [\ell+k a_\alpha,\ u+d b_\alpha+k a_\alpha].
\end{equation}
The first inequality in \eqref{bst:eq:twogaps} says that successive bands are
strictly disjoint. Since $Q_d\ne0$, choose $g\in\supp Q_d$. The nonzero coefficient
at $g+d a_\alpha$ lies in the last band and receives no contribution
from any earlier band. Hence $P(T_1,\ldots,T_m)\ne0$.

A direct bound using the original polynomial and the supports of $T_j$
shows that
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:truncbound}
 \supp(P(T))\subseteq[\ell,u+d a_\alpha].
\end{equation}
Meanwhile every tail $Y_j-T_j$ is either zero or has valuation at least
$a_{\alpha+1}$. Expand each difference $Y^\nu-T^\nu$ as a sum of products
containing at least one such tail. All other series factors have
nonnegative valuation. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:tailbound}
 v(P(Y)-P(T))\ge\ell+a_{\alpha+1}.
\end{equation}
By the second inequality in \eqref{bst:eq:twogaps}, this lower bound is strictly
above the upper bound in \eqref{bst:eq:truncbound}. The nonzero bounded-support
series $P(T)$ cannot be canceled by the tail. Hence $P(Y)\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the proof does not assume]
Neither $P(T)$ nor the earlier parts $A_j$ need have finite support.
No supremum of their supports in $G$ is required; any displayed upper bound
suffices. There is no rearrangement of a non-summable family and no appeal
to a numerical limit. All polynomial expansions are finite, and each
coefficientwise product is a Hahn convolution.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[A one-series version]\label{bst:cor:onegap}
Let $(a_n)$ be positive and strictly increasing, and suppose that for every
$d\ge1$ and $w\in G$, eventually $a_{n+1}>d a_n+w$. If every $c_n\in K$
is nonzero, then $Y=\sum_n c_nt^{a_n}$ is transcendental over $\FF KG$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a putative relation clear denominators to obtain a nonzero polynomial
$P\in\BB KG[X]$. The case of constant $P$ is impossible. The same tail
estimate as \eqref{bst:eq:tailbound} implies $P(T_n)=0$ for every sufficiently
large $n$, since otherwise its bounded support could not be canceled.
Here $T_n$ is the finite truncation through $n$, supported at most at $a_n$.
These truncations are pairwise distinct: the difference of two of them
has the nonzero coefficient $c_j$ at the first newly included exponent.
A nonzero polynomial over the field $\HH KG$ has only finitely many roots. This contradiction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

The following comparison was added in batch 31. The independent-copies report
\cite{RepoIsc} prints the same tail estimate and root count at every cofinality
(its \texttt{isc:thm:cofinalgap}) and claims no priority for it. Let $K$ be an arbitrary field, finite fields
included, let $G\ne0$ be set-sized, and let $(a_\alpha)_{\alpha<\cof(G)}$ be a
strictly increasing cofinal family in $G^{>0}$ such that, for every $d\ge1$
and $w\in G$, eventually $a_{\alpha+1}>d a_\alpha+w$. Such a family exists
(its \texttt{isc:lem:gapscale}). Then $1+\sum_{\alpha<\cof(G)}t^{a_\alpha}$,
with all coefficients $1$, is transcendental over $\FF KG$.
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onegap} also works over any field and allows arbitrary
nonzero coefficients, but it is indexed by $\N$ and its hypothesis makes $(a_n)$
cofinal, so it needs $\cof(G)=\aleph_0$. Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent} covers
every cofinality but needs characteristic zero. The single-series statement at
uncountable cofinality over a field of positive characteristic, in particular a
finite field, is therefore not stated in this report. It does not touch the
multivariable problem, and finite coefficient fields remain outside the
independence theorem (Section~\ref{bst:sec:verification}).

The multivariable argument needs more than this root-count observation.
One must ensure that the top homogeneous polynomial does not vanish on all
chosen coefficient vectors. The next section supplies exactly that
certificate, without any algebraic independence assumption on the
coefficients themselves.

\section{Independent coefficient codes and the sharp theorem}\label{bst:sec:independence}

\subsection{A finite-pattern coding lemma}

The following elementary construction is an independent-family coding
argument. Its role is to realize any prescribed finite tuple of positive
integers at cofinally many positions. It is not being claimed as a new
set-theoretic principle.

\begin{lemma}[Cofinal finite-pattern realization]\label{bst:lem:coding}
For any infinite cardinal $\kappa$, there are functions
\[
 c_A:\kappa\longrightarrow\N_{>0}\qquad(A\subseteq\kappa)
\]
such that for every finite list of distinct subsets $A_1,\ldots,A_m$ and
every $(q_1,\ldots,q_m)\in\N_{>0}^m$, the set
\[
 \{\alpha<\kappa:c_{A_j}(\alpha)=q_j\text{ for all }j\}
\]
has cardinality $\kappa$, and in particular is cofinal in $\kappa$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $D_\kappa$ consist of triples $(s,h,\rho)$ where $s$ is a finite subset
of $\kappa$, $h:\mathcal P(s)\to\N_{>0}$ is a table, and $\rho<\kappa$.
There are $\kappa$ finite subsets of $\kappa$, countably many tables for
each finite $s$, and $\kappa$ choices for $\rho$. Hence
$|D_\kappa|=\kappa$. Fix a bijection
\[
 \alpha\longmapsto(s_\alpha,h_\alpha,\rho_\alpha)
 \quad\text{from }\kappa\text{ to }D_\kappa
\]
and set
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:code}
 c_A(\alpha)=h_\alpha(A\cap s_\alpha).
\end{equation}
For each pair $A_j\ne A_k$, choose a point in their symmetric difference.
The finite set $s$ of these points makes $A_j\cap s$ pairwise distinct.
A table $h$ can therefore take the prescribed value $q_j$ at each of them;
set its remaining values to $1$. Every triple $(s,h,\rho)$ realizes the
target tuple, independently of $\rho$. There are $\kappa$ such triples,
which proves the cardinality assertion. A bounded subset of the initial
ordinal $\kappa$ has cardinality smaller than $\kappa$, so the realizing
indices are cofinal. Regularity of $\kappa$ is not needed for this
coding lemma; cardinality $\kappa$ already rules out a bounded subset
of its initial ordinal. For a single prescribed set, the separating set
$s$ may be empty. Distinct sets give distinct coefficient streams: prescribe
values $1$ and $2$ at one common realizing index.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Integer grids detect polynomials]\label{bst:lem:grid}
Let $E$ be a field of characteristic zero. A nonzero polynomial
$Q\in E[X_1,\ldots,X_m]$ is nonzero at some point of $\N_{>0}^m$.
More precisely, if each variable has degree at most $d$, any product of
$m$ sets of $d+1$ distinct elements of $E$ contains a nonzero value.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For no variables, the assertion concerns a nonzero constant and is immediate.
For one variable, a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $d$ has at most
$d$ roots. For the induction step, regard $Q$ as a polynomial in its last
variable. If it vanishes at every point of the product grid, then for each
fixed tuple of the first $m-1$ variables all its coefficients vanish, by
the one-variable statement. The induction hypothesis applied to those
coefficient polynomials makes each identically zero, a contradiction.
The positive integers are distinct in a characteristic-zero field.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Independence over all bounded-support fractions}

\begin{theorem}[The sharp support theorem]\label{bst:thm:independent}
Let $K$ have characteristic zero and let $0\ne G$ be an ordered abelian
group. Put $\kappa=\cof(G)$. Choose a separated exponent system as in
Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:skeleton} and a coefficient code as in Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:coding}.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:mainfamily}
 Y_A=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}c_A(\alpha)t^{a_\alpha}
 \qquad(A\subseteq\kappa)
\end{equation}
are algebraically independent over $\FF KG$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:lowertrdeg}
 \trdeg_{\FF KG}\HH KG\ \ge\ 2^\kappa.
\end{equation}
Every member has positive integer coefficients and exactly the common
support $\{a_\alpha:\alpha<\kappa\}$, of order type $\kappa$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First, each $Y_A$ is a valid Hahn series: the strictly increasing map
$\alpha\mapsto a_\alpha$ identifies its image with the well-ordered
initial ordinal $\kappa$, and characteristic zero makes every prescribed
positive integer coefficient nonzero. Thus its support is exactly that image.
Fix distinct $A_1,\ldots,A_m$ and a nonzero polynomial over $\FF KG$.
Clear a common nonzero denominator to obtain a nonzero polynomial
$P\in\BB KG[X_1,\ldots,X_m]$. If its total degree is zero, its evaluation
is already nonzero. Otherwise let its degree be $d\ge1$ and choose common
coefficient support bounds $[\ell,u]$.

Its top homogeneous part $P_d$ is a nonzero polynomial over the field
$\HH KG$. By Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:grid}, there is a tuple of positive integers
$q=(q_1,\ldots,q_m)$ such that $P_d(q)\ne0$. Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:coding}
realizes this exact tuple at cofinally many indices $\alpha$. The gap
inequalities \eqref{bst:eq:twogaps} hold at every sufficiently large index.
Choose a realizing index after that threshold. Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:detection}
then gives
\[
 P(Y_{A_1},\ldots,Y_{A_m})\ne0.
\]
This handles every finite subfamily and every nonzero polynomial, which is
algebraic independence. There are $2^\kappa$ subsets $A$, proving the lower
bound. Finally the coefficients are positive integers, hence nonzero in
$K$, so none of the designated exponents disappears.
\end{proof}

For the remaining corollaries in this section, retain the hypotheses
$\operatorname{char}K=0$, $G\ne0$ and $\kappa=\cof(G)$, and the family
$Y_A$ of Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent}.

\begin{corollary}[Optimal cardinality and order type]\label{bst:cor:optimal}
Among all $z\in\HH KG$ transcendental over $\FF KG$, the least support
cardinality is $\cof(G)$ and the least support order type is the initial
ordinal $\cof(G)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A support of cardinality less than $\kappa=\cof(G)$ is bounded above by
definition of cofinality, so its series already belongs to $\BB KG$.
Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent} supplies a transcendental series with support
of cardinality and order type exactly $\kappa$. An ordinal smaller than
the initial ordinal $\kappa$ has smaller cardinality, proving the order-type
assertion as well.
\end{proof}

Thus countable supports suffice exactly when the value group has countable
cofinality. When the cofinality is uncountable, every countable-support
series is already a coefficient in the base, regardless of how sparse its
support looks inside a smaller subgroup.

\begin{corollary}[Arbitrary bounded perturbations]\label{bst:cor:perturb}
For any choices $b_A\in\FF KG$ and $u_A\in\FF KG^\times$, the family
$(u_AY_A+b_A)_{A\subseteq\kappa}$ is algebraically independent over
$\FF KG$. In particular arbitrary bounded-support changes, or deletion
of an arbitrary bounded initial part of each series, preserve independence.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On each finite subfamily the substitutions
$X_A\mapsto u_AX_A+b_A$ define an invertible polynomial change of variables
over the base field. They therefore send nonzero polynomials to nonzero
polynomials. A deleted bounded initial part belongs to $\BB KG$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Independent families in every valuation ball]\label{bst:cor:balls}
Every nonempty ball
\[
 U(f,\delta)=\{z\in\HH KG:v(z-f)>\delta\}
\]
contains $2^\kappa$ elements algebraically independent over $\FF KG$.
It also contains an element of $\BB KG$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $b=f_{\le\delta}\in\BB KG$. Choose $\beta<\kappa$ with
$a_\beta>\delta$, and define
\[
 Z_A=b+\sum_{\alpha\ge\beta}c_A(\alpha)t^{a_\alpha}.
\]
Then $v(Z_A-b)>\delta$ and $v(b-f)>\delta$, with the usual convention for
zero, so every $Z_A$ lies in the ball. Each $Z_A$ differs from $Y_A$ by a
bounded-support series: the omitted initial part is bounded above by
$a_\beta$, and $b$ is bounded. Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:perturb} proves their
independence. The element $b$ itself belongs to the same ball.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A precise limitation of bounded truncation data]
No single bounded truncation can determine algebraicity over $\FF KG$.
The same truncation is shared by a base-field element and by every member
of a family as in Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:balls}. This is an information-theoretic
statement about those data, not a claim that every representation of Hahn
series has an undecidable algebraicity problem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exact transcendence degrees}

\begin{corollary}[Cardinality-matched groups]\label{bst:cor:exactcard}
Suppose $\kappa=\cof(G)=|G|$ and $\abs K\le2^\kappa$, with $K$ of
characteristic zero. Then
\[
 \trdeg_{\FF KG}\HH KG=2^\kappa.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound is Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent}. A Hahn series is a function
from $G$ to $K$, so
\[
 |\HH KG|\le |K|^{|G|}\le(2^\kappa)^\kappa=2^\kappa.
\]
The cardinality of a field bounds its transcendence degree, proving the
upper bound independently of the construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Every nonzero real-exponent subgroup]\label{bst:cor:realgroup}
Let $0\ne G\subseteq\R$ and let $K$ have characteristic zero and
$|K|\le\cc=2^{\aleph_0}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:realtrdeg}
 \trdeg_{\FF KG}\HH KG=\cc.
\end{equation}
In particular this holds for $K=\Q,\R,\C$ and $G=\Z,\Q,\R$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Here $\cof(G)=\aleph_0$, so the lower bound is $\cc$. Every well-ordered
subset $S\subseteq\R$ is countable. Indeed, for each nonmaximal $s\in S$
there is a successor $s^+$ in $S$; choose a rational in $(s,s^+)$. These
intervals are disjoint, producing an injection of $S$ minus a possible
maximum into $\Q$. There are at most $\cc^{\aleph_0}=\cc$ countable
supports in $G$, and at most $|K|^{\aleph_0}\le\cc$ coefficient assignments
to any one support. Thus $|\HH KG|\le\cc$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the full strength is not a counting argument]
For $G=\R$ and $K=\R$ or $\C$, the base $\FF KG$ already has cardinality
$\cc$, since it contains $K$ and lies in a field of cardinality $\cc$.
Equality of these cardinalities gives no lower bound at all on relative
transcendence degree. The separation and coding proof is what supplies it.
Nor is an independent family of maximum possible cardinality automatically
a transcendence basis: maximal cardinality need not mean maximality under
inclusion.
\end{remark}

\section{Explicit countable witnesses and examples}\label{bst:sec:examples}

The coefficient code gives a family of optimal cardinality with integer
coefficients, but its indexing uses a set-theoretic bijection. It is useful
to have additional closed-form witnesses.

\subsection{Factorial gaps with exponential coefficients}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit multiplicative-parameter family]\label{bst:thm:explicit}
Let $u$ be an order unit of $G$ and let $L$ contain $\R$. If
$q_1,\ldots,q_m>0$ are multiplicatively independent real numbers, then
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:qfamily}
 Z_{q_j}=\sum_{n\ge2}q_j^n t^{n!u}\qquad(1\le j\le m)
\end{equation}
are algebraically independent over $\FF LG$. Consequently the series
$Z_p$, indexed by the ordinary prime numbers $p$, are jointly algebraically
independent, with positive integer coefficients.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Multiplicative independence means that
$\prod_jq_j^{m_j}=1$ with $m_j\in\Z$ forces every $m_j=0$.
Let $P\in\BB LG[X]$ be a nonzero polynomial of positive total degree $d$.
At the vector $c(n)=(q_1^n,\ldots,q_m^n)$ its top part is
\[
 P_d(c(n))=\sum_{|\nu|=d}p_\nu Q_\nu^n,
 \qquad Q_\nu=\prod_jq_j^{\nu_j}.
\]
The $Q_\nu$ are distinct nonzero elements of $L$. Enumerate the $r\ge1$
nonzero top coefficients as $p_1,\ldots,p_r$ and their associated
values as $Q_1,\ldots,Q_r$. For any integer $N\ge2$, the matrix
$(Q_i^{N+k})_{0\le k<r,\,1\le i\le r}$ has determinant
\[
 \left(\prod_{i=1}^r Q_i^N\right)
 \prod_{1\le i<j\le r}(Q_j-Q_i)\ne0.
\]
It is therefore invertible over $L$, and also over $\HH LG$.
Consequently the $r$ values $P_d(c(N+k))$, $0\le k<r$, cannot all
vanish, since that would force every $p_i=0$. The factorial gaps satisfy
\eqref{bst:eq:twogaps} for all sufficiently large $n$. Choose $N$ beyond
that threshold and one nonzero evaluation in the displayed block.
Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:detection} proves that $P$ does not vanish on the series.
This finite Vandermonde argument treats the Hahn coefficients directly;
it requires no limiting argument.
Clear denominators for a polynomial over the fraction field. Finally,
unique factorization of ordinary integers makes any finite set of distinct
primes multiplicatively independent.
\end{proof}

An alternative proof uses Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onescale} to reduce to
ordinary Laurent series over $\R(t^u)$. The direct support proof also records the exact obstruction to
cancellation over bounded-support Hahn coefficients.

\subsection{A continuum family with only integer coefficients}

For $\kappa=\aleph_0$ one can choose an effective enumeration of all triples
\[
 (s,h,\rho),\qquad s\subseteq\N\text{ finite},\quad
 h:\mathcal P(s)\to\N_{>0},\quad\rho\in\N.
\]
Thus for each binary sequence $A\subseteq\N$, formula \eqref{bst:eq:code}
gives an integer coefficient stream. Relabeling position $n-2$ by $n\ge2$ (and writing $c_A(n)$ for the
relabeled coefficient),
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:integerfactorial}
 W_A=\sum_{n\ge2}c_A(n)t^{n!u}
\end{equation}
is a continuum-sized independent family whenever $u$ is an order unit.
Each individual stream is computable relative to the characteristic
function of $A$: evaluating a coefficient asks only finitely many membership
questions. The family is not asserted to consist of computable streams;
there are only countably many computable descriptions.

More strikingly, for $u=1$ these witnesses lie in $\Q[[t]]$, yet they remain
independent over $\FF{\C}{\R}$. All ordinary complex constants and all
bounded real-exponent Hahn series have already been admitted in that base.
Cofinal descent explains why this does not erase ordinary formal-series
transcendence.

\section{Rank structure and the unit group}\label{bst:sec:rank}

This section concerns the coefficient ring itself. The field criterion is
proved from the Hahn field construction. The unit classification additionally
uses the known rank-one upper-support theorem, which is isolated explicitly.

\subsection{An exact field criterion}

For a positive element $a\in G$, define its principal convex subgroup
\[
 C(a)=\{g\in G:\abs g\le na\text{ for some }n\ge1\}.
\]
It is proper precisely when $a$ is not an order unit.

\begin{theorem}[Absence of a highest scale]\label{bst:thm:fieldcriterion}
For any field $K$ and nonzero ordered abelian group $G$, the following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ has no order unit;
\item $\displaystyle\BB KG=\bigcup_{C\subsetneq G\text{ convex}}\HH KC$;
\item $\BB KG$ is a field.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume there is no order unit. If $f$ has support in $[\ell,u]$, choose
$a>0$ with $a\ge\abs\ell,\abs u$. Then its support lies in the proper
convex subgroup $C(a)$, so $f\in\HH K{C(a)}$. Conversely, a proper convex
subgroup $C$ is bounded above in $G$: choose a positive $g\notin C$.
Every positive $c\in C$ is smaller than $g$, since otherwise convexity
would put $g$ in $C$. Hence every series in $\HH KC$ has bounded support
in $G$.

Convex subgroups of an ordered abelian group are linearly ordered by
inclusion. To see this, if neither of $C,D$ contained the other, choose
positive $c\in C\setminus D$ and $d\in D\setminus C$; whichever is smaller
belongs to the other's convex subgroup, a contradiction. Thus the union
in (2) is a directed union of fields and is a field.

Finally suppose $u>0$ is an order unit. The bounded-support element
$1-t^u$ has inverse in the Hahn field
\[
 (1-t^u)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}t^{nu}.
\]
This is the usual strongly summable geometric identity. Its support is
unbounded because $u$ is an order unit. The inverse is unique in the Hahn
field, so no inverse can belong to $\BB KG$. Thus (3) excludes an order
unit and implies (1).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Closed coefficient fields at unbounded rank]\label{bst:cor:closedbase}
Suppose $G$ is divisible and has no order unit. If $K$ is real closed, then
$\BB KG$ is real closed. If $K$ is algebraically closed of characteristic
zero, then $\BB KG$ is algebraically closed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A convex subgroup of a divisible ordered abelian group is divisible: the
unique $n$th part of an element of the subgroup has no larger absolute
value, so belongs to the subgroup by convexity. The standard Hahn-field
closedness criterion makes each $\HH KC$ in Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:fieldcriterion}
real closed or algebraically closed, respectively; this is the imported
criterion recorded in \cite[Fact~2.1.1]{LM24}.

A polynomial has only finitely many coefficients, so all of them lie in
one member of the directed union. In the algebraically closed case it has
a root in that member. In the real closed case the same argument supplies
roots of odd-degree polynomials and square roots of positive elements,
which characterize real closedness. The order throughout is the inherited
Hahn order.
\end{proof}

These fields can be proper and dense in the full Hahn field. There is no
conflict with algebraic closedness: the remaining extension is
transcendental. Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent} measures how large it is.
(Added in batch 32.) For divisible $G$ and $K=\R,\C$ the hypothesis ``no
order unit'' is also necessary: Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:closedness} shows that
with an order unit $u$ the element $1-t^u$ has no square root in $\FF{\C}G$.

\subsection{Reduction to the highest Archimedean quotient}

Suppose $G$ has an order unit $u$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:topconvex}
 C=\{g\in G:n\abs g<u\text{ for every positive integer }n\}.
\end{equation}
This is a convex subgroup. It contains zero and is closed under negation.
If $g,h\in C$, applying the defining inequalities at $2n$ gives
$2n(|g|+|h|)<2u$, hence $n|g+h|<u$ for every $n\ge1$.
If $|x|\le|g|$ with $g\in C$, the same inequalities put $x$ in $C$.
It is proper because $u\notin C$.

For $g\notin C$, some $n$ satisfies $u\le n|g|$. Since $u$ is an
order unit, $|g|$ is one too. A convex subgroup containing such a $g$
contains every element bounded in absolute value by a multiple of $|g|$,
so it must be all of $G$. Thus $C$ is the unique maximal proper convex
subgroup. The quotient $G/C$ is nonzero Archimedean: otherwise a positive
class dominated by all multiples below another class would generate a
nonzero proper convex subgroup of the quotient, whose preimage strictly
contains $C$.

\begin{proposition}[Top-rank presentation]\label{bst:prop:toprank}
If $G$ is divisible and has an order unit, choose an ordered embedding of
$H=G/C$ in $\R$ and a $\Q$-linear section of the quotient map. With
$M=\HH KC$, that choice gives isomorphisms
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:iterated}
 \HH KG\cong\HH MH,
 \qquad \BB KG\cong\BB MH.
\end{equation}
The full-field isomorphism is the usual iterated Hahn presentation;
the second is its bounded-support restriction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $G$ and $C$ are $\Q$-vector spaces, choose a complementary subspace.
Order on the quotient dominates order within $C$, so this identifies $G$
with the lexicographic group $H\oplus C$. Regroup a series according to its
$H$ coordinate. Its projected support is well ordered: for any nonempty
subset of that projection, take the least original supported exponent
whose image lies in the subset; its image is least there. Each fiber,
a translated subset of the original support, is well ordered too.
Conversely, to find a least element in any nonempty subset of a
lexicographic support, first choose its least outer coordinate, then its
least inner coordinate in that fiber. Thus well-ordered outer support
and well-ordered fibers give exactly a Hahn series on $G$.

Addition agrees coefficientwise. For multiplication there are finitely
many outer pairs at any fixed outer exponent; within each such pair,
there are finitely many inner pairs at any fixed inner exponent.
These are exactly the original convolution terms. Thus the grouping is
a field isomorphism, including inverses by uniqueness. This standard presentation is also recorded in
\cite[Fact~2.4.2]{LM24}.

If the original support is bounded by $g$, its $H$ projection is bounded
by the image of $g$. Conversely, if its outer support has upper bound
$h\in H$, choose $h'>h$. A lift of $h'$ bounds every fiber, regardless of
its internal $C$ support. Thus boundedness is equivalent on the two sides.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Imported rank-one support theorem]\label{bst:prop:supimport}
If $M$ has characteristic zero and $H\subseteq\R$, then for nonzero
$f,g\in\BB MH$,
\[
 \sup_{\R}\supp(fg)=\sup_{\R}\supp(f)+\sup_{\R}\supp(g).
\]
\end{proposition}
This is the monomial-localized form of
\cite[Proposition~3.5.1]{LM24}. Choose $a,b\in H$ bounding the supports
of $f,g$. Then $t^{-a}f,t^{-b}g$ have nonpositive support. Apply the
imported one-sided theorem over $M$ to them and translate the real
suprema back by $a,b$; the translations cancel to give the displayed
identity. The one-sided theorem is not reproved or claimed as new here.
The supremum is taken in $\R$ and need not be an element of $H$.

\begin{corollary}[Units see exactly the highest rank]\label{bst:cor:units}
Suppose $K$ has characteristic zero and $G$ is divisible with an order
unit, and let $C$ be \eqref{bst:eq:topconvex}. A nonzero $f\in\BB KG$ is a
unit if and only if its support lies in one coset of $C$. Equivalently,
its image in \eqref{bst:eq:iterated} is a single outer monomial with an arbitrary
nonzero coefficient from $\HH KC$. In particular,
\[
 1+t^g\in\BB KG^\times\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g\in C.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a nonzero rank-one bounded series define
\[
 w(f)=\sup_{\R}\supp(f)-\min\supp(f)\ge0.
\]
Proposition~\ref{bst:prop:supimport} and the usual valuation identity give
$w(fg)=w(f)+w(g)$. If $fg=1$, the nonnegative widths satisfy $w(f)+w(g)=w(1)=0$,
so $w(f)=0$. Every exponent of $f$ then lies between its minimum and
that same real supremum, forcing singleton support.
Conversely, if $f=a t^h$ with $a\in M^\times$, its inverse
$a^{-1}t^{-h}$ also has singleton support and is bounded.
Transfer this statement through \eqref{bst:eq:iterated}. For the last claim,
if $g\ne0$ the support is $\{0,g\}$, which lies in one coset precisely
when $g\in C$. If $g=0$, the element is $2$, a nonzero scalar in
characteristic zero, and the same conclusion holds.
\end{proof}

No arbitrary-rank supremum formula has been assumed. In particular the
rank-one width proof should not be repeated in $G$ itself using a guessed
Dedekind completion: at higher rank such a formula requires additional
structure and need not have the same meaning.

\section{Actual surreal and surcomplex realizations}\label{bst:sec:surreal}

\subsection{The normal-form bridge and its scope}

Let $G$ be a set-sized ordered additive subgroup of $\No$. Conway normal
form supplies the embedding
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:conway}
 \iota_G:\HH{\R}G\longrightarrow\No,
 \qquad \sum_g r_gt^g\longmapsto\sum_g r_g\omega^{-g}.
\end{equation}
The reversed sign is essential: increasing Hahn exponents correspond to
decreasing Conway exponents. We use the standard normal-form isomorphism
and its field compatibility, as recorded in \cite[Section~2.3]{LM24} and
developed in \cite{Gonshor}. This is an imported foundational theorem,
not an assertion about convergence of real-indexed sums in the fine
surreal topology.

Applying \eqref{bst:eq:conway} to real and imaginary coefficients gives a field
embedding
\[
 \iota_G^{\C}:\HH{\C}G\longrightarrow\No[i].
\]
Indeed, coefficientwise real and imaginary parts have well-ordered
supports, their union is well ordered, and complex multiplication agrees
with multiplication in the quadratic extension. We henceforth identify
these Hahn fields with their concrete images, but continue to name $G$ in
every bounded-support base.

\subsection{Real-exponent surreal numbers}

Define the concrete subfields
\[
 \mathscr H_\R=\left\{\sum_{g\in S}r_g\omega^{-g}:
       S\subseteq\R\text{ well ordered},\ r_g\in\R\right\},
 \qquad \mathscr H_\C=\mathscr H_\R[i].
\]
Let $\mathscr B_\R$ and $\mathscr B_\C$ require $S$ to be bounded above
\emph{in $\R$}, and put $\mathscr F_\R=\Frac\mathscr B_\R$ and
$\mathscr F_\C=\Frac\mathscr B_\C$.

\begin{theorem}[A continuum of independent actual infinitesimals]\label{bst:thm:actualreal}
There is a family of positive infinitesimal surreal numbers
\[
 \eta_A=\sum_{n\ge2}c_A(n)\omega^{-n!}\qquad(A\subseteq\N),
\]
with positive integer coefficients, algebraically independent even over
$\mathscr F_\C$. Moreover
\[
 \trdeg_{\mathscr F_\R}\mathscr H_\R
   =\trdeg_{\mathscr F_\C}\mathscr H_\C=\cc.
\]
The separate explicitly given family
\[
 \eta_p=\sum_{n\ge2}p^n\omega^{-n!}\qquad(p\text{ an ordinary prime})
\]
is also algebraically independent over $\mathscr F_\C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the countable coefficient code and factorial separated system in the
complex-coefficient theorem. Its coefficients happen to be positive
integers, so the witnesses lie in the real subfield. Their leading term
is a positive real multiple of $\omega^{-2}$; the remaining terms have
strictly smaller Conway monomials. Thus they are positive infinitesimals.
The exact degrees follow from Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:realgroup}, and the prime
family from Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:explicit}, all transported by
\eqref{bst:eq:conway}.
\end{proof}

This is not an assertion of transcendence over all surreals or all
surcomplex numbers: the witnesses themselves belong to those proper-class
fields. It is a relative algebraic theorem over explicitly specified
set-sized subfields.

\subsection{Maximal families at every infinite regular cardinal}

Let $\kappa$ be an infinite regular cardinal, regarded as an initial ordinal,
and form the ordered additive subgroup of actual surreal numbers
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:Gkappa}
 G_\kappa=\bigoplus_{\alpha<\kappa}\Q\,\omega^\alpha.
\end{equation}
The direct sum means finite rational linear combinations. Comparison is
by the largest exponent with a nonzero coefficient. This group has
cardinality $\kappa$, is divisible, has cofinality $\kappa$, and has no
order unit. For the cofinality assertion, any family of fewer than $\kappa$
finite supports uses a bounded set of indices, by regularity; a larger
basis monomial dominates it. Conversely the $\omega^\alpha$ are cofinal.
The absence of an order unit follows by moving one index past the largest
index occurring in a proposed unit.

Let $\mathscr H_\kappa^{\R}$ and $\mathscr H_\kappa^{\C}$ be the real and
complex Hahn fields on this concrete exponent group, interpreted in
$\No$ and $\No[i]$. Let $\mathscr B_\kappa^{\R}$ and
$\mathscr B_\kappa^{\C}$ be their bounded-support rings.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Arbitrarily large, explicit-support surreal families]\label{bst:thm:kappa}
For every infinite regular cardinal $\kappa$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathscr B_\kappa^{\R}$ is a real closed field and
$\mathscr B_\kappa^{\C}$ is an algebraically closed field.
\item There are $2^\kappa$ positive infinitesimal surreal numbers
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:kappaxi}
 \eta_A=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}
     c_A(\alpha)\,\omega^{-\omega^{\alpha+1}}
     \qquad(A\subseteq\kappa),
\end{equation}
all with positive integer coefficients and exactly the displayed common
support, algebraically independent over $\mathscr B_\kappa^{\C}$.
\item Both exact relative transcendence degrees are $2^\kappa$:
\[
 \trdeg_{\mathscr B_\kappa^{\R}}\mathscr H_\kappa^{\R}
 =\trdeg_{\mathscr B_\kappa^{\C}}\mathscr H_\kappa^{\C}
 =2^\kappa.
\]
No element with a support of smaller cardinality is transcendental over
its corresponding bounded-support base.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:closedbase}. For the second, the
concrete choices
\[
 a_\alpha=\omega^{\alpha+1},\qquad b_\alpha=\omega^\alpha
 \quad(\alpha<\kappa)
\]
form a separated exponent system in $G_\kappa$. Earlier $a_\beta$ are at
most $b_\alpha$. For every finite $n$, $a_\alpha>n b_\alpha$, and the
$b_\alpha$ are cofinal. Thus the two inequalities in \eqref{bst:eq:gaps}
follow exactly as in the uncountable construction, and also hold when
$\kappa=\aleph_0$. Apply Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent} over $\C$ and then
the Conway embedding. Since the base ring is already a field, its fraction
field is itself. The leading term of each number is a positive integer
multiple of $\omega^{-\omega}$, proving positivity and infinitesimality.

Finally $|G_\kappa|=\cof(G_\kappa)=\kappa$, while
$|\R|=|\C|=\cc\le2^\kappa$. Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:exactcard} supplies the
upper bounds as well as the equality. Optimality is
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:optimal}.
\end{proof}

No continuum hypothesis or generalized continuum hypothesis is used. The
regularity assumption on $\kappa$ in this concrete example is genuine:
for a singular initial ordinal the direct sum in \eqref{bst:eq:Gkappa} has
cofinality $\cof(\kappa)$, not $\kappa$. The general theorem still applies
to that group with its actual cofinality, but it does not give the displayed
$2^\kappa$ equality by the same argument.

\subsection{Enlarging a workspace can erase the base distinction}

Consider
\[
 G=\R\omega+\R\subset\No,\qquad H=\R\subset G.
\]
Every real number is smaller than $\omega$ in this ordered group, so $H$
is not cofinal. All real-exponent Hahn series therefore have support
bounded above in $G$. In particular every witness of
Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:actualreal} belongs to $\BB{\C}G$, even though it was
transcendental over $\mathscr F_\C$ in the smaller workspace. This is the
concrete collapse in \eqref{bst:eq:collapse}, not a failure of field embedding.

At the full class level every set of surreal exponents is bounded above by
a surreal number. Consequently the naive class-wide version of
``bounded support in $\No$'' imposes no restriction on a Conway normal form.
All the nontrivial statements in this article are deliberately
workspace-relative. A set-sized cofinal support in $G_\kappa$ is not a
cofinal subclass of all of $\No$.
(Added in batch 32.) Section~\ref{bst:gr:sec:rankjump} computes the Galois
consequence of such an enlargement: one dominating exponent absorbs the
whole old Hahn field, hence an algebraic closure of the old complex
fraction field, and Section~\ref{bst:gr:sec:surreal} repeats this along an
explicit countable tower in $\No[i]$.

The valuation topology used for density is likewise the topology of the
named Hahn workspace. For a constructed family member, the bounded initial
Hahn truncations
\[
 Y_{A,<\beta}=\sum_{\alpha<\beta}c_A(\alpha)t^{a_\alpha}
 \quad(\beta<\kappa)
\]
form a convergent net there: each belongs to $\BB KG$, and
$v(Y_A-Y_{A,<\beta})=a_\beta$ is cofinal in $G$.
For $\kappa=\aleph_0$ these are finite partial sums. For uncountable
$\kappa$ they are generally infinite bounded-support series.
Indeed the net of all finite subsums does not converge to $Y_A$ then:
every finite subset omits some index $n<\omega$, leaving a nonzero
coefficient below $a_\omega$, so no such subsum belongs to
$\{z:v(z-Y_A)>a_\omega\}$. It cannot converge to any other Hahn series
either, since convergence would force every coefficient to equal that of
$Y_A$.

Even the convergent initial-truncation net does not converge in the full
surreal fine topology. Choose a surreal exponent $\theta$ above the
set-sized group $G$. Every nonzero truncation error has a leading Hahn
exponent in $G$ and magnitude greater than $\omega^{-\theta}$, so the
fine neighborhood of that radius is never reached. This retains the
workspace distinction in the repository's foundations \cite{RepoCatalogue}
and its notation guide, under ``Topologies and strong summation''
\cite{RepoNotation}.

and its statement comparisons were revisited at \texttt{b4275b5}.

\subsection{Tail-spans and differential transcendence}\label{bst:subsec:tail}

The tail-span report \cite{RepoTail} is another source of algebraic
independence in Hahn fields in the collection. The two reports answer different
base-field problems.

\emph{Base fields.} Its base is a \emph{full} Hahn field $M((t^\Gamma))$
over a smaller characteristic-zero coefficient field $M$, and its transcendence comes from the
coefficients: by its coefficient-field criterion
(\texttt{tail:thm:coefficient}) a series with coefficients in an algebraic
closure of $M$ is algebraic over $M((t^\Gamma))$ exactly when its
coefficients generate a finite extension of $M$. Here the base
$\FF KG$ keeps the coefficient field of the witnesses (and in
Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent} may even enlarge it), and transcendence comes
from the support. The actual numbers make the difference concrete. With
$t=\omega^{-1}$, the witnesses $\eta_A=\sum_{n\ge2}c_A(n)t^{n!}$ and
$\eta_p$ of Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:actualreal} have integer coefficients and
integer exponents, so they are \emph{elements} of the tail-span base
$B_0=\Q((t^\Q))$. Conversely $\sqrt2$, or the bounded real-coefficient series
$\sum_{n\ge2}\sqrt{p_n}\,t^{1-1/n}$, lies in $\FF{\R}{\R}$ but not in
$B_0$. Neither base contains the other, and neither report's independence
theorem implies the other's. At uncountable cofinality the two criteria
separate further: if $\cof(G)>\aleph_0$ and $(\gamma_n)$ is a strictly
increasing sequence in $G$, the series $\sum_n\sqrt{p_n}\,t^{\gamma_n}$ has
countable support, so it lies in $\BB{\overline\Q\cap\R}{G}$ by
Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:cofinality}, while its coefficients generate an infinite
extension of $\Q$, so it is transcendental over $\Q((t^G))$ by
\texttt{tail:thm:coefficient}.

\emph{What overlaps.} Both reports prove a relative transcendence degree
$2^{\aleph_0}$ for set-sized Hahn fields with a countably cofinal exponent
group, with explicit positive infinitesimal surreal witnesses, and both
obtain the upper bound by the same separate cardinality count rather than
from the witnesses: the tail-span report for
$\trdeg_{\Q((t^\Q))}(\overline\Q\cap\R)((t^\Q))$ (\texttt{tail:thm:surreal}),
this report for $\trdeg_{\FF KG}\HH KG$ with $0\ne G\subseteq\R$ and
$\abs K\le\cc$ (Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:realgroup}, Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:actualreal}). Both
state that an independent family of maximal cardinality is not thereby a
transcendence basis, and both are relative to named set-sized workspaces.
Both show that enlarging the exponent group along a noncofinal embedding
changes the answer for the same surreal expansion: its
\texttt{tail:thm:closure} example embeds $\Q$ in lexicographic $\Q^2$ by
$q\mapsto(0,q)$, whose image is bounded above by $(1,0)$, and loses
approximability by algebraic elements; the example $\R\subset\R\omega+\R$ of
Section~\ref{bst:sec:surreal} makes every old series a bounded-support
coefficient and so erases transcendence over the bounded base. The
phenomena differ; the common cause is the noncofinal embedding. Finally,
both warn that finite or bounded prefix data do not decide algebraicity; the
tail-span report proves an undecidability result for computable coefficient
streams (\texttt{tail:prop:undecidable}), while this report proves no
undecidability statement (the remark after Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:balls}).

\emph{What differs.} The tail-span report proves differential independence
for the Berarducci--Mantova derivation and for surcomplex analytic
functions; this report contains no differential or analytic statement. It
classifies all relations among its sums through the cofinite span
(\texttt{tail:thm:tail}); this report does not classify relations, but its
descent theorem transports relation ideals between bases, which has no
counterpart there. Its displayed independent family uses almost-disjoint infinite index
sets. This is a sufficient construction, not a necessary condition for
independence. The unrestricted family $(\xi_A)_{A\subseteq\N_{>0}}$
satisfies the linear relation $\xi_{A\cup B}+\xi_{A\cap B}=\xi_A+\xi_B$
(\texttt{tail:rem:indexfamily}). The family $(\eta_A)$ here is indexed by
all subsets and is independent, because $c_A$ is the finite-pattern code of
Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:coding}, not an indicator function; this is why the
witnesses were renamed (Section~\ref{bst:subsec:provenance}). Its
displayed actual-surreal differential family uses $\Gamma=\Q$; its
coefficient-field and cofinite-span theorems allow arbitrary set-sized
ordered value groups. This report proves that $\cof(G)$ is the exact
support threshold over the bounded base.

\subsection{Entire functions at arbitrary rank}\label{bst:subsec:entire}

The entire-functions report \cite{RepoEntire} uses the same invariant,
$\cof(G)$, and the same order unit (Section~\ref{bst:subsec:terms}), for a
different object: the ring of strongly entire power series in one variable
over $\C((t^\Gamma))$, with $\Gamma$ divisible in its main theorems. The
parallels are these.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The same threshold $\aleph_0$.} There, nonpolynomial entire
series exist exactly when $\cof(\Gamma)=\aleph_0$ (\texttt{ent:cor:cofinality}).
Here, a transcendental element with countable support exists exactly when
$\cof(G)=\aleph_0$ (Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:optimal}). Both rest on the fact
that a countable subset of a group of uncountable cofinality is bounded above.
\item \emph{The order unit.} There, the order unit separates the second and
third cases of the cofinality--rank trichotomy (\texttt{ent:thm:main}) and
decides which restricted series are units (\texttt{ent:thm:units}). Here, it
decides whether $\BB KG$ is a field (Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:fieldcriterion});
the obstruction is the geometric series $(1-t^u)^{-1}=\sum_nt^{nu}$, whose
support is unbounded exactly because $u$ is an order unit.
\item \emph{Cofinal and noncofinal enlargement.} There, a nonpolynomial
entire series stays entire over a larger group exactly when the old group is
cofinal in it (\texttt{ent:thm:main-extension}(b)). Here, the descent map
\eqref{bst:eq:tensor} is injective exactly when the subgroup is cofinal
(Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descentintro}). These are different statements about
different objects, proved independently; this report assumes no
divisibility for its descent theorem.
\item \emph{GCD rings.} The entire ring there is proved to be a GCD domain.
Appendix~\ref{bst:sec:gcd} concerns the bounded-support Hahn ring, a different
ring, and remains conditional on \eqref{bst:hyp:gcd}; nothing in the
entire-functions report supplies that hypothesis.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hidden negative directions}\label{bst:subsec:hidden}

The report on hidden negative directions in surcomplex linear algebra
\cite{RepoHidden}, placed in the same commit as this one, proves a third
kind of independence. Its base $\mathscr P_\Gamma$ is the union of the
fields $\C((t^H))$ over finitely generated subgroups $H\subseteq\Gamma$ (the
Puiseux field when $\Gamma=\Q$). Its binary-prefix almost-disjoint
family of infinite prime sets gives tails
$h_A=\sum_{p\in A}t^{(1-1/p)\delta}$ independent over that field by
fresh prime denominators. This does not assert independence for all
infinite prime sets.

For $\Gamma=\Q$, the fields $\mathscr P_\Q$ and $\FF{\C}{\Q}$ are
incomparable. Each selected $h_A$ has support bounded above by $\delta$,
so it belongs to $\BB{\C}{\Q}\subseteq\FF{\C}{\Q}$, but it lies outside
$\mathscr P_\Q$. Conversely, the witnesses $W_A$ of
\eqref{bst:eq:integerfactorial} with $u=1$ belong to
$\C((t^\Z))\subseteq\mathscr P_\Q$ and are transcendental over
$\FF{\C}{\Q}$. For groups without an order unit the containment behaves
differently: every finitely generated subgroup is bounded above, so
$\mathscr P_\Gamma\subseteq\BB{\C}{\Gamma}$. Neither the ring nor its
fraction field should be confused with a full Hahn coefficient field.

\subsection{A feasible formalization decomposition}

No Lean proof is supplied or claimed. The results nevertheless break into

in Appendix~\ref{bst:sec:gcd} belongs to a separate dependency branch.

\subsection{Questions left by these theorems}

The results do not determine
$\trdeg_{\FF KG}\HH KG$ for every large ordered group: the lower bound
$2^{\cof(G)}$ can be smaller than the cardinality of the Hahn field, and no
general matching upper bound is asserted. For coefficient fields of finite
characteristic, the descent and field criterion still hold, and the
independent-family argument works with a countably infinite alphabet in an
infinite coefficient field. The positive-integer formulation is specific
to characteristic zero. Finite coefficient fields require a different
coding argument and are not covered by the independence theorem here.

A second question is whether stronger naturality can be imposed on the
$2^\kappa$-sized independent family. The existence proof chooses an
enumeration of finite tables. It is uniform once that enumeration is
fixed, but it is not invariant under every automorphism of the exponent
group. The explicit prime family removes that choice at the cost of being
only countable.

Finally, the GCD question below concerns divisibility inside the bounded
ring, while the main theorem concerns algebraic independence over its
fraction field. Neither assertion substitutes for the other. In
particular, proving a ring to be GCD would not make the huge transcendental
extension disappear.

\section{The Galois part: overview, provenance and notation}\label{bst:gr:sec:overview}

Sections~\ref{bst:gr:sec:overview}--\ref{bst:gr:sec:questions} were added
in batch 32. They develop the Galois theory of the bounded-support fraction
fields $\FF{\C}G$ and $\FF{\R}G$, using the descent theorem of
Section~\ref{bst:sec:descent} as their engine. Everything in them comes from
a second manuscript (source~02) unless it is marked \textup{[merge]}; a
\textup{[merge]} mark covers the sentence or paragraph it heads and records
text written when the manuscript was merged into this report.

An algebraically closed ambient field need not reveal the algebraic
complexity of its natural subfields. The surcomplex field $\No[i]$ is an
especially striking setting for this distinction. A prescribed exponent
group gives a full Hahn field, a canonical omnific subring, and the
fraction field of that subring. The first of these is algebraically closed
when the coefficients are complex and the exponent group is divisible.
The third can have Galois extensions with every finite group. The relevant
boundary is not merely the number of available exponents. It is whether a
single exponent controls their Archimedean growth, that is, whether $G$ has
an order unit (Section~\ref{bst:sec:setup}); equivalently, whether $G$ has a
largest Archimedean class. A group can have countable cofinality without
having an order unit.

\subsection{The principal theorems of the Galois part}\label{bst:gr:subsec:main}

\begin{theorem}[Galois rank dichotomy]\label{bst:gr:thm:main}
Let $G$ be a nonzero divisible ordered abelian group.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $G$ has no order unit, $\FF{\C}G$ is algebraically
closed and $\FF{\R}G$ is real closed.
\item If $G$ has an order unit, then for every family $(\Phi_j)_{j\in J}$
of finite groups with $\abs{J}\leq\cc$ there is a Galois extension
$E/\FF{\C}G$ inside $\HH{\C}G$ such that
\[
 \Gal(E/\FF{\C}G)\cong\prod_{j\in J}\Phi_j
\]
as topological groups. In particular, every finite group is realized.
\item In the second case neither $\FF{\C}G$ nor $\FF{\R}G$ has the
corresponding closedness property in \textup{(1)}. Indeed, for an order unit
$u$, $1-t^u$ has no square root in either fraction field, although its root
with constant term $1$ belongs to the ambient real Hahn field.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part (1) is prior material of this report (Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:closedbase}),
and the obstruction in (3) follows quickly from its descent theorem.
\textup{[merge]} The example after Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onescale} proves the
same obstruction for $1+t^u$ by the same argument.
The additional content developed here is the Galois realization in (2), its
simultaneous and explicit variants, and its behavior under rank

\begin{theorem}[Explicit independent radical towers]\label{bst:gr:thm:mainrad}
Suppose $u$ is an order unit of a nonzero ordered abelian group $G$,
and put $s=t^u$. For $a\in\C^\times$ and $n\geq1$, let
\[
 r_{a,n}=\sum_{m\geq0}\binom{1/n}{m}(-a)^m s^m.
\]
For distinct $a_1,\ldots,a_q$ and $n\geq2$,
\[
 \big[\FF{\C}G(r_{a_1,n},\ldots,r_{a_q,n}):\FF{\C}G\big]=n^q,
\]
and the Galois group is $(\Z/n\Z)^q$. The field generated by all
$r_{a,n}$ has Galois group $\prod_{a\in\C^\times}\Zh$.
Divisibility of $G$ is not required for these assertions.
\end{theorem}

\textup{[merge]} The number of radicals is written $q$ here, and in
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:multirad}, because $s$ is the local coordinate;
source~02 writes $s$. The theorem is proved in
Theorems~\ref{bst:gr:thm:multirad} and~\ref{bst:gr:thm:radicaltower}.

The second principal phenomenon goes in the opposite direction.
One new scale absorbs not just a selected root, but an algebraic closure
of the entire old complex fraction field.

\begin{theorem}[One-rank absorption and renewed obstruction]\label{bst:gr:thm:mainjump}
Let $H$ be nonzero and divisible, and form $G=\Q\theta\oplus H$ with the
lexicographic order in which $\theta$ dominates $H$. Then
\[
 \HH KH\subseteq\BB KG\subseteq\FF KG
 \qquad (K=\R,\C).
\]
The field $\FF{\C}G$ contains an algebraic closure of $\FF{\C}H$. For every
finite extension $\Lambda/\FF{\C}H$,
\[
 \Lambda\otimes_{\FF{\C}H}\FF{\C}G\cong\FF{\C}G^{\,[\Lambda:\FF{\C}H]}.
\]
Nevertheless, $\theta$ is an order unit of $G$, so $\FF{\C}G$ again has the
realization property of Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:main}\textup{(2)}. Its own
element $1-t^\theta$ has no square root in $\FF{\C}G$.
\end{theorem}

It is proved at the end of Section~\ref{bst:gr:sec:rankjump}. More generally,
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:transition} proves an exhaustive alternative:
cofinal exponent-group inclusions preserve every finite extension and its
degree, while noncofinal inclusions split every finite extension of the
old complex fraction field. On absolute Galois groups, the induced
restriction is respectively surjective or trivial.

This theorem is about \emph{extension of the scalar field}. It does not
say that a previously nontrivial field extension becomes equal to its
base, or that a Galois automorphism ceases to exist as an abstract map.
Rather, its finite separable algebra becomes a product of copies of the
new base. This distinction will be important in Section~\ref{bst:gr:sec:rankjump}.

\subsection{Relationship with Sections 1--10 and with published work}\label{bst:gr:subsec:prior}

Section~\ref{bst:sec:descent} proves cofinal linear disjointness
(Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent}), including the special case
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:priordescent}
 K((s))\ \text{and}\ \FF KG
      \text{ are linearly disjoint over }K(s)
      \quad\text{when }s=t^u\text{ and }u\text{ is an order unit}
\end{equation}

The normal-form and Hahn-field background is classical; the formulation

The proposed contribution of source~02 is a specific synthesis: local

\section{Omnific fractions and cyclic-coset descent}\label{bst:gr:sec:descent}

\subsection{Supports, summation, and the ambient field}

Source~02's Hahn-series conventions are those of Section~\ref{bst:sec:setup}:
well-ordered supports, coefficientwise addition, convolution, the valuation
$v(f)=\min\supp(f)$ with $v(0)=+\infty$, and the field
$\HH KG$ \cite[Fact~2.1.1]{LM24}. When $K$ is ordered, the sign of $f\ne0$ is
the sign of its leading coefficient. Only the usual Hahn valuation is used,
not an ordinal-valued support degree.

No analytic convergence is being used. For example, if $u>0$, the family
$(c_m t^{mu})_{m\geq0}$ is a legitimate Hahn family because $\{mu:m\geq0\}$
is well ordered and its terms have distinct exponents. Its sum exists even
when the family is bounded above in a larger ordered group. Neither ordinary
complex convergence nor convergence in a fine topology on all of $\No$ is
needed.

\subsection{From an omnific subring to bounded fractions}

The set $\BB KG$ is a ring (Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:bounds}). For a unital subring
$D\subseteq K$ put
\[
 \AAG DKG=D+K((t^{G^{<0}}))\subseteq K((t^{G^{\le0}}))=K+K((t^{G^{<0}})).
\]
It is a subring. For $(D,K)=(\Z,\R)$ it is the canonical omnific subring at
scale $G$, and for $(D,K)=(\Z[i],\C)$ its Gaussian counterpart.

\begin{proposition}[The fraction-field bridge]\label{bst:gr:prop:fractions}
For every nonzero ordered abelian group $G$, every field $K$ and every
unital subring $D\subseteq K$,
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:fracbridge}
 \Frac\AAG DKG=\Frac K((t^{G^{\le0}}))=\Frac\BB KG=\FF KG
\end{equation}
inside $\HH KG$. In particular the field is independent of $D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The inclusions of rings from left to right are immediate. Conversely,
let $f\in\BB KG$, with support bounded above by $b$. Choose $h>0$ with
$h>b$. Such an $h$ exists in every nonzero ordered abelian group: add a
positive element to $\max(b,0)$. Then
\[
 t^{-h}f\in K((t^{G^{<0}})),\qquad
 t^{-h}\in K((t^{G^{<0}}))\setminus\{0\},\qquad
 f=\frac{t^{-h}f}{t^{-h}}.
\]
Thus $\BB KG\subseteq\Frac\AAG DKG$, proving the reverse inclusion after
taking fractions. The argument also covers constant coefficients in $K$ that
do not belong to $D$.
\end{proof}

\textup{[merge]} This is the localization of
Proposition~\ref{bst:prop:localdensity} (which writes
$\BB KG=t^GK((t^{G^{\le0}}))$), with the constant coefficient restricted to
$D$; the independent-copies report prints the case $D=\Z,\Z[i]$ as
\texttt{isc:prop:boundedfrac} \cite{RepoIsc}. The bridge is why the Galois
part concerns omnific arithmetic rather than an unrelated subfield
construction.

\begin{remark}\label{bst:gr:rem:notwhole}
The equality~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:fracbridge} is not the assertion
$\Frac\AAG DKG=\HH KG$. That stronger equality frequently fails. The
distinction is central to the Galois part and to the cofinality discussion
in \cite[Proposition~2.4.5]{LM24}; Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent} measures
the gap.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Complexification]\label{bst:gr:prop:complexify}
For every nonzero ordered abelian group $G$,
\[
 \BB{\C}G=\BB{\R}G+i\,\BB{\R}G,\qquad \FF{\C}G=\FF{\R}G(i).
\]
The fixed field of coefficientwise conjugation on $\FF{\C}G$ is $\FF{\R}G$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Real and imaginary coefficient parts have supports contained in the
original support. This proves the first identity. If a fraction has
numerator $a+ib$ and denominator $c+id$, with all four terms real and
bounded supported, multiplication by $c-id$ expresses it in $\FF{\R}G(i)$.
The denominator $c^2+d^2$ is nonzero because $\HH{\R}G$ is ordered. The
reverse inclusion and the fixed-field assertion follow at once.
\end{proof}

\emph{The basic closedness input.} Source~02 uses the classical result that
$K((t^G))$ is algebraically closed when $K$ is algebraically closed and $G$
is divisible, and is real closed when $K$ is real closed and $G$ is
divisible \cite[Fact~2.1.1]{LM24}, as Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:closedbase} does.
Its use is restricted to those places where a full Hahn field, not a
bounded-support fraction field, appears. The finite Galois constructions in
an order-unit group do not themselves require that the ambient Hahn field be
algebraically closed.

\subsection{Cyclic-coset descent at an order unit}

This subsection reproves the cyclic instance of the cofinal
linear-disjointness theorem (Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent} and
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onescale}). The proof explains exactly why an order
unit preserves local algebraic obstructions in the larger fraction field.

\begin{lemma}[Finite slices]\label{bst:gr:lem:slices}
Suppose $u$ is an order unit of $G$, and $S\subseteq G$ is well ordered and
bounded above. For each coset $c+\Z u$, the set $S\cap(c+\Z u)$ is finite.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $S$ is nonempty, write $a=\min S$ and let $b$ be an upper bound.
Choose an integer $N>0$ such that both $\abs{a-c}$ and $\abs{b-c}$
are at most $Nu$. Any integer $m$ for which $c+mu\in S$ then satisfies
$-N\leq m\leq N$. There are only finitely many such integers.
\end{proof}

In particular, the projection of $f\in\BB KG$ onto a single coset is
$t^cp_c(s)$ for a Laurent polynomial $p_c\in K[s,s^{-1}]$, where $s=t^u$.
In the notation of~\eqref{bst:eq:projection} with $H=\Z u$, $t^cp_c(s)$ is
$t^c\pi_c(f)$. There can be infinitely many nonzero cosets. Their sum is not
replaced by a finite decomposition; only one coset at a time is tested.

Source~02's Theorem~3.2 (local-field descent) states: \emph{if $u$ is an order
unit of $G$ and $s=t^u$, then $K((s))$ and $\FF KG$ are linearly disjoint over
$K(s)$ inside $\HH KG$, and consequently $K((s))\cap\FF KG=K(s)$.} This is
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onescale} with $L=K$; it is printed once, there.

\begin{remark}[Second route to Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onescale} for $L=K$, from source~02]\label{bst:gr:rem:cyclicroute}
Let $x_1,\ldots,x_r\in K((s))$ be linearly independent over $K(s)$, and
suppose $\sum_{j=1}^r a_jx_j=0$ with $a_j\in\FF KG$. Choose a common nonzero
denominator in $\BB KG$. After multiplying through, we may assume
$a_j\in\BB KG$. Fix a representative $c$ of a coset modulo $\Z u$. By
Lemma~\ref{bst:gr:lem:slices}, the $c+\Z u$ component of $a_j$ is
$t^cp_{j,c}(s)$ with $p_{j,c}\in K[s,s^{-1}]$. Every exponent of $x_j$ belongs
to $\Z u$, so multiplication by $x_j$ preserves the coset of an exponent of
$a_j$. Projecting the relation onto $c+\Z u$ therefore gives
$t^c\sum_{j=1}^rp_{j,c}(s)x_j=0$. The convolution coefficients are finite
Hahn sums, so this projection commutes with the finite sum and with these
products. Linear independence over $K(s)$ yields $p_{j,c}=0$ for every $j$.
This is true for every coset, hence every $a_j$ vanishes. This proves linear
disjointness. For the intersection, an element $x\in K((s))\setminus K(s)$
makes $1,x$ linearly independent over $K(s)$. It therefore cannot belong to
$\FF KG$.

\textup{[merge]} This is the mechanism of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent} (Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:projection}), specialized to
$H=\Z u$ and $L=K$: no coefficient functional is needed, every coset is
projected instead of one chosen exponent, and the projected coefficients are
Laurent polynomials by Lemma~\ref{bst:gr:lem:slices}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Exact preservation of algebraic data]\label{bst:gr:cor:transport}
Let $K\subseteq L$ be fields, let $0\ne H\subseteq G$ be cofinal, and put
$F_0=\FF KH$ and $F=\FF LG$. Let $\Lambda/F_0$ be a finite extension contained
in $\HH KH$. Then
\[
 F\otimes_{F_0}\Lambda\ \cong\ F\Lambda,
 \qquad [F\Lambda:F]=[\Lambda:F_0].
\]
Minimal polynomials of elements of $\Lambda$ over $F_0$ remain minimal after
passing to $F$. If $\Lambda/F_0$ is Galois, restriction induces an isomorphism
\[
 \Gal(F\Lambda/F)\cong\Gal(\Lambda/F_0).
\]
The isomorphism preserves the entire subgroup/intermediate-field
correspondence: a subgroup $\Psi$ corresponds to $F\Lambda^\Psi$.
In particular, for an order unit $u$ of $G$, $s=t^u$, $H=\Z u$ and $L=K$,
this applies to every finite extension $\Lambda/K(s)$ embedded in $K((s))$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Linear disjointness (Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent}) makes the multiplication
map on the tensor product injective. Its image is a finite-dimensional domain
over $F$, hence a field and therefore $F\Lambda$. A basis of $\Lambda$ remains
independent, proving the degree assertion. Apply the same argument to
$F_0(\alpha)$ to preserve the minimal polynomial of any $\alpha\in\Lambda$.

In the Galois case each automorphism of $\Lambda/F_0$ extends by the identity
on $F$ to the tensor product, and hence to $F\Lambda$. The resulting
$[\Lambda:F_0]$ automorphisms exhaust the automorphisms of the extension.
For a subgroup $\Psi$, $F\Lambda^\Psi$ is fixed by $\Psi$, and its degree is
the correct index by the first part. Galois correspondence gives equality
with the fixed field.
\end{proof}

\textup{[merge]} Source~02 states this corollary for $H=\Z u$ and $L=K$
(base $K(s)$), and applies the same argument with base $\FF{\C}H$ in
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:transition}; the proof uses only linear
disjointness, which Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent} supplies in the stated
generality. The degree, minimal-polynomial and Galois-group assertions are
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:relations}(4) and the paragraph after it; the
intermediate-field correspondence is the addition.

\begin{remark}[Why intersections alone are not enough]\label{bst:gr:rem:intersections}
The elementary equality $K((s))\cap\FF KG=K(s)$ rules out individual rational
representations of a local branch. For simultaneous extensions, one needs
the stronger linear-disjointness statement. A trivial field intersection,
without appropriate hypotheses, is not a general substitute for linear
disjointness. All subsequent degree and Galois assertions use linear
disjointness, not merely the intersection.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The general cofinal subgroup form}

An ordered subgroup $H\subseteq G$ is cofinal if every $g\in G$ is at most
some $h\in H$ (Section~\ref{bst:sec:setup}). The cyclic subgroup $\Z u$ is
cofinal exactly when $u$ is an order unit. For rank transitions source~02
needs the general theorem, which is Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent}; it
reproves its same-coefficient-field case ($L=K$) rather than relying on an
unaudited manuscript proof. Its statement (source~02, Theorem~3.5):
\emph{for $0\ne H\subseteq G$ cofinal and any field $K$, $\HH KH$ and $\FF KG$
are linearly disjoint over $\FF KH$ inside $\HH KG$.} It is printed once,
as Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent}.

\begin{remark}[Second route to Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent} for $L=K$, from source~02]\label{bst:gr:rem:cofinalroute}
Let $x_1,\ldots,x_r\in\HH KH$ be linearly independent over $\FF KH$. In a
relation $\sum_ja_jx_j=0$ with $a_j\in\FF KG$, clear a common bounded-support
denominator, so that $a_j\in\BB KG$. For a coset $c+H$, let $b_{j,c}$ be
$t^{-c}$ times the projection of $a_j$ to that coset. It is a Hahn series
supported in $H$. If $\supp(a_j)\leq d_j$ in $G$, then
$\supp(b_{j,c})\leq d_j-c$. Cofinality supplies an element of $H$ above
$d_j-c$, so $b_{j,c}\in\BB KH\subseteq\FF KH$. Projecting the relation gives
$\sum_jb_{j,c}x_j=0$ in $\HH KH$. Independence over $\FF KH$ implies that
every $b_{j,c}$ is zero. Every coefficient of every $a_j$ lies in one of these
cosets, so all $a_j$ are zero.

For $H=\Z u$, every upper bounded well-ordered support in $H$ is finite, so
$\BB KH=K[s,s^{-1}]$ and $\FF KH=K(s)$. Thus this proof specializes exactly
to the cyclic-coset argument of Remark~\ref{bst:gr:rem:cyclicroute}. The
general theorem adds no novelty claim, but makes the all-extension
transition theorem (Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:transition}) exhaustive.
\textup{[merge]} The proof is that of Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent} with the
coefficient functional omitted.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite covers and prescribed products}\label{bst:gr:sec:covers}

\subsection{A classical geometric input with a split local place}

Here and below a branch point refers to the usual ramification of a finite
cover of the complex projective line. It does not refer to the Hahn
valuation on $\HH{\C}G$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite groups in a fixed local coordinate]\label{bst:gr:lem:ret}
For every finite group $\Phi$, there is a finite Galois extension
$\Lambda/\C(s)$ with group $\Phi$, unramified at $s=0$ and at infinity, whose
entire field embeds over $\C(s)$ into $\C((s))$. Its possible branch points
can be confined to any sufficiently large prescribed finite subset of
$\C^\times$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The trivial group is immediate. For nontrivial $\Phi$, choose generators
$g_1,\ldots,g_r$ and append $(g_1\cdots g_r)^{-1}$. The resulting tuple
generates $\Phi$ and has product $1$. Add identity entries if more marked
points have been prescribed.

For a finite set $S\subset\C^\times$ of the required size,
$\pi_1(\mathbf P^1(\C)\setminus S,0)$ has loops around the points of $S$
as generators with their product equal to $1$. Map these loops to the tuple
above. The kernel defines a connected regular topological cover with deck
group $\Phi$. The Riemann existence theorem \cite[Theorem~21.3]{MilneEC}
algebraizes this finite cover. Its smooth projective completion is branched
only in $S$. In particular neither $0$ nor infinity is a branch point.

Choose a point above $0$. Its residue field is $\C$, and its ramification
index is $1$. The completed local ring is thus $\C[[s]]$, with the base
coordinate $s$ itself a uniformizer. The injection of the function field
into the fraction field of that completion gives the required embedding
$\Lambda\hookrightarrow\C((s))$. Since the cover is regular, $\Lambda/\C(s)$
is Galois with group $\Phi$. This embeds the whole splitting field, not
merely one root of a polynomial.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-group realization]\label{bst:gr:thm:finitegroups}
If $G$ has an order unit, every finite group is the Galois group of a finite
extension of $\FF{\C}G$ inside $\HH{\C}G$. The extension has a basis
consisting of local Laurent series in a single monomial $s=t^u$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\Lambda/\C(s)$ from Lemma~\ref{bst:gr:lem:ret}, view it inside
$\C((s))\subseteq\HH{\C}G$, and apply Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport}.
A $\C(s)$-basis of $\Lambda$ remains a basis after scalar extension.
\end{proof}

This is an inverse-Galois statement over the specifically defined field
$\FF{\C}G$. It says nothing about the inverse Galois problem over $\Q$. The
source of arbitrary finite groups is the classical complex covering theorem;
the new arithmetic issue is whether their function-field degrees and
symmetries survive the much larger omnific fraction field.

\subsection{All locally split finite Galois covers at once}

Inside the fixed field $\C((s))$, let $\mathcal E_0$ be the compositum of all
finite Galois extensions of $\C(s)$ that it contains. This is an algebraic
Galois extension: every finite collection is contained in a finite Galois
compositum. For an order unit $u$, set
\[
 \mathcal E_G=\FF{\C}G\,\mathcal E_0\subseteq\HH{\C}G.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Galois-envelope preservation]\label{bst:gr:thm:envelope}
The multiplication map identifies $\FF{\C}G\otimes_{\C(s)}\mathcal E_0$ with
$\mathcal E_G$, and restriction induces a topological isomorphism
\[
 \Gal(\mathcal E_G/\FF{\C}G)\cong\Gal(\mathcal E_0/\C(s)).
\]
Every finite Galois diagram in this local envelope, including its
restriction maps and its intermediate fields, is preserved.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport} to every finite Galois
subextension of $\mathcal E_0$. The extensions of automorphisms are uniquely
determined by acting trivially on $\FF{\C}G$, so they commute with
restriction between finite subextensions. Passing to the directed union of
the fields and the inverse limit of their finite Galois groups proves the
assertion. This also proves the topological claim, because both Krull
topologies are the inverse-limit topologies of those finite quotients
\cite[Chapter~7]{MilneFT}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is stronger than a disconnected list of finite-group
realizations. It preserves the relations among all the covers in the
chosen envelope. It does not identify this envelope with an algebraic
closure of $\FF{\C}G$, or identify its Galois group with the full absolute
Galois group. Those additional assertions would need new arguments.

\subsection{Independent products of arbitrary finite groups}

\begin{lemma}[Separation by disjoint branch loci]\label{bst:gr:lem:branches}
Let $\Lambda_1,\ldots,\Lambda_q$ be finite Galois extensions of $\C(s)$.
Suppose their branch loci on $\mathbf P^1(\C)$ are pairwise disjoint. Then
they are jointly linearly disjoint, and
\[
 \Gal(\Lambda_1\cdots\Lambda_q/\C(s))\cong\prod_{j=1}^q\Gal(\Lambda_j/\C(s)).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We argue by induction on $q$. The compositum
$\Lambda_{<q}=\Lambda_1\cdots\Lambda_{q-1}$ is Galois and is unramified
outside the union of the earlier branch loci. This last assertion can be
checked locally: a compositum of finite unramified covers is unramified, and
over a complex point an unramified completion is simply the base Laurent
series field.

The intersection $\Lambda_q\cap\Lambda_{<q}$ is a finite Galois extension of
$\C(s)$. Its branch locus is contained in that of $\Lambda_q$ and also in
that of $\Lambda_{<q}$, hence is empty. A connected finite unramified cover
$Y\to\mathbf P^1_\C$ of degree $d$ must have $d=1$: the Riemann--Hurwitz
formula gives $2g(Y)-2=-2d$, which is impossible for $d>1$. Therefore
$\Lambda_q\cap\Lambda_{<q}=\C(s)$. For these finite Galois extensions, the
restriction formula for a compositum gives
$[\Lambda_q\Lambda_{<q}:\Lambda_{<q}]=[\Lambda_q:\C(s)]$. Induction proves the
product assertion and joint linear disjointness.
\end{proof}

\textup{[merge]} The number of extensions is written $q$ here and
$\Lambda_{<q}$ is source~02's $M$ (Section~\ref{bst:gr:subsec:notation}).

\begin{theorem}[Continuum-sized prescribed products]\label{bst:gr:thm:products}
Suppose $u$ is an order unit of $G$. For every family $(\Phi_j)_{j\in J}$ of
finite groups with $\abs{J}\leq\cc$, there is a Galois extension
$E/\FF{\C}G$ in $\HH{\C}G$ such that
\[
 \Gal(E/\FF{\C}G)\cong\prod_{j\in J}\Phi_j.
\]
One can choose finite Galois subextensions $E_j$ with the specified groups
that are jointly linearly disjoint over $\FF{\C}G$, and take $E$ to be their
compositum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose pairwise disjoint finite subsets $S_j\subset\C^\times$, each large
enough for the construction in Lemma~\ref{bst:gr:lem:ret}. This is possible
since at most $\cc$ finite sets are required. For example, partition a set
of cardinality $\cc$ into $\cc$ countable sets and select the required
finite numbers of points.

Construct $\Lambda_j/\C(s)$ with group $\Phi_j$, branched only in $S_j$, and
embed each $\Lambda_j$ into the same $\C((s))$ by choosing a point above $0$.
Every finite collection is jointly linearly disjoint by
Lemma~\ref{bst:gr:lem:branches}. No common branch point at infinity has been
introduced: infinity was excluded from all branch loci in
Lemma~\ref{bst:gr:lem:ret}.

Put $F=\FF{\C}G$ and $E_j=F\Lambda_j$. The full finite compositum
$\prod_{j\in J_0}\Lambda_j$ lies in $\C((s))$ for every finite
$J_0\subseteq J$. Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport} preserves its degree
and Galois group, so the $E_j$ are also jointly linearly disjoint. The
compositum $E$ is a Galois algebraic extension of $F$, and
\[
 \Gal(E/F)=\varprojlim_{J_0\subseteq J\ \mathrm{finite}}\prod_{j\in J_0}\Phi_j
          =\prod_{j\in J}\Phi_j
\]
with the product topology.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{bst:gr:cor:absquotient}
For an order-unit group $G$, every product in
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:products} is a continuous quotient of
$\AbsGal{\FF{\C}G}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Embed the algebraic extension $E/F$ in an algebraic closure of $F$.
Restriction from the absolute Galois group onto the Galois group of a
Galois subextension is surjective.
\end{proof}

The conclusion is a quotient assertion. A profinite group can have all
finite groups as quotients and still have substantial relations or
cohomological restrictions. In particular, the theorem is not a proof that
$\AbsGal{\FF{\C}G}$ is a free profinite group.

\section{Radical towers and the closedness boundary}\label{bst:gr:sec:radicals}

The construction of Section~\ref{bst:gr:sec:covers} is geometric. This
section supplies concrete normal forms and an elementary independence
argument. It also avoids the need to infer multiradical independence from
single-root examples.

\subsection{Normalized binomial branches}

For $a\in\C^\times$ and $n\geq1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:radical}
 r_{a,n}(s)=\sum_{m\geq0}\binom{1/n}{m}(-a)^m s^m,
 \qquad
 \binom{x}{m}=\frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-m+1)}{m!}.
\end{equation}
These are formal power series, hence Hahn series at $s=t^u$ for any $u>0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{bst:gr:lem:binomial}
The series $r_{a,n}$ is the unique element of $1+s\C[[s]]$ satisfying
$r_{a,n}^n=1-as$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:compat}
 r_{a,mn}^{\,m}=r_{a,n}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a formal series $r=1+\sum_{j\geq1}b_js^j$, the coefficient of $s^j$ in
$r^n$ is $nb_j$ plus an expression in the preceding coefficients. Since the
characteristic is zero, there is a unique recursive solution to $r^n=1-as$.
The binomial coefficients in~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:radical} give this solution by
the formal binomial identity. Alternatively, their recurrence verifies the
differential identity $(1-as)r'=-ar/n$, and hence that $r^n/(1-as)$ has
derivative zero and constant term $1$. The normalized branch of its $n$th
power is therefore as stated. Finally $r_{a,mn}^{\,m}$ has constant term $1$
and its $n$th power is $1-as$, so uniqueness proves~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:compat}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Single-root degree]\label{bst:gr:prop:single}
For $a\ne0$ and $n\geq2$, the polynomial $X^n-(1-as)$ is irreducible over
$\C(s)$ and, when $u$ is an order unit, over $\FF{\C}G$. Its splitting field
in the Hahn field is obtained by adjoining $r_{a,n}$ and has cyclic Galois
group of order $n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At the discrete valuation associated with $s=a^{-1}$, the element $1-as$ has
valuation $1$. Eisenstein's criterion proves irreducibility over $\C(s)$.
Transfer by Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport} proves irreducibility over
$\FF{\C}G$. All roots are $\zeta r_{a,n}$ with $\zeta\in\mu_n\subset\C$, so
the splitting field is the indicated cyclic extension.
\end{proof}

\textup{[merge]} The case $a=-1$, $n=2$ is the example after
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onescale}: $(1+s)^{1/2}=r_{-1,2}$ has degree two over
$\FF LG$ for every characteristic-zero $L$.

\subsection{Independence of all marked linear factors}

\begin{theorem}[Finite multiradical independence]\label{bst:gr:thm:multirad}
Let $a_1,\ldots,a_q$ be distinct nonzero complex numbers and $n\geq2$. Then
\[
 [\C(s)(r_{a_1,n},\ldots,r_{a_q,n}):\C(s)]=n^q,
 \qquad
 \Gal\cong(\Z/n\Z)^q.
\]
If $u$ is an order unit, both conclusions remain true with $\C(s)$ replaced
by $\FF{\C}G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the degree by adjoining the radicals in order. Suppose the first
$j-1$ have been adjoined and set $b=a_j^{-1}$, $w=s-b$. For every $i<j$,
$1-a_i(b+w)$ has nonzero constant term because $a_i\ne a_j$. It has $n$
distinct $n$th roots in $\C[[w]]$: choose an $n$th root of the constant and
apply the normalized binomial construction to the remaining unit.

Consequently the splitting field of the first $j-1$ polynomials admits a
$\C(s)$-embedding into $\C((w))$. Use this embedding to restrict the
$w$-adic valuation to $\Lambda_{j-1}=\C(s)(r_{a_1,n},\ldots,r_{a_{j-1},n})$.
Its value group is $\Z$: it is a subgroup of $\Z$ and already contains the
value $1$ of $s-b$. In this valuation,
\[
 v_w(1-a_js)=1.
\]
Thus $X^n-(1-a_js)$ is Eisenstein over the valuation ring of
$\Lambda_{j-1}$, and its degree over that field is $n$. This proves the
inductive degree $n^q$.

The compositum is the splitting field of the product of these polynomials.
Its automorphisms act by $r_{a_j,n}\mapsto\zeta_n^{e_j}r_{a_j,n}$ and embed
the Galois group in $(\Z/n\Z)^q$. Equality of orders makes this an
isomorphism. Finally the whole compositum lies in $\C((s))$, so
Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport} preserves the result over $\FF{\C}G$.
\end{proof}

\textup{[merge]} The $w$-adic valuation is written $v_w$ to keep it apart
from the Hahn valuation $v$; source~02 writes $v$.

\begin{remark}[A divisor interpretation]\label{bst:gr:rem:divisor}
The same independence is visible in the valuations
$\operatorname{ord}_{s=a_j^{-1}}$. If $\prod_j(1-a_js)^{e_j}$ is an $n$th
power in $\C(s)$, each $e_j$ is divisible by $n$. This is the usual
Kummer-theoretic description \cite[Chapter~5, Kummer theory]{MilneFT}. The
proof above spells out the successive degree argument, including composite
values of $n$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The infinite tower}

For $T\subseteq\C^\times$, let
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:ET}
 E_T=\FF{\C}G\,(r_{a,n}:a\in T,\ n\geq1).
\end{equation}
Choose the compatible roots of unity $\zeta_n=\exp(2\pi i/n)$ among the
ordinary complex constants.

\begin{theorem}[An explicit profinite quotient]\label{bst:gr:thm:radicaltower}
For an order-unit group $G$,
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:radicalgal}
 \Gal(E_T/\FF{\C}G)\cong\prod_{a\in T}\Zh.
\end{equation}
For infinite $T$,
\[
 [E_T:\FF{\C}G]=\max(\abs T,\aleph_0),
 \qquad
 \abs{\Gal(E_T/\FF{\C}G)}=2^{\max(\abs T,\aleph_0)}.
\]
In particular, for $T=\C^\times$ the extension has field degree $\cc$ and its
Galois group has cardinality $2^{\cc}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An automorphism is determined by residues $e_{a,n}\in\Z/n\Z$ through
\[
 r_{a,n}\longmapsto\zeta_n^{e_{a,n}}r_{a,n}.
\]
The compatibility~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:compat} makes these residues compatible
when one integer divides another. Conversely, any compatible family acts on
every finite subextension: choose a common multiple of its finitely many root
orders and use Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:multirad}. These actions agree on
overlaps, so give an automorphism of $E_T$. The finite-level restriction maps
give exactly the topology in~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:radicalgal}.

There are at most $\max(\abs T,\aleph_0)$ finite radical data, and each
produces a finite-dimensional extension, giving that upper bound for the
field degree. When $T$ is infinite, the elements $r_{a,2}$, $a\in T$, are
linearly independent over the base: every finite subset is part of the
standard basis of a multiquadratic extension. This gives the lower bound
$\abs T$. If necessary, unbounded degrees in a single radical tower give the
additional lower bound $\aleph_0$. Finally each $\Zh$ has cardinality $\cc$,
so the displayed group cardinality follows from cardinal exponentiation.
\end{proof}

The comparison of infinite field degree with group cardinality is
intentional. For $T=\C^\times$, the answer is $\cc$ for the former and
$2^{\cc}$ for the latter. They must not be conflated.

\subsection{Closedness without an order unit}

The positive closedness assertion here is prior material of this report
(Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:fieldcriterion} and Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:closedbase}).
Source~02 includes its proof to make the opposite side of the Galois
dichotomy transparent.

\begin{remark}[Source~02's Proposition~7.1, printed once as Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:fieldcriterion}]\label{bst:gr:rem:nounit}
Source~02 states: \emph{if $G$ has no order unit, then}
\[
 \BB KG=\FF KG=\bigcup_{g>0}\HH K{C(g)},
\]
\emph{so $\BB KG$ is a field; conversely, if $G$ has an order unit, $\BB KG$ is
not a field.} Here $C(g)$ is the principal convex subgroup of
Section~\ref{bst:sec:rank}, divisible when $G$ is. This is
Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:fieldcriterion}, and source~02's proof is that proof:
a bounded well-ordered support lies in some $[-g,g]$, hence in $C(g)$; for
$g$ not an order unit some $h>0$ exceeds every $ng$, so every series supported
in $C(g)$ has support bounded above in $G$, whether or not that support is
bounded inside $C(g)$; the principal convex subgroups form a directed family;
and with an order unit $u$ the inverse $\sum_{n\ge0}t^{nu}$ of $1-t^u$ has
unbounded support. \textup{[merge]} The union over the principal convex
subgroups $C(g)$ equals the union over all proper convex subgroups in
Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:fieldcriterion}(2): every $C(g)$ with $g$ not an order
unit is proper, and a proper convex $C$ lies in $C(g)$ for any positive
$g\notin C$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact closedness boundary]\label{bst:gr:thm:closedness}
For a nonzero divisible ordered abelian group $G$,
\[
 \FF{\C}G\text{ is algebraically closed}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 G\text{ has no order unit},
\]
and
\[
 \FF{\R}G\text{ is real closed}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 G\text{ has no order unit}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Without an order unit, Remark~\ref{bst:gr:rem:nounit} expresses the fraction
field as a directed union of full Hahn fields over divisible convex
subgroups. Each is algebraically closed for $K=\C$ and real closed for
$K=\R$, by the classical Hahn-field theorem. A polynomial has only finitely
many coefficients, all in one of these fields. This proves algebraic
closedness in the complex case. In the real case, every positive element
gets a square root and every odd-degree polynomial gets a root in one of the
real-closed subfields, proving real closedness of the union.

With an order unit $u$, Proposition~\ref{bst:gr:prop:single} for $a=1$, $n=2$
proves that $1-t^u$ has no square root in $\FF{\C}G$. It therefore has none
in the real subfield either. But $1-t^u$ is positive in that ordered field.
This rules out both asserted forms of closedness.
\end{proof}

\textup{[merge]} The forward implications are Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:closedbase},
which allows any real closed $K$, and any algebraically closed $K$ of
characteristic zero. The reverse implications hold at that generality too:
$X^2-(1-s)$ is irreducible over $K(s)$ for every field $K$ of characteristic
zero, since $1-s$ has odd degree, so it stays irreducible over $\FF KG$ by
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:relations}(4) with $H=\Z u$ and $L=K$.

\begin{example}[Countable cofinality is not the boundary]\label{bst:gr:ex:countable}
Let $G=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\Q e_n$, ordered by the largest index with nonzero
coefficient, with all $e_n>0$. Then $e_{n+1}>me_n$ for every positive integer
$m$. The group has countable cofinality but no order unit. Hence $\FF{\C}G$ is
algebraically closed.

It is nevertheless smaller than $\HH{\C}G$. The series $\sum_{n\geq0}t^{e_n}$
is a legitimate Hahn series with an upper unbounded support. It does not
belong to $\BB{\C}G$, which in this case is already $\FF{\C}G$. Thus
closedness of the fraction field need not mean equality with the full Hahn
field.
\end{example}

\textup{[merge]} With $e_n=\omega^n$ this group is $G_{\aleph_0}$
of~\eqref{bst:eq:Gkappa}, and the example is Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:kappa}(1) at
$\kappa=\aleph_0$ together with the remark after Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:cofinality}
(countable cofinality does not give an order unit). The series
$\sum_nt^{e_n}$ is even transcendental over $\FF{\C}G$, by
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onegap}, and Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:kappa}(2) gives
$2^{\aleph_0}$ independent elements.

\section{A nonsolvable family, real forms and cardinality}\label{bst:gr:sec:real}

\subsection{An explicit nonsolvable family}

The geometric existence theorem can be complemented by one uniform
polynomial family. Its classical monodromy calculation is elementary; it is
included to make the surcomplex realization concrete.

\begin{theorem}[Symmetric groups at a monomial scale]\label{bst:gr:thm:symmetric}
Let $m\geq2$, let $u$ be an order unit, and put $s=t^u$. Then
\[
 X^m-X-s
\]
is irreducible over $\FF{\C}G$, and its splitting field inside $\HH{\C}G$
has Galois group $S_m$. All its roots belong to $\C[[s]]$. In particular
$m=5$ gives an explicit nonsolvable Galois extension.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First work over $\C(s)$. The polynomial map
$\varphi:\mathbf P^1_x\to\mathbf P^1_s$, $\varphi(x)=x^m-x$, has degree $m$.
The corresponding extension of function fields has degree $m$, so $X^m-X-s$
is irreducible over $\C(s)$.

Its finite critical points satisfy $mc^{m-1}=1$. They are nonzero and
distinct, and $\varphi''(c)=m(m-1)c^{m-2}\ne0$. Their critical values are
\[
 \varphi(c)=-\frac{m-1}{m}\,c,
\]
so are also distinct. A small loop about each finite critical value therefore
exchanges exactly two sheets. The finite local monodromies are
transpositions. They generate the full monodromy group because a loop about
infinity is the inverse of their product. The cover is connected, so the
group they generate is transitive.

A transitive subgroup of $S_m$ generated by transpositions is $S_m$: form the
graph whose edges are the transpositions. Its orbits are the connected
components, so it is connected; transpositions on the edges of a connected
graph generate all permutations. Thus the splitting field over $\C(s)$ has
group $S_m$.

At $s=0$, the roots of $X^m-X$ are $0$ and the $(m-1)$st roots of unity, all
distinct. At $0$ the derivative is $-1$, and at the other roots it is $m-1$.
Formal coefficient recursion therefore lifts each root uniquely to
$\C[[s]]$. The whole splitting field embeds into $\C((s))$. Apply
Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport} to preserve the polynomial's minimal
degree and splitting-field Galois group over $\FF{\C}G$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Small roots and large algebraic complexity]\label{bst:gr:rem:smallroots}
The root $x_0(s)$ with constant term $0$ is determined by
\[
 x_0=x_0^m-s.
\]
For $m=5$ it begins $-s-s^5-5s^9-\cdots$. It is a perfectly explicit
surcomplex infinitesimal at $s=\omega^{-1}$. Its degree over the
real-exponent complex omnific fraction field $\mathscr F_\C=\FF{\C}{\R}$ is
$5$, and the normal closure of its field has group $S_5$. Being represented
by a convergent local complex series, or by a legitimate Hahn sum, does not
make that series a bounded-support fraction.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Real multiquadratic extensions}

\begin{proposition}\label{bst:gr:prop:realquad}
Let $u$ be an order unit and $T\subseteq\R_{>0}$. The field
\[
 \FF{\R}G(r_{a,2}:a\in T)\subseteq\HH{\R}G
\]
is Galois over $\FF{\R}G$ with group $\prod_{a\in T}\Z/2\Z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a finite subset $\{a_1,\ldots,a_q\}$, the degree over $\R(s)$ is at most
$2^q$. Its complexification has degree $2^q$ over $\C(s)$ by
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:multirad}, so its real degree is also $2^q$.
Alternatively, any smaller real linear relation would be a complex one. The
real form of Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport} preserves this degree over
$\FF{\R}G$. Sign changes of the square roots give the full Galois group.
Passing to the directed union gives the product.
\end{proof}

Every normalized root $r_{a,2}$ is positive in the Hahn order, because its
constant term is $1$. A nonidentity automorphism of one of these
multiquadratic extensions need not preserve that order. There is no
contradiction: these are abstract field automorphisms of fields that are not
real closed. \textup{[merge]} The restriction $T\subseteq\R_{>0}$ is
source~02's; the proof uses only that each $a$ is real and nonzero.

\subsection{A generalized-dihedral radical extension}

For $T\subseteq\R_{>0}$, consider the field $E_T$ of~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:ET}.
Coefficientwise conjugation fixes every $r_{a,n}$ and restricts to the
nonidentity element of $\Gal(\FF{\C}G/\FF{\R}G)$.

\begin{theorem}[Real radical symmetry]\label{bst:gr:thm:dihedral}
For an order-unit group $G$ and $T\subseteq\R_{>0}$,
\[
 \Gal(E_T/\FF{\R}G)\cong\left(\prod_{a\in T}\Zh\right)\rtimes C_2,
\]
where the nontrivial element of $C_2$ acts by inversion on every $\Zh$
coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The field $E_T$ is the splitting field, over $\FF{\R}G$, of the family
consisting of $X^2+1$ and all $X^n-(1-at^u)$. Thus it is Galois. By
Proposition~\ref{bst:gr:prop:complexify} and
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:radicaltower}, the kernel of restriction to
$\FF{\C}G$ is $\prod_{a\in T}\Zh$. Conjugation supplies an order-two section
of the quotient $C_2$.

If $\sigma(r_{a,n})=\zeta_n^{e_{a,n}}r_{a,n}$, conjugating this automorphism
by coefficientwise conjugation changes $\zeta_n$ to $\zeta_n^{-1}$ and leaves
$r_{a,n}$ fixed. The resulting coordinate is $-e_{a,n}$, proving the claimed
semidirect action.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An exact cardinality calculation}

\begin{theorem}[Size of the absolute Galois group in real rank]\label{bst:gr:thm:cardinality}
Let $G$ be any nonzero additive subgroup of $\R$. Then
\[
 \abs{\FF{\C}G}=\abs{\FF{\R}G}=\cc,
 \qquad
 \abs{\AbsGal{\FF{\C}G}}=\abs{\AbsGal{\FF{\R}G}}=2^{\cc}.
\]
Each fraction field has exactly $\cc$ distinct quadratic extensions inside
an algebraic closure. Divisibility of $G$ is not necessary for this
statement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every well-ordered subset of $\R$ is countable. One proof assigns to each
non-last element of such a set a rational number strictly between it and its
successor; these disjoint intervals give an injection into $\Q$. There is at
most one last element. Therefore the Hahn supports in $G$ are countable. The
number of countable supports and coefficient assignments is at most
$\cc^{\aleph_0}=\cc$. Ordinary real or complex constants already give the
reverse bound for the fraction fields.

Every positive $u\in G$ is an order unit. By
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:radicaltower}, the complex absolute Galois group has a
quotient of cardinality $2^{\cc}$. By Proposition~\ref{bst:gr:prop:realquad},
the real absolute Galois group has such a quotient as well. An algebraic
closure of an infinite field of cardinality $\cc$ has cardinality $\cc$, and
its automorphism group has cardinality at most $\cc^{\cc}=2^{\cc}$. This
proves equality.

The distinct marked square roots in Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:multirad} and
Proposition~\ref{bst:gr:prop:realquad} give $\cc$ distinct quadratic
extensions: equality of two would contradict their degree-four compositum.
There are at most $\cc$ quadratic polynomials over either base field, which
proves the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\textup{[merge]} The count $\abs{\HH KG}\le\cc$ is the one in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:realgroup}.

No conclusion about the least number of topological generators follows from
this cardinality calculation. Cardinality, topological weight, and profinite
generator rank are different invariants.

\section{One dominating scale splits all old finite extensions}\label{bst:gr:sec:rankjump}

\subsection{Absorbing the entire old Hahn field}

Let $H\subseteq G$ be an ordered subgroup inclusion. Say that
$\theta\in G_{>0}$ \emph{dominates $H$} if $\theta>h$ for every $h\in H$. In
particular, $\theta>nh$ for every $h>0$ in $H$ and every ordinary positive
integer $n$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform monomial denominator]\label{bst:gr:thm:absorption}
If $\theta$ dominates $H$, then for every coefficient field $K$,
\[
 \HH KH\subseteq\BB KG.
\]
More explicitly, for every $f\in\HH KH$ and every unital subring $D\subseteq K$,
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:uniformdenom}
 t^{-\theta}f\in K((t^{G^{<0}})),\qquad
 f=\frac{t^{-\theta}f}{t^{-\theta}}\in\Frac\AAG DKG.
\end{equation}
The same denominator $t^{-\theta}$ works for every old Hahn series.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The ordered inclusion preserves well-ordering of supports. Every old support
is a subset of $H$ and therefore bounded above by $\theta$ in $G$.
Subtracting $\theta$ from its exponents puts the entire support in $G^{<0}$.
Translation preserves well-ordering, giving both assertions
in~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:uniformdenom}.
\end{proof}

The upper bound here is external to the old group. This is precisely why an
old unbounded support can become a numerator with only negative
$t$-exponents. The support enlargement phenomenon itself is already
illustrated in Section~8.4 of this report, and the displayed inclusion is
\eqref{bst:eq:collapse} with $L=K$ \textup{[merge]}. The next theorem makes
its full Galois consequence explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous splitting of every old finite extension]\label{bst:gr:thm:allcollapse}
Assume $H$ is divisible, and let $\theta\in G$ dominate it. Put
$F=\FF{\C}H$ and $F'=\FF{\C}G$. Then $F'$ contains an algebraic closure
$\overline F$ of $F$. For every finite extension $\Lambda/F$ there is an
$F'$-algebra isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:splitall}
 \Lambda\otimes_FF'\cong(F')^{[\Lambda:F]}.
\end{equation}
More generally every finite \emph{\'etale} $F$-algebra becomes a finite
product of copies of $F'$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the full Hahn-field closedness theorem, $\HH{\C}H$ is algebraically
closed. Let $\overline F$ be the subfield of elements of $\HH{\C}H$ algebraic
over $F$. This is an algebraic closure of $F$: the roots in $\HH{\C}H$ of a
polynomial with coefficients algebraic over $F$ are themselves algebraic
over $F$. By Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:absorption}, $\HH{\C}H\subseteq F'$, so
$\overline F\subseteq F'$.

Since the characteristic is zero, a finite extension $\Lambda/F$ is separable
and simple. Write $\Lambda=F(\alpha)$ with irreducible polynomial $p$ of
degree $d$. In $F'[X]$ the polynomial factors as
\[
 p(X)=\prod_{j=1}^d(X-\alpha_j)
\]
with distinct roots $\alpha_j\in\overline F\subseteq F'$. The Chinese
remainder theorem gives
\[
 \Lambda\otimes_FF'\cong F'[X]/(p)\cong\prod_{j=1}^dF',
\]
proving~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:splitall}. A finite \'etale algebra is a finite
product of finite separable field extensions, so the final assertion
follows.
\end{proof}

For an embedded finite Galois extension $\Lambda/F\subseteq F'$, the
isomorphism has the concrete form
\[
 x\otimes a\ \longmapsto\ (\sigma(x)a)_{\sigma\in\Gal(\Lambda/F)}.
\]
The different factors record the different old embeddings. Thus the
symmetry is reflected in the splitting into factors; it is not erased from
the abstract field $\Lambda/F$.

\subsection{An exhaustive cofinality alternative}

For an ordered subgroup inclusion there is no intermediate case between
cofinality and the existence of a dominating element. Indeed, if
$H\subseteq G$ is not cofinal, some $\theta\in G$ is strictly greater than
every element of $H$. Since $0\in H$, this $\theta$ is positive.
\textup{[merge]} This is the last assertion of Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:cofinality}.

\begin{theorem}[All-or-nothing Galois transition]\label{bst:gr:thm:transition}
Let $H\subseteq G$ be an inclusion of nonzero set-sized ordered abelian
groups, with $H$ divisible. Write $F=\FF{\C}H$ and $F'=\FF{\C}G$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $H$ is cofinal in $G$, then for every finite extension $\Lambda/F$
the tensor product $\Lambda\otimes_FF'$ is a field of degree $[\Lambda:F]$ over
$F'$. If $\Lambda/F$ is Galois, its Galois group and all intermediate-field
degrees are preserved.
\item If $H$ is not cofinal in $G$, then for every finite extension
$\Lambda/F$,
\[
 \Lambda\otimes_FF'\cong(F')^{[\Lambda:F]}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
With compatible choices of algebraic closures, the restriction map
$\AbsGal{F'}\to\AbsGal F$ is surjective in case \textup{(1)} and trivial in
case \textup{(2)}. When $\AbsGal F$ is trivial the two conclusions about
this group homomorphism coincide; the criterion is a criterion on the
exponent inclusion, not an assertion that these group properties are then
disjoint.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because $H$ is divisible, $\HH{\C}H$ is algebraically closed and contains an
algebraic closure $\overline F$ of $F$. Every finite extension of $F$ is
isomorphic over $F$ to a subfield of $\overline F$.

In the cofinal case, Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:descent}, with $K=L=\C$, shows that
$\HH{\C}H$ and $F'$ are linearly disjoint over $F$. The same is true of each
$\Lambda$. The tensor and Galois arguments of
Corollary~\ref{bst:gr:cor:transport} apply with base $F$, proving (1).
Restriction is surjective on every finite Galois quotient of $\AbsGal F$. Its
image in $\AbsGal F$ is compact, hence closed, and these finite surjections
make that image dense. It is therefore all of $\AbsGal F$.

In the noncofinal case, choose a dominating $\theta\in G$.
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:allcollapse} proves (2) and puts
$\overline F\subseteq F'$. An automorphism fixing $F'$ fixes $\overline F$
pointwise, so its image in $\AbsGal F$ is trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{bst:gr:rem:binary}
The field-theoretic statements in the two cases are genuinely distinct when
$H$ has an order unit, since $F$ then has nontrivial finite extensions. This
provides a binary classification of how monomial exponent-group inclusions
affect \emph{all} finite Galois data at once. The cofinal support theorem is
prior work; its combination with algebraic closedness of the old full Hahn
field and noncofinal absorption is the Galois transition statement here.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The real counterpart]\label{bst:gr:prop:realclosure}
If $H$ is divisible and $\theta$ dominates it, then $\FF{\R}G$ contains a real
closure of $\FF{\R}H$ compatible with its Hahn order.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The field $\HH{\R}H$ is real closed. Its relative algebraic subfield over
$\FF{\R}H$ is a real closure for the induced order: positive elements have
square roots there, and odd-degree polynomials have roots there, because such
roots in the ambient real closed field remain algebraic over the base. Apply
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:absorption}.
\end{proof}

The real statement does not say that every finite extension of the real base
field splits after this base change. For example, $X^2+1$ still has no root
in an ordered field. The complete splitting theorem is deliberately stated
for complex coefficients.

\subsection{The new group still has an order unit}

Take the lexicographic extension
\[
 G=\Q\theta\oplus H,
\]
where a nonzero $\theta$-coordinate decides the sign before the $H$
coordinate is considered. Then $\theta$ dominates $H$ and is an order unit of
$G$: every $q\theta+h$ lies between $-N\theta$ and $N\theta$ for an integer
$N>\abs q+1$. If $H$ is divisible, so is $G$.

Consequently Theorems~\ref{bst:gr:thm:allcollapse}
and~\ref{bst:gr:thm:products} hold simultaneously. All old finite extensions
split after this scalar extension, yet the new field has extensions
realizing every finite group. When $u$ is an order unit of $H$, the old
missing root $\sqrt{1-t^u}$ is now in the new fraction field, whereas the
normalized root $\sqrt{1-t^\theta}$ is not (Proposition~\ref{bst:gr:prop:single}
at the order unit $\theta$). With Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:absorption}, this
proves Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:mainjump}.

\begin{remark}[What is uniform]\label{bst:gr:rem:uniform}
The scale $\theta$ depends on the old exponent group, not on an individual
polynomial, a finite group, or a selected algebraic extension. A single
$\theta$ absorbs the full old Hahn field and therefore all old algebraic
extensions simultaneously. It does not absorb the full Hahn field over $G$
itself.
\end{remark}

\section{Actual surreal examples and a countable rank tower}\label{bst:gr:sec:surreal}

\subsection{The normal-form embedding}

Source~02 uses the Conway normal-form map~\eqref{bst:eq:conway}, for an
ordered additive subgroup $G\subseteq\No$, which identifies $\HH{\R}G$ with a
subfield of $\No$ and $\HH{\C}G$ with a subfield of $\No[i]$
\cite{Conway,Gonshor,LM24}; it is printed once, in Section~8.1. The image of
$\AAG{\Z}{\R}G$ consists of omnific integers: all growth exponents are
positive except for an integer constant term. Likewise $\AAG{\Z[i]}{\C}G$
maps into $\Oz[i]$. Hence all the fraction-field distinctions of the Galois
part are distinctions between actual surreal or surcomplex numbers. They are
not constructions of a separate model with an unspecified embedding.

\subsection{Real exponents: a root that is not an omnific fraction at that scale}

Take $G=\R$ and $u=1$, so $s=\omega^{-1}$, and consider the fields
$\mathscr F_\R=\FF{\R}{\R}$ and $\mathscr F_\C=\FF{\C}{\R}$ of Section~8.2.
The normalized square root has the Conway expansion
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:sqrtconway}
 \sqrt{1-\omega^{-1}}
 =1-\frac12\omega^{-1}-\frac18\omega^{-2}
 -\frac1{16}\omega^{-3}-\frac5{128}\omega^{-4}-\cdots.
\end{equation}
It belongs to $\HH{\R}{\R}$ but not to $\mathscr F_\R$, nor to $\mathscr F_\C$.
Thus it cannot be represented as a fraction of two omnific integers all of
whose growth exponents are real. The restriction on exponents is essential.

Now take $G'=\Q\omega+\R$. The exponent $\theta=\omega$ dominates all of $\R$.
Formula~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:uniformdenom} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:explicitquotient}
 \sqrt{1-\omega^{-1}}
 =\frac{\displaystyle
   \omega^\omega-\frac12\omega^{\omega-1}
    -\frac18\omega^{\omega-2}-\frac1{16}\omega^{\omega-3}
    -\cdots}{\omega^\omega}.
\end{equation}
The numerator is a legitimate omnific integer. Its exponents
$\omega,\omega-1,\omega-2,\ldots$ are decreasing and positive, and its
coefficient at exponent $0$ is $0$. The denominator is also omnific. Both use
exponents in $G'$.

However,
\[
 \sqrt{1-\omega^{-\omega}}\notin\FF{\R}{G'},
\]
and it does not belong to $\FF{\C}{G'}$ either. The new order unit has
recreated the same obstruction at the new scale.

\textup{[merge]} The larger group is written $G'$ because $G$ is the
generic exponent group. Section~8.4 uses $\R\omega+\R$ instead of
$\Q\omega+\R$; $\omega$ dominates $\R$ and is an order unit there too, so
the same two statements hold in that group.

\subsection{An explicit direct limit of Galois-rich fields}

Define ordered additive subgroups of $\No$ by
\[
 \Omega_0=\R,\qquad
 \Omega_n=\R+\Q\omega+\Q\omega^2+\cdots+\Q\omega^n\quad(n\geq1),
 \qquad \Omega_\infty=\bigcup_{n\geq0}\Omega_n.
\]
All sums here are finite within an element of a group.

\begin{theorem}[A countable tower inside the surreal field]\label{bst:gr:thm:tower}
For $K=\R$ or $\C$,
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:towerinterlace}
 \FF K{\Omega_n}\subsetneq\HH K{\Omega_n}\subseteq\FF K{\Omega_{n+1}},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{bst:gr:eq:towerunion}
 \bigcup_{n\geq0}\FF K{\Omega_n}=\bigcup_{n\geq0}\HH K{\Omega_n}=\FF K{\Omega_\infty}.
\end{equation}
Every $\FF{\C}{\Omega_n}$ realizes every finite group as a Galois group, and
every finite extension of $\FF{\C}{\Omega_n}$ splits over
$\FF{\C}{\Omega_{n+1}}$. The union in~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:towerunion} is
algebraically closed for $K=\C$ and real closed for $K=\R$. It is properly
contained in $\HH K{\Omega_\infty}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The group $\Omega_n$ is divisible and has order unit $\omega^n$ (with
$\omega^0=1$). The exponent $\omega^{n+1}$ dominates all of $\Omega_n$. The
first inclusion in~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:towerinterlace} is strict because the
normalized root of $1-t^{\omega^n}$ belongs to $\HH K{\Omega_n}$ but not to
$\FF K{\Omega_n}$. The second inclusion follows from
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:absorption}. It already gives equality of the first
two unions in~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:towerunion}.

The group $\Omega_\infty$ has no order unit. Any support bounded above and
below in it is contained in some $\Omega_n$: choose $n$ large enough to
contain its two bounds. An exponent with a nonzero coefficient at a higher
power of $\omega$ would exceed one of those bounds in absolute order.
Conversely, the whole group $\Omega_n$ is bounded above by $\omega^{n+1}$ in
$\Omega_\infty$. Hence
\[
 \BB K{\Omega_\infty}=\bigcup_n\HH K{\Omega_n}.
\]
By Remark~\ref{bst:gr:rem:nounit} this bounded ring is its own fraction field,
proving~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:towerunion}. Closedness follows either from
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:closedness} or from the directed union of the closed
fields $\HH K{\Omega_n}$. Finite-group realization and splitting between
successive stages follow from Theorems~\ref{bst:gr:thm:products}
and~\ref{bst:gr:thm:allcollapse}.

Finally the Hahn series
\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}t^{\omega^n}
\]
has well-ordered support unbounded in $\Omega_\infty$. It belongs to
$\HH K{\Omega_\infty}$ but not to its bounded ring, which is the union
in~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:towerunion}.
\end{proof}

Under~\eqref{bst:eq:conway}, the last witness is the actual surreal
infinitesimal $\sum_{n\geq0}\omega^{-\omega^n}$. Thus a proper algebraically
closed subfield of $\No[i]$ can be the union of a countable increasing
sequence of fields, each of which has the full finite-group realization
property. The transition maps, rather than the abstract groups at the
individual stages, are decisive.

\textup{[merge]} The tower plays out, stage by stage, the enlargement of
Section~8.4. Its witness is transcendental, not only outside the union:
with $a_n=\omega^n$ the gap hypothesis of Corollary~\ref{bst:cor:onegap}
holds in $\Omega_\infty$ (every $w\in\Omega_\infty$ is below some
$N\omega^m$), so $\sum_nt^{\omega^n}$ is transcendental over
$\FF K{\Omega_\infty}$, and Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:independent} gives
$2^{\aleph_0}$ algebraically independent elements over this algebraically
closed union.

\subsection{Why the full omnific field has no contradiction}

Every set of surreal exponents has a surreal upper bound. For example, a
Conway cut with that set on its left gives a larger surreal number. Thus for
the full proper-class exponent group $\No$, every individual normal-form
support is upper bounded. The formal bounded-support construction then gives
the whole surreal or surcomplex field, with set-sized supports understood
throughout. \textup{[merge]} Section~8.4 makes the same observation; it is
printed once there, with this argument added here.

The classical identity $\Frac\Oz=\No$ is recovered by the same monomial-shift
argument; it is not a new theorem here \cite[Proposition~2.4.5 and its
discussion of Conway's Theorem~32]{LM24}. The nontrivial Galois phenomena
above live in the specified set-sized fraction subfields. It is never
asserted that $\No[i]$ itself has a nontrivial finite algebraic extension.

\section{Scope, verification and relations of the Galois part}\label{bst:gr:sec:scope}

\subsection{The role of divisibility}

The finite-slice lemma, local descent, finite-group realizations, radical
degrees, and uniform-denominator absorption hold for a general nonzero
ordered abelian group subject to the stated order-unit or new-scale
assumption. Divisibility enters the closedness dichotomy and the universal
splitting theorem because those arguments use algebraic or real closedness of
a full Hahn field.

Without divisibility, absence of an order unit does not by itself make the
full convex-subgroup Hahn fields algebraically closed. Similarly, absorption
of $\HH{\C}H$ need not absorb an algebraic closure of the old field if this
Hahn field is not algebraically closed. It still absorbs every explicitly
constructed extension contained in that Hahn field.

\subsection{Two unrelated senses of ramification}

The points $s=a^{-1}$ used in the radical proof are valuations of function
fields of complex algebraic curves. The Hahn valuation at $s=t^u$ instead
records the least exponent of a series. Every normalized radical
in~\eqref{bst:gr:eq:radical} has Hahn valuation $0$ and constant term $1$. Its
degree is nevertheless detected by ramification at the remote curve point
$a^{-1}$ before descent. This is not a contradiction; the two valuations have
different domains of geometric interpretation.

In particular, the prime ideals of $\AAG{\Z}{\R}G$, its integral closure, and
ramification in that ring have not been classified. Those are ring-theoretic
questions and require more than a function-field Galois group.

\subsection{Finite groups versus the absolute Galois group}

The Galois part gives a large explicit supply of quotients of
$\AbsGal{\FF{\C}G}$, and preserves a complete local Galois envelope. This does
not determine the entire absolute Galois group. Its cohomological dimension,
projectivity, freeness, and all finite embedding problems are not answered by
the present proofs. The exact cardinality calculation in
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:cardinality} must not be read as an isomorphism
classification.

\subsection{Support size and set theory}

The dichotomy does not use CH, GCH, a measurable cardinal, or another
large-cardinal axiom. The continuum bound in Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:products}
comes from choosing independent branch points on the ordinary complex line.
The results are not assertions that large cardinals have no effect on other
surreal structures. Nor do they classify birthday-bounded or cardinally
restricted Hahn fields. In a support-restricted setting, the formation of a
binomial series, a field inverse, or an algebraic closure must be checked
against the restriction.

\subsection{Novelty and verification}

The old descent theorem already implies the preservation of finite algebraic
degrees. Source~02 does not relabel that implication as an independent
breakthrough. Its proposed additional result is the systematic Galois theory
obtained from it, including arbitrary independent finite-group products,
explicit infinite radical groups, and uniform rank-transition splitting. The
elementary pieces make these claims checkable, but do not certify historical
priority. Its ledger and audit (Sections~\ref{bst:gr:subsec:ledger}
and~\ref{bst:gr:subsec:audit}) distinguish the source inputs from new
deductions.

audit, is kept; each also stands at its place above.

\subsection{Audit of possible failure points}\label{bst:gr:subsec:audit}

\emph{Upper bounds must be internal to the named group.} At an order unit,
the support $\{nu:n\geq0\}$ is unbounded in $G$, but becomes bounded after
adjoining a dominating $\theta$. Every use of boundedness specifies the
ambient group.

\emph{Coset projection uses both ends of a support.} A well-ordered support
has a least exponent. Upper boundedness supplies the other end. Both are used
to make a cyclic-coset slice finite. The result is false for arbitrary sets
without a lower bound.

\emph{Degrees require linear disjointness.} Finitely many denominators are
cleared before a linear relation is projected. No attempt is made to project
a quotient term by term. The full finite compositum is transported in the
independent-family arguments.

\emph{The whole local splitting field is embedded.} The finite cover is
unramified over $0$, and the residue field is $\C$. This makes its completion
the base Laurent series field. Embedding a single ramified branch would not
suffice for the Galois-group statement.

\emph{The branch sets include infinity in their accounting.} For the
arbitrary-product construction infinity is unramified in every cover. The
Kummer construction, which may have a common branch point at infinity,
instead uses its separate marked-point Eisenstein proof. These two
independence arguments are not interchangeable without checking their
hypotheses.

\emph{No independence from coefficient transcendence is used.} The radical
coefficients can all be algebraic for rational $a$. Their algebraic
obstruction comes from the support coordinate and function-field degree, not
from choosing transcendental coefficients.

\emph{Divisibility is used exactly at closedness.} The new group
$\Q\theta\oplus H$ is divisible when $H$ is. Full Hahn closedness then gives
the old algebraic closure that is absorbed. Without this hypothesis only the
extensions already present in the old Hahn field are automatically absorbed.

\emph{Infinite groups have their Krull topology.} All displayed infinite
product groups are topological isomorphisms, obtained from explicitly
compatible finite quotient maps. Field degree and group cardinality are
recorded separately.

\emph{The rank tower remains set-sized.} Each $\Omega_n$ and $\Omega_\infty$
is a set, so ordinary field and Galois theory applies. The closing discussion
of all of $\No$ is not used to define an unrestricted proper-class absolute
Galois group.

\emph{Formal verification is not inferred from a repository.} Although the
repository contains Lean formalizations, source~02 was not checked by Lean
and no repository build was performed in its preparation. Finite exact
calculations are not promoted to proofs of infinite statements.

\section{Research questions from the Galois part}\label{bst:gr:sec:questions}

The following are proposed research problems motivated by the proved results

\begin{question}[The complete absolute Galois group]\label{bst:gr:q:absolute}
Determine $\AbsGal{\FF{\C}G}$ for a divisible order-unit group $G$, beginning
with $G=\Q$ and $G=\R$. Is its isomorphism type controlled by the top
Archimedean quotient, by the lower convex subgroups, or by finer support
structure? A first useful invariant would be its cohomological dimension.
Realizing every finite group and having cardinality $2^{\cc}$ do not answer
this question.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[The kernel outside the local envelope]\label{bst:gr:q:kernel}
Study the restriction epimorphism
\[
 \AbsGal{\FF{\C}G}\longrightarrow\Gal(\mathcal E_0/\C(s))
\]
from Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:envelope}. Describe its kernel and compare the
images coming from different order units. Existing results about fundamental
groups of complex curves may identify the target more explicitly, but they do
not automatically identify the kernel.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Finite embedding problems]\label{bst:gr:q:embedding}
Given a finite quotient of $\AbsGal{\FF{\C}G}$ and an epimorphism onto that
quotient from another finite group, determine when a compatible proper
solution exists. The construction by disjoint branch loci solves independent
product problems, not arbitrary prescribed compatibility problems. A useful
intermediate target is an embedding problem entirely contained in the local
envelope.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[The relative algebraic closure inside the Hahn field]\label{bst:gr:q:relclosure}
Give an intrinsic support description of the elements of $\HH{\C}G$ algebraic
over $\FF{\C}G$. The local algebraic series in $t^u$ provide many examples,
but the coefficients of a polynomial over the fraction field may involve
infinitely many cosets. A description using only a single local coordinate is
not justified by source~02.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Minimal enlargement for a prescribed root]\label{bst:gr:q:minimal}
For $f\in\HH KG$, classify the smallest ordered group extensions in which $f$
becomes a fraction of canonical omnific integers. A dominating element always
suffices, but may be much more than is needed for a particular support. An
exact criterion for finite families of algebraic branches would sharpen the
uniform denominator construction.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Integral closure and arithmetic ramification]\label{bst:gr:q:integral}
Let $E/\FF{\C}G$ be one of the explicit cyclic or symmetric extensions.
Determine the integral closure of $\AAG{\Z[i]}{\C}G$ in $E$, and identify the
primes at which this ring extension ramifies. The curve valuations that prove
independence are an input, not an already established classification of
omnific prime ideals.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Real realization with prescribed orderings]\label{bst:gr:q:real}
Beyond the multiquadratic and generalized-dihedral families, which finite
Galois groups can be realized inside $\HH{\R}G$ over $\FF{\R}G$? How many
orderings extend the base Hahn order? Real covers with carefully chosen split
real fibers should give an approach, but complex realization alone does not
prove a real theorem.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Nondivisible groups and positive characteristic]\label{bst:gr:q:charp}
Separate the contribution of missing divisibility from the contribution of an
order unit. In positive characteristic, replace the radical towers by suitable
Artin--Schreier or Witt towers and analyze wild ramification. The complex
covering argument is not available unchanged, and a naive
characteristic-zero transcription would be invalid.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Cardinally restricted supports]\label{bst:gr:q:cardinal}
Repeat the constructions in fields of Hahn series with fewer than $\kappa$
terms. Which steps are preserved by regular uncountable $\kappa$, and what
happens at singular cardinals? It is necessary to check both algebraic
closure operations and the supports of all coefficients appearing in a finite
cover. Counting the displayed radicals alone is not sufficient.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Functorial rank transitions]\label{bst:gr:q:functorial}
Develop a category of exponent groups together with the induced fraction
fields and finite \'etale algebras. A dominating extension sends every finite
\'etale algebra from the old divisible complex stage to a split algebra.
Theorem~\ref{bst:gr:thm:transition} settles the binary behavior for ordered
exponent-group inclusions. Which more general embeddings of these fraction
fields can exhibit intermediate behavior, splitting some finite extensions but
preserving others? Distinguishing monomial embeddings from general field
embeddings is essential here.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Effective computation and certificates]\label{bst:gr:q:effective}
Build algorithms that take a polynomial with a split simple fiber and produce
certified local series, degree information, and the resulting Hahn-field
embedding. Explicit monodromy or group certificates would be needed for a
general finite cover. Such an algorithm should not claim to decide equality of
arbitrary surreal presentations.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Formalization of the support-to-Galois bridge]\label{bst:gr:q:formal}
Formalize the finite-slice lemma and the tensor injectivity proof before
attempting the geometric existence theorem. This gives a useful theorem in any
library with Hahn series, ordered groups, and field extensions. A second stage
can formalize the explicit radical families and rank absorption entirely
algebraically. The Riemann-existence input can then be isolated behind an
exact theorem interface rather than silently assumed by the code.
\end{question}

\section{Normalized GCD descent and a published question}\label{bst:sec:gcd}

This appendix is logically independent of the transcendence theorems. It
provides a full proof of a conditional reduction rather than importing a
recent claimed result as an established premise.

\subsection{The input and the conclusion}

For a characteristic-zero field $K$, let $\overline K$ be an algebraic
closure. Consider the hypothesis
\begin{equation*}\tag{GCD$_{\R,\overline K}$}\label{bst:hyp:gcd}
 \BB{\overline K}{\R}\text{ is a GCD domain}.
\end{equation*}
A GCD domain is an integral domain in which every pair has a greatest common
divisor, understood up to units. The existence of a GCD for a finite
nonempty family follows by induction. By
Proposition~\ref{bst:prop:supimport}, the units of the rank-one bounded-support
ring are exactly the nonzero monomials.

For a nonzero bounded series define
\begin{equation}\label{bst:eq:normalize}
 N(f)=\lc(f)^{-1}t^{-v(f)}f.
\end{equation}
It has least exponent zero and coefficient $1$ there. Two nonzero bounded
series are associates precisely when their normalizations agree.

\begin{theorem}[Normalized GCD descent]\label{bst:thm:gcddescent}
Assume \eqref{bst:hyp:gcd}. For every nonzero ordered subgroup $H\subseteq\R$,
$\BB KH$ is a GCD domain. More precisely, the normalized GCD in
$\BB{\overline K}{\R}$ of any finite family from $\BB KH$, not all zero,
belongs to $\BB KH$ and is a GCD there. No divisibility or completeness
assumption on $H$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Characters separate exponent cosets}

\begin{lemma}\label{bst:lem:characters}
Let $A$ be an abelian group and $L$ an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero. For every $0\ne a\in A$, there is a character
$\chi:A\to L^\times$ with $\chi(a)\ne1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the cyclic group generated by $a$, choose a primitive $m$th root of
unity as the image of $a$ if it has finite order $m>1$, and choose $2$ if
it has infinite order. In either case the resulting character separates
$a$ from zero.

We explain why this character extends. The abelian group $L^\times$ is
divisible, because every nonzero scalar has an $n$th root. Suppose a
character has been defined on a subgroup $D\subseteq A$, and choose
$x\notin D$. If no nonzero integer multiple of $x$ lies in $D$, give $x$
any nonzero image. Otherwise the integers $n$ with $nx\in D$ form $m\Z$
for a least positive $m$. Choose $y\in L^\times$ with
$y^m=\chi(mx)$, and set
\[
 \widetilde\chi(d+nx)=\chi(d)y^n.
\]
If $d+nx=d'+n'x$, then $(n-n')x=d'-d\in D$.
In the nontrivial case $n-n'=km$, so
$\chi(d')=\chi(d)\chi(mx)^k=\chi(d)y^{n-n'}$ and the assigned values
agree. In the other case the representation is unique. The formula is
multiplicative with respect to addition, so it is an extension.
Order the extensions by domain inclusion. Their domains are subgroups of
the set $A$, and the union along a chain is again a character extending
the given one. Zorn's lemma gives a maximal extension; the preceding
one-element construction forces its domain to be all of $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:gcddescent}]
Let $d$ be the normalized ambient GCD of $f_1,\ldots,f_m\in\BB KH$.
For every character $\chi:\R/H\to\overline K^\times$, define
\[
 T_\chi\left(\sum_r a_rt^r\right)
       =\sum_r a_r\chi(r+H)t^r.
\]
This is an automorphism of the Hahn field and of its bounded-support
subring: it preserves support and its multiplicativity follows at each
finite convolution coefficient. Its inverse uses $\chi^{-1}$. It takes
normalized series to normalized series, since the coefficient at exponent
zero remains $1$. It fixes every $f_j$. Consequently $T_\chi(d)$ is another
normalized GCD of the same family. Uniqueness of normalization implies
$T_\chi(d)=d$.

If an exponent $r\notin H$ occurred in $d$, Lemma~\ref{bst:lem:characters}
would supply a character with $\chi(r+H)\ne1$. Its nonzero coefficient
would then change, contradicting the equality. Hence
$\supp(d)\subseteq H$.

Next every automorphism in $\Aut(\overline K/K)$ acts coefficientwise and
again preserves normalization and fixes the input family. Thus it fixes
every coefficient of $d$. In characteristic zero the fixed field of all
these automorphisms is $K$. Indeed, an algebraic element outside $K$ has
a separable minimal polynomial with another conjugate; an embedding taking
it to that conjugate extends to an automorphism of the algebraic closure.
Therefore every coefficient of $d$ lies in $K$. Its support is bounded in
$\R$ and lies in the nonzero subgroup $H$, which is cofinal in $\R$;
it is bounded in $H$. Thus $d\in\BB KH$.

Each quotient $f_j/d$ belongs to $\HH KH$ because this is a field. It is
bounded in the ambient ring by divisibility of the ambient GCD, so
cofinality makes it bounded in $H$. Hence $d$ divides every $f_j$ in the
base ring. If $e\in\BB KH$ is any common divisor, then $e\ne0$ because at least
one input is nonzero. Ambient universality gives $e\mid d$ there. The quotient $d/e$ is again in $\HH KH$ and ambient
bounded, hence in $\BB KH$. This proves base-ring universality. The
all-zero family can separately be assigned GCD zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Relation to the pre-Schreier question}

L'Innocente and Mantova ask, immediately after their Example~9.1.3, whether
$\BB KH$ is pre-Schreier for a divisible Archimedean group $H$
\cite{LM24}. Since a GCD domain is pre-Schreier, Theorem~\ref{bst:thm:gcddescent}
gives a positive answer \emph{conditional on} \eqref{bst:hyp:gcd}.
The latter hypothesis also follows if the one-sided ring
$\overline K((t^{\R^{\le0}}))$ is a GCD domain, since
Proposition~\ref{bst:prop:localdensity} identifies its monomial localization
with $\BB{\overline K}{\R}$, and localization preserves the GCD property.
These two general commutative-algebra implications are standard; the new
argument in this appendix is the normalized character descent.

There is a further conditional consequence at arbitrary divisible rank.
Suppose the real-exponent GCD input holds over the algebraic closure of
\emph{every} characteristic-zero coefficient field. If $G$ has no order
unit, its bounded-support ring is already a field. If it has an order
unit, Proposition~\ref{bst:prop:toprank} writes its bounded ring as
$\BB MH$ with $H\subseteq\R$ and $M=\HH KC$. Applying the descent theorem
to this coefficient field proves that $\BB KG$ is GCD. The required
ambient hypothesis is now about $\BB{\overline M}{\R}$, where
$M=\HH KC$; an input only for $\overline K$ would not suffice. Thus a universal
rank-one input would settle bounded-support GCD arithmetic at every
divisible rank.

\begin{warning}[Recent external claim, not a premise of the main results]
The source audit records that Dan Abramov's public 2026 project
\texttt{gaearon/conway-refinement} advertised a Lean proof that the one-sided real-exponent Hahn ring is GCD
\cite{Abramov}. Its standalone statement

The source audit read the statement and README but did not

explicit hypothesis. It does not claim an independent resolution of

\end{warning}

\subsection{Proof audit: the points most vulnerable to mistakes}

The proofs depend on several distinctions that deserve an explicit final
check. The base is the fraction field of bounded-support series, so every
putative relation must first have its denominators cleared. At limit
indices the earlier partial series are not finite; the proof uses bounded
support, not finite support. A top homogeneous polynomial need not be
nonzero on an arbitrary coefficient sequence, which is why the coding
lemma realizes a complete positive-integer grid cofinally. The coding
family has size $2^\kappa$ but uses only $\kappa$ positions; finite patterns,
not arbitrary infinite patterns, are what it realizes.

Coset projection is not a ring homomorphism; it is linear over the Hahn
field on the subgroup. Likewise the coefficient functional is not a field
homomorphism; it is linear over the smaller coefficient field. Cofinality
is exactly what makes their output coefficients bounded in the smaller
group. For units, upper-support multiplicativity is used only after
passing to an Archimedean quotient. For concrete surreal examples, the
monomial map is Conway's $x\mapsto\omega^x$, and the Hahn valuation sign
is reversed in \eqref{bst:eq:conway}. Finally, a maximal-cardinality independent
family is not claimed to be a transcendence basis.

\subsection{What the deliverable establishes}

The unconditional core is a collection of relative algebraic theorems
with detailed proofs and explicit witnesses, including the sharp support
threshold, coefficient--exponent descent, and arbitrarily large actual
surreal examples. Their mathematical validity can be assessed directly
from the given arguments and the named standard foundations. Their
publication novelty requires further expert literature review. The
optional GCD reduction has an additional stated hypothesis and should not
be promoted into an unconditional open-problem solution by silently
accepting an unverified external claim.
