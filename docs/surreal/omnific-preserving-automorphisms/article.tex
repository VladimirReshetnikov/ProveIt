\begin{abstract}
Which symmetries of a Hahn field preserve its omnific-type integer part, and
what does that integer part remember? Let $k$ have characteristic zero, let
$\Gamma\ne0$ be a divisible ordered abelian group, write $C_\delta$ for the
principal convex subgroup generated by $\delta>0$, and let $\oo\subseteq k$ be
a unital subring. A strong coefficient-fixing $1$-automorphism $\sigma$ of
$\Hahn k\Gamma$ preserves $\oo\oplus\Pi$, where $\Pi$ is the purely infinite
ideal, exactly when, for every $\delta>0$, the shift-$\delta$ coefficient of
its logarithmic character $\log(t^{-g}\sigma(t^g))$ vanishes on $C_\delta$.
The proof uses no exponential--logarithm correspondence and applies verbatim
to the surreal field and $\Oz$. Where that correspondence is available, the
same condition for the operator logarithm $\Log\sigma$ is equivalent; the
two coefficient functionals are different.

Consequences: the stabilizer is trivial exactly in Archimedean rank one; its
common fixed field is the Hahn field on the bottom convex subgroup, and for
$(\No,\Oz)$ it is $\R$; in finite rank $r$ its derived length is at most
$r-1$, with equality for $\Q^r$; explicit successive-scale flows generate a
faithful $\Z\wr\Z$; for every set of surreal parameters the pointwise
stabilizer is nonsolvable and carries a faithful action of $\R^\kappa$ for
every set cardinal $\kappa$. Consequently no set of parameters makes the
Conway monomials, the omega-map or any valuation-representative rule
definable in $(\No,+,\cdot,<,\Oz)$, although the pair defines the three
zero-cut projections and the floor map. A second part proves
algebraic-parameter rigidity of $\Oz$: embeddings into polynomial, Laurent and
finite-type domains land in the coefficients, strong cancellation holds, and
$\ML(\Oz[T_1,\dots,T_n])=\Oz$, while explicit commuting derivations integrate
to formal flows that no polynomial family realizes.

This report merges four independently written manuscripts, prints shared
results once with every source named, and keeps every source's limitations.
It is an AI-assisted draft: it has not been refereed, and nothing in it has
been formalized in Lean.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, results, and conventions}\label{opa:sec:intro}

A Conway normal form carries three kinds of information: coefficients,
exponents, and the monomials that represent the exponents. The omnific
integers keep the purely infinite part of a normal form and an ordinary
integer constant coefficient. This report asks how much of the normal-form
presentation is determined by the surreal field together with this
distinguished ring, and which automorphisms preserve it.

The answer is asymmetric. The coefficient field, the cut between negative
and nonnegative Hahn exponents, the constant coefficient and the floor map
are recoverable from the pair $(\No,\Oz)$; much of this was already proved in
the omnific Diophantine report (\cref{opa:sec:remember}). The monomial
cross-section is not recoverable. Even an arbitrary \emph{set} of named
parameters leaves enough automorphisms to move a monomial to an infinite
series and to defeat every definable choice of valuation representatives.
The mechanism is a family of derivations that change a higher Archimedean
scale by a lower one without ever moving a purely infinite term across the
constant-term boundary. \Cref{opa:part:aut} classifies these
automorphisms and derives the consequences. \Cref{opa:part:par}, from a
separate manuscript, shows the complementary rigidity: $\Oz$ admits no
nonconstant \emph{algebraic} family of embeddings, although it admits
formal flows of all orders.

\subsection{Principal results}\label{opa:sub:results}

Bracketed numbers name the source manuscripts (\cref{opa:sub:provenance});
\mergetag{} marks statements assembled or generalized by the merge, each with
a complete proof. The Hahn conventions are fixed in
\cref{opa:sub:notation}: $K=\Hahn k\Gamma$ with well-ordered supports,
$t^\gamma=\omega^{-\gamma}$ on the surreals, $\Pi$ the series supported in
$\Gamma^{<0}$, $\Ak=k\oplus\Pi$ and $\Ro=\oo\oplus\Pi$, so that
$\mathcal R_\Z=\Oz$ when $k=\R$ and $\Gamma=\No$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{The convex-support criterion} \src{04, 09}\mergetag. For
every characteristic-zero $k$, every unital $\oo\subseteq k$, every nonzero
divisible $\Gamma$ (set-sized, or $\Gamma=\No$), and every strong
$k$-linear $1$-automorphism $\sigma$: $\sigma(\Ro)=\Ro$ if and only if the
logarithmic-character coefficients $a_{\sigma,\delta}$ vanish on $C_\delta$
for every $\delta>0$, if and only if $\sigma$ commutes with the three
zero-cut projections (\cref{opa:thm:criterion}). The proof is 04's and
imports no exponential--logarithm correspondence. For contracting strong
derivations the analogous criterion holds with 09's coefficients $c_\delta$
(\cref{opa:thm:derivation}); where the correspondence is available the
operator form follows (\cref{opa:cor:operator}). The functionals
$a_{\sigma,\delta}$ and $c_\delta$ differ; their vanishing conditions are
equivalent (\cref{opa:ex:functionals}). For single fixed-shift flows 08
proves the criterion without divisibility (\cref{opa:thm:threshold}).
\item \textbf{Rank and fixed fields} \src{04, 08, 09}. The stabilizer
$\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)$ is trivial exactly when $\Gamma$ is Archimedean
(\cref{opa:thm:rank}); for set-sized $\Gamma$ its common fixed field is the
Hahn field on the bottom convex subgroup (\cref{opa:thm:bottom}); and
$\Fix(\UU_\Oz)=\R$, also for all field automorphisms of $\No$ preserving $\Oz$
(\cref{opa:thm:fixed}). For every set $A$ of parameters the elementary shears
fixing $A$ have common fixed field $\Hahn\R{V_A}$, $V_A$ the rational span of
the supports (\cref{opa:thm:hull}).
\item \textbf{Group structure} \src{04, 08, 09}. In finite ordered rank $r$
the stabilizer and its Lie algebra have derived length at most $r-1$, with
equality for $\Q^r$ (\cref{opa:thm:derivedrank,opa:thm:sharp}). Two
successive-scale flows generate a faithful $\Z\wr\Z$ with exact commutator
leading terms (\cref{opa:thm:commutator,opa:thm:wreath}). For every set $P$
of surreal parameters, the pointwise stabilizer of $P$ in $\UU_\Oz$ carries a
faithful action of the full product $\R^\kappa$ for every set cardinal
$\kappa$ and is nonsolvable (\cref{opa:thm:product,opa:thm:nonsolvable}).
\item \textbf{What the pair remembers} \src{08, 09}. Beyond the
reconstruction package of the Diophantine report, the three zero-cut
projections $P_-,P_0,P_+$ on all of $\No$, the floor map and truncation at a
named monomial are parameter-free definable in $(\No,\Oz)$, and every field
automorphism preserving $\Oz$ commutes with them, without strongness
(\cref{opa:thm:projections,opa:cor:allautos}).
\item \textbf{What it forgets} \src{04, 08, 09}. For every set $P$ of
parameters, some strong $1$-automorphism preserving $\Oz$ fixes $P$
pointwise and moves a Conway monomial to an infinite series; so neither the
monomial class nor the omega-map is $P$-definable
(\cref{opa:thm:parameters}), and no $P$-definable rule selects a
representative of each natural-valuation class (\cref{opa:thm:nosection}).
\item \textbf{Surcomplex consequences} \src{04, 08, 09}. The criterion holds
for $\Og$; real shears commute with conjugation; an imaginary-time shear
fixing any set moves the real axis inside the leading-term kernel
(\cref{opa:thm:realaxis}); a wild coefficient lift moves an ordinary real
(\cref{opa:prop:wild}); and the common fixed field of all automorphisms
preserving $\Og$ is $\Q$ (\cref{opa:cor:gaussianfixed}).
\item \textbf{Algebraic-parameter rigidity} \src{02}. Every nonzero element
of $\Oz$ or $\Og$ divides a monomial whose roots of all orders lie in the
ring (\cref{opa:par:cor:ozcover}). Consequently injective homomorphisms into
polynomial and Laurent rings over a domain land in the coefficients
(\cref{opa:par:thm:poly,opa:par:thm:laurent}), polynomial cylinders over
$\Oz$ recover $\Oz$ under every isomorphism
(\cref{opa:par:thm:cancellation}), $\ML(\Oz[\mathbf T])=\Oz$
(\cref{opa:par:thm:ML}), and embeddings of set-sized root-covered rings into
finite-type domains land in a finite field of algebraic constants
(\cref{opa:par:thm:finitetype}). Commuting derivations of $\Oz$ integrate to
formal flows of all orders that no polynomial family realizes, with an exact
transcendence degree for their monomial orbit fields
(\cref{opa:par:thm:nonalg,opa:par:thm:orbitfield}).
\end{enumerate}

These statements are proved here, but proving a statement here is not a claim
that nobody proved it earlier. The general theory of strong Hahn-field
automorphisms and of the exponential--derivation correspondence is prior
work (\cref{opa:sub:imported}); each source names its most distinctive
contributions, and no exhaustive priority claim is made
(\cref{opa:app:nonclaims}).

\subsection{Imported results}\label{opa:sub:imported}

Conway normal forms, real closedness of $\No$ and the omnific integers are
classical \cite{Conway,Gonshor}; factorization theory of $\Oz$ is due to
L'Innocente and Mantova \cite{LM}. The monomial/character/kernel
decomposition of valued Hahn automorphisms is due to Kuhlmann and Serra
\cite{KS}, and its surreal form, with the class-size conventions used here, to
Kaplan, Krapp and Serra \cite{KKS}. The collection's surcomplex automorphism
report (\fn{docs/surcomplex/surcomplex-field-automorphisms}) already
constructs the fixed-shift derivations and flows used below, in greater
generality (its \lbl{saut:thm:shiftflow}), and the four-layer decomposition
of valued automorphisms (\lbl{saut:thm:decomp}).

The correspondence between contracting strong derivations and strong
$1$-automorphisms is due to Bagayoko, Krapp, Kuhlmann, Panazzolo and Serra.

\begin{fact}[{\cite[Theorem 3.13]{BKKPS}; \cite[Fact 4.2]{KKS}}]\label{opa:fact:import}
Let $k$ have characteristic zero and $\Gamma$ be an ordered abelian group.
If $\partial$ is a contracting strongly $k$-linear derivation of
$\Hahn k\Gamma$, then
\[
 \Exp(\partial)(f)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{\partial^n(f)}{n!}
\]
is a strongly $k$-linear $1$-automorphism, and $\partial\mapsto\Exp(\partial)$
is a bijection between contracting strongly $k$-linear derivations and
strongly $k$-linear $1$-automorphisms, with inverse
$\Log(\sigma)=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n+1}(\sigma-\id)^n/n$. Composition
corresponds to the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff operation.
\end{fact}

\begin{remark}[What is proved where]\label{opa:rem:import}
\cite{BKKPS} proves the correspondence for set-sized $\Gamma$. Source 09
attributes the surreal case to \cite[Fact 4.2]{KKS}; the placement dossier,
reading an HTML rendering, doubted that this Fact states the bijection. The
merge re-read the PDF of \cite{KKS} (version~3): Fact~4.2 does state the
bijection, cites \cite[Theorem 3.13]{BKKPS}, and adds that the correspondence
``was established in the case that $G$ is a set, but it also holds for $G$ a
proper class'', without a separate proof. So 09's citation is accurate, and
for $\Gamma=\No$ the operator-logarithm statements rest on that assertion.
The criterion itself does not: its proof (\cref{opa:thm:criterion}) and 04's
correspondence on the common-positive-shift class (\cref{opa:thm:lie}) are
complete and elementwise, and the explicit constructions on $\No$ use one
set-sized well-ordered set of shifts (\cref{opa:prop:uniform}). Every
statement below that uses \cref{opa:fact:import} says so.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Class size and foundations}\label{opa:sub:foundations}

Unless $\Gamma=\No$ is explicitly under discussion, exponent groups are sets,
so fields and automorphism groups are ordinary sets. For $\Gamma=\No$ we work
in NBG with global choice \src{04, 08, 09}; every individual Hahn support and
every summation family is a set. An automorphism of $\No$ is an individual
class map; no set or NBG class whose members are all such proper-class
graphs is formed. A statement about $\UU_\Oz$ is a statement about individual
maps, their compositions, or a set-indexed action encoded by one class
relation; this is the convention of \cite{KKS}. A common-fixed-field
statement is pointwise; nonsolvability means that no ordinary finite derived
length annihilates all iterated commutators; valuation representatives are
treated through an equivalence relation on nonzero elements, not through
proper-class cosets \src{09}. A quotient notation $G/H$ in a full-class
application abbreviates an additive map on $G$ vanishing on $H$ \src{08}.
Bases are chosen only for set-sized vector spaces
(\cref{opa:lem:separation}).

\subsection{Notation and conventions}\label{opa:sub:notation}

\Cref{opa:part:aut} uses the small-$t$ Hahn convention of sources 04 and 09.
\Cref{opa:part:par} keeps its source's large-monomial convention, which is
the convention of the other omnific reports; see \cref{opa:par:sub:conv}.
For actual surreals
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:sign}
 t^\gamma=\omega^{-\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Thus negative $t$-exponents are purely infinite terms and positive ones are
infinitesimal. The tempting false reading is the opposite: in this Part,
$\Pi$ is supported at \emph{negative} $t$-exponents, and a ``positive shift''
$\delta>0$ moves a term towards the infinitesimal side. Source 08 wrote in the
$\omega$-convention with decreasing supports; its statements have been
translated, and its derivation $D_{\varphi,\delta}$ with
$D_{\varphi,\delta}(\omega^g)=\varphi(g)\omega^{g-\delta}$ is
$D_{\delta,\theta}$ below with $\theta(\gamma)=\varphi(-\gamma)=-\varphi(\gamma)$.

parameter monomial $H=\omega^\eta$ and its family $H_j$ are written
$\eta=\omega^\beta$ and $\eta_j$ here, leaving $H_g$ to 04's convex
subgroup; and 08's automorphisms $A_1,B_1$ are written in 04's normalization
$A_s,B_u$, with $A_1^{(08)}=A_{-1}$ and $B_1^{(08)}=B_{-1}$
(\cref{opa:sec:group}). A $1$-automorphism is a field automorphism that
preserves the valuation and the leading coefficient of every nonzero series.
\emph{Strong} means that every Hahn-summable family is sent to a summable
family with the same sum; a strong automorphism is one whose inverse is also
strong (04's convention); \emph{$k$-linear} includes fixing $k$ pointwise.

\part{Automorphisms preserving the omnific integers}\label{opa:part:aut}

\section{Hahn support, the boundary ring, and strong maps}\label{opa:sec:prelim}

\subsection{Summability}

An element of $K=\Hahn k\Gamma$ is a formal sum $f=\sum_{\gamma\in S}a_\gamma
t^\gamma$ with $S\subseteq\Gamma$ well ordered. A set-indexed family
$(f_i)_{i\in I}$ is \emph{Hahn summable} if the union of its supports is well
ordered and each exponent lies in only finitely many of the supports; its sum
is formed coefficientwise with finite sums in $k$. No convergence in the fine
topology of the surreals is involved; a strong sum is a support-controlled
algebraic construction \src{04, 08, 09}.

We use the Neumann support principle in its word form: if $S$ is well ordered
and $T\subseteq\Gamma^{>0}$ is well ordered, then $S+\langle T\rangle_\N$ is well
ordered, and each exponent receives only finitely many contributions from a
choice of an element of $S$ and a finite word in $T$, counted across all word
lengths \cite{BKKPS}. The one-shift case is proved directly.

\begin{lemma}[One-shift summation \src{04, 08}]\label{opa:lem:shift}
If $T\subseteq\Gamma$ is well ordered and $\delta>0$, then $T+\N\delta$ is well
ordered, and each $\gamma\in\Gamma$ has only finitely many representations
$\gamma=g+n\delta$ with $g\in T$ and $n\in\N$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An infinite strictly decreasing sequence $g_j+n_j\delta$ has an infinite
subsequence on which the $n_j$ are nondecreasing; on it the $g_j$ strictly
decrease, contradicting well-ordering of $T$. Infinitely many representations
of one $\gamma$ would have unbounded, hence eventually increasing, values of
$n$, and the corresponding $g=\gamma-n\delta$ would strictly decrease in $T$.
\end{proof}

For $h\in\mm$ the series $\exp(h)=\sum_{n\ge0}h^n/n!$ and
$\log(1+h)=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n+1}h^n/n$ are Hahn summable, and they are
inverse group isomorphisms between $(\mm,+)$ and $(1+\mm,\cdot)$. This local
exponential is the only exponential used in the criterion; neither $\exp$ nor
$\Exp$ asserts anything about the Gonshor exponential of $\No$.

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient polynomial \src{04}]\label{opa:lem:polynomial}
For $h\in\mm$ and $\delta>0$ the expression
$P_{h,\delta}(X)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{X^n}{n!}\coef\delta{h^n}$ is a polynomial
over $k$. For every $q\in\Q$ one has $P_{h,\delta}(q)=\coef\delta{\exp(qh)}$,
and its coefficient of $X$ is $\coef\delta h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The support principle gives only finitely many contributions to the exponent
$\delta$ across all powers $h^n$, not merely within each power. So only
finitely many displayed terms are nonzero. The rest is coefficientwise.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The rings and the zero cut}

The additive decompositions
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:decomp}
 K=\Pi\oplus k\oplus\mm,\qquad \Ak=k\oplus\Pi,\qquad \Ro=\oo\oplus\Pi,
 \qquad \OO=k\oplus\mm
\end{equation}
define the projections $P_-,P_0,P_+$ \src{04, 09}. Membership in $\Pi$ means
that \emph{all} exponents are negative, which is stronger than $v(f)<0$: the
series $t^{-1}+t$ has negative valuation and is not in $\Pi$ \src{04}. $\Pi$ is
a nonunital ideal of $\Ak$ and of $\Ro$; $\Ak$ is not a valuation ring; $P_0$ is
a ring retraction $\Ak\to k$ restricting to $\Ro\to\oo$, but not a ring map on
$K$: $P_0(t^{-1})P_0(t)=0\ne1=P_0(1)$. For $k=\R$, $\oo=\Z$ the ring $\mathcal
R_\Z$ is an integer part of $K$; for $\Gamma=\No$ it is $\Oz$ and its floor
function is the Diophantine report's \lbl{odg:thm:floor}.

The units $\Ak^\times=k^\times$, and more generally the fact that a product of
two elements of $\Ak$ lying in $k^\times$ has constant factors, are the
Diophantine report's constant-product lemma (\lbl{odg:lem:constantproduct},
\lbl{odg:def:lem:units}); sources 04, 08 and 09 reprove it, and it is not
reprinted.

\begin{lemma}[The divisible part \src{04, 08}]\label{opa:lem:divisible}
If $\bigcap_{n\ge1}n\oo=0$ (for example $\oo=\Z$ or $\Z[i]$), then
$\Pi=\bigcap_{n\ge1}n\Ro$, the largest divisible subgroup of $\Ro$; it is
preserved by every ring automorphism of $\Ro$. This holds for every ordered
abelian $\Gamma$, divisible or not.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A series in $\Pi$ is $n$ times a series with the same support. Conversely the
constant coefficient of an element of the intersection lies in
$\bigcap_n n\oo=0$. Every divisible subgroup lies in the intersection.
\end{proof}

For $\Oz$ this is the identity \lbl{odg:eq:divkernel} of the Diophantine
report; it is printed because the threshold theorem uses it
for non-divisible $\Gamma$.

\begin{definition}[Principal convex scale]\label{opa:def:convex}
For $\delta>0$ let $C_\delta=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:|\gamma|\le n\delta\text{ for some }
n\ge1\}$, and write $\delta\bb\epsilon$ if $n\delta<\epsilon$ for all $n\ge1$.
Then $\gamma\notin C_\delta$ if and only if $\delta\bb|\gamma|$;
$C_\delta\subsetneq C_\epsilon$ if and only if $\delta\bb\epsilon$; and
$C_\delta$ is divisible when $\Gamma$ is. For $g\ne0$ the set
$H_g=\{h:n|h|<|g|\text{ for all }n\ge1\}$ is a convex subgroup, and a finite
sum of positive elements of $H_g$ is still smaller than $|g|$ \src{04}.
\end{definition}

\section{The convex-support criterion}\label{opa:sec:criterion}

\subsection{Two coefficient functionals}

Let $\sigma$ be a strong $k$-linear $1$-automorphism. Since $t^{-g}\sigma(t^g)$
has leading term $1$, its local logarithm is defined \src{04}:
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:logchar}
 h_\sigma(g)=\log\bigl(t^{-g}\sigma(t^g)\bigr)\in\mm,\qquad
 a_{\sigma,\delta}(g)=\coef\delta{h_\sigma(g)}\quad(\delta>0),
\end{equation}
so that $\sigma(t^g)=t^g\exp(h_\sigma(g))$. For a contracting strong
$k$-derivation $\partial$ (that is, $v(\partial f)>v(f)$ for $f\ne0$) put
\src{09}
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:opcoef}
 c_\delta(\gamma)=\coef{\gamma+\delta}{\partial(t^\gamma)}\quad(\delta>0),
 \qquad\text{so}\qquad
 \partial(t^\gamma)=t^\gamma\sum_{\delta>0}c_\delta(\gamma)t^\delta .
\end{equation}
For fixed $\gamma$ the nonzero terms have well-ordered set support; no
assumption is made that all shifts together form one well-ordered set.

\begin{lemma}[Additivity \src{04, 09}]\label{opa:lem:additive}
The maps $h_\sigma:\Gamma\to(\mm,+)$, $a_{\sigma,\delta}$ and $c_\delta$ are
additive, hence $\Q$-linear when $\Gamma$ is divisible. If $\Gamma$ is divisible
and an additive $\theta:\Gamma\to k$ vanishes on $(-\delta,0)$, it vanishes on
$C_\delta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The quotient at $g+g'$ is the product of the quotients at $g$ and $g'$, and
$\log$ takes products to sums. For $c_\delta$ the product rule gives
$t^{-\gamma-\eta}\partial(t^{\gamma+\eta})=t^{-\gamma}\partial(t^\gamma)
+t^{-\eta}\partial(t^\eta)$. For the last claim, given $0\ne h\in C_\delta$
choose $N$ with $|h|/N<\delta$; one of $\pm h/N$ lies in $(-\delta,0)$, and
additivity gives $\theta(h)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The functionals differ \mergetag]\label{opa:ex:functionals}
Let $\Gamma=\No$, $k=\R$, and
$\partial=D_{1,q_1}+D_{\omega,q_2}$ (notation of
\cref{opa:sub:notation}; the $t$-exponent $\gamma$ has $q_1(\gamma)$ and
$q_2(\gamma)$ as its coefficients at $\omega$ and $\omega^2$). Take
$\sigma=\Exp(\partial)$, an element of $\UU_\Oz$ by
\cref{opa:prop:uniform}, and $g=\omega^2$. Then
$\partial(t^g)=t^{g+\omega}$ and
$\partial^2(t^g)=t^{g+\omega+1}+t^{g+2\omega}$, and the only word in the
shifts $\{1,\omega\}$ reaching the exponent $\omega+1$ applies the shift
$\omega$ first; $\log(1+u)=u-u^2/2+\cdots$ adds nothing at $\omega+1$, since
$u^2$ starts at $2\omega$. Hence
\[
 t^{-g}\sigma(t^g)=1+t^\omega+\tfrac12t^{\omega+1}+(\text{terms at exponents}>\omega+1),
 \qquad a_{\sigma,\omega+1}(\omega^2)=\tfrac12,
\]
whereas $c_{\omega+1}(\omega^2)=0$: the operator coefficients of $\partial$ at
$g$ are $c_\omega(g)=1$ and zero at every other shift. So
$a_{\sigma,\delta}\ne c_\delta$ in general; 04's condition concerns the
logarithm of the monomial image, 09's the logarithm of the operator. Both
vanishing conditions hold here, and by
\cref{opa:thm:criterion,opa:cor:operator} each is equivalent to preservation
of $\Oz$, hence to the other. The placement dossier checked the example by
exact computation.
\end{example}

\subsection{The criterion}

The following statement combines 09's generality with 04's proof. 04 proved
it for $k=\R,\C$ and $\oo=\Z,\Z[i]$, passing through \cref{opa:lem:divisible};
09 proved it for every characteristic-zero $k$ and unital $\oo$, but through
the operator logarithm (\cref{opa:fact:import}). The first step below is 09's
and removes the need for $\bigcap n\oo=0$; the rest is 04's.

\begin{theorem}[Convex-support criterion \src{04, 09}\mergetag]\label{opa:thm:criterion}
Let $k$ have characteristic zero, let $\oo\subseteq k$ be a unital subring,
let $\Gamma\ne0$ be divisible (a set, or $\Gamma=\No$), and let $\sigma$ be a
strong $k$-linear $1$-automorphism of $K=\Hahn k\Gamma$. The following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},itemsep=1pt]
\item $\sigma(\Ro)=\Ro$;
\item $\sigma(\Ak)\subseteq\Ak$;
\item $\sigma(\Pi)\subseteq\Pi$;
\item $a_{\sigma,\delta}(C_\delta)=0$ for every $\delta>0$;
\item for every $g\ne0$, every exponent $\delta\in\supp h_\sigma(g)$ satisfies
$\delta\bb|g|$;
\item $\sigma$ commutes with $P_-$, $P_0$ and $P_+$.
\end{enumerate}
When they hold, $\sigma(\Pi)=\Pi$, $\sigma(\Ak)=\Ak$, $\sigma(\mm)=\mm$,
the constant coefficient of every series is preserved, and every purely
infinite monomial stays purely infinite, though not necessarily a monomial.
In particular the group $\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)$ does not depend on $\oo$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii)} \src{09}. For $\gamma<0$, $t^\gamma\in\Ro$, so
$\sigma(t^\gamma)\in\Ro\subseteq\Ak$. An element of $\Ak$ is
$c+\sum_{\gamma<0}a_\gamma t^\gamma$ with $c\in k$; strong $k$-linearity writes
its image as $c+\sum a_\gamma\sigma(t^\gamma)$, a summable family of series
supported in $\Gamma^{\le0}$, whose sum is supported there.

\emph{(ii)$\Rightarrow$(iv)} \src{04}. Fix $\delta>0$ and $0\ne g\in C_\delta$;
put $x=-|g|<0$, so $h_\sigma(x)=\pm h_\sigma(g)$, and choose $N\ge1$ with
$|g|\le N\delta$. By \cref{opa:lem:polynomial},
$P(X)=\coef\delta{\exp(Xh_\sigma(x))}$ is a polynomial. For rational
$0<q<1/N$, divisibility gives $qx\in\Gamma^{<0}$ and additivity gives
$\sigma(t^{qx})=t^{qx}\exp(qh_\sigma(x))$, whose coefficient at $qx+\delta$ is
$P(q)$. Since $q|g|<\delta$, this exponent is strictly positive, while
$\sigma(t^{qx})\in\Ak$ has no positive exponents. So $P$ has infinitely many
zeros and vanishes; its linear coefficient is $a_{\sigma,\delta}(x)$, so
$a_{\sigma,\delta}(g)=0$. This tests every coefficient of the logarithm;
testing only the first nonzero one would not suffice.

\emph{(iv)$\Leftrightarrow$(v)} \src{04}. $g\notin C_\delta$ means
$\delta\bb|g|$; consider each nonzero coefficient.

\emph{(v)$\Rightarrow$(iii)} \src{04}. For $g<0$, $\supp h_\sigma(g)\subseteq
H_g^{>0}$, so every exponent of $\exp(h_\sigma(g))$ is $0$ or a finite sum of
elements of $H_g^{>0}$, hence smaller than $|g|$; thus
$\sigma(t^g)=t^g\exp(h_\sigma(g))\in\Pi$. For $f\in\Pi$ write
$\sigma(f)=\sum c_g\sigma(t^g)$ by strongness; all supports are negative.

\emph{(iii)$\Rightarrow$(vi)} \src{04}. A $1$-automorphism maps $\mm$ into
$\mm$, and $\sigma$ fixes $k$; so each summand of \eqref{opa:eq:decomp} is
mapped into itself and $\sigma$ commutes with the projections.

\emph{(vi)$\Rightarrow$(i)} \src{04}. If $\sigma(y)\in\Pi$ with
$y=y_-+c+y_+$, then $0=P_0\sigma(y)=c$ and $0=P_+\sigma(y)=\sigma(y_+)$, so
$y\in\Pi$. With (iii) this gives $\sigma(\Pi)=\Pi$, and since $\sigma$ fixes
$\oo\subseteq k$, $\sigma(\Ro)=\Ro$. The same argument gives
$\sigma(\Ak)=\Ak$ and $\sigma(\mm)=\mm$, and (vi) preserves constant
coefficients. Condition (ii) does not mention $\oo$, which proves the last
assertion.
\end{proof}

Only (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iv) uses divisibility; it divides an \emph{exponent},
while characteristic zero separately permits rational scalars \src{09}. The
argument is elementwise with set-sized supports, so it applies verbatim to
$\Gamma=\No$, $k=\R$, $\oo=\Z$, that is to $\Oz$, and to $\Og$ with $(k,\oo)=
(\C,\Z[i])$ \src{04}. The theorem classifies \emph{existing} strong maps: an
arbitrary additive $h:\Gamma\to\mm$ need not define a strong surjective field map
by $t^g\mapsto t^g\exp h(g)$ \src{04} (\cref{opa:q:characters}).

\begin{theorem}[Boundary-preserving derivations \src{09}]\label{opa:thm:derivation}
Let $\Gamma\ne0$ be divisible and let $\partial$ be a contracting strong
$k$-derivation of $K$. Then $\partial(\Ak)\subseteq\Ak$ if and only if
$c_\delta(C_\delta)=0$ for every $\delta>0$. In that case
$\partial(\Ak)\subseteq\Pi$, $\partial(\Pi)\subseteq\Pi$, and $\partial$ commutes
with $P_-,P_0,P_+$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\partial(\Ak)\subseteq\Ak$ and $-\delta<\gamma<0$, then $t^\gamma\in\Ak$ and
$\gamma+\delta>0$, so $c_\delta(\gamma)=0$; \cref{opa:lem:additive} extends this
to $C_\delta$. Conversely, if $\gamma<0$ and $c_\delta(\gamma)\ne0$, then
$\gamma\notin C_\delta$, so $-\gamma>n\delta$ for all $n$ and in particular
$\gamma+\delta<0$. Every term of $\partial(t^\gamma)$ is negative; constants are
killed; strongness sums over any negative support. Positive exponents stay
positive under positive shifts, which gives the projection identities.
\end{proof}

The condition excludes a boundary term as well as a crossing term: testing
$-\delta/2$ gives $c_\delta(-\delta)=0$. This matters when constant
coefficients are restricted to $\oo$ rather than $k$ \src{09}. Source 04
proves the same criterion for derivations with common positive shifts
(\cref{opa:thm:derivcriterion}), testing $t^{-u}$ for $0<u\le\delta$.

\begin{corollary}[Operator form \src{09}]\label{opa:cor:operator}
Let $\sigma$ be a strong $k$-linear $1$-automorphism of the form
$\sigma=\Exp(\partial)$ for a contracting strong $k$-derivation $\partial$ with
$\Log(\sigma)=\partial$. Then the conditions of \cref{opa:thm:criterion} are
equivalent to $\partial(\Ak)\subseteq\Ak$ and to $c_\delta(C_\delta)=0$ for all
$\delta>0$. For set-sized $\Gamma$ every strong $k$-linear $1$-automorphism
has this form, so
\[
 \UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)=\Exp(\LL_A),\qquad
 \LL_A=\{\partial\text{ contracting strong }k\text{-derivation}:
 c_\delta(C_\delta)=0\text{ for all }\delta>0\}.
\]
For $\Gamma=\No$ the same equality holds if the correspondence of
\cref{opa:fact:import} is granted for proper-class $\Gamma$, as \cite[Fact
4.2]{KKS} asserts (\cref{opa:rem:import}); on the common-positive-shift
class it is proved in \cref{opa:thm:lie}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If the conditions hold, $\sigma(\Ak)=\Ak$; then each $(\sigma-\id)^n$ maps $\Ak$
into $\Ak$, and $\Log(\sigma)$ is a strongly summable family of series
supported in $\Gamma^{\le0}$, so $\partial(\Ak)\subseteq\Ak$. This step is taken
in the $k$-algebra $\Ak$, not in $\Ro$, so no division by integers inside
$\oo$ is needed. \Cref{opa:thm:derivation} converts this into the coefficient
condition. Conversely, under the coefficient condition every iterate of
$\partial$ maps $\Ak$ into $\Pi$ and kills $k$, so $\Exp(\pm\partial)$ preserve
$\Ro$. The last assertions are \cref{opa:fact:import}.
\end{proof}

The two routes are genuinely different. 04's functional is read off the
monomial images and needs no correspondence; 09's is read off the generator
and gives the Lie-theoretic structure of \cref{opa:sec:group}. Neither
condition is stated in terms of the other: each is equivalent to preservation
of $\Ro$ (\cref{opa:ex:functionals}).

\begin{remark}[What has and has not been classified \src{04, 09}]\label{opa:rem:classified}
The classification is inside the \emph{strong, coefficient-fixing,
leading-term-preserving} automorphisms. It does not assert that every abstract
field automorphism preserving $\Ro$ is strong; for the real pair, fixing of
$\R$ is automatic (\cref{opa:cor:allautos}), strongness is not known to be. The
coefficient functionals come from an existing strong map; arbitrary functions
satisfying the vanishing condition cannot be assembled into an operator
without checking summability and invertibility.
\end{remark}

\section{Shears, flows, and the rank dichotomy}\label{opa:sec:shears}

\subsection{Homogeneous shears}

Let $\theta:\Gamma\to(k,+)$ be additive and $\delta>0$. The formula
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:Dshift}
 D_{\delta,\theta}\Bigl(\sum_\gamma a_\gamma t^\gamma\Bigr)
 =\sum_\gamma a_\gamma\theta(\gamma)t^{\gamma+\delta}
\end{equation}
defines a contracting strong derivation: translation keeps supports well
ordered and families summable, and additivity of $\theta$ gives the product
rule on monomials, hence on all series. This is the fixed-shift derivation of
the surcomplex automorphism report, \lbl{saut:thm:shiftflow}, which constructs
its exponential for every additive $\theta$ and every $\delta>0$; its $n$th
iterate has coefficient $\prod_{j<n}\theta(\gamma+j\delta)$. Sources 04, 08 and
09 re-derive it; only 04 credited it. The integer-part condition is the
additional restriction $\theta(C_\delta)=0$, not merely $\theta(\delta)=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous shears \src{04, 09}; after \lbl{saut:thm:shiftflow}]\label{opa:thm:shear}
Suppose $\theta(C_\delta)=0$. Then
$D_{\delta,\theta}^n(t^\gamma)=\theta(\gamma)^nt^{\gamma+n\delta}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:shear}
 \sigma_{\delta,\theta}(t^\gamma):=\Exp(D_{\delta,\theta})(t^\gamma)
 =t^\gamma\exp\bigl(\theta(\gamma)t^\delta\bigr).
\end{equation}
The strong extension $\sigma_{\delta,\theta}$ lies in $\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)$ for every
$\oo$, with inverse $\sigma_{\delta,-\theta}$, and
$\sigma_{\delta,\theta}\sigma_{\delta,\psi}=\sigma_{\delta,\theta+\psi}$ when
$\theta,\psi$ both vanish on $C_\delta$. With $\Phi_s=\sigma_{\delta,s\theta}$
$(s\in k)$, $s\mapsto\Phi_s$ is a homomorphism, injective if $\theta\ne0$.
Conversely $\Exp(D_{\delta,\theta})$ preserves $\Ro$ only if
$\theta(C_\delta)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\theta(\delta)=0$ gives $\theta(\gamma+n\delta)=\theta(\gamma)$ and the iterate
formula. For a series supported on $S$, all terms of the image lie in
$S+\N\delta$; \cref{opa:lem:shift} gives well-ordering and finite
contributions across all $n$, for single inputs and for summable families.
On monomials the prescription is multiplicative by additivity of $\theta$ and
the exponential law; strongness extends this to all series. The map fixes
$t^\delta$ and every coefficient of $\exp(\cdot)$, so applying it termwise gives
$\sigma_{\delta,\theta}\sigma_{\delta,\psi}(t^\gamma)=t^\gamma\exp((\theta+\psi)(\gamma)
t^\delta)$, which gives the group law and the inverse. Every correction has
larger exponent, so leading terms are fixed. Here $h_\sigma(\gamma)=\theta(\gamma)
t^\delta$, whose only coefficient $a_{\sigma,\delta}=\theta$ annihilates
$C_\delta$; apply \cref{opa:thm:criterion}. The coefficient at $\gamma+\delta$
of $\Phi_s(t^\gamma)$ is $s\theta(\gamma)$, which gives injectivity. The converse
is the same criterion, since $a_{\sigma,\delta}=\theta$ is the only functional.
\end{proof}

The summability above is proved directly; no appeal to
\cref{opa:fact:import} is made. For $\gamma<0$ with $\theta(\gamma)\ne0$, every
$\gamma+n\delta$ stays negative, because $-\gamma>n\delta$ for all ordinary $n$:
the whole exponential, not only a Taylor polynomial, stays on the correct side
of the boundary \src{09}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact displacement and fixed field \src{04, 08, 09}]\label{opa:thm:fixedshear}
Let $\theta(C_\delta)=0$, $s\ne0$, and $f=\sum c_\gamma t^\gamma$. If
$T_\theta(f)=\{\gamma\in\supp f:\theta(\gamma)\ne0\}$ is empty, $\Phi_s(f)=f$.
Otherwise let $\gamma_0=\min T_\theta(f)$; then
\[
 v(\Phi_s(f)-f)=\gamma_0+\delta,\qquad
 \coef{\gamma_0+\delta}{(\Phi_s(f)-f)}=s\,\theta(\gamma_0)c_{\gamma_0}.
\]
In particular $\Fix(\Phi_s)=\Hahn k{\ker\theta}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Only exponents with $\theta(\gamma)\ne0$ and powers $n\ge1$ contribute to the
difference, at $\gamma+n\delta\ge\gamma_0+\delta$, with equality only for
$\gamma=\gamma_0$, $n=1$. $\ker\theta$ is a divisible subgroup, so the Hahn
field on it is a subfield.
\end{proof}

This first-displacement computation appears three times in the sources: as
04's exact displacement, as 08's leading term of $T(x)-x$, and inside 09's
fixed-field proof. It is printed once.

\begin{example}[A nonmonomial image \src{04, 09}]\label{opa:ex:omegaomega}
Let $\Gamma=\No$, $\delta=1$, $\theta=q_1$, the coefficient of $\omega$ in an
exponent. Every member of $C_1$ is finite, so $q_1(C_1)=0$. The flow fixes
$\R$ and $\omega=t^{-1}$, and
\[
 \Phi_s(\omega^\omega)=\omega^\omega\exp(-s\omega^{-1})
 =\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(-s)^n}{n!}\omega^{\omega-n}.
\]
For $s\ne0$ this is not a monomial; every exponent $\omega-n$ is positive, so it
is an omnific integer. The sum is a Hahn sum with strictly decreasing
exponents, not a limit in the fine topology.
\end{example}

\subsection{Families of shifts and a proved correspondence}

\begin{proposition}[Uniformly supported shifts \src{09}]\label{opa:prop:uniform}
Let $S\subseteq\Gamma^{>0}$ be a set-sized well-ordered set and
$(\theta_s)_{s\in S}$ additive maps $\Gamma\to k$. Then
$D(f)=\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{s\in S}a_\gamma\theta_s(\gamma)t^{\gamma+s}$ is a contracting
strong derivation, the terms of $\Exp(D)(f)$ are supported in
$\supp f+\langle S\rangle_\N$ and form a summable family, and if
$\theta_s(C_s)=0$ for all $s$ then $\Exp(D)\in\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The support principle applies to $\supp f+S$ and to all finite words in $S$;
every coefficient of $D$, its iterates and products is a finite sum.
Additivity of each $\theta_s$ gives the product rule; replacing one input
support by the union of the supports of a summable input family gives
strongness; each output exponent exceeds its input exponent. Preservation of
$\Ro$ follows from the no-crossing argument of \cref{opa:thm:derivation}
applied at each step of a word in $S$, which shows that $\Exp(\pm D)$ preserve
$\Pi$ and fix $k$.
\end{proof}

All explicit constructions on $\No$ below use one set-sized well-ordered set
of shifts, so they do not depend on the proper-class case of
\cref{opa:fact:import} \src{09}. Source 04 proves the correspondence itself on
a class of this kind.

\begin{definition}[Common positive shifts \src{04}]\label{opa:def:common}
A strong $k$-linear operator $L$ has \emph{common positive shifts} if there is
a well-ordered set $S\subseteq\Gamma^{>0}$ with $\supp L(t^g)\subseteq g+S$ for
every $g$. Write $S^*$ for the additive monoid generated by $S$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Iterated operator summability \src{04}]\label{opa:lem:operator}
If $L$ has common positive shifts, then $(L^nf)_{n\ge0}$ is Hahn summable for
every $f$, formal power series in $L$ define strong operators, and
coefficientwise formal identities between such series hold under composition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\supp L^nf\subseteq\supp f+nS$. Translate $\supp f$ by its minimum and apply the
support principle to the positive well-ordered set $S\cup((\supp f-\min\supp
f)\setminus\{0\})$; a base exponent is used once and later shifts come from
$S$, so every exponent receives finitely many contributions across all paths
and all $n$. For a summable input family use the union of supports; regrouping
is justified because every coefficient has finitely many contributions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Derivations with common shifts \src{04}]\label{opa:thm:derivcriterion}
Let $S\subseteq\Gamma^{>0}$ be well ordered and $a_\delta:\Gamma\to k$ additive
for $\delta\in S$. Then $D=\sum_{\delta\in S}D_{\delta,a_\delta}$ is a strong
$k$-linear derivation with common positive shifts, and every such derivation
has a unique expression of this form. When $\bigcap_nn\oo=0$, $D$ maps $\Ro$ into
itself if and only if $a_\delta(C_\delta)=0$ for all $\delta\in S$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Summability and the Leibniz rule are termwise. Conversely put
$a_\delta(g)=\coef{g+\delta}{D(t^g)}$; the Leibniz rule for $t^{g+h}$ gives
additivity, and strong linearity the expression. If $D(\Ro)\subseteq\Ro$ and
$x\in\Pi$, then $D(x)=nD(x/n)\in n\Ro$ for all $n$, so $D(x)\in\Pi$ by
\cref{opa:lem:divisible}. For $0<u\le\delta$ the coefficient of $D(t^{-u})$ at
the nonnegative exponent $\delta-u$ is $a_\delta(-u)$, with no cancellation
between distinct shifts, so it vanishes; additivity and divisibility extend
this to $C_\delta$. The converse is the no-crossing argument.
\end{proof}

In this theorem $D$ is a derivation; the sources' coefficient ring $D$ is
$\oo$ here, which removes the collision 04 flagged.

\begin{theorem}[Exponential and logarithm on common shifts \src{04}]\label{opa:thm:lie}
On operators with common positive shifts, $D\mapsto\Exp(D)$ and
$\sigma\mapsto\Log(\sigma)=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n+1}(\sigma-\id)^n/n$ are inverse
bijections between strong $k$-linear derivations and strong $k$-linear field
automorphisms whose difference from the identity has common positive shifts;
the shift set may enlarge to $S^*\setminus\{0\}$. They restrict to a
bijection between derivations preserving $\Ro$ and automorphisms preserving
$\Ro$ (for $\bigcap_nn\oo=0$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All series are justified by \cref{opa:lem:operator}. For a derivation $D$, the
iterated Leibniz rule gives multiplicativity of $\Exp D$ after regrouping, and
$\Exp D\Exp(-D)=\id$ gives bijectivity; leading terms are fixed since all other
shifts are positive. Conversely let $L=\sigma-\id$ and
$\sigma^{[X]}=\sum_{n\ge0}\binom XnL^n$. For each ordinary $m\ge0$ this is the
binomial expansion of $\sigma^m$, so for every exponent $\gamma$ the $t^\gamma$
coefficient of $\sigma^{[X]}(fg)-\sigma^{[X]}(f)\sigma^{[X]}(g)$, a polynomial in
$X$ by \cref{opa:lem:operator}, vanishes at all $m\ge0$ and is zero.
Differentiating at $X=0$, with $\frac{d}{dX}\binom Xn\big|_{X=0}=(-1)^{n-1}/n$,
shows that $\Log\sigma$ is a derivation; one-variable formal identities give
the inverse correspondence. If $D$ preserves $\Ro$ it maps $\Pi$ into $\Pi$, so
$\Exp(\pm D)$ do; if $\sigma(\Pi)=\Pi$, then $(\sigma-\id)(\Pi)\subseteq\Pi$ and the
strong logarithmic sum has negative support.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Single-shift flows without divisibility}

Source 08 treats one flow at a time but allows any ordered abelian group and
does not require $\theta(\delta)=0$. Its statements are translated from the
$\omega$-convention (\cref{opa:sub:notation}). For an ordered abelian group $G$,
$(k,\oo)=(\R,\Z)$ or $(\C,\Z[i])$, let $\Pi_G$ be the series supported in
$G^{<0}$ and $\mathcal R_G=\oo\oplus\Pi_G$.

\begin{proposition}[Falling-factorial iterates \src{08}]\label{opa:prop:iterate}
For any additive $\theta:G\to k$ and $\delta>0$,
$D_{\delta,\theta}^n(t^\gamma)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(\theta(\gamma)+j\theta(\delta))\,
t^{\gamma+n\delta}$, and $T^s=\sum_{n\ge0}s^nD_{\delta,\theta}^n/n!$ $(s\in k)$ is a
strong $k$-linear $1$-automorphism with $T^sT^u=T^{s+u}$. If $\theta(\delta)=0$,
$T^s(t^\gamma)=t^\gamma\exp(s\theta(\gamma)t^\delta)$; if $c=\theta(\delta)\ne0$,
$T^s(t^\gamma)=t^\gamma(1+sct^\delta)^{\theta(\gamma)/c}$ as a formal binomial
series. In either case $T^s(t^{-\delta})=t^{-\delta}-s\theta(\delta)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The iterate formula is induction. Summability, strongness and the group law
follow from \cref{opa:lem:shift} as in \cref{opa:thm:shear}, and the pairs
$(n,m)$ with $n+m=r$ are finite in number; multiplicativity follows from the
iterated Leibniz rule. The two monomial formulas are the substitutions of the
iterate. For $\gamma=-\delta$ every product of length at least two has the
factor $\theta(-\delta)+\theta(\delta)=0$.
\end{proof}

This is \lbl{saut:thm:shiftflow} once more, over an arbitrary ordered abelian
$G$; 08 reproves the summability for that reason.

\begin{theorem}[Threshold for single flows \src{08}]\label{opa:thm:threshold}
Let $G$ be any ordered abelian group, $\delta>0$, $\theta:G\to k$ additive and
$s\in k\setminus\{0\}$. The following are equivalent: \textup{(a)}
$T^s(\mathcal R_G)\subseteq\mathcal R_G$; \textup{(b)}
$T^s(\mathcal R_G)=\mathcal R_G$; \textup{(c)} $\theta(C_\delta)=0$. When they hold,
$T^s$ fixes every series supported in $C_\delta$ and preserves the constant
coefficient of every series.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume (a). By \cref{opa:lem:divisible}, $\Pi_G=\bigcap_nn\mathcal R_G$, so
$T^s(\Pi_G)\subseteq\Pi_G$. The constant term of $T^s(t^{-\delta})$ is
$-s\theta(\delta)$, so $\theta(\delta)=0$. If $0>\gamma\in C_\delta$ and
$\theta(\gamma)\ne0$, choose $n$ with $\gamma+n\delta>0$; the coefficient of
$T^s(t^\gamma)$ there is $(s\theta(\gamma))^n/n!\ne0$, and the exponents
$\gamma+m\delta$ are distinct, contradicting $T^s(t^\gamma)\in\Pi_G$. So $\theta$
vanishes on the negative part of $C_\delta$, hence on $C_\delta$. Conversely,
under (c), monomials with exponents in $C_\delta$ are fixed; if $\gamma<0$ lies
outside $C_\delta$, then $\gamma<-n\delta$ for all $n$, so all
$\gamma+n\delta$ stay negative; positive exponents outside $C_\delta$ stay
positive. So no term enters or leaves exponent $0$, $T^{\pm s}(\mathcal R_G)
\subseteq\mathcal R_G$, and (b) follows. (b)$\Rightarrow$(a) is trivial.
\end{proof}

Only divisibility of \emph{coefficients} by ordinary integers is used, so
$G$ need not be divisible. The theorem covers the elementary family only:
even at rank one, exponent rescalings and characters preserve the integer part,
and it does not assert that every leading-term-preserving automorphism is the
exponential of one fixed-shift derivation \src{08}.

\begin{remark}[Divisibility and the ring $\Ak$ \src{09}; corrected]\label{opa:rem:divisibility}
Source 09 remarks that divisibility of $\Gamma$ is essential, because with
$\Gamma=\Z$ the translation below preserves $\Ak(\Z)=k[t^{-1}]$. That is right
for the boundary ring $\Ak$ and wrong if read for the integer-part ring: the
translation $t^{-1}\mapsto t^{-1}-1$ sends $\tfrac12t^{-1}\in\mathcal R_\Z(\Z)=
\Z+t^{-1}\R[t^{-1}]$ to $\tfrac12t^{-1}-\tfrac12\notin\mathcal R_\Z(\Z)$, as
\cref{opa:thm:threshold} predicts. So for $\Gamma=\Z$ the stabilizers of $\Ak$ and
of $\mathcal R_\Z$ differ, and the independence of $\oo$ in
\cref{opa:thm:criterion} genuinely needs divisibility, whereas the
$\mathcal R_\Z$-form of the criterion holds without divisibility for single
flows. Whether the general criterion for $\mathcal R_\Z$ holds for every
non-divisible $\Gamma$ is left open (\cref{opa:q:nondivisible}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Why translations fail}

\begin{example}[Translations \src{04, 08, 09}; the saut example]\label{opa:ex:translation}
Let $\Gamma=\Q$, $\delta=1$, $\theta(\gamma)=\gamma$. Then
$D^n(t^\gamma)=\gamma(\gamma+1)\cdots(\gamma+n-1)t^{\gamma+n}$ and
$\Exp(D)(t^\gamma)=t^\gamma(1-t)^{-\gamma}$; on $t$ this is
$t\mapsto t/(1-t)$, and on $t^{-1}$ it is $t^{-1}\mapsto t^{-1}-1$, which
preserves $k[t^{-1}]$. But
\[
 \Exp(D)(t^{-1/2})=t^{-1/2}(1-t)^{1/2}=t^{-1/2}-\tfrac12t^{1/2}-\tfrac18t^{3/2}-\cdots
\]
has positive support, so neither $\Ak$ nor $\Ro$ is preserved: $\theta(C_1)\ne0$.
The inverse $t\mapsto t/(1+t)$ is 04's example and $X\mapsto X+s$, $X=t^{-1}$, is
08's. This is exactly the transparent example $T_1(t)=t/(1-t)$ of the
surcomplex automorphism report (its $\lambda=\ell$, the constant-coefficient
functional, with $\delta=1$, after \lbl{saut:thm:shiftflow}); on the surreals
$\ell(1)=1\ne0$, so $T_1$ does \emph{not} preserve $\Oz$: it sends
$\omega^{1/2}$ to $\omega^{1/2}-\tfrac12\omega^{-1/2}-\cdots\notin\Oz$. Preservation
of a few familiar infinite integers is not enough; all rational monomial powers
must be tested.
\end{example}

The Taylor coefficient flows of that report (\lbl{saut:thm:coeffflow}) move
real constants and never preserve $\Oz$: this follows from
\cref{opa:cor:allautos}, and directly, for $d(b)=1$ they send
$bt^{-\delta/2}$ to $bt^{-\delta/2}+t^{\delta/2}$.

\subsection{The rank dichotomy and the bottom subgroup}

\begin{theorem}[Rank dichotomy \src{04, 09}; \src{08} for single flows]\label{opa:thm:rank}
For a nonzero divisible ordered abelian group $\Gamma$, any characteristic-zero
$k$ and unital $\oo\subseteq k$: $\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)=\{\id\}$ if and only if $\Gamma$
is Archimedean. For every ordered abelian $G$, the single flows of
\cref{opa:thm:threshold} preserving $\mathcal R_G$ are trivial if $G$ is
Archimedean, and some are nontrivial if $G$ is a set with a proper $C_\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\Gamma$ is Archimedean, $C_\delta=\Gamma$ for all $\delta$, so by
\cref{opa:thm:criterion} every $h_\sigma(g)$ vanishes; $\sigma$ fixes all
monomials, hence everything. If some $C_\delta$ is proper, choose (by a basis
of the set-sized $\Q$-space $\Gamma/C_\delta$, or of the rationalization of the
torsion-free $G/C_\delta$ for 08's case) a nonzero $\Q$-linear $\theta$ into
$\Q\subseteq k$ vanishing on $C_\delta$; \cref{opa:thm:shear} or
\cref{opa:thm:threshold} gives a nonidentity element. For $\Gamma=\No$ explicit
functionals $q_\beta$ replace the choice (\cref{opa:sec:fixed}).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Bottom convex subgroup \src{04}]\label{opa:def:bottom}
$B(\Gamma)=\bigcap_{\delta>0}C_\delta$, possibly zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Common fixed field at arbitrary set-sized rank \src{04}]\label{opa:thm:bottom}
For a set-sized nonzero divisible $\Gamma$, $\Fix\bigl(\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)\bigr)=
\Hahn k{B(\Gamma)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $g\in B(\Gamma)$, every $a_{\sigma,\delta}(g)$ vanishes by
\cref{opa:thm:criterion}, so $t^g$ is fixed, and strong linearity fixes the Hahn
field on $B(\Gamma)$. If $g\in\supp f$ lies outside $B(\Gamma)$, choose $\delta$
with $g\notin C_\delta$ and a $\Q$-linear $\theta$ with $\theta(C_\delta)=0$ and
$\theta(g)=1$; \cref{opa:thm:fixedshear} shows $\Phi_1$ moves $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[\src{04, 09}]\label{opa:ex:Qr}
Let $\Gamma=\Q^r$, ordered so that the \emph{last} nonzero coordinate decides the
sign (09's reverse-lexicographic order; 04 orders by the first coordinate, which
is the same group with coordinates reversed). Then the coordinate vectors satisfy
$0<e_1\bb e_2\bb\cdots\bb e_r$, $C_{e_j}=\Q e_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\Q e_j$, and
$B(\Gamma)=\Q e_1$. For $r=1$ the stabilizer is trivial; for $r\ge2$ it is
nontrivial and fixes exactly $\Hahn k{\Q e_1}$. The presence of a smallest
Archimedean layer, not the number of layers, controls the fixed field. For
$\Gamma=\No$, $B(\No)=0$: for $g\ne0$ take $\delta=|g|/\omega$.
\end{example}

\subsection{Phase shears}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-dimensional phase shears \src{08}]\label{opa:thm:phase}
Let $H$ be a convex subgroup of an ordered abelian group $G$, let $\mm_H$ be the
series supported in $H^{>0}$, and let $L$ be an additive map on $G$ vanishing on
$H$, with values in $\mm_H$ and image in a finite-dimensional $k$-subspace. Then
$T_L(t^g)=t^g\exp(L(g))$ extends strongly to a $k$-linear $1$-automorphism of
the Hahn field that fixes $\Hahn kH$ pointwise, preserves $\mathcal R_G$ and
constant coefficients, and satisfies $T_LT_M=T_{L+M}$, $T_L^{-1}=T_{-L}$,
and $T_L=\id\Leftrightarrow L=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $u_1,\dots,u_d$ span the image of $L$ and $U=\bigcup_j\supp u_j$, a
well-ordered subset of $H^{>0}$. All supports of all powers in all $\exp(L(g))$
lie in the monoid generated by $U$, which is well ordered with finite
representations; with the input support this gives one summable bound, hence
the strong extension. Additivity of $L$ gives multiplicativity. Exponents in
$H$ have zero phase, so $\Hahn kH$ is fixed; in particular each $M(g)$ and its
exponential are fixed, which gives $T_LT_M=T_{L+M}$ on monomials and then
strongly. Injectivity follows from injectivity of $\exp$ on $\mm$. If $g\notin
H$, every exponent of $t^g\exp(L(g))$ lies in $g+H$, which has constant strict
sign by convexity; so positive and negative supports are preserved and no term
reaches exponent $0$.
\end{proof}

The finite-dimensionality is a sufficient uniform support condition and must
not be dropped silently: independent phases for infinitely many exponents need
not form a summable family \src{08}. Taking $H=C_\delta$ and
$L(g)=s\theta(g)t^\delta$ recovers the flows of \cref{opa:thm:threshold}.

\section{The strong stabilizer and all pair automorphisms}\label{opa:sec:factorization}

Call $\sigma$ \emph{valued} if $v(\sigma f)=\tau(v(f))$ for an order automorphism
$\tau$ of $\Gamma$. For a character $\chi:(\Gamma,+)\to(k^\times,\cdot)$ put
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:monomialmap}
 M_{\chi,\tau}\Bigl(\sum a_\gamma t^\gamma\Bigr)=\sum a_\gamma\chi(\gamma)t^{\tau(\gamma)}.
\end{equation}
It is a strong field automorphism preserving $\Ro$, since $\tau$ preserves signs
of exponents and $\chi(0)=1$; $M_{\chi,\tau}M_{\chi',\tau'}=
M_{\chi'\cdot(\chi\circ\tau'),\tau\tau'}$ \src{04, 09}.

\begin{theorem}[Strong valued factorization \src{04, 09}]\label{opa:thm:factorization}
Every strong valued $k$-fixing automorphism $\sigma$ with $\sigma(\Ro)=\Ro$ factors
uniquely as $\sigma=M_{\chi,\tau}\circ u$ with $u\in\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)$, where
$\tau$ is the induced exponent automorphism and $\chi(\gamma)=\lc(\sigma(t^\gamma))$.
The monomial maps form a subgroup normalizing $\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:conj}
 M_{\chi,\tau}D_{\delta,\theta}M_{\chi,\tau}^{-1}=
 D_{\tau(\delta),\,\chi(\delta)\,\theta\circ\tau^{-1}},
\end{equation}
so the convex condition is invariant, since $\tau(C_\delta)=C_{\tau(\delta)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Multiplicativity of leading coefficients gives that $\chi$ is a character.
$u=M_{\chi,\tau}^{-1}\sigma$ fixes the leading term of each monomial; since the
images of distinct monomial terms have strictly ordered valuations, strongness
shows that $u$ fixes the leading term of every series, and $u$ preserves $\Ro$.
The data $\tau$, $\chi$ are forced by $\sigma$, hence so is $u$. Conjugating a
$1$-automorphism by a monomial map gives a $1$-automorphism preserving $\Ro$.
Formula \eqref{opa:eq:conj} is checked on monomials.
\end{proof}

The ambient decomposition is due to Kuhlmann--Serra \cite{KS}, and its
surcomplex form is the four-layer decomposition \lbl{saut:thm:decomp}
$U\rtimes\Hom(\Gamma,\C^\times)\rtimes(\Aut\C\times\Aut\Gamma)$ of the surcomplex
automorphism report; \cref{opa:thm:factorization} is its restriction to the
$\Ro$-stabilizer with the residue layer trivial. Sources 04 and 09 credit
\cite{KS}; the new information is the description of $u$ by
\cref{opa:thm:criterion}. For $k=\R$ and divisible $\Gamma$ every character is
positive, $\chi(\gamma)=\chi(\gamma/2)^2$; for $\Gamma=\Q$ the factor $u$ is absent and
every such real automorphism is $t^q\mapsto c^qt^{\lambda q}$ with $c>0$,
$\lambda\in\Q_{>0}$, so the higher-rank shears are a new phenomenon, not
complicated one-variable substitutions \src{09}. The monomial maps include the
dilations $\mathsf S_a$ of the Diophantine report (\lbl{odg:def:ex:dilation}) and
the character twists (\cref{opa:rem:twists}).

\begin{corollary}[All pair automorphisms \src{08, 09}]\label{opa:cor:allautos}
Every field automorphism of $\No$ preserving $\Oz$ setwise fixes $\R$ pointwise,
preserves order and the natural valuation ring, and commutes with $P_-,P_0,P_+$
and with the floor map of $\Oz$; this needs no strongness. Consequently, among
strong automorphisms, the $\Oz$-stabilizer is exactly
$\{M_{\chi,\tau}\}\ltimes\UU_\Oz$, with $\chi$ positive.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \lbl{odg:def:thm:autreal} of the Diophantine report, every automorphism of
$\Oz$ extends uniquely to $\No$, preserves order, $\R$ and the finite ring, and
fixes $\R$ pointwise; restriction and extension are inverse correspondences
\src{08, 09}. The sets and graphs of \cref{opa:thm:projections} are
parameter-free definable, so they are preserved. Fixing $\R$ pointwise makes a
strong such map $k$-fixing, and preserving the valuation ring makes it valued,
so \cref{opa:thm:factorization} applies.
\end{proof}

\section{Convex rank, solvability, and a wreath product}\label{opa:sec:group}

\subsection{The bracket law}

\begin{lemma}[Homogeneous brackets \src{04, 08, 09}]\label{opa:lem:bracket}
For additive $\theta,\psi:\Gamma\to k$ and $\delta,\epsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:bracket}
 [D_{\delta,\theta},D_{\epsilon,\psi}]=D_{\delta+\epsilon,\ \theta(\epsilon)\psi-\psi(\delta)\theta}.
\end{equation}
If $\theta(C_\delta)=0$ and $\psi(C_\epsilon)=0$, the bracket is zero when
$C_\delta=C_\epsilon$; if $C_\delta\subsetneq C_\epsilon$ it is a multiple of
$D_{\delta+\epsilon,\psi}$, whose shift lies in the $C_\epsilon$ scale and whose
functional vanishes on $C_{\delta+\epsilon}=C_\epsilon$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $t^\gamma$ the two compositions differ at $\gamma+\delta+\epsilon$ by
$\psi(\gamma)\theta(\gamma+\epsilon)-\theta(\gamma)\psi(\gamma+\delta)=
\theta(\epsilon)\psi(\gamma)-\psi(\delta)\theta(\gamma)$; strongness extends the
identity. Equal scales: each functional kills the other's shift. If
$C_\delta\subsetneq C_\epsilon$, then $\psi(\delta)=0$.
\end{proof}

Source 08 records the same identity in its own normalization (its bracket
$[D_{\varphi,\delta},D_{\psi,\varepsilon}]=D_{\psi(\delta)\varphi-\varphi(\varepsilon)\psi,
\delta+\varepsilon}$, which is \eqref{opa:eq:bracket} after the sign change of
\cref{opa:sub:notation}). Elementary derivations with shifts in the same
Archimedean class commute; noncommutativity is an interaction between layers
\src{04}. For general strong derivations the computation applies after
expansion into shift coefficients: the two compositions are strongly summable
and every output coefficient is a finite sum \src{09}.

\subsection{A filtration by convex scales}

Say that $\Gamma$ has \emph{finite ordered rank $r$} if its distinct nonzero
principal convex subgroups are exactly $0<C_1<\cdots<C_r=\Gamma$. The layer of a
shift $\delta$ is the $j$ with $C_\delta=C_j$. A shift in layer $r$ contributes
nothing to $\LL_A$ (\cref{opa:cor:operator}), since its functional vanishes on
$\Gamma$. Let $\FF_j\subseteq\LL_A$ consist of the derivations whose nonzero
shifts have layers at least $j$, so $\LL_A=\FF_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\FF_r=0$.
Selecting the terms of prescribed layers from the images of monomials keeps a
summable subfamily, and the new coefficient functions stay additive, so the
$\FF_j$ are strongly closed Lie ideals \src{09}.

\begin{lemma}[\src{09}]\label{opa:lem:rankfiltration}
$[\FF_j,\FF_j]\subseteq\FF_{j+1}$ and $[\LL_A,\FF_j]\subseteq\FF_j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{opa:lem:bracket} to pairs of shifts: equal layers cancel and unequal
layers give a term in the larger layer.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-rank bound \src{09}]\label{opa:thm:derivedrank}
If the set-sized group $\Gamma$ has finite ordered rank $r$, then $\LL_A$ and
$\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)$ have derived length at most $r-1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inductively $\LL_A^{(n)}\subseteq\FF_{n+1}$. For the group, put
$G_j=\Exp(\FF_j)$; by the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff part of
\cref{opa:fact:import} each $G_j$ is a subgroup, and the logarithm of a
commutator of two elements of $G_j$ is a strongly summable Lie series each of
whose terms contains a bracket of two elements of $\FF_j$, hence lies in
$\FF_{j+1}$. So $[G_j,G_j]\subseteq G_{j+1}$, and $G_r=\{\id\}$.
\end{proof}

The Lie bound is elementary; the group bound uses the imported
correspondence, which \cite{BKKPS} proves for set-sized $\Gamma$.

\subsection{Sharpness, including the abstract group}

In $\Q^r$ ordered as in \cref{opa:ex:Qr}, let $\theta_i$ extract the $i$th
coordinate, $X_{j,i}=D_{e_j,\theta_i}$ for $j<i$, and
\[
 a_0=0,\qquad a_k=\sum_{h=1}^k2^{k-h}e_h,\qquad
 Y_{k;j,i}=D_{a_k+e_j,\theta_i}\quad(k<j<i\le r).
\]
These satisfy the criterion, since $C_{e_j}=\Q e_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\Q e_j$.

\begin{lemma}[Balanced brackets \src{09}]\label{opa:lem:balanced}
$Y_{k;j,i}\in\LL_A^{(k)}$, and $[Y_{k-1;k,j},Y_{k-1;j,i}]=Y_{k;j,i}$ for $k\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At $k=0$ this is the definition. In the bracket,
$\theta_j(a_{k-1}+e_j)=1$ and $\theta_i(a_{k-1}+e_k)=0$, and the shift is
$2a_{k-1}+e_k+e_j=a_k+e_j$; apply \eqref{opa:eq:bracket} and induct.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Mixed-parameter detection \src{09}]\label{opa:lem:mixed}
Take finitely many homogeneous positive-shift derivations and an iterated Lie
bracket $W$ of them. Replace each leaf occurrence $D_\ell$ by $\Exp(s_\ell D_\ell)$
with independent scalar parameters and each bracket by the group word
$ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, giving $\mathcal W(\mathbf s)$. In its action on a monomial,
the coefficient of the product of all leaf parameters is the action of $W$. If
$W\ne0$, some specialization $\mathbf s\in k^m$ gives a nonidentity group word.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Formally, $(1+sD+\cdots)(1+uE+\cdots)(1-sD+\cdots)(1-uE+\cdots)=1+su[D,E]+{}$terms
of other multidegrees; induct on the bracket tree, using that the subtrees have
disjoint parameter sets and that a group word is the identity when a required
leaf parameter is zero. Choose a monomial and an output exponent on which $W$
acts nontrivially. Only a finite set of shifts occurs, so by the support
principle the coefficient at that output is a polynomial in the parameters,
nonzero by the first part. An infinite field has a point where it does not
vanish. Repeated leaves are handled by their separate parameters.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp derived length \src{09}]\label{opa:thm:sharp}
For $\Gamma=\Q^r$ as above, every characteristic-zero $k$ and every unital
$\oo\subseteq k$, the group $\UU_{\Ro}(\Gamma)$ and its Lie algebra $\LL_A$ have
derived length exactly $r-1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $r=1$ use \cref{opa:thm:rank}. For $r\ge2$,
$0\ne Y_{r-2;r-1,r}\in\LL_A^{(r-2)}$ by \cref{opa:lem:balanced}, and
\cref{opa:lem:mixed} turns its balanced bracket into a nonidentity element of the
$(r-2)$nd derived subgroup. The upper bound is \cref{opa:thm:derivedrank}.
\end{proof}

The lower bound does not identify the abstract derived subgroup with the
exponential of the derived Lie algebra; it uses independent parameters and a
nonzero mixed coefficient. The upper bound uses BCH and strongly closed ideals
\src{09}.

\subsection{Metabelian and not nilpotent: one group, printed once}

On $\Gamma=\No$ put $D=D_{1,q_1}$, $E=D_{\omega,q_2}$ and $E_n=D_{\omega+n,q_2}$. Both
$D$ and $E$ satisfy the criterion: $q_1$ kills the finite exponents and $q_2$
kills every exponent bounded by an ordinary multiple of $\omega$.

\begin{proposition}[\src{04, 09}]\label{opa:prop:lienilp}
$[D,E_n]=E_{n+1}$ and $[E_m,E_n]=0$; the $E_n$ are nonzero and linearly
independent over $\R$. So the Lie algebra spanned by $D,E_0,E_1,\ldots$ is
metabelian and not nilpotent. The same holds in rank three: in $\Q^3$ with
$X=D_{e_1,\theta_2}$, $Y=D_{e_2,\theta_3}$ one has $(\ad X)^nY=D_{e_2+ne_1,\theta_3}\ne0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$q_1(\omega+n)=1$, $q_2(1)=q_2(\omega+n)=0$; apply \eqref{opa:eq:bracket}.
$E_n(t^{\omega^2})=t^{\omega^2+\omega+n}$ gives independence. The rank-three case is
the same computation \src{09}.
\end{proof}

Put $A_s=\Exp(sD)$ and $B_u=\Exp(uE)$ for $s,u\in\R$, and $[F,G]=FGF^{-1}G^{-1}$.
Here $e^{st}$ denotes the Hahn series $\exp(st)$ in the monomial $t=\omega^{-1}$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact commutators \src{04, 08}]\label{opa:thm:commutator}
$A_sB_uA_s^{-1}=\Exp\bigl(ue^{st}E\bigr)$ and
$[A_s,B_u]=\Exp\bigl(u(e^{st}-1)E\bigr)$. With $C_0=B_u$ and $C_{n+1}=[A_s,C_n]$,
\[
 C_n=\Exp\bigl(u(e^{st}-1)^nE\bigr),\qquad
 C_n(t^{-\omega^2})=t^{-\omega^2}\exp\bigl(-u(e^{st}-1)^nt^\omega\bigr),
\]
so for $s,u\ne0$, $C_n(t^{-\omega^2})-t^{-\omega^2}$ has leading term
$-us^nt^{-\omega^2+\omega+n}$. Hence $\langle A_s,B_u\rangle$ is metabelian and not
nilpotent. At $s=u=-1$, the leading term is $(-1)^n\omega^{\omega^2-\omega-n}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$D$ fixes $t$ (as $q_1(1)=0$) and $A_s(t^\omega)=t^\omega e^{st}$. On monomials,
$A_sEA_s^{-1}(t^\gamma)=q_2(\gamma)t^{\gamma+\omega}e^{st}$, because $E$ kills every
series in $t$ and $q_1(\gamma+\omega)-q_1(\gamma)=1$; so $A_sEA_s^{-1}=e^{st}E$, and
exponentiating gives the first formula. $E$ kills $\R[[t]]$, so the derivations
$h(t)E$, $h\in\R[[t]]$, commute and their exponentials add coefficients, which
gives the second formula and, by induction, $C_n$. All shifts lie in the monoid
generated by the well-ordered set $\{1\}\cup(\omega+\N)$, so
\cref{opa:thm:lie} justifies all sums. The maps $\Exp(h(t)E)$ form an abelian group
normalized by $A_s$, which acts by $h\mapsto e^{st}h$; so the generated group is
abelian-by-abelian. Since $q_2(-\omega^2)=-1$, $C_n(t^{-\omega^2})$ is as displayed,
and $(e^{st}-1)^n$ has leading term $s^nt^n$; the leading term of the difference
is therefore nonzero at every depth, so the lower central series never
terminates.
\end{proof}

Source 08 works with $A_1^{(08)}=A_{-1}$ and $B_1^{(08)}=B_{-1}$ and proves
faithfulness, which identifies the group. Write $v=t=\omega^{-1}$,
$u_0=t^\omega=\omega^{-\omega}$, $P=t^{-\omega}=\omega^\omega$, $M=t^{-\omega^2}=\omega^{\omega^2}$,
and for $h$ supported in $C_\omega^{>0}$ let $N_h(t^\gamma)=t^\gamma\exp(-q_2(\gamma)h)$, a
phase shear (\cref{opa:thm:phase}) with $N_hN_{h'}=N_{h+h'}$, fixing $v,u_0,P$ and
with $N_h(M)=M\exp(h)$. Then $B_{-1}=N_{u_0}$, $A_{-1}(u_0)=u_0e^{-v}$,
$A_{-1}(P)=Pe^{v}$, and $A_{-1}N_hA_{-1}^{-1}=N_{A_{-1}(h)}$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite exponential independence \src{08}]\label{opa:lem:vandermonde}
For a Laurent polynomial $p\in\Z[X,X^{-1}]$ put $h_p=u_0\,p(e^{-v})$ and
$\mathcal N_p=N_{h_p}$. Then $p\mapsto p(e^{-v})$ is injective, $p\mapsto\mathcal N_p$
is an injective homomorphism, and $A_{-1}\mathcal N_pA_{-1}^{-1}=\mathcal N_{Xp}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $\sum_{n\in F}k_ne^{-nv}=0$ with $|F|=r$ distinct integers, comparing the
coefficients of $v^j$, $0\le j<r$, gives a Vandermonde system with distinct nodes
$-n$, so all $k_n=0$. Injectivity of $\mathcal N$ follows from
$\mathcal N_p(M)=M\exp(h_p)$, and $A_{-1}(h_p)=u_0e^{-v}p(e^{-v})=h_{Xp}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A faithful $\Z\wr\Z$ \src{08}]\label{opa:thm:wreath}
The map $\Z[X,X^{-1}]\rtimes\Z\to\UU_\Oz$, $(p,m)\mapsto\mathcal N_pA_{-1}^m$, with
$(p,m)(q,n)=(p+X^mq,m+n)$, is an injective homomorphism onto
$\langle A_{-1},B_{-1}\rangle$. So this group is the restricted wreath product
$\Z\wr\Z$, of derived length exactly two. It extends $\C$-linearly to $\SC$
preserving $\Og$, and it already lives on the set-sized exponent group
$\Q\omega^2+\Q\omega+\Q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\Cref{opa:lem:vandermonde} gives the multiplication law, and
$\mathcal N_{X^n}=A_{-1}^nB_{-1}A_{-1}^{-n}$ shows that the image is generated by
$A_{-1},B_{-1}$. If $\mathcal N_pA_{-1}^m=\id$, applying it to $P$ gives
$Pe^{mv}=P$, so $m=0$, and then to $M$ gives $h_p=0$, so $p=0$. Finite Laurent
polynomials under multiplication by $X$ are $\bigoplus_\Z\Z$ with the shift.
The group is nonabelian and its normal subgroup $\mathcal N$ is abelian with
cyclic quotient.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[All nonzero times \mergetag]\label{opa:rem:alltimes}
The same proof gives $\langle A_s,B_u\rangle\cong\Z\wr\Z$ for all real $s,u\ne0$:
here $B_u=N_{-ut^\omega}$, $A_sN_hA_s^{-1}=N_{A_s(h)}$ and
$A_s(t^\omega)=t^\omega e^{st}$, so with $h_p=-ut^\omega p(e^{st})$ the map
$(p,m)\mapsto N_{h_p}A_s^m$ obeys the same law; the Vandermonde nodes $ns$ are
distinct, and $A_s^m(P)=Pe^{-mst}$ forces $m=0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Every set stabilizer is not nilpotent \src{04, 08}]\label{opa:cor:localnonabelian}
For every set $P\subseteq\No$, the pointwise stabilizer of $P$ in $\UU_\Oz$ contains a
subgroup isomorphic to $\Z\wr\Z$, metabelian and not nilpotent.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose ordinals $0<\beta<\beta'$ above every inner exponent of every outer
exponent of every member of $P$ (\cref{opa:lem:avoid}), and replace $\omega$,
$\omega^2$ by $\epsilon=\omega^\beta$, $\omega^{\beta'}$, using the functionals
$q_\beta$, $q_{\beta'}$ and the shifts $1$, $\epsilon$. Both functionals vanish on
the exponents of $P$ and satisfy the criterion, and $q_\beta(\epsilon)=1$,
$q_\beta(1)=q_{\beta'}(\epsilon)=q_{\beta'}(1)=0$; the proofs are unchanged.
\end{proof}

These examples live in rank three, consistent with \cref{opa:thm:derivedrank}
(derived length at most two there) \src{08, 09}.

\section{Fixed fields and arbitrarily large stabilizers}\label{opa:sec:fixed}

\subsection{Exponent coefficient functionals}

For $\beta\in\No$ let $q_\beta(\gamma)=[\omega^\beta]\gamma$ be the coefficient of
$\omega^\beta$ in the Conway normal form of the exponent $\gamma$ (the \emph{inner}
normal form; the outer support of $f=\sum c_\gamma t^\gamma$ consists of the
exponents $\gamma$). Normal-form addition makes $q_\beta:\No\to\R$ additive; it
need not commute with infinite sums inside the exponent group, and only finite
additivity is used \src{04}. If $0<\delta\bb\omega^\beta$ then $q_\beta(C_\delta)=0$:
a nonzero $\gamma$ with $|\gamma|\le n\delta$ has leading Conway exponent at most that
of $\delta$, which is below $\beta$ \src{04, 08, 09}.

\begin{lemma}[Parameter avoidance \src{04, 08, 09}]\label{opa:lem:avoid}
Let $P\subseteq\SC$ be a set, $E$ the union of the outer supports of the real and
imaginary parts of its members, and $T$ the union of the inner supports of the
members of $E$. Then $E$ and $T$ are sets, and for every ordinal $\beta>0$ above
all members of $T$, $q_\beta$ vanishes on $E$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Replacement and union; every set of surreals has a strict upper bound, for
example an ordinal above all their birthdays. This bounds a set of supports; it
does not choose a bound for all exponents at once.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The common fixed field \src{04, 08, 09}]\label{opa:thm:fixed}
$\Fix(\UU_\Oz)=\R$, and already the elementary shears $\sigma_{\delta,q_\beta}$ have
common fixed field $\R$. The same holds for all field automorphisms of $\No$
preserving $\Oz$. The common fixed elements in $\Oz$ are $\Z$, and the common
fixed subring of all ring automorphisms of $\Oz$ is $\Z$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All these maps fix $\R$ (\cref{opa:cor:allautos}). If $f\notin\R$, some nonzero
$\gamma\in\supp f$ has $q_\beta(\gamma)\ne0$ for some $\beta$, for instance its leading
inner exponent \src{08}. Put $\theta=q_\beta$ and $\delta=\omega^{\beta-1}$; then
$\theta(C_\delta)=0$ and $\theta(\delta)=0$, and \cref{opa:thm:fixedshear} shows that
$\sigma_{\delta,\theta}$ moves $f$: its first displacement is
$a_\lambda\theta(\lambda)t^{\lambda+\delta}$, $\lambda$ the least exponent of $f$ with
$\theta(\lambda)\ne0$. Ring automorphisms of $\Oz$ are restrictions of field
automorphisms preserving $\Oz$ (\lbl{odg:def:thm:autreal}).
\end{proof}

Source 04 reaches the same conclusion through \cref{opa:lem:separation} with
$V=0$; the explicit choice above is 08's and 09's.

\subsection{Relative fixed fields and the Hahn hull}

\begin{lemma}[Set-sized coefficient separation \src{04}]\label{opa:lem:separation}
Let $V\subseteq\No$ be a set-sized $\Q$-subspace and $g\in\No\setminus V$. There are
$\delta>0$ and an additive class map $a:\No\to\R$ with $a(V)=0$,
$a(C_\delta)=0$ and $a(g)=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $T=\bigcup_{x\in V\cup\{g\}}\supp_\omega(x)$, a nonempty set of inner exponents,
and $p_T(x)=([\omega^\beta]x)_{\beta\in T}\in\R^T$. It is injective on the set-sized
space $W=V+\Q g$, so $\ell_0(p_T(v+qg))=q$ is a well-defined $\Q$-linear functional
on $p_T(W)$; extend it through a basis of the \emph{set} $\R^T$ to $\ell$ and put
$a=\ell\circ p_T$. Choose $\beta_0$ below every member of $T$ and
$\delta=\omega^{\beta_0}$; then $p_T(C_\delta)=0$.
\end{proof}

For a set $A\subseteq\Hahn k\No$ ($k=\R$ or $\C$) let $S_A$ be the union of the outer
supports, $V_A=\Span(S_A)$ and $\HH(A)=\Hahn k{V_A}$; let $\EE_A$ be the group
generated by the elementary shears $\Phi_s$ (with real $s$) that fix $A$
pointwise, in the sense of \cref{opa:sub:foundations}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact elementary hull \src{04}]\label{opa:thm:hull}
For every set $A\subseteq\Hahn k\No$, $\Fix(\EE_A)=\HH(A)=\Hahn k{V_A}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{opa:thm:fixedshear} a nonidentity shear with functional $a$ fixes all of
$A$ iff $a$ vanishes on $S_A$, iff $a(V_A)=0$; such shears fix every series
supported in $V_A$. If $f\notin\HH(A)$, take $g\in\supp f\setminus V_A$ and apply
\cref{opa:lem:separation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Monomial parameters \src{04}]\label{opa:cor:monoparams}
For a set $S\subseteq\No$ with $V=\Span(S)$, the pointwise stabilizer in $\UU_\Oz$
of $\{t^g:g\in S\}$ has common fixed field exactly $\Hahn\R V$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The elementary subgroup already has this fixed field. Conversely an ordered
field automorphism fixing $t^g$ fixes its positive rational powers, hence all
$t^v$, $v\in V$, and strong linearity fixes every series supported on $V$.
\end{proof}

Naming $\omega=t^{-1}$ gives the fixed field $\Hahn\R\Q$, much larger than
$\R(\omega)$; naming all monomials with real exponents gives $\Hahn\R\R$ \src{04}.
On sets, $A\mapsto\HH(A)$ is extensive, monotone and idempotent, and $\HH(A)$ is
the smallest Hahn subfield with divisible exponent group containing every
monomial of the normal forms of $A$ \src{04}. For nonmonomial parameters
\cref{opa:thm:hull} computes the fixed field of $\EE_A$ only: a general strong
automorphism fixing a series may do so by cancellation, and $\HH(A)$ is not
asserted to be the model-theoretic definable closure \src{04}
(\cref{opa:q:relative}).

\subsection{Product actions and nonsolvable stabilizers}

\begin{theorem}[Faithful $\R^\kappa$ actions \src{09}]\label{opa:thm:product}
For every set $P\subseteq\No$ and every set cardinal $\kappa$ there is a faithful
action of the full product $\R^\kappa$ by strong $1$-automorphisms preserving
$\Oz$ and fixing $P$ pointwise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $E,T$ be as in \cref{opa:lem:avoid}, and choose an ordinal $\beta$ above
$\kappa$ and all of $T$. For $j<\kappa$ put $\delta_j=\omega^j$,
$\eta_j=\omega^{\beta+j}$ (ordinal addition in the index) and $\theta_j=q_{\beta+j}$.
Then $\theta_j|_E=0$, $\theta_j(C_{\delta_j})=0$, $\theta_j(\delta_\ell)=0$ and
$\theta_j(\eta_\ell)$ is the Kronecker delta. For $\mathbf a\in\R^\kappa$ let
$D_{\mathbf a}(t^\gamma)=t^\gamma\sum_{j<\kappa}a_j\theta_j(\gamma)t^{\delta_j}$; the shifts
form one well-ordered set, so \cref{opa:prop:uniform} applies with no finiteness
condition on $\mathbf a$. $D_{\mathbf a}$ kills the exponents of $P$. The
$D_{\mathbf a}$ commute by \eqref{opa:eq:bracket} and add, so
$\mathbf a\mapsto\Exp(D_{\mathbf a})$ is a homomorphism, and
$\Exp(D_{\mathbf a})(t^{\eta_j})=t^{\eta_j}\exp(a_jt^{\delta_j})$ detects every $a_j$.
The result is one class relation encoding a set-indexed action.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nonsolvable stabilizers \src{09}]\label{opa:thm:nonsolvable}
For every set $P\subseteq\No$ the pointwise stabilizer of $P$ in $\UU_\Oz$ is
nonsolvable; for every $r\ge2$ it contains a subgroup whose derived length is at
least $r-1$, and exactly $r-1$ granted \cref{opa:fact:import} for $\Gamma=\No$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose ordinals $\beta\le\eta_1<\cdots<\eta_r$ above $T$, and put $e_j=\omega^{\eta_j}$,
$\theta_i=q_{\eta_i}$. Then $e_1\bb\cdots\bb e_r$, $\theta_i(e_j)$ is the Kronecker
delta, and each $D_{e_j,\theta_i}$ ($j<i$) satisfies the criterion and kills
$P$. The balanced brackets of \cref{opa:lem:balanced} are unchanged, so
\cref{opa:lem:mixed} shows that the group generated by all
$\Exp(sD_{e_j,\theta_i})$, $s\in\R$, has derived length at least $r-1$; this
already gives nonsolvability. The upper bound applies the filtration by the
$r-1$ scales $C_{e_1},\dots,C_{e_{r-1}}$ as in \cref{opa:thm:derivedrank}; its
group step uses BCH.
\end{proof}

\section{What the pair \texorpdfstring{$(\No,\Oz)$}{(No, Oz)} remembers}\label{opa:sec:remember}

Sources 04, 08 and 09 each reconstruct the coefficient field from $\Oz$; 08 and
09 add first-order definitions of the ordinary integers and of $\Pi$. All of
this is now in the omnific Diophantine report, placed after the three pins,
and is cited rather than reprinted:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item $\Pi$ is defined in $\Oz$ and $\Og$ by $\exists y\,(x^2=2y^2)$
(\lbl{odg:def:thm:ideal}); the first-order definitions of $\Pi$ and of the graph
of $\ct$ through ordinary integers are \lbl{odg:cor:definablect}, and a
positive-existential definition of $\Z$ is \lbl{odg:thm:guard}; Pell rigidity is
\lbl{odg:cor:pell};
\item the multiplier identity $(\Pi:\Pi)=\Ak$, $k=\{0\}\cup\Ak^\times$ and its
internal first-order form (\lbl{odg:def:thm:multiplier},
\lbl{odg:def:cor:internal}); $\Frac\Oz=\No$ (\lbl{odg:thm:fractions});
\item the pure ring $\Oz$ interprets $\No$ with $\R$, the order, the finite ring,
the infinitesimals and the standard part (\lbl{odg:def:thm:realrecovery}); the
value group (\lbl{odg:def:cor:valuegroup}); every automorphism of $\Oz$ extends
uniquely to $\No$ and fixes $\R$ pointwise (\lbl{odg:def:thm:autreal});
\item for $\Og$: $\C$ is definable (\lbl{odg:def:cor:internal}), every field
automorphism of $\C$ lifts coefficientwise and restriction to $\C$ splits
(\lbl{odg:def:prop:coeffaut}); a phase twist shows that neither $\No$ nor
conjugation is definable from $\Og$ (\lbl{odg:def:lem:twist},
\lbl{odg:def:thm:norealaxis}).
\end{itemize}
In the sources: 04's intrinsic coefficient invariance, 08's characteristic
reconstruction, its restriction correspondence and its split restriction to
$\Aut\C$, and 09's corollary on all pair automorphisms re-derive
\lbl{odg:def:thm:multiplier}, \lbl{odg:def:thm:autreal} and
\lbl{odg:def:prop:coeffaut}. 08's formula
$J(x):\Leftrightarrow\forall n\,(\mathrm{Int}(n)\wedge n\ne0\Rightarrow\exists y\,x=ny)$
is the definition \lbl{odg:cor:definablect} in structure (present at 08's pin);
09's order-free formula
$\Phi_J(a):\Leftrightarrow a\in\Oz\wedge\forall u,v\in\Oz\,[u^2-2v^2=1\Rightarrow\exists
b\in\Oz\,a=ub]$ is a variant using all Pell first coordinates; both are dominated
by the one-witness existential formula \lbl{odg:def:thm:ideal}, and 08's
existential definition of $\Z$ (six witnesses, two inequations) by
\lbl{odg:thm:guard}. 09 observes that the order symbol can be omitted, positivity
being definable by nonzero squares. The following extras are not in the
Diophantine report.

\begin{lemma}[Order on $\Oz$ through fractions \src{08}]\label{opa:lem:positive}
For $z\in\Oz$, $z\ge0$ if and only if $\exists a,b\in\Oz\,(b\ne0\wedge a^2=zb^2)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $a^2=zb^2$ with $b\ne0$ then $z=(a/b)^2\ge0$. Conversely a square root of
$z\ge0$ exists in the real closed field $\No$ and is a quotient $a/b$ of omnific
integers (\lbl{odg:thm:fractions}).
\end{proof}

The formula is existential with one inequation, consistent with the Diophantine
report's result that the nonnegative cone is not positive-existentially
definable (\lbl{odg:prop:notdiophantine}).

\begin{theorem}[Zero-cut projections and floor \src{08, 09}]\label{opa:thm:projections}
In $(\No,+,\cdot,<,\Oz)$, equivalently in the fraction field interpreted in the
pure ring $\Oz$, the graphs of the projections $P_-,P_0,P_+$ on all of $\No$
(so $P_0=\ct$ extended to $\No$) and of the floor map $x\mapsto\lfloor x\rfloor_\Oz$
are parameter-free definable. For every monomial $m=t^\alpha$, truncation below
$\alpha$, $T_{<\alpha}(f)=m\,P_-(f/m)$, is definable with the single parameter $m$.
The same definitions work in $(\Hahn\R\Gamma,\mathcal R_\Z)$ for every nonzero
divisible ordered abelian $\Gamma$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\Pi$, $\R$, $\OO$ and $\mm$ are definable by the results cited above (in the
$t$-convention $\OO$ is the finite ring and $\mm$ the infinitesimals). For $x\in\No$,
$P_-(x)$ is the unique $j\in\Pi$ with $x-j\in\OO$: existence by splitting the
support at $0$, uniqueness from $\Pi\cap\OO=0$. Then $P_0(x)$ is the unique $c\in\R$
with $x-j-c\in\mm$, since $\R\cap\mm=0$, and $P_+=\id-P_--P_0$ \src{09}. The floor
graph is $y\in\Oz\wedge y\le x<y+1$, unique because distinct omnific integers
differ by at least one \src{08}; its value is \lbl{odg:thm:floor}. Dividing by
$m$ translates exponents by $-\alpha$, $P_-$ keeps those below $0$, and
multiplying by $m$ restores them \src{09}. For the set-sized field the proof uses
only real coefficients, the Hahn order (the field is real closed since
$\Gamma$ is divisible), and the definitions of $\Pi$ and $\R$: the formula of
\lbl{odg:def:thm:ideal} works for every $\Gamma$, and the multiplier identity
\lbl{odg:def:thm:multiplier} holds inside the whole Hahn field by the same
one-monomial proof \src{09}.
\end{proof}

Every pair automorphism therefore preserves $\Pi\oplus\R\oplus\mm$ summandwise,
fixes the real summand, and satisfies $\ct(\sigma x)=\ct(x)$ and
$\sigma(\lfloor x\rfloor)=\lfloor\sigma x\rfloor$ (\cref{opa:cor:allautos}).

\begin{remark}[Zero truncation is not the normal form \src{08, 09}]\label{opa:rem:zerotruncation}
$P_0$ is definable on all of $\No$ but is not multiplicative there. Definability of
$P_-$ does not define the set of monomials, and truncation at a named monomial is
not a uniform definable choice of such a boundary at every valuation; the
distinction matters in comparison with work on truncation \cite{Camacho}.
Recovering every coefficient by $[\omega^g]x=\ct(\omega^{-g}x)$ would need the
monomial map $g\mapsto\omega^g$, and \cref{opa:sec:nondef} shows that this is a genuine
obstruction.
\end{remark}

Source 08's Question 2, whether the reconstruction of $\C$ from the pure ring
$\Og$ is first-order, is answered positively by \lbl{odg:def:cor:internal}, whose
formula $\exists y\,(x^2=2y^2)$ defines $\Pi_\C$ in $\Og$ and whose multiplier
formula then defines $\C$; 09 gives an independent route, its Pell-divisibility
formula with the Gaussian Pell solutions (such as $(i,i)$) handled, followed by
the idealizer. The question is not repeated here.

\section{What the pair forgets: monomials and representatives}\label{opa:sec:nondef}

\begin{theorem}[Set-parameter invisibility \src{04, 08, 09}]\label{opa:thm:parameters}
For every set $P\subseteq\No$ there is a nonidentity strong real-linear
$1$-automorphism $\sigma$ of $\No$ with $\sigma(\Oz)=\Oz$ that fixes
$P\cup\R\cup\{\omega\}$ pointwise and sends a positive Conway monomial in $\Oz$ to a
nonmonomial in $\Oz$. Explicitly, with $\beta$ as in \cref{opa:lem:avoid} and
$\eta=\omega^\beta$, one may take $\sigma=\sigma_{1,q_\beta}$, and then
\begin{equation}\label{opa:eq:moveH}
 \sigma(t^{-\eta})=t^{-\eta}\exp(-t)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(-1)^n}{n!}t^{-\eta+n},
 \qquad\text{that is}\qquad
 \sigma(\omega^{\omega^\beta})=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(-1)^n}{n!}\,\omega^{\omega^\beta-n}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, in $\mathfrak S=(\No,0,1,+,\cdot,<,\Oz)$ neither the monomial class
$\Mon=\{\omega^x:x\in\No\}$ nor the graph of $x\mapsto\omega^x$ is definable with
parameters from any set, even after naming $\R$ as a predicate, every ordinary
real, $\omega$, and any further set of surreal constants.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$q_\beta$ kills $C_1$ since $\beta>0$, so $\sigma\in\UU_\Oz$ by \cref{opa:thm:shear}. It
fixes each monomial with exponent in $E$, hence every member of $P$ by strongness,
and it fixes $\R$ and $t^{-1}=\omega$ because $q_\beta(-1)=0$. Since
$q_\beta(-\eta)=-1$, \eqref{opa:eq:moveH} follows; all exponents $-\eta+n$ are negative
because $\eta$ is infinite, and the terms at $-\eta$ and $-\eta+1$ show that the image
is not a monomial. A class definable from parameters fixed pointwise by an
automorphism of $\mathfrak S$ is invariant under it; $\sigma$ preserves every symbol
and moves an element of $\Mon$ out of $\Mon$. A definable graph of the omega-map
would make its range definable by an existential quantifier.
\end{proof}

The three sources give the same construction: 04 with $a=[\omega^\beta]\cdot$ and
explicit fixing of $\R\cup\{\omega\}$, 08 with the witness
$\omega^{\omega^\alpha}\mapsto\sum_n\omega^{\omega^\alpha-n}/n!$ (its sign convention), 09
with $q_\eta$. An individual formula uses finitely many parameters; the theorem is
the stronger statement that one automorphism fixes an entire prescribed set.
Nothing is claimed after naming a proper class of parameters: naming every
surreal would remove the symmetry \src{04, 08}. Expanding the structure by the
already definable order, floor or zero-truncation maps cannot remove the
obstruction \src{08}.

\begin{corollary}[\src{04, 09}]\label{opa:cor:puring}
The monomial class is not determined, as an automorphism-invariant subclass, by
$\Oz$, its ordered fraction field and any set of parameters; the standard
normal-form section of the natural valuation is not first-order recoverable
from these data. The Conway monomials lying in $\Oz$ are not definable in the
pure ring $\Oz$ with parameters from any set.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The range of the section is $\Mon$. For the pure ring, restrict the witness of
\cref{opa:thm:parameters} to $\Oz$, with parameters in $\Oz$; automorphisms of $\Oz$
and field automorphisms of $\No$ preserving $\Oz$ correspond
(\lbl{odg:def:thm:autreal}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{No definable representative rule}

Write $x\sim_vy$ if $v(x)=v(y)$ on $\No^\times$. A \emph{valuation representative
rule} is a function $s:\No^\times\to\No^\times$ with $v(s(x))=v(x)$ and
$s(x)=s(y)$ whenever $x\sim_vy$. For a set-sized field this is a section of the
quotient by $\OO^\times$; the formulation also makes sense on the class $\No$
without treating proper-class equivalence classes as elements. No
multiplicativity, positivity or monomial-image assumption is imposed \src{09}.

\begin{lemma}[A whole fiber moves \src{09}]\label{opa:lem:fiber}
For $\sigma$ and $\eta$ as in \cref{opa:thm:parameters}, every $f$ with $v(f)=-\eta$
satisfies $v(\sigma f-f)=-\eta+1$ and $\lc(\sigma f-f)=-\lc(f)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is \cref{opa:thm:fixedshear} with $\gamma_0=-\eta$ and $\theta(-\eta)=-1$: later
input terms contribute beyond $-\eta+1$, higher iterates at $-\eta+n$, $n\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No definable section \src{09}]\label{opa:thm:nosection}
For every set $P\subseteq\No$, no $P$-definable function on $\No^\times$ is a
valuation representative rule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $x=t^{-\eta}$. Since $\sigma$ fixes valuations, $\sigma x\sim_vx$ and
$s(\sigma x)=s(x)$, while $P$-definability gives $s(\sigma x)=\sigma(s(x))$. So $s(x)$
would be a fixed point of $\sigma$ with valuation $-\eta$, contradicting
\cref{opa:lem:fiber}.
\end{proof}

This is the strongest nondefinability statement of the sources; it covers any
representative rule, including one-term ones, and does not follow from character
twists. The nondefinability is relative to the field-and-integer-part language;
Conway's normal forms remain set-theoretically defined in the ambient foundation
\src{09}.

\begin{remark}[Character twists, one-term series and the $\RV$ sort \src{04, 02}]\label{opa:rem:twists}
Bare nondefinability of the coefficient-one monomials already follows from
character twists: for a nonzero additive $a:\No\to\R$ with $a(V_A)=0$
(\cref{opa:lem:separation}), $\sum c_gt^g\mapsto\sum c_ge^{a(g)}t^g$ preserves $\Oz$,
fixes $A$ and sends some monomial to a non-unit multiple of itself \src{04}. These
twists are the $M_{\chi,\id}$ of \cref{opa:thm:factorization}; they are 02's
$\tau_{\chi,t}$ (\cref{opa:par:rem:actual}), the real form of the Diophantine
report's phase twist (\lbl{odg:def:lem:twist}), and the collection's character
twists (\lbl{saut:thm:decomp}); they are established and not claimed new. What
the shears add is motion inside the leading-term kernel with more than one
nonzero term. In particular the class of nonzero one-term series
$\{c\omega^g:c\in\R^\times,g\in\No\}$ is not definable from the ordered field,
$\Oz$ and set parameters: the shear sends a one-term series to a multiterm one,
which rescaling cannot do \src{04}. Nondefinability also survives adding the sort
$\RV(K)=K^\times/(1+\mm)$ with its multiplication, the quotient map and \emph{every}
element of that sort named, since a $1$-automorphism acts trivially on it (for the
class $\No$ this is a metatheoretic expansion, read formula by formula). This
must be distinguished from naming a representative map $\RV(K)\to K$, which the
Conway monomials partly constitute and which would destroy the symmetry by design:
$\Phi_s(t^{-\omega})$ has leading term $t^{-\omega}$, yet $\Phi_s$ moves $t^{-\omega}$
\src{04}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Contrasts in the collection]\label{opa:rem:contrasts}
The single dilation $S_2(\sum a_gt^g)=\sum a_gt^{2g}$ \emph{defines} the canonical
monomials, every coefficient and truncation in the language of fields with one
automorphism (\lbl{dsup:thm:definability} in
\fn{docs/surcomplex/single-dilation-hahn-support}); $(\No,\Oz)$ does not, even with
set parameters. The two expansions differ exactly on the monomial section, and
there is no conflict: the shears do not commute with $S_2$, in line with that
report's centralizer theorem (\lbl{dsup:thm:centralizer}). The shears are not
exponential: by \lbl{cor:question54} of the rigidity report
\fn{docs/surreal/exponential-automorphism-rigidity} (from its \lbl{thm:no}), every
exponential $1$-automorphism of $\No$ is the identity, which is stronger than
\cite[Proposition 5.2]{KKS} (strongly linear exponential $1$-automorphisms are
trivial), the result cited by 09. The shears are compatible with that report's
displacement lemma: their displacement $-t^{-\eta+1}$ is infinite. Nor do they
preserve the omega-map, whose compatibility questions \cite[Questions 5.6,
5.7]{KKS} are untouched here.
\end{remark}

\section{Surcomplex and Gaussian consequences}\label{opa:sec:complex}

Identify $\SC$ with $\Hahn\C\No$ by taking coefficientwise real and imaginary
parts; the union of the two supports is well ordered. Then
$\Og=\Z[i]\oplus\Pi_\C=\Oz+i\Oz$, which is not an ordered ring; conjugation is
coefficientwise \src{04, 08, 09}.

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian stabilizer and conjugation-compatible witnesses \src{04, 09}]\label{opa:thm:complex}
\Cref{opa:thm:criterion} applies to $(k,\oo)=(\C,\Z[i])$ with the same condition.
The real-coefficient shears and actions of \cref{opa:sec:fixed,opa:sec:nondef} extend
$\C$-linearly, commute with conjugation, preserve $\No$ and $\Og$; for every set of
surcomplex parameters they fix the parameters and give nondefinability of the
monomials and of any valuation representative rule, even with the real axis,
conjugation and every ordinary complex constant named. Their common fixed field
is $\C$, and for every set $A\subseteq\SC$ the real elementary shears fixing $A$ have
common fixed field $\Hahn\C{V_A}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The criterion was proved for every characteristic-zero $k$ and unital $\oo$, and its
proof uses no order on coefficients. The functionals $q_\beta$ are real, so the
derivations and their exponentials commute with conjugation and restrict to the
real automorphisms on $\No$. Use the union of the real and imaginary supports in
\cref{opa:lem:avoid}; the moved monomial is real, and \cref{opa:lem:fiber} holds with
any nonzero complex leading coefficient. For the fixed field, the first
displacement of \cref{opa:thm:fixedshear} is a product of a nonzero real functional
value and a nonzero complex coefficient. The hull statement is \cref{opa:thm:hull}
with $k=\C$ \src{04}.
\end{proof}

For these real-axis-preserving maps the surreal modulus is equivariant,
$|\sigma(z)|=\sigma(|z|)$, since $|z|$ is the nonnegative square root of $z\overline z$;
this is not a statement that moduli are fixed \src{09}.

\begin{theorem}[The real axis inside the leading-term kernel \src{04}]\label{opa:thm:realaxis}
For every set $A\subseteq\SC$ there is a strong $\C$-linear $1$-automorphism of $\SC$
that preserves $\Og$, fixes $A$ pointwise and does not preserve $\No$. Hence $\No$
is not definable in $(\SC,0,1,+,\cdot,\Og)$ with parameters from any set, even with
all ordinary complex constants named and the Hahn valuation ring as a predicate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $\beta$ above the inner exponents of the outer supports of $A$, and the flow
$\Phi_s$ of \cref{opa:thm:shear} with $\theta=q_\beta$, $\delta=1$ and the imaginary time
$s=i$. It fixes $A$ and is strong, $\C$-linear, leading-term preserving and
$\Og$-preserving. With $\eta=\omega^\beta$,
$\Phi_i(t^{-\eta})=t^{-\eta}\exp(-it)=t^{-\eta}-it^{-\eta+1}-\tfrac12t^{-\eta+2}+\cdots$
has a nonreal coefficient, although $t^{-\eta}$ is real; all its exponents are
negative. A $1$-automorphism preserves every valuation, hence the valuation ring.
\end{proof}

Phase twists preserving $\Og$ and moving the real axis are already in the
collection (\lbl{saut:thm:phase}; \lbl{odg:def:lem:twist},
\lbl{odg:def:thm:norealaxis}); the new points are arbitrary set-parameter fixing
\emph{inside the complete leading-term kernel} and multiterm images. The real
shears commute with conjugation and the imaginary-time shear does not; the two
conclusions must not be combined into a claim that one automorphism commutes with
conjugation and moves its fixed field \src{04}.

\begin{proposition}[A wild coefficient lift \src{08}]\label{opa:prop:wild}
There is a strong automorphism of $\SC$ that preserves $\Og$, fixes $\Z[i]$ and every
Conway monomial, and sends an ordinary real number outside $\R$. So neither $\R$ nor
$\No$ is invariant under all field automorphisms preserving $\Og$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a real $r$ transcendental over $\Q$ and a transcendence basis of $\C$ over
$\overline\Q$ containing it. On the rational function field it generates, fix
$\overline\Q$ and the other basis elements and send $r\mapsto r+i$ (inverse
$r\mapsto r-i$), and extend to an automorphism $\rho$ of $\C$ by the set-sized
algebraic-closure extension theorem; the extension is onto, since its image is
algebraically closed and $\C$ is algebraic over the base. The coefficientwise lift
$\hat\rho$ (\lbl{odg:def:prop:coeffaut}) preserves $\Og$ and monomials and sends
$r$ to $r+i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Gaussian fixed field \src{08, 09}]\label{opa:cor:gaussianfixed}
The common fixed elements in $\SC$ of all field automorphisms preserving $\Og$ are
exactly $\Q$; in $\Og$ they are $\Z$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The shears of \cref{opa:thm:complex} reduce a common fixed point to $\C$. Every field
automorphism of $\C$ lifts coefficientwise and preserves $\Og$
(\lbl{odg:def:prop:coeffaut}). A nonrational algebraic number is moved by a
conjugate embedding extended to $\C$; a transcendental one by a translation of a
transcendence basis element; rationals are fixed. Finally $\Q\cap\Og=\Z$.
\end{proof}

This agrees with \lbl{saut:prop:fixedfields} of the surcomplex automorphism report,
$\Fix(\Aut\SC)=\Q$, for the larger collection of all field automorphisms. The real
and complex answers differ because a real automorphism preserves order and cannot
move an ordinary real, whereas a complex one can unless real structure is required;
the explicit witnesses of \cref{opa:thm:complex} fix all of $\C$ \src{09}. Three
mechanisms move the real axis while preserving $\Og$: the phase twist of the
Diophantine report (fixes $\C$, preserves supports), the imaginary-time shear of
\cref{opa:thm:realaxis} (fixes $\C$ and all leading terms), and the wild lift of
\cref{opa:prop:wild} (fixes monomials, moves $\R$). The real shears of
\cref{opa:thm:complex} preserve the real axis.

\section{Scope, answered questions, and further questions}\label{opa:sec:scope}

\subsection{What is settled, and status in other reports}\label{opa:sub:status}

For a strong infinitesimal automorphism, preservation of the integer part is
exactly a convex-scale annihilation condition; the size of the residual symmetry is
measured by a sharp solvability bound in finite rank and by arbitrarily large
parameter-fixing actions on $\No$; and the omnific predicate recovers the
coefficient fields and zero-cut truncation but no definable valuation
cross-section \src{09}. An attempt to reconstruct Conway monomials by a
first-order formula in the pair cannot succeed with any set of constants; a
classification of strong $\Oz$-automorphisms by coefficient and exponent actions
alone misses the higher-rank factor $\UU_\Oz$; and an alleged rank-one
nontrivial $1$-automorphism preserving a divisible-exponent integer part must violate
a support hypothesis, as \cref{opa:ex:translation} shows \src{09}.

The results bear on three questions of other reports. They are recorded here;
the other reports are not edited by this merge.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \lbl{osq:q:invisible} of \fn{docs/surreal/set-sized-quotients-of-omnific-integers}
asks, in its second clause, how much an automorphism of $\Oz$ can do inside $\Pi$
while fixing $\ct$. \emph{Partly answered:} every automorphism of $\Oz$ fixes $\R$
and $\ct$ (\lbl{odg:def:thm:autreal}, \cref{opa:cor:allautos}); the \emph{strong}
ones are exactly $\{M_{\chi,\tau}\}\ltimes\UU_\Oz$, with $\UU_\Oz$ classified by
\cref{opa:thm:criterion}; they move monomials to infinite series, fix any set of
parameters, carry $\R^\kappa$ and have nonsolvable stabilizers
(\cref{opa:thm:parameters,opa:thm:product,opa:thm:nonsolvable}). Nonstrong
automorphisms, and the first clause of that question, remain open.
\item \lbl{odg:def:q:realform} of the Diophantine report asks which natural
structure on $\Og$ rules out the coefficient-fixing twists and determines the real
omnific subring. \emph{Negative information, not an answer:} naming the valuation
ring, every complex constant, the $\RV$ sort, or requiring leading-term fixing does
not suffice (\cref{opa:thm:realaxis}); and in $(\SC,\Og)$ even $\R\subseteq\C$ is not
invariant (\cref{opa:prop:wild}).
\item The surcomplex automorphism report's research direction ``effective
descriptions inside the leading-term kernel'' (its section \lbl{saut:sec:questions})
is \emph{not} answered. Its $\Oz$-stabilizing part is now described by coefficient
functionals (\cref{opa:thm:criterion,opa:cor:operator}), with finite support
certificates on the common-positive-shift class (\cref{opa:thm:lie}); no
exhaustion or generation theorem is proved (\cref{opa:q:generation}). Its
omega-map compatibility questions are untouched.
\end{itemize}
The novelty statements of the sources were checked against the tree at the merge:
no report contains a classification of $\Oz$-preserving automorphisms, the rank
dichotomy or set-parameter nondefinability of monomials, and \lbl{osq:q:invisible}
was recorded as open. Their reconstruction and definability claims were stale
(\cref{opa:sec:remember}).

\subsection{Further questions}\label{opa:sub:questions}

These questions arise from the proofs; they are not presented as named historical
problems \src{04, 08, 09}.

\begin{question}[Strongness \src{04, 09}]\label{opa:q:strong}
Which ring automorphisms of $\Oz$ extend to strong field automorphisms of $\No$?
Does preservation of $\Oz$ force strongness, or is there a nonstrong
$\Oz$-preserving example? Equivalently for 09: characterize the nonstrong
automorphisms of $(\No,\Oz)$ that commute with all set-indexed normal-form sums.
\end{question}
\Cref{opa:cor:allautos} removes coefficient motion from this question but does not
settle preservation of infinite sums; nothing is inferred from the existence of
nonstrong automorphisms of the ambient field \cite{KKS}.

\begin{question}[Admissible logarithmic characters \src{04}]\label{opa:q:characters}
Give testable necessary and sufficient conditions on an additive $h:\Gamma\to\mm$
for $t^g\mapsto t^g\exp h(g)$ to define a strong bijective field map. Within that
class the integer-part condition is exactly $a_\delta(C_\delta)=0$.
\end{question}
Common positive support is one sufficient regime; the convex condition alone is not
asserted to solve the global problem. This is the problem the surcomplex report
describes under ``what monomial prescriptions do not automatically give''.

\begin{question}[Full relative fixed fields \src{04}]\label{opa:q:relative}
For a set $A$ of parameters, determine $\Fix((\UU_\Oz)_A)$. It is contained in
$\Hahn\R{V_A}$, with equality for monomial parameters
(\cref{opa:cor:monoparams}); which conditions on $A$ force equality?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Generation and exhaustion \src{04, 08}]\label{opa:q:generation}
How much of $\UU_\Oz$ is generated by elementary shears and their
support-controlled limits, and can an intrinsic summation convention make this
precise without one common shift set for all inputs? Can all strong
leading-term-fixing $\Oz$-preserving automorphisms be described by a necessary and
sufficient uniform support condition on their monomial phases?
\end{question}
No density or generation theorem is claimed; fine-topology convergence is not a
substitute for a strong summation rule; the finite-dimensional phase condition of
\cref{opa:thm:phase} is sufficient only.

\begin{question}[Weaker normal-form data \src{08}]\label{opa:q:weaker}
Which normal-form data weaker than the omega-map remove the monomial
nondefinability? Set-sized collections of constants do not
(\cref{opa:thm:parameters}).
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Non-divisible exponent groups \mergetag]\label{opa:q:nondivisible}
For non-divisible $\Gamma$ and $\oo=\Z$, is a strong $k$-linear $1$-automorphism
preserving $\mathcal R_\Z$ characterized by a convex-annihilator condition, as
single flows are (\cref{opa:thm:threshold})?
\end{question}
This records the scope correction of \cref{opa:rem:divisibility}.

\part{Algebraic-parameter rigidity of the omnific integers}\label{opa:part:par}

This part is manuscript 02. Its question is different from that of
\cref{opa:part:aut}: not which automorphisms preserve a support condition, but
whether $\Oz$ admits nonconstant \emph{algebraic} families of embeddings. It does
not: the decisive property is that every nonzero element divides an element with
roots of every order. It does admit formal families of all orders. The relation to
\cref{opa:part:aut} is recorded in \cref{opa:par:sec:relation}.

\section{Conventions and the root-covered property}\label{opa:par:sec:foundations}

\subsection{Conventions of this part}\label{opa:par:sub:conv}

Ring homomorphisms are unital, domains are commutative integral domains, and
$\N=\{0,1,2,\dots\}$; degrees of polynomials are ordinary integers. This part keeps
its source's \emph{large-monomial} convention, which is that of the other omnific
reports: for a characteristic-zero field $k$, a unital subring $\oo\subseteq k$ and a
nonzero divisible ordered abelian group $\Gamma$,
\[
 k((X^\Gamma))=\Bigl\{\sum_{g\in S}a_gX^g:S\subseteq\Gamma\text{ reverse well ordered}\Bigr\},
 \qquad X^g=t^{-g},
\]
so the leading exponent $\ell(x)=\max\supp x$ is the largest, and
$\ell(xy)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)$. The ideal $\Pi_k(\Gamma)$ consists of the series supported
in $\Gamma^{>0}$ (the same set as $\Pi$ in \cref{opa:part:aut}), and
$\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)=\oo+\Pi_k(\Gamma)$ is the ring $\Ro$ of \cref{opa:part:aut},
the Diophantine report's $\mathcal R_\oo(\Bbbk,\Gamma)$. For surreals $X=\omega$, so
$\Oz=\mathcal R_\Z(\R,\No)$ and $\Og=\mathcal R_{\Z[i]}(\C,\No)$. Renamings from the
source: its $R_\Gamma(D,k)$ and $\mathcal P_\Gamma(k)$ are
$\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)$ and $\Pi_k(\Gamma)$; its principal convex subgroup $H(a)$ is
$C(a)$, the transcendence report's notation ($C_\delta$ in \cref{opa:part:aut}; the
quotient report's $H_a$ means the opposite, the elements infinitesimal relative to
$a$); its covering element $c$ is
$z$; its bounded-scale field $K_b$ is $K_{\mathrm{bd}}$ and its stages $H_j$ are
$\Gamma_{\le j}$. Elsewhere in this part the letters $R,B,A,K,F,L$ denote rings and
fields local to each statement.

The general theorems are proved for sets. Their conclusions for $\Oz$, $\Og$, $\No$
and $\SC$ use class functions and set-supported normal forms. A statement about one
polynomial contains finitely many coefficients and variables, so the degree
arguments apply to a class-sized coefficient domain unchanged. No proper-class
scheme $\operatorname{Spec}(\Oz)$ and no class compactness or Noetherian theorem is
used; the finite-type theorem is a theorem about sets, applied to the set-sized
models of \cref{opa:par:sec:workspaces}. Expressions involving $\Aut(\Oz)$ or
$\ML$ of a class ring abbreviate statements about each automorphism or derivation;
$(b,x)\mapsto D_b(x)$ is one definable class operation. For cancellation against a
class-sized $B$, the transcendence-degree arguments involve only finite tuples of
indeterminates inside one rational-function field \src{02}.

\begin{definition}[\src{02}]\label{opa:par:def:rootcover}
A domain $R$ is \emph{root-covered} if for every $0\ne a\in R$ there are $b\in R$ and
$z=ab\ne0$ such that for every $n\ge1$ some $z_n\in R$ has $z_n^n=z$. (No
compatibility between the $z_n$ is required; the terminology is local to this
report.)
\end{definition}

Fields are root-covered. The interesting examples have few units: $\Oz^\times=\{\pm1\}$
and $\Og^\times=\{\pm1,\pm i\}$, and in general $\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)^\times=\oo^\times$ by
additivity of leading exponents (the Diophantine report's \lbl{odg:prop:units},
\lbl{odg:def:lem:units}).

\begin{lemma}[Localization \src{02}]\label{opa:par:lem:localize}
If $R$ is root-covered and $S\subseteq R\setminus\{0\}$ is multiplicative, then $S^{-1}R$ is
root-covered.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$(a/s)(sb)=z$, and the roots $z_n$ remain.
\end{proof}

Root-covered is sufficient for the conclusions below and is not asserted
necessary; failure of the monomial criterion is not used to claim failure of
cancellation or stable rigidity \src{02}.

\section{The monomial-divisibility criterion}\label{opa:par:sec:hahn}

For $a>0$ in $\Gamma$ let $C(a)=\{g:|g|\le na\text{ for some }n\ge1\}$; $a$ is an
\emph{order unit} if $C(a)=\Gamma$.

\begin{theorem}[Monomial divisibility \src{02}; after \lbl{bst:thm:fieldcriterion}]\label{opa:par:thm:scale}
For $\Gamma\ne0$ divisible and $R=\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)$ the following are equivalent:
\textup{(i)} $\Gamma$ has no order unit; \textup{(ii)} for every $0\ne x\in R$ some
monomial $X^c$ with $c>0$ is divisible by $x$ in $R$. Then the quotient can be chosen
in $\Pi_k(\Gamma)$, and $R$ is root-covered.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume (i). The support of $x$ lies in $[0,a]$ for some $a>0$. The inverse of $x$ in
the Hahn field has support in the subgroup generated by $\supp x$, hence in $C(a)$
(write $x=\alpha X^h(1+\varepsilon)$ and expand the geometric series). Choose $c>na$ for all
$n$; then $X^cx^{-1}$ has strictly positive support, lies in $\Pi_k(\Gamma)$, and
$x\cdot X^cx^{-1}=X^c$. Divisibility of $\Gamma$ gives $(X^{c/n})^n=X^c$. Conversely,
if $a$ is an order unit and $x=1+X^a$, the unique Hahn quotient
$X^c/(1+X^a)=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n-1}X^{c-na}$ has a nonzero coefficient at a negative
exponent, so it is not in $R$.
\end{proof}

This is the transcendence report's field criterion \lbl{bst:thm:fieldcriterion}
(\fn{docs/surreal/transcendence-over-bounded-support}: the bounded-support ring is a
field if and only if $\Gamma$ has no order unit, by the same two arguments, the
inverse support in a proper convex subgroup and the geometric series of $1-t^u$),
restricted to positive-support elements; its ``order unit'' is defined identically.
Source 02's audit read only the openings of the omnific reports and did not see
it. The positive-support phrasing is what the rigidity theorems use.

\begin{example}[\src{02}; \cite{LM}]\label{opa:par:ex:omega}
In $\No$, $\omega>n$ for all ordinary $n$, so
$\omega^\omega/(1+\omega)=\omega^{\omega-1}-\omega^{\omega-2}+\omega^{\omega-3}-\cdots$ is an
omnific integer: $1+\omega$ divides $\omega^\omega$. With exponent group $\Q$ no exponent
can absorb the tail. The example is L'Innocente and Mantova's; the distinction is
a larger Archimedean scale, not convergence.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[Omnific root coverage \src{02}]\label{opa:par:cor:ozcover}
$\Oz$ and $\Og$ are root-covered: every nonzero element divides a positive real
monomial $\omega^c$, with quotient of strictly positive support.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
This is immediate from the global monomial clearing \lbl{odg:thm:fractions} of the
Diophantine report, applied to $x^{-1}$: some $\omega^c$ makes $\omega^cx^{-1}$ purely
infinite. Source 02 gives the same argument through \cref{opa:par:thm:scale}, using
a strict upper bound of the set $\{na:n\ge1\}$; the coefficients' order is not used.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-type domains carry no varying embedding}\label{opa:par:sec:finitetype}

The results of this section concern set-sized fields and domains.

\begin{lemma}[Unbounded roots \src{02}]\label{opa:par:lem:rootsfield}
Let $F/K$ be finitely generated and $u\in F^\times$. If $u$ has $n$th roots in $F$ for
an unbounded set of $n$, then $u$ is algebraic over $K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise extend $u$ to a transcendence basis $u,t_2,\dots,t_r$, with
$[F:E]=d<\infty$ for $E=K(u,t_2,\dots,t_r)$. $Z^n-u$ is Eisenstein at the prime $u$ of
$K(t_2,\dots,t_r)[u]$, hence irreducible over $E$ by Gauss's lemma, so an $n$th root
has degree $n\le d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Algebraic constants \src{02}]\label{opa:par:lem:constants}
If $F/K$ is finitely generated, the relative algebraic closure $K'$ of $K$ in $F$ is
finite over $K$; if $A$ is an integral $K$-algebra with $\Frac A=F$, then
$C_A=A\cap K'$ is a field finite over $K$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With a transcendence basis $\mathbf t$ and $[F:K(\mathbf t)]=d$, every finitely generated
$L\subseteq K'$ has $[L:K]=[L(\mathbf t):K(\mathbf t)]\le d$ (adjoining algebraically
independent indeterminates preserves linear independence; $\mathbf t$ stays
independent over $L$). An $L$ of maximal degree contains every element of $K'$. For
$0\ne a\in C_A$ the minimal polynomial has nonzero constant term, which expresses
$a^{-1}$ as a polynomial in $a$ over $K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finitely many unit translates \src{02}]\label{opa:par:lem:translates}
Let $A$ be a finitely generated integral $K$-algebra and $f\in A$ transcendental over
$K$. Only finitely many $\lambda\in K$ make $f-\lambda$ a unit of $A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Extend $f$ to a transcendence basis $f,t_2,\dots,t_r$ of $\Frac A$, and put
$B_0=K[f,t_2,\dots,t_r]$. Generators of $A$ are algebraic over $\Frac B_0$; clearing
denominators gives $0\ne d\in B_0$ with $A$ integral over $B=B_0[1/d]$ in the common
overring. If $(f-\lambda)^{-1}\in A$, it lies in $\Frac B_0$ and is integral over the
integrally closed $B$, so $f-\lambda$ divides a power of $d$ in the UFD $B_0$, hence
divides $d$; the primes $f-\lambda$ are pairwise nonassociate. The argument does not
assume $B\subseteq A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-type embedding rigidity \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:finitetype}
Let $R$ be a characteristic-zero root-covered domain, $K$ a characteristic-zero
field, and $A$ a finitely generated integral $K$-algebra. Every injective
homomorphism $\phi:R\to A$ has image in the field
$C_A=\{x\in A:x\text{ algebraic over }K\}$, finite over $K$, and extends uniquely to
$\Frac R\to C_A$. If $K$ is relatively algebraically closed in $\Frac A$, then $C_A=K$.
Neither $K$-linearity of $\phi$ nor a map $K\to R$ is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $a\ne0$ take $ab=z$ and $z_n^n=z$. Then $\phi(z)\ne0$ has roots of all orders, so it
is algebraic (\cref{opa:par:lem:rootsfield}) and a unit of $A$
(\cref{opa:par:lem:constants}); hence $\phi(a)$ is a unit. If $f=\phi(a)$ were
transcendental, every $f-m=\phi(a-m)$, $m\in\Z$, would be a unit (as $a-m\ne0$),
contradicting \cref{opa:par:lem:translates}. So $\phi(R)\subseteq C_A$, and nonzero
images are invertible there.
\end{proof}

The theorem is nontrivial only for set-sized $R$, such as the Hahn models of
\cref{opa:par:sec:workspaces}; it is not a disguised statement that a proper class
cannot inject into a set. One must not replace $C_A$ by the whole relative algebraic
closure: $A=\Q[x,\sqrt2x]$ has $\Frac A=\Q(\sqrt2)(x)$ but $C_A=\Q$, since elements of
$A$ have rational constant term; no normality of $A$ is assumed \src{02}. If $K$ is
relatively algebraically closed in $\Frac A$, every embedding $R\to A$ has image in
$K$, and none exists if $\Frac R$ does not embed in $K$.

\begin{theorem}[Pointed parameter spaces \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:pointed}
Let $R$ be a set-sized characteristic-zero root-covered domain, $F=\Frac R$, $A$ a
finite-type integral $F$-algebra with an $F$-algebra map $\varepsilon:A\to F$. If a
ring homomorphism $\psi:R\to A$ has $\varepsilon\psi$ equal to the inclusion, then
$\psi(x)=x$ for all $x$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\psi$ is injective, so $\psi(R)\subseteq C_A$. The restriction of $\varepsilon$ to the
field $C_A$ is injective and fixes $F$; for $a\in C_A$, $a-\varepsilon(a)\in C_A$ has
image $0$, so $C_A=F$.
\end{proof}

Examples are $A=F[s_1,\dots,s_r,1/h]$ with an $F$-point where $h\ne0$; relations,
singularities and localizations are allowed, and relative algebraic closedness is not
required. The hypothesis is one common finite-type algebra containing the images of
all of $R$, not separate formulas in unrelated algebras. For a finite extension
$L/F$, $L[s]$ contains the root-covered $L$ as constants and has no $F$-valued
augmentation when $L\ne F$ \src{02}.

\section{Polynomial and Laurent coefficient rigidity}\label{opa:par:sec:coefficient}

These proofs work over arbitrary coefficient domains, need no finite generation and
assume no preservation of infinite sums \src{02}.

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial coefficient rigidity \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:poly}
If $R$ is root-covered and $B$ a domain, every injective $\phi:R\to B[T_1,\dots,T_n]$
has image in $B$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\deg\phi(z)=n\deg\phi(z_n)$ for all $n$ forces $\deg\phi(z)=0$, and
$\deg\phi(a)+\deg\phi(b)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Laurent units \src{02}]\label{opa:par:lem:laurentunits}
For a domain $B$, the units of $B[U_1^{\pm1},\dots,U_q^{\pm1}]$ are the
$b\mathbf U^{\mathbf e}$ with $b\in B^\times$ and $\mathbf e\in\Z^q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The widths $M_j-m_j$ of the $U_j$-exponents are nonnegative and additive, so a unit
has a single exponent vector.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Laurent coefficient rigidity \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:laurent}
If $R$ is root-covered and $B$ a domain, every injective
$\phi:R\to B[\mathbf U^{\pm1}]$ has image in $B$; hence so does every injective
$\phi:R\to B[\mathbf T,\mathbf U^{\pm1}]$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The largest and smallest $U_j$-exponents of $\phi(z)$ are divisible by every $n$, so
$\phi(z)$ is a nonzero constant, a unit over $\Frac B$, and $\phi(a)$ is a Laurent
monomial by \cref{opa:par:lem:laurentunits}. If $a\ne-1$ the same holds for $\phi(a)+1$,
which forces $\phi(a)$ to be constant. For mixed targets apply
\cref{opa:par:thm:poly} over $B[\mathbf U^{\pm1}]$ first.
\end{proof}

Injectivity and a domain target are needed: $x\mapsto x+sD(x)$ into $R[s]/(s^2)$ can
be nonconstant with identity specialization (\cref{opa:par:sec:formal}). No
noninjective analogue for all targets is claimed \src{02}.

\begin{theorem}[Strong coefficient invariance \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:cancellation}
Let $R$ be root-covered, $B$ any commutative unital ring, and
$\Phi:R[X_1,\dots,X_p,U_1^{\pm1},\dots,U_q^{\pm1}]\to
B[Y_1,\dots,Y_{p'},V_1^{\pm1},\dots,V_{q'}^{\pm1}]$ an isomorphism with
$p+q=p'+q'<\infty$. Then $\Phi(R)=B$, $q=q'$ and $p=p'$. In particular, for
$R=\Oz$ or $\Og$, every isomorphism $R[\mathbf X_n]\cong B[\mathbf Y_n]$ carries $R$
onto $B$: $R$ is strongly invariant and cancels against an arbitrary coefficient
ring with the same number of variables. If $B$ is also root-covered, the equality of
total variable counts can be dropped.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The target is a domain, so $B$ is, and \cref{opa:par:thm:laurent} gives
$\Phi(R)\subseteq B$. With $F=\Frac\Phi(R)\subseteq L=\Frac B\subseteq E$,
$\trdeg_FE=p+q=p'+q'=\trdeg_LE$, so $L/F$ is algebraic. $E$ is a rational function
field over $F$, which has no new algebraic constants (a nonconstant rational function
is transcendental, one variable at a time), so $B\subseteq F$, and comparing coefficients
gives $B\subseteq\Phi(R)$. Polynomial variables add no units, and the quotient unit
groups give $\Z^q\cong\Z^{q'}$. If both rings are root-covered, apply the first step
to $\Phi$ and $\Phi^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

Equality of total variable counts cannot be dropped for arbitrary $B$:
$R[X_1,X_2]=B[X_2]$ with $B=R[X_1]$. The general statements do not solve the
unrestricted cancellation problem for finitely generated algebras; the
strong-invariance terminology and history are older \cite{Hirano} \src{02}.

\begin{theorem}[Automorphisms of polynomial and torus rings \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:aut}
For a root-covered $R$, every automorphism of $R[\mathbf T]$ or $R[\mathbf U^{\pm1}]$
preserves $R$; for polynomial extensions, restriction to $R$ is split surjective onto
$\Aut R$, with kernel the $R$-algebra automorphisms. Every automorphism of $R[T]$ is $T\mapsto uT+b$ with $u\in R^\times$,
$b\in R$, over a unique $\sigma\in\Aut R$; every automorphism of
$R[\mathbf U^{\pm1}]$ is $U_i\mapsto u_i\prod_jU_j^{m_{ji}}$ over $\sigma$, with
$u_i\in R^\times$ and $(m_{ji})\in\GL_q(\Z)$. For $\Oz$, $u\in\{\pm1\}$; for $\Og$,
$u\in\{\pm1,\pm i\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{opa:par:thm:laurent} to an automorphism and its inverse; coefficientwise
extension is the section. In one variable, $\deg g\deg f=1$; units give the torus
formula via \cref{opa:par:lem:laurentunits}.
\end{proof}

The affine and torus formulas are standard once coefficients are preserved; the new
omnific conclusion is that \emph{every} ring automorphism preserves $\Oz$. No
classification of multivariable polynomial automorphisms over $R$ and no claim about
the Jacobian conjecture follows \src{02}.

\section{Locally nilpotent derivations, coactions and gradings}\label{opa:par:sec:lnd}

A derivation $D$ of a domain is \emph{locally nilpotent} if every element is killed by
some iterate, and $\ML(A)=\bigcap_{D\in\LND(A)}\ker D$. Derivations are ring
derivations, hence $\Z$-linear; $A$ need not contain $\Q$.

\begin{lemma}[\src{02}]\label{opa:par:lem:lnddegree}
For a locally nilpotent $D$ of a characteristic-zero domain,
$\deg_D(a)=\max\{j:D^ja\ne0\}$ satisfies $\deg_D(ab)=\deg_Da+\deg_Db$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$D^{r+s}(ab)=\binom{r+s}rD^ra\,D^sb\ne0$, and higher iterates vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stable locally nilpotent rigidity \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:ML}
Let $R$ be a characteristic-zero root-covered domain. Every locally nilpotent
derivation of any domain containing $R$ kills $R$, and
$\ML(R[T_1,\dots,T_n])=R$ for every finite $n$. In particular
$\ML(\Oz[\mathbf T])=\Oz$, $\ML(\Og[\mathbf T])=\Og$, and $\Oz$ has no nonzero locally
nilpotent derivation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\deg_D(z)=m\deg_D(z_m)$ for all $m$ forces $\deg_Dz=0$, hence $\deg_Da=0$. The partial
derivatives $\partial/\partial T_j$ are locally nilpotent with common kernel $R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No coactions or gradings \src{02}]\label{opa:par:cor:coaction}
If $R$ is root-covered, a homomorphism $\psi:R\to R[s_1,\dots,s_r]$ with identity
specialization at $\mathbf s=0$, or $\theta:R\to R[u_1^{\pm1},\dots,u_r^{\pm1}]$ with
identity specialization at $\mathbf u=1$, is the constant inclusion; so there are no
nontrivial additive-group or split-torus coactions of finite rank, and every
$\Z^r$-grading of $R$ is concentrated in degree zero.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Identity specialization makes the map injective; apply
\cref{opa:par:thm:poly,opa:par:thm:laurent}. A grading gives
$\sum x_{\mathbf m}\mapsto\sum x_{\mathbf m}\mathbf u^{\mathbf m}$.
\end{proof}

A Hahn expansion $\sum a_gX^g$ is not a grading: it has infinitely many terms, and the
no-grading result concerns finite homogeneous decompositions. \Cref{opa:par:thm:ML}
does not say that $R[\mathbf T]$ has no locally nilpotent derivation, and it is not a
solution of a general stable-rigidity conjecture \cite{CD} \src{02}.

\section{Euler derivations and formal flows}\label{opa:par:sec:formal}

The parameter $s$ in this section is a new formal indeterminate, not a surreal and not
a Hahn monomial.

\begin{proposition}[Support-preserving Euler derivations \src{02}]\label{opa:par:prop:Euler}
For additive $\chi:\Gamma\to(k,+)$, $D_\chi(\sum a_gX^g)=\sum\chi(g)a_gX^g$ is a
$k$-linear derivation of $k((X^\Gamma))$ that preserves $\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)$, kills
$\oo$ and maps $\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)$ into $\Pi_k(\Gamma)$; and
$D_\chi D_\psi=D_\psi D_\chi$ for additive $\chi,\psi$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The coefficient of $X^h$ in $xy$ is a finite sum of $a_gb_{h-g}$, and
$\chi(h)=\chi(g)+\chi(h-g)$ splits it; $\chi(0)=0$.
\end{proof}

In the notation of \cref{opa:part:aut}, $D_\chi$ is the shift-zero derivation
$t^\gamma\mapsto\chi(-\gamma)t^\gamma$. Division by $n!$ is allowed on $\Pi_k(\Gamma)$, and
$D_\chi^n(x)\in\Pi_k(\Gamma)$ for $n\ge1$, so
\begin{equation}\label{opa:par:eq:formalexp}
 \EE_{\chi,s}(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{D_\chi^n(x)}{n!}s^n
\end{equation}
has coefficients in $\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)$; no division of an integer constant occurs.

\begin{theorem}[Compatible formal flows \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:formal}
$\EE_{\chi,s}$ is a unital ring homomorphism $\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)\to
\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)[[s]]$ with identity specialization, satisfying
$\EE_{\chi,s}\EE_{\chi,t}=\EE_{\chi,s+t}$; its coefficientwise extension fixing $s$ is
an automorphism of $\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)[[s]]$ with inverse from $-s$, and it induces
compatible automorphisms of $\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)[s]/(s^{N+1})$ for every $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$D_\chi^n(xy)/n!=\sum_rD_\chi^r(x)/r!\cdot D_\chi^{n-r}(y)/(n-r)!$, first in the Hahn
field; the coefficient of $s^rt^m$ in both sides of the flow law is
$D_\chi^{r+m}(x)/(r!m!)$; each coefficient uses finitely many inputs. Substituting $t=-s$
is valid since both parameters have positive order.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nonalgebraization at the first jet \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:nonalg}
If $\Gamma$ has no order unit and $\chi\ne0$, the reduction of $\EE_{\chi,s}$ modulo $s^2$
is nonconstant and is induced by no homomorphism
$\psi:\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)\to\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)[s]$ with
$\ev_{s=0}\psi=\id$, and $D_\chi$ is not locally nilpotent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Some $g>0$ has $\chi(g)\ne0$; then $D_\chi^n(X^g)=\chi(g)^nX^g\ne0$. By
\cref{opa:par:thm:scale} the ring is root-covered, and by \cref{opa:par:cor:coaction}
every such $\psi$ is constant.
\end{proof}

Formal automorphisms exist to \emph{all} orders; the obstruction appears only when a
single polynomial family must realize even the first-order part. A formal power
series ring is not of finite type, and $R[s]/(s^2)$ is not a domain, so there is no
conflict with \cref{opa:par:thm:finitetype} \src{02}. The flows involve no analytic
convergence, no fine-topology limit and no identification of $s$ with a surreal
infinitesimal, and they are not an exponentiation of a derivation \emph{inside} the
Hahn field \src{02}.

\subsection{The monomial orbit field}

Let $R=\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)$ be set sized, $F=\Frac R$ (which contains $k$:
$a=aX^g/X^g$) and $W=\chi(\Gamma)$, a $\Q$-subspace of $k$. In $F((s))$ put
\begin{equation}\label{opa:par:eq:orbit}
 L_\chi=F\Bigl(\frac{\EE_{\chi,s}(X^g)}{X^g}:g>0\Bigr)=F(e^{ws}:w\in W),
 \qquad e^{ws}=\sum_nw^ns^n/n!.
\end{equation}
It uses only transformed monomials; it is not claimed to contain the image of every
Hahn series \src{02}.

\begin{lemma}[Formal exponential independence \src{02}]\label{opa:par:lem:expindep}
For distinct $\lambda_j\in F$ the $e^{\lambda_js}$ are $F$-linearly independent; for
$\Q$-independent $w_1,\dots,w_r$ the $e^{w_js}$ are algebraically independent over $F$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Comparing coefficients of $s^n$, $n<m$, gives a Vandermonde system. A polynomial
relation is a linear relation among $e^{(\alpha\cdot\mathbf w)s}$ with distinct exponents.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Monomial orbit field \src{02}]\label{opa:par:thm:orbitfield}
$\trdeg_FL_\chi=\dim_\Q W$. If $\chi\ne0$, $L_\chi$ lies in no finitely generated
extension of $F$ inside $F((s))$, so $\EE_{\chi,s}:R\to F[[s]]$ factors through no
finite-type $F$-algebra with an $F$-algebra second map.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A $\Q$-basis $(w_j)$ of $W$ gives algebraically independent $e^{w_js}$, and each $e^{ws}$
has a power equal to a monomial in finitely many of them, so $L_\chi$ is algebraic over
$F(e^{w_js})$. For $0\ne w\in W$, $e^{ws}$ is transcendental and has roots $e^{(w/n)s}$ in
$L_\chi$, so \cref{opa:par:lem:rootsfield} excludes a finitely generated overfield, even
when $\dim_\Q W=1$. A factorization would put the image of $R$ in a finite-type domain
$B\subseteq F[[s]]$ with $\Frac B\supseteq L_\chi$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An omnific family of commuting directions}

\begin{theorem}[Commuting omnific derivations \src{02}; after \lbl{osq:prop:classder}]\label{opa:par:thm:Db}
For $b\in\No$, $D_b(\sum a_g\omega^g)=\sum q_b(g)a_g\omega^g$ is an $\R$-linear derivation
of $\No$ and, with complex coefficients, a $\C$-linear derivation of $\SC$ commuting
with conjugation; it preserves $\Oz$ and $\Og$. The $D_b$ commute, and every finite
family with distinct indices is linearly independent over $\Oz$, respectively $\Og$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\Cref{opa:par:prop:Euler} with $\chi=q_b$. For $m_c=\omega^{\omega^c}\in\Oz$,
$D_b(m_c)=m_c$ if $b=c$ and $0$ otherwise; applying $\sum_ja_jD_{b_j}=0$ to $m_{b_\ell}$
gives $a_\ell m_{b_\ell}=0$ in a domain.
\end{proof}

These derivations, with the test elements $m_c$, are the quotient report's
class-valued derivations \lbl{osq:prop:classder}
(\fn{docs/surreal/set-sized-quotients-of-omnific-integers}), which combine that report's
sources 06, 08 and 10 and state independence over $\R$; the same evaluation gives
independence over the omnific ring, as 02 states, and 02 adds their commutation and
flows. In the $t$-convention of \cref{opa:part:aut}, $D_b(t^\gamma)=-q_b(\gamma)t^\gamma$.

\begin{corollary}[Multi-parameter flows \src{02}]\label{opa:par:cor:multiflow}
For distinct $b_1,\dots,b_r$ and $R=\Oz$ or $\Og$, the map
\[
 x\longmapsto\sum_{\alpha\in\N^r}\frac{D_{b_1}^{\alpha_1}\cdots D_{b_r}^{\alpha_r}(x)}
 {\alpha_1!\cdots\alpha_r!}\,s_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots s_r^{\alpha_r}
 \quad\text{into }R[[s_1,\dots,s_r]]
\]
obeys the additive formal group law; its coordinate directions are independent, and no nonzero
coordinate direction is induced by a polynomial family on $R$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Compose one-parameter flows, which commute; independence is \cref{opa:par:thm:Db}, and
the last claim is \cref{opa:par:thm:nonalg} in one parameter.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[\src{02}]\label{opa:par:ex:flow}
For $b=0$, $q_0(g)$ is the real constant coefficient of the exponent $g$:
$D_0(\omega^r)=r\omega^r$ for real $r$, $D_0(\omega^\omega)=0$, and
$\EE_{0,s}(\omega)=\omega+\omega s+\frac\omega2s^2+\frac\omega6s^3+\cdots$, every coefficient in
$\Oz$ although $\frac12,\frac16\notin\Oz$; ``$\omega e^s$'' abbreviates this series and is
not the surreal exponential. Also
$D_0\bigl(\omega^\omega/(1+\omega)\bigr)=-\omega^{\omega+1}/(1+\omega)^2$, again omnific, supported at
$\omega-n$ for positive ordinary $n$ (checked coefficientwise to 29 terms by the
placement dossier).
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[A continuum of independent orbit elements \src{02}]\label{opa:par:cor:continuum}
For $R=\Oz$, $F=\No$ (or $R=\Og$, $F=\SC$), the monomial orbit field $F(e^{rs}:r\in\R)$
of $D_0$ has transcendence degree $2^{\aleph_0}$ over $F$, in the sense of a set-sized
transcendence basis with finite algebraic relations.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$q_0(r)=r$, and $e^{rs}=\EE_{0,s}(\omega^r)/\omega^r$ for $r>0$. A $\Q$-basis of $\R$, made
positive, has $2^{\aleph_0}$ elements; \cref{opa:par:lem:expindep} holds over the class
field $F$ since each relation uses finitely many coefficients.
\end{proof}

Only a lower bound is asserted for the field generated by the formal images of all
Hahn series \src{02}.

\begin{remark}[Actual automorphisms remain abundant \src{02}]\label{opa:par:rem:actual}
For additive $\chi:\No\to\R$ and ordinary $t\in\R$,
$\tau_{\chi,t}(\sum a_g\omega^g)=\sum e^{t\chi(g)}a_g\omega^g$ is a support-preserving
automorphism of $\No$ and $\SC$ preserving $\Oz$ and $\Og$, with
$\tau_{\chi,t}\tau_{\chi,u}=\tau_{\chi,t+u}$ and positive multipliers. These character
twists are established \cite{KKS} and not claimed new (\cref{opa:rem:twists}); they
prevent reading the rigidity theorems as $\Aut(\Oz)=1$. What fails is realization of a
family by a homomorphism into a polynomial or finite-type parameter algebra: with
$\chi=q_0$ the twists send $\omega^r$ to $e^{tr}\omega^r$ for all real $r$ at once, which no
single polynomial parameter algebra can carry.
\end{remark}

\section{Set-sized workspaces and a fraction-field warning}\label{opa:par:sec:workspaces}

Let $\Gamma=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}\Q e_j$, ordered by the sign of the coefficient at the
largest index, so $e_{j+1}>ne_j$ for all $n$: $\Gamma$ is divisible with no order unit.
For $(\oo,k)=(\Z,\R)$ or $(\Z[i],\C)$, $R=\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)$ is a root-covered set
(\cref{opa:par:thm:scale}), so all the results of this part apply to it in ordinary set
theory. The order embedding $e_j\mapsto\omega^j$ of exponent groups induces
$X^{e_j}\mapsto\omega^{\omega^j}$ and embeds $R$ in $\Oz$, respectively $\Og$; the coordinate
projections give commuting derivations already here, and replacing $\Q e_j$ by $\R e_j$
gives a set-sized realization of \cref{opa:par:cor:continuum} \src{02}.

Let $\Gamma_{\le j}=\bigoplus_{\ell\le j}\Q e_\ell$ (convex) and
$K_{\mathrm{bd}}=\bigcup_jk((X^{\Gamma_{\le j}}))$.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-scale fraction field \src{02}; after \lbl{bst:prop:localdensity}]\label{opa:par:prop:fracworkspace}
For this $R$, $\Frac R=K_{\mathrm{bd}}\subsetneq k((X^\Gamma))$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An element of $R$ with positive leading exponent in $\Gamma_{\le j}$ has all its exponents
in $\Gamma_{\le j}$ by convexity, so quotients lie in $K_{\mathrm{bd}}$. Conversely
$y\in k((X^{\Gamma_{\le j}}))$ gives $X^{e_{j+1}}y\in R$. Finally $\sum_{j\ge0}X^{-e_j}$ is a Hahn
series in no $k((X^{\Gamma_{\le j}}))$.
\end{proof}

This is the transcendence report's localization proposition
\lbl{bst:prop:localdensity} together with its field criterion (condition (2) of
\lbl{bst:thm:fieldcriterion}) for this group. For the full class ring,
$\Frac\Oz=\No$ and $\Frac\Og=\SC$ (\lbl{odg:thm:fractions}); that fact does not justify
replacing $K_{\mathrm{bd}}$ by the full Hahn field, since a fixed set-sized group may lack
an upper bound for a set of its scales even without an order unit \src{02}.

\section{Relation to Part I and the questions of this part}\label{opa:par:sec:relation}

The two parts are complementary. \Cref{opa:part:aut} concerns derivations with
\emph{positive} shifts and their exponentials inside the Hahn field; this part concerns
shift-zero (support-preserving) derivations and needs an external formal parameter.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item 02's question on formal directions below is partly answered by
\cref{opa:part:aut}: the positive-shift derivations preserving $\Oz$ are classified
exactly, by $c_\delta(C_\delta)=0$ (\cref{opa:thm:derivation}) and, on common positive
shifts, by $a_\delta(C_\delta)=0$ (\cref{opa:thm:derivcriterion}). Those with a common
well-ordered set of shifts integrate \emph{inside} $\No$ to automorphisms of $\Oz$
(\cref{opa:thm:lie}), so they are integrable by an honest automorphism rather than only
by a formal flow; for an arbitrary contracting strong one this holds granted
\cref{opa:fact:import} for $\Gamma=\No$ (\cref{opa:rem:import}). The shift-zero derivations, the Hasse--Schmidt extensions of other
derivations of $\Oz$, and derivations with no common well-ordered shift set are not
covered.
\item There is no conflict with \cref{opa:par:thm:ML}: the shift derivations
$D_{\delta,\theta}$ restrict to derivations of $\Oz$ but are not locally nilpotent, since
$D_{\delta,\theta}^n(t^\gamma)=\theta(\gamma)^nt^{\gamma+n\delta}\ne0$ when $\theta(\gamma)\ne0$; and the
flows $\Phi_s$ are not polynomial in $s$, since $\Phi_s(t^{-\eta})$ involves every power
of $s$.
\item The character twists of \cref{opa:par:rem:actual} are the factor $M_{\chi,\id}$ of
\cref{opa:thm:factorization}.
\end{itemize}
\Cref{opa:par:thm:aut} concerns $\Aut(\Oz[T])$, not $\Aut(\Oz)$, and does not bear on
\lbl{osq:q:invisible}. The rational self-maps of $\Oz$ studied in the quotient report
are maps of the ring given by rational functions, not ring automorphisms of $\Oz[T]$; the
objects differ. Derivations into set-sized modules vanish (\lbl{osq:thm:derivations}),
which does not conflict with \cref{opa:par:thm:Db}, whose values lie in the class module
$\Pi$.

\begin{question}[Beyond the scale criterion \src{02}]\label{opa:par:q:orderunit}
For a nonzero divisible $\Gamma$ \emph{with} an order unit, which rings
$\mathcal R_\oo(k,\Gamma)$ remain strongly invariant or stably rigid?
\end{question}
\Cref{opa:par:thm:scale} shows that their elements need not divide monomials; it does not
show that cancellation or locally nilpotent rigidity fails.

\begin{question}[Formal directions \src{02}; re-scoped]\label{opa:par:q:directions}
Which derivations of $\Oz$ preserve its purely infinite ideal, and which admit an
iterative integral Hasse--Schmidt extension $\Oz\to\Oz[[s]]$? How much larger is this
class than the Euler family of \cref{opa:par:thm:Db}?
\end{question}
\emph{Status.} Partly answered for positive shifts by \cref{opa:part:aut}, as recorded
above; open otherwise. The factorial argument of \cref{opa:par:thm:formal} is a
sufficient condition for the displayed family and is not asserted to classify all
integrable derivations or all formal automorphisms tangent to the identity.

\begin{question}[Reduced parameter algebras \src{02}]\label{opa:par:q:reduced}
How far does coefficient rigidity extend to reduced parameter rings with several
irreducible components when a prescribed specialization stays injective on the source?
\end{question}
The present proofs apply to domain quotients on which the map stays injective; a
component can kill root-cover elements, and the dual-number example shows that
nilpotent parameter rings need a different statement.

\section{Non-claims, by source}\label{opa:app:nonclaims}

No source claims to solve a named conjecture, and none claims Lean verification, peer
review or historical priority; the finite suites check identities only. The report
keeps every limitation stated by each source.

\paragraph{Source 09.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item The classification is only within strong, coefficient-fixing,
leading-term-preserving automorphisms; it does not assert that an abstract
automorphism preserving $\Ro$ is strong, and nonstrong pair automorphisms are not
classified (the definability recovery applies to them; the logarithmic classification
does not).
\item Coefficient functionals come from an existing strong derivation; arbitrary
functions satisfying the vanishing condition are not asserted to assemble into an
operator.
\item The shears are plain-field automorphisms and are not claimed to preserve the
Gonshor exponential.
\item No set or NBG class of class-function graphs is formed; actions are encoded by
single class relations; supports are sets.
\item The exponential--logarithm correspondence and the factorization pattern are
imported, not reproved or claimed; the explicit many-shift constructions avoid the
class version of the import.
\item No new factorization theorem for omnific integers is claimed; \cite{LM} addresses a
different problem.
\item $\Oz$ is not identified with a model of full ordinary arithmetic.
\item Truncation at a named monomial is not a uniform definable choice of boundary.
\item Nondefinability is relative to the field-and-integer-part language; Conway normal
forms remain set-theoretically defined.
\item Hahn-sum formulas are not claims of convergence in the fine topology.
\item Modulus equivariance is not a claim that moduli are fixed.
\item The repository comparison was bounded and targeted; search coverage is not a
proof of novelty.
\item The exact-arithmetic program checks finite identities only; it does not decide
well-ordering, prove the correspondence, verify class-theoretic statements or certify
novelty; the formalization order is a proposal, not implemented modules.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Source 04.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item It does not classify abstract (nonstrong) automorphisms of $\Oz$; the fraction-field
extension of an abstract automorphism is not known to be strong.
\item It imposes no exponential, omega-map, birthday or simplicity structure, and does not
revisit the collection's exponential-rigidity question as if unanswered.
\item The criterion classifies existing maps; it is not a construction for arbitrary
logarithmic characters, and the convex condition alone is not asserted to solve global
admissibility.
\item The relative fixed field is computed for the elementary subgroup only; the full
stabilizer only for monomial parameters; the Hahn hull is not asserted to be definable
closure.
\item Class maps are treated individually; no class of all automorphisms is formed.
\item Nothing is claimed after naming a proper class of parameters.
\item Bare nondefinability of coefficient-one monomials already follows from character
twists; the nondefinability statement alone is not advertised as a new mechanism.
\item $\Frac\Oz=\No$ is a full-class statement; a set-sized Hahn field need not be the
fraction field of its integer-part ring.
\item Its intrinsic coefficient invariance was not claimed to be first order (now
superseded by the Diophantine report).
\item Real and imaginary shear times preserve different structures; its Gaussian result is
not the first surcomplex automorphism moving the real axis.
\item No generation or density theorem; fine-topology convergence is not strong summation.
\item The fixed-shift exponential, the general monomial/kernel decomposition and the
exponential--derivation correspondence are prior work.
\item Its finite checks are not proofs and do not implement arbitrary normal forms; no Lean
file, no repository checkout or build; historical priority unverified.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Source 08.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item The threshold covers the elementary family only; rank-one rescalings and characters
preserve the integer part; not every leading-term-preserving automorphism is claimed to be
the exponential of one fixed-shift derivation.
\item Finite-dimensional phase images are a sufficient condition only.
\item No first-order Gaussian reconstruction was claimed (one now exists,
\cref{opa:sec:remember}).
\item The automorphisms are not asserted to commute with the surreal exponential, the
Berarducci--Mantova derivation or the omega-map; the wreath product does not contradict
rigidity for those structures.
\item Parameter avoidance fails for set-sized fields with the whole field named; a
universe-relative collection is not identified with the proper class.
\item No classification of the full stabilizer, and no solution of additional-structure
problems.
\item The reconstruction is not a homomorphism $\Oz\to\R$.
\item The nondefinability is not a statement about adding an arbitrary class predicate, nor
about automorphisms required to commute with the omega-map.
\item Strong summation is not topological convergence.
\item Pell rigidity, the existence of nontrivial Hahn automorphisms, the
derivation--exponential construction and the monomial-character viewpoint are not claimed
new; some elementary lemmas may be known; priority is provisional.
\item Its questions are not named historical problems.
\item No Lean; not refereed; the checks are regression tests, not proofs of well-ordering,
parameter avoidance or definability.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Source 02.}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item Root-covered is sufficient, not claimed necessary; failure of the monomial criterion
is not used to claim failure of cancellation or rigidity.
\item The finite-type theorems are theorems about sets; no $\operatorname{Spec}(\Oz)$ and no
class compactness; ``$\Aut(\Oz)$'' and $\ML$ of class rings are shorthand; the finite-type
theorem is not a statement that a class cannot inject into a set.
\item It does not classify $\Aut(\Oz)$, all derivations, or multivariable polynomial
automorphisms, and makes no Jacobian-conjecture claim.
\item Not every homomorphism into every target is constant: injectivity and domain targets
are required; no noninjective analogue is given.
\item It does not claim that $R[\mathbf T]$ has no locally nilpotent derivation; a Hahn
expansion is not a grading.
\item The orbit-field formula concerns monomials; only a lower bound holds for the full
formal image.
\item Formal flows involve no analytic convergence and no fine-topology limits, $s$ is not a
surreal, and they are not exponentiation inside a Hahn field.
\item The cancellation theorem does not solve the unrestricted cancellation problem;
$\ML(R[\mathbf T])=R$ is not a solution of a general stable-rigidity conjecture.
\item Not new: character twists \cite{KKS}, strong-invariance terminology \cite{Hirano}, the
Makar--Limanov framework \cite{CD}, Euler derivations, Vandermonde arguments, and the
example of \cite{LM}.
\item No named factorization conjecture is claimed solved; the literature search is not a
priority certificate.
\item The questions are not presented as published open problems; no Lean, no peer review;
the checks are finite identities.
\end{enumerate}

Counts: 13 non-claims from 09, 13 from 04, 12 from 08, 11 from 02.
