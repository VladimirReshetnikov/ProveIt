# Proof and scope audit

This records a self-audit of the written argument, not an independent referee
report or a proof-assistant certificate.

## Imported foundations

1. Conway/Gonshor normal forms: set-supported Hahn arithmetic, the leading-term
   order, and realization of arbitrary permitted normal forms.
2. The surreal cut property for set-sized left and right sets.
3. Real closedness of the surreal field, used for the periodic quadratic root.
4. Existence of the ordinary real continued fraction for an infinite positive
   integer sequence.

No advanced, unrefereed result from the Surreal repository is required. The
omnific floor formula, finite cylinder identities, degree formulas, and
support-projection argument are supplied in the article.

## Dependency chain and critical checks

### 1. Floor, continuants, and cylinders

The integer-constant/negative-infinitesimal boundary is corrected in the floor
formula. A positive nonunit omnific digit is at least 2. The first digit is
allowed to be negative or zero. It contributes to the digit field but not to
the denominator degree. Every continued-fraction index is an ordinary finite
natural number.

The determinant identity makes the finite prefix map injective. Both cylinder
endpoints are included in the analysis, and the cylinders themselves are
open. Set-sized lower and upper endpoint sets give a surreal realization.

### 2. The exact fiber

The two-step buffer treats separately the delicate case in which the next
digit is 1. The bound is stronger than merely knowing the cylinder width.
The necessity direction combines all later cylinder widths with Fibonacci
lower bounds on the continuant denominators; one cylinder alone does not
imply a strict valuation inequality. The sufficiency direction uses the
buffer from both endpoints. This yields a translate of an ideal of the
finite valuation ring, not an ideal of the full field.

### 3. Full Hahn realization

If denominator degrees eventually stop growing, every later digit is an
ordinary integer and a real tail supplies the realization. Otherwise a
full-surreal realization is projected onto the exponent group. The projection
is an additive subseries operation and is explicitly not treated as a ring
homomorphism. The omitted support is beyond every cylinder precision, so the
fiber theorem certifies the projected point. This proof requires a full Hahn
field; it does not prove existence in arbitrary smaller fields.

The trivial value group is handled separately. In a nontrivial ordered group,
cofinality may be made strict by first adding a positive element.

### 4. Primality and algebraic rigidity

The complement of the error-value upper cut is additively closed exactly when
every cumulative degree is eventually doubled. If that fails, the displayed
monomial has nonzero square-zero image in a set-sized quotient. Quotient
language does not create an object whose elements are proper classes.

For a nonzero algebraic error, a polynomial relation has at least two terms
of minimal valuation. Their coefficient ratio is already an invisible base-
field element. This proves algebraic visibility without an unproved valuation-
extension theorem.

Integer polynomials in omnific digits remain omnific; cancellation cannot
increase the greatest exponent. The first digit must be bounded separately
because it does not occur in the denominator-degree sum. Failure of either
condition produces an explicit invisible element of the digit field.

A nonperiodic code is not asserted to have any algebraic realization. The
result is empty/singleton/countably infinite, with conditional existence
stated explicitly.

### 5. Periodic codes

The period matrix has positive off-diagonal entries and a quadratic with
exactly one positive root, which is greater than 1. This provides genuinely
repeating complete quotients. Periodic *digits* alone do not force equality
of complete quotients. The algebraic-fiber classification is relative to the
field generated by the digits, not to the whole ambient surreal field.

The maximum prefix scale and the period degree are distinct quantities. A
large prefix can permit infinitely many algebraic points sharing an ordinary
periodic tail. Pure periodicity supplies the opposite, unique-algebraic case.
The order-unit threshold for periodic uniqueness is stronger than countable
cofinality for general uniqueness.

### 6. Algebraic independence and surcomplex scope

All coefficient supports are first collected into one set-sized rational
span. New perturbation exponents are rationally independent modulo that span.
Distinct polynomial monomials therefore occupy disjoint support cosets and
cannot cancel. Translation by a fixed representative takes place over a Hahn
coefficient field that already contains that representative.

The construction gives each prescribed set-sized independent family; it does
not posit a proper-class transcendence basis. Surcomplex results use real and
imaginary coordinate codes. Unequal precision ideals are not invariant under
multiplication by i. Equal ideals define a possibly nonprincipal intersection
of valuation balls, not necessarily one ball with a realized radius.

## Finite testing

`verification.json` records 81,256 exact assertions with fixed seed 20260923.
They check finite rational, finite generalized-polynomial, and symbolic
identities. Randomized case generation is deterministic at the recorded seed.

These tests do not verify arbitrary Hahn supports, the complete written proof,
proper-class claims, independence from every polynomial relation, or novelty.
No Lean build was run, and no Lean source is presented as checked.

## Claims deliberately not made

- That basic surreal continued-fraction nonuniqueness is new.
- That every eventually periodic itinerary consists solely of quadratic points.
- That every quadratic surreal has an eventually periodic omnific itinerary.
- That a simplest separator automatically equals the period-fixed root.
- That unrestricted surreal fibers can be unique.
- That countable cofinality implies an order unit.
- That a support projection is multiplicative.
- That finite computation proves class-sized or arbitrary-support theorems.
- That the proposed new classifications have independently certified priority.
