# Exact Precision and Algebraic Rigidity of Omnific Continued Fractions

**AI-assisted research draft · 23 September 2026**

A standalone 22-page article on ordinary omega-indexed continued fractions
with Conway omnific integer digits, their exact surreal fibers, full Hahn-field
realizations, and algebraicity over the field generated by the digits.

## Main results

For a digit sequence a, write d_n = deg(q_n), the greatest growth exponent in
the nth continuant denominator. Let v be minus the greatest growth exponent.

1. **Exact fiber, Theorem 4.4.** Every realization x has precisely the fiber
   x + I_a, where I_a consists of zero and all errors e with v(e) > 2d_n for
   every ordinary natural n. A two-step endpoint buffer is proved explicitly.
2. **Full Hahn realization, Theorem 5.1.** Every admissible code in a full
   real-coefficient Hahn field K_G is realized in K_G. Its realization is
   unique exactly when the denominator degrees are cofinal in G.
3. **Algebraic trichotomy, Theorem 7.3.** Over the countable digit field, a
   nonempty algebraic fiber is a singleton exactly when the cumulative degrees
   absorb their own doubles and eventually see the degree of the first digit.
   Otherwise the algebraic fiber is countably infinite. The theorem does not
   assume algebraic existence for an arbitrary nonperiodic code.
4. **Periodic classification, Theorems 8.2 and 8.4.** Eventually periodic codes
   always have a distinguished algebraic realization of degree at most two.
   The article gives an exact one-versus-countably-many criterion. Uniqueness
   in a full Hahn field detects an order unit, a stronger requirement than
   the countable-cofinality threshold for arbitrary codes.
5. **Hidden independence, Theorem 10.1.** Every full surreal fiber contains
   algebraically independent families of every set cardinality over every
   prescribed set-sized coefficient field. Section 11 proves a coordinatewise
   surcomplex version and an exact fractional-linear precision-transport law.

The article also identifies when the precision ideal is prime and gives
explicit nonzero nilpotent error classes when it is not.

## Status and historical attribution

This is an unrefereed research draft. Basic nonuniqueness of surreal continued
fractions and the large associated classes are **not new**: Yves Roggeman's
1985 paper is credited and discussed. The proposed precision, Hahn-realization,
and algebraic-fiber classifications have written proofs, but their novelty
and priority have not been independently certified. No certified solution to
a named longstanding open problem is claimed.

## Reproduce the finite checks

Use Python 3.10 or later and SymPy. The recorded run used Python 3.13.5 and
SymPy 1.14.0.

```sh
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python code/verify.py --output audits/verification.json
```

The fixed-seed run passes **81,256 exact finite assertions**. These check
rational continuants and cylinders, the two-step buffer, generalized-polynomial
degree laws, symbolic identities, and a Catalan Laurent expansion. They do
not constitute a surreal-number implementation, a formal proof, or a test of
all infinite supports. See the JSON output for the full scope.

## Build the PDF

A reasonably complete TeX Live installation with `latexmk` and pdfLaTeX is
required. The source uses standard packages including `newtx`, `tcolorbox`,
`microtype`, `hyperref`, and AMS packages. No external images, bibliography
processor, or network access are needed.

```sh
latexmk -pdf -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error article.tex
```

Alternatively, use `make pdf`, `make check`, or `make all`. `make clean`
removes LaTeX auxiliary files while retaining the PDF and verification output.

## Scope restrictions

All continued-fraction indices are ordinary finite natural numbers. There is
no unstated limit-stage algorithm. Algebraicity and uniqueness always name
their coefficient and ambient fields. Full Hahn fields are not interchangeable
with arbitrary proper subfields. The surcomplex construction is coordinatewise,
not a nearest-Gaussian-integer algorithm. Quotient rings are interpreted in
set-sized Hahn workspaces; no class of proper-class cosets is assumed.

No Lean certification, independent referee review, or proof of a general
Lagrange converse is included.
