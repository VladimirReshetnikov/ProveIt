# Proof audit

This records the manuscript's own proof review. It is not an independent referee
report or a formal verification certificate.

## 1. Main dependency chain

Theorems A and C depend on the following explicit chain:

1. Surreal normal-form existence and Hahn operations (standard background).
2. Small positive lower bounds for set families of positive surreals, or the
   regular-cardinal leading-index bound in the explicit exponent group.
3. Strong summability and positivity of the small-difference division formula.
4. The small-ring-image obstruction for the nonunital purely infinite ring.
5. Root-kernel reconstruction using all n(n-1) root maps and a third index.
6. The split-retraction normal-closure identity for the elementary kernel.
7. Restriction to each conjugate of the purely infinite root-generated subgroup.
8. Euclidean reduction over Z or Z[i] to identify the constant elementary group.

The ring obstruction in steps 2–4 is credited to the existing repository work
and reproved in the article. No claim is made to have invented recovery of ring
structure from root commutators in general.

## 2. Division and support checks

For p with positive reverse well-ordered support S, choose delta > 0 with
m*delta < s for all positive integers m and s in S. For eta < epsilon < delta,
put Delta = epsilon-eta. The quotient terms have exponents

    s - epsilon - r*Delta,  r >= 0.

Positivity follows from epsilon+r*Delta < (r+1)*delta < s.

For reverse well ordering, any increasing sequence of quotient exponents has
an infinite subsequence along which the natural-number indices r do not
decrease. The corresponding s then increase, contradicting the support condition.
For local finiteness at an exponent u, infinitely many indices r would give
an increasing sequence s = u+epsilon+r*Delta in S. Both requirements of
strong summability are proved; positivity alone would not be enough.

The geometric product is checked coefficientwise. No convergence in the full
surreal fine topology is invoked.

## 3. Root-kernel reconstruction checks

The common kernel J of the finitely many root maps is additive. For a in J,
b in I, and i != j, choose k distinct from both. The commutator relation gives
that ab is killed by the (i,j) root map. Doing this for every pair proves
J is an ideal.

The tuple image T(I) lies in the actual set H^(n(n-1)). Equality of tuples is
equivalent to difference in J. Ring multiplication is transported from the
parameters, after proving the ideal condition. It is NOT multiplication in
the target group H. This avoids the otherwise invalid inference that the
finite product group already has a ring structure.

If I is a class, no set of class-sized cosets is postulated. The tuple image
itself supplies the small carrier. The group need not be represented linearly
and the homomorphism is not assumed continuous or induced by a ring map.

## 4. Internal kernel versus ambient maps

Let U be generated by the purely infinite root elements, and N its normal
closure in the elementary group. A map N -> H is killed on EACH conjugate
of U, by composing its restriction with conjugation U -> gUg^{-1}.
Those conjugates generate N. This proves internal invisibility; it does not
assume the map extends to the ambient elementary group.

The equality of N with the constant-term kernel uses splitting B = D + I
and finite-word replacement e_ij(d+p) = e_ij(d)e_ij(p). The manuscript never
asserts that U itself is normal or that U=N.

## 5. Structural properties checked separately

- Perfectness: every purely infinite parameter is a product of two such
  parameters. Each root is therefore a root commutator. The normal closure
  of a perfect subgroup is perfect.
- Torsion-freeness: a finite-order matrix is diagonalizable over the ambient
  field containing C, with ordinary roots of unity as eigenvalues. Its trace
  is an ordinary constant, hence equals its constant term n. All eigenvalues
  must be 1 by the ordinary unit-circle argument.
- Centerlessness: commuting with all nonzero root matrices forces a scalar
  matrix. Its scalar and inverse lie in A, so it is a unit. The leading-exponent
  argument proves A^x = D^x, and constant term then forces scalar 1.
- Non-simplicity: q^m*omega^(1/2) witnesses each strict inclusion for q=1+omega;
  omega^(1/2)/q has negative leading exponent. The series for
  omega^omega/(1+omega)^m has positive, reverse well-ordered support.
- Set-sized normal subgroups and conjugacy classes: their conjugation actions
  have set-sized automorphism or permutation targets; invisibility and the
  independently proved trivial center give the conclusions.

## 6. Set-sized fragments

Gamma_kappa is the direct sum of kappa copies of Q, ordered by the LEAST
nonzero coordinate. Regularity bounds the leading indices of fewer than kappa
positive exponents. A later basis vector is smaller than every finite fraction
of each such exponent. There remain kappa many later basis vectors for the
collision argument.

The geometric quotient has at most max(aleph_0, |support(p)|) terms. Since kappa
is uncountable, this remains below kappa. The support calculation is therefore
internal to the specified ring. Regularity and uncountability have different
roles and are not dropped.

At kappa=aleph_1 the real-coefficient countable-support ring and elementary
kernel have cardinality continuum. No claim of a sharper minimum detecting
cardinality is made without the additional hypothesis in the exact-threshold
transfer proposition.

A target group of size < kappa differs from an action on a set of size < kappa:
for infinite X, |Sym(X)|=2^|X|. The manuscript uses the latter cardinal for
actions and continuum for finite-dimensional complex linear targets.

## 7. Steinberg and profinite checks

The Steinberg theorem uses only the stated presentation, its split retraction,
and root-normal-generation. The kernel of constant term is NOT called K_2.
No centerlessness or torsion-freeness is transferred to this kernel.

The identification of finite quotients of E_n(Oz) with those of SL_n(Z) is
proved by factorization. The subsequent identification of the profinite
completion with SL_n(Zhat) imports the classical Bass–Milnor–Serre theorem.
No corresponding equality with SL_n(widehat{Z[i]}) is asserted for the
Gaussian case.

## 8. Counterexamples and exclusions

Finite-support evaluation, summing coefficients, sends e_12(omega) to
nonidentity e_12(1) over the ordinary coefficient field. Thus the infinite-tail
closure hypothesis cannot simply be removed.

A single root subgroup has nonzero coefficient-extraction maps to R. Thus
invisibility does not pass to arbitrary subgroups of the kernel.

Class-sized targets are excluded: the natural matrix inclusions are faithful.
For set-sized kernels, the identity map detects elements at a sufficiently
large target cardinal. The theorems do not concern all of SL_n(A) without an
additional generation or quotient argument, and do not settle rank two.

## 9. Computation

`verify.py` passed 2,111 deterministic checks using exact fractions and finite
Laurent polynomials. Integer constant coefficients and rational nonconstant
coefficients are used for the positive-support ring samples. Unit diagonal
identities additionally use rational units over a field. The complete counts
are in `verification_results.json`.

These computations test signs, indices, finite matrix multiplication, and
finite geometric remainders. They do not prove the universal theorems, verify
class theory, or replace the written infinite-support arguments.
