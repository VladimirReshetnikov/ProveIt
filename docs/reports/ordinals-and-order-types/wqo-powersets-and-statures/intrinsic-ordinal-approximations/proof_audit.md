# Proof-audit guide

This guide identifies the logical dependencies of the proposed proofs. It records an internal mathematical audit, not an independent referee report or a formal proof certificate. The detailed arguments are in `article.tex` / `article.pdf`.

## Definitions that must not be silently changed

1. Work in ZFC and quotient quasi-orders by mutual comparability before taking point ranks.
2. `P_f(A)` uses Hoare domination: each source element lies below some target element. It includes the empty set and is identified with the finitely generated downsets of `A`.
3. Ideals are nonempty, directed, and downward closed. `Idl(empty)` is empty. The full downset poset includes the empty downset.
4. An order embedding preserves and reflects order. It is stronger than the order-reflecting maps sometimes denoted by an arrow in the source literature. No Scott continuity is required of the witness embeddings.
5. Height is the supremum of point ranks plus one. Maximal order type is the rank of the empty root of the bad-sequence tree.
6. Multisets use injective occurrence-wise embedding, not the multiset termination order.
7. In `1+a`, addition is ordinary ordinal addition with the one on the left. Natural multiplication and natural addition are separate operations.

## Imported ingredients

- The de Jongh–Parikh characterization of maximal order type by a maximal linear extension.
- Its Cartesian-product theorem, `o(B x C) = o(B) natural_product o(C)`.
- Higman's lemma, used for standard wpo closure properties.
- The classical Cantor-normal-form calculation of finite Milner–Rado sums. The supremum characterization is stated explicitly.
- The source paper's Theorem 5.10, used only to identify the new intrinsic value with the original weakened maximal order type on elementary wqos.
- Hilton's topological pigeonhole formulas, used only for the final numerical comparison, not for proving the powerset-height formulas.

The finite ideal-decomposition theorem, ideal-completion identities, algebraic-dcpo representation, and bounded-construction ideal descriptions are proved in the article rather than treated as black boxes. They are not all claimed to be new.

## Dependency audit

### 1. Directed finite covers

A nonempty directed set covered by finitely many downsets is contained in one of them. Choose a point outside each proposed cover member and then a common upper bound. This lemma supplies uniformity; choosing a different matching for each tuple is not enough in the word and multiset arguments.

### 2. All downsets and ideal completion

For a wpo `B`, an infinite descending sequence of downsets produces a bad sequence by choosing a point lost at each step. Well-founded induction on the downset poset then decomposes every downset into finitely many ideals: a nondirected downset splits into two proper downsets using a pair without a common upper bound.

Union identifies `P_f(Idl(B))` with all downsets of `B`; order reflection uses the directed finite-cover lemma. Directed union identifies `Idl(P_f(B))` with that same poset. The empty downset corresponds to the nonempty ideal `{empty}` in the latter identity.

The bad-sequence residuals are cofinal among proper downsets. Hence the point rank of a downset `D` in the full downset poset is `o(D)`, and its total height is `o(B)+1`.

**Important:** `Idl(B)` need not be a wpo for an arbitrary wpo `B`. The proof only needs it to be one when it embeds in the target wpo. The full downset poset is always well-founded here, even when it has infinite antichains.

### 3. Lower bound and locality

A witness `Idl(B) -> A` induces an embedding of finitary Hoare powersets, giving `o(B)+1 <= h(P_f(A))`.

For exactness, local representations must be downward closed. The finitely generated downsets of such a representation form a downset in the target's finitely generated-downset poset, so point ranks agree. Finite-generator coverage then yields the whole height as a supremum of these exact local heights.

A finitely generated downset of `Idl(B)`, generated by ideals `I_1,...,I_m`, is `Idl(C)` for `C = union I_j`. The finite-cover lemma proves this identification. Thus the local condition can be tested on the finitely generated downsets themselves, and is inherited by all downsets.

### 4. Canonical cores

An ideal completion is algebraic, with principal ideals precisely its compact elements. Conversely an algebraic dcpo is the ideal completion of its compact-element subposet. Nonempty directed subsets are used in the dcpo convention.

For a finitely generated downset `D` of a locally ideal-representable `A`, its entire lower cone in `P_f(A)` is `P_f(D)`. The ideal identity therefore makes its rank exactly `o(K(D))`. This proves the pointwise formula, not merely an upper bound for the total height.

### 5. Closure under constructors

Products and disjoint unions commute with nonempty ideal completion. The finitary-powerset closure uses the two completion identities.

For bounded words, every ideal has a largest occurring length `k`. Its length-`k` part is a product ideal of `B^k`, cofinal in the whole ideal. Finite-cover uniformization proves the claimed order on the ideal descriptions.

For bounded multisets, lift the maximal-cardinality part through the finite permutation quotient `B^k -> M_k(B)`. Its inverse image is a finite union of product ideals. Each image is a directed downset; finite-cover uniformization selects one image. A second use of uniformization establishes a single injective matching for order comparisons.

The global word and multiset constructions are handled by choosing a finite length/cardinality bound for each finite family; they are not asserted to commute globally with ideal completion without that bound.

### 6. Ordinal products

`Idl(1+a)` has order type `a+1` under the nonempty-ideal convention. For finite `a` this uses `1+a=a+1`; for infinite `a` it uses `1+a=a`.

Each principal box in a product of ordinal chains is therefore an ideal completion of the product of the corresponding cores. The maximal-order-type product theorem gives its exact point rank. Every finitely generated downset lies below a principal box, so these ranks are cofinal for the height.

The zero-factor case is separated; an empty supremum would otherwise miss the empty finite set. Every added `+1` is retained at the correct rank/height stage.

For monomial factors, natural multiplication adds the leading exponents by natural sum. Unbounded finite coefficients give cofinality at the next exponent; the Milner–Rado supremum then yields the displayed closed formula. For uncountable exponents the proof ranges over all predecessor tuples, not just a countable sampled sequence.

### 7. Finite caps

For `A=lambda+F`, every finitely generated downset either stays in the chain prefix or contains that whole prefix and a nonempty downset of `F`. This gives total height `lambda+|F|`.

For an arbitrary ideal-embedding witness, the principal images in the prefix form a chain of type `beta <= lambda`; the remaining points form a finite cap `G`. Reflection forces `B=beta+G`. If `beta<lambda`, finite additions stay below the limit `lambda`.

If `beta=lambda`, that whole prefix is itself a nonprincipal ideal. Its image cannot be bounded in the target prefix, hence uses a point `x` of `F`. Every remaining principal image must lie strictly above `x`. This bounds `|G|+1` by the size of `x`'s upper cone. The matching lower bound uses `B=lambda+F_{>x}`.

This proof is why replacing omega by a long finite chain cannot validate the counterexample: the required nonprincipal ideal disappears entirely.

## Boundaries that remain open in this note

- The full source question about all weakened invariants and arbitrary wqos is not settled.
- Agreement of the new value with the original weakened maximal order type does not identify the two approximation relations as relations.
- Local ideal representability is sufficient for exactness; its necessity for equality in general is not asserted.
- No general compositional ordinal evaluator is supplied for every expression in the enlarged constructor class.
- The two additional candidate invariants in Section 9 are not proved to equal the source's weakened height and width.
- The triple-counterexample in Section 9 does not involve the original maximal order type; adding it distinguishes the displayed pair.
- The topological-pigeonhole comparison is a numerical consequence of formulas, not a new proof of a partition theorem.
- Novelty and mathematical correctness remain subject to external review. Passing finite checks does not certify any infinite theorem.
