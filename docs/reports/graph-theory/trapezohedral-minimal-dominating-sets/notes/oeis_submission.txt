Date: September 20, 2026

The generating function attributed to Joerg Arndt (Jan 07 2026) follows
from the classification below. This draft does not claim global priority.

Model the graph with poles u,v and a rim a1,b1,...,an,bn. Edges are
u-ai, v-bi, ai-bi, bi-a(i+1), cyclically. Minimality means inclusion-
minimal among ALL dominating sets, with induced connectedness imposed
separately; subsets are not counted up to symmetry.

Every counted set contains exactly one pole. Both poles make every
selected rim vertex lack a private neighbor; neither pole forces a
consecutive rim path, which is too short to dominate or too long to
be minimal. Fix u in D and v outside D. Every selected b-vertex must
use v as its private neighbor, so there is exactly one, say bj.

The selected a-positions form a cyclic 0-1 word without 00 or 111,
hence a cycle of blocks 01 and 011. Mark the edge at bj. A mark is
legal exactly between the ones of an 011 block, or on either side
of the singleton one in an 01 block. Every such marked word for
n>=3 gives a connected minimal dominating set. Private neighbors:
v for bj; unmarked neighboring b-vertices across 1-0 edges for the
selected a-vertices; a zero not incident with the mark for u.

The marked block has weight 2*x^2+x^3, followed by a sequence of
unmarked blocks of weights x^2,x^3. Its initial zero has n possible
labeled positions. The two pole choices give

    a(n) = 2*n*[x^n] (2*x^2+x^3)/(1-x^2-x^3), n>=3.

Thus the generating function is 2*x*D_x of the displayed rational
function, minus the spurious degree-two term 8*x^2. Simplification
is exactly the conjectured generating function.

Additional formulas:

Let p(m)=[x^m]1/(1-x^2-x^3), with p(m)=0 for m<0. Then
    a(n)=2*n*(2*p(n-2)+p(n-3)), n>=3.

A finite sum is
    a(n)=2*n*sum_{r,s>=0,2*r+3*s=n}
      (2*binomial(r+s-1,s)+binomial(r+s-1,s-1)).
Binomials with out-of-range lower indices are zero.

A recurrence with a(3)=6, a(4)=16, a(5)=30 is
    (n-2)*(n-3)*a(n)
      = n*(n-3)*a(n-2)+n*(n-2)*a(n-3), n>=6.

Let lambda be the real root of lambda^3-lambda-1=0 and
C=2*(2*lambda+1)/(2*lambda+3). Then
    a(n)=C*n*lambda^n+O(n*lambda^(-n/2)),
and for every n>=45,
    a(n)=floor(C*n*lambda^n+1/2).

the exact three-root expression, and the uniform rounding bound.
Exhaustive checks through n=10 compare actual subset families,
not only counts. All 40 source terms n=3..42 match.
