DRAFT MATHEMATICAL ANNOTATION FOR OEIS A348410
Not submitted. Intended for human review alongside article.pdf.

The conjectured supercongruences a(m*p^r) == a(m*p^(r-1)) (mod p^(3*r))
for primes p >= 5 and m,r >= 1 follow from a more general coefficient
congruence. For integers u,v, set

  A_{u,v}(n) = [x^n] (1-x)^(-u*n) * (1+x)^(-v*n).

If p is odd, N > 0, and p divides N, then

  A_{u,v}(N) == A_{u,v}(N/p)
  (mod p^(3*valuation(N,p)-epsilon_p)),

where epsilon_3 = 1 and epsilon_p = 0 for p >= 5.
The original sequence is A_{2,1}. In particular,
a(m*3^r) == a(m*3^(r-1)) (mod 3^(3*r-1)).

Proof outline. Write log F(x) = sum_{j>=1} b_j*x^j/j with
F=(1-x)^(-u)*(1+x)^(-v) and b_j=u+v*(-1)^j. For odd p, b_{pj}=b_j.
The part H_p supported on indices not divisible by p is p-integral,
and F(x)^N = F(x^p)^(N/p)*exp(N*H_p(x)). Through modulus
p^(3*valuation(N,p)-epsilon_p), the exponential can be truncated after
its quadratic term; its linear term cannot contribute to degree N.
For p|M, the coefficient C_p(M)=[x^M]H_p(x)^2 has valuation at least
valuation(M,p)-epsilon_p. Reduce 1/(i*(M-i)) to -1/i^2, and use the
parity of M to reduce the weights to reciprocal-square sums on complete
blocks. Multiplication by 2 on the units modulo p^s shows that these
sums are divisible by p^(s-epsilon_p). Finally, logarithmic
differentiation gives

  valuation([x^j]F(x)^L,p) >= max(0,valuation(L,p)-valuation(j,p)).

Apply these estimates to each product in
sum_{m=1}^L [x^(L-m)]F(x)^L * C_p(p*m), where L=N/p.
The coefficient valuation and the reciprocal-square valuation add to
at least valuation(N,p)-epsilon_p. Multiplication by N^2/2 completes
the proof.

The bounds are sharp as uniform assertions: a(5)-a(1)=375 and
a(3)-a(1)=18. See article.pdf, Sections 2–4, for the full proof.
