A348410:

For every prime p and positive integers m,r,
  a(m*p^r) == a(m*p^(r-1)) (mod p^(3*r - valuation(12,p))).
In particular, the binary modulus is 2^(3*r-2) and the ternary modulus is
3^(3*r-1). The loss at 2 is necessary already at r=2, since a(4)-a(2)=80;
the loss at 3 is necessary at r=1, since a(3)-a(1)=18.

Let b(n) = (1/n^3)*Sum_{d|n} moebius(d)*a(n/d). Then 12*b(n) is a
nonnegative integer for every n>=1, and 12 is the smallest positive integer
clearing all its denominators. Indeed, b(3)=2/3 and b(4)=5/4.

The sequence 12*b(n), n>=1, starts
  12, 6, 8, 15, 36, 94, 276, 858, ... .
The supplied data/denominator12.csv contains the first 1000 exact values.

The integer P(n)=n^2*b(n) counts primitive rotation orbits of words of
length n in the alphabet of pairs (u,v) of nonnegative integers, each
letter having weight u+2v, with total word weight n. It is divisible by
n^2/gcd(n^2,12).

General parameter-family comment, potentially relevant to A352373:

Put A(alpha,beta;n) = [x^n](1-x)^(-alpha*n)(1+x)^(-beta*n), where alpha
and beta are arbitrary integers. For odd m and r>=2,
  A(alpha,beta;m*2^r) - A(alpha,beta;m*2^(r-1))
  == 2^(3*r-3)*alpha*beta*binomial((alpha+beta+1)*m-1,m-1)
     (mod 2^(3*r-2)).
Binomial coefficients with negative upper arguments are generalized
binomial coefficients. If alpha and beta are both odd and m=1, the
valuation of the difference is exactly 3*r-3 at every r>=2.

Not a proved comment:
The stronger equality valuation(a(2^r)-a(2^(r-1)),2)=3*r+1 for r>=4 is
conjectural in this archive, checked through r=15. Do not post it as a theorem.
