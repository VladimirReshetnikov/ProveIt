Proposed correction note for OEIS A000364
Prepared 19 September 2026; NOT submitted to the OEIS.

The conjecture in the comment dated May 8 2023 is false as written:
phi(27) = 18, but a(1) = 1 and
  a(19) = 23489580527043108252017828576198947741 = 10 (mod 27).

A correct replacement is:

For every positive integer m, (a(n) mod m) is eventually periodic with
least eventual period dividing phi(m). It is purely periodic for n >= 1
if and only if the odd part of m is cube-free. Thus 27 is the smallest
exceptional modulus.

More explicitly, for odd primes p and exponents e >= 1, the least
eventual period is
  d(p^e) = p^(e-1) * lcm(2, (p-1)/2),
and the first periodic index, indexing from a(0), is
  s(p^e) = 1 + max { 0 <= n < ceil(e/2) : 2*n + v_p(a(n)) < e }.
For powers of two the sequence is purely periodic from n=0, with least
period 1 for moduli 2 and 4, and 2^(b-1) modulo 2^b for b >= 3.
For general m, take the least common multiple of the local periods and
the maximum of the local first periodic indices.

The initial defect has the congruence
  a(n+phi(m)) - a(n) = -(-1)^((p-1)/2)*p^(2*n)*a(n) (mod p^e)
whenever p^e divides m and p is odd. This is consistent with classical
Kummer congruences, including the Euler factor; see Metsankyla (2014),
page 161. The attached research report supplies detailed proofs and
additional references, including recent work by Gulec on the full
up/down sequence.

Before submitting this note, independently check the report and the
priority of the period formula. Do not present the classical congruences
or the finite-moment identity as new results.
