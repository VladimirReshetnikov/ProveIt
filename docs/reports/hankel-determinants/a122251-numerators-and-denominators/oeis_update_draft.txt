Relevant entries: A122251 (general prime conjecture), A122249 (p=2).

Proposed comment:

The conjectured formula holds for every prime p. More generally, for positive
integers a,m with gcd(a,m)=1, the reduced numerator of
H_n(m,a)=det(1/(a+m(i+j)))_{0<=i,j<=n} is

    product_{p|m} p^(n(n+1)*v_p(m)+2*sum_{k=0}^n v_p(k!)),

independent of a. Here n is the Hankel index, so the matrix size is n+1.

Proof of the prime case:

Cauchy's determinant formula gives

    H_n(p,1) = p^(n(n+1))*(product_{k=0}^n k!)^2
               / product_{i,j=0}^n (1+p(i+j)).

The denominator is coprime to p. For any other prime q and d=q^e, write
N=n+1=td+b, 0<=b<d. Let r be the residue -p^(-1) modulo d. Each residue occurs
t times among 0,...,N-1, with one extra occurrence for residues 0,...,b-1.
Thus the number of denominator factors divisible by d is

    d*t^2 + 2*t*b + #{(u,v):0<=u,v<b, u+v=r mod d}
    >= d*t*(t-1)+2*t*b
    = 2*sum_{k=0}^n floor(k/d).

Summing over e shows that every factor q!=p in the factorial numerator
cancels. The reduced numerator is consequently

    p^(n(n+1)+2*sum_{k=0}^n v_p(k!))
    = (p^2)^(sum_{k=1}^n sum_{j>=0} floor(k/p^j)),

which is the recorded formula; all terms beyond j=n vanish. The same argument
with residue -a*m^(-1) applies to any q not dividing m, while primes dividing
m cannot divide a+ms under the coprimality assumption. This proves the
stated composite-step and offset extension.
