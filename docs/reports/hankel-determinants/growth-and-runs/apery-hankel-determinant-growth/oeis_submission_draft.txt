September 13, 2026. This does not concern the other arithmetic conjectures.

Suggested mathematical update:

Let C=(1+sqrt(2))^4 and lambda=C/4. Then
log(a(n)) = n*(n+1)*log(lambda) + O(n).
In particular, a(n)^(1/n^2) tends to lambda.

Proof outline:

Edgar's positive moment-density theorem gives A_k=int_0^C x^k phi(x) dx,
with total mass 1. Write c=1/C. The density is positive on (0,c) and (c,C),
is logarithmically divergent at 0, has square-root vanishing at C, and has
one-sided Frobenius leading order among 0,1/2,1 at c. Hence, for some eta>0,
phi(x)>=eta*((x-c)*(x-C))^2 almost everywhere.

Let h_k be the minimum squared norm of a monic degree-k polynomial for this
measure. Gram factorization gives a(n)=prod_(k=0)^n h_k and h_0=1.
The monic Chebyshev trial polynomial gives h_k<=4*lambda^(2k), k>=1.
Multiplying any monic degree-k polynomial by (x-c)*(x-C) and comparing with
the monic Legendre norm gives
h_k>=eta*C^(2k+5)/((2k+5)*binomial(2k+4,k+2)^2)
   >=eta*C*lambda^4*lambda^(2k).
The last inequality uses (2j+1)*binomial(2j,j)^2<=16^j.
Multiplication over k=1,...,n proves the stated logarithmic estimate.
