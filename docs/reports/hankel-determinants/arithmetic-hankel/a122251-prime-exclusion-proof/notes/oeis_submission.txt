Suggested comment (subject to editorial review):

The numerator conjecture holds for every prime p. More generally, if
m >= 0, d,b >= 1, and gcd(d,b)=1, the reduced numerator of
D_m(d,b) = det(1/(b+d*(i+j)))_{0<=i,j<m}
is Product_{ell|d, ell prime} ell^(v_ell(d)*m*(m-1)
+ 2*Sum_{j=0..m-1} v_ell(j!)). Thus it is a square and is independent
of the coprime shift b. Setting d=p, b=1, and m=n+1 proves the formula
in A122251 and its specialization in A122249.

Compact proof:

Cauchy's determinant identity gives
D_m(d,b)=d^(m*(m-1))*Product_{j=0..m-1}(j!)^2 /
Product_{0<=i,j<m}(b+d*(i+j)).
Every prime ell dividing d is coprime to the denominator, so its valuation
is exactly the asserted one. For a prime ell not dividing d, let h=ell^a
and write m=q*h+r, 0<=r<h. The number of pairs with
h | b+d*(i+j) equals the number whose sum lies in one specified residue
class modulo h. This count is h*q^2+2*q*r+E, where E>=0 counts the
pairs of surplus residues. The contribution of h to the squared
superfactorial is 2*Sum_{j=0..m-1} floor(j/h)
=h*q*(q-1)+2*q*r. The denominator contribution exceeds this by
h*q+E >= 0. Sum over a>=1. Thus no prime not dividing d can survive
in the reduced numerator.

For d=p prime and m=n+1, the exponent is
n*(n+1)+2*Sum_{j=1..n} v_p(j!)
=2*Sum_{j=1..n} Sum_{a>=0} floor(j/p^a), as conjectured.
