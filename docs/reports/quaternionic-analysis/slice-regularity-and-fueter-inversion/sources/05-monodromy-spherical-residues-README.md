# Literature and source audit

Date of audit: 21 September 2026.

## Starting attachment

The user-supplied `quaternionic_analysis.tex` is an expository synthesis dated
20 September 2026. Its constructive axial inverse is stated on a simply
connected upper-half-plane domain. Its following paragraph explicitly notes
possible nonzero periods on a nonsimply connected base. The present article
addresses that gap and develops related singularity and energy results.
The original manuscript was read in full. It is not treated as a publication
establishing novelty of the present results, nor is it redistributed.

## Primary literature examined

1. R. Ghiloni and A. Perotti, *Slice regular functions on real alternative
   algebras*, Advances in Mathematics 226 (2011), 1662-1691.
   arXiv:1008.4318. Used for the standard stem framework, not for novelty.

2. F. Colombo, I. Sabadini, and F. Sommen, *The inverse Fueter mapping theorem*,
   Communications on Pure and Applied Analysis 10 (2011), 1165-1181.
   DOI:10.3934/cpaa.2011.10.1165. The official publisher record and abstract were
   inspected. They establish that inverse integral representations are prior
   work; the complete paper was not audited theorem by theorem in this session.

3. F. Colombo, D. Pena Pena, I. Sabadini, and F. Sommen, *A new integral formula
   for the inverse Fueter mapping theorem*, Journal of Mathematical Analysis and
   Applications 417 (2014), 112-122. arXiv:1302.0685.
   The full arXiv text was available. In particular, Theorem 3 on PDF page 7 was
   inspected in text and as a page image. Its stated planar base is a rectangle
   bounded away from the real axis. The article also discusses kernel ambiguity.

4. B. Dong and T. Qian, *Further Aspects of the Fueter Mapping Theorem*,
   arXiv:1805.02966 (2018).
   The HTML text, including the introduction and the explicit contour-based
   surjectivity formulation in Theorem 3.6, was inspected. Broad same-domain
   surjectivity should not be assumed without attention to branch conventions.
   The new draft proves its own exact criterion rather than relying on that
   surjectivity statement.

5. A. De Martino and S. Pinton, *A variation of the inverse Fueter theorem and
   the generalized polyanalytic Cauchy-Kovalevskaya extension of order 2*,
   arXiv:2608.07711v1, 7 August 2026.
   The current preprint was inspected, particularly its stated questions,
   definitions, and scope. It addresses inverse factorizations and polyanalytic
   extensions, not the exact global period problem formulated in this draft.
   Its questions P1-P3 are not claimed here as unresolved or newly solved.

## Bibliographic leads not fully audited

- A. K. Common and F. Sommen, *Axial monogenic functions from holomorphic
  functions*, JMAA 179 (1993), 610-629; DOI:10.1006/jmaa.1993.1372.
- B. Dong, K. I. Kou, T. Qian, and I. Sabadini, *On the inversion of Fueter's
  theorem*, Journal of Geometry and Physics 108 (2016), 102-116.

Publisher/author records and citations established these bibliographic leads.
An attempted author-hosted download of the 2016 full text did not succeed.
No claim about the absence of a result in either complete paper is justified by
this audit. Related inverse constructions using spherical monogenics and
biaxial functions were also identified as background leads, not exhaustively
compared.

## Search limitations

Searches combined inverse Fueter, periods, monodromy, multiply connected domains,
cohomology, axial monogenic functions, integrable singularities, logarithmic
singularities, and spherical residues. Several narrow searches returned mostly
irrelevant results. Those results were not used as evidence.

This was not a complete MathSciNet/zbMATH or full-text corpus search. It cannot
establish that the results are absent from all literature. The candidate-original
claim is therefore deliberately limited to the explicit combined formulations
not located in the sources checked. All important results are independently
proved in the article, but originality still requires specialist verification.
The draft does not claim to have refuted a particular published theorem without
a separate full hypothesis and branch audit.
