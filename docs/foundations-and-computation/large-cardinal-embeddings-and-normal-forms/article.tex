point $\kappa$ induces two ordered-field embeddings $J,H_j:\No\to\No$.

\[

\begin{abstract}
Large-cardinal embeddings preserve every finitary arithmetic identity, but
need not preserve the external strong sums that define surreal normal forms.
We make this distinction exact. Let $j:V\to M$ be an amenable elementary
embedding into a transitive inner class, with critical point $\kappa$. Its
restriction to surreal sign sequences gives an ordered-field embedding $J$.
The same action on Conway monomials has a unique strongly $\R$-linear
extension $H_j$. If $x$ has normal form indexed by $\theta$, then $J(x)-H_j(x)$
is precisely the subseries of the transformed normal form indexed by
$j(\theta)\setminus\imset j\theta$. Consequently the equalizer of $J$ and
$H_j$ is exactly the real-closed field of surreal numbers with fewer than
$\kappa$ outer normal-form terms. Moreover, their two image fields intersect
exactly in the common image of that equalizer, and are its incomparable
logarithmic and strong-sum closures. For a positive strongly
summable family, preservation by $J$ holds exactly when the index set has
cardinality less than $\kappa$.

The defect has a first term at the first missing index $\kappa$. For
$h_A=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\omega^{-\alpha}$, $A\subseteq\kappa$, its coefficient
at $\omega^{-\kappa}$ is the indicator of membership in the normal measure
derived from $j$. This gives a coefficient realization of classical
ultrafilter evaluation and a precise measurable-cardinal criterion for the
associated Hadamard mask algebra. The field results transfer to surcomplex
numbers and to omnific integers; a purely infinite omnific defect encodes the
same measure while its constant term vanishes. We also obtain a logarithmic
defect homomorphism, an exact exponential compatibility locus, and a
supercompact seed-coefficient construction. The
article distinguishes these contributions from standard Hahn reindexing,
classical derived measures, and the repository's earlier work on birthday
expansions and forcing. Twelve further research questions and a formalization
plan conclude the account.

\mergetag{} This report merges three manuscripts on the same pair of maps.
The text above is the abstract of the base manuscript 07. Manuscript 06
adds descent: the companion value $H_j(x)$ belongs to the target model
exactly when the seed $\imset j\lambda$ does, where $\lambda$ is the
normal-form length of $x$; in a set ultrapower this is closure of $M$ under
$\lambda$-sequences, and for a normal-measure ultrapower it gives an exact
three-way classification at support sizes $<\kappa$, $\kappa$, $>\kappa$,
without GCH. It also adds bounded and omnific measure codes, and
support-cover and exact-descent forms of strong compactness and
supercompactness. Manuscript 09 adds a large-cardinal-free theory of the
short-support field (real closed at every uncountable cardinal, closed and
nowhere dense, with an omnific integer part), algebraically independent
omnific masks over that whole field from almost-disjoint families
($2^\kappa$ of them in one set-sized Hahn field when $2^{<\kappa}=\kappa$), a
compatible-field-embedding criterion for measurability, seed coefficients
over arbitrary index sets, and an omnific prefix-tree form of weak
compactness. The strong-compactness, supercompactness and weak-compactness
statements are credited translations of classical ultrafilter and tree
characterizations. As a consequence, if there is a measurable cardinal then
there is a field embedding of $\No$ that preserves and reflects the omnific
integers and their constant terms but is not strongly additive. There are
34 research questions in all, and a list of every source's non-claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{The questions answered and the novelty boundary}\label{lce:sec:intro}

\subsection{The distinction that drives the construction}
For a set-theoretic elementary embedding, the image $j(I)$ of an infinite set
is generally larger than its pointwise image $\imset jI$. For a surreal
normal form, this is not merely a difference between two encodings of the
same sum. The additional indices can carry additional nonzero monomials.
The resulting discrepancy is visible in arithmetic, even when every original
summand is individually fixed.

This article answers the following concrete question under a measurable-cardinal
hypothesis: \emph{how much of a surreal number is determined by the action of
an embedding on all real coefficients and all monomials?} The answer is
exactly its short-support part. There are two explicitly associated embeddings
that agree on all those generators, and even on every ordinal, but disagree
on every surreal whose outer normal form has at least $\kappa$ terms.
The distinction is a support-cardinality threshold, not a birthday cutoff.

Conway's normal-form theorem and the generalized-series presentation of
$\No$ are the classical background \cite{Conway,Gonshor,vdDE,EhrlichNames}.
Strongly additive maps and monomial reindexing also have a substantial
literature. In particular, Kaplan--Krapp--Serra distinguish strong from
non-strong automorphisms and study their relation to extra surreal structure
\cite{KKS}. The contribution proposed here is not the bare possibility of
non-strong behavior. It is the exact critical-point formula, equalizer,
intersection, and coefficient reconstruction for the specific large-cardinal
construction.

\subsection{Assumption and headline statements}
Throughout the main construction, assume that
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:setting}
 j:V\longrightarrow M\subseteq V
\end{equation}
is an elementary class embedding, $M$ is transitive and contains every
ordinal, and $\kappa=\crit(j)$ exists. Class parameters are allowed in the
Separation and Replacement instances used below. A sufficient, familiar
instance is a normal ultrapower embedding from a measurable cardinal.
One can carry out that instance in $\ZFC$ using the measure as a set
parameter; the use of classes is notation for definable constructions.
The general class-embedding version can be read in $\GBC$ with the indicated
embedding as an amenable class. These hypotheses are discussed in
Section~\ref{lce:sec:foundations}.

Let
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:shortfield}
 \Sk=\set{x\in\No:\abs{\supp(x)}<\kappa},
\end{equation}
where $\supp(x)$ means the set of \emph{outer exponents} in its Conway normal
form. Zero has empty support. Define $J(x)=j(x)$ on surreal sign sequences,
and define
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:Hintro}
 H_j\left(\sum_{\alpha<\theta}r_\alpha\omega^{a_\alpha}\right)
   =\sum_{\alpha<\theta}r_\alpha\omega^{J(a_\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
The central conclusions, proved in Sections~\ref{lce:sec:companion}--\ref{lce:sec:positive},
are
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Eq}(J,H_j)&=\Sk,\label{lce:eq:eqintro}\\
 J(\No)\cap H_j(\No)&=J(\Sk)=H_j(\Sk),\label{lce:eq:interintro}\\
 J\left(\sum_{i\in I}x_i\right)=\sum_{i\in I}J(x_i)
  &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \abs I<\kappa
   \quad(x_i>0\text{ and strongly summable}).\label{lce:eq:posintro}
\end{align}
The two image fields in \eqref{lce:eq:interintro} are incomparable, not merely
different. Their common image is real closed. Coefficient projection
canonically identifies the two images as immediate valued extensions of
that common field (Theorem~\ref{lce:thm:projection}). An intrinsic version
shows that every positive strong sum of at least $\kappa$ image elements
escapes the elementary image (Theorem~\ref{lce:thm:escape}).

For $A\subseteq\kappa$, put $h_A=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\omega^{-\alpha}$.
One then obtains
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:Uintro}
 \coef{-\kappa}{\bigl(J(h_A)-H_j(h_A)\bigr)}
    =\begin{cases}1,&\kappa\in j(A),\\0,&\kappa\notin j(A).
      \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus the usual derived measure is recovered from a distinguished coefficient
of the arithmetic defect. The derived measure itself is classical
\cite{Neeman}; the coefficient realization is the new construction being
proposed.

\subsection{The headline statements of the other two sources \mergetag}
\label{lce:sub:headlines}
Manuscripts 06 and 09 prove the equalizer and the defect formula
independently, under essentially the same hypotheses, and state them in
their own headline theorems. We print both, with the numbering of this
report in the proof pointers. The support cardinal $\abs{\supp(x)}$, the
number of nonzero terms in the Conway normal form of $x$, is 06's
$\operatorname{sc}(x)$; the short-support field $\Sk$ is 06's $K_{<\kappa}$
and 09's $F_\kappa$ (Section~\ref{lce:sec:conventions}).

\begin{theorem}[Main comparison and descent \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:thm:overview}
Under the standing embedding hypotheses \eqref{lce:cs:eq:standing}, the
following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $J$ and $H_j$ are ordered-field embeddings fixing $\R$ and agreeing
on all monomials. The map $H_j$ preserves every set-indexed strong sum;
$J$ preserves every such sum indexed by a set of cardinality $<\kappa$,
but not every one indexed by $\kappa$.
\item For every surreal $x$,
$J(x)=H_j(x)\Longleftrightarrow\abs{\supp(x)}<\kappa$.
\item For every normal form of ordinal length $\lambda$,
$H_j(x)\in\No^M\Longleftrightarrow\imset j\lambda\in M$.
When $j$ is a set-ultrapower embedding, these conditions are equivalent
to ${}^{\lambda}M\cap V\subseteq M$.
\item If $j$ is the ultrapower by a normal measure on $\kappa$, then
$H_j(x)\in\No^M\Longleftrightarrow\abs{\supp(x)}\le\kappa$.
Consequently equality, unequal descent, and failure of descent occur
exactly at support sizes $<\kappa$, $\kappa$, and $>\kappa$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
The proof is distributed among \thref{lce:thm:companion},
\thref{lce:thm:defect}, \thref{lce:thm:positive},
\thref{lce:cs:thm:landing}, \leref{lce:cs:lem:seed-closure} and
\thref{lce:cs:thm:normal-trichotomy}. No cardinal-arithmetic hypothesis such
as GCH is needed for the last clause \src{06}.

Two further manifestations of the same phenomenon are in 06's
introduction. For $x=p(x)+r(x)+\eps(x)$, with $p(x)$ purely infinite,
$r(x)\in\R$ and $\eps(x)$ infinitesimal,
\[
 \frac{H_j(\exp x)}{\exp(H_j x)}=\exp\bigl(J(p(x))-H_j(p(x))\bigr),
\]
so the Hahn lift commutes with exponentiation at $x$ exactly when the
positive-exponent part has fewer than $\kappa$ terms
(\thref{lce:thm:expdefect}, proved by 06 and 07). Exact descent of
$H_j(h_\lambda)$ characterizes a supercompactness witness, whereas an
internally short binary-support \emph{cover} characterizes a strong
compactness witness; multiplication by a monomial gives the same tests
inside the omnific integers (Section~\ref{lce:cs:sec:compactness}) \src{06}.

\begin{theorem}[Main package \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:package}
Assume that $\kappa$ is measurable and let $j:V\to M$ be the transitive
ultrapower embedding by a normal measure on $\kappa$. There are ordered
field embeddings $J,H_j:\No\to\No$ with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Both fix $\R$ and agree on every Conway monomial. The map $J$
commutes with Conway's omega-map, and $H_j$ preserves every set-indexed
strongly summable family.
\item Their exact equalizer is $\Eq(J,H_j)=\Sk$. For a normal form of length
$\theta$, the difference is the subseries indexed by
$j(\theta)\setminus\imset j\theta$ in the normal form of $J(x)$.
\item The map $J$ preserves every strongly summable family of cardinality
less than $\kappa$, but fails to preserve a specified family of size
$\kappa$ consisting of purely infinite omnific monomials.
\item The equalizers on omnific integers and on the specified
complexification are $\Oz\cap\Sk$ and $\Sk(i)$, respectively.
\item There are $2^\kappa$ explicitly described omnific integers,
algebraically independent over $\Sk$, on each of which $J$ and $H_j$
disagree.
\item The normal measure can be recovered from one designated normal-form
coefficient of $J(Y_A)$, where $A\subseteq\kappa$ and $Y_A$ is an explicit
omnific code of $A$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Proof pointers: (i) \prref{lce:prop:J} and \thref{lce:thm:companion};
(ii) \thref{lce:thm:defect}; (iii) Theorems~\ref{lce:thm:positive}
and~\ref{lce:sg:thm:threshold}; (iv) Theorems~\ref{lce:thm:Oz}
and~\ref{lce:thm:complex}; (v) \thref{lce:sg:thm:maximal} and
Corollary~\ref{lce:sg:cor:combined}; (vi) \thref{lce:sg:thm:measure}.
According to 09, part (ii), its full difference formula, and the
coefficient-independent algebraic construction underlying part (v) are its
main proposed contributions. The hypotheses and the exact formulas matter.
Merely constructing a Hahn lift, observing that elementary embeddings fail
to preserve some long sequences, or translating an ultrafilter into a
numerical code would not by itself constitute the same result \src{09}.
Part (ii) is proved independently by all three sources and printed once, as
\thref{lce:thm:defect}.

\subsection{What is not being asserted}\label{lce:sub:nonasserted}
We do not establish that arbitrary non-strong surreal embeddings require a
measurable cardinal. We do not prove a new consistency result about
measurability, supercompactness, or elementary self-embeddings of the set
universe. We do not identify the two embeddings as automorphisms. Nor do we
claim that their equality on monomials implies equality on a completed
field: the whole point is that the relevant external sums need not be
preserved by $J$.

The proofs are mathematical arguments, not output from a theorem prover.
The accompanying finite checks test finite identities used in the arguments;
they cannot test an elementary embedding of the universe or a nonprincipal
complete ultrafilter. A targeted literature and repository comparison appears
in Section~\ref{lce:sec:position}; it is not an exhaustive priority search.

\src{09} The results do not establish the existence or consistency of any
large cardinal. They do not produce a nontrivial elementary embedding
$(V,\in)\to(V,\in)$, and do not settle the outstanding structure questions
for all surreal exponential automorphisms. The research questions at the end
include genuine gaps in the present theory; they are not presented as an
exhaustive list of previously published open problems.

\src{06} Manuscript 06 is an AI-assisted research draft with written proofs,
not a refereed or machine-checked paper. Its proposed contributions are the
exact comparison, defect and descent statements, not the underlying
Hahn-lifting or ultrapower-seed machinery. They were not located in the
sources it inspected; this is not a priority certification or a claim to
resolve a named published conjecture. All its large-cardinal implications
retain their hypotheses; no consistency strength beyond the corresponding
classical ultrafilter principle is claimed. Appendix~\ref{lce:app:nonclaims}
lists every non-claim of every source.

\section{Foundations, normal forms, and the meaning of a sum}\label{lce:sec:foundations}

\subsection{Surreal conventions}
We use the sign-sequence realization of $\No$: a surreal is a function from
an ordinal to $\{-,+\}$. Its birthday $\bd(x)$ is the domain of that function.
The numerical order is the first-disagreement order, with
$-<\text{undefined}<+$. Simplicity is proper prefix inclusion.
Ordinals are identified with all-plus sequences.
The distinction between an ordinal and its all-plus sign code will normally
be suppressed, but all applications of $j$ commute with that identification.

The Conway monomial map is denoted by
\[
 \Om(a)=\omega^a.
\]
Here $\omega^a$ is the Conway omega-map, not an abbreviation for
$\exp(a\log\omega)$ with an arbitrarily chosen exponential structure.
It satisfies $\omega^{a+b}=\omega^a\omega^b$, and is strictly increasing.
Every nonzero surreal has a unique normal form
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:NF}
 x=\sum_{\alpha<\theta} r_\alpha\omega^{a_\alpha},
 \qquad r_\alpha\in\R\setminus\{0\},\quad
 a_\alpha>a_\beta\quad(\alpha<\beta<\theta),
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ is an ordinal. All supports in this article are sets, even
though the full coefficient and exponent universes are classes.
Write $\len(x)=\theta$ for the length of \eqref{lce:eq:NF} and
$\supp(x)=\{a_\alpha:\alpha<\theta\}$.
For $x\ne0$, define
\[
 \LT(x)=r_0\omega^{a_0},\qquad v(x)=-a_0.
\]
This is the additive natural valuation with values in $(\No,+)$; $v(0)=\infty$.
The infinitesimal ideal of the finite-surreal valuation ring is
\[
 \infideal=\{0\}\cup\{x\in\No:v(x)>0\}.
\]
These conventions use decreasing omega-exponents; sources that use $t^g$
with increasing support have the opposite sign convention for exponents.

\begin{definition}[Strong summability]\label{lce:def:strong}
A set-indexed family $(x_i)_{i\in I}$ is \emph{strongly summable} if
$\bigcup_{i\in I}\supp(x_i)$ is reverse well ordered and each exponent occurs
in the support of only finitely many members. Its strong sum is the unique
normal form obtained by adding those finitely many coefficients at each
exponent and discarding zero coefficients. A map is \emph{strongly
$\R$-linear} if it is $\R$-linear and preserves all such external set-indexed
sums, including their summability.
\end{definition}

The field laws for generalized series, in particular the finiteness of
contributions in a product, are part of the Hahn--Neumann support theorem
\cite{Gonshor,vdDE,KKS}. We use them for sets of exponents. There is no
summation over a proper-class support.

\begin{remark}[Not order-topological convergence]\label{lce:rem:topology}
A displayed strong sum is not defined as the supremum of its partial sums,
and is not asserted to be an order-topological limit in all of $\No$.
For example, the sum $h_\kappa$ constructed below exists by
Definition~\ref{lce:def:strong}. This makes no assertion that its partial sums
converge against every positive surreal neighborhood. Confusing these two
notions would invalidate the threshold argument.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Elementary embeddings and external sets}
The standard large-cardinal facts used here are that the critical point of
\eqref{lce:eq:setting} is an uncountable inaccessible cardinal, that $j$ fixes
$V_\kappa$, and that a measurable cardinal supplies such an embedding by a
normal ultrapower \cite{Neeman}. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:fixedcoeff}
 j(\R)=\R,\qquad j(r)=r\ (r\in\R),\qquad \R^M=\R^V.
\end{equation}
We take these large-cardinal facts as background, not as results of this
article. When a normal measure $U$ is chosen, the ultrapower is first formed
on set functions with domain $\kappa$ and then collapsed to its transitive
class model. Countable completeness gives well-foundedness. All individual
images and all set-indexed images used below are sets.

We distinguish $j(I)$, an element of $M$, from the external pointwise image
$\imset jI=\{j(i):i\in I\}$, formed in $V$. The latter need not belong to
$M$. Every cardinality without a superscript is computed in $V$. In
particular, $\abs{\supp(x)}<\kappa$ always concerns the original external
support. No cardinal arithmetic in $M$ is silently substituted for it.

\paragraph{06's class conventions and standing hypotheses \src{06}.} The
concrete large-cardinal statements concern a countably complete ultrafilter
on a set and its well-founded ultrapower. They can be read in $\ZFC$ using
definable classes. For statements about an arbitrary class embedding, work
in $\GBC$ with the embedding as a class parameter, or assume the
corresponding class-parameter Separation and Replacement instances. In
particular, restrictions of $j$ to sets and their ranges are sets. No object
consisting of all class embeddings is formed. 06's standing hypotheses are
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:standing}
 j:V\longrightarrow M\subseteq V,
 \qquad M\text{ transitive},\quad M\models\ZFC,
 \quad\On^M=\On^V,
 \quad\crit(j)=\kappa,
\end{equation}
with $j$ elementary for set theory; the canonical sign-sequence presentation
identifies $\No^M$ with a subclass of $\No^V$, and when $j$ comes from an
ultrapower its values are understood after the Mostowski collapse. For a
function $a:\lambda\to V$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:array-evaluation}
 j(a)(j(\alpha))=j(a(\alpha))\qquad(\alpha<\lambda),
\end{equation}
but $j(a)$ has values at every index below $j(\lambda)$, not just at
indices in $\imset j\lambda$. Hypotheses \eqref{lce:cs:eq:standing} are
those of \eqref{lce:eq:setting}, with the model-theoretic reading made
explicit.

Where 07 takes the critical-point facts as background, 06 proves them; the
proof is short and is printed here, since Section~\ref{lce:cs:sec:descent}
uses the power-set clause.

\begin{lemma}[Critical point facts \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:lem:critical}
Under \eqref{lce:cs:eq:standing}, the cardinal $\kappa$ is uncountable,
regular, and strong limit. The embedding fixes every real number, and $M$
and $V$ have the same reals. Moreover $\Pow(\kappa)^M=\Pow(\kappa)^V$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define the derived ultrafilter $U_j=\{A\subseteq\kappa:\kappa\in j(A)\}$. It
is a set under the class conventions. Preservation of complements makes it
an ultrafilter. It is nonprincipal since $j(\alpha)=\alpha$ for
$\alpha<\kappa$. For a family of fewer than $\kappa$ members, $j$ fixes the
indexing ordinal, so $\kappa$ belongs to the image of their intersection.
Thus $U_j$ is $\kappa$-complete. No set of size $<\kappa$ belongs to it, by
intersecting the complements of its singletons. The critical point cannot
be finite or $\omega$, since these ordinals are fixed by their
set-theoretic definitions.

A cofinal sequence in $\kappa$ of length $<\kappa$ would partition $\kappa$
into fewer than $\kappa$ bounded, hence null, sets, contradicting
completeness. For the strong-limit property, suppose $\mu<\kappa$ and choose
distinct subsets $A_\xi\subseteq\mu$ for $\xi<\kappa$. For each coordinate
$\eta<\mu$, choose the $U_j$-large side of the partition of $\kappa$
according to whether $\eta\in A_\xi$. Intersecting these $\mu$ large sets
yields a large set on which all $A_\xi$ agree, contradicting their
distinctness. Consequently $2^\mu<\kappa$. The same standard argument shows
that $\kappa$ is a cardinal; alternatively, a bijection from a smaller
ordinal onto $\kappa$ contradicts the definition of the critical point after
applying $j$.

By induction on rank below $\kappa$, using inaccessibility and the fact
that sets of size $<\kappa$ have pointwise images, $j$ fixes $V_\kappa$
pointwise. In particular it fixes all real numbers. Every ambient real
therefore belongs to $M$, and transitivity gives the other inclusion.
Finally, for $A\subseteq\kappa$, the identity $A=j(A)\cap\kappa$ puts $A$ in
$M$. Since every member of the internal power set is an actual subset, the
two power sets agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the target has all real coefficients \src{09}]
\label{lce:sg:rem:reals}
A second route to the reals clause: a real can be coded by a subset of
$\omega$, and $j$ fixes that subset coordinate by coordinate since
$j(\omega)=\omega$. Thus every real of $V$ belongs to $M$ and
$\R^M=\R^V$, so \leref{lce:lem:absolute} applies to the target. It does not
assert that $M$ contains every set of reals or every set of old surreals.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[The first missing index \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:lem:index}
For any set $I$,
\[
 j(I)=\imset jI\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\abs I<\kappa.
\]
For any ordinal $\theta$, define
$P_\theta=j(\theta)\setminus\imset j\theta$. Then
\[
 P_\theta=\varnothing\quad(\theta<\kappa),\qquad
 \min P_\theta=\kappa\quad(\theta\ge\kappa).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a bijection $e:\mu\to I$, where $\mu$ is the cardinality of $I$.
If $\mu<\kappa$, then $j(\mu)=\mu$, and every member of $j(I)$ has the
form $j(e)(\alpha)=j(e(\alpha))$ for some $\alpha<\mu$.
Conversely, suppose $\mu\ge\kappa$. The ordinal $\kappa$ belongs to
$j(\mu)$, but is not $j(\alpha)$ for any ordinal $\alpha$: below $\kappa$
all ordinals are fixed, while for $\alpha\ge\kappa$ we have
$j(\alpha)\ge j(\kappa)>\kappa$. Thus $j(e)(\kappa)$ is in $j(I)$ but
not in $\imset jI$, by injectivity of $j(e)$.

For ordinal $\theta<\kappa$, every index is fixed. If $\theta\ge\kappa$,
then $j(\theta)>\kappa$, all smaller indices are in $\imset j\theta$, and
$\kappa$ is not. This proves both assertions.
\end{proof}
06 (its Lemma ``The first omitted index'') and 09 state and prove the
ordinal clause with the same argument; the first clause, for an arbitrary
set $I$, is 07's.

\subsection{The absoluteness needed to compare the two fields}
There are two universe changes in play. Applying $j$ moves an input into
$M$. Then the identity inclusion regards the resulting old surreal sign
sequence as a surreal of $V$. We need the standard constructions to agree
under this second passage.

\begin{lemma}[Absolute surreal constructions \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:lem:absolute}
Let $M\subseteq V$ be transitive models or inner classes of $\ZFC$ with the
same ordinals and the same reals. On inputs that belong to $M$, the identity
inclusion $\No^M\subseteq\No^V$ preserves order, the field operations, the
Conway omega-map, and canonical normal forms. An $M$-set strongly summable
family in $M$ is externally strongly summable in $V$, with the same sum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order and prefix comparison are comparisons of ordinal-length sign
functions, so they are absolute. The simplest separator of a given
\emph{set} cut is constructed by following the forced signs until the
first permissible termination; this construction depends only on its
input sign sequences. Thus the cut construction is absolute. Well-founded
recursion on canonical sign options gives the same negation, addition,
multiplication, and omega-map in both universes. Inverses then agree by
uniqueness in the ordered field.

For normal forms we use the constructive sign-sequence version of the
Conway--Gonshor normal-form theorem \cite[Chapter~5]{Gonshor}, not just the
existence of an unspecified isomorphism with a Hahn field. The conversion
from a reverse well-ordered list of real-coefficient monomials to its
surreal sign sequence is given by the normal-form sign expansion: ordinal
sign concatenations with the prescribed deletions determined by the input
exponent signs and preceding terms. The ordinal operations, restrictions,
and sign comparisons in that construction are absolute on a given set of
inputs. Hence an $M$-normal form represents the same sign sequence in $V$.
Uniqueness of the normal form in $V$ shows that it acquires no extra terms
on passing to $V$.

Finally, a reverse well ordering of an $M$-set has an ordinal order-type
witness in $M$, which remains a witness in $V$; finiteness of an old fiber
is absolute. The old family is therefore still strongly summable. Its
coefficientwise finite sums agree, as do the normal forms just considered.
\end{proof}

All three sources prove this lemma. 06's proof follows the same
constructive route as the one above, adds that the sign-recursion algorithm
and its ordinal stopping stage are absolute because the rank bound on the
option sign lengths bounds the construction in both universes, and includes
Gonshor's exponential in the statement: it is treated by its canonical
option recursion with finite Taylor polynomials, the real constants and
natural indices agreeing (this is \leref{lce:lem:exptransfer}). 06 states
the lemma under \eqref{lce:cs:eq:standing}, and 09 for a transitive inner
class of $\ZFC$ containing all ordinals and all reals of $V$. 09's proof is
genuinely different at the limit step and does not invoke the sign-expansion
algorithm; it is printed as a second route.

\begin{proof}[Second route \src{09}]
Sign comparison and sign restriction are absolute. Every sign prefix of an
old sign sequence is old: it is obtained by restricting that sequence to an
ordinal in $M$.

For the simplest-number operation, let a nonempty convex class be given by
an absolute condition on old parameters and suppose that $M$ has a member
of that class. Its externally simplest member is an initial sign segment of
every member, in particular of the internally simplest member. It therefore
belongs to $M$. Membership is absolute, so internal minimality forces the
two simplest members to coincide. Applied recursively to the canonical
option sets, this proves absoluteness of addition, negation, multiplication
and the omega-map. Inversion then follows from uniqueness of a
multiplicative inverse. The relevant recursions use sign prefixes, finite
arithmetic, and sets of options; their domains for any specified input are
sets. For the omega-map one can use its defining cut with positive integers
and their reciprocals, so there is no quantification over newly available
coefficients.

For normal forms, let $(r_\alpha,a_\alpha)_{\alpha<\theta}\in M$ be a
normal-form sequence, and argue by induction on $\theta$. The zero and
successor steps use finite field operations. Suppose that $\theta$ is a
nonzero limit and all proper partial sums $p_\eta$ have already been
identified in the two universes. Consider the convex class defined by
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:limit-cylinder}
 \abs{z-p_{\alpha+1}}<\frac{\omega^{a_\alpha}}{n}
 \qquad(\alpha<\theta,\ 1\le n<\omega).
\end{equation}
Both universes have their own proposed full sum in this class. The
condition is absolute on old $z$ because its quantifiers range over the old
ordinal $\theta$ and the ordinary natural numbers, and every boundary in
\eqref{lce:sg:eq:limit-cylinder} is already absolute.

In either universe, a member of this class has the prescribed sequence as
an initial segment of its normal form. To see this, compare its normal form
with the prescribed sequence at the first exponent or coefficient at which
they differ; the corresponding inequality in
\eqref{lce:sg:eq:limit-cylinder} fails. A discrepancy after all prescribed
terms is allowed, but then the prescribed full sum is a normal-form
truncation of that member. The classical truncation-simplicity theorem says
that a proper normal-form truncation is simpler than the original surreal;
see \cite[Theorem~19.2(4)]{Berarducci}. It follows that the full sum is the
simplest member of \eqref{lce:sg:eq:limit-cylinder} in each universe. The
simplest-member argument of the first paragraph now identifies the two full
sums. This completes the induction. This reasoning is also consistent with
the usual recursive construction of normal-form sums, whose limit stage
chooses a simplest approximant \cite[Theorem~10.2]{Berarducci}.

For $x\in M$, take its normal-form sequence computed in $M$; it sums
externally to the same $x$, and uniqueness of normal forms gives the claim
about extraction of coefficients and exponents. Finally, for a strongly
summable family in $M$, its union of supports has in $M$ an ordinal
enumeration witnessing reverse well-ordering. This witness is still valid in
$V$. The finitely many occurrences at each exponent and their coefficient
sums are absolute. The resulting normal-form sequence belongs to $M$, so its
sum is absolute by the preceding argument.
\end{proof}
09 stresses the point of this route: it is not enough to say that normal
forms are definable and therefore absolute, since general definable objects
need not be absolute \src{09}. 06 adds that the lemma is a foundational
interface, not a claim that the full Hahn fields of $M$ and $V$ coincide.

\begin{remark}[Old monomials, new sums \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:rem:interface}
An external sequence of old monomials may fail to be an element of $M$,
and its sum may be a new surreal. That failure is precisely what the
descent theorem of Section~\ref{lce:cs:sec:descent} detects.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Where a classical theorem is used]\label{lce:rem:classicalinput}
The normal-form sign-expansion theorem is a classical input to
\leref{lce:lem:absolute}; it is not reproved from the axioms of surreal games
here. This is the one place where an arbitrary Hahn-field identification
would be insufficient: the identification must be the canonical Conway
normal form. A formal verification should first prove this absoluteness
lemma from that canonical construction, rather than assuming that every
isomorphism chosen in $M$ is absolute.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[An instance for the definable-surreals report \mergetag]
\label{lce:rem:dsnsupport}
\leref{lce:lem:absolute} supplies the absoluteness that the
definable-surreals report leaves unproved in its question on intermediate
support fields (\lbl{dsn:q:support} in
\fn{docs/foundations-and-computation/definable-surreals-and-omnific-integers}).
There, a proper subfield $K\subsetneq\No$ with $\omega\in K$,
$\omega^{-(\alpha+1)}\in K$ for every ordinal $\alpha$, and
$\supp(a)\subseteq K$ for $0\ne a\in K$ (\lbl{dsn:eq:Khyp}) carries an
$\On$-indexed algebraically independent omnific family
(\lbl{dsn:thm:amplify}), and the report records that $\No^M$ for a proper
same-ordinal inner model $M$ ``would satisfy'' these hypotheses if the omega
map and normal forms were absolute, while the universes report proves only
the arithmetic and cuts (\lbl{univ:prop:absolute}). Let $M$ be a transitive
inner class of $\ZFC$ with $\On^M=\On^V$ and $\R^M=\R^V$, and $M\ne V$. Then
$K=\No^M$ satisfies \lbl{dsn:eq:Khyp}: it is a subfield containing the
ordinal $\omega$ by the arithmetic part of the lemma; each
$\omega^{-(\alpha+1)}$ computed in $M$ is the ambient one by the omega-map
part; and the normal form of $a\in\No^M$ computed in $M$ is its ambient
normal form, so its exponents are elements of $\No^M$. It is proper: a sign
sequence of length $\alpha$ codes a subset of $\alpha$, so $\No^M=\No^V$
would put every set of ordinals of $V$ into $M$, and a transitive inner
model of $\ZFC$ containing every set of ordinals is all of $V$. Hence the
support-subfield amplification applies to $\No^M$. The targets $M$ of the
embeddings \eqref{lce:eq:setting} are instances (\leref{lce:cs:lem:critical});
for the ultrapower by a normal measure $U$, $M\ne V$ since $U\notin M$.
Same-ordinal inner models without all reals, such as $L$ when
$\R\not\subseteq L$, and $\mathrm{HOD}$ when it lacks a real, are not
covered: all three sources state the lemma for models with the same reals
(06 through \eqref{lce:cs:eq:standing} and \leref{lce:cs:lem:critical}), and
this report does not claim more.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The elementary-induced surreal embedding \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:prop:J}
The map $J:\No\to\No$, $J(x)=j(x)$ on sign codes, is an ordered-field
embedding that fixes $\R$, and
\[
 J(\omega^a)=\omega^{J(a)},\qquad
 \bd(J(x))=j(\bd(x)).
\]
It preserves and reflects prefix simplicity. It is elementary in the
ordered-field language.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Elementary transfer into $M$ preserves the defining surreal constructions;
\leref{lce:lem:absolute} then identifies their values with the values in $V$.
The all-plus coding of ordinals is definable, so it commutes with $j$.
The domain of $j(x)$ is $j(\dom x)$, proving the birthday formula.
Prefix simplicity is a definable statement about sign functions and is
absolute after transfer. Fixing the reals follows from \eqref{lce:eq:fixedcoeff}.
An embedding between real-closed ordered fields is elementary by
quantifier elimination for $\RCF$. Applied formula by formula, the same
argument works for these class fields: it makes no demand for a class-sized
satisfaction set.
\end{proof}
06 (its Theorem ``The two lift constructions'') and 09 (its Proposition
``The set-induced action'') prove the same statement, and add two clauses:
the range of $J$ is contained in $\No^M$, and $J$ maps $\Oz$ into $\Oz$
(\thref{lce:thm:Oz}). 09 also records here the external normal form
\eqref{lce:eq:Jnormal} of $J(x)$, whose domain is $j(\theta)$, not
$\imset j\theta$, and notes that injectivity follows either from
elementarity of equality or from strict order preservation \src{06, 09}.

\begin{remark}[The first moved birthday \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:rem:firstmoved}
Every surreal of birthday less than $\kappa$ is fixed by $J$: its sign
sequence has a fixed domain of length less than $\kappa$ and fixed values.
The ordinal $\kappa$ itself is moved to $j(\kappa)$. Thus $\kappa$ is also
the least birthday of a surreal moved by $J$. This is a \emph{fixed-point}
statement about $J$, distinct from the \emph{equalizer} statement about $J$
and $H_j$ below.
\end{remark}

\section{The field of short normal forms \src{09, 06}}\label{lce:sg:sec:short}

This section needs no elementary embedding: $\kappa$ is an arbitrary
uncountable cardinal, and $\Sk$ is defined by \eqref{lce:eq:shortfield}.
07 proves that $\Sk$ is real closed through the two embeddings
(\thref{lce:thm:intersection}); 09 proves it directly for every
uncountable $\kappa$, regular or singular. Because $\kappa$ is a cardinal,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:length-card}
 \len(x)<\kappa\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \abs{\supp(x)}<\kappa,
\end{equation}
since an ordinal of cardinality below $\kappa$ is itself below $\kappa$. We
use the ``large monomial'' convention: for a set-sized divisible ordered
subgroup $G\subseteq(\No,+)$, the normal forms supported in $G$ form the Hahn
field $\Hahn G$ with reverse well-ordered supports; this is the
increasing-support convention with $t^g=\omega^{-g}$. The standard Hahn-field
theorem says that $\Hahn G$ is real closed when $G$ is divisible
\cite{Conway,Gonshor,Berarducci}.

\begin{theorem}[Short-support real closed field \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:shortfield}
For every uncountable cardinal $\kappa$, regular or singular, $\Sk$ is a
real closed ordered subfield of $\No$. It contains $\R$, every Conway
monomial and every ordinal. It is closed under normal-form truncation, but
is not a birthday cutoff $\{x:\bd(x)<\lambda\}$ for any ordinal $\lambda$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $S$ be the union of the supports of finitely many elements of $\Sk$.
Then $\abs S<\kappa$. The rational vector space $G=\Span(S)\subseteq\No$ is a
set-sized divisible ordered group, with $\abs G\le\max(\abs S,\aleph_0)<\kappa$.
Every Hahn series supported in $G$ consequently belongs to $\Sk$. The
selected elements lie in the real closed field $\Hahn G$.

The fields so obtained form a directed family: replace two exponent groups
by their rational span to include both. Their union is exactly $\Sk$. This
proves closure under the field operations. Positive square roots and roots
of odd-degree polynomials can be found in one such real closed subfield
containing the finitely many coefficients. Thus the union is real closed.
No union of $\cf(\kappa)$ unrelated supports was taken, so no regularity
assumption has been used.

Real numbers and monomials have support of size at most one. The Cantor
normal form of an ordinal is finite and agrees with its surreal normal form,
so ordinals also belong to $\Sk$. Truncation only deletes support. Finally,
$\Sk$ contains ordinals of arbitrarily large birthday but omits a normal form
of length $\kappa$, for instance $h_\kappa$ of \eqref{lce:eq:heta} (09 uses
the omnific number $Y_\kappa$ of \thref{lce:sg:thm:threshold}). No ordinal
birthday cutoff contains ordinals of arbitrarily large birthday, while the
full class $\No$ also contains the omitted number.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Truncation-closed short fields \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:cor:shortfields}
Let $\kappa$ be the critical point of \eqref{lce:cs:eq:standing}. The class
$\Sk$ is exactly the equalizer of $J$ and $H_j$, and it is a
truncation-closed subfield containing every monomial. The class
$\Skle=\{x\in\No:\abs{\supp(x)}\le\kappa\}$ is also a truncation-closed
subfield.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The equalizer assertion is \thref{lce:thm:defect}, and an equalizer of field
embeddings is a subfield. Direct support counting also proves both field
statements. Unions and pairwise sums of fewer than $\kappa$ support
exponents still have size $<\kappa$. For an inverse write
$x=r\omega^a(1+\eps)$ and use $(1+\eps)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}(-\eps)^n$. Its
support lies in the additive monoid generated by $\supp(\eps)$, of
cardinality at most $\max(\aleph_0,\abs{\supp(\eps)})$. Regularity and
uncountability give the strict bound. The same estimate gives the nonstrict
bound $\kappa$ for $\Skle$. Truncation only removes terms. Every monomial has
one term.
\end{proof}
\mergetag{} The counting proof uses only that $\kappa$ is uncountable: the
product of two cardinals below an infinite $\kappa$ is below $\kappa$, and
the monoid bound needs no regularity. So both field statements hold for
every uncountable cardinal, in agreement with \thref{lce:sg:thm:shortfield};
06 states them at the critical point, where they are needed.

For example, $\omega^a$ belongs to the equalizer no matter how large the
birthday or support of $a$ is. The map need not fix this monomial: it sends
it to $\omega^{J(a)}$. Equality of the two lifts is different from being a
fixed point of either lift \src{06}.

\begin{remark}[The countable boundary \src{09}]
The finite-support collection $\mathscr S_{<\omega}$ is not a field. For
example, $1-\omega^{-1}$ has finite support, but its inverse is the strongly
summable geometric series $\sum_{n<\omega}\omega^{-n}$. This explains the
uncountability hypothesis in \thref{lce:sg:thm:shortfield}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Individual transcendence \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:cor:transcendence}
If $x\in\No\setminus\Sk$, then $x$ is transcendental over $\Sk$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A real closed field has no proper ordered algebraic extension. A surreal
algebraic over $\Sk$ would generate such an extension and must already
belong to $\Sk$.
\end{proof}
The corollary is much weaker than algebraic independence of many specified
long-support numbers. Two transcendental numbers can satisfy a polynomial
relation. Section~\ref{lce:sg:sec:independence} proves the stronger
assertion by an explicit coefficient-separation argument \src{09}.

\begin{proposition}[Order-closed but not an order-dense approximation \src{09}]
\label{lce:sg:prop:closed-support}
For uncountable $\kappa$, the class $\Sk$ is closed and nowhere dense in the
order topology of $\No$. It is not definable, even with finitely many
surreal parameters, in the pure ordered-field language.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $x\notin\Sk$ and its first $\kappa$ normal-form terms
$r_\alpha\omega^{a_\alpha}$. Choose an exponent $b<a_\alpha$ for all
$\alpha<\kappa$, using a cut below this set of exponents. If
$\abs{y-x}<\omega^b$, then every exponent in the support of $y-x$ is at most
$b$, and hence is smaller than every $a_\alpha$. Thus all the first $\kappa$
terms of $x$ remain unchanged in $y$, and $y\notin\Sk$. The complement is
open.

To see that $\Sk$ has no interior, fix a nonzero $z\notin\Sk$. If an
interval about $y\in\Sk$ of radius $\eps>0$ were contained in $\Sk$, choose a
positive ordinal $\beta>\abs z/\eps$. Then $\beta\in\Sk$ and
$0<\abs{z/\beta}<\eps$, but $y+z/\beta\notin\Sk$: otherwise field closure
would imply $z\in\Sk$. This is a contradiction. Closedness and empty
interior give nowhere density.

Quantifier elimination for real closed fields makes every definable
one-variable set a finite union of intervals and points \cite{Marker}. The
class $\Sk$ is infinite and has no interior, so it cannot be such a set.
\end{proof}
The proposition explains why agreement on monomials plus order continuity
does not force the two embeddings to agree everywhere
(\prref{lce:sg:prop:continuity}). Strong summability is a different
extension principle from approximation in the order topology \src{09}.

\begin{proposition}[Short-support integer part \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:prop:integerpart}
For uncountable $\kappa$, the ring $\Ik=\Oz\cap\Sk$ is an integer part of
$\Sk$: it is discretely ordered and, for every $x\in\Sk$, there is $z\in\Ik$
with $z\le x<z+1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $x=p+r+\eps$, where $p$ is its positive-exponent part, $r\in\R$ is its
constant coefficient, and $\eps$ is its negative-exponent part. The last term
is zero or infinitesimal. Put $n=\lfloor r\rfloor$, except that if $r\in\Z$
and $\eps<0$, put $n=r-1$. Then $z=p+n$ has the required inequalities. Its
support is a subset of $\supp(x)$ together with possibly zero, so $z\in\Ik$.
The normal-form description \eqref{lce:eq:Oz} of $\Oz$ shows closure under
the ring operations. A nonzero omnific integer is either an ordinary nonzero
integer or has infinite magnitude, so $1$ is the least positive one.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}[Three unrelated bounds \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:warn:bounds}
The conditions $\bd(x)<\kappa$, $\abs{\supp(x)}<\kappa$, and ``the support of
$x$ is bounded in an exponent group'' are not interchangeable. The
short-support field has no birthday bound and includes arbitrarily large
exponents. In the main independence example
(\thref{lce:sg:thm:AD}), all selected exponent supports lie in the order
interval $(0,1]$, while their cardinality is $\kappa$.
\end{warning}

\section{The strong companion}\label{lce:sec:companion}

\subsection{General Hahn reindexing}
We record the standard mechanism in the form needed here. It does not use
large cardinals.

\begin{lemma}[Reindexing exponents]\label{lce:lem:reindex}
Let $f:(\No,+,<)\to(\No,+,<)$ be an additive order embedding. Then
\[
 T_f\left(\sum_a r_a\omega^a\right)=\sum_a r_a\omega^{f(a)}
\]
defines a strongly $\R$-linear ordered-field embedding of $\No$ into itself.
Its image consists exactly of the surreal numbers whose exponent supports
are contained in $f(\No)$. It preserves and reflects the condition of being
an omnific integer.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An order embedding carries each reverse well-ordered set to a reverse
well-ordered set of the same order type. Thus the displayed normal form
exists and is unique. Coefficientwise addition and scalar multiplication
commute with reindexing. For multiplication, $f(a+b)=f(a)+f(b)$, and
injectivity shows that exactly the same finitely many contributing pairs
occur at corresponding exponents. Hence $T_f(xy)=T_f(x)T_f(y)$. Also
$T_f(1)=1$, so inverses are preserved by the field equation
$T_f(x)T_f(x^{-1})=1$ when $x\ne0$. A nonzero leading coefficient is
unchanged; signs, strict order, and injectivity follow.

For a strongly summable family, the union of its supports and every finite
coefficient fiber are simply reindexed by $f$. This proves strong
$\R$-linearity for arbitrary external set-indexed families.

One inclusion in the image description is immediate. For the other, take a
set support $S\subseteq f(\No)$. Injectivity and class-parameter Replacement
form the set of its unique inverse images. That set is reverse well ordered,
and reindexing its series gives the desired preimage. No assertion is made
that the inverse-image class of an arbitrary class is a set.

An omnific integer has only nonnegative exponents and has integer
coefficient at exponent zero. An additive order embedding fixes zero,
preserves and reflects nonnegativity, and does not change coefficients.
This proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

The preceding proof is the usual Hahn reindexing argument; compare
\cite{vdDE,KKS} and the exponent-embedding results described in the
repository \cite{Repo}. Its role is to produce a canonical comparison map,
not to supply the critical-point obstruction.

\begin{definition}[Strong companion and defect]\label{lce:def:companion}
For the embedding in \eqref{lce:eq:setting}, let $H_j=T_J$ and set
\[
 D_j(x)=J(x)-H_j(x),\qquad \Gam=J(\No).
\]
The subgroup $\Gam$ is being used as an \emph{exponent group} when it
occurs inside a support condition. Write
\[
 \mathcal H(\Gam)=\{y\in\No:\supp(y)\subseteq\Gam\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Existence and uniqueness of the companion \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:thm:companion}
The map $H_j$ is a strongly $\R$-linear ordered-field embedding and
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:Himage}
 H_j(\No)=\mathcal H(\Gam).
\end{equation}
It is the unique strongly $\R$-linear map that agrees with $J$ on every
Conway monomial. The maps $J$ and $H_j$ agree on every real number and on
every ordinal, as well as on every monomial.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \leref{lce:lem:reindex} to the additive order embedding $J$.
For any strongly $\R$-linear map with the stated monomial values, applying
strong linearity to the normal form \eqref{lce:eq:NF} forces
\eqref{lce:eq:Hintro}, proving uniqueness.

Agreement on monomials follows from \prref{lce:prop:J}. Real coefficients are
fixed. Every ordinal has a finite Cantor normal form
$\omega^{\beta_0}n_0+\cdots+\omega^{\beta_{m-1}}n_{m-1}$, which is also its
Conway normal form. Finite additivity therefore gives agreement on every
ordinal, irrespective of its size.
\end{proof}
06 (Theorem ``The two lift constructions'') and 09 (Proposition ``Canonical
strong lift'') prove the same theorem with the same reindexing argument; 09
notes that Replacement for $j$ makes the image of a support a set, and that
the proof is the usual Hahn lifting argument, included to identify exactly
which hypotheses are used. Its novelty is not claimed: the proposed
contribution is its comparison with $J$ \src{06, 09}. Both also note that
$H_j$ maps $\Oz$ into $\Oz$ (\leref{lce:lem:reindex}).

\begin{remark}[A crucial noncommutation]\label{lce:rem:omega}
The formula for the companion is
\[
 H_j(\omega^a)=\omega^{J(a)},
\]
not $\omega^{H_j(a)}$ in general. In the former expression, $J$ acts on an
\emph{exponent}; in $H_j(x)$, it reindexes the outer exponents of $x$.
Failing to distinguish these two levels would incorrectly make the two
embeddings identical.
\end{remark}

\section{The exact normal-form defect}\label{lce:sec:defect}

\subsection{The transformed index set}
Fix a nonzero $x$ with normal form \eqref{lce:eq:NF}. Regard the coefficient
list $r$ and the exponent list $a$ as set functions with domain $\theta$.
Put
\[
 r^*=j(r),\qquad a^*=j(a),\qquad
 P_\theta=j(\theta)\setminus\imset j\theta.
\]
The transformed lists have domain $j(\theta)$. By elementary transfer and
\leref{lce:lem:absolute}, the full \emph{external} normal form of $J(x)$ is
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:Jnormal}
 J(x)=\sum_{\beta<j(\theta)}r^*_\beta\omega^{a^*_\beta}.
\end{equation}
For every $\alpha<\theta$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:oldindices}
 r^*_{j(\alpha)}=r_\alpha,\qquad
 a^*_{j(\alpha)}=J(a_\alpha).
\end{equation}
The extra indices in $P_\theta$ will be called \emph{missing-image indices}.
They are ordinary set indices in $V$, not informal nonstandard positions.

\begin{theorem}[Exact defect and first term \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:thm:defect}
For $x$ as above,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:defectformula}
 D_j(x)=\sum_{\beta\in P_\theta}r^*_\beta\omega^{a^*_\beta}.
\end{equation}
Every exponent occurring on the right lies outside $\Gam=J(\No)$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:exactequalizer}
 D_j(x)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\theta<\kappa
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\abs{\supp(x)}<\kappa.
\end{equation}
If $\theta\ge\kappa$, then
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:firstterm}
 \LT(D_j(x))=r^*_{\kappa}\omega^{a^*_{\kappa}},\qquad
 v(D_j(x))=-a^*_{\kappa}.
\end{equation}
The same statements include $x=0$ by giving it empty support.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The normal form \eqref{lce:eq:Jnormal} is a strongly summable family. Split it
into the subfamilies on $\imset j\theta$ and $P_\theta$. Subfamilies of a
strongly summable family remain strongly summable. By
\eqref{lce:eq:oldindices}, the first subfamily sums to $H_j(x)$.
Subtracting it leaves exactly \eqref{lce:eq:defectformula}.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $a^*_\beta=J(b)$ for some
$\beta\in P_\theta$ and $b\in\No$. Then in $M$,
$j(b)\in\ran(j(a))$. Elementarity gives $b\in\ran(a)$ in $V$, so
$b=a_\alpha$ for some $\alpha<\theta$. But then
$a^*_\beta=J(a_\alpha)=a^*_{j(\alpha)}$. Since $a^*$ is strictly decreasing,
it is injective, and $\beta=j(\alpha)$, a contradiction. Thus all the
missing-image exponents lie outside $\Gam$.

All $r^*_\beta$ are nonzero, and all $a^*_\beta$ are distinct. There is
therefore no cancellation between the terms of \eqref{lce:eq:defectformula}.
The defect vanishes precisely when $P_\theta$ is empty, which by
\leref{lce:lem:index} is precisely when $\theta<\kappa$.
Since $\kappa$ is an initial ordinal, the latter condition is equivalent to
$\abs\theta<\kappa$. The bijection $\alpha\mapsto a_\alpha$ converts it
into the last condition of \eqref{lce:eq:exactequalizer}.

For $\theta\ge\kappa$, the least missing-image index is $\kappa$.
The exponent list is strictly decreasing, so its term at that index is
the leading term of the defect, proving \eqref{lce:eq:firstterm}.
\end{proof}
This is 06's ``missing-index formula'' and ``exact equalizer'' and 09's
``exact normal-form discrepancy'', proved by all three sources by splitting
the transformed normal form into its pointwise-image subseries and the
complementary subseries. 09 adds that the complement of a subset of a
well-ordered ordinal is still well ordered, so both subseries are legitimate
Hahn sums read in the inherited increasing order of the indices. The
exclusion of image exponents is 07's. All three sources stress that every
coefficient of the transformed normal form is nonzero; otherwise a missing
index could disappear and the equalizer conclusion would fail.

\begin{corollary}[Same leading scale, different tails \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:cor:leading}
For every $x\ne0$,
\[
 \LT(J(x))=\LT(H_j(x))=r_0\omega^{J(a_0)},\qquad
 v(J(x))=v(H_j(x))=J(v(x)).
\]
If $D_j(x)\ne0$, its valuation is strictly larger than this common value.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Index zero is fixed and is never a missing-image index. The first missing
index, when present, is later in a strictly decreasing exponent list.
\end{proof}
06 states the same as $D_j(x)\prec H_j(x)$ and $J(x)/H_j(x)\in1+o(1)$,
where $y\prec z$ means $\abs y<c\abs z$ for every positive real $c$; 09 as
\eqref{lce:sg:eq:relative} below. 09 adds a sharper form.

\begin{corollary}[Agreement to all earlier normal-form positions \src{09}]
\label{lce:sg:cor:earlier}
If $\len(x)\ge\kappa$, then $J(x)$ and $H_j(x)$ have the same first
$\kappa$ terms, but are not equal. The discrepancy is strictly dominated by
every monomial occurring at one of those earlier positions:
\[
 \abs{D_j(x)}\prec\omega^{J(a_\alpha)}\qquad(\alpha<\kappa).
\]
For all $x\ne0$, the two images have the same leading term and
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:relative}
 \frac{J(x)}{H_j(x)}=1+\eps_x,
 \qquad \eps_x=0\text{ or }\abs{\eps_x}<1/n\text{ for every }n\ge1.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every index below $\kappa$ is fixed and lies in the pointwise-image
subseries. The first missing exponent lies strictly below all their
exponents. Division by the common leading monomial establishes the stated
Archimedean comparisons. If $x$ has fewer than $\kappa$ terms the images are
equal; otherwise the same argument at index zero proves
\eqref{lce:sg:eq:relative}.
\end{proof}
This separates leading-scale information from exact Hahn information. Even
the relative error is always infinitesimal, while the absolute discrepancy
can itself be an infinite omnific integer (\thref{lce:sg:thm:threshold})
\src{09}. The word ``small'' needs care here: the defect is infinitesimal
\emph{relative to the original value}; it need not itself be an
infinitesimal. Multiplying a bounded witness by a large monomial can make
its defect purely infinite without changing its leading coefficient
\src{06}.

\begin{warning}[Equalizer is not fixed field \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:warn:fixed}
An element of $\Sk$ need not be fixed by either map. For example, $\kappa$
has finite Cantor normal form, so it belongs to $\Sk$ and
$H_j(\kappa)=J(\kappa)=j(\kappa)\ne\kappa$. The equalizer consists of inputs
on which the two procedures agree, not inputs on which they are the
identity.
\end{warning}

\begin{proposition}[The fixed class of the Hahn lift \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:prop:fixed}
For a real or complex normal form $x=\sum_{\alpha<\lambda}r_\alpha\omega^{a_\alpha}$,
\[
 H_j(x)=x\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 J(a_\alpha)=a_\alpha\ \text{for every }\alpha<\lambda.
\]
Thus the fixed class of the Hahn lift consists exactly of the set-supported
series over the fixed exponent group $\Fix(J)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two normal forms have the same length and the same coefficient array.
Equality of their values forces equality of their unique ordered exponent
arrays, giving necessity. Sufficiency is termwise. The fixed exponent group
is an ordered subgroup because $J$ preserves addition and negation.
\end{proof}
The measure codes $h_A$ and $\tilde h_A$ of Section~\ref{lce:sec:measure}
exemplify this observation: their exponents are fixed, even though
transporting the entire code can detect the measure. There is no conflict
with the equalizer theorem. ``Fixed by $H_j$'' and ``sent to the same value
by $J$ and $H_j$'' are different conditions \src{06}. (06 places this
proposition in its surcomplex section; the complex case uses
Section~\ref{lce:sec:complex}.)

\begin{corollary}[Exact omega-map compatibility locus \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:cor:omegalocus}
For every $x\in\No$,
\[
 H_j(\omega^x)=\omega^{H_j(x)}
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in\Sk.
\]
Thus $J$ preserves the omega-map globally but is not strongly additive,
whereas $H_j$ is strongly additive but does not preserve the omega-map
globally.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \prref{lce:prop:J} and the companion formula,
$H_j(\omega^x)=\omega^{J(x)}$. The omega-map is injective, so the desired
equality is equivalent to $J(x)=H_j(x)$. Apply \thref{lce:thm:defect}.
The existence of a long-support witness is established explicitly in
Section~\ref{lce:sec:witness}.
\end{proof}
06 (its Corollary on compatibility with the monomial map) and 09 (the first
part of its Theorem ``Exact omega-equivariance locus of the lift'') prove the
same criterion. 09 adds an incompatibility for the prescribed monomial
action.

\begin{theorem}[Omega-map versus strong additivity \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:omega-locus}
There is no ordered field embedding $E:\No\to\No$ agreeing with $J$ on all
monomials that both commutes with the omega-map everywhere and preserves
every strongly summable family of size $\kappa$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $E$ had the two stated properties, then for every $x$,
\[
 \omega^{E(x)}=E(\omega^x)=J(\omega^x)=\omega^{J(x)},
\]
so $E=J$ by injectivity of the omega-map. The monomial family
$(\tau_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa}$ of \thref{lce:sg:thm:threshold} (or the
family $(\omega^{-\alpha})_{\alpha<\kappa}$ of \thref{lce:thm:witness})
contradicts the asserted summation property.
\end{proof}
This is an incompatibility for a \emph{specified} monomial action. It is not
a theorem that all omega-preserving strongly additive embeddings are
impossible; the identity is an immediate counterexample to that broader
statement \src{09}.

\subsection{Why small support is not small birthday}
Every monomial belongs to $\Sk$, however large its exponent or birthday.
For example, $\omega^b\in\Sk$ for every surreal $b$. This excludes any
interpretation of \eqref{lce:eq:exactequalizer} as agreement only on a
set-sized initial segment of $\No$.
Conversely, the simplest witness below has $\kappa$ outer terms and falls
outside the equalizer even though all its individual monomials are fixed
by $J$. The cardinal bound applies to the number of terms, not to the
size of a single exponent.

The distinction from the classical bounded birthday fields $\No(\lambda)$
is substantial. Van den Dries--Ehrlich analyze those fields and their
restricted analytic and exponential closures \cite[Proposition~4.7 and
Corollary~5.5]{vdDE}. We are not renaming those set-sized fields; $\Sk$ is a
proper class because it contains every monomial.
Section~\ref{lce:sg:sec:short} develops $\Sk$ without the embedding, and
Warning~\ref{lce:sg:warn:bounds} separates it from birthday bounds and from
order-bounded supports \src{09}.

\section{The equalizer field and the exact image intersection}\label{lce:sec:intersection}

\begin{theorem}[Equalizer and common image]\label{lce:thm:intersection}
Let $\Common=J(\Sk)=H_j(\Sk)$. Then
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Eq}(J,H_j)&=\Sk,\label{lce:eq:equalizer}\\
 J(\No)\cap H_j(\No)&=\Common,\label{lce:eq:intersection}\\
 \Common&=\{y\in\No:\abs{\supp(y)}<\kappa
                   \text{ and }\supp(y)\subseteq\Gam\}.\label{lce:eq:Cdescription}
\end{align}
Both $\Sk$ and $\Common$ are real-closed subfields of $\No$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The equalizer assertion is \thref{lce:thm:defect}. Suppose
$y=J(x)\in H_j(\No)$. The image description \eqref{lce:eq:Himage} says that
all exponents in the normal form of $y$ lie in $\Gam$. If $x$ had at
least $\kappa$ normal-form terms, \thref{lce:thm:defect} would exhibit a
nonzero term of $J(x)$ with exponent outside $\Gam$. Hence $x\in\Sk$,
and $y=J(x)=H_j(x)\in\Common$. The opposite inclusion is immediate.
Reindexing preserves the cardinality of support, and the inverse-support
construction in \leref{lce:lem:reindex} proves \eqref{lce:eq:Cdescription}.

The equalizer of two field embeddings is a subfield, so $\Sk$ is a
subfield. To prove real closedness without relying on a separate
bounded-support theorem, let $0\ne p\in\Sk[X]$. If $r_1<\cdots<r_m$ are all
its distinct real roots in $\No$, then $J(r_1)<\cdots<J(r_m)$ and
$H_j(r_1)<\cdots<H_j(r_m)$ are both the full ordered list of real roots
of the same transformed polynomial. Indeed, the images of $\No$ under
these embeddings are real closed, and an ordered extension of a real-closed
field cannot add a new real algebraic element. Each ordered position
therefore agrees: $J(r_i)=H_j(r_i)$. Every such root lies in $\Sk$.

In particular, each positive member of $\Sk$ has its positive square root
in $\Sk$, and each odd-degree polynomial over $\Sk$ has a root there.
This characterizes real closedness. The isomorphic common image
$\Common$ is real closed as well.
\end{proof}
The real closedness of $\Sk$ has a second, embedding-free proof, valid at
every uncountable cardinal: \thref{lce:sg:thm:shortfield} \src{09}.

\begin{remark}[Domain equalizer versus image intersection]\label{lce:rem:twocommon}
The equalizer is $\Sk$, whereas the intersection is its \emph{image}
$\Common$. The two are not being identified. An arbitrary short-support
series can have exponents outside $J(\No)$, and then it is not in
$\Common$. In \eqref{lce:eq:Cdescription}, both the support bound and the
exponent-image condition are essential.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Transcendental separation]\label{lce:cor:transcendence}
Every element of $J(\No)\setminus\Common$ is transcendental over
$H_j(\No)$, and every element of $H_j(\No)\setminus\Common$ is
transcendental over $J(\No)$. In particular, each is transcendental over
$\Common$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both image fields are real closed. An element of one outside their
intersection is not in the other. A real-closed field has no proper
algebraic ordered extension, so that element cannot be algebraic over
the other field.
\end{proof}
The same argument over the domain field is 09's
Corollary~\ref{lce:sg:cor:transcendence}: every surreal outside $\Sk$ is
transcendental over $\Sk$.

\subsection{A canonical isomorphism by erasing nonimage exponents}
Define a coefficient projection on all surreals by
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:projection}
 \Pi_j(y)=\sum_{a\in\supp(y)\cap\Gam}
                  (\coef{a}{y})\,\omega^a.
\end{equation}
This retains a subseries; it is not generally an initial normal-form
truncation. Its support is a set, formed by Separation with the embedding
parameter.

\begin{theorem}[Isomorphic immediate image fields]\label{lce:thm:projection}
The projection $\Pi_j$ is strongly $\R$-linear and idempotent, with image
$H_j(\No)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:projectionJ}
 \Pi_j\circ J=H_j.
\end{equation}
Its restriction is an ordered-field isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:fieldiso}
 \Pi_j\res J(\No):J(\No)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }H_j(\No)
\end{equation}
that fixes $\Common$ pointwise and fixes every Conway monomial in either
image. Both image fields are immediate valued extensions of $\Common$:
they have the same value group $\Gam$ and residue field $\R$.
The projection is not a field homomorphism on all of $\No$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Taking a subseries is compatible with coefficientwise finite sums, and
commutes with an arbitrary strong sum: one either retains or deletes all
contributions at each exponent. This proves strong $\R$-linearity.
The image and idempotence follow from \eqref{lce:eq:Himage}.
The defect theorem states that $J(x)$ consists of $H_j(x)$ supported on
$\Gam$, plus a series supported outside $\Gam$. Therefore
$\Pi_j(J(x))=H_j(x)$.

The maps $J$ and $H_j$ are ordered-field isomorphisms onto their respective
images. Hence \eqref{lce:eq:projectionJ} identifies the restriction in
\eqref{lce:eq:fieldiso} with $H_j\circ J^{-1}$, which is an ordered-field
isomorphism. It fixes every element of the intersection, since those
supports are contained in $\Gam$. The monomials in either image are
exactly $\{\omega^{J(a)}:a\in\No\}$, and they already lie in $\Common$.
For the assertion about the monomials of $J(\No)$, a monomial image must
have normal-form length one and leading coefficient one, so its preimage
also does.

Every nonzero member of either image has valuation in $\Gam$, and the
monomials just listed realize every value in $\Gam$. They belong to
$\Common$, so its value group is the same. All three fields contain the
constant copy of $\R$ and have residue field $\R$. Equality of these
value groups and residue fields is precisely immediacy; the value groups
here are allowed to be classes.

Finally choose $a\notin\Gam$, for instance $a=\kappa$. Then also
$-a\notin\Gam$, and
\[
 \Pi_j(\omega^a)=\Pi_j(\omega^{-a})=0,
 \qquad \Pi_j(\omega^a\omega^{-a})=\Pi_j(1)=1.
\]
Thus global multiplicativity fails. The fact that multiplicativity holds
on $J(\No)$ is a genuine restriction on the tails of that image field.
\end{proof}

This gives transcendence, not algebraic independence between the two image
fields. Exact intersection alone does not establish linear disjointness;
that stronger question is left open in Section~\ref{lce:sec:questions}.

\section{Explicit witnesses and incomparability}\label{lce:sec:witness}

For every ordinal $\eta$, define the genuine strong sum
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:heta}
 h_\eta=\sum_{\alpha<\eta}\omega^{-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
The set $\{-\alpha:\alpha<\eta\}$ is strictly decreasing in its displayed
order, and each exponent occurs once, so this is a legitimate normal form.
We always use surreal negation and subtraction in exponents; they are not
ordinal subtraction.

\begin{theorem}[The basic witness]\label{lce:thm:witness}
For $h_\kappa$ one has
\begin{align}
 H_j(h_\kappa)&=h_\kappa,\label{lce:eq:Hh}\\
 J(h_\kappa)&=h_{j(\kappa)},\label{lce:eq:Jh}\\
 D_j(h_\kappa)&=
  \sum_{\kappa\le\beta<j(\kappa)}\omega^{-\beta}
  =\omega^{-\kappa}(1+\eps),\qquad 0<\eps\in\infideal.
  \label{lce:eq:Dh}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\[
 h_\kappa\in H_j(\No)\setminus J(\No),\qquad
 h_{j(\kappa)}\in J(\No)\setminus H_j(\No).
\]
Thus $J(\No)$ and $H_j(\No)$ are incomparable real-closed subfields of
$\No$, although the embeddings agree on all reals, ordinals, and monomials.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $\alpha<\kappa$ is fixed, so $J(-\alpha)=-\alpha$, and
\eqref{lce:eq:Hh} follows. The definition of the whole list in
\eqref{lce:eq:heta} transfers to the list of length $j(\kappa)$, giving
\eqref{lce:eq:Jh}. Their difference is \eqref{lce:eq:Dh}. Factoring out its first
monomial leaves 1 plus a nonempty series with strictly negative exponents
and positive coefficients. That remainder is a positive infinitesimal.
It is nonempty because $j(\kappa)$ is a limit cardinal strictly above
$\kappa$.

If $h_\kappa=J(x)$, then the length of its normal form is
$j(\len(x))=\kappa$, contradicting that $\kappa$ is not in the ordinal
range of $j$. Hence $h_\kappa$ is not in $J(\No)$.
On the other hand, the normal form of $h_{j(\kappa)}$ contains the exponent
$-\kappa$. This exponent is not $J(b)$ for any surreal $b$: if it were,
$J(-b)=\kappa$, and elementarity would make $-b$ an ordinal, again
contradicting the missing-index fact. The image description for $H_j$
therefore excludes $h_{j(\kappa)}$ from its image.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A short-support element outside the common image]
By \prref{lce:prop:J}, $J(\kappa)=j(\kappa)>\kappa$. Consequently
$\omega^{-\kappa}$ is not in $\Common$: its exponent is outside $\Gam$.
This is a one-term surreal, so it nevertheless belongs to $\Sk$.
It concretely illustrates Remark~\ref{lce:rem:twocommon}. The two assertions
``$x$ has short support'' and ``$x$ lies in the common image'' really are
different assertions.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Not a geometric series \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:rem:geometric}
The expression $h_\lambda$ is a transfinite Hahn sum. It must not be
simplified as though every ordinal exponent were a natural multiple of $1$.
In particular the ordinary geometric-series identity for
$\sum_{n<\omega}\omega^{-n}$ does not identify $h_\kappa$ with that
countable sum. Surreal addition of ordinal exponents is not ordinary
ordinal addition.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Both maps are proper \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:prop:proper}
Both $J$ and $H_j$ are proper embeddings of $\No^V$ into itself.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The normal-form length of $J(x)$ is $j(\len(x))$. No ordinal has image
$\kappa$, by \leref{lce:lem:index}; hence $h_\kappa$ is not in the range of
$J$. Every exponent in the support of an $H_j$-image belongs to $J(\No)$.
The monomial $\omega^{h_\kappa}$ therefore cannot be in the range of $H_j$.
More explicitly, injectivity on exponent supports would force a preimage to
have one term, with exponent $a$ satisfying $J(a)=h_\kappa$, which is
impossible.
\end{proof}
The first half is contained in \thref{lce:thm:witness}, whose second half
uses the witness $h_{j(\kappa)}$ instead of $\omega^{h_\kappa}$. Thus $J$,
although preserving the monomial map and exponentiation, is not an
automorphism. These results do not answer the automorphism classification
in Question~5.6 of \cite{KKS}. Nor does an elementary embedding of sets into
a proper inner model contradict a theorem excluding a nontrivial elementary
embedding of the universe into itself \src{06}.

\section{The exact strong-summation threshold}\label{lce:sec:positive}

The previous results compare the images of a \emph{single normal form}.
There is a separate, equally sharp statement about an arbitrary family
whose individual members may themselves have very long supports.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-family threshold \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:thm:positive}
Let $F:I\to\No$ be an external set-indexed strongly summable family.
Then $(J(F(i)))_{i\in I}$ is strongly summable. If $\abs I<\kappa$, then
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:smallfamily}
 J\left(\sum_{i\in I}F(i)\right)=\sum_{i\in I}J(F(i)).
\end{equation}
If in addition $F(i)>0$ for every $i\in I$, then
\eqref{lce:eq:smallfamily} holds \emph{if and only if} $\abs I<\kappa$.
For $\abs I\ge\kappa$ in this positive case,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:positivegap}
 J\left(\sum_{i\in I}F(i)\right)-\sum_{i\in I}J(F(i))>0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The family $j(F)$ is strongly summable over $j(I)$ in $M$, and hence
externally in $V$ by \leref{lce:lem:absolute}. Its sum is
$J(\sum_{i\in I}F(i))$. Restrict it to the old-index part $\imset jI$:
its values there are $j(F)(j(i))=J(F(i))$. This proves external
summability of the termwise image family. Splitting the full family yields
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:familydefect}
 J\left(\sum_{i\in I}F(i)\right)-\sum_{i\in I}J(F(i))
    =\sum_{u\in j(I)\setminus\imset jI}j(F)(u).
\end{equation}
If $\abs I<\kappa$, the right side is empty by \leref{lce:lem:index}.

Suppose now that every $F(i)>0$. Transfer gives $j(F)(u)>0$ for all
$u\in j(I)$. A nonempty strongly summable family of positive surreals has
positive sum: take the largest exponent in the union of its supports.
Every term contributing at that exponent has it as a leading exponent,
so its coefficient is positive; only finitely many contribute, and their
sum is positive. When $\abs I\ge\kappa$, the missing-index part is
nonempty by \leref{lce:lem:index}. Applying this observation to
\eqref{lce:eq:familydefect} proves \eqref{lce:eq:positivegap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the positivity hypothesis is necessary]
Let $I=\kappa\times\{0,1\}$ and put
$F(\alpha,0)=\omega^{-\alpha}$,
$F(\alpha,1)=-\omega^{-\alpha}$. This is strongly summable and has sum
zero. The termwise image also sums to zero, so preservation holds even
though $\abs I=\kappa$. One cannot replace the positive-family conclusion
by an unconditional cardinality criterion for signed families.
The exact criterion in general is that the missing-index sum in
\eqref{lce:eq:familydefect} vanish.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[First failure cardinal \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:cor:firstfailure}
The least cardinality of a positive strongly summable family on which $J$
fails to commute with strong summation is exactly $\kappa$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All smaller families are preserved, and the monomial family defining
$h_\kappa$ is a positive witness of size $\kappa$.
\end{proof}
06 (its strong-additivity threshold) and 09 (the first part of its ``Exact
threshold'') prove the small-family half by enumerating the family by an
ordinal $\mu<\kappa$: the transported family has domain $j(\mu)=\mu$ and
term $J(x_\alpha)$ at each old index, and \leref{lce:lem:absolute} turns the
internal identity into the external one. There is no restriction on the
support of any individual member. Both give the witness $h_\kappa$ (06's
$u_\kappa$, 09's $Q_\kappa$): the coefficient at exponent $-\kappa$ is one in
$J(h_\kappa)$ and zero in $\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}J(\omega^{-\alpha})$.

There is no conflict between this theorem and the defect of an individual
long-support $x$. A singleton family $\{x\}$ is always preserved by $J$.
Its normal-form decomposition may have $\kappa$ or more terms, and that
is the different family on which $J$ and $H_j$ disagree.

\subsection{An omnific monomial witness \src{09}}
For every ordinal $\alpha$ put
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:scales}
 q_\alpha=\omega^{-\alpha},\qquad
 \tau_\alpha=\omega^{q_\alpha}=\omega^{\omega^{-\alpha}}.
\end{equation}
Both $(q_\alpha)$ and $(\tau_\alpha)$ are strictly decreasing monomial
families on every ordinal domain. Each $q_\alpha$ is positive, so every
$\tau_\alpha$ is larger than all positive real numbers. In particular,
$\tau_\alpha\in\Oz$. For an ordinal $\theta$, define
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:QZ}
 Y_\theta=\sum_{\alpha<\theta}\tau_\alpha.
\end{equation}
It is a strong sum of length $\theta$ and is purely infinite whenever
$\theta>0$. Here ``purely infinite'' means that its normal form has only
positive exponents, not that its sign must be positive for every possible
choice of coefficients; $Y_\theta$ is positive because all its coefficients
equal one.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit threshold witnesses \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:threshold}
The map $J$ does not preserve all strongly summable families of cardinality
$\kappa$. More explicitly,
\begin{align}
 J(h_\kappa)&=h_{j(\kappa)},&
 H_j(h_\kappa)&=h_\kappa,
 &D_j(h_\kappa)&=\sum_{\kappa\le\beta<j(\kappa)}\omega^{-\beta},
 \label{lce:sg:eq:Qdefect}\\
 J(Y_\kappa)&=Y_{j(\kappa)},&
 H_j(Y_\kappa)&=Y_\kappa,
 &D_j(Y_\kappa)&=\sum_{\kappa\le\beta<j(\kappa)}\tau_\beta.
 \label{lce:sg:eq:Zdefect}
\end{align}
The leading discrepancies are respectively $\omega^{-\kappa}$ and
$\tau_\kappa=\omega^{\omega^{-\kappa}}$. Every individual summand in the
original $\kappa$-families is fixed by $J$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The constructions \eqref{lce:sg:eq:scales} and \eqref{lce:sg:eq:QZ} are
uniform definable ordinal-indexed constructions. Hence
$J(h_\kappa)=h_{j(\kappa)}$ and $J(Y_\kappa)=Y_{j(\kappa)}$. For each
$\alpha<\kappa$ the ordinal $\alpha$ is fixed, and the omega identity gives
$J(q_\alpha)=q_\alpha$ and $J(\tau_\alpha)=\tau_\alpha$. The strong lift
therefore fixes both original sums. Subtraction leaves exactly the
indicated tails, whose first indices are $\kappa$.
\end{proof}
The row \eqref{lce:sg:eq:Qdefect} is 07's \thref{lce:thm:witness}. Its
discrepancy is infinitesimal even in absolute magnitude. The discrepancy in
\eqref{lce:sg:eq:Zdefect} is instead an infinite omnific integer. It is still
infinitesimal \emph{relative} to the common leading term $\tau_0=\omega$.
This supplies two different geometric manifestations of the same
missing-index mechanism.

For a cardinal $\lambda$, let ``$<\lambda$-strong'' mean preservation of
every strongly summable family indexed by a set of size less than
$\lambda$. \thref{lce:thm:positive} and \thref{lce:sg:thm:threshold} state
that $J$ is $<\kappa$-strong and is not $<\lambda$-strong for any cardinal
$\lambda>\kappa$. They do not claim that every individual family of size
$\kappa$ fails; many do not \src{09} (compare the signed family of the
remark after \thref{lce:thm:positive}).

\begin{proposition}[Order continuity does not enforce strong additivity \src{09}]
\label{lce:sg:prop:continuity}
Both $J$ and $H_j$ are continuous for the order topology on $\No$.
Nevertheless, $J$ is not strongly additive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The failure of strong additivity is \thref{lce:sg:thm:threshold}. For
continuity at zero, take any $\delta>0$ and choose a positive ordinal
$\beta>1/\delta$. Elementary embeddings are nondecreasing on ordinal
magnitudes: $j(\beta)\ge\beta$, as follows by induction from order
preservation. The ordinal $\beta$ has finite normal form, so
$H_j(\beta)=J(\beta)=j(\beta)$. For either map $E\in\{J,H_j\}$ and
$\abs x<1/\beta$, therefore,
\[
 \abs{E(x)}<1/E(\beta)\le1/\beta<\delta.
\]
This proves continuity at zero; translation gives continuity everywhere.
This is an interval-based statement about a class order, not an assertion
that its topology is a set of open sets.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An intrinsic closure theorem for the image}
The threshold can be stated without first specifying a family in the
domain of $J$. This strengthens the noncommutation result to an actual
failure of membership in the image.

\begin{theorem}[Strong-sum closure and positive escape]\label{lce:thm:escape}
Let $(y_i)_{i\in I}$ be an external strongly summable family in $J(\No)$,
and let $x_i$ be the unique preimage with $J(x_i)=y_i$. Then $(x_i)$ is
strongly summable and
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:membershipsum}
 \sum_{i\in I}y_i\in J(\No)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{i\in I}y_i=J\left(\sum_{i\in I}x_i\right).
\end{equation}
In particular, $J(\No)$ is closed under all external strongly summable
families of cardinality less than $\kappa$. If every $y_i>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:positiveescape}
 \sum_{i\in I}y_i\in J(\No)
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\abs I<\kappa.
\end{equation}
In contrast, $H_j(\No)$ is closed under every external set-indexed strong
sum of its members.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Replacement with the embedding parameter gives the set-indexed preimage
family. For every $a$, the normal-form defect theorem implies
\[
 \coef{J(a)}{y_i}=\coef{a}{x_i}.
\]
Thus $\imset J{\bigcup_i\supp(x_i)}$ is a subset of the reverse
well-ordered union $\bigcup_i\supp(y_i)$. Pulling this suborder back by
the order embedding $J$ proves reverse well ordering of
$\bigcup_i\supp(x_i)$. An exponent $a$ occurs in $x_i$ exactly when
$J(a)$ occurs in $y_i$, so the coefficient fibers are finite as well.
Hence the preimage family is strongly summable.

Let $x=\sum_i x_i$ and $y=\sum_i y_i$. At every exponent $J(a)$ the
finite coefficient sums give
\[
 \coef{J(a)}{y}=\sum_i\coef{a}{x_i}
        =\coef{a}{x}=\coef{J(a)}{J(x)}.
\]
Therefore $J(x)-y$ has support disjoint from $\Gam$.
But every nonzero element of $J(\No)$ has its leading exponent in
$\Gam$, by Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:leading}. If $y\in J(\No)$, the
difference $J(x)-y$ is in that field and must consequently be zero.
This proves the forward implication in \eqref{lce:eq:membershipsum}; the
reverse implication is immediate.

For $\abs I<\kappa$, Theorem~\ref{lce:thm:positive} gives equality, without
requiring positive terms. If all $y_i>0$, then all $x_i>0$, and that
theorem makes equality equivalent to $\abs I<\kappa$. Finally, a strong
sum of series supported on $\Gam$ is again supported on $\Gam$;
\eqref{lce:eq:Himage} proves the closure assertion for $H_j(\No)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Descent of the companion to the target model \src{06}}
\label{lce:cs:sec:descent}

The value $H_j(x)$ always exists in the ambient field. The question of this
section, which is 06's, is whether it exists in the target model. In a
set-ultrapower the answer measures considerably more than the size of an
arithmetic error: it detects closure under external sequences. In this
section $\lambda$ denotes the normal-form length of $x$, and by
\eqref{lce:eq:Jnormal} and \eqref{lce:eq:oldindices},
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:H-nf}
 H_j(x)=\sum_{\alpha<\lambda}r_\alpha\omega^{J(a_\alpha)}
       =\sum_{\beta\in\imset j\lambda}j(r)(\beta)\,\omega^{j(a)(\beta)},
\end{equation}
the latter sum read in increasing order of the index $\beta$. It exists
externally because $j$ preserves the exponent ordering. Only $J$ is
\emph{a priori} valued in $\No^M$.

\subsection{Landing is equivalent to the seed}

\begin{theorem}[Universal landing criterion \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:thm:landing}
Let $x$ have normal-form length $\lambda$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:landing}
 H_j(x)\in\No^M\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\imset j\lambda\in M.
\end{equation}
In particular the answer depends only on the cardinality of the support,
not on the actual coefficients or exponents. At a fixed support cardinality
there are no exceptional normal forms that land when others do not.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $s=\imset j\lambda$ belongs to $M$. The transported
arrays $j(r)$ and $j(a)$ are in $M$. Restrict them to $s$ inside $M$, using
its increasing enumeration to obtain an ordinal-indexed normal form. The
value of this normal form belongs to $\No^M$ and, by
\leref{lce:lem:absolute}, equals the external expression
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:H-nf}. It is $H_j(x)$.

Conversely suppose $y=H_j(x)$ belongs to $\No^M$. Its normal form computed
in $M$ is its external normal form. In particular its exponent support $S$
belongs to $M$. The array $j(a)$ belongs to $M$ and is injective on
$j(\lambda)$. By Separation in $M$, the set
\[
 t=\{\beta<j(\lambda):j(a)(\beta)\in S\}
\]
belongs to $M$. Externally, the support formula \eqref{lce:cs:eq:H-nf} and
injectivity of $j(a)$ identify $t$ exactly with $\imset j\lambda$. This proves
the converse, including the empty support case.

Finally, if $f:\mu\to\lambda$ is a bijection, then $j(f)$ maps $\imset j\mu$
bijectively onto $\imset j\lambda$. Each seed is obtained from the other using
$j(f)$ or its inverse, both belonging to $M$. Thus seed membership, and hence
landing, is unchanged when the support length is replaced by its
cardinality.
\end{proof}

The forward implication is stronger than a statement about canonical
$0$--$1$ series alone. Even an elaborate normal form with arbitrary nonzero
real coefficients cannot conceal a missing seed. Its exponents, compared
with the transported original exponent array, recover that seed exactly
\src{06}.

\begin{corollary}[Landing spectrum \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:cor:spectrum}
Define the class of cardinals
\[
 \mathcal L(j)=\{\mu:\imset j\mu\in M\}.
\]
It is downward closed and contains every cardinal $\mu\le\kappa$. Moreover
\[
 H_j^{-1}(\No^M)=\{x:\abs{\supp(x)}\in\mathcal L(j)\}
\]
is a truncation-closed subfield of $\No^V$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For ordinals $\mu\le\lambda$, $\imset j\mu=\imset j\lambda\cap j(\mu)$, giving
downward closure. Below $\kappa$ the seed equals the ordinal, and
$\imset j\kappa=\kappa$, so all these seeds belong to $M$. The displayed
equality follows from \thref{lce:cs:thm:landing}. The inverse image of a
subfield under a field embedding is a subfield. Truncation decreases the
support cardinality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why the seed measures sequence closure in an ultrapower}
The next lemma is a standard ultrapower fact, included with its proof
because it explains precisely what is being imported from set theory. It is
stated as Fact~7 in \cite{Williams}.

\begin{lemma}[Seed--closure equivalence \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:lem:seed-closure}
Suppose $j:V\to M$ is the well-founded ultrapower by a countably complete
ultrafilter on a set $I$. For any ordinal $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:seed-closure}
 \imset j\lambda\in M\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 {}^\lambda M\cap V\subseteq M.
\end{equation}
Here the right side means that every ambient set function with domain
$\lambda$ and values in $M$ belongs to $M$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $M$ is closed under such sequences, the sequence
$\langle j(\alpha):\alpha<\lambda\rangle$ belongs to $M$, so its range
$\imset j\lambda$ does too. This direction does not require the ultrapower
hypothesis.

For the other direction, let $s=\imset j\lambda\in M$ and take an ambient
sequence $\langle x_\alpha:\alpha<\lambda\rangle$ of elements of $M$. Choose
representatives $f_\alpha:I\to V$ with $x_\alpha=[f_\alpha]_U$, where
brackets include the transitive collapse. This choice is legitimate for a
set of indices: choose bounded-rank representatives first and then apply
ordinary Choice. Pack them into a single set function $F$ with
$F(\alpha,i)=f_\alpha(i)$. Let $d=[\mathrm{id}_I]_U\in M$ be the usual
ultrapower seed for $I$. The evaluation identity for ultrapowers gives
\[
 j(F)(j(\alpha),d)=x_\alpha.
\]
The increasing enumeration $e:\lambda\to s$ belongs to $M$; its order type
is the actual ordinal $\lambda$, by absoluteness of well-orders. Inside $M$
form
\[
 \alpha\longmapsto j(F)(e(\alpha),d).
\]
This is exactly the original ambient sequence. Hence it belongs to $M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One number tests all sequences \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:cor:one-number}
For a set-ultrapower embedding and a surreal $x$ with support cardinal
$\mu$,
\[
 H_j(x)\in\No^M\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad{}^\mu M\cap V\subseteq M.
\]
Thus descent of one such normal form implies descent of every surreal whose
support has at most $\mu$ terms.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Combine \thref{lce:cs:thm:landing}, \leref{lce:cs:lem:seed-closure} and
Corollary~\ref{lce:cs:cor:spectrum}.
\end{proof}
The word ``one'' refers to a test relative to the specified embedding and
its target. It does not mean that a single pure-field formula with no
access to $j$, $M$, or normal-form data defines supercompactness \src{06}.

\subsection{Normal measures: the exact three-way classification}

\begin{lemma}[Cover obstruction \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:lem:cover-obstruction}
Let $j:V\to M$ be a set-ultrapower on an index set $I$ with critical point
$\kappa$. Let $\lambda\ge\kappa$ be an ambient cardinal. Suppose that
$s\in M$ satisfies
\[
 \imset j\lambda\subseteq s\subseteq j(\lambda),
 \qquad M\models\abs s<j(\kappa).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:index-obstruction}
 \lambda\le\abs I\cdot\kappa.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Represent $s$ by a function $f:I\to V$. By the ultrapower theorem, the
assertion $s\in j(P_\kappa(\lambda))$ implies that $f(i)\in P_\kappa(\lambda)$
on a measure-one set. Change $f$ to the empty set outside that set. For every
$\alpha<\lambda$, membership $j(\alpha)\in s$ says
\[
 \{i\in I:\alpha\in f(i)\}\in U.
\]
In particular this set is nonempty. Consequently
$\lambda=\bigcup_{i\in I}f(i)$. Each $f(i)$ has cardinality $<\kappa$, so the
cardinality of the union is at most $\abs I\cdot\kappa$.
\end{proof}
This elementary counting argument is useful because it does not confuse an
internal support bound with an external one. An internally short cover can
be externally large, but not too large for its representing index set
\src{06}.

\begin{lemma}[The image of the critical point is large \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:lem:j-kappa-large}
Under \eqref{lce:cs:eq:standing}, $j(\kappa)>(2^\kappa)^V$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The ordinal $j(\kappa)$ is a strong-limit cardinal in $M$ by
\leref{lce:cs:lem:critical} and elementarity. Let $\delta=(2^\kappa)^M$. The
set $\Pow(\kappa)$ is the same in both models by
\leref{lce:cs:lem:critical}, so a bijection in $M$ from $\delta$ onto that
power set is an actual bijection in $V$. Thus $\delta\ge(2^\kappa)^V$ as ordinals.
Since $M$ regards $\kappa<j(\kappa)$ and $j(\kappa)$ as strong limit, it has
$\delta<j(\kappa)$. Combine these inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Normal-measure trichotomy \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:thm:normal-trichotomy}
Suppose $U$ is a normal nonprincipal $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter on
$\kappa$ and $j:V\to M=\Ult(V,U)$ is its transitive ultrapower. For every
surreal $x$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:normal-landing}
 H_j(x)\in\No^M\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\abs{\supp(x)}\le\kappa.
\end{equation}
More precisely:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Support cardinal & Comparison & Descent of $H_j(x)$\\
\midrule
$<\kappa$ & $J(x)=H_j(x)$ & belongs to $\No^M$\\
$=\kappa$ & $J(x)\ne H_j(x)$ & belongs to $\No^M$\\
$>\kappa$ & $J(x)\ne H_j(x)$ & does not belong to $\No^M$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In all three rows $J(x)$ belongs to $\No^M$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{lce:cs:cor:spectrum}, every cardinal at most $\kappa$
belongs to the landing spectrum. We prove that the ambient successor
$\kappa^+$ does not. Suppose to the contrary that $s=\imset j{\kappa^+}\in M$.
As an ordered set of ordinals, $s$ has order type $\kappa^+$, and this order
type is absolute to $M$. By \leref{lce:cs:lem:j-kappa-large},
\[
 \kappa^+\le(2^\kappa)^V<j(\kappa).
\]
The increasing enumeration therefore witnesses in $M$ that $s$ has
cardinality $<j(\kappa)$. Apply \leref{lce:cs:lem:cover-obstruction} with
$I=\kappa$ and $\lambda=\kappa^+$. It gives $\kappa^+\le\kappa\cdot\kappa=\kappa$,
a contradiction.

Downward closure now excludes every larger cardinal. The universal landing
criterion proves \eqref{lce:cs:eq:normal-landing}, and the exact equalizer
theorem gives the three rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Slightly stronger scope \src{06}]
Normality is not needed in the counting proof of
\thref{lce:cs:thm:normal-trichotomy}. The same conclusion holds for a
well-founded ultrapower on an index set of cardinality $\kappa$ with
critical point $\kappa$. The normal-measure formulation is retained because
it also identifies the recovered measure in Section~\ref{lce:sec:measure}
with the original ultrafilter.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Landing domain versus equalizer \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:cor:landing-domain}
For a normal-measure ultrapower on $\kappa$, the maximal domain on which the
Hahn lift lands in the target is exactly $\Skle$, whereas the equalizer of
the two lifts is exactly $\Sk$. The target is closed under ambient
$\kappa$-sequences but not under all ambient $\kappa^+$-sequences.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use \thref{lce:cs:thm:normal-trichotomy}, \thref{lce:thm:defect} and
\leref{lce:cs:lem:seed-closure}.
\end{proof}
No assertion that $j(\kappa)$ is inaccessible in $V$ was used. It is
inaccessible in $M$, and that is exactly the property needed for the
internal support bound. These two assertions should not be interchanged
\src{06}.

\section{Recovering a normal measure from one coefficient}\label{lce:sec:measure}

\subsection{Boolean masks at ordinal exponents}
For each $A\subseteq\kappa$, define
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:maskA}
 h_A=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\omega^{-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
The notation is unambiguous: the index set is ordered by the ordinal order,
and zeros are omitted from the full $\kappa$-list. In particular,
$h_\varnothing=0$ and $h_\kappa$ agrees with \eqref{lce:eq:heta}.

\begin{theorem}[The measure-coefficient formula]\label{lce:thm:measure}
For every $A\subseteq\kappa$,
\begin{align}
 H_j(h_A)&=h_A,\label{lce:eq:maskfixed}\\
 J(h_A)&=\sum_{\beta\in j(A)}\omega^{-\beta},\label{lce:eq:maskJ}\\
 \coef{-\kappa}{D_j(h_A)}&=\mathbf 1_{\{\kappa\in j(A)\}}.
 \label{lce:eq:measurecoef}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:derivedU}
 U_j=\set{A\subseteq\kappa:\coef{-\kappa}{D_j(h_A)}=1}
\end{equation}
is the nonprincipal normal $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter on $\kappa$
derived from $j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original exponents $-\alpha$, $\alpha<\kappa$, are fixed by $J$, so
\eqref{lce:eq:maskfixed} follows from the companion formula. Transferring the
whole mask construction gives \eqref{lce:eq:maskJ}. Since $\kappa$ is not an
original index, $h_A$ has no coefficient at $\omega^{-\kappa}$.
The coefficient in $J(h_A)$ is 1 precisely when $\kappa\in j(A)$.
This proves \eqref{lce:eq:measurecoef} and identifies \eqref{lce:eq:derivedU}
with the classical derived measure.

For completeness, its properties can be read directly from this last
identification. Since $\kappa\in j(\kappa)$, the whole space is in $U_j$
and the empty set is not. Elementary transfer of complements and finite
intersections makes it an ultrafilter. For $\alpha<\kappa$,
$j(\{\alpha\})=\{\alpha\}$ does not contain $\kappa$, so it is
nonprincipal. If $\mu<\kappa$ and $A_\xi\in U_j$ for $\xi<\mu$, then
$j(\mu)=\mu$ and
\[
 \kappa\in\bigcap_{\xi<\mu}j(A_\xi)
       =j\left(\bigcap_{\xi<\mu}A_\xi\right),
\]
which proves $\kappa$-completeness.

Finally, suppose $A\in U_j$ and $f:A\to\kappa$ is regressive, meaning
$f(\alpha)<\alpha$ for all nonzero $\alpha\in A$. Then
$j(f)(\kappa)<\kappa$, so $j(f)(\kappa)=\eta=j(\eta)$ for some
$\eta<\kappa$. Therefore
\[
 \kappa\in j\bigl(\{\alpha\in A:f(\alpha)=\eta\}\bigr).
\]
The indicated fiber is in $U_j$, proving normality.
\end{proof}

The ultrafilter properties just proved are standard properties of derived
measures \cite{Neeman}. The new information is their simultaneous realization
by \eqref{lce:eq:measurecoef} within one explicitly given surreal coefficient
space. No real-valued probability measure on all surreal sets is being
introduced.

All three sources recover the derived normal measure from one coefficient,
with the same proof of its ultrafilter, completeness and normality
properties (06 in \leref{lce:cs:lem:critical} and its recovery theorem, 09
in its ``Normal-measure coefficient formula''). They use three different
codes. 07's $h_A$ has coefficients $0,1$ and is bounded. 06's code has
coefficients $1,2$, so that its support has exactly $\kappa$ terms for
every $A$; 09's code is omnific from the start. We print the two further
codes with the computations that differ.

\begin{theorem}[A bounded code of constant length \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:thm:recover}
For $A\subseteq\kappa$ put
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:cA}
 \tilde h_A=h_\kappa+h_A=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}(1+\mathbf 1_A(\alpha))\,\omega^{-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Every coefficient is one or two, so the support has exactly $\kappa$ terms,
independently of $A$, and $1<\tilde h_A<3$. For any embedding satisfying
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:standing}, $U_j$ is a normal measure on $\kappa$,
$H_j(\tilde h_A)=\tilde h_A$, and
\begin{align}
 \coef{-\kappa}{D_j(\tilde h_A)}&=1+\mathbf 1_{U_j}(A),\label{lce:cs:eq:measure-coeff}\\
 \st\bigl(\omega^\kappa D_j(\tilde h_A)\bigr)&=1+\mathbf 1_{U_j}(A),\label{lce:cs:eq:measure-st}
\end{align}
where $\st$ is the standard part of a finite surreal. If $j$ is the
ultrapower by a normal measure $U$ on $\kappa$, then $U_j=U$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The bounds hold because the tail after the constant term is a nonzero
positive infinitesimal and the constant term is at most two. The measure
properties are those proved in \thref{lce:thm:measure} (06 proves them in
\leref{lce:cs:lem:critical} and by the same regressive-function argument).
All exponents $-\alpha$ with $\alpha<\kappa$ and all coefficients in
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:cA} are fixed by $J$, so its Hahn lift is $\tilde h_A$
itself. The transported whole code has normal form
\[
 J(\tilde h_A)=\sum_{\beta<j(\kappa)}(1+\mathbf 1_{j(A)}(\beta))\,\omega^{-\beta}.
\]
The first $\kappa$ terms are exactly $\tilde h_A$. Its first remaining
coefficient, at $-\kappa$, is two if $\kappa\in j(A)$ and one otherwise.
This proves \eqref{lce:cs:eq:measure-coeff}. Multiplication by
$\omega^\kappa$ shifts that exponent to zero; all later exponents become
negative. The standard part therefore extracts the same coefficient,
proving \eqref{lce:cs:eq:measure-st}.

For a normal ultrapower on $\kappa$, the class of the identity function is
the ordinal $\kappa$. Indeed every smaller ultrapower ordinal has a
regressive representative on a large set and hence is constant modulo $U$,
whereas every constant below $\kappa$ is smaller than $[\mathrm{id}]_U$.
Thus $\kappa\in j(A)$ if and only if $A\in U$, which gives $U_j=U$.
\end{proof}
The same seed calculation is the step ``the measure derived at the seed
$\kappa$ is the original measure'' in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:distinctU}; 09 cites it from \cite{Holland}.

\begin{theorem}[An omnific code \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:measure}
For $A\subseteq\kappa$ define the omnific mask
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:mask}
 Y_A=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\omega^{q_\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\tau_\alpha,
\end{equation}
with $q_\alpha$ and $\tau_\alpha$ as in \eqref{lce:sg:eq:scales}. Its
support is $\{q_\alpha:\alpha\in A\}$ and its coefficients are all one; for
$A=\kappa$ it is $Y_\kappa$ of \eqref{lce:sg:eq:QZ}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:measure}
 \coef{q_\kappa}{J(Y_A)}=\coef{q_\kappa}{D_j(Y_A)}
 =\begin{cases}1,&A\in U_j,\\0,&A\notin U_j,\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\coef{q_\kappa}{x}$ is the coefficient of $\tau_\kappa=\omega^{q_\kappa}$
in $x$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Uniform definability of the mask construction and
\leref{lce:lem:absolute} give
\[
 J(Y_A)=\sum_{\beta\in j(A)}\tau_\beta.
\]
For $\alpha<\kappa$ all the exponents $q_\alpha$ are fixed by $J$, so
$H_j(Y_A)=Y_A$. Since $Y_A$ has no term $\tau_\kappa$, subtracting it does not
change the coefficient at $\tau_\kappa$. That coefficient is one exactly when
$\kappa\in j(A)$.
\end{proof}
This gives a faithful numerical presentation of a normal measure once the
set-induced embedding is available. It is not a proof that an arbitrary
field embedding produces a normal measure. The set-theoretic origin and its
action on every subset code are indispensable hypotheses \src{09}.

\begin{corollary}[Distinguishing normal measures \src{06, 07}]\label{lce:cor:distinctU}
Suppose $U$ and $W$ are distinct normal measures on the same $\kappa$, with
normal ultrapower embeddings $j_U$ and $j_W$. Their coefficient functionals
\[
 A\longmapsto\coef{-\kappa}{D_{j_U}(h_A)},\qquad
 A\longmapsto\coef{-\kappa}{D_{j_W}(h_A)}
\]
are distinct. Thus the first failure cardinal does not determine the
coefficient-level defect data.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $A$ in exactly one of $U,W$. In the normal ultrapower construction,
the measure derived at the seed $\kappa$ is the original measure.
Apply \thref{lce:thm:measure}.
\end{proof}
06 proves the same with its bounded codes, and adds that the Hahn lifts fix
those codes pointwise.

\begin{corollary}[Faithful bounded coding \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:cor:faithful}
Distinct normal measures on the same $\kappa$ induce different transport
maps on the set $\{\tilde h_A:A\subseteq\kappa\}\subset(1,3)$. Every
associated Hahn lift fixes that entire set pointwise.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $A$ which belongs to one measure and not to the other. The
coefficient in \eqref{lce:cs:eq:measure-coeff}, and hence the coefficient of
the transported code at $-\kappa$, differs. The fixed-point assertion was
proved in \thref{lce:cs:thm:recover}.
\end{proof}
This recovers the ultrafilter from a coefficient observable rather than by
recovering all of set theory from birthday data. The coefficient extraction
and the monomial scale are specified additional structure; neither is
claimed definable in the pure field language \src{06}.

\begin{remark}[A zero probe does not mean a zero defect \src{07, 09}]
If $A\subseteq\kappa$ has cardinality $\kappa$ but does not belong to
$U_j$, then $D_j(h_A)\ne0$ by the exact support theorem, although its
coefficient at $\omega^{-\kappa}$ is zero. Such sets exist: partition
$\kappa$ into two sets of size $\kappa$, at most one of which is in the
ultrafilter. The first missing \emph{normal-form index} is always
$\kappa$ for a long support, but its associated \emph{exponent} depends
on the exponent list. The fixed mask probe need not be the leading
nonzero term of every mask defect.
09 makes the same warning for its masks: if $A$ has size $\kappa$ and
$A\notin U_j$, then $\coef{q_\kappa}{D_j(Y_A)}=0$ but $D_j(Y_A)\ne0$; other
additional exponents remain, and the distinguished coefficient records
measure membership, not the entirety of the support discrepancy.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Real-valued masks and the Hadamard product}
For $f:\kappa\to\R$, set
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:hf}
 h_f=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa} f(\alpha)\omega^{-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This includes zero coefficients with the convention that they are suppressed
in the canonical normal form. Let $\mathcal B_\kappa$ denote the set of
all these masks. Give it its coefficientwise, or Hadamard, product
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:hadamard}
 h_f\odot h_g=h_{fg},\qquad (fg)(\alpha)=f(\alpha)g(\alpha).
\end{equation}
Together with ordinary addition and real scalar multiplication, this makes
$\mathcal B_\kappa$ an $\R$-algebra isomorphic to $\R^\kappa$.
Its multiplicative unit is $h_\kappa=h_{\mathbf 1}$, \emph{not} the surreal
number 1. The map from scalars into this algebra is
$r\mapsto r h_\kappa$.

\begin{theorem}[Ultrafilter evaluation as a defect coefficient \src{07, 09}]\label{lce:thm:character}
The functional
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:chi}
 \chi_j(h_f)=\coef{-\kappa}{\bigl(J(h_f)-H_j(h_f)\bigr)}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:chievaluation}
 \chi_j(h_f)=j(f)(\kappa).
\end{equation}
It is a unital $\R$-algebra homomorphism
$(\mathcal B_\kappa,+,\odot)\to\R$. Its value is the unique $r\in\R$
for which $f^{-1}(\{r\})\in U_j$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All original exponents are fixed, so $H_j(h_f)=h_f$. The whole transformed
mask is
\[
 J(h_f)=\sum_{\beta<j(\kappa)}j(f)(\beta)\omega^{-\beta}.
\]
Reading its coefficient at $-\kappa$ proves
\eqref{lce:eq:chievaluation}. The value lies in $\R$, since $j(f)$ takes
values in $j(\R)=\R$. If $r=j(f)(\kappa)$, then $j(r)=r$ and
\[
 \kappa\in j\bigl(f^{-1}(\{r\})\bigr),
\]
which gives the asserted fiber. Two distinct fibers cannot both belong to
an ultrafilter. Transferring pointwise addition and multiplication gives
\[
 j(f+g)(\kappa)=j(f)(\kappa)+j(g)(\kappa),\qquad
 j(fg)(\kappa)=j(f)(\kappa)j(g)(\kappa).
\]
Constant functions take their constant real value. These are exactly the
claimed algebra-homomorphism laws under \eqref{lce:eq:hadamard}.
\end{proof}
09 proves the same evaluation for weighted omnific masks, with a different
proof that the fiber exists.

\begin{proposition}[Weighted omnific evaluation \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:prop:weighted}
For $f:\kappa\to\R$ put
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:weighted}
 Y_f=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}f(\alpha)\tau_\alpha,
\end{equation}
with zero coefficients omitted. The map $f\mapsto Y_f$ is an injective
$\R$-linear map into the purely infinite part of $\Oz$, and
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:weight-eval}
 \coef{q_\kappa}{J(Y_f)}=j(f)(\kappa).
\end{equation}
There is a unique $r\in\R$ such that $f^{-1}(\{r\})\in U_j$, and the right
side of \eqref{lce:sg:eq:weight-eval} equals that $r$. Hence this
coefficient is the character of $\R^\kappa$ associated with $U_j$, when
$\R^\kappa$ has pointwise addition and multiplication.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The normal-form coefficient at $\tau_\kappa$ after applying $j$ to
\eqref{lce:sg:eq:weighted} is exactly $j(f)(\kappa)$, including the
possibility that it is zero. Since $\kappa$ is measurable (by
\leref{lce:cs:lem:critical}, or by \thref{lce:thm:measure}), $\abs\R<\kappa$.
The partition of $\kappa$ into the nonempty fibers of $f$ has fewer than
$\kappa$ parts. If no fiber belonged to $U_j$, the intersection of their
complements would belong to $U_j$ by completeness and would be empty. At
most one fiber can belong to an ultrafilter, proving existence and
uniqueness. Applying $j$ to that fiber identity gives $j(f)(\kappa)=j(r)=r$.
Values of sums and pointwise products are therefore the sums and products
of these selected real values, either directly by $j$ or by intersecting
the corresponding large fibers.
\end{proof}
The image of $\R^\kappa$ in \eqref{lce:sg:eq:weighted} is naturally an
algebra under coefficientwise, or Hadamard, multiplication
$Y_f\odot Y_g=Y_{fg}$. It is \emph{not} a subalgebra under ordinary surreal
multiplication. For masks, $Y_A\odot Y_B=Y_{A\cap B}$, but in general
$Y_AY_B\ne Y_{A\cap B}$: the latter product adds exponents and creates terms
$\omega^{q_\alpha+q_\beta}$. Thus the ultrafilter interpretation uses
explicitly specified coefficient operations; it cannot be smuggled into the
ordinary ring language by treating masks as idempotents. A field has no
nontrivial ordinary idempotents \src{09}.

\status{\textbf{Product warning.} The character theorem concerns the Hadamard
product $\odot$. It does not say that coefficient extraction at
$\omega^{-\kappa}$ is multiplicative for ordinary surreal multiplication.
Ordinary multiplication convolves exponents; Hadamard multiplication
multiplies coefficients at matching exponents.}

Infinite direct-product homomorphisms and their ultrafilter factorizations
are a classical subject; see, for example, Bergman \cite{Bergman}.
The next result records an exact large-cardinal criterion in this particular
mask presentation. Its underlying ultrafilter characterization is not claimed
to be new.

\begin{theorem}[A mask criterion for measurability]\label{lce:thm:measurablecriterion}
Let $\kappa$ be an uncountable cardinal, without initially assuming an
embedding $j$. The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\kappa$ is measurable.
\item There is a unital $\R$-algebra homomorphism
$\chi:(\mathcal B_\kappa,+,\odot)\to\R$ such that
$\chi(h_{\{\alpha\}})=0$ for every $\alpha<\kappa$, and for every
pairwise disjoint family $(A_\xi)_{\xi<\mu}$ with $\mu<\kappa$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:smallidempotent}
 \chi\left(h_{\bigcup_{\xi<\mu}A_\xi}\right)
    =\sum_{\xi<\mu}\chi(h_{A_\xi}).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The scalar sum in \eqref{lce:eq:smallidempotent} has at most one nonzero term,
so no infinite real summation convention is needed. Under measurability,
$\chi$ can be chosen to be the defect functional \eqref{lce:eq:chi} of a
normal ultrapower embedding.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume (i) and choose a normal measure and its ultrapower. The functional
from \thref{lce:thm:character} vanishes on singleton masks. Its value on each
indicator mask is 0 or 1 according to membership in the measure.
For fewer than $\kappa$ disjoint sets, their union belongs to a
$\kappa$-complete ultrafilter exactly when one of them belongs to it:
otherwise all their complements have intersection in the ultrafilter.
This proves \eqref{lce:eq:smallidempotent}.

Conversely, assume (ii). Each $h_A$ is a Hadamard idempotent, so
$\chi(h_A)^2=\chi(h_A)$ and hence $\chi(h_A)\in\{0,1\}$.
Define $U=\{A\subseteq\kappa:\chi(h_A)=1\}$. The identities
\[
 h_A\odot h_B=h_{A\cap B},\qquad
 h_{\kappa\setminus A}=h_\kappa-h_A
\]
show that $U$ is an ultrafilter. For example, upward closure follows from
$h_A\odot h_B=h_A$ when $A\subseteq B$, and exactly one of a set and
its complement has value 1. The singleton hypothesis makes it
nonprincipal.

To verify $\kappa$-completeness, let $A_\xi\in U$ for $\xi<\mu<\kappa$.
Set $B_\xi=\kappa\setminus A_\xi$, and disjointify the family by
\[
 C_\xi=B_\xi\setminus\bigcup_{\eta<\xi}B_\eta.
\]
Each $C_\xi\subseteq B_\xi$ has value zero: multiplying its idempotent
by $h_{B_\xi}$ leaves it unchanged. By \eqref{lce:eq:smallidempotent},
$\bigcup_{\xi<\mu}C_\xi=\bigcup_{\xi<\mu}B_\xi$ also has value zero.
Its complement $\bigcap_{\xi<\mu}A_\xi$ is therefore in $U$.
Thus $U$ is a nonprincipal $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter on $\kappa$,
which is the usual measure characterization of measurability.
\end{proof}

This criterion detects measurability only because the mask algebra and its
small-union condition explicitly retain a $\kappa$-indexed Boolean algebra.
It is not a characterization of measurability in the pure ordered-field
language of $\No$.

\subsection{A compatible-field-embedding criterion \src{09}}
09 gives a second criterion, of a different form: its witness is an
ordered field embedding of all of $\No$, not a functional on a mask
algebra, and its extra operation is a Hadamard product on all surreals.
For arbitrary surreals define
\[
 x\odot y=\sum_{a\in\supp(x)\cap\supp(y)}\coef{a}{x}\,\coef{a}{y}\,\omega^a.
\]
The intersection is reverse well-ordered, so this operation is always
defined. It restricts to the operation on the masks $h_f$ and $Y_f$
introduced above.

\begin{theorem}[Compatible-field-embedding criterion for measurability \src{09}]
\label{lce:sg:thm:numerical}
Fix an uncountable cardinal $\kappa$. In a class framework with Separation
and Replacement for the maps in question, the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The cardinal $\kappa$ is measurable.
\item There is an ordered field embedding $E:\No\to\No$ fixing $\R$ and
commuting with Conway's omega-map such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $E(\tau_\alpha)=\tau_\alpha$ for every $\alpha<\kappa$;
\item $E$ preserves every strongly summable family of cardinality less than
$\kappa$;
\item for all $A,B\subseteq\kappa$, $E(Y_{A\cap B})=E(Y_A)\odot E(Y_B)$;
\item $\coef{q_\kappa}{E(Y_\kappa)}=1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
In the forward direction the witness $E$ can be chosen definable from a
normal measure. In the reverse direction no elementary embedding of set
universes is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume measurability, choose a normal measure and take $E=J$.
\thref{lce:thm:positive} and \thref{lce:sg:thm:threshold} give (a) and (b),
and \eqref{lce:sg:eq:Zdefect} gives (d). Hadamard multiplication is
definable from normal forms and real coefficient multiplication. Its value
for old inputs is absolute by \leref{lce:lem:absolute}; thus $J$ preserves
it. Since $Y_A\odot Y_B=Y_{A\cap B}$, condition (c) follows.

Conversely, let $E$ satisfy (ii) and put
\[
 \chi_E(A)=\coef{q_\kappa}{E(Y_A)},\qquad
 U_E=\{A\subseteq\kappa:\chi_E(A)=1\}.
\]
Separation for the graph of $E$ makes $U_E$ a set. Taking $A=B$ in (c)
shows $\chi_E(A)^2=\chi_E(A)$. As a real idempotent, $\chi_E(A)$ is zero or
one. Condition (d) gives $\chi_E(\kappa)=1$, and additivity of the field
embedding, applied to $Y_A+Y_{\kappa\setminus A}=Y_\kappa$, gives
$\chi_E(A)+\chi_E(\kappa\setminus A)=1$. Condition (c) further gives
$\chi_E(A\cap B)=\chi_E(A)\chi_E(B)$. These identities show that $U_E$ is an
ultrafilter. Its singleton values are zero by (a), since $\tau_\alpha$ has no
coefficient at $\tau_\kappa$ for $\alpha<\kappa$. Thus $U_E$ is nonprincipal.

To prove completeness, take $\nu<\kappa$ and sets $A_\xi\in U_E$ for
$\xi<\nu$. Put $B_\xi=\kappa\setminus A_\xi$ and disjointify them:
\[
 C_\xi=B_\xi\setminus\bigcup_{\eta<\xi}B_\eta.
\]
Each $C_\xi\subseteq B_\xi$ has $\chi_E(C_\xi)=0$ by the ultrafilter
identities. The family $(Y_{C_\xi})_{\xi<\nu}$ is strongly summable: its
support union is a subset of the fixed decreasing scale
$(q_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa}$ and each exponent occurs at most once. Its sum
is $Y_B$, where $B=\bigcup_{\xi<\nu}B_\xi$. By (b) its image family is
strongly summable and sums to $E(Y_B)$. Taking the coefficient at
$\tau_\kappa$ gives zero, since every summand has zero there and coefficient
extraction of a strong sum is a finite coefficient calculation.
Consequently $\chi_E(B)=0$, so $\bigcap_{\xi<\nu}A_\xi\in U_E$.

We have produced a nonprincipal $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter on $\kappa$,
which is exactly measurability. The extra ordered-field and omega-map
compatibility in (ii) was not needed for this converse, but the forward
construction shows that it can be required simultaneously.
\end{proof}
The implication from the coefficient identities to a measure is the
classical ultrafilter criterion expressed in normal-form coordinates. The
simultaneous compatibility with ordinary surreal field arithmetic, the
omega-map, and all small strong sums is supplied by the large-cardinal
construction. The theorem is an infinitary criterion with a specified
coefficient operation and all subset masks, not a first-order criterion in
the bare field or omnific-ring language. Determining which of these extra
requirements can be removed is Question~\ref{lce:sg:q:converse} \src{09}.
\mergetag{} \thref{lce:thm:measurablecriterion} and
\thref{lce:sg:thm:numerical} share the ultrafilter argument (idempotents,
disjointification, small unions); they differ in what carries it, a
character of an abstract mask algebra (07) or an ordered field embedding of
$\No$ compatible with $\odot$ on masks (09). Neither removes the coefficient
algebra.

\section{Omnific integers and a purely infinite measure code}\label{lce:sec:omnific}

\subsection{The ring and its short-support part}
The omnific integers form the discretely ordered subring
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:Oz}
 \Oz=\left\{\sum_a r_a\omega^a:
      \supp(x)\subseteq\No^{\ge0},\quad r_0\in\Z\right\}.
\end{equation}
A missing zero exponent has coefficient zero. Nonzero coefficients at
positive exponents are arbitrary reals, not necessarily ordinary integers.
This is Conway's normal-form description \cite{Conway,Gonshor}.
We use it rather than an informal criterion such as ``having no fractional
part,'' which can hide a convention at exponent zero.
Put
\[
 \Ik=\Oz\cap\Sk.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Omnific equalizer, intersection, and fractions \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:thm:Oz}
The maps $J$ and $H_j$ preserve and reflect membership in $\Oz$, and
\begin{align}
 \{z\in\Oz:J(z)=H_j(z)\}&=\Ik,\label{lce:eq:Ozeq}\\
 J(\Oz)\cap H_j(\Oz)&=J(\Ik)=H_j(\Ik),\label{lce:eq:Ozinter}\\
 \Frac(\Ik)&=\Sk,\label{lce:eq:Ozfrac}\\
 \Frac\bigl(J(\Oz)\cap H_j(\Oz)\bigr)&=\Common.\label{lce:eq:commonfrac}
\end{align}
Also $\ct(J(z))=\ct(H_j(z))=\ct(z)$ for every $z\in\Oz$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $H_j$ this preservation and reflection is part of
\leref{lce:lem:reindex}. For $J$, transfer the canonical normal form and the
condition in \eqref{lce:eq:Oz}. Nonnegativity of all exponents and the integer
condition on the constant coefficient are absolute; equivalently, apply
elementarity to the normal-form description and then
\leref{lce:lem:absolute}. A coefficient at exponent zero is fixed, and a new
missing-image exponent cannot equal zero because $0\in\Gam$.
The same reasoning gives the constant-term identity for both maps.

The equalizer formula is \thref{lce:thm:defect} restricted to $\Oz$.
If $y\in J(\Oz)\cap H_j(\Oz)$, write $y=J(z)$ with $z\in\Oz$.
The field intersection theorem forces $z\in\Sk$, so $z\in\Ik$.
Conversely, both maps have the same value on any $z\in\Ik$.
This proves \eqref{lce:eq:Ozinter}.

To prove \eqref{lce:eq:Ozfrac}, take nonzero $x\in\Sk$. Its support is a
set, so choose $d>0$ such that $d>-a$ for every $a\in\supp(x)$.
The monomial $q=\omega^d$ and the shifted series $p=\omega^d x$ have
only strictly positive exponents. Their constant coefficients vanish,
and both are in $\Oz$. Their supports have cardinality less than
$\kappa$, so $p,q\in\Ik$ and $x=p/q$. The reverse inclusion follows
because $\Sk$ is a field containing $\Ik$. Applying the field embedding
$J$ gives \eqref{lce:eq:commonfrac}.
\end{proof}
Preservation of $\Oz$ and the equalizer \eqref{lce:eq:Ozeq} are also proved
by 06 (\thref{lce:cs:thm:integer-lifts}) and 09 (its ``Restricted
equalizers''). The constant-term identity is 09's Proposition ``Constant
coefficients survive'', stated for all $x\in\No$: $\coef{0}{J(x)}=
\coef{0}{H_j(x)}=\coef{0}{x}$, so $D_j(z)$ has constant coefficient zero for
every $z\in\Oz$. For $J$, coefficient extraction is absolute by
\leref{lce:lem:absolute} and definable, so its value is the image of the
fixed real $\coef{0}{x}$; for $H_j$, injectivity of the exponent map and
$J(0)=0$ show that only the old exponent zero can contribute \src{09}. It is
also the case $a=0$ of \prref{lce:prop:invisible}. For arbitrary surreals
the constant-coefficient operation is not a field homomorphism: positive
and negative exponents can multiply to zero. On $\Oz$, whose exponents are
nonnegative, it is a ring homomorphism to $\Z$. The large discrepancies here
therefore lie in its purely infinite kernel. This does not conflict with
claims about maps to set-sized rings in the repository: the embeddings in
this article have proper-class codomains \src{09}.

\subsection{A defect with no finite residue}
For $A\subseteq\kappa$, define
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:zA}
 z_A=\omega^\kappa h_A
     =\sum_{\alpha\in A}\omega^{\kappa-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
Every exponent is strictly positive, since $\alpha<\kappa$.
Thus $z_A$ is a purely infinite omnific integer when $A$ is nonempty,
and is zero otherwise. Here and below $\kappa-\alpha$ is the difference
in the surreal ordered field.

\begin{theorem}[Omnific recovery of the measure]\label{lce:thm:Ozmeasure}
For $A\subseteq\kappa$,
\begin{align}
 D_j(z_A)&=\omega^{j(\kappa)}D_j(h_A),\label{lce:eq:zdefect}\\
 \coef{j(\kappa)-\kappa}{D_j(z_A)}
      &=\mathbf 1_{\{A\in U_j\}},\label{lce:eq:zmeasure}\\
 \ct(D_j(z_A))&=0.\label{lce:eq:zct}
\end{align}
In particular, for $z_\kappa=\omega^\kappa h_\kappa$,
\[
 D_j(z_\kappa)=
   \sum_{\kappa\le\beta<j(\kappa)}\omega^{j(\kappa)-\beta}>0,
 \qquad
 \LT(D_j(z_\kappa))=\omega^{j(\kappa)-\kappa}.
\]
Every nonzero term of every $D_j(z_A)$ is purely infinite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two embeddings agree on $\omega^\kappa$, with common value
$\omega^{j(\kappa)}$, and both are multiplicative. This proves
\eqref{lce:eq:zdefect}. Multiplication by that monomial shifts exponents by
$j(\kappa)$, so \eqref{lce:eq:zmeasure} follows from
\eqref{lce:eq:measurecoef}. Moreover $j(A)\cap\kappa=A$, so the defect of
$h_A$ has its possible indices only in
$j(A)\setminus A\subseteq[\kappa,j(\kappa))$.
After shifting, all exponents $j(\kappa)-\beta$ are strictly positive,
which proves \eqref{lce:eq:zct} and the last assertion. Taking $A=\kappa$
gives the explicit nonempty series and its leading term.
\end{proof}

The constant-term map therefore loses this large-cardinal information,
even though one specified infinite-scale coefficient recovers it.
This is compatible with the repository's emphasis on constant-term
factorizations in omnific arithmetic \cite{RepoQuotients}; it does not
supply a new factorization theorem for set-sized quotient rings.

06 shifts its bounded code the same way. 09's code $Y_A$ of
\thref{lce:sg:thm:measure} is omnific from the start, with exponents
$q_\alpha\in(0,1]$ instead of $\kappa-\alpha$.

\begin{corollary}[Omnific measure codes \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:cor:omnific-code}
The elements
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:zA}
 \tilde z_A=\omega^\kappa\tilde h_A=z_\kappa+z_A
    =\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}(1+\mathbf 1_A(\alpha))\,\omega^{\kappa-\alpha}
\end{equation}
are purely infinite omnific integers. Their transport defects satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:omnific-recovery}
 \coef{j(\kappa)-\kappa}{D_j(\tilde z_A)}=1+\mathbf 1_{U_j}(A).
\end{equation}
Thus the same measure is recoverable from the two induced maps on $\Oz$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $\alpha<\kappa$, the surreal difference $\kappa-\alpha$ is positive. This
proves membership in $\Oz$. Both embeddings send $\omega^\kappa$ to
$\omega^{j(\kappa)}$. Multiplicativity gives
$D_j(\tilde z_A)=\omega^{j(\kappa)}D_j(\tilde h_A)$, and
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:omnific-recovery} follows by shifting the coefficient in
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:measure-coeff}. Preservation of the ring by both embeddings
is \thref{lce:thm:Oz}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Omnific restrictions \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:thm:integer-lifts}
Both $J$ and $H_j$ preserve the ring $\Oz$ of omnific integers and restrict
to injective unital ring maps. Their surcomplex extensions preserve
$\Oz[i]$. The equalizer and landing criteria remain exact on each of these
rings, with support computed in its real or complex normal form. For a
normal-measure ultrapower, all three support regimes of
\thref{lce:cs:thm:normal-trichotomy} occur already in $\Oz$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the normal-form description \eqref{lce:eq:Oz}, $J$ carries the
transported array of positive exponents to an array of positive exponents
by elementarity and absoluteness. The unique constant coefficient, when
present, is an ordinary integer and is fixed. Thus $J(x)$ is omnific. The
same argument for $H_j$ is termwise: positivity of exponents and the integer
constant term are preserved. Ring preservation and injectivity follow from
the field-embedding theorem.

For $\Oz[i]$ apply the real result to the two coordinates. The exact
criteria follow by restriction of the real and complex theorems
(\thref{lce:thm:defect}, \thref{lce:cs:thm:landing} and
\thref{lce:cs:thm:complex}). For the three regimes, the omnific normal forms
$\zeta_\mu=\omega^\mu h_\mu=\sum_{\alpha<\mu}\omega^{\mu-\alpha}$ have
support cardinality $\abs\mu$. Take respectively a nonzero finite cardinal,
$\kappa$, and $\kappa^+$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Preserving is not fixing \src{06}]
Preserving $\Oz$ does not mean preserving its elements pointwise. Nor is
this a theorem that all endomorphisms of the omnific integers come from
large cardinals. It constructs and classifies the behavior of a specified
pair arising from an elementary universe embedding. The distinction is
important in comparison with the repository's independent work on omnific
automorphisms and set-sized quotients.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[An $\Oz$-reflecting embedding that is not strong \mergetag]
\label{lce:rem:opa}
The omnific-preserving-automorphisms report asks whether every field
embedding $f:\No\hookrightarrow\No$ with $f^{-1}(\Oz)=\Oz$ must be strongly
additive, and what changes if also $\ct\circ f=\ct$
(\lbl{opa:as:q:embeddingstrong} in
\fn{docs/surreal/omnific-preserving-automorphisms}, where $J$ is written
$\hat\jmath$). If there is a measurable cardinal, or more generally an
embedding as in \eqref{lce:eq:setting} available as a class, then $J$
answers both clauses negatively. It is an ordered field embedding fixing
$\R$ (\prref{lce:prop:J}); it preserves and reflects $\Oz$, and preserves
constant terms (\thref{lce:thm:Oz}); it carries every strongly summable
family to a strongly summable family, but the family
$(\omega^{-\alpha})_{\alpha<\kappa}$ has image sum $h_\kappa\ne
J(h_\kappa)=h_{j(\kappa)}$ (\thref{lce:thm:positive}, \thref{lce:thm:witness};
the omnific family $(\tau_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa}$ of
\thref{lce:sg:thm:threshold} does the same inside $\Oz$). So $J$ is not
strongly additive. It is not onto (\prref{lce:cs:prop:proper}), so there is
no conflict with that report's theorem that every ring automorphism of
$\Oz$ extends uniquely to a strongly $\R$-linear automorphism of $\No$
(\lbl{opa:as:thm:main}). A positive answer to the first clause is therefore
not provable in the base theory unless that theory with a measurable
cardinal is inconsistent. Whether a counterexample exists without large
cardinals is not addressed here, and nothing here bears on that
question's clauses about dense images or images that are neither closed nor
discrete. The pair $(J,H_j)$ also shows that preservation and reflection of
$\Oz$ together with agreement on all monomials does not determine an
embedding: $H_j$ has the same properties and is strong.
\end{remark}

\section{Surcomplex numbers and the Gaussian omnific lattice}\label{lce:sec:complex}

Let $\SC=\No(i)$ with a fixed named element $i$ satisfying $i^2=-1$,
and write conjugation as $\overline{x+iy}=x-iy$.
A surcomplex normal form has complex coefficients and a reverse
well-ordered set of surreal exponents. Its support is the union of the
supports of its real and imaginary coordinates: a complex coefficient
vanishes exactly when both real coordinates vanish.
Define
\[
 \Jc(x+iy)=J(x)+iJ(y),\qquad
 \Hc(x+iy)=H_j(x)+iH_j(y).
\]
These definitions fix the chosen orientation $i$; they are not a statement
about arbitrary automorphisms of the pure surcomplex field.

\begin{theorem}[Surcomplex transfer \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:thm:complex}
The maps $\Jc,\Hc:\SC\to\SC$ are field embeddings fixing $\C$ and
commuting with conjugation. The second is strongly $\C$-linear.
They are elementary in the pure field language, and
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Eq}(\Jc,\Hc)&=\Sk(i),\label{lce:eq:complexeq}\\
 \Jc(\SC)\cap\Hc(\SC)&=\Common(i).\label{lce:eq:complexinter}
\end{align}
Both fields on the right are algebraically closed. The image fields on
the left of \eqref{lce:eq:complexinter} are incomparable. Their intersection
with the Gaussian omnific lattice satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:Gaussian}
 \Jc(\Oz[i])\cap\Hc(\Oz[i])=J(\Ik)[i].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The quadratic-extension multiplication law proves the field-homomorphism
claims coordinatewise. Uniqueness of real and imaginary parts gives
injectivity and commutation with conjugation. The strong summation claim
is the real statement on both coordinates. Since $\No$ is real closed,
$\SC$ and both image fields are algebraically closed of characteristic
zero. Quantifier elimination for $\ACF$ gives elementarity, formula by
formula, as before.

Equality of $\Jc(x+iy)$ and $\Hc(x+iy)$ is equivalent to both
$J(x)=H_j(x)$ and $J(y)=H_j(y)$. This is precisely $x,y\in\Sk$.
Equivalently, the union of the two supports has size less than $\kappa$:
a finite union of sets of size less than an infinite cardinal still has
size less than that cardinal. This proves \eqref{lce:eq:complexeq}.
The same coordinate comparison and \thref{lce:thm:intersection} prove
\eqref{lce:eq:complexinter}. The fields $\Sk$ and $\Common$ are real closed,
so adjoining $i$ makes them algebraically closed.

The real witnesses $h_\kappa$ and $h_{j(\kappa)}$ still witness
incomparability. If a real element is the image of $x+iy$, its imaginary
coordinate is zero, forcing $y=0$; allowing a complex preimage cannot
repair the failure of a real preimage. Finally apply
\thref{lce:thm:Oz} separately to the two integer coordinates to obtain
\eqref{lce:eq:Gaussian}.
\end{proof}

The exact defect formula also holds directly with complex coefficients:
for $z=\sum_{\alpha<\theta}c_\alpha\omega^{a_\alpha}$, set
$c^*=j(c)$ after identifying $\C$ with $\R^2$. Then
\[
 \Jc(z)-\Hc(z)=
   \sum_{\beta\in P_\theta}c^*_\beta\omega^{a^*_\beta}.
\]
Nonzero complex coefficients at distinct exponents cannot cancel. Thus
long complex support, not a sign or order on complex numbers, is what
forces disagreement. The Boolean measure masks from
Section~\ref{lce:sec:measure} already lie on the real axis and require no
complex choice beyond the named inclusion.

\begin{corollary}[Conjugation and modulus]\label{lce:cor:modulus}
If $|z|=(z\overline z)^{1/2}\in\No^{\ge0}$, then
\[
 |\Jc(z)|=J(|z|),\qquad |\Hc(z)|=H_j(|z|).
\]
Every surcomplex number in either image outside $\Common(i)$ is
transcendental over $\Common(i)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both maps preserve conjugation and the unique nonnegative square root.
The transcendence assertion follows because $\Common(i)$ is algebraically
closed.
\end{proof}

06 and 09 prove the transfer and the equalizer $\Sk(i)$ as well; 09 notes
that the same $\kappa$-sized real omnific family $(\tau_\alpha)$ witnesses the
failure of full strong additivity for $\Jc$, and 06 that properness is
witnessed by the real elements of \prref{lce:cs:prop:proper}: a nonreal
preimage of a real value is impossible, since the injective real-coordinate
map would have to send a nonzero imaginary part to zero. 06 adds descent.

\begin{theorem}[Surcomplex descent \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:thm:complex}
For every $z\in\SC$ of complex normal-form length $\lambda$ (the support
being the union of the supports of the two coordinates),
\[
 \Hc(z)\in\SC^M\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\imset j\lambda\in M.
\]
The missing-index formula and the normal-measure trichotomy
(\thref{lce:cs:thm:normal-trichotomy}) hold with complex coefficients. In a
set-ultrapower, descent is again equivalent to closure under sequences of
the support cardinality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply $j$ to the packed complex coefficient and real exponent arrays. The
transformed coefficient array remains nonzero at every index. The termwise
image retains exactly the indices $\imset j\lambda$, so normal-form
uniqueness proves the equalizer and defect formulas. For descent, if the
complex number belongs to $\SC^M$, its two coordinates and their normal
forms belong to $M$, hence their combined complex support does as well. The
preimage under $j(a)$ used in \thref{lce:cs:thm:landing} recovers
$\imset j\lambda$. Conversely an internal seed permits evaluation of both
coordinates inside $M$. The sequence and normal-measure conclusions follow
unchanged, by \leref{lce:cs:lem:seed-closure} and the proof of
\thref{lce:cs:thm:normal-trichotomy}.
\end{proof}
The local analytic conclusion of \prref{lce:cs:prop:formal} therefore
applies to complex formal power series at surcomplex infinitesimals. No
choice of a global surcomplex logarithm or exponential at arbitrary
infinite arguments is needed. In particular no assertion about such a
global function is hidden in the notation $\SC$ \src{06}. For surcomplex
numbers the field-theoretic transfer is automatic once the real result is
known, but descent still depends on the real exponent support and on the
same seed \src{06}.

\section{Algebraic independence over the short-support field \src{09}}
\label{lce:sg:sec:independence}

This section does not assume a measurable cardinal or an elementary
embedding. Fix any uncountable cardinal $\kappa$. The exponent scale
$q_\alpha=\omega^{-\alpha}$ of \eqref{lce:sg:eq:scales} has two useful
features: it is decreasing, and its members are linearly independent over
$\Q$. The latter follows immediately from uniqueness of finite Conway normal
forms, since the $q_\alpha$ are distinct monomials. The omnific masks $Y_A$
are those of \eqref{lce:sg:eq:mask}.

\begin{definition}[Almost disjoint families \src{09}]
A family $\mathcal A\subseteq[\kappa]^\kappa$ is $\kappa$-almost disjoint if
$\abs{A\cap B}<\kappa$ whenever $A,B\in\mathcal A$ are distinct.
\end{definition}

\subsection{A coordinate separation lemma}
\begin{lemma}[Avoiding all coefficient directions \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:lem:separate}
Let $G_\kappa=\bigoplus_{\alpha<\kappa}\Q q_\alpha$ and let
$\Xi\subseteq(\No,+)$ be a rational vector subspace of cardinality less
than $\kappa$. There is a set $B\subseteq\kappa$ of size less than $\kappa$
such that, for every $\alpha\notin B$, the coordinate map
\[
 G_\kappa\longrightarrow\Q,\qquad
 \sum_\xi c_\xi q_\xi\longmapsto c_\alpha
\]
extends to a linear functional $\ell_\alpha:\Xi+G_\kappa\to\Q$ that
vanishes on $\Xi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a rational basis of $\Xi\cap G_\kappa$. Every vector in this basis
has finite support in the displayed basis of $G_\kappa$. Let $B$ be the
union of these finite supports. The number of basis vectors is at most
$\abs\Xi$, so $\abs B<\kappa$, with the finite cases interpreted in the
evident way. This estimate does not require $\kappa$ to be regular.

For $\alpha\notin B$ the $\alpha$-coordinate vanishes on $\Xi\cap
G_\kappa$. Define $\ell_\alpha(\xi+v)$ to be that coordinate of $v$ for
$\xi\in\Xi$ and $v\in G_\kappa$. If $\xi+v=\xi'+v'$, then $v-v'\in\Xi\cap
G_\kappa$, so the coordinate is independent of the representation. The
resulting map is linear and kills $\Xi$.
\end{proof}
The lemma is what allows arbitrary short-support surreal coefficients, not
just coefficients whose exponents already lie in $G_\kappa$ \src{09}.

\subsection{The independence theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Almost-disjoint omnific independence \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:AD}
For every uncountable cardinal $\kappa$ and every $\kappa$-almost-disjoint
family $\mathcal A\subseteq[\kappa]^\kappa$, the family
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:AD-family}
 \{Y_A:A\in\mathcal A\}
\end{equation}
is algebraically independent over the entire field $\Sk$ inside $\No$. Its
members are positive purely infinite omnific integers of normal-form length
$\kappa$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose distinct $A_1,\ldots,A_n\in\mathcal A$ and a nonzero polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:P}
 \Phi(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=\sum_{\mathbf m}c_{\mathbf m}X_1^{m_1}\cdots X_n^{m_n}
 \in\Sk[X_1,\ldots,X_n].
\end{equation}
Only finitely many coefficients are nonzero. Let $S$ be the union of their
normal-form supports and set $\Xi=\Span(S)$. Then $\abs\Xi<\kappa$, exactly
as in the proof of \thref{lce:sg:thm:shortfield}. Obtain $B$ and the
coordinate functionals from \leref{lce:sg:lem:separate}.

For each $i$ choose
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:private-index}
 \alpha_i\in A_i\setminus\Bigl(B\cup\bigcup_{k\ne i}A_k\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Such a choice is possible because the removed part of $A_i$ has cardinality
less than $\kappa$: it is a finite union of small intersections and the
small set $B$. The indices $\alpha_i$ are distinct. More importantly,
$q_{\alpha_i}$ occurs in the support of $Y_{A_i}$ and in none of the other
selected masks.

Choose a multi-index $\mathbf m=(m_1,\ldots,m_n)$ with nonzero coefficient
whose total degree $d=m_1+\cdots+m_n$ is maximum among the nonzero terms of
$\Phi$. Choose one exponent $d_0\in\supp(c_{\mathbf m})$, and let
$\varrho\ne0$ be its real coefficient. We claim that the exponent
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:distinguished-exp}
 \gamma=d_0+\sum_{i=1}^n m_i q_{\alpha_i}
\end{equation}
has coefficient exactly $\varrho$ in $\Phi(Y_{A_1},\ldots,Y_{A_n})$.

A contribution to this coefficient from a term with multi-index
$\mathbf n$ consists of an exponent $d'\in\supp(c_{\mathbf n})$ and, for
each of its $n_i$ factors of $Y_{A_i}$, a choice of one exponent $q_\beta$
with $\beta\in A_i$. The total exponent is $d'$ plus all these chosen
exponents. It belongs to the set-sized group $\Xi+G_\kappa$. Apply the $n$
functionals $\ell_{\alpha_i}$ to a putative equality of this total exponent
with \eqref{lce:sg:eq:distinguished-exp}. Each functional kills $d'$ and
$d_0$. It counts exactly the number of selected factors whose exponent is
$q_{\alpha_i}$. That count must be $m_i$.

The counts sum to $d$, so at least $d$ factors have already been used. On
the other hand the maximality of $d$ gives $\sum_i n_i\le d$. Hence exactly
$d$ factors occur, all of them at the specified private indices. Because
$\alpha_i$ belongs only to $A_i$ among the selected sets, the equality forces
$n_i=m_i$ for every $i$. There are no further factors, and the exponent
equation now forces $d'=d_0$.

Within the powers $Y_{A_i}^{m_i}$ all $m_i$ factors must select the same term
$\tau_{\alpha_i}$. The coefficient of that choice is one; there is no further
multinomial multiplicity. Thus the only contribution to the coefficient at
$\gamma$ is $\varrho$, as claimed. All convolutions used here are legitimate
finite coefficient convolutions by Hahn summability. The argument also
covers $d=0$, in which case the polynomial is a nonzero constant. We have
proved that every nonzero polynomial \eqref{lce:sg:eq:P} evaluates
nontrivially.

Each mask has positive exponents and coefficients one, so is a positive
purely infinite omnific integer. A subset of $\kappa$ of cardinality
$\kappa$ has inherited order type $\kappa$: its order type is at most
$\kappa$ and cannot be a smaller ordinal of the same cardinality. Therefore
its normal-form length is $\kappa$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why cancellation cannot be hidden in the coefficients \src{09}]
A coefficient in $\Sk$ can contain exponents unrelated to any $q_\alpha$.
Such exponents can create complicated additive relations. The proof does
not assume they are absent. It puts their entire rational span into $\Xi$
and chooses private coordinates annihilating $\Xi$. The maximal
\emph{total} degree then exhausts every factor, leaving exactly one nonzero
coefficient. Using only distinct leading exponents of the masks would not
establish this stronger coefficient-field assertion.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Maximal cardinality inside a fixed Hahn field}
\begin{theorem}[A $2^\kappa$ family \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:maximal}
Suppose $\kappa$ is uncountable and $2^{<\kappa}=\kappa$. Let
\[
 G_\kappa=\bigoplus_{\alpha<\kappa}\Q q_\alpha,
 \qquad K_\kappa=\Hahn{G_\kappa}.
\]
Then $K_\kappa$ contains $2^\kappa$ positive purely infinite omnific integers,
algebraically independent over $\Sk$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:trdeg}
 \abs{K_\kappa}=2^\kappa,
 \qquad \trdeg\bigl(\Sk K_\kappa/\Sk\bigr)=2^\kappa.
\end{equation}
The compositum in \eqref{lce:sg:eq:trdeg} is the subfield of $\No$ generated
by $\Sk$ and $K_\kappa$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The binary tree $2^{<\kappa}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}2^\alpha$ has
cardinality $\kappa$. Fix a bijection $\rho:2^{<\kappa}\to\kappa$. For each
branch $b\in2^\kappa$ put
\[
 A_b=\{\rho(b\res\alpha):\alpha<\kappa\}.
\]
Each $A_b$ has size $\kappa$. Distinct branches share only their prefixes
through their first disagreement; their sets therefore intersect in fewer
than $\kappa$ points. The family $(A_b)_{b\in2^\kappa}$ is
$\kappa$-almost disjoint, so \thref{lce:sg:thm:AD} applies. Every resulting
$Y_{A_b}$ is supported in $G_\kappa$, hence belongs to $K_\kappa$. No
order-preserving property of $\rho$ is needed: any subset of the ordinal
$\kappa$ has a well-ordered increasing enumeration.

The group $G_\kappa$ has cardinality $\kappa$, and $\abs\R\le\kappa$ follows
from $2^{<\kappa}=\kappa$. A Hahn series is specified by a coefficient
function on that group, so
\[
 \abs{K_\kappa}\le\abs\R^\kappa\le\kappa^\kappa=2^\kappa.
\]
The constructed masks already give the reverse inequality. They provide the
lower bound for the transcendence degree in \eqref{lce:sg:eq:trdeg}; the set
$K_\kappa$, which generates the extension, gives the upper bound.

Although the base field is a proper class, this last use of transcendence
degree causes no size problem. Algebraic independence of a subset of the
\emph{set} $K_\kappa$ is a definable set-theoretic condition: each
polynomial involves only finitely many surreal coefficients. Zorn's lemma on
subsets of $K_\kappa$ gives a maximal independent set over $\Sk$.
Maximality makes the set-generated extension algebraic over that
independent set. The usual exchange argument is finitary and applies
unchanged. Thus the transcendence basis under discussion is a set.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complex coefficients and measurable cardinals \src{09}]
\label{lce:sg:cor:combined}
The families in Theorems~\ref{lce:sg:thm:AD} and~\ref{lce:sg:thm:maximal}
are also algebraically independent over $\Sk(i)$ inside $\SC$. If $\kappa$ is
measurable, \thref{lce:sg:thm:maximal} applies, and its $2^\kappa$ omnific
integers all lie outside the equalizer of $J$ and $H_j$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Split a putative polynomial relation with coefficients in $\Sk(i)$ into
real and imaginary parts. At least one part is a nonzero polynomial over
$\Sk$, contradicting real algebraic independence. A measurable cardinal is
strongly inaccessible, hence $2^{<\kappa}=\kappa$ \cite{Kanamori}. Every
constructed mask has support of size $\kappa$, so \thref{lce:thm:defect}
gives the disagreement assertion.
\end{proof}
The independence theorem itself is therefore not a large-cardinal theorem.
For example, it applies at $\omega_1$ under CH. The measurable-cardinal
hypothesis adds the two embeddings whose disagreement it witnesses \src{09}.
\mergetag{} This is transcendence over the \emph{domain} equalizer $\Sk$; it
does not decide 07's Question~\ref{lce:q:jointdependence} on the joint
algebraic dependence of the two \emph{image} fields over $\Common$.

\section{Defect algebra and a logarithmic homomorphism}\label{lce:sec:log}

\subsection{What all old-image coefficients fail to see}
\begin{proposition}[Coefficient invisibility]\label{lce:prop:invisible}
For every $a,x\in\No$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:oldcoeff}
 \coef{J(a)}{J(x)}=\coef{a}{x}=\coef{J(a)}{H_j(x)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $J$ and $H_j$ preserve the coefficient at exponent zero.
They preserve the finite-surreal valuation ring and its infinitesimal
ideal, and induce the identity on its residue field $\R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $a$ is in the support of $x$, its unique original index gives the
coefficient in \eqref{lce:eq:oldindices}. A missing-image index cannot supply
exponent $J(a)$ by \thref{lce:thm:defect}. If $a$ is not in the support, the
same range-reflection argument used in that theorem excludes $J(a)$ from
the support of $J(x)$. The identity for $H_j$ is its definition.
Setting $a=0$ gives the constant coefficient. The valuation identities
in Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:leading}, together with $J(0)=0$ and order
preservation, give preservation of the valuation ring and its maximal
ideal. On a finite surreal, its residue is its coefficient at zero.
\end{proof}

The constant-coefficient case $a=0$ is also 09's (see the note after
\thref{lce:thm:Oz}).
Thus \eqref{lce:eq:measurecoef} detects genuinely new exponent positions. No
coefficient extraction at an exponent in $J(\No)$ can see the defect.
Both embeddings nevertheless have the same value-group action and the
same residue action.

\begin{proposition}[Twisted product law \src{06, 07, 09}]\label{lce:prop:twisted}
The additive map $D_j$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:twisted}
 D_j(xy)=J(x)D_j(y)+D_j(x)H_j(y).
\end{equation}
For $c\in\Sk$ one has $D_j(cx)=J(c)D_j(x)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand the right side as
$J(x)J(y)-J(x)H_j(y)+J(x)H_j(y)-H_j(x)H_j(y)$.
The middle terms cancel and multiplicativity gives the left side.
The scalar identity follows because $D_j(c)=0$.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{lce:eq:twisted} is a twisted derivation identity, not an
ordinary Leibniz rule for a derivation on $\No$. No claim is made that
$D_j$ is a Berarducci--Mantova derivation or a strongly additive derivation.

06 (Proposition ``Algebra of the defect'') and 09 (Proposition ``Defect
identities'') state the same law in the equivalent symmetric form
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:product-defect}
 D_j(xy)=H_j(x)D_j(y)+H_j(y)D_j(x)+D_j(x)D_j(y),
\end{equation}
obtained by substituting $J=H_j+D_j$ in $J(xy)=J(x)J(y)$. 09 adds that $D_j$
is additive and $\R$-linear with kernel $\Sk$, and that for $x\ne0$
\[
 D_j(x^{-1})=-\frac{D_j(x)}{J(x)H_j(x)},
\]
the difference of $J(x)^{-1}$ and $H_j(x)^{-1}$; the quadratic last term in
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:product-defect} cannot be discarded \src{09}. 06 shows that
$D_j$ is not a derivation relative to $H_j$: if the ordinary $H_j$-Leibniz
identity held, then putting $x=y=h_\kappa$ would force
$D_j(h_\kappa)^2=0$, contradicting $D_j(h_\kappa)\ne0$
(\thref{lce:thm:witness}) in a field \src{06}.

\subsection{Passing from a multiplicative discrepancy to an additive one}
For $x\ne0$, let
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:eta}
 \eta_j(x)=\frac{J(x)}{H_j(x)}.
\end{equation}
By Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:leading}, this ratio lies in $1+\infideal$.
For $u\in\infideal$, the formal series
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:logseries}
 \log(1+u)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}u^n
\end{equation}
is strongly summable by the Hahn--Neumann lemma. The usual formal
power-series identities are legitimate after such infinitesimal
substitution; in particular, this logarithm takes products in
$1+\infideal$ to sums \cite{Gonshor,vdDE}. Only this near-one logarithm
is needed, not a preservation theorem for the global surreal exponential.

\begin{theorem}[Logarithmic defect]\label{lce:thm:logdefect}
The map
\[
 L_j:\No^\times\longrightarrow(\infideal,+),\qquad
 L_j(x)=\log\eta_j(x)
\]
is a group homomorphism with kernel $\Sk^\times$. If $x$ has normal form
\eqref{lce:eq:NF} of length $\theta\ge\kappa$, then
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:LTlog}
 \LT(L_j(x))=
   \frac{r^*_{\kappa}}{r_0}\,
       \omega^{a^*_{\kappa}-J(a_0)}.
\end{equation}
Thus a nonzero additive infinitesimal invariant records every departure
from the equalizer, after multiplicative normalization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because both embeddings are multiplicative,
$\eta_j(xy)=\eta_j(x)\eta_j(y)$. The logarithm identity gives
$L_j(xy)=L_j(x)+L_j(y)$.
If $\eta_j(x)=1+u$ with $u\ne0$, then the first term of
\eqref{lce:eq:logseries} is $u$, while all higher powers have strictly larger
valuation. Hence the logarithm is nonzero and has leading term $\LT(u)$.
It follows that $L_j(x)=0$ precisely when $J(x)=H_j(x)$, proving the
kernel assertion.

Finally $u=D_j(x)/H_j(x)$. Its leading term is the quotient of the
leading term in \eqref{lce:eq:firstterm} by the common leading term from
Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:leading}, namely \eqref{lce:eq:LTlog}. Its exponent is
negative since $a^*_{\kappa}<a^*_0=J(a_0)$, so it is infinitesimal as
required.
\end{proof}

For the basic witness,
\[
 \LT(L_j(h_\kappa))=\omega^{-\kappa}.
\]
The homomorphism formulation avoids introducing a quotient whose
individual equivalence classes might be proper classes. We use its
kernel as a class predicate; no extra axiom for forming hyperclasses or
class-sized quotient objects is needed.

\section{An exact exponential compatibility locus}\label{lce:sec:exponential}
This section adds the canonical Gonshor exponential. It is independent of
the proof of the main defect and image-intersection theorems, but the same
support calculation yields a second exact boundary.

Write every surreal uniquely as
\[
 x=p(x)+r(x)+\eps(x),
\]
where $p(x)$ has only positive exponents, $r(x)\in\R$, and
$\eps(x)\in\infideal$. Thus $p(x)$ is its purely infinite part.
We use the classical exponential facts that $\exp(p)$ is a Conway monomial
for purely infinite $p$, that the exponential on infinitesimals is its
strong power series, and that the inverse logarithm of a monomial is
purely infinite \cite[Chapter~10]{Gonshor}\cite[Section~5]{vdDE}.

\begin{lemma}[Exponential transfer \src{06, 07}]\label{lce:lem:exptransfer}
The elementary-induced embedding satisfies
\[
 J(\exp x)=\exp J(x),\qquad
 J(\log y)=\log J(y)\quad(y>0).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Gonshor's exponential is defined by a uniform recursive cut on the
canonical options of its argument, using exponentials of simpler options,
finite Taylor polynomials, field operations, and their inequalities.
The cut absoluteness argument from \leref{lce:lem:absolute} applies to this
recursion as well. Therefore the exponential computed in $M$ on an old
argument equals the one in $V$. Elementary transfer of the defining
recursion gives the first identity. The second follows from the uniqueness
of the inverse of the strictly increasing bijection $\exp$.
\end{proof}
06 includes the exponential in its absoluteness lemma, with the same
recursion argument (the note after \leref{lce:lem:absolute}). 06 also
proves the infinitesimal part of the next theorem as a separate
proposition, for both maps and for power series in several variables.

\begin{proposition}[Formal evaluation \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:prop:formal}
Let $A\in\R[[T_1,\ldots,T_n]]$ and let $\eps_1,\ldots,\eps_n$ be
infinitesimal surreals. With strong formal evaluation,
\begin{align*}
 H_j(A(\eps_1,\ldots,\eps_n))&=A(H_j(\eps_1),\ldots,H_j(\eps_n)),\\
 J(A(\eps_1,\ldots,\eps_n))&=A(J(\eps_1),\ldots,J(\eps_n)).
\end{align*}
The same statements hold with complex coefficients and surcomplex
infinitesimals after the extension of Section~\ref{lce:sec:complex}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Neumann lemma makes the family of evaluated monomial terms strongly
summable. It is countable, since there are finitely many variables. Both
embeddings fix every coefficient and preserve finite products. For $H_j$ use
strong additivity; for $J$ use \thref{lce:thm:positive}, since
$\aleph_0<\kappa$. Ordered-field embeddings fixing the reals preserve the
condition of being infinitesimal. The coefficientwise proof over $\C$ is
identical.
\end{proof}
This includes the Taylor exponential on $\infideal$ and the logarithm on
$1+\infideal$. Arbitrarily large supports of the individual infinitesimals
cause no problem: the \emph{number of Taylor terms} is still countable. This
is another distinction between support size and summation-family size
\src{06}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact exponential defect \src{06, 07}]\label{lce:thm:expdefect}
For every $x\in\No$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:expdefect}
 \frac{H_j(\exp x)}{\exp(H_j(x))}
      =\exp\bigl(D_j(p(x))\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:explocus}
 H_j(\exp x)=\exp(H_j(x))
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \abs{\supp(p(x))}<\kappa.
\end{equation}
The companion commutes with the exponential on every finite surreal,
including finite surreals with arbitrarily long infinitesimal support.
For a positive $y=r\omega^a(1+u)$, with $r>0$ real and $u\in\infideal$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:logcompat}
 \log(H_j(y))-H_j(\log y)=D_j(\log(\omega^a)).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The companion preserves the purely infinite, real, and infinitesimal
parts separately, because it preserves the sign of exponents. Since
$\exp(p(x))$ is a single monomial,
\[
 H_j(\exp(p(x)))=J(\exp(p(x)))=\exp(J(p(x))).
\]
Strong linearity and infinitesimal power-series evaluation give
$H_j(\exp(\eps(x)))=\exp(H_j(\eps(x)))$, while real exponential
values are fixed. Applying $H_j$ to
$\exp x=\exp(p(x))e^{r(x)}\exp(\eps(x))$ and dividing by
$\exp(H_j(x))$ proves \eqref{lce:eq:expdefect}. Injectivity of the
exponential and \thref{lce:thm:defect} give \eqref{lce:eq:explocus}.
For finite $x$, its purely infinite part is zero.

Finally use
$\log y=\log r+\log(\omega^a)+\log(1+u)$.
The near-one logarithm commutes with $H_j$ by its strong power-series
formula. Also
$\log(H_j(\omega^a))=\log(J(\omega^a))=J(\log(\omega^a))$.
Subtracting the two decompositions gives \eqref{lce:eq:logcompat}.
\end{proof}
06 proves \eqref{lce:eq:expdefect} and \eqref{lce:eq:explocus} with the
same factorization, using \prref{lce:cs:prop:formal} for the infinitesimal
factor, and records the intermediate formula
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:exp-formula}
 H_j(\exp x)=\exp\bigl(J(p(x))+r(x)+H_j(\eps(x))\bigr).
\end{equation}
It notes that a purely infinite series may have either sign: ``purely
infinite'' describes its support, not its positivity; and it cites the
classical facts that the exponential maps the group of purely infinite
series onto the monomial group and agrees with the real exponential and the
infinitesimal Taylor exponential on the other two parts
\cite{Gonshor,BKMM}. The identity
\eqref{lce:eq:explocus} fails at every purely infinite input with at least
$\kappa$ nonzero terms \src{06}. The logarithmic identity
\eqref{lce:eq:logcompat} is 07's.

\begin{corollary}[A scale-sized exponential discrepancy \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:cor:exp-scale}
The ratio in \eqref{lce:eq:expdefect} is always a monomial. It is either
exactly one, a positive infinitesimal, or larger than every positive real
number. In particular
\[
 H_j(\exp x)\sim\exp(H_j x)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H_j(\exp x)=\exp(H_j x),
\]
where $y\sim w$ means $y/w\in1+\infideal$ for positive $y,w$ (06 writes
$p\sim q$).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Both $J(p(x))$ and $H_j(p(x))$ are purely infinite. Their difference is
therefore purely infinite, possibly zero. Gonshor exponentiation maps this
additive group to the monomial group. A nonidentity positive monomial is
either infinitesimal or infinitely large relative to the reals. Apply
\eqref{lce:eq:expdefect}.
\end{proof}
The two criteria of Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:omegalocus} and
\eqref{lce:eq:explocus} are different. For example, $h_\kappa$ has $\kappa$
terms but is finite, so the Hahn lift fails to commute with $\omega^x$ at
$h_\kappa$, while commuting with $\exp x$ there. For the purely infinite
omnific integer
\[
 \zeta_\kappa=z_\kappa=\omega^\kappa h_\kappa=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}\omega^{\kappa-\alpha},
\]
both identities fail \src{06}.

\begin{corollary}[Exponential closure without logarithmic closure]\label{lce:cor:expclosure}
The fields $\Sk$ and $\Common$ are closed under the Gonshor exponential
but not under logarithms of positive elements. With this induced
exponential, neither is an elementary substructure of $(\No,\exp)$,
although both are elementary ordered subfields of $\No$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $x\in\Sk$, then $p(x)\in\Sk$. By the preceding theorem and
\leref{lce:lem:exptransfer},
\[
 H_j(\exp x)=\exp(H_j(x))=\exp(J(x))=J(\exp x).
\]
The equalizer theorem yields $\exp x\in\Sk$. The same closure for
$\Common=J(\Sk)$ follows from exponential transfer.

The purely infinite element $z_\kappa$ of Section~\ref{lce:sec:omnific} has
$\kappa$ outer terms, so it is not in $\Sk$. But $\exp(z_\kappa)$ is
a positive monomial and hence belongs to $\Sk$. Its unique logarithm
in $\No$ is $z_\kappa$, which is absent. Similarly
$J(\exp(z_\kappa))$ lies in $\Common$, whereas its logarithm
$J(z_\kappa)$ does not, by injectivity of $J$ and the description of
$\Common$. Thus the sentence asserting that every positive element has
an exponential preimage holds in $(\No,\exp)$ and fails in both smaller
exponential structures. Their ordered-field elementarity follows from
real closedness.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One image is exponentially closed and the other is not]
\label{lce:cor:Hnotexp}
The image $J(\No)$ is closed under both exponential and logarithm.
The image $H_j(\No)$ is not closed under exponential.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows immediately from \leref{lce:lem:exptransfer}.
For the second, use the classical order-preserving bijection
$g:\No^{>0}\to\No$ specified by
\[
 \exp(\omega^b)=\omega^{\omega^{g(b)}}\quad(b>0).
\]
The purely infinite exponential formula says that
$\exp(\sum r_\alpha\omega^{b_\alpha})
=\omega^{\sum r_\alpha\omega^{g(b_\alpha)}}$ when all $b_\alpha>0$;
see \cite[Theorem~10.13]{Gonshor} and \cite[Lemma~5.2, proof]{vdDE}.
Define
\[
 p_\kappa=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}\omega^{g^{-1}(-\alpha)}.
\]
The exponents are strictly decreasing and positive, so this is a purely
infinite normal form and $\exp(p_\kappa)=\omega^{h_\kappa}$.
The definition of $g$, together with transfer of exponential and the
omega-map, shows that $J(g(b))=g(J(b))$ and hence that $J$ commutes with
$g^{-1}$. Since $-\alpha$ is fixed for $\alpha<\kappa$, all the displayed
exponents are fixed. Therefore $H_j(p_\kappa)=p_\kappa$, so
$p_\kappa\in H_j(\No)$.

But $h_\kappa\notin J(\No)=\Gam$ by \thref{lce:thm:witness}.
The monomial $\omega^{h_\kappa}$ consequently has its exponent outside
$\Gam$ and is not in $H_j(\No)$ by \eqref{lce:eq:Himage}.
This proves the failure of exponential closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two canonical closures of the common field]\label{lce:thm:twoclosures}
Among class subfields of $\No$ containing $\Common$, the least one closed
under logarithms of positive elements is $J(\No)$, and the least one
closed under external set-indexed strong sums is $H_j(\No)$.
More explicitly, every element of $J(\No)$ has the form
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:singlelog}
 \frac{\log m_1}{m_2},
 \qquad m_1,m_2\in\{\omega^{J(a)}:a\in\No\}\subseteq\Common,
\end{equation}
and every element of $H_j(\No)$ is a strong sum of elements of
$\Common$. These two closures are incomparable and intersect in exactly
$\Common$.
In contrast, both the logarithmic field closure and the strong-sum closure
of the domain equalizer $\Sk$ are all of $\No$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The field $J(\No)$ contains $\Common$ and is logarithmically closed by
\leref{lce:lem:exptransfer}. Take $J(x)\in J(\No)$.
Choose $d>0$ so that $z=\omega^d x$ is purely infinite, exactly as in
\thref{lce:thm:Oz}. Then $\exp z$ is a monomial. Both $\exp z$ and
$\omega^d$ have one-term support and hence lie in $\Sk$.
Set $m_1=J(\exp z)$ and $m_2=J(\omega^d)$; they are monomials in
$\Common$. Exponential transfer gives
\[
 J(x)=\frac{J(z)}{J(\omega^d)}=\frac{\log m_1}{m_2}.
\]
Any logarithmically closed class subfield containing $\Common$ must
therefore contain every $J(x)$, proving minimality and
\eqref{lce:eq:singlelog}.

The field $H_j(\No)$ is strongly closed by \thref{lce:thm:escape}. Every
normal-form term $r\omega^{J(a)}$ in one of its members already lies in
$\Common$, so strong closure of any class containing $\Common$ forces
that member to be included. This proves the other minimality assertion.
Incomparability and the intersection were proved earlier.

For $\Sk$, the identical shift argument without $J$ expresses every
$x\in\No$ as a quotient of the logarithm of a monomial in $\Sk$ by
another monomial in $\Sk$. Also every surreal is a strong sum of real
multiples of monomials, all of which lie in $\Sk$. This proves both
final assertions. The minimality statements are explicit universal
properties; they do not require forming an intersection of a collection
of all classes.
\end{proof}

The two image fields are therefore canonically isomorphic as ordered
valued fields over $\Common$, but their actual placement in $\No$ has
different compatibility with the canonical exponential. Neither their
shared residue field and value group nor their common monomials detect
this difference. The support criterion \eqref{lce:eq:explocus} is different
from the whole-support equalizer criterion: the long-support finite
number $h_\kappa$ lies in the former locus and not the latter.

\section{Supercompactness as a seed-coefficient system}\label{lce:sec:supercompact}

The first support threshold depends only on the critical point. Thus it
cannot by itself distinguish a measurable embedding from a stronger
embedding with the same critical point. A richer family of coefficient
probes does retain the classical seed information used for
supercompactness.

\subsection{Coding an arbitrary index set \src{09}}
The measure coefficient used the special seed $\kappa\in j(\kappa)$. A seed
can instead be an arbitrary element of an embedded set. Let $I$ be a
nonempty set, fix a bijection $e:\theta\to I$ from an ordinal, and put
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:seed-mask}
 Y_{A,e}=\sum_{\substack{\alpha<\theta\\e(\alpha)\in A}}\tau_\alpha
 \qquad(A\subseteq I).
\end{equation}
For $s\in j(I)$ let $\beta_s=(j(e))^{-1}(s)<j(\theta)$.

\begin{theorem}[Seed coefficient formula \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:seed}
For every $A\subseteq I$,
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:seed-coeff}
 \coef{q_{\beta_s}}{J(Y_{A,e})}=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s\in j(A).
\end{equation}
The coefficient is always zero or one. The resulting collection
$U_s=\{A\subseteq I:s\in j(A)\}$ is a $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter on $I$.
It may be principal for some choices of $s$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Applying $j$ to the defining mask gives
\[
 J(Y_{A,e})=\sum_{\substack{\beta<j(\theta)\\j(e)(\beta)\in j(A)}}\tau_\beta.
\]
At the unique position $\beta_s$ its coefficient is one precisely when
$s\in j(A)$. Complements and finite intersections give an ultrafilter, and
intersections indexed by a fixed ordinal below $\kappa$ give
$\kappa$-completeness, as in \thref{lce:thm:measure}. If $s=j(a)$ for some
$a\in I$, then the resulting ultrafilter is principal at $a$, explaining the
qualification.
\end{proof}
Changing the enumeration $e$ merely changes which coefficient position
carries the same seed. It does not change the derived ultrafilter. At the
same time, a single derived ultrafilter need not determine the whole
embedding $j$ or its target $M$; the theorem recovers exactly the
ultrafilter data stated, not an arbitrary ambient embedding from one bit
\src{09}. When $s$ is not of the form $j(a)$, the index $\beta_s$ is a
missing-image index and the coefficient \eqref{lce:sg:eq:seed-coeff} is a
coefficient of the defect $D_j(Y_{A,e})$, as in the next theorem.

\subsection{The supercompact seed \src{07}}
Assume $\lambda\ge\kappa$, $j(\kappa)>\lambda$, and $M$ is closed under
$\lambda$-sequences from $V$. Let
\[
 I=P_\kappa(\lambda)=\{a\subseteq\lambda:\abs a<\kappa\},\qquad
 s=\imset j\lambda.
\]
Closure gives $s\in M$, and in $M$ it has size at most $\lambda<j(\kappa)$,
so $s\in j(I)$. These are the standard hypotheses and seed for a
$\lambda$-supercompactness measure \cite{Neeman}.
Choose a bijection $e:\theta\to I$. Let $\beta_s<j(\theta)$ be the
unique index satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:seedindex}
 j(e)(\beta_s)=s.
\end{equation}
For $A\subseteq I$, define the mask
\[
 m_A=\sum_{\substack{\alpha<\theta\\e(\alpha)\in A}}\omega^{-\alpha}.
\]
The construction depends on the enumeration $e$, which is explicitly part
of the data.

\begin{theorem}[Seed extraction]\label{lce:thm:supercompact}
The index $\beta_s$ is a missing-image index:
$\beta_s\notin\imset j\theta$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{lce:eq:seedcoef}
 \coef{-\beta_s}{D_j(m_A)}
     =\mathbf 1_{\{s\in j(A)\}}\qquad(A\subseteq I).
\end{equation}
Consequently the seed coefficient recovers the fine normal
$\kappa$-complete ultrafilter
\[
 U_s=\{A\subseteq P_\kappa(\lambda):s\in j(A)\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\beta_s=j(\alpha)$, then $s=j(e(\alpha))=j(a)$ for some
$a\in P_\kappa(\lambda)$. Since $\abs a<\kappa$,
\leref{lce:lem:index} gives $j(a)=\imset ja$. But
$s=\imset j\lambda$, so injectivity of $j$ implies $a=\lambda$, a
contradiction. Thus $\beta_s$ is missing from the pointwise index image.

The transformed mask has coefficient 1 at $-\beta_s$ exactly when
$j(e)(\beta_s)=s$ belongs to $j(A)$. The companion has its exponents
only at positions $-j(\alpha)$, so it contributes zero there. This gives
\eqref{lce:eq:seedcoef}.

Ultrafilter and $\kappa$-completeness follow by the same Boolean and
short-index argument as in \thref{lce:thm:measure}, with $s$ replacing
$\kappa$. For each $\xi<\lambda$, the seed contains $j(\xi)$, so
\[
 \{a\in P_\kappa(\lambda):\xi\in a\}\in U_s;
\]
this is fineness. If $A\in U_s$ and $f:A\to\lambda$ satisfies
$f(a)\in a$, then $j(f)(s)\in s=\imset j\lambda$.
Write $j(f)(s)=j(\xi)$ for some $\xi<\lambda$. The fiber where
$f(a)=\xi$ then belongs to $U_s$, proving normality.
\end{proof}

Fine normal $\kappa$-complete ultrafilters on $P_\kappa(\lambda)$ are the
classical ultrafilter characterization of supercompactness, due to Solovay;
see \cite{Neeman}. We have not discovered a new large-cardinal equivalence.
The contribution is that the seed can be read as a coefficient of the
same defect construction, and that its index is provably outside the
termwise image. An intrinsic, enumeration-free coefficient geometry for
these seed systems is a further research question, not a result claimed
here.

\subsection{Covering seeds and exact seeds \src{09}}
For a cardinal $\lambda\ge\kappa$, an ultrafilter $U$ on $P_\kappa(\lambda)$ is
\emph{fine} if $\{a:\xi\in a\}\in U$ for every $\xi<\lambda$. It is
\emph{normal} if, whenever $A\in U$ and $f:A\to\lambda$ satisfies
$f(a)\in a$, there is a $\xi<\lambda$ such that $\{a\in A:f(a)=\xi\}\in U$.
These are the standard ultrafilter notions \cite{Kanamori,Reflecting}.

\begin{proposition}[Covering seeds and exact seeds \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:prop:fine}
Let $I=P_\kappa(\lambda)$ and suppose $s\in j(I)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item If $\imset j\lambda\subseteq s$, then $U_s$ is fine and
$\kappa$-complete.
\item If $s=\imset j\lambda$ belongs to $M$ and is an element of $j(I)$,
then $U_s$ is normal and fine.
\item For a fine ultrafilter on $P_\kappa(\lambda)$, the ultrapower seed
represented by the identity map contains every $j(\xi)$ for $\xi<\lambda$.
If the ultrafilter is normal, that seed is exactly $\imset j\lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each $\xi<\lambda$, let $A_\xi=\{a\in I:\xi\in a\}$. Its image is the
set of $b\in j(I)$ containing $j(\xi)$. A covering seed belongs to each
$j(A_\xi)$, proving fineness; completeness is \thref{lce:sg:thm:seed}.

For normality in (ii), apply $j$ to $f(a)\in a$ on a $U_s$-large set. Then
$j(f)(s)\in s=\imset j\lambda$. Thus $j(f)(s)=j(\xi)$ for some $\xi<\lambda$,
and $s$ lies in the image of the corresponding constant fiber. That fiber
belongs to $U_s$.

For (iii), write the seed as $[\mathrm{id}]_U$ before transitive collapse.
Fineness says $[\mathrm{const}_\xi]_U\in[\mathrm{id}]_U$, which gives one
inclusion after collapse. Conversely, any element of this seed is
represented by a function $f$ for which $f(a)\in a$ on a large set.
Normality makes $f$ constant there, so its collapsed class is some
$j(\xi)$. This proves equality for a normal fine ultrafilter.
\end{proof}
Part (ii) is the ultrafilter half of \thref{lce:thm:supercompact}, proved
there by the same argument; 09 derives the membership $s\in j(I)$ the same
way: a supercompactness embedding with $j(\kappa)>\lambda$ and
$M^\lambda\subseteq M$ has $\imset j\lambda\in M$, and in $M$ this set has
order type and cardinality $\lambda<j(\kappa)$, because its external
increasing enumeration has order type $\lambda$ and the order type of a
well-ordered set in a transitive model is absolute.

The usual ultrafilter characterization of strong compactness is the
existence, for every $\lambda\ge\kappa$, of a fine $\kappa$-complete
ultrafilter on $P_\kappa(\lambda)$. Supercompactness has the corresponding
normal-fine characterization \cite{Kanamori}. Through
\thref{lce:sg:thm:seed}, these existing characterizations become
coefficient-selection principles on the specified omnific masks. The
difference between a seed \emph{covering} $\imset j\lambda$ and an available
seed \emph{equal} to $\imset j\lambda$ is retained explicitly \src{09}.

\begin{warning}[Translation is not a new consistency equivalence \src{09}]
The preceding paragraph still quantifies over set embeddings, seeds and
subset codes. It is a numerical presentation of established large-cardinal
mechanisms, not a characterization in the bare ordered-field language of
$\No$ or the bare ring language of $\Oz$. Eliminating that extra structure
is a substantive further question.
\end{warning}

\section{Strong compactness as a support cover; supercompactness as exact
descent \src{06}}\label{lce:cs:sec:compactness}

\subsection{The set-theoretic principles and their witnesses}
Fix an uncountable cardinal $\kappa$ and a cardinal $\lambda\ge\kappa$, and
use the fine and normal ultrafilters on $P_\kappa(\lambda)$ of the previous
section. To avoid ambiguity about local terminology, in this section the
\emph{$(\kappa,\lambda)$ strong-compactness principle} means existence of a
fine $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter on $P_\kappa(\lambda)$. The corresponding
\emph{supercompactness principle} requires the ultrafilter to be normal as
well. Requiring the respective principles for every cardinal
$\lambda\ge\kappa$ gives the usual global large cardinal properties. The
proofs below also identify the usual sequence-closed elementary embedding
characterization of $\lambda$-supercompactness. The seed construction is
standard \cite{Neeman,Williams}; 06 gives the argument to make the exact
translation to surreal supports explicit. The letter $W$ denotes a fine
ultrafilter in this section only.

\begin{lemma}[Cover and exact-seed characterizations \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:lem:compact-seeds}
The $(\kappa,\lambda)$ strong-compactness principle is equivalent to
existence of a set-ultrapower embedding $j:V\to M$ of critical point
$\kappa$ and a set $s\in M$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:coverseed}
 \imset j\lambda\subseteq s\subseteq j(\lambda),
 \qquad M\models\abs s<j(\kappa).
\end{equation}
Such an embedding necessarily has $j(\kappa)>\lambda$. The supercompactness
principle is equivalent to existence of such a set-ultrapower embedding with
$j(\kappa)>\lambda$ and $\imset j\lambda\in M$, or equivalently with
${}^{\lambda}M\cap V\subseteq M$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Start with a fine $\kappa$-complete ultrafilter $W$ on $P_\kappa(\lambda)$.
Its ultrapower is well-founded: a putative infinite descending membership
chain would, by countable completeness, yield a pointwise descending
membership chain on a nonempty common measure-one set, contradicting
Foundation. Take its transitive collapse. The seed $s=[\mathrm{id}]_W$ is an
element of $j(P_\kappa(\lambda))$, so it is a subset of $j(\lambda)$ of
internal size $<j(\kappa)$. Fineness says that it contains every $j(\alpha)$
for $\alpha<\lambda$.

Completeness fixes all ordinals below $\kappa$: any function into such an
ordinal is constant modulo $W$, since otherwise the intersection of the
complements of all its fibers would be large and empty. Induction on the
ordinal gives the assertion in the transitive collapse. On the other hand,
if $j(\kappa)=\kappa$, then an internal enumeration of $s$ of length
$<\kappa$ would be an external enumeration of a set containing
$\lambda\ge\kappa$ distinct elements, which is impossible. Hence the
critical point is exactly $\kappa$. More generally, an internal cardinal
bound $\nu<j(\kappa)$ for $s$ gives an external injection of $\lambda$ into
$\nu$. Since $\lambda$ is an ambient cardinal, $\lambda\le\nu<j(\kappa)$.

Conversely, suppose an embedding and $s$ as in \eqref{lce:cs:eq:coverseed}
are given, and let $U_s=\{X\subseteq P_\kappa(\lambda):s\in j(X)\}$ (06's
$W_s$). Because $s\in j(P_\kappa(\lambda))$, this is an ultrafilter.
Preservation of intersections indexed below the critical point gives
$\kappa$-completeness. Containment of $\imset j\lambda$ in $s$ gives
fineness. This proves the cover characterization.

If the initial fine ultrafilter is normal, every member of
$[\mathrm{id}]_W$ is represented by a selector $f(a)\in a$ on a large set.
Normality makes it equal to a constant $j(\alpha)$. Fineness gives the
converse inclusion, so $[\mathrm{id}]_W=\imset j\lambda\in M$. Conversely, if
$s=\imset j\lambda$ and $j(\kappa)>\lambda$, then its increasing enumeration
in $M$ has length $\lambda<j(\kappa)$, so it satisfies
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:coverseed}. For a selector $f$, the value $j(f)(s)\in s$
equals $j(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha<\lambda$. The corresponding fiber belongs
to $U_s$, proving normality. Finally apply \leref{lce:cs:lem:seed-closure}
to obtain the sequence-closure formulation. A sequence-closed embedding not
initially presented as an ultrapower also yields this normal ultrafilter by
the same exact-seed argument, and hence yields a set-ultrapower witness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Binary normal forms and coefficientwise covers}
For $t\subseteq\theta$ a set of ordinals, put
\[
 B(t)=\sum_{\beta\in t}\omega^{-\beta},
\]
with its indices in increasing order. A \emph{binary series below $\theta$}
has this form. On this family define
\[
 B(t)\lecoeff B(t')\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\subseteq t'.
\]
Equivalently every coefficient of the difference $B(t')-B(t)$ is
nonnegative. This coefficientwise order is not the ordinary numerical order
on $\No$. In particular a small leading coefficient inequality cannot
replace a support inclusion. For the canonical detector $h_\lambda$ of
\eqref{lce:eq:heta},
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:detector-lifts}
 J(h_\lambda)=h_{j(\lambda)},\qquad
 H_j(h_\lambda)=B(\imset j\lambda).
\end{equation}
The second expression is always defined externally, even when it fails to
belong to $M$.

\begin{theorem}[Support-cover characterization of strong compactness \src{06}]
\label{lce:cs:thm:strong-cover}
The $(\kappa,\lambda)$ strong-compactness principle holds if and only if there
is a set-ultrapower embedding $j:V\to M$ of critical point $\kappa$ and a
binary series $\widehat B\in\No^M$ below $j(\lambda)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:hahn-cover}
 H_j(h_\lambda)\lecoeff\widehat B\lecoeff J(h_\lambda),
 \qquad M\models\abs{\supp(\widehat B)}<j(\kappa).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $s$ is the cover seed in \leref{lce:cs:lem:compact-seeds}, let
$\widehat B=B(s)$, computed in $M$. Absoluteness identifies it with the
ambient binary series, and the containments of sets in
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:coverseed} become the coefficientwise inequalities in
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:hahn-cover}. The support map $\beta\mapsto-\beta$ is a
bijection in $M$, so the internal cardinal bounds are identical.

Conversely, take the normal form of $\widehat B$ inside $M$ and negate its
exponents to obtain $s\subseteq j(\lambda)$ in $M$. The inequalities in
\eqref{lce:cs:eq:hahn-cover} say exactly that $\imset j\lambda\subseteq s$.
The internal bound on the support gives the bound on $s$. Apply
\leref{lce:cs:lem:compact-seeds}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Single-number characterization of supercompactness \src{06}]
\label{lce:cs:thm:super-descent}
The $(\kappa,\lambda)$ supercompactness principle holds if and only if there
is a set-ultrapower embedding $j:V\to M$ with $\crit(j)=\kappa$,
$j(\kappa)>\lambda$, and
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:super-test}
 H_j(h_\lambda)\in\No^M.
\end{equation}
For this equivalence $h_\lambda$ may be replaced by \emph{any} fixed surreal
of support cardinal $\lambda$. It may in particular be replaced by the
omnific integer
\begin{equation}\label{lce:cs:eq:vlambda}
 \zeta_\lambda=\omega^\lambda h_\lambda=\sum_{\alpha<\lambda}\omega^{\lambda-\alpha}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \thref{lce:cs:thm:landing}, \eqref{lce:cs:eq:super-test} is exactly the
assertion $\imset j\lambda\in M$. Now use \leref{lce:cs:lem:compact-seeds}.
The landing criterion depends only on support cardinality, proving the
replacement claim. Every exponent in \eqref{lce:cs:eq:vlambda} is positive,
so $\zeta_\lambda$ is omnific. Alternatively, use the identity
$H_j(\zeta_\lambda)=\omega^{j(\lambda)}H_j(h_\lambda)$ and the fact that the
nonzero multiplying monomial and its inverse both belong to $\No^M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Omnific support covers \src{06}]\label{lce:cs:cor:integer-cover}
The criterion \eqref{lce:cs:eq:hahn-cover} is equivalent to existence of a
purely infinite binary-coefficient omnific integer $\widehat B^+\in\Oz^M$
whose support lies among the exponents $\{j(\lambda)-\beta:\beta<j(\lambda)\}$
and such that
\[
 H_j(\zeta_\lambda)\lecoeff\widehat B^+\lecoeff J(\zeta_\lambda),
 \qquad M\models\abs{\supp(\widehat B^+)}<j(\kappa).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Multiply all three series in \eqref{lce:cs:eq:hahn-cover} by
$\omega^{j(\lambda)}$. This translates the support bijectively, preserves
coefficientwise inclusion and internal cardinality, and makes every
exponent positive. Dividing by that monomial reverses the construction.
\end{proof}
\mergetag{} In this corollary $\lecoeff$ is read on the shifted binary
series, whose exponents are $j(\lambda)-\beta$; 06 uses the same symbol.

\subsection{What the distinction says, and what it does not say}
Strong compactness provides an internally short support \emph{containing}
the transported old indices. Supercompactness makes the exact set of old
indices an internal support. The cover may include extra monomials; exact
descent permits none. This is the mathematical distinction behind the two
criteria, rather than a change of terminology for numerical approximation.

At $\lambda=\kappa$ a normal measure supplies both tests. At
$\lambda=\kappa^+$, an ultrapower indexed just by $\kappa$ cannot supply even
the cover, by \leref{lce:cs:lem:cover-obstruction}. Stronger witnesses, when
they exist, must use sufficiently large index sets. One must not conclude
that a measurable cardinal fails a stronger large-cardinal property merely
because its particular normal-measure ultrapower has the short landing
spectrum from \thref{lce:cs:thm:normal-trichotomy}.

These are equivalences with classical principles, not new consistency
proofs. For example, the global existence of exact-descent witnesses for
every $\lambda$ is equivalent to supercompactness, and hence has exactly that
hypothesis and relative consistency strength. No assertion that any of these
large cardinals exists is made in $\ZFC$ alone \src{06}. For omnific
integers the statements are not merely about an ambient field: the measure
and compactness detectors can themselves be taken to be omnific integers
\src{06}. \mergetag{} 07's \thref{lce:thm:supercompact} reads the same
normal seed as a defect coefficient; 06's theorems read it as membership of
one value in $M$; 09's \prref{lce:sg:prop:fine} records the covering and
exact seeds as ultrafilters. All three are translations of the classical
fine and normal-fine characterizations.

\section{Weak compactness as an omnific prefix principle \src{09}}\label{lce:sg:sec:weak}

There is a second large-cardinal mechanism that does not start with a
measure: compactness of trees. 09 gives its explicit normal-form version,
while retaining the exact topology and the known set-theoretic content.

\subsection{Length-preserving binary codes}
For $\sigma\in2^\alpha$ put
\begin{equation}\label{lce:sg:eq:prefix-code}
 w_\sigma=\sum_{\xi<\alpha}(1+\sigma(\xi))\,\tau_\xi.
\end{equation}
Every coefficient is one or two, so the length of the normal form is
exactly $\alpha$. This convention is important. If one used coefficients
zero and one without also recording the length, appending a zero bit would
not change the number and the coding of tree nodes would not be injective.
For $\beta\le\alpha$, the normal-form truncation of $w_\sigma$ to length
$\beta$ is $w_{\sigma\res\beta}$. Thus the prefix order of binary strings is
literally normal-form truncation on these omnific codes.

\begin{definition}[Omnific prefix compactness at $\kappa$ \src{09}]
Let $\kappa$ be strongly inaccessible. The principle $\OPC(\kappa)$ says:
whenever $\mathcal T$ is a set of numbers of the form $w_\sigma$ with
$\sigma\in2^{<\kappa}$, is closed under normal-form truncation, and has a
node of every length $\alpha<\kappa$, there is a number
\[
 w_b=\sum_{\xi<\kappa}(1+b(\xi))\,\tau_\xi,
 \qquad b\in2^\kappa,
\]
all of whose proper normal-form truncations belong to $\mathcal T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[A transported weak-compactness criterion \src{09}]\label{lce:sg:thm:weak}
For a strongly inaccessible cardinal $\kappa$,
\[
 \OPC(\kappa)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \kappa\text{ has the tree property}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \kappa\text{ is weakly compact}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The last equivalence is the standard inaccessible-cardinal tree
characterization of weak compactness \cite{Kanamori}. For the first,
transport $\mathcal T$ through \eqref{lce:sg:eq:prefix-code}. At level
$\alpha<\kappa$ there are at most $2^{\abs\alpha}<\kappa$ nodes. Hence it is
a $\kappa$-tree, and the tree property yields the desired branch.

Conversely, binary prefix trees suffice; this avoids an implicit
assumption about unique limit nodes in an arbitrary tree. Let $\mathcal R$
be a $\kappa$-tree, with each level $\mathcal R_\alpha$ of size less than
$\kappa$. For each $\alpha$ choose a nonzero ordinal $\delta_\alpha<\kappa$
and an injection $\iota_\alpha:\mathcal R_\alpha\to2^{\delta_\alpha}$. Such
a choice is possible, for example by taking $\delta_\alpha$ large enough to
code a well-ordering of that level. Define
$\Delta_\alpha=\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}\delta_\beta$ by ordinal addition.
Regularity gives $\Delta_\alpha<\kappa$, and these lengths are cofinal in
$\kappa$.

For $t\in\mathcal R_\alpha$, concatenate the labels
$\iota_\beta(t\res\beta)$ of its predecessors at all levels $\beta\le\alpha$,
including the label of $t$ itself. Here $t\res\alpha=t$, and each earlier
restriction denotes the unique predecessor at that height. Take the binary
tree of all prefixes of all such concatenated codes. Its height is
$\kappa$, and each level has fewer than $\kappa$ members by the strong-limit
property. Applying $\OPC(\kappa)$ through \eqref{lce:sg:eq:prefix-code}
yields a binary branch.

At every completed block $\Delta_\alpha$, that branch determines a unique
node of $\mathcal R_\alpha$, since $\iota_\alpha$ is injective. Any
sufficiently long code witnessing the branch prefix confirms that the nodes
determined at two completed blocks are in the predecessor relation. They
therefore form a cofinal branch of $\mathcal R$, as required.
\end{proof}
This is an exact coding of a classical tree principle. It is not a new
proof of the relative consistency of weak compactness. Its value is to show
which additional organization of omnific numbers carries the compactness
information \src{09}.

\subsection{The branch is not an order-topological limit}
The topology naturally associated with \eqref{lce:sg:eq:prefix-code} is the
prefix or truncation topology: a basic neighborhood fixes an initial
normal-form segment. It is not the order topology inherited from $\No$.
Indeed, $\Oz$ is discrete in the inherited order topology because
\[
 (z-1/2,z+1/2)\cap\Oz=\{z\}\qquad(z\in\Oz).
\]
Thus the branch theorem cannot be rephrased as ordinary convergence of the
omnific partial sums. Moreover every proper prefix has support smaller than
$\kappa$, whereas the completed branch has support exactly $\kappa$.
Demanding a branch \emph{inside} $\Sk$ would fail even for the full binary
tree, regardless of weak compactness.

In generalized analysis, Carl--Galeotti--L\"owe relate a specifically
\emph{weak} $\kappa$-Bolzano--Weierstrass principle to the tree property for
suitable ordered fields, including their surreal-based field $\R_\kappa$
\cite{CGL}. Their hypotheses and notion of weak convergence must not be
replaced by the usual real Bolzano--Weierstrass theorem. The prefix
principle above makes a related distinction in a different, explicitly
combinatorial setting \src{09}.

\section{What large cardinals change, and what they do not}\label{lce:sec:interpretation}

\subsection{No change to finite algebraic truth}
Both $J$ and $H_j$ are elementary ordered-field embeddings. Their complex
lifts are elementary embeddings of algebraically closed fields. Consequently
they preserve every first-order formula in those respective field
languages. The distinction made in this article is in additional structure:
normal forms, external supports, indexed strong sums, and their coefficient
probes. It would be wrong to describe \eqref{lce:eq:Dh} as a failure of an
ordinary field axiom.

Likewise, a measurable cardinal does not alter the formula for adding two
specified old surreal sign sequences. It supplies a nontrivial elementary
embedding of the surrounding set universe, and that map moves certain
\emph{entire set-indexed presentations}. The finite identities still
transfer; the pointwise index set does not exhaust the transferred one.

\subsection{Why there is no Kunen contradiction}
The set-theoretic map is $j:V\to M$, not an elementary self-embedding
$V\to V$. After forming surreal numbers in $M$, we compose with the
identity inclusion into the ambient $\No^V$. That inclusion is elementary
in the \emph{ordered-field reduct}, by real closedness. It is not being
claimed elementary in a structure that recovers all of the surrounding
set theory from birthdays or other codes.

This matters when comparing with the repository's birthday-enriched
structures \cite{RepoBirthday}. An order-field embedding can preserve
specific operations and a birthday identity on its image without making
the full ambient birthday expansion an elementary extension. The extra
quantifiers of that expansion can range over new sign codes. Accordingly,
our construction is not an exception to Kunen-type obstructions for a
set-theoretically faithful elementary self-embedding.

09 makes the same point \src{09}. By quantifier elimination for real closed
fields, both $J$ and $H_j$ are elementary in the ordered-field language;
similarly their complexifications are elementary in the language of
algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero \cite{Marker}. For these
definable class structures, the assertion is interpreted formula by
formula, or syntactically via quantifier elimination; it is not an
assertion that a set-sized satisfaction relation for all classes has been
constructed. The birthday function, the omega-map, normal-form truncation
and an entire summable family are additional data, and pure field
elementarity does not force their preservation. In particular, composing
$j:V\to M$ with the inclusion $M\subseteq V$ does not yield an elementary
set-theoretic self-embedding of $V$. The two field embeddings do not evade
the usual restrictions on such self-embeddings. The enriched structures
considered in the repository and the announced Chen--Hamkins--Yang work
concern a stronger language \cite{RepoBirthday,CHY}.

\mergetag{} The birthday-cutoffs report proves the relevant obstruction for
the full birthday-expanded class: every class elementary self-embedding of
it is the identity, by transfer of Kunen's inconsistency
(\lbl{hset:thm:kunen} in
\fn{docs/foundations-and-computation/birthday-cutoffs-and-hereditary-sets}).
Its correspondence between elementary embeddings of $H_\kappa$ and of the

have coordinates outside $\imset j{\bd(x)}$. Neither result is used above,
and neither implies the defect formula.

\subsection{A hierarchy of information}
The first failure cardinal of positive strong summation is $\kappa$.
The first missing coefficient on the masks $h_A$ recovers the normal
measure $U_j$. At a supercompact seed, the wider mask family $m_A$
recovers the associated fine normal measure. These are three different
levels of data:
\[
 \text{critical point}
 \quad\longleftarrow\quad
 \text{normal-measure coefficient functional}
 \quad\longleftarrow\quad
 \text{seed-coefficient systems}.
\]
The arrows mean forgetting information, not an implication that every
normal measure extends to every seed system. In particular, two distinct
normal measures can have the same critical cardinal and the same equalizer
field $\Sk$, while their defect functionals differ by
Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:distinctU}.

\subsection{Choice and the scope of set formation}
The proof does use Choice, already present in $\ZFC$, to enumerate an
arbitrary set in \leref{lce:lem:index} and to use the conventional measure
characterizations. It uses Replacement with the embedding parameter to
form pointwise images and inverse images of \emph{sets}. It does not
choose a basis for the entire proper class $\No$, does not appeal to a
class version of Zorn's lemma, and does not form the collection of all
class endomorphisms as a set or class of its own. A definable normal
ultrapower provides all the specific maps needed.
\mergetag{} The merged material keeps this scope. 09's
\thref{lce:sg:thm:maximal} uses Zorn's lemma only on subsets of the set

\subsection{What is visible at each level of structure \src{09}}
\begin{proposition}[Pure field sentences do not detect large cardinals \src{09}]
\label{lce:sg:prop:purefield}
Every parameter-free sentence about $\No$ in the ordered-field language has
the truth value prescribed by the complete theory of real closed fields.
Every parameter-free field sentence about $\SC$ has the truth value
prescribed by algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero.
Accordingly, an additional large-cardinal axiom cannot change any of these
truth values, provided the same ordinary axioms of surreal arithmetic are
being used.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Real closed fields and algebraically closed fields of fixed characteristic
have complete theories, as follows from quantifier elimination
\cite{Marker}. The canonical surreal structures satisfy the respective
theories. The same syntactic decision of each sentence therefore applies in
every ambient model of the stated foundations.
\end{proof}
This standard observation rules out an overly simple research program: one
cannot discover a parameter-free polynomial-field sentence whose truth in
the surreals switches exactly when a measurable cardinal exists. The
positive theorems instead concern class embeddings, normal-form
coefficients, families of prescribed cardinality and tree branches \src{09}.

\paragraph{Why this is not simply a change of notation \src{09}.} Several
individual ingredients are standard. Nevertheless the exact equalizer puts
them together in a restrictive way. The two maps have identical actions on
every monomial, identical leading coefficients, the same induced action on
values, and infinitesimally close relative outputs. Both are elementary as
pure ordered-field embeddings and both preserve the omnific ring. Yet their
agreement locus is a sharply defined, proper-class real closed subfield,
and they disagree on an explicitly maximal-cardinality independent family
inside one fixed set-sized Hahn field. The discrepancy is not an arbitrary
error term. Its support is the exact complement $j(\theta)\setminus\imset
j\theta$ in an internally transformed ordinal index set. Thus the theorem
converts a specific set-theoretic failure of pointwise surjectivity into a
full normal-form identity, which contains more information than
preservation or failure of one infinite sum alone.

\subsection{What has actually been established \src{06}}
The central conclusions are exact rather than asymptotic. The first
comparison threshold is strict support size $<\kappa$; the normal-measure
landing threshold is nonstrict support size $\le\kappa$. The defect is the
actual series on the omitted indices, not an error estimate. Every nonzero
coefficient survives. The landing criterion holds for each individual input
and is insensitive to coefficients. These features distinguish the results
from a generic construction of an embedding or a general assertion that
large cardinals produce nonstandard algebraic structures.

The exponential theorem identifies a different threshold, on only the
positive-exponent part. This supplies a concrete separation between
preserving all strong sums and preserving global exponential structure: the
Hahn lift has the first property and fails the second on exactly the
specified inputs. The set-transport map has the opposite global pattern: it
preserves Gonshor exponentiation, but its strong summation law stops at the
critical cardinal.

\subsection{Hypotheses which cannot be silently removed \src{06}}
The target must be transitive and the canonical constructions must be
identified through the absoluteness interface. The seed--closure converse
uses a \emph{set-ultrapower} presentation; it was not proved for an
arbitrary elementary embedding into an arbitrary inner model. The
no-landing result above $\kappa$ uses an index set of cardinality $\kappa$,
not merely that the critical point is $\kappa$.

The support sizes in the comparison theorems are external cardinalities.
The bound in the compactness cover theorem is an internal cardinal bound in
$M$. The parameter $\lambda$ in the compactness theorems is an ambient
cardinal. These distinctions prevent several tempting but incorrect
shortcuts in the proofs.

No automorphism classification, Kunen inconsistency result, new
large-cardinal consistency theorem, or first-order characterization in the
pure surreal field is established. In particular, one should not infer a
large cardinal from the existence of an arbitrary non-strong field map; such
behavior already has constructions unrelated to the universe embeddings
used here.

\subsection{Relation to current automorphism work}
Kaplan--Krapp--Serra \cite{KKS} provide a contemporary framework for
strongly additive maps, monomial actions, valuation structure, and
additional surreal operations. We use their definitions and general Hahn
context, not a particular non-strong automorphism construction from that
paper. Our maps are nonsurjective embeddings, and their valuations are
transported by $J$ rather than fixed pointwise. Results that assume
bijectivity or pointwise preservation of the value group must not be
applied to this pair without checking those hypotheses.

No claim is made to settle one of that paper's numbered questions.
Rather, our construction answers a new precisely stated family of
structural questions under an explicit large-cardinal hypothesis. A
specialist comparison with the unpublished details of related projects
would be appropriate before submitting a priority claim.

\section{Further research questions and concrete next steps}\label{lce:sec:questions}

The following questions are proposed by this article. They are not
presented as an established list of famous open problems. Each isolates
something not proved above and indicates what a meaningful answer would
require.

\begin{question}[Optimal consistency strength]\label{lce:q:strength}
Suppose two ordered-field embeddings $P,Q:\No\to\No$ fix $\R$, agree
on all ordinals and monomials, $Q$ is strongly linear, and their equalizer
and image intersection satisfy the formulas of
\thref{lce:thm:intersection} for an uncountable $\kappa$. Suppose also that
$P$ fixes every ordinal below $\kappa$, moves $\kappa$, and commutes with
the omega-map. Must $\kappa$ be measurable, or can such a pair exist
without an ambient elementary embedding of set theory?
\end{question}
The obstruction to a converse is precise: the first defect coefficient
is not automatically a character for the Hadamard product merely because
$P$ is multiplicative for ordinary surreal multiplication.
\thref{lce:thm:measurablecriterion} solves the converse only when the
coefficient-algebra structure and its small-union law are available.

\begin{question}[Classification of strong companions]\label{lce:q:classification}
For an arbitrary real-fixing omega-map-compatible ordered-field embedding
$P$, classify the equalizer of $P$ with the Hahn reindexing map $T_P$.
Which equalizers are cardinal support bounds, and which depend on the
shape, cofinality, or iterated structure of the exponent support?
\end{question}
The construction $T_P$ is always available by \leref{lce:lem:reindex}.
What is special to the present large-cardinal setting is the exact
missing-index formula and the absence of cancellation in it.

\begin{question}[Recovering an embedding from fewer coefficient probes]\label{lce:q:probes}
What is the least family of masks and coefficient positions that determines
$j$ on a specified hereditary-size universe $H_\lambda$? Can normal-form
probes of bounded complexity recover more than the derived normal measure
at the critical point?
\end{question}
Using all subsets of all ordinals with all coefficients would retain
far more information than a single normal measure. The meaningful task
is to specify a restricted family and prove a sharp reconstruction or
nonreconstruction theorem.

\begin{question}[Measure order from defect data]\label{lce:q:mitchell}
Can the Mitchell ordering, or another comparison relation between normal
measures on $\kappa$, be recovered from a natural algebraic relation between
their coefficient functionals $\chi_j$, without naming the ambient
ultrapower models as extra predicates?
\end{question}
Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:distinctU} gives injective detection of individual
measures. It gives no criterion for their relative position in an
ultrapower hierarchy.

\begin{question}[Intrinsic supercompact seed systems]\label{lce:q:seeds}
Can the seed construction of \thref{lce:thm:supercompact} be formulated
without choosing an enumeration of $P_\kappa(\lambda)$? Is there a natural
coordinate-free compatibility law for the resulting systems as $\lambda$
varies that detects the appropriate closure of $M$?
\end{question}
Any proposal must preserve fineness and normality and must distinguish
an actual seed in $j(P_\kappa(\lambda))$ from an arbitrary missing-image
index. Merely having many extra coefficients does not imply
supercompactness.

\begin{question}[Joint algebraic dependence of the images]\label{lce:q:jointdependence}
Are $J(\No)$ and $H_j(\No)$ linearly disjoint over $\Common$? More
modestly, for a finite tuple $x$, can one decide the transcendence degree
of $H_j(\No)(J(x))$ over $H_j(\No)$ from the missing-image supports?
\end{question}
Every single element outside the intersection is transcendental over
the opposite real-closed image field. That does not decide joint algebraic
independence of a tuple, and the exact intersection theorem is not a
substitute for a disjointness proof.

\begin{question}[Alternating the two closures]\label{lce:q:closures}
Starting with $\Common$, what field is obtained by alternating logarithmic
field closure and external strong-sum closure? Do finitely many alternations
stabilize, and can the result be characterized directly from the exponent
image $J(\No)$?
\end{question}
\thref{lce:thm:twoclosures} determines each first closure exactly. Their
incomparability makes the mixed closure a genuinely different question.
One should distinguish closure under sums from closure under all normal-form
subseries or the omega-map.

\begin{question}[Differential compatibility]\label{lce:q:differential}
For a specified canonical surreal derivation, what is the exact locus on
which $J$ or $H_j$ commutes with it? Can a differential analogue of
\eqref{lce:eq:explocus} be expressed using support or the iterated logarithmic
structure of the exponents?
\end{question}
The exponential locus is settled here. A derivation may depend on further
normal-form and logarithmic data, so its preservation is not a consequence
of being an ordered-field embedding. No differential claim should be
imported without verifying the actual chosen derivation.

\begin{question}[Definability in weaker signatures]\label{lce:q:definability}
Which of $\Sk$, $\Common$, and the coefficient projection $\Pi_j$ are
definable in natural reducts of the structure with the maps $J,H_j$?
Can any of the large-cardinal information survive after the embedding
symbols and the Hadamard product are forgotten?
\end{question}
With both maps named, $\Sk$ is the immediate equalizer formula. The
question concerns losing symbols, not that trivial definition. Pure
field elementarity alone cannot recover the external strong-sum threshold.

\begin{question}[Extending the coefficient-projection isomorphism]\label{lce:q:projection}
Under what explicit additional hypotheses does the isomorphism
$\Pi_j:J(\No)\to H_j(\No)$ extend to an automorphism of a larger
specified surreal subfield, or of the full ordered field? Which extension
requirements on monomials, simplicity, or exponentiation obstruct it?
\end{question}
The given isomorphism fixes the common field and all common monomials.
It is not globally multiplicative on $\No$, so its existing definition
cannot simply be reused as the desired extension.

\begin{question}[Forcing and lifted embeddings]\label{lce:q:forcing}
If an embedding $j$ lifts through a forcing extension, which of its old
coefficient defects are preserved and which new supports appear? Can the
lifting criterion be expressed through a commuting square of strong
companions and coefficient projections?
\end{question}
This would connect the present arithmetic construction to the repository's
cross-universe saturation work. One must first specify the ground and
extension, verify the lift, and distinguish new index sets from changed
cardinalities; no automatic preservation is asserted.

\begin{question}[A reusable formal theorem package]\label{lce:q:formal}
Can the exact defect, image intersection, and closure theorems be formalized
in a way that separates abstract ordered Hahn algebra from the set-theoretic
embedding interface? What is the smallest verified interface sufficient
for the elementary-embedding application?
\end{question}
The proposed architecture in Appendix~\ref{lce:app:formalization} treats the
critical-point and absoluteness obligations as explicit theorems to be
proved, not as silently trusted axioms. This would make the genuinely
new algebraic consequences independently reusable and auditable.

\subsection{Questions of manuscript 06 \src{06}}
06's questions are proposed directions, not assertions that each is a
previously published open problem. They are designed to distinguish new
mathematical content from immediate reformulations of the theorems already
proved.

\begin{question}[An intrinsic converse without a universe embedding]\label{lce:cs:q:converse}
Can a suitably enriched surreal structure characterize which pairs of maps
arise as $(J,H_j)$ from an elementary universe embedding?
\end{question}
The coefficient formula recovers a candidate measure from a map on the codes
$\tilde h_A$, but field multiplication alone does not give coefficientwise
intersection of binary supports. Determine a minimal additional operation or
relation---for example controlled coefficientwise multiplication on a
designated coding family---which makes the ultrafilter laws follow
intrinsically. A genuinely new converse would have to construct the
ultrafilter or embedding, not assume it in the language from the outset.
(Compare Question~\ref{lce:q:strength} and Question~\ref{lce:sg:q:converse};
the criteria \thref{lce:thm:measurablecriterion} and
\thref{lce:sg:thm:numerical} add exactly such a coefficientwise operation.)

\begin{question}[Which landing spectra occur?]\label{lce:cs:q:spectra}
For a fixed critical point $\kappa$, classify the downward-closed classes
$\mathcal L(j)$ realized by set-ultrapower embeddings. How does this
spectrum constrain the least possible cardinality of an index set?
\end{question}
The normal-measure answer is exactly the cardinals at most $\kappa$. The
index-size obstruction supplies a necessary bound whenever an internally
short cover exists. Beyond these statements, investigate which successor,
limit, or singular endpoints can occur and whether two embeddings with the
same landing spectrum can have substantially different support-cover
spectra.

\begin{question}[Extender targets and failure of the seed--closure converse]
\label{lce:cs:q:extender}
For elementary embeddings not generated by one set-ultrapower seed, what
additional hypothesis makes $\imset j\lambda\in M$ imply closure under all
external $\lambda$-sequences of elements of $M$?
\end{question}
The surreal landing theorem still reduces to seed membership, but the proof
of sequence closure uses a single evaluation parameter $[\mathrm{id}_I]$. An
extender presentation may require a family of parameters. A useful theorem
would identify the necessary seed support and express it as a strengthened
descent condition for a family of normal forms.

\begin{question}[Higher coefficients and derived measures]\label{lce:cs:q:higher}
What structural information about an embedding is recoverable from the
whole coefficient profile of $J(\tilde h_A)$ as $A$ varies?
\end{question}
The explicit normal form already gives
$\coef{-\beta}{J(\tilde h_A)}=1+\mathbf 1_{j(A)}(\beta)$ for every
$\beta<j(\kappa)$. Thus it records the ultrafilters derived from all such
seeds. The nontrivial next problem is to recover relations among these
ultrafilters---factor maps, equivalences, or iteration data---from relations
among coefficient observables, rather than merely restating the membership
formula. (Related: Questions~\ref{lce:q:probes} and~\ref{lce:sg:q:seedhull}.)

\begin{question}[Iteration and multilevel transport defects]\label{lce:cs:q:iteration}
Develop a coherent comparison theory for iterated elementary embeddings and
successive Hahn lifts, keeping track of which intermediate values actually
belong to the next domain.
\end{question}
A formal composition of termwise lifts is straightforward in a common
ambient universe, but iteration through inner models introduces descent
conditions. Determine whether a hierarchy of first missing indices gives an
invariant of the iteration independent of the chosen presentations. Any
proposed cocycle identity must specify these domains before performing
algebra with the defects. (Related: Question~\ref{lce:sg:q:iteration}.)

\begin{question}[Canonical derivations and transport]\label{lce:cs:q:derivations}
How do $J$ and $H_j$ interact with the Berarducci--Mantova derivation or
other specified strongly additive surreal derivations?
\end{question}
First prove the necessary absoluteness or naturality theorem for the chosen
derivation; it is not a consequence of field elementarity alone. Then
compare differentiation of the missing-index formula with transport of
derivatives. The goal is an exact commutation locus comparable to
\eqref{lce:eq:explocus}, not a generic statement about differentiating
formal sums. (Related: Questions~\ref{lce:q:differential}
and~\ref{lce:sg:q:exp}.)

\begin{question}[Birthday cost of a critical-support detector]\label{lce:cs:q:birthday}
For a fixed $\kappa$, what is the least birthday of a surreal with $\kappa$
nonzero normal-form terms? What is the least birthday of an omnific witness
to failure of exponential compatibility?
\end{question}
06's theorems use the explicit $h_\kappa$ and $\zeta_\kappa$, but do not
optimize their birthdays. Sharp answers would connect this support-based
theory to the repository's birthday investigations without confusing the
two invariants. Normal-form length, support cardinal, and sign length must
be tracked separately throughout.

\begin{question}[Forcing and lifted coefficient codes]\label{lce:cs:q:forcing}
When an elementary embedding lifts through a forcing extension, how do the
code families $\tilde h_A$ and their defect coefficients change for new
subsets $A\subseteq\kappa$?
\end{question}
For old subsets the original measure code is fixed data, while new subsets
require an extension of the measure. Formulate a preservation theorem
distinguishing persistence of old coefficients, extension of the coding
domain, and changes to the landing spectrum. This could connect the present
theory to forcing across surreal universes without assuming that
ground-model and extension normal-form families coincide. (Related:
Question~\ref{lce:q:forcing}.)

\begin{question}[Local restrictions and omega-preserving automorphisms]
\label{lce:cs:q:local}
Which set-sized restrictions of $J$ extend to automorphisms of a surreal
structure containing the monomial map, or both the monomial map and Gonshor
exponentiation?
\end{question}
The full $J$ cannot be such an automorphism by \prref{lce:cs:prop:proper}. A
local extension theorem, or a concrete local obstruction, would nevertheless
bear on the richer automorphism questions in \cite{KKS}. Homogeneity of the
pure ordered field does not by itself settle the question for the
monomial-enriched structure.

\subsection{Questions of manuscript 09 \src{09}}
09's questions are proposed by that manuscript. Except where a published
source is explicitly named, no claim is made that each is a previously
formulated open problem. Their purpose is to specify what a next theorem
should say and which extra hypotheses would have to be controlled.

\begin{question}[Removing coefficientwise multiplication]\label{lce:sg:q:converse}
How far can the extra coefficient algebra be removed from a numerical
criterion for measurability? In the compatible-field-embedding criterion of
\thref{lce:sg:thm:numerical}, is preservation of Hadamard multiplication on
all masks implied by weaker assumptions about order, the omega-map,
truncations, and $<\kappa$-strong additivity?
\end{question}
A useful first target is preservation only of disjointness of mask
supports, together with binary-valued coefficients at the designated probe.
Any proposed converse should explicitly explain how it yields a \emph{set}
of subsets of $\kappa$ and why that set is closed under fewer than $\kappa$
intersections. Bare field elementarity cannot supply this by itself.

\begin{question}[The bare exact-equalizer principle]\label{lce:sg:q:equalizer}
For which uncountable cardinals $\kappa$ can there exist ordered field
embeddings $E,H:\No\to\No$ agreeing on every monomial, with $H$ fully strong
and $\Eq(E,H)=\Sk$, without assuming that $E$ comes from a set embedding?
What is the consistency strength of adding omega compatibility for $E$?
\end{question}
The current theorem proves one implication from measurability. It does not
prove that the equalizer statement alone implies measurability, nor that it
is impossible at an ordinary uncountable cardinal. Separating the algebraic
condition from an encoded ultrafilter would clarify the genuine
large-cardinal content of the main construction. (Related:
Question~\ref{lce:q:strength}, which adds more conditions on the pair.)

\begin{question}[Iterated discrepancies]\label{lce:sg:q:iteration}
Given an iteration of normal ultrapowers, can its successive missing-index
sets be described by a normal-form calculus that is functorial under
composition? Which terms distinguish the pointwise image of a pointwise
image from the full iterated image of an ordinal index set?
\end{question}
A concrete starting problem is a two-step iteration with critical points
$\kappa$ and $j(\kappa)$ inside the appropriate models. Each step has its own
formula \eqref{lce:eq:defectformula}; the difficulty is comparing the strong
lift of the composite with the composite of the two strong lifts when their
domains and internal series differ. Statements about all maps must specify
the universe in which each summability assertion is evaluated.

\begin{question}[What one coefficient forgets]\label{lce:sg:q:seedhull}
Which additional families of seed coefficients determine the seed hull or
an extender associated with $j$, rather than only one normal measure? Can
the passage from one seed to several seeds be expressed by explicitly
compatible operations on the corresponding omnific masks?
\end{question}
A single normal measure does not in general specify every possible
embedding that derives it. A successful reconstruction theorem should state
exactly which factor of the embedding is recovered. Compatibility under
projections between finite tuples of seeds is a natural first milestone.

\begin{question}[Mitchell-order information]\label{lce:sg:q:mitchell}
Can comparison or iteration of measures be recognized in a system of
coefficient probes that is stable under the induced surreal embeddings? In
particular, can membership of one measure in another ultrapower be encoded
without merely reinstating the full membership relation as an extra
predicate?
\end{question}
This is deliberately stronger than coding each ultrafilter separately. The
problem is to represent the relation between the codes in a useful surreal
language, not simply to assign another number to another set. (Related:
Question~\ref{lce:q:mitchell}.)

\begin{question}[Beyond $2^{<\kappa}=\kappa$]\label{lce:sg:q:AD}
When $2^{<\kappa}>\kappa$, what is the largest family of explicitly
constructed positive omnific masks algebraically independent over $\Sk$? Can
one still attain $2^\kappa$ in $K_\kappa$ by replacing the almost-disjoint
branch construction?
\end{question}
\thref{lce:sg:thm:AD} applies to every available $\kappa$-almost-disjoint
family, so one route is combinatorial. Another would replace private
coordinates by a stronger support-separation argument. A complete answer
should distinguish limitations of the particular masks from limitations on
the transcendence degree itself.

\begin{question}[Singular support cutoffs]\label{lce:sg:q:singular}
For singular uncountable $\kappa$, which analogues of the equalizer and
summability-threshold results can be realized by abstract Hahn embeddings?
Can cofinality and support cardinality produce different equality loci?
\end{question}
The field $\Sk$ is already real closed for singular $\kappa$
(\thref{lce:sg:thm:shortfield}). An ordinary nontrivial elementary embedding
of the universe cannot have such a critical point under the present
hypotheses. Thus an affirmative algebraic construction at singular $\kappa$
would necessarily use a mechanism different from the normal-ultrapower one.

\begin{question}[Other coefficient fields and ordered groups]\label{lce:sg:q:fields}
How much of \thref{lce:sg:thm:AD} survives for Hahn fields with coefficients
of positive characteristic, nondiscrete valuation on the coefficients, or
exponent groups without the displayed free rational subspace?
\end{question}
The selected coefficient in the proof has multiplicity one, so it avoids one
familiar source of cancellation in positive characteristic. The main
remaining issue is to formulate the correct small-support coefficient base
and to supply enough independent exponent coordinates. This offers a
plausible extension beyond surreal numbers without changing the core
separation argument.

\begin{question}[Exponential and differential refinements]\label{lce:sg:q:exp}
How do the pair $(J,H_j)$ and the discrepancy interact with the Gonshor
exponential and with a specified surreal derivation? Can one calculate an
exact equality locus for either operation analogous to
Corollary~\ref{lce:cor:omegalocus}?
\end{question}
09 proves no compatibility with these operations, and adds that an approach
through their recursive definitions must verify absoluteness, and an
approach through transseries must verify all relevant summability
conditions. The questions about enriched automorphisms in \cite{KKS} provide
context, but those automorphisms and the generally nonsurjective embeddings
here should not be identified. \mergetag{} \emph{Status.} For the
exponential this is answered by \thref{lce:thm:expdefect} (06, 07): the
exact locus of $H_j\circ\exp=\exp\circ H_j$ is
$\abs{\supp(p(x))}<\kappa$, and $J$ commutes with $\exp$ everywhere
(\leref{lce:lem:exptransfer}), with the absoluteness of the recursion
verified there. The derivation half is open, as in
Questions~\ref{lce:q:differential} and~\ref{lce:cs:q:derivations}.

\begin{question}[Set-sized restrictions]\label{lce:sg:q:setsized}
For which pairs of birthday cutoffs can the main pair of embeddings be
restricted to set-sized real closed surreal fields, retaining the same
support equalizer on the common domain? What are the optimal birthday
bounds on the images and the discrepancy?
\end{question}
The identity $\bd(J(x))=j(\bd(x))$ is a starting bound for one map. The
strong lift may need a different support-to-birthday estimate. A complete
theorem should name its cutoffs and closure properties rather than appeal
to the proper-class theorem informally. (Related:
Question~\ref{lce:cs:q:birthday}.)

\begin{question}[Omnific arithmetic without masks]\label{lce:sg:q:masks}
Can any part of the measure-recovery mechanism be expressed using only
ordinary omnific addition, multiplication, order and a small number of
named maps or parameters? In particular, how much of normal-form
coefficient extraction is definable in such a structure?
\end{question}
The lack of nontrivial idempotents in an integral domain makes a naive
identification with a Boolean algebra impossible. One could instead seek
interpreted quotients or definable families. This direction should be
compared with the repository's omnific definability and quotient projects,
not assumed to follow from them.

\begin{question}[Higher compactness in truncation spaces]\label{lce:sg:q:compactness}
Can stronger branch principles on two-parameter or seed-indexed families of
omnific normal forms distinguish weak compactness, strong compactness and
supercompactness using one coherent truncation-space framework?
\end{question}
The distinction between a covering seed and an exact seed suggests a
specific invariant to retain. Any topological formulation must specify the
topology and the allowed cardinalities of covers or branches; the ordinary
inherited order topology on $\Oz$ is too discrete for the prefix argument.
(06's support covers and exact descent, Section~\ref{lce:cs:sec:compactness},
are one such pair of invariants, stated with embeddings rather than
branches.)

\begin{question}[A checked exact-equalizer theorem]\label{lce:sg:q:formal}
Can the main theorem be formalized with an abstract elementary-embedding
interface whose only large-cardinal inputs are the critical-point and
normal-form transport properties, while separately proving the bridge from a
genuine ultrapower?
\end{question}
A practical first deliverable would formalize Hahn subseries partition,
strong exponent lifts, and the private-coordinate independence argument in a
set-sized Hahn field. The second deliverable would formalize
\leref{lce:lem:index} and the exact comparison theorem for an
ordinal-indexed normal-form transport. Only the third would connect that
interface to actual surreal sign sequences and ultrapowers. This ordering
would keep the algebraic verification independent of the largest
foundational infrastructure task. (Related: Question~\ref{lce:q:formal}.)

\section{Conclusion}
The difference between $j(I)$ and $\imset jI$ becomes a literal normal-form
difference in surreal arithmetic. Comparing the elementary-induced map
with the strongly linear map having the same monomial values exposes that
difference without sacrificing finite algebra.

The outcome is stronger than a single counterexample to summation
preservation. There is an exact equalizer, an exact intersection, a
canonical isomorphism of two incomparable immediate image fields, and an
intrinsic positive-family escape threshold. Their common field has two
different canonical enlargements: logarithmic closure produces the
elementary image, while strong-sum closure produces its Hahn companion.
One coefficient of the difference recovers the derived normal measure;
shifting the code makes it purely infinite and omnific. The same mechanism
works over complex coefficients and at supercompact seeds.

These statements answer the structural questions posed in this article
under a measurable-cardinal hypothesis. They are offered for independent
verification and comparison with the literature, not as a claim that the
large-cardinal hierarchy or a named longstanding conjecture has been
settled. The strongest next step is to test the converse: how much of the
set-theoretic embedding can be forced by the arithmetic signature alone?

\src{06} The more global issue is not whether the termwise series
exists---it always exists in the ambient surreal field---but whether it
exists in the target model. For every individual normal form that question
is exactly seed membership. In a set-ultrapower it is exactly sequence
closure. Normal measures give the strict/non-strict two-threshold picture,
while fine and normal fine measures give support covers and exact descent
at larger cardinals. The constructions remain meaningful in the surcomplex
field and in the omnific-integer rings, where explicit coefficient codes
recover normal measures. Thus large-cardinal hypotheses have a precise
manifestation in arithmetic of canonical infinite supports, without invoking
a new finite algebraic law, a blanket automorphism theorem, or an unproved
consistency claim.

\src{09} The short-support field is not merely an incidental agreement set.
It is real closed, has a canonical omnific integer part, and admits an
explicit large algebraically independent family of long-support omnific
extensions. At a measurable cardinal these are simultaneously independent
witnesses to the failure of full strong additivity, and the measure itself
appears as a coefficient of the same sort of number. The remaining
challenge is to reduce the extra language needed to observe these effects.
Normal-form support, coefficientwise multiplication, truncation trees and
seed data make the set-theoretic information visible. The pure field
language does not. The exact boundary between those two levels is a
concrete research program rather than a reason to conflate ordinary surreal
arithmetic with the entire large-cardinal hierarchy.
