\begin{abstract}
For an integer $d\ge2$, exponent dilation sends
$\sum a_\gamma t^\gamma$ to $\sum a_\gamma t^{d\gamma}$.
We prove that a nonconstant Hahn series algebraically dependent with its
own dilate over the constant field must belong to a single ordinary Laurent
series subfield. This holds in characteristic zero over every ordered
abelian value group, without an Archimedean or divisibility assumption.
If $F(y,S_dy)=0$, the necessary ramification is at most $\deg_YF$.
The proof combines Newton--Puiseux branches with a formal B\"ottcher
coordinate and a least-support-term argument; every evaluation is justified
by strong Hahn summability, not by a contraction principle.

For the negative-support ring this becomes an exact classification:
first-order autonomous algebraic dependence occurs precisely for polynomials
in one monomial. The least degree in the dilated variable is exactly the
degree in the primitive monomial. Consequently every infinite-support
omnific integer is algebraically independent of each of its nontrivial
integer dilates. We also obtain a finite-profile description for a fixed
algebraic equation, an exact Newton-weight existence test, complete
rational-map solutions, and a sharp infinitesimal-tail obstruction to
omnific solutions. Higher order behaves differently: an infinite-support
omnific integer has exact autonomous order two, and examples realize every
finite order. A research agenda identifies the remaining classification,
effectivity, positive-characteristic, and formalization problems.

\mtag{} Two later manuscripts (Sections~19--27) prove that support rank at
least $m$ forces any $m$ distinct positive dilates to be algebraically
independent, so that the autonomous order of a finite-support omnific integer
is its rational support rank, in every rank.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research position and the questions answered}\label{adr:sec:position}

An omnific integer can have a transfinite normal form, infinitely many
nonzero coefficients, and exponents at mutually incomparable Archimedean
scales. A polynomial in one monomial has none of this support complexity.
The principal question of this article is whether an algebraic relation
between a number and its own exponent-dilated copy can distinguish these
situations.

The dilation is
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:dilationintro}
 S_d\!\left(\sum_\alpha a_\alpha\omega^{g_\alpha}\right)
   =\sum_\alpha a_\alpha\omega^{d g_\alpha}.
\end{equation}
It is a field automorphism on $\No$ and $\SC$, and fixes ordinary constants.
It is \emph{not} the map $y\mapsto y^d$. For example,
\[
 S_2(\omega+1)=\omega^2+1,
 \qquad (\omega+1)^2=\omega^2+2\omega+1.
\]
The question is about polynomial equations over the ordinary constant
field, with $S_d$ additionally available. It is not a statement that an
infinite omnific integer is algebraic over $\R$.

\subsection{The main answer in omnific notation}

For $x\in\Oz\setminus\Z$ and $d\ge2$, we prove the equivalence
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:headline}
 \begin{split}
 &x\text{ and }S_dx\text{ are algebraically dependent over }\R\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x=P(\omega^\delta)\text{ for some }\delta>0,
 \quad P\in\R[T],\quad P(0)\in\Z.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The same statement holds for Gaussian omnific integers with $\C$ and
$\Z[i]$ in place of $\R$ and $\Z$. Thus an infinite normal-form support is
an unconditional obstruction to \emph{every} first-order autonomous
algebraic relation. A finite support can also obstruct a relation: its
positive exponents must be commensurable, not merely comparable in size.

There is an exact quantitative refinement. Normalize $\delta$ so that the
nonzero positive exponents of $x$ generate $\delta\Z$. Then, writing
$x=P(\omega^\delta)$ and $M=\deg P$,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:headline-degree}
 \min\{\deg_YF:0\ne F\in\R[X,Y],\ F(x,S_dx)=0\}=M.
\end{equation}
In particular, the answer is independent of the chosen integer $d\ge2$.
This is a classification of a substantial class of equations, rather than
one specially selected identity.

\subsection{Literature and the novelty boundary}

Hahn summation and its support calculus are classical \cite{Neumann}.
Newton--Puiseux factorization is classical; Mannaa--Coquand give a modern
constructive treatment \cite{MC}. Formal B\"ottcher coordinates are also
classical; the needed characteristic-zero theorem is recorded explicitly
by Salerno--Silverman \cite{SS}. We use these inputs openly and give an
explicit coordinate construction adapted to strong Hahn evaluation.

There is an established literature on autonomous Mahler equations,
including Nishioka--Nishioka \cite{NN}, and on nonlinear formal Mahler
solutions \cite{GG}. Chyzak--Dreyfus--Dumas--Mezzarobba \cite{CDDM} and
Faverjon--Roques \cite{FR} address linear Mahler equations and generalized
series. The later works of Faverjon--Poulet \cite{FP} and
Faverjon--Roques \cite{FRPurity} develop basis algorithms and generalized
Mahler-series structure for linear systems; they are cited here for
context from their abstracts, not used as proof dependencies.
These works prevent any claim that Mahler equations, B\"ottcher
coordinates, or non-Puiseux solutions are new topics. The contribution
proposed here is the \emph{arbitrary-rank support-collapse theorem}, the
resulting exact omnific criterion, and their quantitative synthesis.
\wtag{} The full text of \cite{NN} was read when the manuscript joined the
collection. It records a 1983 criterion of Mahler \cite{Mahler83} for the
existence of a convergent power-series solution of a constant-coefficient
relation $P(f(z),f(z^d))=0$, a rank-one, positive-support antecedent of the
existence statements of \cref{adr:sec:profiles}; see
Appendix~\ref{adr:app:literature}.

For omnific arithmetic, L'Innocente--Mantova \cite{LM} provide the relevant
published factorization framework. Our result is not a factorization theorem
and does not settle the remaining general factorization conjectures.
The user's encyclopedia link \cite{Wiki} is useful orientation, but none

\section{Hahn fields, normal forms, and the two support directions}
\label{adr:sec:setup}

\begin{definition}
Let $k$ be a field and $\Gamma$ an ordered abelian group. The full Hahn
field $\K=k((t^\Gamma))$ consists of formal sums
\[
 y=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}a_\gamma t^\gamma,
 \qquad a_\gamma\in k,
\]
whose support is a well-ordered subset of $\Gamma$. For $y\ne0$, set
$v(y)=\min\supp y$ and let $\lc(y)$ be the coefficient there. Put
$v(0)=+\infty$.
\end{definition}

Throughout the main classification, $\operatorname{char}k=0$ and $d$ is
an ordinary integer at least two. No order on $k$ is required. Dilation
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:Sd}
 S_d\Bigl(\sum a_\gamma t^\gamma\Bigr)=
 \sum a_\gamma t^{d\gamma}
\end{equation}
is a strong injective field endomorphism; it is an automorphism when
$\Gamma$ is $d$-divisible. Its elementary valuation law is
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:val-dilation}
 v(S_dy)=d\,v(y).
\end{equation}

The valuation ring and its maximal ideal are
\[
 \mathcal O=\{y:v(y)\ge0\},\qquad
 \mathfrak m=\{y:v(y)>0\}.
\]
For $y\in\mathcal O$, its residue is its coefficient at zero, an element
of $k$. In contrast, define
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:A}
 \A=\{y\in\K:\supp y\subseteq\Gamma_{\le0}\}.
\end{equation}
This is a subring, but it is \emph{not} the valuation ring. A nonconstant
element of $\A$ has negative valuation. The absence of \emph{positive}
exponents, not just a bound on the leading exponent, will be decisive.

\begin{definition}
For $\delta\in\Gamma_{>0}$, the notation $k((t^\delta))$ means the image
of the ordinary Laurent series field $k((Z))$ under $Z\mapsto t^\delta$.
Thus its elements have support contained in $\delta\Z$, bounded below
in the integer indexing. A series with this property is called
\emph{cyclically supported} here.
\end{definition}

Cyclic support is stronger than support in one Archimedean class and
stronger than a one-dimensional rational span. The set
$\{-1,-1/2,-1/4,\ldots\}$ spans a one-dimensional $\Q$-space but is not
contained in any cyclic subgroup of $\Q$.

\subsection{Translation to surreal numbers}

Conway normal forms use decreasing powers of $\omega$. The correspondence
with the increasing Hahn convention is
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:omega-bridge}
 t^\gamma\longmapsto\omega^{-\gamma}.
\end{equation}
For any set-sized ordered additive subgroup $\Gamma\subseteq\No$, it
identifies $\R((t^\Gamma))$ with the corresponding normal-form subfield
of $\No$; complexifying gives $\C((t^\Gamma))\subseteq\SC$.
Every individual surreal or surcomplex number is contained in such a
workspace: take the divisible subgroup generated by its set-sized support.
The usual normal-form arithmetic is the algebraic input here
\cite{LM,Repo}.

The omnific integers have the description
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:Oz}
 \Oz=\left\{n+\sum_{\alpha<\lambda}r_\alpha\omega^{g_\alpha}:
 n\in\Z,\ r_\alpha\in\R,\ g_\alpha>0,
 \ (g_\alpha)_{\alpha<\lambda}\text{ strictly decreasing}\right\},
\end{equation}
with set-sized support and zero coefficients omitted. Thus the only
additional restriction beyond the real negative-support ring is the
integrality of the coefficient at zero. For $\Goz$ the coefficients are
complex and that constant lies in $\Z[i]$.

\subsection{Size and topology conventions}

All field-theoretic proofs below take place in a set-sized Hahn field.
The statements for $\No$ and $\SC$ are then elementwise consequences.
No proper-class polynomial ring, class-sized tensor product, or
class-indexed analytic sum is required.

Also, $v(x)>0$ need not imply that $v(x^n)$ tends cofinally to infinity
in $\Gamma$. For example, in the lexicographically ordered group
$\Q\oplus\Q$, the multiples of $(0,1)$ never reach $(1,0)$.
Nevertheless the geometric series in $x$ is strongly summable. This
separation between support summability and topological convergence is
essential throughout.

\section{The support calculus and a monomial recognizer}
\label{adr:sec:support}

A family $(f_i)$ of Hahn series is \emph{strongly summable} when the union
of its supports is well ordered and every exponent occurs in the support
of only finitely many family members. Its sum is then defined coefficientwise.

\begin{lemma}[Formal evaluation]\label{adr:lem:evaluation}
Let $x\in\mathfrak m$. Every formal series $f(Z)\in k[[Z]]$ can be evaluated
at $x$ as a strong Hahn sum. This gives a $k$-algebra homomorphism.
For $x\ne0$ it extends to an injective map $k((Z))\to\K$, and
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:evaluation-value}
 v(f(x))=\ord_Z(f)\,v(x)\qquad(f\ne0).
\end{equation}
Formal composition is respected whenever the inner series has zero
constant coefficient. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:commute-eval}
 S_d(f(x))=f(S_dx).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Neumann support lemma says that the monoid generated by a well-ordered
subset of $\Gamma_{>0}$ is well ordered and that each exponent has only
finitely many representations by finite words from that subset, allowing
all word lengths \cite{Neumann}. Apply it to $\supp x$. It justifies the
family of powers and the regroupings for products and formal composition.
There are only finitely many negative powers in a Laurent series, so these
cause no extra infinite-sum issue.

If the initial term of $f$ is $aZ^m$, its evaluation has initial valuation
$m\,v(x)$: every later power has strictly larger valuation. This proves
\eqref{adr:eq:evaluation-value} and injectivity. Exponent dilation preserves
well ordering, finite occurrence, and coefficientwise sums, proving
\eqref{adr:eq:commute-eval}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Constants are relatively algebraically closed]
\label{adr:lem:constants}
Every element of $\K$ algebraic over $k$ belongs to $k$. In particular,
every nonconstant Hahn series is transcendental over $k$, and
$\operatorname{Fix}(S_d)=k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero algebraic element $z$ satisfies a polynomial with nonzero
constant and leading coefficients in $k$. If $v(z)>0$, the constant term
is the unique term of least valuation; if $v(z)<0$, the highest-degree
term is. Both are impossible. Hence $v(z)=0$. Let $c\in k$ be its
residue. If $z-c\ne0$, this is an algebraic element of positive valuation,
again impossible.

For a nonconstant fixed element $y$, use $x=y-c$ if $v(y)\ge0$, with
$c$ its residue, and $x=1/y$ if $v(y)<0$. In both cases $x\ne0$ has
positive valuation and is fixed. The equation $d\,v(x)=v(x)$ contradicts
$d>1$ in an ordered group.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Monomial recognizer]\label{adr:lem:monomial}
Suppose $w\ne0$ and $S_dw=w^d$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:monomial-recognizer}
 w=a t^\eta,\qquad a\in k^\times,\quad a^{d-1}=1,
 \quad\eta\in\Gamma.
\end{equation}
Conversely every such monomial is a solution.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $w=a t^\eta(1+\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon\in\mathfrak m$.
Initial coefficients give $a=a^d$. Dividing the original identity by
$a t^{d\eta}$ gives
\[
 1+S_d\varepsilon=(1+\varepsilon)^d.
\]
If $\varepsilon\ne0$ and $\rho=v(\varepsilon)>0$, then the nonconstant
part of the left side has valuation $d\rho$, whereas that of the right
side has valuation $\rho$: its initial coefficient is multiplied by the
nonzero scalar $d$. Since $d\rho>\rho$, this is impossible.
\end{proof}

The least-term proof eliminates possible tails at \emph{all} other scales
at once. It neither examines the terms sequentially nor assumes that an
iteration approaches its limit in the ambient valuation topology.
The $d=2$ instance is the repository's monomial recognizer
\cite{RepoDilation}. \wtag{} That is \lbl{dsup:lem:monomialrecognition},
proved there by the same least-term comparison; the equality
$\operatorname{Fix}(S_d)=k$ of \cref{adr:lem:constants} is
\lbl{dsup:eq:dilationfixed} (\cref{adr:sec:collection}).

\section{The classical ramification input and its degree bound}
\label{adr:sec:puiseux}

We use the Newton--Puiseux theorem in the following standard form:
if $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, then
\[
 \bigcup_{e\ge1}k((U^{1/e}))
\]
is an algebraic closure of $k((U))$ \cite{MC}. Thus every nonzero
polynomial in $k((U))[V]$ splits into linear factors there. The next
lemma spells out both the quantitative point and its transport into a
Hahn field.

\begin{lemma}[Evaluated Puiseux branch]\label{adr:lem:puiseux}
Assume $k$ algebraically closed, $\Gamma$ divisible, and $x\in\K$ with
$v(x)=\gamma>0$. Let $0\ne G(U,V)\in k[U,V]$ have $V$-degree $D$, and
suppose $G(x,z)=0$. Then there exist an integer $1\le e\le D$, an
$e$th root $u$ of $x$ in $\K$, and $\phi(Z)\in k((Z))$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:puiseux-branch}
 z=\phi(u),\qquad G(Z^e,\phi(Z))=0.
\end{equation}
Moreover $v(u)=\gamma/e$, and
$v(z)=\ord_Z(\phi)\,\gamma/e$ when $z\ne0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply Newton--Puiseux to $G(U,V)$ over $k((U))$. In a common extension
$k((U^{1/N}))$ it factors as a nonzero leading coefficient times finitely
many factors $V-\psi_j(U^{1/N})$.

An $N$th root of $x$ exists in the Hahn field without using its full
algebraic closedness: write $x=a t^\gamma(1+\varepsilon)$, choose
$b^N=a$, and use
\[
 b t^{\gamma/N}(1+\varepsilon)^{1/N},
\]
where the binomial series is evaluated by \cref{adr:lem:evaluation}.
Evaluation at this root sends the Puiseux factorization to a genuine
factorization in $\K$. Its leading coefficient is nonzero by the
injectivity in \cref{adr:lem:evaluation}. Consequently $z$ equals one of
the evaluated roots.

Choose the smallest denominator $e$ for that root. Its series can be
written as $\phi(U^{1/e})$, with $e\mid N$; the corresponding power of
the chosen $N$th root gives $u$. Minimality means that the automorphisms
$U^{1/e}\mapsto\zeta U^{1/e}$, for $\zeta^e=1$, give exactly $e$
distinct conjugates of this series. Indeed a stabilizing $\zeta$ must
satisfy $\zeta^j=1$ for every exponent $j$ occurring in $\phi$, and
minimality says their common divisor with $e$ is one. All these conjugates
are roots of $G$. Hence $e\le D$. The valuation statements follow from
\cref{adr:lem:evaluation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The bound is on the denominator of \emph{one selected branch}, not on a
common denominator for every root. A common splitting denominator can be
larger than $D$. Confusing these two denominators would invalidate the
sharp degree theorem below.
\end{remark}

\section{A support-safe B\"ottcher construction}\label{adr:sec:bottcher}

The formal-coordinate theorem in this section is classical
\cite[Proposition~1]{SS}. We include a construction so that no unstated
convergence hypothesis enters its Hahn-field application. All sums in
the construction are first formed in the ordinary formal ring $k[[Z]]$.

\begin{lemma}[Normalized formal coordinate]\label{adr:lem:bottcher}
Let $h(Z)=Z^dU(Z)$, where $U\in1+Zk[[Z]]$ and
$\operatorname{char}k=0$. There is a unique
$b(Z)\in Z+Z^2k[[Z]]$ with
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:bottcher-b}
 b(h(Z))=b(Z)^d.
\end{equation}
Its compositional inverse $H$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:bottcher-H}
 H(Z^d)=h(H(Z)).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:bottcher-log}
 L(Z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{1}{d^{n+1}}
       \log U\bigl(h^{\circ n}(Z)\bigr),\qquad
 b(Z)=Z\exp L(Z).
\end{equation}
The $n$th summand has order at least $d^n$ unless it is zero, so each
coefficient receives only finitely many contributions. Reindexing gives
\[
 dL(Z)=\log U(Z)+L(h(Z)).
\]
Therefore
\[
 b(h(Z))=Z^dU(Z)\exp L(h(Z))=Z^d\exp(dL(Z))=b(Z)^d.
\]
The linear coefficient of $b$ is one, so it has a unique compositional
inverse, which gives \eqref{adr:eq:bottcher-H}.

For uniqueness, any normalized coordinate has the form $Z\exp M(Z)$.
The difference $D=L-M$ satisfies $D(h)=dD$. A nonzero $D$ of positive
order would have different orders on these two sides, namely
$d\ord D$ and $\ord D$. Thus $D=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[All Hahn solutions of a superattracting germ]
\label{adr:prop:germ}
Let $h(Z)=A Z^d+O(Z^{d+1})\in k[[Z]]$, $A\ne0$, and let $x\ne0$
have positive valuation. Then $S_dx=h(x)$ is solvable with prescribed
leading coefficient $a\in k^\times$ and valuation $\delta\in\Gamma_{>0}$
if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:leading-a}
 A a^{d-1}=1.
\end{equation}
For each such pair there is exactly one solution:
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:germ-profile}
 x=aH_a(t^\delta),
\end{equation}
where $H_a$ is the normalized inverse B\"ottcher coordinate of
$h_a(Z)=a^{-1}h(aZ)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Leading coefficients force \eqref{adr:eq:leading-a}. With this condition,
$h_a(Z)=Z^d+O(Z^{d+1})$. Write $u=x/a$, and let $b_a$ be the coordinate
of \cref{adr:lem:bottcher}. Strong evaluation and its commutation with $S_d$
give
\[
 S_d(b_a(u))=b_a(S_du)=b_a(h_a(u))=b_a(u)^d.
\]
The left-hand argument $b_a(u)$ has valuation $\delta$ and leading
coefficient one. By \cref{adr:lem:monomial} it is exactly $t^\delta$.
Inversion yields \eqref{adr:eq:germ-profile} and uniqueness. Conversely,
\eqref{adr:eq:bottcher-H}, evaluated strongly at $t^\delta$, verifies that
this expression is a solution.
\end{proof}

This is an exhaustive statement in the full Hahn field. The coordinate
series is ordinary, but the element to which it is applied may initially
have arbitrary support. The recognizer proves that every extra support
scale disappears after the coordinate change.

\section{The arbitrary-rank support-collapse theorem}\label{adr:sec:main}

\begin{theorem}[First-order autonomous support rigidity]
\label{adr:thm:collapse}
Let $k$ be any field of characteristic zero, $\Gamma$ any ordered abelian
group, and $d\ge2$. Suppose $y\in\K\setminus k$ and
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:F-main}
 F(y,S_dy)=0,\qquad 0\ne F\in k[X,Y].
\end{equation}
Define a positive-valuation coordinate
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:coordinate}
 x=\begin{cases}
 y-c,&v(y)\ge0,\quad c=\operatorname{res}(y),\\
 1/y,&v(y)<0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \gamma=v(x)>0.
\end{equation}
Then for some integer $e$ with $1\le e\le\deg_YF$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:collapse}
 \delta=\gamma/e\in\Gamma,
 \qquad y\in k((t^\delta)).
\end{equation}
In particular, the support is finite or has order type $\omega$ and is
contained in a cyclic subgroup of $\Gamma$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First assume $k$ algebraically closed and $\Gamma$ divisible. Let
$D=\deg_YF$. Since $y$ is transcendental over $k$, $D\ge1$.
The equation becomes $G(x,S_dx)=0$ for a nonzero polynomial $G$ with
$\deg_VG\le D$. In the finite case use
\[
 G(U,V)=F(c+U,c+V).
\]
In the infinite case, if $a=\deg_XF$, use
\[
 G(U,V)=U^aV^D F(U^{-1},V^{-1});
\]
monomial factors can be removed. These substitutions give nonzero
polynomials, and $x,S_dx$ are nonzero.

Apply \cref{adr:lem:puiseux}. There are $1\le e\le D$, an element $z$ with
$z^e=x$, and a Laurent series $\phi$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:mainbranch}
 S_dx=\phi(z),\qquad v(z)=\gamma/e.
\end{equation}
But $v(S_dx)=d\gamma$. Consequently
\[
 \ord_Z\phi=de,
 \qquad \phi(Z)=B Z^{de}(1+u(Z)),
 \quad B\ne0,\quad u\in Zk[[Z]].
\]
Choose an $e$th root of $B$ and form a formal $e$th root of this series.
Every such root has the form
\[
 h(Z)=A Z^d(1+u(Z))^{1/e}\in k[[Z]],\qquad A^e=B.
\]
Since $(S_dz)^e=S_dx=\phi(z)$, the ratio between $S_dz$ and one evaluated
root $h(z)$ is an $e$th root of unity. Multiplying $A$ by that root of
unity gives an $h$ with the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:z-germ}
 S_dz=h(z).
\end{equation}
By \cref{adr:prop:germ},
$z=aH(t^{\gamma/e})$ for an ordinary formal power series $H$ and a
nonzero constant $a$. Thus $x=z^e$ belongs to
$k((t^{\gamma/e}))$, as does $y=c+x$ or $y=1/x$. This proves the
assertion in the auxiliary algebraically closed, divisible setting.

For general $k$ and $\Gamma$, embed the Hahn field coefficientwise and
exponentwise into
\[
 \overline{k}((t^{\Gamma_{\Q}})),
 \qquad \Gamma_{\Q}=\Gamma\otimes_{\Z}\Q
\]
with its natural ordered divisible hull. The proof gives an integer
$e\le D$ and support contained in $(\gamma/e)\Z$. The intersection
\[
 \Gamma\cap(\gamma/e)\Z=m(\gamma/e)\Z
\]
for a positive integer $m$ dividing $e$: it is a nonzero subgroup of a
cyclic group and contains $\gamma$. Put $e'=e/m$. Then
$1\le e'\le D$, $\gamma/e'\in\Gamma$, and the original support is
contained in $(\gamma/e')\Z$. Its coefficients are still in $k$, by
uniqueness of Hahn coefficients. The support is bounded below in that
cyclic group. Renaming $e'$ as $e$ proves \eqref{adr:eq:collapse}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does not assert]
Membership in a cyclic Laurent subfield is a necessary condition for
\eqref{adr:eq:F-main}, not a claim that every ordinary Laurent series satisfies
some autonomous Mahler equation. Nor does the support conclusion turn
strong evaluation into ambient valuation convergence. The exponent group
can have arbitrarily high rank even when the individual solution is
forced into one cyclic subgroup.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite profiles, coefficient descent, and an existence test}
\label{adr:sec:profiles}

The proof gives more than a necessary support condition. For a fixed
polynomial equation it supplies a finite collection of ordinary formal
shapes; the only remaining continuous scale parameter is the monomial
at which a shape is evaluated.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-profile classification]\label{adr:thm:profiles}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero, $d\ge2$, and
$0\ne F\in k[X,Y]$. There is a finite, possibly empty collection
\[
 \mathscr P(F,d)\subset k((Z))\setminus k
\]
such that for every nonzero divisible ordered group $\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:profiles}
 \{y\in\K\setminus k:F(y,S_dy)=0\}
  =\{f(t^\delta):f\in\mathscr P(F,d),\ \delta\in\Gamma_{>0}\}.
\end{equation}
Each profile is constructed from a Puiseux branch and a normalized
B\"ottcher coordinate. A profile at infinity has pole order at most
$\deg_YF$. The list need not be free of duplicates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Remove all factors $Y-X$ from $F$, obtaining $F_0$. A nonconstant solution
cannot satisfy $S_dy=y$ by \cref{adr:lem:constants}, so this removal does not
change the nonconstant solution set. The polynomial $F_0(X,X)$ is not
identically zero. Consequently there are only finitely many possible
finite residues $c$, namely its roots.

For each such $c$, form $G(U,V)=F_0(c+U,c+V)$. Also form the reciprocal
polynomial at infinity as in the proof of \cref{adr:thm:collapse}. For each
of these finitely many polynomials, choose its finitely many Puiseux roots
$V=\phi(U^{1/e})$. Keep only the nonzero branches satisfying
$\ord_Z\phi=de$. For each kept branch, there are finitely many formal
$e$th roots
\[
 h(Z)^e=\phi(Z),\qquad h(Z)=A Z^d+O(Z^{d+1}),
\]
and finitely many constants $a$ with $Aa^{d-1}=1$. Let $H_a$ be the
coordinate in \cref{adr:prop:germ}. Put on the list
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:profile-shapes}
 f(Z)=c+(aH_a(Z))^e
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 f(Z)=(aH_a(Z))^{-e},
\end{equation}
according to the finite or infinite chart.

The proof of \cref{adr:thm:collapse} shows that every nonconstant solution
has one of these forms after evaluation at a positive monomial.
Conversely let $z=aH_a(t^\delta)$. Then $S_dz=h(z)$, so with $x=z^e$,
\[
 S_dx=h(z)^e=\phi(z),\qquad G(x,S_dx)=0.
\]
Undoing the chart gives the original equation. The pole-order bound is
the branch denominator bound $e\le\deg_YF$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Coefficient descent]\label{adr:cor:coefficients}
Let $k_0\subseteq L$ be characteristic-zero fields and
$0\ne F\in k_0[X,Y]$. If $y\in L((t^\Gamma))\setminus L$ satisfies
$F(y,S_dy)=0$, then every Hahn coefficient of $y$ is algebraic over $k_0$.
In particular, an equation over $\overline{\Q}$ admits no nonconstant
solution having a coefficient transcendental over $\overline{\Q}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Work in an algebraic closure $\overline L$ and let $k'$ be the algebraic
closure of $k_0$ inside $\overline L$. Construct the finite profiles over
$k'$. Their Puiseux factorizations still split the relevant polynomials
over $\overline L((U))$; the roots of unity and leading-coefficient roots
are already in $k'$. Thus the same exhaustion proof gives profiles with
all coefficients in $k'$, even for a solution originally in the larger
coefficient field. Evaluation at a monomial changes exponents, not
coefficients. The original coefficients lie in $L\cap k'$.
\end{proof}

This is a descent statement for a \emph{fixed coefficient field of the
equation}. It does not contradict the existence of $a\omega^\delta$ as
a first-order solution over $\R$ for arbitrary real $a$: the corresponding
equation generally contains $a$ among its coefficients.

\begin{theorem}[Exact local Newton-weight criterion]\label{adr:thm:newton}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero and $\Gamma\ne0$
divisible. For
\[
 0\ne G(U,V)=\sum g_{ij}U^iV^j\in k[U,V],
\]
there is a nonzero $x\in\mathfrak m$ satisfying $G(x,S_dx)=0$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:newton-weight}
 \min\{i+dj:g_{ij}\ne0\}
 \quad\text{is attained by at least two monomials.}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $v(x)=\gamma>0$, the valuation of a term is $(i+dj)\gamma$.
A unique least term cannot cancel, proving necessity.

For sufficiency use the ordinary Newton polygon, or equivalently the
Puiseux factorization. The weighted initial polynomial
\[
 I(W)=\sum_{i+dj=w}g_{ij}W^j,
 \qquad w=\min(i+dj),
\]
has at least two terms. Distinct active monomials have distinct $j$.
After its monomial factor is removed, it has a nonzero constant term and
positive degree, hence a nonzero root over $k$.
Factoring $G$ into Puiseux linear factors shows that these nonzero roots
are exactly the leading coefficients of the branches whose $U$-order is
$d$. More explicitly, a factor with root order below $d$ contributes a
nonzero constant to the weighted initial product, one above $d$ contributes
$W$, and one equal to $d$ contributes $W-A$ with $A\ne0$. The product
has a nonzero root precisely in the last case.

Thus some branch is $V=\phi(U^{1/e})$ with $\ord_Z\phi=de$.
The construction in \cref{adr:thm:profiles} gives a nonzero positive-valuation
solution at every chosen positive monomial scale.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scale-independent existence]\label{adr:cor:existence}
For a fixed $F$ over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field,
existence of a nonconstant solution is the same in every nonzero divisible
Hahn value group. It is decided by finitely many tests
\eqref{adr:eq:newton-weight}: the finite diagonal-residue charts after removal
of $Y-X$ factors, and the chart at infinity. For algebraic-number
coefficients this is an effective decision procedure.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The charts and their number are as in \cref{adr:thm:profiles}.
Each chart has a solution exactly when \cref{adr:thm:newton} applies.
The test uses polynomial arithmetic, roots of one ordinary polynomial,
and comparison of finitely many integer weights. With exact algebraic
coefficients, all the required equality tests and root calculations are
effective. A nonzero divisible ordered group always has a positive element.
\end{proof}

The criterion decides \emph{existence}, not whether an arbitrary encoded
infinite Hahn series satisfies the equation. It also does not make an
arbitrary complex coefficient into computable input.

\section{The exact classification in the negative-support ring}
\label{adr:sec:negative}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial-in-one-monomial criterion]
\label{adr:thm:negative}
Let $\operatorname{char}k=0$, $d\ge2$, and $y\in\A\setminus k$.
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item $y$ and $S_dy$ are algebraically dependent over $k$;
\item $y=P(t^{-\delta})$ for some $\delta\in\Gamma_{>0}$ and $P\in k[T]$;
\item the support of $y$ is finite and its nonzero exponents generate a
      cyclic subgroup of $\Gamma$.
\end{enumerate}
If a relation $F(y,S_dy)=0$ is given, the polynomial in \textup{(ii)}
can be chosen of degree at most $\deg_YF$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $y\notin k$ and its support has no positive exponent, $v(y)<0$.
Put $\gamma=-v(y)$. By \cref{adr:thm:collapse}, for some $e\le\deg_YF$ its
support lies in $(\gamma/e)\Z$. It also lies between $-\gamma$ and zero.
There are only $e+1$ points of this cyclic subgroup in that interval.
Consequently
\[
 y=\sum_{j=0}^e a_jt^{-j\gamma/e},
\]
which proves (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii) with the degree bound.

For (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i), write $T=t^{-\delta}$. Then
$y=P(T)$ and $S_dy=P(T^d)$ belong to the one-variable rational function
field $k(T)$, whose transcendence degree is one. More explicitly, the
nonzero polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:resultant}
 R_P(X,Y)=\Res_T(P(T)-X,\ P(T^d)-Y)
\end{equation}
vanishes on $(y,S_dy)$. Its degree in $Y$ is $\deg P$, so it is not
identically zero. Conditions (ii) and (iii) are equivalent by taking a
positive generator of the subgroup generated by the finitely many support
exponents.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dilation independence]\label{adr:cor:d-independent}
For $y\in\A$, dependence of $y,S_dy$ for one integer $d\ge2$ is
equivalent to dependence for every such integer. If $y$ has infinite
support, every pair $y,S_dy$ is algebraically independent over $k$.
The same independence conclusion holds for a finite support of rational
rank at least two.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The support condition in \cref{adr:thm:negative} does not mention $d$.
For a finite subset of a torsion-free abelian group, the generated subgroup
is cyclic exactly when its rational span has dimension at most one.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the support restriction is indispensable]
A Laurent series can have infinitely many positive exponents even when
it is cyclically supported. Intersecting with $\A$ cuts off all of them.
Thus the theorem is not a cardinality argument. Its force comes from a
\emph{discreteness conclusion} followed by a \emph{two-sided support bound}.
A merely bounded, nondiscrete support can still be infinite.
\end{remark}

\section{Sharp algebraic degrees and recovery of a monomial}
\label{adr:sec:degree}

\begin{definition}
For a nonconstant finite-support $y\in\A$ satisfying the criterion, let
$\delta>0$ be the positive generator of the group generated by its
nonzero exponents. Write $y=P(t^{-\delta})$. The polynomial $P$ is
\emph{primitive in its exponents}: the greatest common divisor of its
positive supported degrees is one. Define $M(y)=\deg P$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The exact relation degree]\label{adr:thm:degree}
For every $d\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:exact-degree}
 \min_{\substack{0\ne F\in k[X,Y]\\F(y,S_dy)=0}}\deg_YF=M(y).
\end{equation}
Moreover, if $T=t^{-\delta}$ is the primitive monomial, then
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:recover}
 k(y,S_dy)=k(T).
\end{equation}
The irreducible relation has bidegree
$(\deg_X,\deg_Y)=(dM(y),M(y))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $M=M(y)$ and let a relation of $Y$-degree $D$ be given.
The degree-bounded conclusion of \cref{adr:thm:negative} supplies
$y=Q(t^{-\delta'})$ of degree $e\le D$.
The support-generated subgroup $\delta\Z$ is contained in
$\delta'\Z$, so $\delta=m\delta'$ for an integer $m\ge1$.
Comparison of the largest support magnitude gives
$M\delta=e\delta'$, hence $e=Mm\ge M$. Therefore $D\ge M$.
The resultant \eqref{adr:eq:resultant} gives the reverse inequality.

For the field statement, the extension
$k(T)/k(P(T))$ has degree $M$. One elementary proof is that
$P(T)-X$ is irreducible in $k(X)[T]$: its ideal in $k[T,X]$ has quotient
$k[T]$, and Gauss's lemma applies. If
$L=k(P(T),P(T^d))$, the just-proved minimum degree says
\[
 [L:k(P(T))]=M.
\]
The tower law then forces $L=k(T)$. Finally
$[k(T):k(P(T^d))]=dM$, which is the degree of the irreducible relation
in $X$; its degree in $Y$ is $M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An ordinary polynomial field identity]
\label{adr:cor:polynomial-field}
Let $P\in k[T]\setminus k$, and let
$g=\gcd\{j\ge1:[T^j]P\ne0\}$. In characteristic zero,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:polynomial-field}
 k(P(T),P(T^d))=k(T^g)\qquad(d\ge2).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $P(T)=Q(T^g)$, with $Q$ primitive in its exponents, and apply
\cref{adr:thm:degree} to $Q$ in an ordinary Laurent Hahn field.
\end{proof}

This corollary can be read entirely in classical rational-function
algebra. We give it as a consequence and consistency check of the support
argument, not as a certified priority claim in polynomial decomposition
theory. The restriction to polynomials is real: for
$P(T)=T+T^{-1}$ one has $P(T^2)=P(T)^2-2$, and the generated field is
$k(T+T^{-1})$, a proper subfield of $k(T)$.

\begin{example}[A genuinely quadratic relation]
For $y=T^2+T$ and $d=2$, the primitive degree is two. The relation
\begin{align}\label{adr:eq:sample-resultant}
 0={}&Y^2-(2X^2+6X+2)Y\\
     &+X^4+2X^3+2X^2\nonumber
\end{align}
is obtained by elimination and has the least possible $Y$-degree.
There is no rational function $R\in k(X)$ with $S_2y=R(y)$.
Thus the implicit algebraic classification is strictly broader than the
explicit rational-map classification in \cref{adr:sec:rational}.
\end{example}

\section{Consequences for surreal and Gaussian omnific integers}
\label{adr:sec:omnific}

\begin{theorem}[Omnific dilation classification]\label{adr:thm:omnific}
Let $d\ge2$ and $x\in\Oz\setminus\Z$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item $x,S_dx$ are algebraically dependent over $\R$;
\item $x,S_dx$ are algebraically dependent over $\C$ inside $\SC$;
\item for some $\delta\in\No_{>0}$,
      $x=P(\omega^\delta)$ with $P\in\R[T]$ and $P(0)\in\Z$;
\item $x$ has finite normal-form support, and its positive exponents are
      commensurable.
\end{enumerate}
With the primitive exponent step, its least relation degree is $\deg P$.
For $x\in\Goz\setminus\Z[i]$, the identical classification holds with
$P\in\C[T]$, $P(0)\in\Z[i]$, and dependence over $\C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a set-sized divisible subgroup of $\No$ containing the exponent
support, and use \eqref{adr:eq:omega-bridge}. Apply
\cref{adr:thm:negative,adr:thm:degree}. In the real case, a complex polynomial
relation splits into its real and imaginary coefficient polynomials;
at least one is a nonzero real relation. Conversely a real relation is
a complex one. The coefficient at zero imposes exactly the integer or
Gaussian-integer condition and no condition on the other coefficients.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All distinct forward dilates]\label{adr:cor:pairs}
If $x$ is an infinite-support omnific or Gaussian omnific integer, then
for every $d\ge2$ and every pair of integers $0\le a<b$,
\[
 S_d^a x\quad\text{and}\quad S_d^b x
\]
are algebraically independent over $\C$ (and over $\R$ in the real case).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first member still has infinite support and the second is its
$S_{d^{b-a}}$ dilate. Apply \cref{adr:thm:omnific}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Finite but incommensurable]
The number
\[
 x=\omega^{\sqrt2}+\omega+1
\]
is an omnific integer, and $x,S_dx$ are algebraically independent for
every integer $d\ge2$: the positive exponents $1,\sqrt2$ are
$\Q$-linearly independent. This particular $x$ is also prime by the
published theorem of L'Innocente--Mantova \cite[Theorem~B]{LM}.
Its primality is cited, not proved or claimed as a contribution here.
The dilation independence is a different property.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Comparable scales do not suffice]
Both
$\omega^{\sqrt2}+\omega$ and $\omega^{3/2}+\omega$
have two finite real exponents in the same Archimedean class of the
exponent group. Only the second is a polynomial in one monomial:
with $T=\omega^{1/2}$ it is $T^3+T^2$. Its least relation degree is
three; the first has no relation at all.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[One rational span, unbounded denominators]
The valid normal form
\[
 u_d=\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{d^{-n}}
\]
has infinite support of order type $\omega$. All its exponents lie in
$\Q$, but not in any cyclic subgroup. It is algebraically independent
of $S_du_d$. In \cref{adr:sec:order} we show that its first three transforms
satisfy an exact polynomial relation. Pairwise independence must not be
confused with independence of a longer tuple.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A coefficient-field obstruction]
Let $a\in\R$ be transcendental over $\Q$ and $\delta>0$. Although
$x=a\omega^\delta$ satisfies
$S_dx=a^{1-d}x^d$ over $\R$, it has no first-order autonomous polynomial
relation over $\Q$. This follows from \cref{adr:cor:coefficients}.
The constants allowed in a relation materially change the answer.
\end{example}

\section{Complete solutions for rational maps}\label{adr:sec:rational}

We now classify the explicit equations $S_qy=R(y)$, with
$q\in\Q_{>1}$, $\Gamma$ divisible, and $R\in k(Z)$ nonconstant.
The polynomial relation theorem allows implicit ramification; in this
explicit case there is no such support ramification.

\begin{theorem}[Rational-map classification]\label{adr:thm:rational}
Assume $k$ algebraically closed of characteristic zero and $\Gamma\ne0$
divisible. A nonconstant solution of
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:rational}
 S_qy=R(y)
\end{equation}
exists exactly when $R$ has a fixed point $c\in\mathbb P^1(k)$ whose
local degree is $q$. In particular, $q$ must be an integer $d\ge2$.
For every such fixed point, let
\[
 M_c(Y)=Y-c\quad(c\in k),\qquad M_\infty(Y)=1/Y,
\]
and write
\[
 h=M_c\circ R\circ M_c^{-1}
   =A Z^d+O(Z^{d+1}).
\]
All nonconstant solutions associated with $c$ are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:rational-solutions}
 y=M_c^{-1}\bigl(aH_a(t^\gamma)\bigr),
 \qquad \gamma\in\Gamma_{>0},\quad Aa^{d-1}=1,
\end{equation}
where $H_a$ is the normalized inverse B\"ottcher coordinate of
$a^{-1}h(aZ)$. Constant solutions are simply the finite fixed points of
$R$ where it is defined.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $v(y)\ge0$, let $c$ be its residue. A pole of $R$ at $c$ would give
$v(R(y))<0$, impossible. Reducing the equation modulo the maximal ideal
therefore gives $R(c)=c$. For $v(y)<0$, the equation requires $\infty$
to be a fixed point. In either case $u=M_c(y)$ is nonzero of positive
valuation, and
\[
 S_qu=h(u).
\]
If $m\ge1$ is the local degree, comparison of valuations gives
$q\,v(u)=m\,v(u)$, hence $q=m$. Since $q>1$, this is an integer $d\ge2$.
The germ now has the form in \cref{adr:prop:germ}, which gives all solutions
and their converse. Because $k$ is algebraically closed, the required
leading coefficients $a$ exist.
\end{proof}

In particular, not every nonlinear rational map admits a nonconstant
solution. The relevant property is a \emph{superattracting fixed point
of exactly the dilation degree}, not just a nonlinear term somewhere
in the formula. Over a non-algebraically-closed field the same description
applies whenever the fixed point and the required leading-coefficient
root lie in that field. For real solutions, these are genuine real-root
conditions, not automatic consequences of the complex theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Rational-map rigidity in the negative-support ring]
\label{adr:thm:rational-negative}
Let $\operatorname{char}k=0$, $y\in\A\setminus k$, and $d\ge2$.
If $S_dy=R(y)$ for $R\in k(Z)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:affine-monomial}
 y=\alpha t^{-\gamma}+b,
 \qquad
 R(Z)=\alpha^{1-d}(Z-b)^d+b,
\end{equation}
with $\alpha\in k^\times$, $b\in k$, and $\gamma\in\Gamma_{>0}$.
Conversely every such pair satisfies the equation.
For omnific solutions $b\in\Z$, and for Gaussian omnific solutions
$b\in\Z[i]$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Writing $R=A/B$ in reduced form, the equation
$B(y)S_dy-A(y)=0$ has degree one in the dilated variable. The denominator
is nonzero since $y$ is transcendental over $k$. By the degree bound in
\cref{adr:thm:negative}, $y$ is affine in one monomial, giving the first
formula. Substitute this into the equation, and use transcendence of
the monomial to obtain the displayed rational-function identity.
The converse is direct substitution. The constant-term restrictions
are exactly the definitions of the two omnific rings.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Simultaneous rational equations]\label{adr:cor:simultaneous}
Suppose the same nonconstant $y\in\A$ satisfies
$S_py=P(y)$ and $S_qy=Q(y)$ for integers $p,q\ge2$ and rational functions
$P,Q$. Then, with one common $\alpha,b,\gamma$,
\[
 y=\alpha t^{-\gamma}+b,\quad
 P(Z)=\alpha^{1-p}(Z-b)^p+b,\quad
 Q(Z)=\alpha^{1-q}(Z-b)^q+b.
\]
In particular, $P$ and $Q$ commute. Even without the negative-support
restriction, simultaneous nonconstant solutions force $P\circ Q=Q\circ P$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{adr:thm:rational-negative}; the representation is unique from
its leading monomial and constant term. For the last assertion, commute
the dilations and use the fact that they fix the coefficients:
$(P\circ Q)(y)=(Q\circ P)(y)$. Transcendence of $y$ turns equality at
$y$ into equality of rational functions.
\end{proof}

\section{A sharp infinitesimal-tail obstruction}\label{adr:sec:tail}

The rational-map obstruction has a quantitative form. It identifies the
first infinitesimal term that prevents an otherwise legitimate surreal
solution from being an omnific integer.

\begin{theorem}[First forbidden tail]\label{adr:thm:tail}
Let $P\in\C[Z]$ have degree $d\ge2$, with leading coefficients
$p_d,p_{d-1}$. Put
\[
 b=-\frac{p_{d-1}}{d p_d},\qquad
 p_d\alpha^{d-1}=1,
 \qquad Q(X)=\alpha^{-1}(P(\alpha X+b)-b).
\]
Then $Q$ is monic and centered. If $Q\ne X^d$, write
\[
 Q(X)=X^d+cX^{d-r}+\text{lower powers},
 \qquad 2\le r\le d,\quad c\ne0.
\]
For each positive scale $\gamma$, the infinite solution with leading
coefficient $\alpha$ has expansion, with $T=t^{-\gamma}$,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:tail}
 y=\alpha T+b-\frac{\alpha c}{d}T^{1-r}+O(T^{-r}).
\end{equation}
Its first positive-valuation term has valuation exactly $(r-1)\gamma$.
In particular it does not belong to the negative-support ring.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The normalization eliminates the coefficient of $X^{d-1}$ and makes the
leading coefficient one. The infinity case of \cref{adr:thm:rational}
gives a unique normalized Laurent solution
\[
 F(T)=T+\sum_{n\ge0}b_nT^{-n},\qquad F(T^d)=Q(F(T)),
\]
with $y=\alpha F(T)+b$. Comparison at degree $d-1$ gives $b_0=0$.
Successive comparison at degrees $d-2,d-3,\ldots,d-r+1$ gives
$b_1=\cdots=b_{r-2}=0$. At degree $d-r\ge0$, the left side has no
term, and the right-side coefficient is $d b_{r-1}+c$. Therefore
$b_{r-1}=-c/d\ne0$. This proves the expansion and the exact valuation.
\end{proof}

Here $O(T^{-r})$ denotes an ordinary formal Laurent tail with powers at
most $-r$; it is not an analytic estimate at all surreal magnitudes.
After $T=t^{-\gamma}$, this notation has the exact support meaning
required by the theorem.

\subsection{Examples and exact coefficients}

For $P(Z)=Z^2+1$, the unique infinite branch at each positive scale begins
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:quadratic-example}
 y=T-\frac12T^{-1}-\frac38T^{-3}-\frac3{16}T^{-5}
      -\frac{45}{128}T^{-7}+\cdots.
\end{equation}
It is a perfectly well-defined real surreal solution of
$S_2y=y^2+1$ after $T=\omega^\gamma$, but is not omnific.
There are no nonconstant omnific solutions to this equation.

For $P(Z)=Z^2-2$ there is an exact finite Laurent identity:
\[
 y=T+T^{-1},\qquad S_2y=T^2+T^{-2}=y^2-2.
\]
Again the infinitesimal term prevents an omnific solution. This example
also explains why the full Hahn-field conclusion is Laurent-series
rigidity, not polynomial rigidity.

For $P(Z)=Z^3+Z$ one obtains
\[
 y=T-\frac13T^{-1}-\frac8{81}T^{-5}-\frac8{243}T^{-7}+\cdots.
\]
By contrast, $P(Z)=(Z-3)^2+3$ has the omnific solutions
$y=3+\omega^\gamma$, since its centered normalization is exactly $Z^2$.
The finite exact checks supplied with the article verify the displayed
coefficients and the coordinate identities through the stated truncation
orders; \cref{adr:thm:tail} proves the infinite statement.

\section{A strict hierarchy of autonomous algebraic orders}
\label{adr:sec:order}

\begin{definition}
For a fixed dilation $S_d$, define the \emph{autonomous algebraic order}
of $y$ over $k$ to be the least $r\ge0$ for which
\[
 y,S_dy,\ldots,S_d^r y
\]
are algebraically dependent over $k$. If no such $r$ exists, set the
order to $+\infty$. Constants have order zero. This definition concerns
algebraic dependence of a transform tuple; it is not the usual order of
an arbitrary variable-coefficient linear equation.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[An infinite-support element of exact order two]
\label{adr:thm:order-two}
For each integer $d\ge2$, the omnific integer
\[
 u_d=\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{d^{-n}}
\]
has exact autonomous algebraic order two over $\C$ for $S_d$. It satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:order-two}
 S_d^2u_d-S_du_d=(S_du_d-u_d)^d.
\end{equation}
Every two distinct forward transforms are nevertheless algebraically
independent over $\C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Its decreasing normal-form exponents make the sum legitimate. Dilation
is strong, and direct comparison of supports gives
\[
 S_du_d=u_d+\omega^d.
\]
Applying $S_d$ once more gives
$S_d^2u_d-S_du_d=\omega^{d^2}=(\omega^d)^d$, proving
\eqref{adr:eq:order-two}. The polynomial
$Z-Y-(Y-X)^d$ is nonzero, so the order is at most two.
By \cref{adr:thm:omnific}, the infinite support makes $u_d,S_du_d$
algebraically independent. Thus the order is exactly two.
The last assertion is \cref{adr:cor:pairs}.
\end{proof}

Decreasing-denominator generalized series and their linear Mahler
identities are already present in the literature; compare
\cite[Remark~2.18]{CDDM} and \cite{GG}. The series pattern is therefore
not offered as a new construction. The point here is the rigorous
\emph{minimal autonomous order} and the resulting sharp boundary of the
first-order classification in the omnific setting.
\wtag{} In the collection, the linear identity $S_2u_2-u_2=\omega^2$ is
the Conway form, after one application of $S_2$, of the second series in
the single-dilation report's example \lbl{dsup:ex:opposite}
(\cref{adr:sec:collection}).
\mtag{} Source~03 re-proves the theorem without \cref{adr:thm:omnific}, by a
character argument for the transcendence of $u_d$ over $\C(\omega)$
(\cref{adr:sr:prop:order-two}), and it is the case $\rho=1$ of source~02's
infinite supports of rank $\rho$ and exact order $\rho+1$
(\cref{adr:sr:thm:telescopes}).

\begin{theorem}[Every finite order occurs]\label{adr:thm:all-orders}
Let $g_1,\ldots,g_r$ be positive surreal exponents linearly independent
over $\Q$, and let $a_1,\ldots,a_r\in\R^\times$. Then
\[
 x=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\omega^{g_j}
\]
has exact autonomous algebraic order $r$ over $\C$ for every integer
$d\ge2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $T_j=\omega^{g_j}$. The $T_j$ are algebraically independent over
$\C$, since distinct monomials in them have distinct normal-form
exponents. For $0\le i<r$, put
\[
 Y_i=\sum_{j=1}^r a_jT_j^{d^i}.
\]
Their Jacobian determinant is
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:jacobian}
 \left(\prod_{j=1}^r a_j\right)
 \left(\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}d^i\right)
 \det\bigl(T_j^{d^i-1}\bigr)_{0\le i<r,\,1\le j\le r}.
\end{equation}
The last determinant is nonzero: its permutation terms have distinct
exponent vectors, since the integers $d^i-1$ are distinct.
In characteristic zero, a nonzero Jacobian implies algebraic
independence. For completeness, a polynomial relation of least total
degree would, on differentiation, yield a nontrivial linear relation
between the differentials $dY_i$. At least one evaluated partial
derivative is nonzero, because otherwise a nonzero partial derivative
would be a relation of smaller degree. This contradicts the determinant.

Thus the first $r$ transforms are independent. The first $r+1$ lie in
the field $\C(T_1,\ldots,T_r)$ of transcendence degree $r$, so they are
dependent. The least order is exactly $r$.
\end{proof}

\mtag{} When $a_1=\cdots=a_r=1$, the independence of $Y_0,\ldots,Y_{r-1}$ is
the algebraic independence of the power sums of degrees $1,d,\ldots,d^{r-1}$
in $r$ variables, a special case of Mel\'anov\'a--Sturmfels--Winter
\cite[Proposition~3]{MSW} (any $m\le n$ power sums of distinct positive
degrees in $n$ variables are independent, by a Jacobian computation with
Vandermonde minors). The original manuscript did not cite it; no priority is
claimed for that case. The theorem is also the independent-exponent case of
the exact finite-support formula \cref{adr:sr:thm:finite}.

\begin{proposition}[Bounds from the rational rank of finite support]
\label{adr:prop:rank-bounds}
Let $x$ be a nonconstant finite-support omnific integer and let $r$ be
the dimension over $\Q$ of the span of its positive exponents. Its
order is at most $r$. It is exactly one for $r=1$ and exactly two for
$r=2$; for $r\ge3$, the present results give the bounds $2\le\text{order}\le r$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a rational basis of the exponent span and clear the finitely many
denominators. Then every transform of $x$ lies in a rational function
field generated by $r$ algebraically independent monomials. This gives
the upper bound. The first-order classification gives precisely the
remaining assertions.
\end{proof}

\mtag{} \emph{Status (batch 33).} The order is exactly $r$ in every rank:
\cref{adr:sr:cor:omnific}, proved independently by sources~02 and~03, gives
equality for $r\ge3$ as well, for every positive rational dilation other
than $1$, and for all finite-support surreal and surcomplex numbers. The
bounds stated above remain true; they are no longer the best known.

\section{Boundaries that the conclusions cannot cross}\label{adr:sec:boundaries}

\subsection{Positive characteristic}
If $k=\mathbb F_p$ and the dilation is $S_p$, then
\[
 S_py=y^p
\]
for every Hahn series $y$: Frobenius acts coefficientwise on these
strong sums. Hence completely arbitrary well-ordered supports can satisfy
a degree-one autonomous relation in the dilated variable. The
characteristic-zero hypothesis cannot be dropped. In the proof, both
Newton--Puiseux and the nonvanishing of the coefficient $d$ are substantive.

\subsection{A named nonconstant coefficient}
The infinite-support series $u_d$ satisfies the linear equation
\[
 S_du_d-u_d=\omega^d.
\]
The right side is not in the ordinary constant field. Thus there is no
extension of the support-collapse theorem to unrestricted nonconstant
coefficients. The distinction from variable-coefficient Mahler algorithms
\cite{FR,CDDM} is structural, not a matter of proof technique.
\wtag{} At $d=2$ this equation is the Conway form of
\lbl{dsup:ex:opposite} after one application of $S_2$
(\cref{adr:sec:collection}).

\subsection{Identity dilation and arbitrary rational dilations}
For $d=1$, every series satisfies $S_1y-y=0$, so the theorem is false.
For a nonintegral rational $q>1$, \cref{adr:thm:rational} rules out explicit
rational-map solutions. That argument does not classify implicit
relations $F(y,S_qy)=0$. In the Puiseux step the leading order becomes
$qe$, and taking an $e$th root need not produce an ordinary integer-order
germ. The proof given here must not be quoted as covering that case.
\wtag{} Implicit relations of this kind do have nonconstant solutions:
by the single-dilation report's \lbl{dsup:lem:rationalrecognition}, for
divisible $\Gamma$ and $q=a/b$ the nonzero solutions of $(S_qx)^b=x^a$
are exactly the monomials $\mu_{\abs{a-b}}(k)\,t^\Gamma$. They satisfy the
cyclic-support conclusion trivially; the general implicit case remains
open (\cref{adr:sec:collection}).

\subsection{Full first-order theories}
The decision procedure below is for one specified autonomous equation
with algebraic-number coefficients. It says nothing about arbitrary
quantifier alternations in an expanded surreal field, arbitrary systems
of omnific Diophantine equations, or equality of arbitrary infinite
normal-form descriptions. In particular, it does not contradict the
undecidability phenomena discussed in the repository's single-dilation
report \cite{RepoDilation}.

\section{Effective finite shapes and a decision procedure}
\label{adr:sec:effective}

The sharp degree bound turns the omnific problem into a finite algebraic
problem, even though a general omnific integer is specified by a
potentially transfinite normal form.

\begin{theorem}[Finitely many bounded polynomial shapes]
\label{adr:thm:finite-polynomials}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero,
$0\ne F\in k[X,Y]$, and $d\ge2$. Put $D=\deg_YF$.
The set
\begin{equation}\label{adr:eq:polynomial-shapes}
 \mathcal P_{\le D}(F,d)=
 \{P\in k[T]:1\le\deg P\le D,
          \ F(P(T),P(T^d))=0\}
\end{equation}
is finite. Every nonconstant solution in a negative-support Hahn ring
has the form $P(t^{-\delta})$ with $P$ in this finite set and $\delta>0$.
The analogous statement holds elementwise for omnific and Gaussian
omnific integers, retaining the appropriate coefficient and constant-term
conditions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a degree $M$. For a polynomial $P$ of that degree satisfying the
identity, view $P(t^{-1})$ in $k((t^\Q))$. It has valuation $-M$.
By \cref{adr:thm:profiles} it equals $f(t^\delta)$ for one of finitely many
infinity profiles $f$ of pole order $e$. Valuation comparison forces
$\delta=M/e$. Thus, for each fixed $M$ and profile, there is at most
one possible polynomial $P$. Finiteness follows on taking
$1\le M\le D$. Exhaustion of all negative-support solutions is precisely
the degree-bounded assertion of \cref{adr:thm:negative} and transcendence of
the chosen monomial. Filtering the coefficients gives the omnific versions.
\end{proof}

The bound on the polynomial degree is essential to this finiteness
statement. For example, $S_dy=y^d$ is satisfied by $P(T)=T^n$ for
arbitrarily large $n$. These do not create new shapes modulo the free
monomial scale, since $(t^{-\delta})^n=t^{-n\delta}$.

\begin{theorem}[Decidable nonconstant omnific solvability]
\label{adr:thm:decidable}
Let $F\in\overline{\Q}[X,Y]\setminus\{0\}$ be supplied by exact
algebraic-number coefficients and let $d\ge2$. There is an algorithm to
decide whether $F(x,S_dx)=0$ has a nonconstant solution in $\Oz$, or in
$\Goz$. It produces a finite list of polynomial shapes whose positive
surreal monomial evaluations give all such solutions. Every coefficient
of those shapes is an algebraic number.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $D=\deg_YF=0$, no nonconstant solution exists. Otherwise, for each
$1\le M\le D$, introduce unknowns $a_0,\ldots,a_M$ and form
\[
 P(T)=a_0+a_1T+\cdots+a_MT^M.
\]
Set every coefficient in $T$ of $F(P(T),P(T^d))$ equal to zero and impose
$a_M\ne0$, for instance by adding an auxiliary inverse variable.
These are finite polynomial systems with algebraic-number coefficients.
By \cref{adr:thm:finite-polynomials}, each has only finitely many solutions
in $\C$. A finite algebraic set over $\overline\Q$ has algebraic-number
coordinates. Its points can be computed by standard zero-dimensional
polynomial-system methods. Alternatively, separate real and imaginary
parts and use exact real-algebraic point isolation \cite{BPR}; a nonzero
complex leading coefficient is imposed by inverting its squared modulus.

For $\Oz$, retain precisely the shapes with every coefficient real and
$a_0\in\Z$. For $\Goz$, retain those with $a_0\in\Z[i]$.
Reality, equality, and integrality of exact algebraic numbers are
decidable. The retained list is nonempty exactly when a nonconstant
solution exists. Its evaluations at $T=\omega^\delta$ give all solutions
by \cref{adr:thm:omnific}. A witness may always be produced already at
$T=\omega$.
\end{proof}

The algorithm is a termination result, not a practical complexity claim.
The coefficient systems can be large, and a production implementation
would exploit Puiseux branches and the finite-profile structure before
using general elimination. The supplied Python program implements exact
checks of representative identities, not this complete elimination
algorithm.

\subsection{A finite computational specification}

The equation expanded in the proof has degree at most
$M(\deg_XF+d\deg_YF)$ in $T$. Thus there is an explicit finite bound on
the number of coefficient equations. Every returned candidate is
certified simply by the exact polynomial identity
$F(P(T),P(T^d))=0$, the leading-coefficient test, and the constant-term
condition. No truncation of an unknown infinite surreal number is used
as a certificate of support termination.

The Newton-weight test of \cref{adr:cor:existence} is a different algorithm:
it decides nonconstant solvability in the full surcomplex field, where
infinite Laurent tails are allowed. The two procedures can therefore
legitimately give different answers, as for $S_2x=x^2+1$.

\section{Further research questions and priorities}\label{adr:sec:questions}

The questions below are proposed research directions arising from this
article. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, they are not claimed to be
previously published named open problems. Their purpose is to specify
concrete next theorems rather than to suggest that the present first-order
classification covers a wider theory than it actually does.

\begin{question}[Exact order from finite support; high priority]
For a finite-support omnific integer whose positive exponents have rational
rank $r\ge3$, is its autonomous order always $r$? The answer is established
here for $r\le2$ and for sums supported on a rationally independent set.
The unresolved case allows several supported monomials in the same
$r$-dimensional exponent span. A useful first target is rank three with
four support terms. The bound in \cref{adr:prop:rank-bounds} isolates the
precise remaining gap.
\end{question}

\mtag{} \emph{Status (batch 33): answered, yes, in every rank.} Sources~02
and~03 prove that the order of a finite-support omnific or Gaussian omnific
integer is the rational rank of its positive exponents, with arbitrary
dependent supports, for every integer $d\ge2$ and every rational $d>0$,
$d\ne1$ (\cref{adr:sr:cor:omnific}); the rank-three, six-term example of
\cref{adr:sr:ex:rank-three-03} has order three.

\begin{question}[The support spectrum at order two; high priority]
Which well-ordered Hahn supports can occur for omnific solutions of
$F(x,S_dx,S_d^2x)=0$ over the ordinary constants? Infinite support of
order type $\omega$ occurs by \cref{adr:thm:order-two}. Determine whether
fixed equation degree or fixed autonomous order bounds the possible
support order types, denominator growth, or rational rank. The goal is
a classification or a sharp no-bound theorem, not an extrapolation from
the one telescoping example.
\end{question}

\mtag{} \emph{Status (batch 33): the rational-rank component is answered.} A
relation of autonomous order $n$, with any support, forces rational support
rank at most $n$ (\cref{adr:sr:cor:relation}, both sources), and the bound
is sharp. Source~02 exhibits infinite supports of every rank $\rho\ge1$ with
exact order $\rho+1$ (\cref{adr:sr:thm:telescopes}). Whether fixed degree or
order bounds the support order type or the denominator growth remains open;
\cref{adr:thm:order-two} shows unbounded denominators at order two.

\begin{question}[Formal verification of the core theorem; high priority]
Formalize the chain from strong Hahn evaluation through the monomial
recognizer, the formal B\"ottcher coordinate, and the evaluated Puiseux
branch to \cref{adr:thm:collapse}. The most substantial imported obligation
is a usable Newton--Puiseux theorem with the \emph{individual branch}
degree bound. A staged formalization can state that theorem as an explicit
parameter before replacing it with a verified construction; it should
not silently add it as an axiom and call the entire result verified.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[An efficient shape solver]
Implement \cref{adr:thm:decidable} for rational or algebraic coefficients,
using the Newton-weight filter, Puiseux branches, and polynomial degree
bounds before general elimination. Prove bounds in terms of the input
bidegree, coefficient height, and $d$. Can the finite candidate list be
computed without constructing any nonpolynomial B\"ottcher profile?
Can equivalent shapes under monomial rescaling be canonically minimized?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Nonintegral rational dilation]
Classify autonomous implicit equations $F(y,S_qy)=0$ for
$q\in\Q_{>1}\setminus\Z$. The explicit rational case has no nonconstant
solutions, but a ramified correspondence can have the required rational
Newton slope. Determine whether an appropriate generalized B\"ottcher
coordinate still forces a controlled support subgroup, or permits
unbounded denominators already at order one.
\end{question}

\wtag{} \emph{Status in the collection: open, with one partial datum.}
The single-dilation report's \lbl{dsup:lem:rationalrecognition} solves
the particular relation $(S_qx)^b=x^a$, $q=a/b$, exactly: for divisible
$\Gamma$ its nonzero solutions are $\mu_{\abs{a-b}}(k)\,t^\Gamma$. So
the class of implicit relations with nonintegral $q$ is not empty of
nonconstant solutions, and for this relation the solutions are single
monomials. Nothing is known here about general $F$.
\mtag{} \emph{Status (batch 33): one further partial datum.} By
\cref{adr:sr:rem:question-five}, every solution of $F(y,S_qy)=0$ over $k$, for
any rational $q>0$, $q\ne1$, and any support, has rational support rank at
most one. This says nothing about denominators; the question stays open.

\begin{question}[Tame positive-characteristic variants]
When $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$ and $p\nmid d$, which support-collapse
statements survive under restrictions on Puiseux ramification? Separate
failure caused by wild algebraic branches from failure of the coordinate
construction. The Frobenius example with $d=p$ shows that no unrestricted
analogue is possible, but does not settle the tame cases.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Systems and higher-dimensional correspondences]
For a tuple $\mathbf y$ satisfying
$\mathbf S_d\mathbf y=\mathbf R(\mathbf y)$, what bounds the number of
independent support scales? In one dimension, a superattracting germ is
formally conjugate to a monomial. Higher-dimensional germs need not have
such a coordinate. Identify hypotheses under which a finite-rank support
module, rather than one cyclic group, is forced.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Controlled nonconstant coefficients]
Replace the ordinary constant field by a finitely generated monomial field
$k(t^{\Delta})$. A linear equation already admits unbounded denominators,
so cyclic support is too strong. Find the correct substitute: perhaps a
bound on the support module generated under multiplication and division
by $d$, relative to $\Delta$, together with an ordinal support bound.
Distinguish finite-generation assertions from mere countability.
\end{question}

\mtag{} \emph{Status (batch 33): the relative-rank part is answered.} A
relation among $m$ distinct positive rational dilates with coefficients in
$k(t^\Delta)$ forces support rank at most $m-1$ modulo $\Q\Delta$
(\cref{adr:sr:rem:question-eight}; stated by source~02, and implied by the
relative theorem of both sources). The support module, denominator and ordinal
parts remain open.

\begin{question}[Arithmetic and convergence of the profiles]
For algebraic coefficients, all profile coefficients are algebraic by
\cref{adr:cor:coefficients}. Determine effective denominator and height bounds,
and compare their Archimedean and non-Archimedean analytic convergence.
The underlying B\"ottcher arithmetic is already an established subject
\cite{SS}; the new target is a uniform statement through the ramified
correspondence and the finite-profile list.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Simultaneous algebraic correspondences]
Describe the intersection of the finite-profile solution classes for two
multiplicatively independent dilations. The rational omnific case is
completely rigid by \cref{adr:cor:simultaneous}, but implicit correspondences
and full Laurent tails allow more geometry. Determine which compatibility
conditions replace commutation of two rational maps.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Definability of bounded shape complexity]
For each fixed bound $M$, describe the locus of omnific elements whose
least first-order relation degree is at most $M$, in the language with
one named dilation and in weaker languages. The repository already
recovers normal-form data from a dilation \cite{RepoDilation}; the
question here is the complexity and uniformity of the \emph{bounded-degree}
stratification. Do not confuse a countable union of definable loci with
one first-order definable set.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Interaction with surreal exponential and derivation]
The dilation in this article is defined by the normal-form exponent group,
not by the surreal exponential. Study how the finite-profile classes
behave under the surreal exponential and under specified surreal
exponential-compatible derivations. Even a negative closure theorem would
clarify which parts of difference algebra and differential algebra can be
combined without changing the constant field or losing support control.
\end{question}

The recommended order is to formalize the support-collapse theorem,
implement the finite-shape solver, and then attack the rank-three and
order-two problems. Each has a precise success criterion and can be
pursued without a general theory of analysis on the entire proper class.
\mtag{} The rank-three problem is now solved (Research question~1), and the
rank part of the order-two problem (Research question~2); the further
questions of the additions are
Research questions~\ref{adr:sr:q:udl}--\ref{adr:sr:q:birthday}
in \cref{adr:sr:sec:questions}.

\subsection{Conclusion}

The main structural distinction is now exact. At autonomous order one,
an algebraic relation with an integer exponent dilation collapses an
arbitrary Hahn support to a cyclic Laurent scale. In the omnific ring,
that collapse forces a finite polynomial in one monomial, and the
primitive polynomial degree is recoverable as an algebraic extension
degree. For a fixed equation there are only finitely many shapes modulo
scale, giving an effective solvability reduction for algebraic input.

At order two the finite-support conclusion already fails. This sharp
change is a useful organizing principle for further surreal difference
algebra. It supplies both positive classifications and explicit negative
results without relying on a problematic global topology or treating a
proper class as an ordinary analytic field.

\subsection{Conventions and renamed symbols}
\label{adr:sr:sec:conventions}

\mtag{} Both sources write Hahn series with \emph{growth} support,
$\sum c_g X^g$ (source~03) or $\sum a_\gamma\mathsf t^\gamma$ (source~02),
with reverse well-ordered supports, largest exponents leading and
$X^g=\mathsf t^g=t^{-g}$. This report uses the \emph{valuation} convention
of \cref{adr:sec:setup}: $\K=k((t^\Gamma))$ with well-ordered supports and
$t^\gamma\mapsto\omega^{-\gamma}$ \eqref{adr:eq:omega-bridge}. The two sources'
Hahn-field statements are converted: every support is negated, ``reverse
well ordered'' becomes ``well ordered'', every greedy maximum becomes a
minimum, and every ``largest exponent'' of a determinant becomes its
valuation. Support rank is unchanged, since negation does not change a

\subsection{The setting}
\label{adr:sr:sec:setting}

\begin{definition}[Ordered scalar fields and exponent spaces \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:def:setting}
Let $\mathcal F\subseteq k$ be an ordered subfield of a field $k$; in
particular $\operatorname{char}k=0$. An \emph{ordered $\mathcal F$-vector
space} is an $\mathcal F$-vector space $\Gamma$ whose additive group is
totally ordered, with multiplication by every positive element of $\mathcal F$
preserving strict inequalities. For a set-sized such $\Gamma$ put
$\K=k((t^\Gamma))$, the full Hahn field with well-ordered supports, and for an
$\mathcal F$-subspace $H\subseteq\Gamma$ let $K_H(k)=k((t^H))\subseteq\K$.
Define the \emph{relative support rank}
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:rank}
 \rho_{\mathcal F,H}(y)=\dim_{\mathcal F}\frac{H+\operatorname{span}_{\mathcal F}\supp y}{H},
 \qquad \rho(y)=\rho_{\Q,0}(y).
\end{equation}
The zero exponent contributes nothing. Source~02 works throughout with
$\mathcal F=\Q$; source~03 allows any $\mathcal F$. The case $\mathcal F=\Q$
suffices for every exact finite-support statement, and $\mathcal F=\R$,
$k=\C$ is used for the stronger independence examples.
\end{definition}

The Hahn support facts used are the standard ones \cite{Neumann}: a finite
sum of well-ordered subsets of $\Gamma$ is well ordered, and a fixed exponent
has only finitely many representations as a sum of one element from each of
finitely many well-ordered sets. Source~03 notes the two-set case directly:
infinitely many decompositions $u=g+h$ would, after selection, give a strictly
increasing sequence of $g$'s and a strictly decreasing sequence of $h$'s,
contradicting well-ordering; the finite case follows by induction. A family
of series is strongly summable when the union of its supports is well ordered
and each exponent lies in only finitely many supports; its sum is
coefficientwise. Source~02 stresses that strong summation must not be
confused with convergence of partial sums in a valuation topology: bounded
increasing valuations often give strong sums without that convergence
(compare \cref{adr:sec:setup}). No order on $k$ is needed; $k$ may be $\C$.

For $a\in\mathcal F_{>0}$, multiplication by $a$ is an order automorphism of
$\Gamma$, so
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:dilation}
 S_a\Bigl(\sum_\gamma c_\gamma t^\gamma\Bigr)=\sum_\gamma c_\gamma t^{a\gamma}
\end{equation}
is a strong $k$-automorphism of $\K$, additive and multiplicative because
$a(g+h)=ag+ah$, with
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:semigroup}
 S_aS_b=S_{ab},\qquad S_1=\id,\qquad S_a^{-1}=S_{1/a},\qquad S_a(K_H(k))=K_H(k).
\end{equation}
It agrees with \eqref{adr:eq:Sd} for $a=d$ and with the $S_q$ of
\cref{adr:sec:rational} for $a=q$. It is not $y\mapsto y^a$, not argument
dilation of an analytic function, and not the surreal exponential; the
single-dilation report studies these exponent dilations and their
definability properties \cite{RepoDilation}. ``Autonomous'' means that a
relation has coefficients in the stated base, usually $k$; in the relative
version the base is $K_H(k)$. A relation with coefficients involving further
monomials is not a relation over $k$, and passing to a larger core changes
the relevant rank.

\begin{definition}[Autonomous algebraic order over a base \src{02, 03}]
\label{adr:sr:def:aord}
For $d\in\Q_{>0}\setminus\{1\}$ and a subfield $\mathfrak B$ of the ambient
field, put
\[
 \operatorname{aord}_{d,\mathfrak B}(y)
  =\min\{n\ge0:\ y,S_dy,\ldots,S_d^ny\ \text{are algebraically dependent over }\mathfrak B\},
\]
with value $\infty$ when no such $n$ exists; elements of $\mathfrak B$ have
order zero. For $\mathfrak B=k$ and integer $d\ge2$ this is the autonomous
algebraic order of \cref{adr:sec:order}. An infinite family is algebraically
independent when each finite subfamily is; for the orbit
$\{S_d^ny\}$ this is \emph{difference transcendence} over $\mathfrak B$. It is
not differential transcendence under any chosen derivation.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Surreal transfer.} Every surreal has a unique Conway normal form
$x=\sum_{g}r_g\omega^g$ with set-sized reverse well-ordered support of
exponents $g$ and real $r_g\ne0$, and every such series is a surreal
\cite[Chapter~5]{Gonshor}; a surcomplex number has the analogous complex
expansion, obtained from the union of its two real supports. Under
\eqref{adr:eq:omega-bridge}, a set-sized ordered $\mathcal F$-subspace
$\Gamma\subseteq\No$ gives the embedded fields $\R((t^\Gamma))\subseteq\No$ and
$\C((t^\Gamma))\subseteq\SC$. Finitely many given numbers, or the members of a
given set, have a set-sized union of supports, so every finite algebraic
relation among them lives in one set-sized Hahn field. In $\omega$-notation
$S_a(\sum r_g\omega^g)=\sum r_g\omega^{ag}$, and \eqref{adr:eq:Oz} shows that
positive real and rational dilations preserve $\Oz$ and $\Goz$; neither
positivity nor integrality of the nonconstant coefficients is required. All
powers $\omega^g$ are Conway's canonical monomials, not values of Gonshor's
exponential.

\paragraph{Set theory.} The Hahn-field theorems are set-sized algebra in ZFC.
Linear functionals are extended by the basis theorem on a set-sized space
only. For proper-class surreal conclusions each proposed finite relation is
placed in one set-sized workspace; no global basis of $\No$ over $\Q$ or $\R$,
no proper-class sum and no proper-class determinant is used. The
ordinal-indexed families below are given by explicit formulas, and their
algebraic independence means that every finite subfamily is independent: a
class statement with set-sized proof instances, not a class-sized polynomial
ring.

\section{The greedy leading Jacobian term and the rank obstruction}
\label{adr:sr:sec:rank}

\subsection{Euler derivations and the Jacobian criterion}

\begin{lemma}[Support-preserving Euler derivations \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:euler}
Let $\lambda:\Gamma\to k$ be $\mathcal F$-linear. Then
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:euler}
 \partial_\lambda\Bigl(\sum_\gamma c_\gamma t^\gamma\Bigr)
   =\sum_\gamma c_\gamma\lambda(\gamma)t^\gamma
\end{equation}
is a $k$-linear derivation of $\K$ that commutes with strong sums, and
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:commutation}
 \partial_\lambda S_a=a\,S_a\partial_\lambda\qquad(a\in\mathcal F_{>0}).
\end{equation}
If $\lambda(H)=0$, then $\partial_\lambda$ vanishes on $K_H(k)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The support of the right side of \eqref{adr:sr:eq:euler} is contained in the
original support, so the series exists; additivity and $k$-linearity are
coefficientwise. For $f=\sum c_\gamma t^\gamma$ and $h=\sum e_\beta t^\beta$
the coefficient of $t^\delta$ in $\partial_\lambda(fh)$ is the finite sum
\[
 \sum_{\gamma+\beta=\delta}c_\gamma e_\beta\lambda(\gamma+\beta)
 =\sum_{\gamma+\beta=\delta}c_\gamma e_\beta\bigl(\lambda(\gamma)+\lambda(\beta)\bigr),
\]
the coefficient of $(\partial_\lambda f)h+f\,\partial_\lambda h$. The same
support-subset property and finite coefficient sums give commutation with
strong sums, and $\lambda(a\gamma)=a\lambda(\gamma)$ gives
\eqref{adr:sr:eq:commutation}. An element of $K_H(k)$ has all its exponents in
$H$, so its image is zero when $\lambda(H)=0$.
\end{proof}

Source~03 takes $\lambda$ with values in $\mathcal F$ and source~02 takes
$\Q$-linear $\lambda$ with values in $k$; the statement above covers both.
These are deliberately chosen coordinate derivations. They need not be
Hardy-type derivations and are not the Berarducci--Mantova derivation or an
analytic derivative of surreal functions, nor are they asserted to be
exponential-compatible. Their role is algebraic: they separate independent
exponent directions while annihilating the chosen coefficient core. General
series derivations have an established theory \cite{KM}; this simple
construction is proved so that no further differential-algebra theorem is a
premise.

\begin{lemma}[An invertible derivative matrix detects independence \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:jacobian}
Let $\mathfrak B\subseteq\mathfrak L$ be fields of characteristic zero, let
$\partial_1,\ldots,\partial_m$ be derivations of $\mathfrak L$ vanishing on
$\mathfrak B$, and let $z_1,\ldots,z_m\in\mathfrak L$. If
$\det[\partial_i(z_j)]_{j,i}\ne0$, then $z_1,\ldots,z_m$ are algebraically
independent over $\mathfrak B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise choose a nonzero $F\in\mathfrak B[Y_1,\ldots,Y_m]$ of least total
degree with $F(z_1,\ldots,z_m)=0$; its degree is positive. Applying
$\partial_i$ gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m\frac{\partial F}{\partial Y_j}(z_1,\ldots,z_m)\,\partial_i(z_j)=0
 \qquad(1\le i\le m).
\]
Invertibility forces every evaluated partial derivative to vanish. In
characteristic zero some formal partial derivative of the nonconstant $F$ is
a nonzero polynomial of smaller total degree, a smaller relation. This is a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

The proof shows the two hypotheses at work: the derivations must kill the
\emph{whole} coefficient field of the proposed relation, and characteristic
zero prevents all partial derivatives of a minimal relation from vanishing.
The relative core $K_H(k)$ is chosen to meet the first; the characteristic-$p$
argument of \cref{adr:sr:sec:charp} replaces the second. The same criterion
closes the proof of \cref{adr:thm:all-orders}, and source~02 compares it with
the classical form \cite[Theorem~6]{BMS}.

\subsection{Greedy selection and strict rearrangement}

Let $\Sigma\subseteq\Gamma$ be well ordered, and assume that its image in
$\Gamma/H$ spans a space of dimension at least $m$. Define successively
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:greedy}
 b_j=\min\bigl(\Sigma\setminus(H+\operatorname{span}_{\mathcal F}\{b_1,\ldots,b_{j-1}\})\bigr),
 \qquad 1\le j\le m.
\end{equation}
Each set is nonempty by the rank hypothesis and has a least element by
well-ordering. The images of $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ in $\Gamma/H$ are independent,
and $b_1<b_2<\cdots<b_m$. Elements of $\Sigma\cap H$, however small, are
skipped: they cannot contribute to derivatives annihilating $K_H(k)$.
Extending these images to a basis of the set-sized space $\Gamma/H$ gives
$\mathcal F$-linear maps $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m:\Gamma\to\mathcal F$ with
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:dual}
 \lambda_i(H)=0,\qquad\lambda_i(b_j)=\delta_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Write $\vec\lambda(\gamma)=(\lambda_1(\gamma),\ldots,\lambda_m(\gamma))\in\mathcal F^m$.
The remaining basis coordinates are discarded; a term with zero projected
determinant simply disappears from the Jacobian expansion.

\begin{lemma}[Coordinatewise dominance of the greedy basis \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:dominance}
If $a_1<\cdots<a_m$ are elements of $\Sigma$ with
$\vec\lambda(a_1),\ldots,\vec\lambda(a_m)$ linearly independent over
$\mathcal F$, then $a_j\ge b_j$ for $1\le j\le m$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $a_j<b_j$, then $a_1,\ldots,a_j$ are all below $b_j$. By the minimality
defining $b_j$, every element of $\Sigma$ below $b_j$ lies in
$H+\operatorname{span}_{\mathcal F}\{b_1,\ldots,b_{j-1}\}$, so its
$\vec\lambda$-image lies in the span of the first $j-1$ coordinate vectors,
and the $j$ images $\vec\lambda(a_1),\ldots,\vec\lambda(a_j)$ would be
dependent.
\end{proof}

This is the part of the greedy principle for linear matroids \cite{Edmonds}
that is needed. It compares the $j$th selected element with the $j$th
element of \emph{every} competing independent selection, which is stronger
than optimizing one unweighted sum. The proof is finite dimensional even when
$\Sigma$ has uncountable order type, and no traversal of an infinite support
is claimed as an algorithm.

\begin{lemma}[Strict rearrangement \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:rearrangement}
Let $a_1<\cdots<a_m$ lie in an ordered $\mathcal F$-vector space and let
$q_1>\cdots>q_m>0$ lie in $\mathcal F$. For every permutation $\pi$,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^mq_ja_{\pi(j)}\ \ge\ \sum_{j=1}^mq_ja_j,
\]
with equality only for the identity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $i<j$ but $a_{\pi(i)}>a_{\pi(j)}$, exchanging the two assigned elements
lowers the sum by $(q_i-q_j)(a_{\pi(i)}-a_{\pi(j)})>0$, and sorting a
nonidentity permutation needs at least one such exchange. Only the
ordered-vector-space axioms are used: no Archimedean hypothesis and no
real-valued weight.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Unique minimizing support tuple \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:prop:unique}
Let $q_1>\cdots>q_m>0$ in $\mathcal F$. Among all ordered tuples
$(g_1,\ldots,g_m)\in\Sigma^m$ with $\det[\lambda_i(g_j)]_{j,i}\ne0$, the
sum $\sum_jq_jg_j$ has the unique minimum
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:eta}
 \eta=q_1b_1+\cdots+q_mb_m,
\end{equation}
attained only at $(g_1,\ldots,g_m)=(b_1,\ldots,b_m)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero determinant makes the $g_j$ distinct; sort them as
$a_1<\cdots<a_m$. \Cref{adr:sr:lem:rearrangement} gives
$\sum_jq_jg_j\ge\sum_jq_ja_j$, and \cref{adr:sr:lem:dominance} with $q_j>0$
gives $\sum_jq_ja_j\ge\sum_jq_jb_j$. The first inequality is strict unless the
tuple is already increasing; the second is strict if some $a_j>b_j$, because a
sum of nonnegative elements with a positive summand is positive in an ordered
group. So equality forces $g_j=b_j$ for all $j$, and the greedy tuple has
identity projected matrix by \eqref{adr:sr:eq:dual}.
\end{proof}

The proposition excludes the dangerous case in which several extreme terms
have the same exponent and cancel: it identifies the extremal \emph{ordered
tuple}, not merely an extremal value of a weight on possibly cancelling terms.

\subsection{The leading Jacobian term}

\begin{theorem}[The leading Jacobian term \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:leading}
Let $y=\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma}c_\gamma t^\gamma\in\K$, choose the greedy
exponents and dual functionals as above, and let $q_1>\cdots>q_m>0$ in
$\mathcal F$. Put $J_{ji}=\partial_{\lambda_i}(S_{q_j}y)$. Then
$v(\det J)=\eta$, and
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:leading}
 [t^\eta]\det J=\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^mq_j\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^mc_{b_j}\Bigr)\ne0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{adr:sr:lem:euler}, $J_{ji}=q_j\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma}c_\gamma
\lambda_i(\gamma)t^{q_j\gamma}$. Expanding the determinant and collecting one
support exponent from each row, an ordered tuple $(g_1,\ldots,g_m)$
contributes
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:expansion}
 \Bigl(\prod_jq_j\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_jc_{g_j}\Bigr)\det[\lambda_i(g_j)]_{j,i}\,
 t^{\sum_jq_jg_j}.
\end{equation}
The regrouping is legitimate: there are finitely many permutations, each
product has support in the finite sum of the well-ordered sets $q_j\Sigma$,
and each coefficient receives finitely many contributions; no conditionally
convergent series is rearranged. Tuples with zero projected determinant
contribute zero. By \cref{adr:sr:prop:unique} the unique least exponent among
the others is $\eta$, from $(b_1,\ldots,b_m)$, whose projected matrix is the
identity. Each $c_{b_j}$ is nonzero by definition of the support, and each
$q_j$ is nonzero in the characteristic-zero field $k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Support-rank obstruction \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:main}
Let $y\in\K$ and let $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ be distinct elements of
$\mathcal F_{>0}$, $m\ge1$. If $\rho_{\mathcal F,H}(y)\ge m$, then
$S_{a_1}y,\ldots,S_{a_m}y$ are algebraically independent over $K_H(k)$.
Consequently, for every finite list of distinct positive dilations,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:lower}
 \trdeg_{K_H(k)}K_H(k)(S_{a_1}y,\ldots,S_{a_m}y)\ge\min\{m,\rho_{\mathcal F,H}(y)\},
\end{equation}
where the minimum is $m$ when the rank is infinite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ by the rank hypothesis. The derivations
$\partial_{\lambda_i}$ vanish on $K_H(k)$ by \eqref{adr:sr:eq:dual} and
\cref{adr:sr:lem:euler}, and their matrix on the dilates, with the dilations
listed in decreasing order, is invertible by \cref{adr:sr:thm:leading}.
\Cref{adr:sr:lem:jacobian} applies, and reordering a tuple does not affect
independence. For rank smaller than $m$ apply this to a subset of that many
dilations; for rank zero the bound is zero.
\end{proof}

Source~03 proves this for every ordered $\mathcal F$; source~02 proves it for
$\mathcal F=\Q$ (its relative support-rank obstruction), with a different
order of choices, recorded as a second route.

\begin{theorem}[Vector-valued greedy determinant \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:vector}
Let $\Sigma\subseteq\Gamma$ be well ordered and let $c_\gamma\in k^m$,
$\gamma\in\Sigma$, span $k^m$; ignore zero vectors. Choose
\[
 g_1=\min\{\gamma:c_\gamma\ne0\},\qquad
 g_j=\min\{\gamma:c_\gamma\notin\operatorname{span}_k(c_{g_1},\ldots,c_{g_{j-1}})\}
 \quad(2\le j\le m).
\]
Then $g_1<\cdots<g_m$, and if $h_1<\cdots<h_m$ index independent vectors then
$h_j\ge g_j$ for each $j$. For $q_1>\cdots>q_m>0$ in $\Q$, the matrix
$M_{ij}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma}c_{\gamma,j}t^{q_i\gamma}$ has nonzero
determinant, with
\[
 v(\det M)=\sum_{j=1}^mq_jg_j,\qquad
 [t^{v(\det M)}]\det M=\det(c_{g_i,j})_{1\le i,j\le m}\ne0.
\]
Multiplying row $i$ by $q_i$ multiplies the coefficient by $\prod_iq_i$ and
leaves the valuation unchanged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The choices exist because the family has rank $m$. Every vector with exponent
below $g_j$ lies in the span of the first $j-1$ selected vectors, since
otherwise it would be eligible in place of $g_j$. If $h_j<g_j$, the $j$
independent vectors indexed by $h_1,\ldots,h_j$ would lie in that
$(j-1)$-dimensional span. Strict ascent holds because exponents below an
earlier selected exponent were already ineligible and the selected exponent
itself lies in the enlarged span.

A choice of exponents $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$, one per row, contributes
$\det(c_{\gamma_i,j})_{i,j}\,t^{q_1\gamma_1+\cdots+q_m\gamma_m}$; the union of
possible exponents lies in $q_1\Sigma+\cdots+q_m\Sigma$ and each coefficient
has finitely many contributions. Dependent choices contribute zero. For an
independent choice sorted as $h_1<\cdots<h_m$,
\cref{adr:sr:lem:rearrangement} and the dominance just proved give
$\sum_iq_i\gamma_i\ge\sum_jq_jh_j\ge\sum_jq_jg_j$, with the first equality only
for the increasing assignment and the second only when every $h_j=g_j$, by
positivity of the weights. Exactly one independent row choice attains the
minimum, with rows $c_{g_1},\ldots,c_{g_m}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Source~02's ordering, and its route to \cref{adr:sr:thm:main}]
\label{adr:sr:rem:ascending}
\mtag{} Source~02 lists the parameters increasingly, $0<q_1<\cdots<q_m$, and
states its leading exponent as $\sum_jq_{m+1-j}g_j$ with coefficient
$(-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\det(c_{g_i,j})$: reversing the rows of $M$ gives the sign.
This is the same statement. For finite $\Sigma$ the greedy basis is the usual
greedy basis of a linear matroid \cite{Edmonds}, and the proof shows why an
infinite well-ordered ground set adds no difficulty when the requested rank is
finite. \emph{Second route to \cref{adr:sr:thm:main} (source~02, for
$\mathcal F=\Q$).} Choose $m$ supported exponents independent modulo $H$, take
coordinate functionals to $\Q$ on their span modulo $H$, and extend them to
$\Gamma/H$ by extending a rational basis; composing with $\Gamma\to\Gamma/H$
gives $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m:\Gamma\to\Q\subseteq k$ vanishing on $H$,
of rank $m$ on the selected exponents. With $y=\sum c_\gamma t^\gamma$ put
$\vec c_\gamma=c_\gamma\vec\lambda(\gamma)\in k^m$; these span $k^m$, since the
selected square minor is a nonzero rational determinant times nonzero
coefficients. The Euler matrix is
$\bigl(\partial_{\lambda_j}S_{q_i}y\bigr)_{i,j}
=\bigl(q_i\sum_\gamma\vec c_{\gamma,j}t^{q_i\gamma}\bigr)_{i,j}$, whose
determinant is nonzero by \cref{adr:sr:thm:vector}; conclude with
\cref{adr:sr:lem:jacobian}. Here the functionals are fixed first and the
greedy basis is chosen among their images, whereas source~03 chooses the greedy
basis first and makes the functionals dual to it. Both routes need a rank-$m$
family of coefficient vectors, not merely $m$ nonzero coefficients, and
discard zero Euler vectors before the greedy choice.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the argument handles difficult supports \src{03, 02}]
The least exponent of $y$ alone may not suffice: it may lie in the core, or
several of the smallest exponents may span one direction. Greedy selection
skips exactly those, and an arbitrary independent set would not do, because
terms outside it could dominate and cancel; coordinatewise dominance settles
both issues. Different independent subsets can produce the same nonleading
exponent and cancel; the theorem does not claim that all determinant terms are
distinct, only that one extremal nonzero contribution is isolated. Coefficient
signs and phases never enter the selection: they multiply one nonzero leading
coefficient after cancellation has been excluded, so arbitrary real or complex
coefficients are allowed with no positivity or genericity assumption.
Infinite order types, accumulation toward a cut, and the absence of an
Archimedean embedding of $\Gamma$ create no extra case.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Every relation bounds support rank \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:cor:relation}
If a nonzero $F\in K_H(k)[Y_1,\ldots,Y_m]$ satisfies
$F(S_{a_1}y,\ldots,S_{a_m}y)=0$ for distinct $a_j\in\mathcal F_{>0}$, then
$\rho_{\mathcal F,H}(y)\le m-1$. In particular, for rational $d>0$, $d\ne1$,
a nonzero relation $F(y,S_dy,\ldots,S_d^ny)=0$ over $K_H(k)$ forces
$\rho_{\Q,H}(y)\le n$, and
\[
 \operatorname{aord}_{d,k}(y)=n<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ \rho(y)\le n.
\]
If $\rho_{\mathcal F,H}(y)$ is infinite, all distinct positive
$\mathcal F$-dilates of $y$ are jointly algebraically independent over
$K_H(k)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Rank at least $m$ contradicts \cref{adr:sr:thm:main}; the parameters
$1,d,\ldots,d^n$ are distinct because $d>0$ and $d\ne1$. An infinite rank
satisfies the hypothesis of \cref{adr:sr:thm:main} for every finite $m$.
\end{proof}

In the absolute case this is a necessary condition on the normal form of
every solution, not a statement about chosen examples. It is sharp: sums of
$n$ monomials with independent exponents have order exactly $n$
(\cref{adr:thm:all-orders}). It does not say that rank at most $n$ suffices
for a relation of order $n$, and it bounds neither the degree of a relation
nor the support's order type or denominators.

\begin{remark}[Research question~5: a partial datum]
\label{adr:sr:rem:question-five}
\mtag{} Neither source states the following. Let $\Gamma$ be any ordered
abelian group in which $S_q$ is defined, $q\in\Q_{>0}\setminus\{1\}$, and let
$y\in\K\setminus k$ satisfy $F(y,S_qy)=0$ for a nonzero $F\in k[X,Y]$, with
$\operatorname{char}k=0$. Embed $\K$ coefficientwise into
$k((t^{\Gamma\otimes\Q}))$; this commutes with $S_q$ and keeps the relation.
\Cref{adr:sr:cor:relation} with $m=2$, $\mathcal F=\Q$, $H=0$ gives
$\rho(y)\le1$: the support of $y$ spans at most a one-dimensional rational
space. For integer $q=d\ge2$ this is weaker than \cref{adr:thm:collapse}. For
nonintegral $q$ it is new information on Research question~5: no nonconstant
solution can have two rationally independent support directions, whatever the
generalized B\"ottcher theory turns out to be. It says nothing about
denominators, which is the other half of that question, and is consistent with
the monomial solutions recorded in \cref{adr:sec:collection}. Research
question~5 stays open.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Research question~8: the relative part]
\label{adr:sr:rem:question-eight}
Source~02 records that its relative theorem answers a part of Research
question~8. If the coefficients of a relation lie in the monomial field
$k(t^\Delta)$ of a subgroup $\Delta\subseteq\Gamma$, they lie in $K_H(k)$ for
$H=\Q\Delta$, so a nonzero relation among $m$ distinct positive rational
dilates forces rank at most $m-1$ \emph{modulo the rational span of} $\Delta$.
\mtag{} Source~03's \cref{adr:sr:thm:main} gives the same, over any
$\mathcal F$. Neither conclusion bounds the support order type, the
denominators, or the support module generated under multiplication and
division by $d$ relative to $\Delta$, which that question also asks for; those
parts remain open. A relation with such coefficients can exist where none
exists over $k$: $S_du_d-u_d=\omega^d$ (\cref{adr:sec:boundaries}).
\end{remark}

\section{Finite support: the exact order in every rank}
\label{adr:sr:sec:finite}

\begin{lemma}[Independent monomials over a Hahn core \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:monomials}
If $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_\rho\in\Gamma$ are $\Q$-linearly independent modulo a
$\Q$-subspace $H$, then $t^{\beta_1},\ldots,t^{\beta_\rho}$ are algebraically
independent over $K_H(k)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For distinct integer multi-indices $\nu\ne\mu$ the cosets
$H+\sum_i\nu_i\beta_i$ and $H+\sum_i\mu_i\beta_i$ are disjoint. A nonzero
coefficient in $K_H(k)$ times the corresponding monomial has nonempty support
in its own coset, so a nonzero polynomial relation would be a sum of nonzero
series with pairwise disjoint supports, which cannot vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-support rank \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:finite}
Let $\mathcal F=\Q$, and let $y\in\K$ have finite support with
$\rho=\rho_{\Q,H}(y)$. For every finite set $\mathcal Q\subseteq\Q_{>0}$,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:exact}
 \trdeg_{K_H(k)}K_H(k)(S_qy:q\in\mathcal Q)=\min\{\rho,|\mathcal Q|\}.
\end{equation}
The field generated over $K_H(k)$ by all positive rational dilates of $y$ has
transcendence degree $\rho$. For every rational $d>0$, $d\ne1$,
$\operatorname{aord}_{d,K_H(k)}(y)=\rho$; in particular
$\operatorname{aord}_{d,k}(y)=\rho(y)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound is \cref{adr:sr:thm:main}. For the upper bound choose
$\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_\rho$ spanning the image of the finite support $\Sigma$
modulo $H$ and write $\gamma=h_\gamma+\sum_iu_{\gamma i}\beta_i$ with
$h_\gamma\in H$, $u_{\gamma i}\in\Q$, for $\gamma\in\Sigma$. Since $\mathcal Q$
and $\Sigma$ are finite there is an integer $N\ge1$ with
$Nqu_{\gamma i}\in\Z$ for all occurring $q,\gamma,i$. With $z_i=t^{\beta_i/N}$,
\[
 t^{q\gamma}=t^{qh_\gamma}\prod_iz_i^{Nqu_{\gamma i}},\qquad t^{qh_\gamma}\in K_H(k),
\]
so every $S_qy$ lies in $K_H(k)(z_1,\ldots,z_\rho)$, a rational function field
of transcendence degree $\rho$ by \cref{adr:sr:lem:monomials}; the generated
field has transcendence degree at most $\rho$ and at most $|\mathcal Q|$.
Every monomial $t^\gamma$ with $\gamma$ in the rational span of $\Sigma$ is
algebraic over $K_H(k)(t^{\beta_1},\ldots,t^{\beta_\rho})$, so the field of
all positive rational dilates lies in an algebraic extension of that field and
has transcendence degree exactly $\rho$. Finally, the first $\rho$ consecutive
transforms are independent and the first $\rho+1$ dependent; for $\rho=0$,
$y\in K_H(k)$ and the order is zero.
\end{proof}

Source~03 proves the relative statement \eqref{adr:sr:eq:exact}; source~02
proves the absolute one ($H=0$) with the statement on all dilates, choosing a
rational basis $e_1,\ldots,e_\rho$ of the support span and writing a finite
list of dilates as Laurent polynomials in $t^{e_j/N}$. The two proofs are the
same. The substance is the lower bound, which must hold for \emph{every}
nonzero coefficient pattern: the proof exhibits one noncancelling determinant
term rather than invoking genericity. Cancellation among support terms and
rational dependencies among exponents are allowed, and the coefficients may
satisfy arbitrary algebraic relations.

\begin{corollary}[The uniform matroid of the rational orbit \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:cor:uniform}
For a finite-support $y$ with $\rho=\rho(y)>0$, a set of distinct positive
rational dilates of $y$ is algebraically independent over $k$ if and only if
it has at most $\rho$ elements. So the algebraic matroid on
$\{S_qy:q\in\Q_{>0}\}$ is the uniform matroid of rank $\rho$. The same holds
over $K_H(k)$ with $\rho=\rho_{\Q,H}(y)$.
\end{corollary}

There are no exceptional finite subsets of distinct positive rational
parameters. The statement says which subsets are independent; it computes no
minimal equation and no extension degree, and it does not say that $\rho$
dilates recover every support monomial by rational functions. An infinite
collection of rational dilations still generates a field of transcendence
degree $\rho$, but that field can be an infinite algebraic extension of the
field of $\rho$ chosen observations: the denominators of the dilations need
not have a common bound.

\begin{corollary}[Omnific autonomous order; Research question~1 \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:cor:omnific}
Let $x\in\Oz$ or $x\in\Goz$ have finite support, and let $\rho$ be the
$\Q$-dimension of the span of its nonzero exponents. For every integer
$d\ge2$, and every rational $d>0$ with $d\ne1$, the autonomous algebraic order
of $x$ over $\C$ is $\rho$; for real $x$ its order over $\R$ is also $\rho$.
Any $m$ distinct positive rational dilates of $x$ generate a field of
transcendence degree $\min\{m,\rho\}$ over $\C$. The same holds for every
finite-support surreal or surcomplex number, with negative exponents allowed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All supports involved lie in the set-sized $\Q$-span of the finite original
support. Apply \cref{adr:sr:thm:finite} with $k=\C$ and $H=0$, and with
$k=\R$ for the real statement. Source~02 adds that a determinant with nonzero
real leading coefficient stays nonzero after scalar extension to $\C$, so the
real and complex statements agree.
\end{proof}

This is the equality asked for in Research question~1, including its
unresolved case of several supported monomials in one $\rho$-dimensional
exponent span, and it makes \cref{adr:prop:rank-bounds} an equality in every
rank. The restriction to omnific integers is unnecessary. For $\rho=1$ and
$\rho=2$ it recovers \cref{adr:prop:rank-bounds} and, with $m=2$, the
finite-support case of \cref{adr:cor:d-independent}, by a proof that uses none
of \cref{adr:sec:puiseux,adr:sec:bottcher,adr:sec:main}.

\subsection{Finite determination is not reconstruction}
\label{adr:sr:sec:reconstruction}

For $\rho$ finite-support observations generating a field of transcendence
degree $\rho$, the ambient monomial field is finitely generated and algebraic,
hence a finite extension, over the observation field. The monomial coordinates
are therefore determined generically up to finitely many algebraic
possibilities. This is weaker than a birational reconstruction theorem, and
much weaker than finiteness or uniqueness of every numerical fibre; special
fibres can be exceptional even for power sums \cite{MSW}. A polynomial
invariant under a nontrivial permutation of its monomial coordinates keeps
that symmetry after every dilation, so its observations cannot distinguish the
permuted coordinates: exact rank does not remove finite symmetries
(source~03). Uniform algebraic independence alone does not give birational
recovery, and the finite extension degrees are additional information
(source~02).

\section{Rational functions of finitely many monomials}
\label{adr:sr:sec:rational}

A series can have infinite support because a rational expression was expanded
as a Hahn series. Such series still have exact order equal to rank. The two
sources prove this by different routes, both printed: source~03 descends the
function to its support subgroup and computes the rank from a reduced
quotient; source~02 computes it from a coefficient matrix that needs no
reduction and bounds the order by a Jacobian rank.

Throughout, $\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma$ is a finitely generated, hence free,
subgroup, $k(\Lambda)=\operatorname{Frac}(k[t^\Lambda])\subseteq\K$, and
$\Q\Lambda$ is its rational span. A rational function in $k(\Lambda)$ has Hahn
support in $\Lambda$: dividing by the least monomial of the denominator and
expanding the geometric series uses only sums and differences of elements of
$\Lambda$ (the Neumann expansion of \cref{adr:lem:evaluation}).

\subsection{First route: rational support descent \src{03}}

\begin{lemma}[Rational support descent \src{03}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:descent}
Let $W$ be a $\Q$-subspace of $\Q\Lambda$. In $\K$,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:descent}
 k(\Lambda)\cap k((t^W))=k(\Lambda\cap W).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Projection onto the exponents in $W$ is an additive map $\pi_W$ of the Hahn
field, and $\pi_W(hv)=h\,\pi_W(v)$ whenever $h$ is supported in $W$: a
summand of the coefficient at $w\in W$ uses an exponent $s\in W$ of $h$, so the
exponent $w-s$ from $v$ also lies in $W$; this is checked coefficientwise on
finite convolutions. Let $f=P/Q\in k(\Lambda)$ be supported in $W$, pick
$u\in\supp Q$, and put $P'=t^{-u}P$, $Q'=t^{-u}Q$. The Laurent polynomial $Q'$
has a nonzero constant coefficient, so $\pi_W(Q')\ne0$, and projecting
$fQ'=P'$ gives $f=\pi_W(P')/\pi_W(Q')$. Both projections are Laurent
polynomials supported in $\Lambda\cap W$. The reverse inclusion holds because
the series supported in $W$ form a field.
\end{proof}

The projection must not be mistaken for a ring retraction of the whole Hahn
field: projection onto exponent zero kills $t^g$ and $t^{-g}$ for $g\ne0$ but
not their product. Only linearity when one factor is supported in the
subgroup is used.

\begin{theorem}[Exact order for rational monomial functions \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:rational}
Let $f\in k(\Lambda)$, viewed in $\K$; its support may be infinite. Put
$\rho=\dim_\Q\operatorname{span}_\Q\supp f$. For distinct positive rationals
$q_1,\ldots,q_m$,
\[
 \trdeg_kk(S_{q_1}f,\ldots,S_{q_m}f)=\min\{\rho,m\}.
\]
In particular $\operatorname{aord}_{d,k}(f)=\rho$ for every rational $d>0$,
$d\ne1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof \src{03}]
Put $W=\operatorname{span}_\Q\supp f$. The support of $f$ lies in $\Lambda$,
so \cref{adr:sr:lem:descent} gives $f\in k(\Lambda\cap W)$. The subgroup
$\Lambda\cap W$ is finitely generated, as a subgroup of the free group
$\Lambda$ of finite rank, and has rank $\rho$, because it contains the support
spanning $W$ and lies in the $\rho$-dimensional space $W$. For finitely many
rational dilations choose a common denominator $N$; all dilates lie in the
monomial rational function field of the rank-$\rho$ group
$N^{-1}(\Lambda\cap W)$, which gives the upper bound $\rho$. The lower bound is
\cref{adr:sr:thm:main}, applied to the possibly infinite support of $f$. The
order statement follows as in \cref{adr:sr:thm:finite}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A finite rank formula from a reduced quotient \src{03}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:quotient}
Write a nonzero $f\in k(\Lambda)$ as $P/Q$ with coprime nonzero Laurent
polynomials $P,Q\in k[t^\Lambda]$, and put $\Sigma_{P,Q}=\supp P\cup\supp Q$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:quotient}
 \operatorname{span}_\Q\supp f=\operatorname{span}_\Q\{a-b:a,b\in\Sigma_{P,Q}\}.
\end{equation}
So the dimension of the difference span is the exact order in
\cref{adr:sr:thm:rational}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $W$ and $V$ be the left and right sides. For $b\in\Sigma_{P,Q}$, both
$t^{-b}P$ and $t^{-b}Q$ are supported in $V$, so their quotient lies in the
Hahn field over $V$, and $W\subseteq V$.

Conversely, let $\lambda:\Lambda\to\Z$ be an integral linear functional
vanishing on $W\cap\Lambda$. With an independent indeterminate $\tau$, the
substitution $t^a\mapsto\tau^{\lambda(a)}t^a$ is an automorphism of
$k(\tau)[t^\Lambda]$ and a support-preserving coefficient twist of the
associated Hahn field. Every exponent of $f$ lies in $W$, so the twist fixes
$f$; writing $P_\tau,Q_\tau$ for the twisted numerator and denominator,
$P_\tau/Q_\tau=P/Q$. The Laurent polynomial ring $k(\tau)[t^\Lambda]$ is a
unique factorization domain, coprimality of $P,Q$ persists under the purely
transcendental scalar extension, and the substitution preserves it. Two
coprime pairs representing one fraction differ by a unit, so
$P_\tau=c(\tau)t^uP$ and $Q_\tau=c(\tau)t^uQ$ with $c(\tau)\in k(\tau)^\times$
and $u\in\Lambda$. The twist does not change supports, so
$u+\supp P=\supp P$, and a nonempty finite subset of a torsion-free group is
not invariant under a nonzero translation; hence $u=0$. Comparing nonzero
coefficients gives $\tau^{\lambda(a)}=c(\tau)$ for every $a\in\Sigma_{P,Q}$, so
$\lambda(a-b)=0$ for all $a,b\in\Sigma_{P,Q}$. Finally $W$ is a rational
subspace of $\Q\Lambda$: a vector of $\Q\Lambda$ outside $W$ is separated from
$W$ by a rational functional vanishing on $W$, which becomes integral on
$\Lambda$ after clearing denominators. So every difference $a-b$ lies in $W$,
and $V\subseteq W$.
\end{proof}

The factorization facts used are elementary: a Laurent polynomial ring is a
localization of a polynomial unique factorization domain, its units are
nonzero scalars times monomials, and an irreducible coefficient polynomial
stays prime after adjoining and inverting a purely transcendental parameter,
since its quotient ring stays a domain. No geometric assumption on $k$ is
needed. Reduction is essential: the unreduced $(T_1+T_2)/(T_1+T_2)$ has
nonzero difference rank, whereas the function is constant with order zero.
For a polynomial take $Q=1$; the extra exponent zero makes the difference span
the linear span of the support, recovering \cref{adr:sr:thm:finite}.

\subsection{Second route: the coefficient matrix \src{02}}

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_s$ be algebraically independent and let $f=P/Q$ with
Laurent polynomials $P,Q\in k[X_1^{\pm1},\ldots,X_s^{\pm1}]$, $Q\ne0$; a
common factor need \emph{not} have been cancelled. Define the vector Laurent
polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:Bvector}
 B_j=Q\,X_j\frac{\partial P}{\partial X_j}-P\,X_j\frac{\partial Q}{\partial X_j},
 \qquad B=(B_1,\ldots,B_s)=\sum_ub_uX^u,\quad b_u\in k^s,
\end{equation}
and put
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:rhocm}
 \rho_{\mathrm{cm}}(f)=\dim_k\operatorname{span}_k\{b_u:u\in\Z^s\};
\end{equation}
only finitely many $b_u$ are nonzero.

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient matrix equals support rank \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:rhocm}
Let $e_1,\ldots,e_s\in\Gamma$ be rationally independent and
$y=f(t^{e_1},\ldots,t^{e_s})$. Then $\rho(y)=\rho_{\mathrm{cm}}(f)$. In
particular the rank does not depend on the Hahn ordering of the abstract
exponent lattice or on the chosen fraction $P/Q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With $\Lambda=\Z e_1+\cdots+\Z e_s$, the support of $y$ lies in $\Lambda$. For
$\vec v\in k^s$ let $\lambda_{\vec v}$ be the $\Q$-linear functional with
$\lambda_{\vec v}(e_j)=v_j$, extended to $\Gamma$. On the rational function
field, $\partial_{\lambda_{\vec v}}$ acts as $\sum_jv_jX_j\partial_{X_j}$, so
\[
 \partial_{\lambda_{\vec v}}y=0
 \iff\sum_jv_jB_j=0
 \iff\vec v\cdot b_u=0\ \text{for every }u.
\]
By uniqueness of Hahn coefficients,
$\partial_{\lambda_{\vec v}}y=0$ if and only if $\vec v\cdot\vec n=0$ for every
supported lattice coordinate vector $\vec n\in\Z^s$. So the coefficient vectors
$b_u$ and the support coordinate vectors have the same annihilator in $k^s$,
hence spans of the same dimension; the rank of a family of rational vectors
does not change on extension from $\Q$ to a field of characteristic zero,
because every minor is rational. Both invariances follow from these
descriptions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Jacobian rank for rational functions \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:jacobianrank}
For $f_1,\ldots,f_m\in k(T_1,\ldots,T_s)$ in characteristic zero, the rank of
their ordinary, or logarithmic, Jacobian over $k(T_1,\ldots,T_s)$ equals
$\trdeg_kk(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A transcendence basis among the $f_i$ has independent differentials: extend it
to a transcendence basis of $k(T_1,\ldots,T_s)/k$; the coordinate derivations
of the purely transcendental subfield extend uniquely across the finite
separable algebraic extension, since differentiating a minimal polynomial
determines the derivative, and applying them proves independence. Every
remaining $f_i$ is separable algebraic over the field of that basis, and
differentiating its minimal polynomial puts its differential in their span.
The $dT_j$ form a basis of the module of differentials, and multiplying column
$j$ by the nonzero $T_j$ does not change rank. Compare the polynomial version
\cite[Theorem~6]{BMS}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Second proof of \cref{adr:sr:thm:rational} \src{02}]
Write $f$ as $P/Q$ in rationally independent monomials $t^{e_1},\ldots,t^{e_s}$
as above, so that $\rho(y)=\rho_{\mathrm{cm}}(f)$ by \cref{adr:sr:lem:rhocm}.
The lower bound follows from \cref{adr:sr:thm:main}. For the upper bound choose
$N$ with every $Nq_i$ a positive integer and put $T_j=t^{e_j/N}$, so that
$S_{q_i}y=f(T_1^{Nq_i},\ldots,T_s^{Nq_i})$. The $i$th logarithmic Jacobian row
is
\[
 \frac{Nq_i}{Q(T^{Nq_i})^2}\,B(T^{Nq_i}),
\]
which lies in the $k(T_1,\ldots,T_s)$-span of the fixed $k$-space spanned by
the $b_u$, of dimension $\rho_{\mathrm{cm}}(f)$. So the Jacobian has rank at
most $\rho_{\mathrm{cm}}(f)$, and \cref{adr:sr:lem:jacobianrank} gives the upper
bound. The order statement follows as in \cref{adr:sr:thm:finite}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the second upper bound is restricted \src{02}]
For an arbitrary Hahn series, Euler derivations need not span all
algebraically relevant derivations of the field generated by its dilates: a
rank-one support can have order two (\cref{adr:thm:order-two}). The
finitely generated rational function field of \cref{adr:sr:lem:jacobianrank}
is essential to the upper bound, and it is not applied to an arbitrary infinite
Hahn expansion.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{An exact algorithm and its input contract \src{02}.}
The invariant is computable by finite exact algebra.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2em]
\item Supply a finite rationally independent exponent basis and a rational
Laurent expression $P/Q$ over an effective field of characteristic zero.
\item Compute the $s$ Laurent polynomials \eqref{adr:sr:eq:Bvector} and
collect equal exponent vectors.
\item Form the matrix with rows $b_u$ and compute its exact rank.
\item Return that rank as the autonomous order, and use
\cref{adr:sr:thm:rational} for every requested finite tuple of dilations.
\end{enumerate}
This terminates for the specified representation. It makes no claim that
arbitrary surreal expressions have decidable rational linear relations, or
that equality in an arbitrary coefficient field is effective; rational and
exactly represented algebraic-number coefficients meet the input contract. A
noninjective exponent parametrization must first be reduced to a certified
rationally independent basis. For finite support one may skip differentiation
and take the rank of the support coordinate matrix. For a rational profile the
coefficient matrix matters: the union of the supports of an unreduced
numerator and denominator can give the wrong answer. The supplied verifier
checks examples; it is not a surreal computer algebra system.

\begin{example}[Examples of both routes \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:ex:rational}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item $f=T_1/T_2$ has reduced union support $\{(1,0),(0,1)\}$ and a
one-dimensional difference span, so its order is one (source~03).
\item $f=T_1/(T_1+T_2)$: $(B_1,B_2)=(T_1T_2,-T_1T_2)$ and
$\rho_{\mathrm{cm}}(f)=1$. The displayed quotient involves two independent
monomials, but $f=1/(1+T_2/T_1)$ has one intrinsic scale; where $T_2/T_1$ is
infinitesimal its expansion is $\sum_{n\ge0}(-1)^n(T_2/T_1)^n$, of infinite
support, rank one and order one, and the opposite ordering changes the
expansion but not the rank (source~02).
\item $f=(T_1+T_2)/(1+T_1T_2)$: $B_1=T_1-T_1T_2^2$ and $B_2=T_2-T_1^2T_2$, whose
coefficient vectors span $k^2$ (source~02); its reduced difference rank is two
(source~03). Its expansion is generally infinite; any two distinct positive
rational dilates are independent, any three dependent, and the order is two.
Source~02's verifier checks that multiplying numerator and denominator by
$1+T_1+2T_2$ changes neither example's invariant, and its recorded run also
lists $(T_1^2T_2^3+1)/(T_1^2T_2^3-2)$, of rank one, and the constant $5/2$, of
rank zero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{example}

\section{Worked examples and finite certificates}
\label{adr:sr:sec:examples}

In this section $T_1,T_2,T_3$ are independent monomials, abstract Laurent
variables or $T_j=\omega^{e_j}$ for rationally independent positive surreal
$e_j$. Exponent vectors refer to $T$-monomials, and an auxiliary integer weight
$w$ orders them; ``greatest $w$-weight'' corresponds to least valuation when
$T^a$ is given valuation $-w\cdot a$. The two sources' rank-three certificates
are different and both are printed.

\subsection{Two rank-three certificates}

\begin{example}[Source~03's certificate]
\label{adr:sr:ex:rank-three-03}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:worked03}
 f=T_1+T_2+T_3+T_1T_2-2T_2T_3+3T_1^2T_3.
\end{equation}
Its six supported exponent vectors span $\Q^3$ but are far from independent.
Order them by $w=(1,6,36)$. Greedy selection by decreasing weight gives
$b_1=(0,1,1)$, $b_2=(2,0,1)$, $b_3=(0,0,1)$, with coefficient product
$(-2)(3)(1)=-6$ and
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\2&0&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}=-2.
\]
With the ordinary logarithmic coordinate derivations
$T_1\partial_{T_1},T_2\partial_{T_2},T_3\partial_{T_3}$ and the dilations in the
order $4,2,1$, the leading Jacobian exponent is $4b_1+2b_2+b_3=(4,4,7)$, and
the coefficient of $T_1^4T_2^4T_3^7$ is
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:worked03coeff}
 (4\cdot2\cdot1)(-6)(-2)=96.
\end{equation}
The extra determinant factor, compared with \eqref{adr:sr:eq:leading}, comes
from using coordinate derivations instead of the dual derivations normalized
on the greedy basis; the unique-extremum argument is unchanged. So
$f,S_2f,S_4f$ are independent and $f,S_2f,S_4f,S_8f$ are dependent. The exact
expansion in source~03's program has $97$ nonzero terms; only the one term
\eqref{adr:sr:eq:worked03coeff} is needed for the proof. For an omnific
instance put $T_1=\omega$, $T_2=\omega^\omega$, $T_3=\omega^{\omega^2}$; the
directions $1,\omega,\omega^2$ are rationally independent by uniqueness of
their own normal forms, and in decreasing normal-form order
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:worked03omnific}
 x=-2\omega^{\omega^2+\omega}+3\omega^{\omega^2+2}+\omega^{\omega^2}
   +\omega^{\omega+1}+\omega^\omega+\omega.
\end{equation}
All exponents are positive and the constant coefficient is zero, so
$x\in\Oz$. It has autonomous order three for \emph{every} integer $d\ge2$.
This is exactly the kind of dependent support that Research question~1 left
open.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Source~02's certificate]
\label{adr:sr:ex:rank-three-02}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:worked02}
 f=T_1^2T_3+2T_1T_2-3T_3+5T_2+7T_1+11.
\end{equation}
Its five nonconstant supported exponents, $e_1,e_2,e_3,e_1+e_2,2e_1+e_3$ for
$T_j=\omega^{e_j}$, span dimension three but are not independent;
\cref{adr:sr:thm:finite} shows that $f,S_2f,S_4f$ are independent, that
adding $S_8f$ creates a relation, and that the order is exactly three. With
the weight $(1,4,16)$, refined lexicographically to break ties, the greedy
vectors are $b_1=(2,0,1)$, $b_2=(0,0,1)$, $b_3=(1,1,0)$, with coefficients
$1,-3,2$. The logarithmic Jacobian with rows
$\bigl(T_j\partial_{T_j}f(T_1^q,T_2^q,T_3^q)\bigr)_{j}$ for $q=1,2,4$, in this
order, has the unique leading monomial
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:worked02lead}
 -96\,T_1^9T_2T_3^6,\qquad(9,1,6)=4b_1+2b_2+b_3.
\end{equation}
Source~02's verifier expands the determinant independently and finds $45$
nonzero terms, with exactly this leading term. \mtag{} The sign differs from
\eqref{adr:sr:eq:worked03coeff} only because the rows are listed with
increasing $q$ (\cref{adr:sr:rem:ascending}); in the order $4,2,1$ the
coefficient is $(4\cdot2\cdot1)(1\cdot(-3)\cdot2)\det(b_i)=8\cdot(-6)\cdot(-2)=96$.
The auxiliary order need not be the actual order of the surreal $e_j$: the
certificate proves that the abstract Laurent polynomial is nonzero, and
evaluation at rationally independent monomials is injective. So once a
rationally independent exponent basis is certified, a finite certificate
need not decide the order of arbitrary surreal expressions.
\end{example}

\subsection{Further examples \src{03}}

\begin{example}[The linear span, not the affine span]
For $f=T_1T_2+T_1T_2^2$ the support $\{(1,1),(1,2)\}$ has affine span of
dimension one but linear span of dimension two, so any two distinct positive
rational dilates are independent and the order is two. Factoring out $T_1T_2$
does not lower the order: the common monomial factor is itself dilated and
carries another exponent direction. The theorem concerns the linear span of
the support, not the affine dimension of a Newton polytope or its number of
edges. A constant shift is different: adding a constant changes only the zero
exponent, which contributes nothing to rank, and translates every dilate by
the same constant, so it neither creates nor destroys independence.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A relative example]
Let $\Gamma$ contain rationally independent $h,\beta,\gamma$, let $H=\Q h$, and
let $f=t^h+t^\beta+t^\gamma+2t^{h+\beta+\gamma}$. Its absolute rank is three
and its rank modulo $H$ is two. Three distinct positive rational dilates are
independent over $k$, while over $K_H(k)$ any two are independent and any
three dependent. The core knows the whole $h$-direction, including arbitrary
Hahn series supported on it; this is what relative rank measures.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Power sums: a known case with explicit recovery]
\label{adr:sr:ex:power-sums}
For $f=T_1+T_2+T_3$ let $p_j=T_1^j+T_2^j+T_3^j$ and let
$\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3$ be the elementary symmetric polynomials. Newton's
identities give
\begin{align}
 \sigma_1&=p_1, & \sigma_2&=\frac{p_1^2-p_2}{2},\label{adr:sr:eq:newton12}\\
 \sigma_3&=\frac{p_4-\sigma_1^4+4\sigma_1^2\sigma_2-2\sigma_2^2}{4\sigma_1},
 \label{adr:sr:eq:newton3}
\end{align}
a rational identity, checked from $p_3=\sigma_1^3-3\sigma_1\sigma_2+3\sigma_3$
and $p_4=\sigma_1p_3-\sigma_2p_2+\sigma_3p_1$. So
$k(p_1,p_2,p_4)=k(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)$. Given these three observations
generically, $T_1,T_2,T_3$ are the roots of $Z^3-\sigma_1Z^2+\sigma_2Z-\sigma_3$,
determined up to their six permutations, and all further power sums follow
from $p_n=\sigma_1p_{n-1}-\sigma_2p_{n-2}+\sigma_3p_{n-3}$ ($n\ge4$); in
particular $S_8f=p_8$ is rational in $f,S_2f,S_4f$. These identities are
classical and illustrate the difference between an order theorem and a
reconstruction theorem. The independence of power sums is
\cite[Proposition~3]{MSW}, not a result of either source.
\end{example}

\subsection{A certificate that avoids full expansion \src{03}}
\label{adr:sr:sec:certificate}

Let $f=\sum_{a\in\Sigma}c_aT^a$ with exact rational coefficients and
$\Sigma\subseteq\Z^\rho$, $\operatorname{span}_\Q\Sigma=\Q^\rho$ (rational
exponent coordinates are first cleared by one common denominator), and let
$q_1>\cdots>q_\rho>0$ be rationals. A short independence certificate consists
of a separating integer weight, a greedy basis, its determinant, and one
exponent and coefficient. Let $N_0=\max_{a\in\Sigma,i}\abs{a_i}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:weight}
 B_0=2N_0+2,\qquad w=(1,B_0,B_0^2,\ldots,B_0^{\rho-1}).
\end{equation}
The values $w\cdot a$ are distinct on $\Sigma$: for two different vectors, at
the largest index $j$ where they differ the weighted contribution has absolute
value at least $B_0^j$, while all lower contributions total at most
$2N_0(B_0^j-1)/(B_0-1)<B_0^j$. Sort $\Sigma$ by decreasing $w\cdot a$ and keep a
vector exactly when it raises the rational rank; this gives a basis
$b_1,\ldots,b_\rho$. For the ordinary logarithmic coordinate derivations the
certified term is
\begin{align}
 \eta&=\sum_{j=1}^\rho q_jb_j,\label{adr:sr:eq:certificate-eta}\\
 c_*&=\Bigl(\prod_jq_j\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_jc_{b_j}\Bigr)\det[b_{ji}]_{j,i},
 \label{adr:sr:eq:certificate-c}
\end{align}
the unique term of greatest $w$-weight with nonzero determinant contribution.
The proof of \cref{adr:sr:prop:unique} is applied with the scalar weights
$w\cdot a$ in this finite setting; no claim that $w$ preserves every comparison
of an ambient surreal group is needed. Since $c_*\ne0$ the Jacobian is not the
zero Laurent polynomial, and an embedding into any Hahn field with independent
monomial coordinates preserves this. For fewer than $\rho$ observations, take
the full greedy basis, invert its matrix, and use the first $m$ dual
functionals; the coefficient is then $\prod_jq_j\prod_jc_{b_j}$, as in
\cref{adr:sr:thm:leading}. Source~03's program checks both forms.

\paragraph{What is effective \src{03}.} With finite rational-coordinate input,
the certificate uses a sort, at most $\abs{\Sigma}$ exact rank-increase tests,
and one determinant or matrix inverse; standard exact linear algebra makes this
polynomial time in the encoded input size, with separating weights of bit
length $O(\rho\log(N_0+1))$. It certifies independence; it does not expand a
possibly huge minimal relation, and the full expansions in the program are
deliberately more expensive and are not the proposed method. For a surreal
expression whose exponent dependencies are unresolved, an integer-coordinate
presentation is additional input: neither source decides arbitrary equality or
rational dependence of symbolic surreal exponents, and the greatest-element
choices in the infinite-support proof are existence arguments, not a
termination claim for encodings of infinite normal forms.

\section{Infinite supports}
\label{adr:sr:sec:infinite}

\subsection{Difference transcendence from infinite rank}

\begin{corollary}[Infinite-rank orbits \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:cor:infinite}
If $\rho_{\mathcal F,H}(y)$ is infinite, then $\{S_ay:a\in\mathcal F_{>0}\}$ is
algebraically independent over $K_H(k)$. In particular, for $\mathcal F=\Q$,
$y$ satisfies no nonzero autonomous relation of any finite order over $K_H(k)$
for any rational $d>0$, $d\ne1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A relation involves finitely many members; apply \cref{adr:sr:thm:main}, or
\cref{adr:sr:cor:relation}.
\end{proof}

This difference transcendence is over the stated base only. It is not
differential transcendence under a chosen derivation, and it claims no
independence from further surreal parameters, which need the relative version
(\cref{adr:sr:sec:fresh}).

\begin{theorem}[A countable omnific support with an independent real orbit \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:Xi}
The omnific integer
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:Xi}
 \Xi=\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{\omega^{-n}}=\omega+\omega^{\omega^{-1}}+\omega^{\omega^{-2}}+\cdots
\end{equation}
has $\{S_a\Xi:a\in\R_{>0}\}$ algebraically independent over $\C$
(source~03). The same holds for $\Xi-\omega=\sum_{n\ge1}\omega^{\omega^{-n}}$
with rational dilations, $\{S_q(\Xi-\omega):q\in\Q_{>0}\}$ (source~02). Both
elements have support of order type $\omega$ and are difference transcendental
for every rational $d>0$, $d\ne1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exponents $\omega^{-n}$ form a strictly decreasing sequence of positive
surreals, so the support is legitimate, and the element is omnific by
\eqref{adr:eq:Oz}. The exponents are linearly independent over $\R$: a
nontrivial finite real combination of the distinct monomials $\omega^{-n}$ is a
normal form with distinct inner exponents $-n$ and cannot vanish. Let $\Gamma$
be their set-sized $\R$-span, put $\mathcal F=\R$, $k=\C$, $H=0$, and apply
\cref{adr:sr:cor:infinite}; for rational dilations one may use $\mathcal F=\Q$
and the rational span, as source~02 does.
\end{proof}

The two appearances of $\omega$ have different roles: the inner monomials are
independent \emph{exponents}, the outer monomials are the terms of the omnific
integer. The support is countable, but its exponent directions have infinite
dimension even over $\R$, which is why continuum many positive real dilations
can be independent. This is not a statement about classical transcendence of
real exponentials.

\begin{corollary}[Forward, backward and several dilations \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:cor:shifts}
For every $d\in\Q_{>0}\setminus\{1\}$ the elements $S_d^n(\Xi-\omega)$,
$n\in\Z$, are algebraically independent over $\C$. For multiplicatively
independent positive rationals $d_1,\ldots,d_s$ the family
$\{S_{d_1}^{n_1}\cdots S_{d_s}^{n_s}(\Xi-\omega):(n_1,\ldots,n_s)\in\Z^s\}$ is
algebraically independent over $\C$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
These are subfamilies of the positive rational orbit, with distinct
parameters under the stated hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Bounded denominators: two character arguments}

\begin{lemma}[Character extension \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:characters}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero. Every homomorphism
from a subgroup $A$ of an abelian group $G$ to $k^\times$ extends to $G$, and a
finite abelian group $A$ has exactly $\abs{A}$ such characters.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take a maximal extension by Zorn's lemma. If $x$ is outside its domain and no
positive multiple $nx$ lies in the domain, give $x$ any nonzero value;
otherwise take the least such $n$ and give $x$ an $n$th root of the value at
$nx$. This extends the character to the domain plus $\Z x$, contradicting
maximality. The count follows from a decomposition into cyclic groups, since a
cyclic group of order $n$ has exactly $n$ characters: $k$ contains $n$
distinct $n$th roots of unity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite denominator index for algebraic Hahn elements \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:denominators}
Let $\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma$ be free abelian of finite rank and
$y\in k((t^{\Q\Lambda}))$ algebraic of degree at most $N$ over $k(t^\Lambda)$.
Then
\[
 \mathcal D_y=(\Lambda+\langle\supp y\rangle_\Z)/\Lambda
\]
is finite of order at most $N$. Consequently $\supp y\subseteq N'^{-1}\Lambda$
for some integer $1\le N'\le N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First let $k$ be algebraically closed. Each character
$\chi:\Q\Lambda/\Lambda\to k^\times$ defines the strong Hahn automorphism
$\tau_\chi(\sum c_\gamma t^\gamma)=\sum c_\gamma\chi(\gamma+\Lambda)t^\gamma$,
fixing $k(t^\Lambda)$. All $\tau_\chi(y)$ are roots of one nonzero polynomial
of degree at most $N$ over that field, so the character orbit of $y$ has at
most $N$ elements. Two characters act equally on $y$ exactly when their
restrictions to $\mathcal D_y$ agree: equality is detected at every nonzero
coefficient, and those support classes generate $\mathcal D_y$. Every
character of $\mathcal D_y$ extends to $\Q\Lambda/\Lambda$ by
\cref{adr:sr:lem:characters}, so if $\mathcal D_y$ is finite its
$\abs{\mathcal D_y}$ characters give that many distinct conjugates, and
$\abs{\mathcal D_y}\le N$. If $\mathcal D_y$ were infinite, its elements would
have unbounded orders: for fixed $e$ the $e$-torsion of
$\Q\Lambda/\Lambda\cong\Q^\rho/\Z^\rho$ has only $e^\rho$ elements, so a uniform
bound would put $\mathcal D_y$ in a finite group. An element of order $e>N$
has $e$ characters on its cyclic subgroup, which extend and give more than $N$
conjugates, a contradiction. Taking $N'=\abs{\mathcal D_y}$ kills every support
class. For general $k$, embed coefficientwise in an algebraic closure: the
polynomial keeps degree at most $N$, and the support and index are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A rank-one character proof \src{03}]
\label{adr:sr:lem:denominators}
If $f\in\C((t^\Q))$ is algebraic over $\C(t)$, then
$\supp f\subseteq\frac1N\Z$ for some integer $N\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each integer $j$, $\chi_j(g)=\exp(2\pi ijg)$ is a character of $(\Q,+)$,
and $\tau_j(\sum c_gt^g)=\sum c_g\chi_j(g)t^g$ is a support-preserving field
automorphism fixing $\C(t)$, since $\chi_j(1)=1$. If $F(f)=0$ for a nonzero
$F\in\C(t)[Y]$, all $\tau_j(f)$ are among its finitely many roots, so
$\tau_a(f)=\tau_b(f)$ for some integers $a>b$ and $\tau_{a-b}(f)=f$. At a
nonzero coefficient at $g$ this gives $\exp(2\pi i(a-b)g)=1$, so
$(a-b)g\in\Z$; take $N=a-b$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{adr:sr:lem:denominators} is the case $\Lambda=\Z$, $k=\C$ of
\cref{adr:sr:thm:denominators}, without the degree bound; both are printed
because their proofs differ (explicit exponential characters against extended
abstract characters). Both conditions are necessary, not sufficient: a series
with bounded denominators, or on a fixed lattice, can be transcendental over
the monomial field. They are character-orbit versions of a familiar
ramification obstruction, and neither source claims the character method as
new.

\subsection{Rank one, order two}

\begin{proposition}[The rank-one order-two example \src{03}]
\label{adr:sr:prop:order-two}
For $d\ge2$ the omnific integer $u_d=\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{d^{-n}}$ of
\cref{adr:thm:order-two} has rational support rank one and autonomous order
two under $S_d$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof \src{03}; a second route to \cref{adr:thm:order-two}]
The support is decreasing, positive and in $\Q$, with one-dimensional rational
span. Its denominators are unbounded, so \cref{adr:sr:lem:denominators}, read
in $\C((t^\Q))$ through $\omega=t^{-1}$, shows that $u_d$ is transcendental
over $\C(\omega)$. Shifting the Hahn sum gives $S_du_d-u_d=\omega^d$, so
$\C(u_d,S_du_d)=\C(u_d,\omega^d)$, of transcendence degree two: $\omega^d$ is
transcendental over $\C$, and $u_d$ is transcendental over $\C(\omega)$,
hence over $\C(\omega^d)$. So the first two transforms are independent, while
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:rankone}
 S_d^2u_d-S_du_d=(S_du_d-u_d)^d
\end{equation}
makes the first three dependent.
\end{proof}

This proof uses neither \cref{adr:thm:omnific} nor the support-collapse theorem
behind it. The example does not contradict \cref{adr:sr:cor:relation}, since
order two allows rank one. It refutes the unwarranted extension of the
finite-support \emph{upper} bound to infinite supports, which is why the
infinite-support theorem is a lower bound, not an equality. Infinite support
alone does not force difference transcendence either:
\cref{adr:sr:ex:rational}(b) has infinite support and order one (source~02).
Infinite support, ordinal support type, unbounded denominators (an infinite
index modulo a lattice) and infinite rational rank are four different
properties, and only the last forces independence of all dilates.

\subsection{Rank \texorpdfstring{$\rho$}{rho}, order \texorpdfstring{$\rho+1$}{rho+1}: multi-scale telescopes}

Fix an integer $d\ge2$, rationally independent $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_\rho\in\Gamma_{>0}$
and nonzero $a_1,\ldots,a_\rho\in k$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:telescopes}
 U_j=\sum_{n\ge0}t^{-\gamma_jd^{-n}},\qquad \mathcal Z_\rho=\sum_{j=1}^\rho a_jU_j.
\end{equation}
Each support $\{-\gamma_jd^{-n}\}$ is strictly increasing, a finite union of
well-ordered sets is well ordered, and no two terms from different $j$ share
an exponent, by rational independence. For $\Gamma\subseteq\No$ and real
$a_j$, \eqref{adr:eq:omega-bridge} gives
$\mathcal Z_\rho=\sum_ja_j\sum_n\omega^{\gamma_jd^{-n}}$.

\begin{theorem}[Support rank $\rho$, autonomous order $\rho+1$ \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:telescopes}
The series $\mathcal Z_\rho$ has infinite support, $\rho(\mathcal Z_\rho)=\rho$,
and $\operatorname{aord}_{d,k}(\mathcal Z_\rho)=\rho+1$. For real nonzero $a_j$
and positive surreal $\gamma_j$ it is an omnific integer, and its order is
$\rho+1$ over $\C$ as well.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Strong reindexing of the supports gives
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:delta}
 w_\rho:=(S_d-1)\mathcal Z_\rho=\sum_{j=1}^\rho a_jt^{-d\gamma_j},
\end{equation}
a finite-support element of rank $\rho$, so
$w_\rho,S_dw_\rho,\ldots,S_d^{\rho-1}w_\rho$ are independent by
\cref{adr:sr:thm:finite}. Let $\Lambda=\sum_j\Z\gamma_j$ and
$K_0=k(t^{\gamma_1},\ldots,t^{\gamma_\rho})$. The support classes of
$\mathcal Z_\rho$ modulo $\Lambda$ include $\gamma_j/d^n+\Lambda$, of exact
order $d^n$, so the group $\mathcal D_{\mathcal Z_\rho}$ of
\cref{adr:sr:thm:denominators} is infinite and $\mathcal Z_\rho$ is
transcendental over $K_0$. All the listed dilates of $w_\rho$ lie in $K_0$, so
$\mathcal Z_\rho,w_\rho,S_dw_\rho,\ldots,S_d^{\rho-1}w_\rho$ are $\rho+1$
independent elements. The invertible triangular change of variables taking
successive differences identifies the field they generate with
$k(\mathcal Z_\rho,S_d\mathcal Z_\rho,\ldots,S_d^\rho\mathcal Z_\rho)$, so the
first $\rho+1$ transforms are independent. Every nonnegative transform lies in
$K_0(\mathcal Z_\rho)$ by \eqref{adr:sr:eq:delta} and its iterates, a field of
transcendence degree $\rho+1$, so the first $\rho+2$ are dependent.
Membership in $\Oz$ follows from positivity of all growth exponents and zero
constant term, and the argument is unchanged after extending real
coefficients to $\C$.
\end{proof}

The case $\rho=1$, $a_1=1$, $\gamma_1=1$ is $u_d$ of \cref{adr:thm:order-two},
so this is a third route to that theorem. The decreasing-denominator
construction itself is in Chyzak--Dreyfus--Dumas--Mezzarobba
\cite[Remark~2.18]{CDDM}, with a shifted starting index; source~02 claims only
the exact-order calculation for every finite number of independent scales.

\begin{example}[An explicit third-order equation \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:ex:third}
Take $\rho=2$, $d=2$, $a_1=a_2=1$, write $\mathcal Z=\mathcal Z_2$, and put
$w_1=S_2\mathcal Z-\mathcal Z$, $w_2=S_2^2\mathcal Z-S_2\mathcal Z$,
$w_3=S_2^3\mathcal Z-S_2^2\mathcal Z$. With $A=t^{-2\gamma_1}$ and
$B=t^{-2\gamma_2}$ these are $A+B$, $A^2+B^2$ and $A^4+B^4$, so
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:third}
 2w_3-w_2^2-2w_1^2w_2+w_1^4=0.
\end{equation}
Source~02's verifier expands the left side to zero.
\cref{adr:sr:thm:telescopes}, not this identity, proves that no lower-order
relation exists.
\end{example}

\paragraph{The finite-truncation boundary \src{02}.}
For $U_N=\sum_{n=0}^Nt^{-\gamma d^{-n}}$,
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:boundary}
 (S_d-1)U_N=t^{-d\gamma}-t^{-\gamma d^{-N}}.
\end{equation}
Dropping the last term at a finite stage is wrong. The infinite identity
follows from strong cancellation of the reindexed supports, not from the last
monomial tending to zero in a valuation topology; the exponents are bounded,
so it does not. This is the identity of Appendix~\ref{adr:app:checks} at
$\gamma=1$.

\subsection{A family with a candidate gap \texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{l} \src{03}}

For $\ell\ge1$ put
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:udl}
 u_{d,\ell}=\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{d^{-\ell n}}.
\end{equation}
The same support argument makes it an omnific integer of rational rank one.
Directly $S_d^\ell u_{d,\ell}-u_{d,\ell}=\omega^{d^\ell}$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:udl-relation}
 S_d^{\ell+1}u_{d,\ell}-S_du_{d,\ell}=\bigl(S_d^\ell u_{d,\ell}-u_{d,\ell}\bigr)^d,
\end{equation}
an upper bound $\ell+1$ on its order. Equality for general $\ell$ is not
proved; \cref{adr:sr:prop:order-two} is the case $\ell=1$. It is a concrete
target for the gap between rank and order on one rational exponent line,
without the false claim that rank classifies infinite supports.

\begin{remark}[The two hierarchies are different]
\label{adr:sr:rem:udl}
\mtag{} Source~02's \cref{adr:sr:thm:telescopes} does not settle the order of
$u_{d,\ell}$. It realizes the gap order $-$ rank $=1$ at every rank $\rho$,
using $\rho$ rationally independent scales, whereas $u_{d,\ell}$ has rank one
and would realize the gap $\ell$. Its dilates $S_d^iu_{d,\ell}$, $0\le i<\ell$,
live on one rational line, so the transcendence argument over a lattice field
used there does not separate them. Source~02 asks the parallel question for its
iterated telescopes (Research question~\ref{adr:sr:q:iterated}). Whether the
order at fixed rank can exceed rank plus one on an omnific integer is open.
\end{remark}

\section{Joint independence and fresh parameters}
\label{adr:sr:sec:fresh}

\begin{theorem}[Joint independence for separated support spans \src{03, 02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:blocks}
Let $y_1,\ldots,y_s\in\K$ and $V_j=(H+\operatorname{span}_{\mathcal F}\supp y_j)/H\subseteq\Gamma/H$,
and suppose $V_1+\cdots+V_s$ is a direct sum. For each $j$ choose
$m_j\le\dim_{\mathcal F}V_j$ distinct positive $a_{j1},\ldots,a_{jm_j}\in\mathcal F$.
Then all $S_{a_{j\nu}}y_j$ ($1\le j\le s$, $1\le\nu\le m_j$) are jointly
algebraically independent over $K_H(k)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In each support choose the first $m_j$ greedy exponents modulo $H$. Because
the sum is direct, the corresponding coordinate functionals can be chosen to
annihilate $H$ and every $V_l$, $l\ne j$, while being dual to the selected
exponents of $y_j$; extend them to $\Gamma$ by a set-sized basis extension.
The derivative matrix with rows all chosen dilates and columns all these
Euler derivations is block diagonal: a functional chosen for $V_j$ kills every
exponent of $y_l$, $l\ne j$, and its positive multiples. Each diagonal block
has nonzero determinant by the proof of \cref{adr:sr:thm:leading}, so the whole
determinant is nonzero, and \cref{adr:sr:lem:jacobian} applies.
\end{proof}

Source~02 states this for $\mathcal F=\Q$ with $y_\alpha\in K_{V_\alpha}(k)$,
$\operatorname{span}_\Q\supp y_\alpha=V_\alpha$ and $H+\sum_\alpha V_\alpha$
direct, for an arbitrary index family of which finitely many members are used;
its functionals are extended by zero on $H$ and the other $V_\beta$ and each
block is \cref{adr:sr:thm:vector}. Since a relation involves finitely many
members, the formulations are equivalent. Disjoint supports do not suffice:
two disjoint sets of exponents can span the same direction, and the hypothesis
is independence of the \emph{spans modulo the core}.

\subsection{An explicit ordinal family \src{03}}

For each ordinal $\alpha$ and $n\ge0$ put
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:ordinal}
 g_{\alpha,n}=\omega^{\alpha+1/(n+2)},\qquad \Xi_\alpha=\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{g_{\alpha,n}},
\end{equation}
with surreal addition in $\alpha+1/(n+2)$, a value strictly between $\alpha$ and
$\alpha+1$. For fixed $\alpha$ the $g_{\alpha,n}$ are positive and strictly
decreasing, so $\Xi_\alpha\in\Oz$. The inner exponents are distinct even across
ordinals: for $\alpha<\beta$,
$\alpha+1/(n+2)<\alpha+1\le\beta<\beta+1/(m+2)$. So the $g_{\alpha,n}$ are
distinct Conway monomials, and every finite set of them is linearly
independent over $\R$.

\begin{theorem}[A class of independent real-dilation orbits \src{03}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:ordinal}
The family $\{S_a\Xi_\alpha:\alpha\in\mathbf{On},\ a\in\R_{>0}\}$ is
algebraically independent over $\C$: every finite subfamily with distinct
pairs $(\alpha,a)$ is.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A proposed relation uses finitely many ordinals and finitely many dilations of
each. The $\R$-spans of those supports are in direct sum, each infinite
dimensional, with set-sized total span. Apply \cref{adr:sr:thm:blocks} with
$\mathcal F=\R$, $k=\C$, $H=0$.
\end{proof}

Source~03 proposes the explicit orbitwise and relative structure as new, not
the bare existence of many algebraically independent surreals. The family is
not asserted to be a transcendence basis, or to generate $\SC$.

\begin{theorem}[Freshness beyond every set-sized Hahn core \src{03}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:tail}
Let $\mathfrak P\subseteq\SC$ be a set. There are a set-sized $\R$-subspace
$H\subseteq\No$ and an ordinal $\alpha_0$ with $\mathfrak P\subseteq\C((t^H))$,
under \eqref{adr:eq:omega-bridge}, such that
\[
 \{S_a\Xi_\alpha:\alpha\ge\alpha_0,\ a\in\R_{>0}\}
\]
is algebraically independent over the \emph{entire} Hahn field $\C((t^H))$,
hence over the field generated by $\C$ and $\mathfrak P$ and over all positive
real dilates of the parameters.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $H$ be the $\R$-span of the union of the normal-form supports of the
members of $\mathfrak P$; it is a set and places $\mathfrak P$ in the stated
field. Every $h\in H$ has a set-sized normal form, and the union of all these
second-level supports is a set. Ordinals are cofinal in the surreals, so
choose $\alpha_0$ above every exponent of that union (equivalently, bound the
birthdays first). For $\alpha\ge\alpha_0$ the monomial $g_{\alpha,n}$ has its
single normal-form exponent above $\alpha_0$, and distinct such monomials have
distinct exponents; a nontrivial real combination of them has a nonzero normal
form supported above $\alpha_0$ and is not in $H$. So these $g_{\alpha,n}$ are
linearly independent modulo $H$, with the ordinal blocks in direct sum. For a
proposed finite relation let $\Gamma$ be the $\R$-span of $H$ and the
finitely many countable supports involved, and apply
\cref{adr:sr:thm:blocks} over $K_H(\C)$. As $H$ is an $\R$-subspace, positive
real dilations preserve its Hahn field, which contains the dilates of
$\mathfrak P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One countable-support witness over any set of parameters \src{03}]
\label{adr:sr:cor:witness}
For every set $\mathfrak P\subseteq\SC$ some $z\in\Oz$ of countable support has
its whole positive real dilation orbit algebraically independent over
$\C(\mathfrak P)$: take $z=\Xi_{\alpha_0}$.
\end{corollary}

The parameter set may be of any size as long as it is a set. There is no
claim of independence over the proper class $\SC$, which contains $z$; and
there is no single ordinal threshold for every parameter set: the explicit
family is fixed, but its independent tail depends on the prescribed set.

\subsection{Set-sized families over a prescribed base \src{02}}

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary set-sized families over a prescribed base \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:kappa}
Let $\mathfrak B$ be a set-sized subfield of $\SC$ and $\kappa$ a set
cardinal. There are $\Upsilon_\alpha\in\Oz$, $\alpha<\kappa$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:kappa}
 \{S_q\Upsilon_\alpha:\alpha<\kappa,\ q\in\Q_{>0}\}
 \ \text{is algebraically independent over }\mathfrak B,
\end{equation}
each with positive support of order type $\omega$ and all coefficients one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $H$ be the rational span of the union of the outer normal-form supports of
the elements of $\mathfrak B$: a set-sized rational subspace of $\No$ with
$\mathfrak B\subseteq\C((t^H))$. Let $\mathcal I$ be the union of the inner
normal-form supports of all $h\in H$, also a set. Choose an ordinal $\nu_0$
with $-\nu_0<\tau$ for every $\tau\in\mathcal I$; it exists because a set of
surreals is bounded above by an ordinal (bound the lengths of their sign
sequences and apply this to the negatives), and any positive ordinal will do
if $\mathcal I$ is empty. For $\alpha<\kappa$ and $n\ge0$ put
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:fresh}
 \beta_{\alpha,n}=\nu_0+\omega\alpha+n+1,\qquad
 h_{\alpha,n}=\omega^{-\beta_{\alpha,n}},\qquad
 \Upsilon_\alpha=\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{h_{\alpha,n}}.
\end{equation}
The additions and the product in $\beta_{\alpha,n}$ are \emph{ordinal}
operations; the minus sign and the two monomials are surreal. The
$\beta_{\alpha,n}$ are pairwise distinct and increase with $n$ for fixed
$\alpha$, so $\Upsilon_\alpha$ has the claimed omnific support. A nonzero
finite rational combination of the $h_{\alpha,n}$ has inner support at the
distinct exponents $-\beta_{\alpha,n}$, all below $-\nu_0$, while every $h\in H$
has inner support in $\mathcal I$, above $-\nu_0$; uniqueness of normal forms
shows that the whole family is rationally independent modulo $H$, and that a
combination vanishes only when all coefficients do. With
$V_\alpha=\operatorname{span}_\Q\{h_{\alpha,n}:n\ge0\}$, the sum of $H$ and the
$V_\alpha$ is direct and each $V_\alpha$ is infinite dimensional. Apply
\cref{adr:sr:thm:blocks} to each finite subfamily, in the set-sized Hahn field
of that direct sum; independence over $K_H(\C)$ implies independence over
$\mathfrak B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Free omnific difference algebras \src{02}]
\label{adr:sr:cor:free}
The assignment $X_{\alpha,q}\mapsto S_q\Upsilon_\alpha$ gives injective ring
homomorphisms
\begin{align}
 \Z[X_{\alpha,q}:\alpha<\kappa,\ q\in\Q_{>0}]&\hookrightarrow\Oz,\label{adr:sr:eq:freeZ}\\
 \Z[i][X_{\alpha,q}:\alpha<\kappa,\ q\in\Q_{>0}]&\hookrightarrow\Goz.\label{adr:sr:eq:freeG}
\end{align}
For each $r\in\Q_{>0}$ they are equivariant for the permutation
$X_{\alpha,q}\mapsto X_{\alpha,rq}$ and $S_r$. Restricting to $q=d^n$,
$n\in\Z$, gives a free inversive difference polynomial ring for $S_d$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Algebraic independence gives injectivity. Positive support and integral,
respectively Gaussian integral, constant coefficients are preserved by these
polynomial operations and by every $S_r$; equivariance is $S_rS_q=S_{rq}$.
\end{proof}

There is no embedding of $\C$ into $\Oz$ here. With coefficients in $\C$ the
polynomial ring embeds into the nonnegative-growth-support surcomplex ring
(the ring $\A$ for $k=\C$), and its fraction field into $\SC$. With
coefficients in the prescribed $\mathfrak B$, independence gives ordinary
polynomial and rational function field embeddings into $\SC$; equivariance
with a fixed coefficient field additionally needs a specified compatible
action on $\mathfrak B$. The theorem quantifies over set-sized bases and
families; it constructs no set of proper-class transcendence degree and asserts
no independence over all of $\SC$, which is impossible. It also does not make
$(\SC,S_d)$ existentially difference closed: $S_dx-x=1$ has no solution, since
the left side has zero constant coefficient. Large free subalgebras and
solvability of difference equations are different properties.

\mtag{} The two constructions differ: source~03 places its fresh exponents
\emph{above} an ordinal bound (large inner exponents, real dilations, one
ordinal-indexed family), source~02 places them \emph{below} the negative of
one (infinitesimal inner exponents, rational dilations, $\kappa$ many orbits
with coefficients one, and ring embeddings). Both separate a set-sized base by
bounding the second-level supports of its exponent span, the localization
also used by the independent-copies report (\lbl{isc:cor:parameters}); neither
this report nor that one uses the other's results.

\section{Prime characteristic}
\label{adr:sr:sec:charp}

Characteristic zero is essential to \cref{adr:sr:thm:main}. For coefficients
in $\mathbb F_p$, Frobenius gives $S_pf=f^p$; for $y=T_1+T_2$ with independent
monomials, $S_py=T_1^p+T_2^p=y^p$, a dependence at rank two
(\cref{adr:sec:boundaries}). Both the factor $q=p$ in the logarithmic
Jacobian and the minimal-degree argument fail. There is nevertheless an exact
finite-support formula after grouping dilations by their $p$-free parts.

\begin{theorem}[Prime-field finite-support formula \src{03}]
\label{adr:sr:thm:prime}
Let $\Lambda$ be a finitely generated ordered abelian group and
$f=\sum_{a\in\Sigma}c_at^a\in\mathbb F_p[t^\Lambda]$ with nonzero coefficients
and $\Sigma$ generating $\Lambda$ as an abelian group; let
$\rho=\operatorname{rank}_\Z\Lambda$. For positive integers
$n_1,\ldots,n_m$ put $n_j^{(p)}=n_j/p^{v_p(n_j)}$ and
$s=\abs{\{n_1^{(p)},\ldots,n_m^{(p)}\}}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{adr:sr:eq:prime}
 \trdeg_{\mathbb F_p}\mathbb F_p(S_{n_1}f,\ldots,S_{n_m}f)=\min\{\rho,s\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The torsion-free group $\Lambda$ has a $\Z$-basis, identifying its group
algebra with a Laurent polynomial ring in $\rho$ variables; the images of
$\Sigma$ span $\mathbb F_p^\rho$, since $\Sigma$ generates $\Lambda$. The
coordinates modulo $p$ give derivations $\partial_i(t^a)=(a_i\bmod p)t^a$. For
distinct positive integers $u_1>\cdots>u_\ell$ prime to $p$, $\ell\le\rho$,
select a greedy independent support tuple using the projected vectors over
$\mathbb F_p$ and dual functionals there. Dominance uses only linear rank, so
it holds over $\mathbb F_p$; rearrangement uses the ordered group $\Lambda$ and
positive \emph{integer} weights $u_j$, not an order on $\mathbb F_p$, each
exchange moving the exponent by a positive integer multiple of a positive
element of $\Lambda$. The unique extremal determinant term has nonzero
coefficient: each $u_j$ is nonzero modulo $p$, each selected $c_a$ is nonzero,
and the projected determinant is nonzero. So the derivative matrix is
invertible. The minimal-relation argument of \cref{adr:sr:lem:jacobian} still
applies over the perfect field $\mathbb F_p$: if all formal partial
derivatives of a minimal relation vanished, every exponent in it would be
divisible by $p$, and since coefficients have $p$th roots in $\mathbb F_p$ it
would be $F_1^p$ for a smaller relation $F_1$; otherwise a nonzero partial
derivative gives the usual contradiction. So the $\ell$ observations are
independent. The bound $\rho$ comes from the Laurent monomial field, the bound
$\ell$ from the number of observations. Finally
$S_{n_j}f=(S_{n_j^{(p)}}f)^{p^{v_p(n_j)}}$, so the field generated by one
observation for each distinct $p$-free part contains the original observation
field and is algebraic over it; the transcendence degrees agree.
\end{proof}

The lattice must be the one \emph{generated by the support}, not an arbitrary
larger one: $T_1^p+T_2^p$ has zero ordinary coordinate derivatives in
$\mathbb F_p(T_1,T_2)$, but in its support lattice, with monomials
$T_1^p,T_2^p$, it has full rank two. The theorem is for prime-field
coefficients: other characteristic-$p$ coefficients bring Frobenius twists and
are not covered. Source~03 includes this complement with proof as a structural
comparison, not as a separately certified priority claim; source~02 asserts no
positive-characteristic version.

\section{Scope, questions and non-claims of the additions}
\label{adr:sr:sec:scope}

\subsection{Boundaries}
\label{adr:sr:sec:boundaries}

\paragraph{Positivity and distinctness \src{03, 02}.} Negative dilation does
not preserve general Hahn supports, and even for finite support mixed signs
destroy the conclusion: $f=T_1+T_1^{-1}+T_2+T_2^{-1}$ has rank two but
$S_{-1}f=f$, so the multipliers $1,-1$ give a dependent pair. Strict
positivity is used in the coordinatewise comparison and in the rearrangement.
Repeated multipliers repeat an observation, and $d=1$ makes $S_d$ the identity,
so the exact order fails except accidentally in the smallest ranks. A zero
multiplier is not in the dilation group and would collapse all monomials to
one, generally incompatible with infinite summation.

\paragraph{Rational rank and real rank \src{03}.} The lower bound of
\cref{adr:sr:thm:main} holds over any $\mathcal F$, including $\R$, but the
exact upper bounds of \cref{adr:sr:thm:finite,adr:sr:thm:rational} use
rational dilations and rational rank, and do not become exact with real rank
and real dilations. The finite-support $f=\omega$ has real rank one, yet $f$
and $S_{\sqrt2}f=\omega^{\sqrt2}$ are independent over $\C$: a relation would
need two distinct nonnegative integer pairs with equal exponents $i+j\sqrt2$.
The real-rank bound promises only transcendence degree one there;
\cref{adr:sr:thm:Xi} uses \emph{infinite} real rank on purpose.

\paragraph{The coefficient field is part of the statement \src{02}.} A
nonconstant coefficient can create a relation absent over $k$:
$S_du_d-u_d=\omega^d$ is linear once $\omega^d$ is a coefficient. The relative
theorem controls directions outside the core's exponent space, but an
absolute-order calculation is not preserved under arbitrary enlargement of the
base. In \cref{adr:sr:thm:tail,adr:sr:thm:kappa} the base is fixed first and
the fresh scales chosen afterwards; exchanging these quantifiers would give an
impossible statement.

\paragraph{No conclusions about other structures \src{03, 02}.} The Euler
derivations prove nothing about differential transcendence under the natural
surreal derivation. Exponent dilation is not the surreal exponential, and no
exponential-field automorphism is classified. The omnific statements concern
normal-form support and algebraic difference equations, not factorization,
primality, Diophantine decidability, integral-closure or normalization fibres,
holonomic functions under argument dilation, analytic convergence on $\No$,
birthdays, or the first-order theory of the surreals with dilations; source~03's
exploratory reading of quotient and normalization reports supplies no theorem
on those subjects. The finite algorithms assume exact finite exponent
coordinates and supply no notation system for all surreals; the ordinal family
is not a maximal transcendence basis; and written proofs and exact experiments
are different kinds of evidence.

is justified only when one factor is supported in the subgroup; positivity and
distinctness of the weights are both invoked. Neither source supplies Lean
code, and neither chain has been checked by the repository's build.

\subsection{Research questions of the additions}
\label{adr:sr:sec:questions}

The questions below continue the numbering of \cref{adr:sec:questions}. They
are the sources' proposed directions, not previously published open problems.
Where the two sources ask essentially the same question it is printed once,
with both formulations.

\begin{question}[Exact order of the cyclic-denominator family; high priority; source~03]
\label{adr:sr:q:udl}
For $u_{d,\ell}$ in \eqref{adr:sr:eq:udl}, is the order under $S_d$ exactly
$\ell+1$? \Cref{adr:sr:eq:udl-relation} gives the upper bound, and
\cref{adr:sr:prop:order-two} settles $\ell=1$. A first target is independence
of $u_{d,2},S_du_{d,2},S_d^2u_{d,2}$ over $\C$, which would begin to measure the
order-rank gap on one rational exponent line.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Difference transcendence on one exponent line; source~03]
Construct, with a transparent proof, an omnific integer of rational rank one
that is difference transcendental under a fixed $S_d$, or show that a proposed
broad class of rank-one supports cannot be. The infinite-rank constructions do
not settle this. A coefficient-twist or lacunarity argument controlling several
transforms at once, rather than algebraicity over one monomial field, may help.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[The order spectrum at fixed rank; source~02]
For fixed rational rank $\rho$, which autonomous orders occur among
infinite-support Hahn series, and among omnific integers? Rational monomial
profiles have order $\rho$ and the telescopes of \cref{adr:sr:thm:telescopes}
order $\rho+1$; an infinite rational profile need not be omnific, so these are
not yet realizations in one class. Can every integer at least $\rho$ occur? Can
infinite order occur at rank one? A classification must separate the
support-lattice index from the rank.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Iterated telescopes on one rational scale; source~02]
\label{adr:sr:q:iterated}
For $s\ge0$ let $u_d^{[s]}=\sum_{n\ge0}\binom ns\omega^{d^{-n}}$, with
$\binom ns=0$ for $n<s$. Reindexing gives $(S_d-1)u_d^{[s]}=u_d^{[s-1]}$ for
$s\ge1$ and $(S_d-1)u_d^{[0]}=\omega^d$, so the order is at most $s+2$. Is it
exactly $s+2$? The character obstruction gives transcendence over the monomial
field but not joint independence of the intermediate $u_d^{[j]}$, which is not
proved.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Support structure at fixed order; sources~03 and~02]
Source~03: with $\rho\le2$ now forced for order-two equations, which
well-ordered supports in a fixed two-dimensional rational exponent space occur?
Does a fixed relation bound any support invariant beyond rank? Separate order
type, denominator growth and accumulation at nonprincipal cuts; this is the
remaining geometric and combinatorial part of Research question~2. Source~02: a
relation in $m$ dilates forces rank at most $m-1$; what further restrictions
follow from a bound on the degree? Candidate invariants are denominator growth,
the subgroup generated under $d$ and $d^{-1}$, and ordinal type. The rank
theorem is not a claim that supports lie in finitely many arithmetic
progressions; find a structure theorem or families ruling one out.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Degrees of observation fields; high priority; sources~03 and~02]
Source~03: for reduced $f=P/Q$ of rank $\rho$ and distinct positive integers
$n_1,\ldots,n_\rho$, compute or sharply bound
$[k(\Lambda_f):k(S_{n_1}f,\ldots,S_{n_\rho}f)]$, with
$\Lambda_f=\Lambda\cap\operatorname{span}_\Q\supp f$ for a fixed finite monomial
lattice $\Lambda$ presenting $f$. Rank proves finiteness once a common lattice
is fixed, but not the degree or Galois group; how much is explained by finite
monomial symmetries and how much by other coincidences? Power-sum fibres
\cite{MSW} show that dimension and degree are different problems. Source~02:
for a finite profile of rank $\rho$ determine
$[k(t^\Lambda):k(S_{q_1}y,\ldots,S_{q_\rho}y)]$ for the support-generated
lattice $\Lambda$ when the parameters make the smaller field a subfield of
$k(t^\Lambda)$ (for nonintegral parameters first choose a common lattice
containing all scaled supports). When is the extension trivial? How do lattice
and coefficient symmetries and Newton polytopes determine its degree and
ramification? Uniform independence alone gives no birational recovery.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[The full field of observations; source~03]
For fixed $f$ and $d\ge2$, describe the field generated by all $S_d^jf$,
$j\ge0$. Does a finite initial segment generate it, and when can the monomial
field be recovered modulo an explicitly computable finite symmetry group? The
theorem gives its transcendence degree, not its position in a rational monomial
field. Distinguish integer $d$, where one lattice contains all forward
transforms, from nonintegral rational $d$, where unbounded denominators can
matter.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Small relations from certificates; sources~03 and~02]
Source~03: design an algorithm taking a sparse reduced rational function to a
low-degree relation among its first $\rho+1$ transforms, with a compressed
elimination certificate, degree bounds in each observation and a proof of
minimality in a chosen sense; the greedy certificate may organize an
elimination order without pretending to supply the relation. Source~02: given
a finite profile of rank $\rho$ and $\rho+1$ positive integer parameters,
construct a small relation among the dilates; can mixed volumes or sparse
elimination control its multidegrees and heights? For rational profiles, can a
canonical minimal monomial quotient be recovered from the annihilator of
\eqref{adr:sr:eq:Bvector}?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Overlapping support spans; sources~03 and~02]
Find a necessary and sufficient support-and-coefficient criterion for joint
independence of several different series and their dilates when their support
spans overlap; \cref{adr:sr:thm:blocks} is only sufficient. A support-only
answer is impossible: $T_1+T_2$ and $T_1-T_2$ are independent in characteristic
zero, two copies of $T_1+T_2$ are not, and merely disjoint supports do not
suffice. Source~03 suggests a leading-term test for the coloured Jacobian whose
rows come from different coefficient patterns and dilation lists; source~02
suggests mixed matroid data.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Finite supports under real dilations; source~03]
For finite-support surcomplex $f$ and distinct positive reals
$a_1,\ldots,a_m$, determine the dependence pattern from the rational relations
among all products $a_jg$, $g\in\supp f$. The rational theorem and the
real-rank lower bound are two constraints, not a classification; one monomial
already detects rational independence of the scalars.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Beyond scalar exponent dilation; source~02]
For an ordered additive automorphism $\theta$ of the exponent space, when can
the leading-determinant method control $y,S_\theta y,\ldots$? Scalar dilation
admits strict rearrangement, which a general shear need not. Find hypotheses
replacing positive scalar ordering and classify the simplest resonance
obstructions.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Character twists and moving coefficients; source~02]
Replace $S_q$ by
$\sigma(\sum c_\gamma t^\gamma)=\sum\varphi(c_\gamma)\chi(\gamma)t^{q\gamma}$,
with $\varphi$ acting on coefficients and $\chi$ a multiplicative character.
When do iterates keep the uniform algebraic matroid? Which parts of the proof
survive when the coefficient vectors of different rows are no longer equal up
to scalars?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Frobenius-twisted coefficients; source~03]
Extend \cref{adr:sr:thm:prime} to coefficients in $\mathbb F_{p^e}$ or a
general perfect field. Dilation by $p$ then interacts with the inverse
Frobenius twist on coefficients, and $p$-free parts alone need not keep all
information; seek an invariant combining lattice rank with coefficient
Frobenius orbits.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Positive characteristic with inseparability separated; source~02]
Can a useful rank bound be recovered after quotienting out Frobenius
relations, or with Hasse derivatives and $p$-independence? Even parameters
prime to $p$ may interact through inseparability, and a valid result must
separate that from Hahn support cancellation.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Effective infinite-support certificates; source~03]
Specify representations of infinite supports for which the greedy choices and
their dominance have finite, mechanically checkable certificates, for example
recursive supports with an effective rank oracle, or finite grammars for nested
monomial scales. Separate summability, equality of exponents, and finding the
least support element outside a span; none should be treated as decidable
merely because the theorem is finite dimensional.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Formalization and proof certificates; high priority; sources~03 and~02]
Formalize the finite greedy-dominance lemma, strict rearrangement in an
ordered vector space, the derivative-matrix criterion, and then the Hahn
summability argument (\cref{adr:sr:sec:dependencies}). A first milestone is the
finite Laurent polynomial theorem with an executable certificate checker that
records a rational support basis, the selected greedy exponents and the unique
nonzero leading coefficient, avoiding the full Jacobian; a second is the
relative well-ordered-support theorem, without postulating its nonvanishing
conclusion as an axiom. Finite tests are not formal verification.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Orbit-free extensions and further surreal operations; source~03]
Use the tail of \cref{adr:sr:thm:tail} to study extensions of set-sized
difference fields inside $\SC$ with all positive rational or real dilations
named. Which free orbit extensions can also satisfy prescribed conditions
involving Conway simplicity, the natural surreal derivation, or the surreal
exponential? The construction controls exponent-linear algebra and algebraic
relations only.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Birthday bounds and restricted universes; source~02]
\label{adr:sr:q:birthday}
\Cref{adr:sr:thm:kappa} works over every set-sized base but gives no optimized
birthday bound. For a birthday-bounded base and a cardinal $\kappa$, how low in
the surreal hierarchy can such a family be placed? Which initial subfields
contain enough fresh inner scales for the construction?
\end{question}

\section{A worked elimination identity}\label{adr:app:elimination}

Let $X=T^2+T$ and $Y=T^4+T^2$. From $T^2=X-T$ one obtains
\[
 T^4=(X-T)^2=X^2+X-(2X+1)T,
\]
and hence
\[
 Y=X^2+2X-2(X+1)T.
\]
Therefore
\[
 T=\frac{X^2+2X-Y}{2(X+1)}.
\]
The denominator is nonzero in $k(T)$ in characteristic zero. Substitution
into $T^2+T-X=0$ and clearing denominators gives
\[
 Y^2-(2X^2+6X+2)Y+X^4+2X^3+2X^2=0.
\]
This simultaneously exhibits the relation and reconstructs the primitive
monomial from the pair. The degree theorem proves minimality; the
calculation alone would not rule out a hidden linear relation.

\section{Reproducing the finite checks}\label{adr:app:checks}

The file \texttt{verify.py} requires Python 3.9 or later and SymPy.
It uses only exact arithmetic. Run
\begin{verbatim}
python -m pip install sympy
python verify.py
\end{verbatim}
The second command writes \texttt{verification\_results.json} beside the
script and prints the same report. The recorded run used Python 3.13.5
and SymPy 1.14.0. The script is not an implementation of infinite Hahn
series and does not pretend that truncation is an infinite proof.
\wtag{} In this directory the script is \rpath{code/verify.py} and the
recorded run is \rpath{data/verification_results.json}; see
Appendix~\ref{adr:app:files} before rerunning.

For the inverse B\"ottcher checks, set $L(z)=zF(1/z)$ for a monic centered
polynomial $Q(X)=\sum q_jX^j$ of degree $d$. The checked identity is
\[
 L(z^d)=\sum_{j=0}^d q_j z^{d-j}L(z)^j.
\]
The coefficient of degree $m$ on the right contains $d[ z^m ]L$ plus
already known coefficients. Since $d\ne0$, this is an exact recursion.
The program independently recomputes the two sides after constructing
the coefficients and verifies equality through degree 32.

For the finite sums $u_{d,N}=\sum_{n=0}^N\omega^{d^{-n}}$, the checked
identity is deliberately the correct truncated one,
\[
 S_du_{d,N}-u_{d,N}=\omega^d-\omega^{d^{-N}},
\]
not the infinite identity with its boundary term silently deleted.
The infinite identity follows separately from coefficientwise comparison
of the full well-ordered supports.

\paragraph{Literature checked in the merge.} Proposition~3 of
Mel\'anov\'a--Sturmfels--Winter was read in the arXiv HTML version: for $m$
distinct positive integers $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ and $m\le n$, the map
$\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^m$ given by the power sums

those power sums; the proof shows that every maximal minor of the Jacobian is

(\cite{Gonshor,Edmonds,KM,BMS}) are cited as the sources cite them and were
not re-read for this report.
