# Literature and provenance audit

Date of research and manuscript: September 21, 2026.

## Requested basis

The complete supplied manuscript `surcomplex_analysis(3).tex` was retrieved and read. Its displayed title is *Surcomplex Analysis: Infinitesimal Calculus, Hahn-Coherent Holomorphy, and Contour Theory*, and its displayed date is September 21, 2026.

The new article retains that manuscript's distinction between fine-local analytic functions, uniform Hahn-coherent functions over ordinary domains, and all-scale coherent functions. It uses the middle category and works in fixed set-sized exponent workspaces. The inherited local results used are coherent evaluation and gluing, local preparation, exact monad root counting, deformed division, and the pole-free-fraction lemma. They are credited and reproved in the new article in the form needed for the global arguments.

The source's suggested directions include approximation/global support control and multivariable finite-mapping questions. This continuation develops the global one-variable divisor, interpolation, and gluing questions. It does not claim to have completed the separate multivariable program.

## Primary sources actually consulted

### Poonen: Hahn-field algebraic closure

Bjorn Poonen, *Maximally complete fields*, L'Enseignement Mathématique (2) 39 (1993), 87–106.

Author PDF: https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/amsval.pdf

Inspected Corollary 4 and its context, including a rendered page: a Hahn/Mal'cev–Neumann field with divisible value group and algebraically closed residue field is algebraically closed. This is a foundational input, not a new claim.

### Müller–Strohmaier: terminology and category boundary

Jörn Müller and Alexander Strohmaier, *The theory of Hahn meromorphic functions, a holomorphic Fredholm theorem and its applications*, Analysis & PDE 7 (2014), no. 3, 745–770.

Author manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0236
HTML full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1205.0236v3
DOI: https://doi.org/10.2140/apde.2014.7.745

Inspected the abstract and the early definitions and algebra results, especially Definitions 2.3 and 2.5. Their analytic functions are represented by normally convergent generalized expansions. In the present article the Hahn monomials are constant coefficient scales and the coefficients are ordinary holomorphic functions on a common base. The two settings are not identified, and the word "admissible" has separately specified meanings. No claim that the present theorems subsume their Fredholm theory is made.

### Ehrlich–Kaplan: prior surcomplex exponential work

Philip Ehrlich and Elliot Kaplan, *Surreal ordered exponential fields*.

Author manuscript: https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07739

Inspected the abstract and bibliographic record, not the full paper. The abstract explicitly discusses canonical exponential functions on suitable surcomplex counterparts. Consequently the present article makes no novelty claim about global surcomplex exponentials. This source is not a premise for the divisor or cohomology proofs.

### McMullen: scalar entire and meromorphic functions

Curtis T. McMullen, *Advanced Complex Analysis*, Harvard Math 213a course notes, November 30, 2023.

Author PDF: https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/class/harvard/213a/23/html/home/course/course.pdf

Inspected the Mittag–Leffler statement and correcting-polynomial construction (Theorem 3.26), surrounding discussion of Weierstrass realization, and the notes' metadata. These are ordinary scalar inputs. The new article supplies the scalar constructions and derives its discrete Hermite lemma explicitly; no ordinary Weierstrass, Mittag–Leffler, or scalar Cousin theorem is claimed as new.

### Stacks Project: degree-one cohomology and the local-stalk issue

https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02FQ
https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09NT

Consulted the standard torsor/first-cohomology framework and the Picard classification with its local-stalk qualification. The new article proves that the Laurent Hahn coefficient sheaf has nonlocal stalks and therefore explicitly restricts to locally free rank-one modules there. Its nonnegative-exponent model has local stalks, so the usual Picard terminology applies to that model. This distinction is necessary for a correct theorem statement.

## Additional credited foundations

Gonshor's surreal-number monograph and Neumann's ordered-division-ring paper are credited as standard foundational references, also cited by the supplied manuscript. They were not newly read cover to cover during this task. No comprehensive review of their contents is claimed.

## Novelty searches and what they do not prove

Targeted searches combined terms including:

- surcomplex analysis, Hahn-coherent holomorphy, global divisors;
- Hahn series, sheaf cohomology, Cousin problems;
- Hahn, Picard group, holomorphic line bundles;
- Hahn meromorphic, Mittag–Leffler, interpolation.

Many broad results were unrelated or used "Hahn" as a person's name in another context. No matching statement of the exact symmetric-support criterion, the restricted global interpolation algebra, the singular-support Cousin criterion, or the ordered-group line-bundle dichotomy was located in the primary material consulted. This is a limited search result, not a proof that no prior formulation exists. Related results may be stated under formal geometry, generalized-series sheaves, deformation theory, or different support conventions. The bibliography is not an exhaustive survey of those subjects.

## Candidate original contributions and their exact limits

1. Necessary-and-sufficient realization of finite infinitesimal clusters over a closed discrete ordinary subset, controlled by symmetric polynomial coefficient supports.
2. A global deformed interpolation map with precisely identified support-restricted image and a constructive coefficientwise right inverse.
3. A corresponding necessary-and-sufficient moving-pole Mittag–Leffler criterion relative to an admissible pole bound.
4. The exact additive Cousin criterion on the specified puncture-and-disk cover, using only the exponents carrying nonremovable coefficient singularities.
5. Explicit cohomology classes and a sharp vanishing/nonvanishing line-bundle dichotomy for ordered exponent groups, with a correctly distinguished integral model and Laurent coefficient sheaf.

The paper proves these statements within its definitions. It does not claim a complete calculation of first cohomology in every noncyclic case, a universal criterion on arbitrary covers, an all-scale theory of essential singularities, or a solution of a named published conjecture. Finite symbolic tests are consistency checks only.
