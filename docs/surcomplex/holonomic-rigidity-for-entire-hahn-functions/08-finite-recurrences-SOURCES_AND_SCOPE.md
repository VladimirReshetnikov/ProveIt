## Primary literature

**Richard P. Stanley, Differentiably Finite Power Series (1980).**
https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/45.pdf

The opening three printed pages were visually inspected, including Theorem 1.5
and its coefficient conversion. The D-finite/P-recursive framework is standard
background; our proof supplies the actual valuation-sensitive conversion used.

**Jean-Pierre Ramis, About the growth of entire functions solutions of linear
algebraic q-difference equations (1992).**
https://www.numdam.org/item/AFST_1992_6_1_1_53_0/
https://www.numdam.org/item/AFST_1992_6_1_1_53_0.pdf

Bibliographic metadata and the opening French and English abstracts were
inspected, including the scope in ordinary complex analysis and the stated
simultaneous differential/difference consequences. This is a contextual source,
not a source claimed to establish the arbitrary-rank Hahn theorem.

**Jorn Muller and Alexander Strohmaier, The theory of Hahn meromorphic functions,
a holomorphic Fredholm theorem and its applications (2014).**
https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0236
https://doi.org/10.2140/apde.2014.7.745

The abstract and bibliographic information were consulted to distinguish this
analytic convergence framework from strong formal Hahn evaluation.

**Alessandro Berarducci and Vincenzo Mantova, Surreal numbers, derivations and
transseries (2018).**
https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00315
https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/769

The abstract and metadata were consulted. The source is cited to identify the
scalar surreal derivation, not to transfer a result about that derivation to
our external formal variable derivative.

**Olivier Bournez and Quentin Guilmant, Surreal fields stable under exponential,
logarithmic, derivative and anti-derivative functions (2022).**
https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08396

The abstract and metadata were consulted for the Hahn normal-form viewpoint
and set-sized surreal field context. No operation-closure theorem from this
paper is needed for the central recurrence proofs.

## Priority assessment

Targeted combinations of Hahn, D-finite, non-Archimedean entire functions,
and q-difference terms were searched. Several broad searches returned
irrelevant material and were not treated as evidence that no prior theorem
exists. Exact-title retrieval was more informative.

The proposed original contribution is the precise arbitrary-rank order-unit
criterion, the uniform strong-support obstruction, and the stated extensions.
The review does not certify priority. In particular, no novelty is claimed
for the D-finite/P-recursive equivalence, the classical partial theta function,
standard Hahn operations, or every possible rank-one specialization.

The article is a new-theorem research attempt under the alternative explicitly
allowed by the request, not a claim to have settled a named published conjecture.
