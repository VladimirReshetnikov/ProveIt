# Proof audit

## Hypotheses that must remain attached to the results

- k has characteristic zero and is trivially valued in K = k((t^Gamma)).
- Gamma is a nonzero, set-sized ordered abelian group. Divisibility is not required for the main descent/rigidity proofs.
- E uses strong summability of the whole evaluation family at every point of one fixed K.
- M means Frac(E), embedded in K((z)), not an unspecified sheaf of meromorphic functions.
- D = d/dz kills every scalar in K. It is not the Berarducci–Mantova derivation.
- The universal all-order theorem assumes **no order unit**, not merely non-Archimedean order or infinite rational rank.
- The continuum construction uses the reverse-lexicographic direct sum of countably many copies of Q.

## Proof chain

1. Entire criterion: all weighted leading values v(a_n)+n*gamma tend to positive infinity in the full value group. For necessity, all-scale summability is essential; use a more exterior scale to turn infinitely many bounded leading values into a descending sequence.
2. Polynomial coarse reduction: entire coefficient values tend to infinity, so only finitely many can survive reduction at a proper coarsening. Normalize a nonzero denominator by a coefficient of globally minimal original valuation.
3. Rationality descent: reduce the formal identity g/c=(h/c)u coefficientwise. Its convolutions are finite. The reductions of g/c and h/c are polynomials and the latter is nonzero. The coefficient field F embeds injectively in the residue field. Rationality over the larger residue field descends by a finite-dimensional linear-system argument.
4. Singular coefficient recurrence: choose a minimal-rank differential relation, so its evaluated separant is a nonzero formal series. It need not have nonzero constant term. Define s=min(ord(A_j)-j) and Q(T)=sum C_j*(T falling j). Different falling-factorial degrees make Q nonzero.
5. Tail isolation: for n>s+2r, all terms quadratic in the unknown tail have order at least 2(n-r)>n+s. Extracting z^(n+s) isolates Q(n)*a_n. Put the finitely many integer roots of Q in the initial segment, then induct in a fixed finite coefficient field.
6. Finite scalar parameters: a relation over K(z) has only finitely many scalar coefficients after clearing denominators. The coefficient principle therefore puts the solution's Laurent coefficients in a field finitely generated over Q.
7. Valuation rank: rationally independent values force algebraic independence of representatives. Thus the whole finitely generated field's value group has finite rational rank and lies in a principal convex subgroup. No order unit makes this subgroup proper.
8. Apply the exact descent theorem to obtain rationality in M and polynomiality in E.

## The independent-family argument

A minimal dependent finite subfamily is used, not merely an arbitrary relation. This makes substitution of the other functions injective on the coefficient polynomial ring, so the selected function satisfies a genuine nonzero equation over the Laurent field generated by the other coefficient sequences and finitely many extra constants. The coefficient-field principle would reduce its coefficient transcendence degree to a finite number. Infinitely many exclusive, algebraically independent exponent monomials contradict that.

The binary-prefix construction gives infinitely many exclusive coefficients for each member of any finite set of distinct branches. Strong entireness is proved separately by the largest-coordinate comparison. The continuum upper bound is a separate cardinality argument, not inferred from the family being independent.

## Pitfalls explicitly avoided

- A Hahn parameter may have infinite support; this does not give infinitely many algebraically accessible parameters.
- Values of initial field generators do not necessarily span all field values: subtraction can reveal new directions. The proof uses a rank bound on the whole field.
- The numerator and denominator need not have coefficients in F. Their common coarsened reduction is the key step.
- No splitting of the value-group quotient or identification of its residue field with a chosen Hahn field is assumed.
- A nonzero evaluated separant is not the same as a nonzero separant at zero.
- The singular indicial polynomial is not the repository's global residue corner polynomial.
- Infinite field-descent linear systems have finitely many unknowns, and their augmented rows have a finite spanning subset. This is why a solution descends.
- No point-value independence over K is claimed; the values are in K.
- Entireness can fail after an exterior valuation scale is added. An explicit decreasing-leading-exponent witness is provided.
- Nothing is claimed about an arbitrary scalar differential equation on the full surreal class.

## Open boundary

The proper-coarsening argument need not apply when Gamma has an order unit. For example, the strongly entire partial theta series sum t^(n^2) z^n over C((t^Q)) has coefficients in Q(t), whose values generate a cofinal convex subgroup. Its unrestricted higher-order status is not settled here. The article also does not claim a necessary condition for its general coefficient-field descent theorem.

## Actual verification

The finite check program passed eight groups (8,673 cases, mostly prefix-pair comparisons). It uses exact symbolic expressions, integers, and rational numbers. It provides finite checks of identities and examples only. It does not prove any arbitrary-support or continuum theorem.

The article's substantive proofs have been checked by re-deriving their algebraic steps during preparation, but not by an independent referee or formal proof assistant.
