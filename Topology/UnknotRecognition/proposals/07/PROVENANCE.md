# Source provenance

The baseline is the `fast/fastunknot` directory of the user's uploaded
`Knots.zip`, not one of the six older implementations also in that archive.
Its seven Python modules in `baseline/fastunknot/` are byte-for-byte copies.
The uploaded ZIP has SHA-256 `2ad0af3659139ee8ded79de11814e243e7b3fbf48f221b78c95fd65d53828f92`.
No baseline source was modified to make the comparison look better.

`fastunknot/` is the modified version. Its original algorithms, input formats,
R1/R2 operations, polynomial routines, and cobordism implementation are derived
from that baseline; this is not a claim to have invented Bar-Natan scanning.
`tests/test_inherited.py` retains the original 19 tests, with explicit wrappers
that disable the two new filters when asserting old method names and limits.
The independent dense Khovanov reference in that suite is inherited too.
`tests/test_acceleration.py` supplies the new property and regression tests.

The example PDs and braid/grid inputs are derived from the uploaded examples.
`random5_36.json` is the 36-crossing five-strand braid in the original
`fast/results/benchmark.json`, converted using the original PD convention.
`conway_sum_*.json` and `unknot_chain_*.json` are generated by the included
benchmark script. Their construction is not a source of expected answers;
the connected-sum theorem and the independent small-diagram tests supply that.

The input archive's MIT No Attribution license is retained in `LICENSE`.
The new code and this report are distributed under the same license.
Third-party Lackenby papers/slides are cited, not bundled or relicensed here.

## Distinct slide versions

The submitted `docs/quasipolynomial-talk.pdf` is the **February 2021** version;
its physical page 13 states `n^O(log n)`. The author's March 2021 Oxford variant
(`quasipolynomial-talk-oxford.pdf`) states `2^O((log n)^3)` instead. The report
uses the February version that the user actually supplied, and does not conflate
the two bounds. The July 2026 arXiv paper is version 1 of arXiv:2607.23350.

## Measurement provenance

`results/benchmark.json` is the raw output of `tools/benchmark.py` on this run.
It contains successful samples, censored runs, chosen orders, ranks, work
counters, and process peak RSS. Importing the module, JSON parsing, and diagram
validation happen before the timer for both versions. Every timed sample runs
in a fresh process; no cross-sample cache warming was used. Completed cases
have three samples; a capped case stops after its first capped sample.
The original archive's 600-second timeout was on another platform; it is not
used as a same-machine speedup numerator.
