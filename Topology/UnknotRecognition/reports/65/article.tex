\begin{abstract}
A frontier can have Bell-many distinct connectivity states without requiring Bell-many
representatives for a particular terminal question. We study the question relevant to a
compressing-disc search: which partial surfaces can be completed to one disk by gluing along
specified, disjoint boundary arcs? For compact surfaces with boundary, the defect
$\delta(S)=\ccomp(S)-\chi(S)$ satisfies an exact gluing identity: defects add, together with
the cycle rank of the component-attachment graph. Thus a disk completion requires disk
pieces and a tree attachment graph, with no orientability hypothesis.

For $r$ labelled arcs, the resulting disk-completion matrix has exact rank $2^{r-1}$ over
$\F$. Its block with $c$ components has exact rank $\binom{r-1}{c-1}$. An explicit
root-transversal factorization gives a deterministic, minimum-weight representative
\emph{subset} of these sizes, preserving every disk-completion query. The sizes are sharp
even for unrestricted representative subsets. We prove a typed composition theorem,
a $3^b$ bound when active labels vary inside a pool of size $b$, and a signed-connectivity
extension. A supplied finite surface-assembly grammar can consequently be searched in
$2^{O(b)}$ times its encoded polynomial overhead, instead of enumerating all partitions.
Under a complete polynomial-size geometric grammar and $b=O((\log n)^2)$, this would
give a quasi-polynomial recognition route.

The delivery includes a weighted reducer, an independent algebraic certificate checker,
a complete finite-grammar solver, and an independent triangulated-surface replay.
Thirty-six test methods pass; additional audits check 813,297 direct compatibility-matrix
entries, 1,000 weighted families, 1,000 literal surface gluings, and 200 complete grammars.
Paired abstract-grammar experiments show $\MinBenchRatio$--$\MaxBenchRatio$ times lower median running times,
while a one-query control is slower after compression. These are not timings of the
maintained knot recognizer. The general rank-based method is classical; the contribution
is its sharp disk-specific formulation, topological contract, and checked implementation.
No complete bounded-width grammar for all knot exteriors is established here.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Status.} Proved local and supplied-grammar theorems; tested standalone code.
No proof-assistant verification, native recognizer integration, or general
quasi-polynomial unknot-recognition bound is claimed.

\section{Research position and the precise improvement}\label{sec:position}
\subsection{The current project already has a geometric source bridge}
This article continues \texttt{Topology/UnknotRecognition} in ProveIt. The reviewed
repository revision is
\begin{center}\small\texttt{3a90fb34146c915328ab8eac6250cc2514f74ed0}.\end{center}
At this revision, the maintained material includes source-checked diagram-to-exterior
construction and a source-bound normal-surface component census
\cite{proveit-exterior,proveit-weighted}. In particular, the canonical pulling construction
uses $20\max(1,n)$ tetrahedra for an $n$-crossing validated planar diagram. A supplied
normal surface can be decomposed and tested for compressing-disc components using Euler
characteristic and two torus-boundary parities. These are important advances: it would be
incorrect to describe the entire diagram-to-manifold provenance step as still absent.

The remaining search problem is different. Fast verification of one supplied normal vector
does not construct a complete, efficiently searchable collection of vectors or hierarchy
moves. The present work addresses one source of search growth, namely the number of
connectivity alternatives carried through a surface frontier. It neither replaces the
existing compressed orbit engine nor reproves its weighted census; the classical
compressed-orbit context is supplied by Agol, Hass, and Thurston \cite{aht}.

The predecessor \emph{Certified Port Quotients and Guarded Interval Attachments}
\cite{port-report} gives a sharp $\Bell(r+1)$ state bound for a fixed-source coning model.
Those states are physically distinguishable by suitable subsequent operations. It asks
whether useful restricted continuations permit $2^{O(r)}$ rather than
$2^{O(r\log r)}$ search. We obtain such a bound for a \emph{different, explicitly narrower
observation}: minimum-cost completion to a disk along genuine boundary arcs. We do not
identify all physical states, and we do not preserve arbitrary coning predicates.

\subsection{What changes, and what does not}
A partition of $r$ labels says which frontier arcs lie on the same partial disk. An exact
state table can contain $\Bell(r)$ partitions. Our reducer retains at most $2^{r-1}$
\emph{actual candidates} of one geometric type, while preserving the optimum for every
possible disk-union cap on those arcs. A discarded candidate need not have the same
behavior as any single retained candidate. Instead, for each cap that completes it, at
least one no-more-expensive retained candidate completes that cap. This is the critical
logical distinction between state equivalence and representative families.

The main steps are a topological reduction to a tree, an exact matrix factorization,
a cost-ordered basis argument, and a replacement proof inside a finite assembly grammar.
The first three do not assume a special knot class. The global application does require
that a valid geometric producer expose its partial surfaces through the stated interface.
We give counterexamples showing why dropping this requirement is unsound.

\subsection{Relation to established methods and the novelty boundary}
Deterministic rank-based compression of connectivity dynamic programs, including weighted
variants, is established by Bodlaender, Cygan, Kratsch, and Nederlof \cite{bckn}.
Representative families for linear matroids and their exterior-algebra constructions are
also classical algorithmic tools; Fomin, Lokshtanov, Panolan, and Saurabh give systematic
algorithms and applications \cite{flps,products}. Our reduced incidence minors are a
concrete graphic-matroid instance of that machinery. We do not claim the greedy basis
principle, Gaussian elimination, or single-exponential connectivity dynamic programming
as new inventions.

The disk-specific completion matrix is not the unrestricted ``union is a forest''
compatibility matrix studied in work on the Tutte polynomial \cite{mannens}.
We require a \emph{connected tree}, work over $\F$, and keep component count as a grading.
The explicit root-transversal coordinates make the exact ranks and matching lower bounds
transparent. The research contribution here is the combined disk-topology statement,
sharp completion kernel, typed replacement theorem, safety analysis, and reproducible
project-facing implementation. We establish these assertions in full below; we have not
established worldwide priority for every equivalent algebraic formulation.

Lackenby's hierarchy paper is relevant geometric context, not a complexity theorem we
silently assume. Its Section~9 explicitly distinguishes a bound on certain algorithmic
steps from a running-time estimate for all operations \cite{lackenby}. Our conditional
corollary will name construction, type-count, width, and bit-size obligations separately.

\section{Surfaces, interfaces, and topological defect}\label{sec:defect}
\subsection{The geometric interface contract}
All surfaces are finite compact PL surfaces, possibly disconnected and nonorientable.
Every connected component has nonempty boundary. An attachment arc is a subspace
homeomorphic to a closed interval properly contained in a boundary circle. In a single
surface, different attachment arcs are disjoint \emph{including their endpoints}.
They have disjoint collars, and gluing pairs them by homeomorphisms; corners are then
smoothed. No other points are identified. These conditions ensure that the quotient is
again a compact surface with boundary. Boundary intervals not used for this attachment
remain available for later stages.

One label denotes one connected geometric arc. It does not denote a union of separated
arcs, an entire point-incidence port, or a binary-compressed bundle of many parallel
arcs. Translating the repository's compressed incidence data into this interface requires
an additional geometric theorem. The same word ``port'' is not such a theorem.

Write $c(S)=\ccomp(S)$ and define
\[
                   \delta(S)=c(S)-\chi(S).
\]
For a connected orientable surface of genus $g$ with $h\ge1$ boundary circles,
$\delta=2g+h-1$. For a connected nonorientable surface with $k\ge1$ crosscaps and
$h\ge1$ boundary circles, $\delta=k+h-1$. Thus $\delta(S)\ge0$, and
$\delta(S)=0$ precisely when every component of $S$ is a disk. These formulas may
also be read as the elementary classification-of-surfaces test used in the following proof.

\begin{theorem}[Exact defect under boundary-arc gluing]\label{thm:defect}
Let $S$ and $T$ satisfy the preceding contract, and glue $r$ pairs of boundary arcs
between them. Let $G$ be the bipartite multigraph whose vertices are the connected
components of $S$ and $T$, and whose edges are the $r$ glued pairs. Include isolated
vertices when a component has no arc used in this particular attachment. For
$W=S\cup_{\mathrm{arcs}}T$,
\begin{align}
 c(W)&=c(G), & \chi(W)&=\chi(S)+\chi(T)-r,\label{eq:chi}\\
 \delta(W)&=\delta(S)+\delta(T)+\beta_1(G), &
 \beta_1(G)&=r-c(S)-c(T)+c(G).\label{eq:defect}
\end{align}
In particular, $W$ is a union of disks if and only if $S$ and $T$ are unions of disks
and $G$ is a forest. It is one disk if and only if the pieces are unions of disks and
$G$ is a tree.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A path in the quotient moves within connected components of the original surfaces and
crosses glued intervals. Such a path determines a walk in $G$. Conversely, an edge of
$G$ joins the indicated surface components in the quotient. This proves the component
identity, including isolated vertices.

Choose compatible triangulations in which each gluing interval is a subcomplex.
Euler-characteristic inclusion--exclusion subtracts one for each glued interval,
because an interval has Euler characteristic one. The intervals are pairwise disjoint,
so no additional multiple-intersection terms occur. This gives the second identity in
\eqref{eq:chi}. Substitute it into $c(W)-\chi(W)$ and add and subtract
$c(S)+c(T)$ to obtain \eqref{eq:defect}. The expression for $\beta_1(G)$ is the
usual edge--vertex--component formula for a multigraph, and is nonnegative.

There is free boundary in each component: finitely many pairwise disjoint proper closed
intervals cannot cover a boundary circle, and a free boundary point survives gluing.
Thus the nonnegativity and disk characterization of $\delta$ apply to $W$ as well as
to $S,T$. Every term on the right of \eqref{eq:defect} is nonnegative. Their sum
vanishes exactly when both inputs are disk unions and $G$ has no cycle. Adding
connectedness gives the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Defect cannot be repaired]\label[corollary]{cor:persistent}
During a sequence of attachments satisfying the contract, an existing positive surface
defect cannot be removed by attaching more surfaces with boundary. Every partial
surface on a successful disk assembly is therefore a union of disks. A partial component
with no remaining attachment arc cannot become connected to later material.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:defect} successively. For the second statement, a component with no
remaining arc is an isolated vertex in every future attachment graph. If additional
material must be part of the same final disk, such a component prevents success.
\end{proof}

A component that has lost its last live arc can still be a \emph{terminal} disk when no
other material remains. It must be handled by a separate terminal rule, not silently
deleted from an intermediate partition. The delivered layered grammar always has a
nonempty outgoing interface until its final cap, so its intermediate rejection of sealed
components is safe.

\subsection{Orientation and parallel-arc examples}
No orientation signs are needed to recognize the tree case. Inductively remove a leaf
of the attachment tree: it is a disk glued along one boundary interval to the rest.
This attachment makes a disk boundary connected sum, irrespective of the chosen
homeomorphism of the interval. One may choose the orientation of the leaf after the
orientation of the remaining component is fixed.

By contrast, two disks glued along two separated intervals have
$\chi=1+1-2=0$ and a connected attachment graph with one cycle. Suitable gluing maps
give an annulus; other choices give a M\"obius band. Neither can later be made a disk
by the permitted operations. Collapsing those two intervals to one abstract port would
falsely predict a disk. The cycle term is not a cosmetic correction: it is precisely the
information a parallel-arc compression must preserve.

\section{The disk-completion matrix}\label{sec:matrix}
\subsection{Partitions and the completion predicate}
Fix $r\ge1$ and $U=\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}$, with distinguished label $0$.
Let $\Part(U)$ be its set of partitions. A live disk-union frontier has one labelled
arc for each element of $U$, and every disk component meets at least one arc.
It determines $p\in\Part(U)$: labels lie in one block exactly when their arcs lie
on the same disk. Let $|p|$ denote the number of blocks.

For $p,q\in\Part(U)$, form the bipartite graph $G(p,q)$ with one vertex for each
block of $p$ or $q$, and an edge labelled $u$ joining the two blocks containing $u$.
Let $p\vee q$ be the least common coarsening, whose blocks are the connected classes
generated by the two equivalence relations. Write $\one_U=\{U\}$ for the one-block
partition. Define the zero--one matrix
\begin{equation}
 D_r(p,q)=\ind{p\vee q=\one_U\ \text{and}\ |p|+|q|=r+1}.
 \label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
Every vertex of $G(p,q)$ has an incident edge. It is connected exactly when
$p\vee q=\one_U$. Its vertex count is $|p|+|q|$ and its edge count is $r$.
Consequently \eqref{eq:D} is exactly the indicator that $G(p,q)$ is a tree.
By \cref{thm:defect}, it is exactly the topological disk-completion predicate.

This interpretation depends only on which disks carry the labelled intervals. It does
not require a common cyclic order on all the intervals, noncrossing partitions, or a
planar embedding of $G(p,q)$. Any restrictions imposed by an ambient 3-manifold enter
through the set of permitted candidates and caps and their geometric types.

\subsection{Root transversals}
Let
\[
 \mathcal R=\{R\subseteq U:0\in R\},\qquad d=|\mathcal R|=2^{r-1}.
\]
A set $R$ is a transversal of $p$ if it contains exactly one element of each block.
Define
\begin{equation}
 C(p,R)=\ind{R\text{ is a transversal of }p},\qquad
 R^\star=\{0\}\cup(U\setminus R).
 \label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
The map $R\mapsto R^\star$ is an involution of $\mathcal R$. Let $J$ be its
permutation matrix. The vector $C(p,\cdot)$ is supported on sets of size $|p|$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact root-transversal factorization]\label{thm:factor}
Over $\F$,
\begin{equation}
             D_r=CJC^{\mathsf T}.
             \label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the parity of the number of sets $R\ni0$ such that $R$ is a
transversal of $p$ and $R^\star$ is a transversal of $q$ is one precisely when
the two disk unions complete to a disk.
\end{theorem}
We give a minors proof, rather than assuming an unproved rule about gluing arbitrary
partition encodings.

\begin{lemma}[Rooted forest minor]\label[lemma]{lem:minor}
Let $F$ be a forest on $U$ with $c$ components, and let $B_F$ be an oriented vertex--edge
incidence matrix over $\F$ with the row for vertex $0$ removed. If
$A\subseteq U\setminus\{0\}$ has size $r-c$, then the square minor $B_F[A,*]$
has determinant one if and only if $U\setminus A$ contains exactly one vertex from
each component of $F$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A tree component with $v$ vertices contributes $v-1$ columns. Its full incidence
matrix has rank $v-1$: a leaf elimination proves that deleting any one row gives a
square matrix of determinant one. If no row is deleted from some component, the
selected rows of that component contain a dependence. If two or more rows are deleted
from some component, fewer than $v-1$ selected rows remain in that component, so its
columns cannot be independent. Thus a nonsingular global square minor requires
exactly one omitted row per component. Under that condition it is block diagonal,
after row and column permutations, with the rooted-tree matrices just described.
Each determinant is one over $\F$, proving the converse as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:factor}]
If $C(p,R)C(q,R^\star)$ is nonzero, then
$|p|=|R|$ and $|q|=|R^\star|=r+1-|R|$. Hence both sides of
\eqref{eq:factor} vanish when $|p|+|q|\ne r+1$.

Assume the block counts are complementary. Choose an arbitrary tree on each block
of $p$ and of $q$, obtaining forests $F_p,F_q$ on $U$ with $r-|p|$ and
$r-|q|$ edges. Keep edges from the two forests distinct, even if they have identical
endpoints. The concatenated reduced incidence matrix
\[
                         B=[B_{F_p}\mid B_{F_q}]
\]
is square of size $r-1$. Its determinant is one precisely when the resulting
multigraph is a tree. The graph has $r-1$ edges and has connected classes
$p\vee q$, so this is precisely $D_r(p,q)=1$.

Expand the determinant by the columns belonging to $F_p$. A term selects a row set
$A$ of size $r-|p|$ for those columns and the complementary rows for $F_q$.
All Laplace signs disappear in characteristic two. By \cref{lem:minor}, the first
minor is $C(p,U\setminus A)$. Put $R=U\setminus A$; the omitted rows for the
second forest are $\{0\}\cup A=R^\star$. Its minor is $C(q,R^\star)$.
Summing the products gives \eqref{eq:factor}. The empty $0\times0$ determinant
has value one, covering $r=1$ without an exception.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact total and graded ranks}
\begin{theorem}[Sharp rank in every component grading]\label{thm:rank}
For every $r\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \rk_{\F}D_r=2^{r-1}.
 \label{eq:rank}
\end{equation}
For $1\le c\le r$, the submatrix with row partitions having $c$ blocks and
column partitions having $r+1-c$ blocks has rank
\begin{equation}
                         \binom{r-1}{c-1}.
                         \label{eq:graded}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The factorization bounds the total rank by $d$. The homogeneous support of a row
with $c$ blocks lies in the $\binom{r-1}{c-1}$ coordinates of size $c$, giving the
graded upper bound.

For $R\in\mathcal R$, let $\pi_R$ be the partition with one block
$(U\setminus R)\cup\{0\}$ and singleton blocks $\{u\}$ for all
$u\in R\setminus\{0\}$. A transversal containing $0$ must choose $0$ from the
first block and must choose each of the singleton vertices. Therefore
\[
                         C(\pi_R,\cdot)=e_R.
\]
The submatrix of $D_r$ indexed by these $d$ partitions is the permutation matrix
$J$. In the $c$-block grading, the rows indexed by $|R|=c$ and columns indexed by
$\pi_{R^\star}$ similarly give a permutation matrix of order
$\binom{r-1}{c-1}$. The lower bounds match the upper bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The first nontrivial dependence]\label[example]{ex:r3}
At width three, write partitions in restricted-growth notation. The partitions
$001$, $010$, and $011$ have root vectors
\[
 C(001)=e_{\{0,2\}},\qquad C(010)=e_{\{0,1\}},\qquad
 C(011)=e_{\{0,1\}}+e_{\{0,2\}}.
\]
Together with $000$ and $012$, the five partitions span four dimensions.
The dependence is not an equality of physical surfaces; it is a linear identity
for their completion tests.
\end{example}

\section{Minimum-weight representative subsets and certificates}\label{sec:representatives}
\subsection{The exact guarantee}
A candidate is a tuple $(p,w,\omega)$: a frontier partition, an integer cost written in
binary, and an actual witness describing the partial surface. All candidates in one
reduction call must share one geometric and boundary type. This type requirement is
formalized in \cref{sec:composition}; it cannot be certified from $p$ alone.

For a finite family $\mathcal A$ and a cap partition $q$, put
\[
 \operatorname{opt}(\mathcal A,q)
   =\min\{w(a):a\in\mathcal A,\ D_r(p_a,q)=1\},
\]
with minimum $+\infty$ for the empty set. A subset $\mathcal A'\subseteq\mathcal A$
is \emph{minimum disk-representative} if these optima agree for every
$q\in\Part(U)$. Costs may be negative: finiteness of the family, not positivity,
is what is needed.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp weighted disk-completion kernel]\label{thm:weighted}
Every finite family of candidates on $r\ge1$ arcs has a minimum disk-representative
subset $\mathcal A'$ with
\[
 |\mathcal A'|\le2^{r-1},\qquad
 |\{a\in\mathcal A':|p_a|=c\}|\le\binom{r-1}{c-1}.
\]
Such a subset is found deterministically by scanning candidates in nondecreasing cost
and retaining precisely those root vectors independent of the vectors retained so far.
The output consists of original candidates, not formal sums of witnesses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a total order refining cost order. During Gaussian elimination retain an original
candidate if and only if its vector is independent of earlier retained vectors. Every
discarded vector is an $\F$-linear combination of retained vectors whose costs are
no larger. All nonzero vectors have a well-defined component grading, and the
coordinate spaces of different gradings are disjoint. Hence an independent collection
has at most the stated number of vectors in each grading; summing gives $2^{r-1}$.

Consider any candidate $a$ completed by $q$. If $a$ is retained, nothing needs to be
shown. Otherwise write
\[
 C(p_a,\cdot)=\sum_{b\in B_a}C(p_b,\cdot),\qquad
                         w(b)\le w(a).
\]
Pair with $JC(q,\cdot)^{\mathsf T}$. By \cref{thm:factor}, the left side is one.
Therefore at least one summand is one. That summand comes from a retained candidate
completed by $q$ at no greater cost. Apply this observation to an optimum candidate.
The reverse inequality follows because the retained family is a subset of the original
family. This proves equality of optima, including the case of no completion.
\end{proof}

This argument is deterministic. It does not conclude that an odd number of solutions
exists because some randomized evaluation is nonzero. The parity identity is used to
certify that a \emph{particular successful row} has a successful retained replacement.
No valid witness is replaced by an XOR of surfaces.

\begin{theorem}[Representative-size lower bound]\label{thm:lower}
The bounds in \cref{thm:weighted} cannot be reduced in general, even by a method
that is not based on linear algebra, as long as it must output a subset preserving all
disk-completion queries. The same holds separately in each component grading.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take one cost-zero candidate for each $\pi_R$ of \cref{thm:rank}.
The query $q=\pi_{R^\star}$ completes $\pi_R$ and no other member of this
family, since the induced completion matrix is $J$. Omitting that candidate changes
its query optimum from zero to $+\infty$. Thus all $2^{r-1}$ candidates are
necessary. Restricting to $|R|=c$ gives the graded lower bound.
\end{proof}

The lower bound concerns worst-case representative \emph{subsets}. It is not a
lower bound on the running time of every algorithm for one particular cap, nor a
claim that all knot-derived frontiers realize the sharp family. A single supplied cap
can be tested directly against a family in polynomial time per candidate, without
any exponential-dimensional preprocessing.

\subsection{A certificate that checks the discarded rows}
A positive witness alone proves a feasible assembly, but not that pruning preserved the
best one. The reducer therefore optionally returns the following evidence: the list of
retained original indices and, for every input row $a$, a list $B_a$ of retained indices
such that
\begin{equation}
 C(p_a,\cdot)=\bigoplus_{b\in B_a}C(p_b,\cdot),
 \qquad w(b)\le w(a)\quad(b\in B_a).
 \label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
A retained row can cite itself. Distinct indices are required inside each expansion.
The checker receives the original ordered partitions and costs independently of the
certificate, reconstructs all root vectors, and checks every identity and cost comparison.
It also checks the total and graded output-size bounds. A certificate for a modified
source is not accepted merely because some digest has a plausible shape.

To verify the pruning guarantee, it is not necessary to trust the producer's pivot
order or to recompute an optimal basis. Indeed, any retained subset satisfying
\eqref{eq:certificate} has the required replacement property. Independence is a
convenient way to \emph{produce} a small subset, while the linear expansion and
cost dominance are a simple way to \emph{check} it.

The delivered checker is deliberately separate. The producer builds root vectors by
choosing one root from each block not containing $0$. The checker scans all root subsets
and counts the roots in each block. The producer tests compatibility by disjoint sets;
the independent direct predicate uses breadth-first connectivity and the graph's
edge--vertex count. Tests disable the producer while the checker runs and mutate source
partitions, costs, indices, expansions, and schema fields. These are independent code
paths, not a claim that shared mathematical mistakes are impossible.

\subsection{Encoded cost, memory, and input dependence}\label{sub:cost}
Let $L$ be the number of input candidates, $B$ the maximum bit length of an integer
cost, and $W$ the total encoded witness length or the size of a shared witness DAG.
Let $d=2^{r-1}$. A transparent implementation may form each vector by scanning its
$d$ coordinates and testing transversality in $O(r)$ elementary operations. Thus
$O(Lrd\,\poly(\log(r+1)))$ bit work is a safe construction bound.

Naive elimination uses at most $d$ pivots per input row and XORs vectors with at most
$d$ bits. Tracking a retained-row expansion also uses at most $d$ bits per basis
vector. Hence
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(Ld^2+Lrd\,\poly(\log(r+1))
       +LB\log(L+1)+\text{input and witness costs}\right)
 \label{eq:kernel-cost}
\end{equation}
is a conservative bit bound. It does not treat a $d$-bit Python integer as one
constant-time object. The implementation's packed integers can improve constants,
not the stated guarantee. Witnesses may be kept by immutable indices or parent pointers;
copying long witness arrays must instead be charged explicitly.

The basis and its expansions use $O(d^2)$ bits. An explicit certificate with up to
$d$ original indices per row uses $O(Ld\log(L+1))$ bits. The input itself occupies
$\Omega(L)$ space, and certificates retained from every stage must also be counted.
One may stream certificates or check them before discarding a stage, but that is a
separate storage policy. The size bound counts \emph{retained candidates}, not total
working bits.

There is no $2^{O(r)}$ total-time theorem for an arbitrarily large explicit input list
without charging its length. In particular, constructing all $\Bell(r)$ partitions
and then reducing them still costs at least $\Bell(r)$ input operations. The
single-exponential search theorem in \cref{sec:composition} assumes candidates are
built from already reduced child families and a bounded explicit rule set.

\section{Active interfaces and signed connectivity}\label{sec:extensions}
\subsection{A pool of potentially active arc labels}
A geometric interface may specify a pool $P$ of $b$ possible labels, while an individual
state uses only a subset $A\subseteq P$. In general different active sets cannot be
merged: a future attachment may require an arc absent from one of them. Reduce each
nonempty active set separately, after relabelling one of its elements as the distinguished
root.

\begin{corollary}[The active-set sum]\label[corollary]{cor:active}
For one fixed external type and a pool of $b$ labels, the total number of representatives
across nonempty active sets is at most
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq P}2^{|A|-1}
                  =\frac{3^b-1}{2}.
 \label{eq:active}
\end{equation}
If one additional empty-interface terminal record is needed, the bound is
$(3^b+1)/2$. These bounds apply per type; independent homology or geometry types
multiply the count.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:weighted} on each active set and sum the binomial expansion of
$(1+2)^b$. The empty interface is a separate terminal case, not a partition of positive
width to which the root-vector construction is applied.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:active} does not identify arbitrary partial partitions arising in
coning. The predecessor's $\Bell(b+1)$ physical-state distinction remains valid.
Here a table of \emph{families} is compressed for one completion predicate under a
different transition contract.

\subsection{An exact signed-connectivity rank}
For some extensions, signs on the interface must be retained. A signed partition is a
pair $(p,a)$ where $a\in\F^U$, with two assignments identified if they differ by
flipping all signs in any whole block of $p$. It describes the within-component
relations
\[
                         x_u+x_v=a_u+a_v\quad(u,v\text{ in one block of }p).
\]
Use one fixed framing for interface labels. Any reversal needed on the cap side is
transported into its assignment before these equations are combined.

Let $\mathcal X=\{x\in\F^U:x_0=0\}$ and set
\[
 H((p,a),x)=\ind{x\text{ satisfies all within-block equations of }(p,a)}.
\]
Define $M(s,t)$ to be one when the combined unsigned partition is connected and the
combined signed equations are consistent, and zero otherwise.

\begin{theorem}[Signed root-coloring factorization]\label{thm:signed}
Over $\F$,
\[
                          M=HH^{\mathsf T},\qquad \rk_{\F}M=2^{r-1}.
\]
Thus minimum-cost representatives of size at most $2^{r-1}$ also suffice for all
connected-and-consistent signed completion queries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The $(s,t)$ entry of $HH^{\mathsf T}$ is the parity of the common root-fixed
solutions. An inconsistent system has no solutions. A consistent one with $k$
connected classes has $2^{k-1}$ solutions after fixing $x_0=0$, because each class
has one free flip and the class containing $0$ has its flip fixed. Its parity is one
exactly when $k=1$. This proves the factorization and the upper rank bound.

For a one-block partition and any chosen root-fixed assignment $a$, the vector
$H((\one_U,a),\cdot)$ is the unit vector at $a$. These $2^{r-1}$ signed states
give an identity submatrix of $M$. This proves equality. The same cost-ordered
basis and pairing proof as \cref{thm:weighted} gives the representative statement.
\end{proof}

This theorem concerns connectedness and consistency, not disk topology. A connected
orientable annulus satisfies an orientation consistency test but has positive defect.
Euler information must not be discarded. Conversely, in the genuine disk-completion
case the tree condition already permits compatible orientations, so signs are not a
necessary extra exponential factor. The delivery includes signed vectors and an
independent signed compatibility predicate, with exhaustive small tests. It does
\emph{not} include a general signed, higher-genus surface-assembly solver.

\section{Safe composition in a finite assembly grammar}\label{sec:composition}
\subsection{What a geometric type must guarantee}
The weighted theorem is a statement about a family and every cap. To use it repeatedly
inside a search, one must know that an actual remaining computation can be treated as
such a cap. We make that condition explicit.

\begin{definition}[Typed boundary-arc assembly grammar]\label[definition]{def:grammar}
A finite typed assembly grammar is an acyclic construction specification with the
following data and requirements.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item A partial object is a realized compact surface with boundary and a finite labelled
live-arc interface satisfying \cref{sec:defect}. A live nonterminal object has no
component without a live arc. Its state includes a geometric type, its active labels,
its partition, its integer cost, and a recoverable witness.
\item Local rules glue prescribed pairs of interface arcs between disjoint partial
objects or prescribed pieces. The rules maintain the interval and collar contract.
They include all boundary identifications relevant to later stages; there are no
unrecorded point or edge identifications.
\item Within a fixed type and active set, the admissible complementary objects and
arc maps are independent of the internal connectivity partition. Any required
boundary framing, cyclic order, ambient region, boundary-chain constraint, matching
condition, or parity label is included in the type or separately certified to be
irrelevant to all future rules.
\item Costs add under assembly. A terminal success is one disk with any prescribed
external constraints encoded by the terminal type. A region decomposition, when
ambient embedding matters, keeps interiors of pieces in disjoint regions and ensures
that matching boundary types can actually be glued as embedded surfaces.
\item The specification is complete for its \emph{own stated language}: every allowed
assembly is generated by its finite rules. Completeness for all compressing discs
in a manifold is an additional assertion, not part of this definition.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Here geometric admissibility means that the specified gluing and external constraints
are well defined; it does not mean that the result is a disk independently of its
partition. That latter question is still decided by $D_r$. Computational DAG sharing
reuses descriptions, not an unrecorded identification of physical surface pieces.

Item (iii) is stronger than saying that two states have the same number of ports.
For example, a transition allowed only when two specified arcs are on the same current
component does not satisfy it unless that information has been included in the type.
Adding enough type information can restore correctness while destroying a small type
count. Both issues must be tracked.

\begin{theorem}[Replacement is safe in every grammar context]\label{thm:composition}
In a typed boundary-arc assembly grammar, replacing a family of live disk-union
candidates of one type and active set by a minimum disk-representative subset
preserves the minimum cost of every successful terminal assembly using that family.
Repeated replacements at intermediate stages preserve the global optimum within
the grammar.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a successful terminal assembly using a candidate $S$ at the selected stage.
Regard everything outside $S$ in that assembly as one complementary surface $T$,
with the already specified identifications among its own pieces performed. The type
and collar contract says that its entire contact with $S$ is the labelled arc interface.
There are no hidden additional intersections. Components of $S$ meet the interface
by the live-state convention. Since the result is one disk and $S$ is a proper
nonterminal part, components of $T$ also meet the interface. The complementary
surface satisfies the surface-with-boundary contract.

Apply \cref{thm:defect} to this final gluing. The complement $T$ must be a union
of disks, and its labelled arcs give a cap partition $q$. The chosen $S$ satisfies
$D_r(p_S,q)=1$. By representativity, some retained candidate $S'$ of the same type
is completed by $q$ at cost at most that of $S$. Item (iii) ensures the very same
outside assembly and arc maps remain legal for $S'$, and item (iv) ensures that any
ambient embedding and terminal type constraints are preserved. By the disk-completion
criterion, their union is a disk. Additivity of cost gives a no-more-expensive successful
assembly.

The retained family is a subset, so replacing it cannot introduce a cheaper assembly
that did not already belong to the original language. Hence the optimum is unchanged.
This argument applies to each replacement in a finite acyclic evaluation order,
proving the iterated statement.
\end{proof}

The proof uses the whole remaining context, not just the next local move. A retained
candidate may react differently to an individual intermediate operation than a discarded
one. That causes no error when the type contract holds: the successful whole completion
can still be substituted, and \cref{cor:persistent} ensures that its intermediate pieces
will pass the disk-union and live-component tests. This is exactly why arbitrary
history-dependent guards need separate treatment.

\subsection{A complete sequential grammar implemented in this delivery}
The implemented grammar starts with a nonempty finite family of disk unions on $r_0$ arcs.
Layer $i$ has an explicit list of options, each a disk union with $r_{i-1}$ incoming
arcs and $r_i\ge1$ outgoing arcs. The incoming arcs are glued to the previous frontier
by the fixed labels. A final family of disk-union caps consumes the last outgoing
interface. Costs of the selected initial object, layer options, and cap are added.

At one transition, build the component graph of the current object and the selected
layer option. If adding an edge creates a cycle, reject: positive defect is permanent.
Otherwise each component of that forest gives a disk. Restrict its labels to the
outgoing arcs; if any graph component has no outgoing arc, reject the transition because
later material still has to be attached. Canonicalize the resulting output partition.
Layer components that have only outgoing arcs are permitted: they are new disks that
may be connected later. The graph includes them as isolated vertices until that happens.

After each layer, both the exact baseline and the reduced solver keep only the cheapest
witness for an identical partition. The reduced solver additionally applies
\cref{thm:weighted}. At the cap, both evaluate \eqref{eq:D} directly. By
\cref{thm:composition}, both solvers return the same minimum cost or both find no
assembly in the given grammar. A literal polygon-and-triangle replay checks a returned
witness independently of the frontier transitions.

This is a complete search in an explicit finite language, not a simulation in which
success has been assumed in advance. It is nevertheless an \emph{abstract surface}
language until actual embedded pieces and a source manifold are bound to it. The
code accordingly returns \texttt{FOUND\_ABSTRACT\_DISK} or
\texttt{NO\_DISK\_IN\_GRAMMAR}. Neither is an unknot-recognition verdict.

\subsection{End-to-end accounting within a supplied grammar}
\begin{theorem}[Single-exponential typed-grammar search]\label{thm:complexity}
Consider a supplied acyclic binary assembly grammar of explicit input bit length $I$,
with $H$ evaluation nodes, at most $A$ geometric types per node, and at most $b\ge1$
live arcs in each type. Assume at most $K$ explicit local choices per ordered pair
of child types. Let $P$ bound the encoded work per local validation, state construction,
integer cost operation, and witness-DAG update; also let $P$ bound the full encoded
size and verification cost of a required terminal witness. Then a
deterministic minimum-cost disk-assembly search can be performed in
\begin{equation}
                         2^{O(b)}\poly(I,H,A,K,P)
                         \label{eq:grammar-bound}
\end{equation}
bit time, with independently checkable pruning evidence and recoverable witnesses.
The constants in the polynomial do not depend on $b$. Any cost of discovering or
constructing this supplied grammar from a different input is additional.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Reduce each leaf family by type, charging its full explicit length and the
output-sensitive bound \eqref{eq:kernel-cost}. Summed over the leaves, the number
of input candidates is at most $I$, so this contributes at most
$2^{O(b)}\poly(I,P)$. No Bell-sized list is generated implicitly.

Each child family has at most $A2^{b-1}$ candidates. A binary node therefore creates
at most $KA^2 4^{b-1}$ ordered combinations before local tests. Collect successful
ones by output type and active interface, and reduce each collection. Formula
\eqref{eq:kernel-cost}, with $d\le2^{b-1}$, gives a conservative factor at most
another $4^{b-1}$, up to the polynomial input, cost, and sorting factors. Thus a
bound of the form
\[
                 O\!\left(HKA^2 16^b\,\poly(b,P,\log(HKA+2))\right)
\]
is sufficient for the internal nodes. We do not claim this base is optimized.
If varying active sets are not already counted as types, \cref{cor:active}
replaces the per-type bound by $O(3^b)$; the same argument remains single
exponential. Apply \cref{thm:composition} after each reduction. Finally check
terminal records and replay a minimum witness. The parameter $P$ includes any
required expanded witness size, not merely its DAG size.
\end{proof}

The input-length parameter is essential. A binary-compressed grammar description
whose explicit expansion is huge is not covered by pretending that expansion is
free. Similarly, small representative tables do not bound the discovery cost of
new geometric types or the cost of expanding a shared witness into an actual
surface certificate.

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial and quasi-polynomial regimes]\label[corollary]{cor:qp}
Suppose an $n$-crossing input has a complete geometric disk-search grammar satisfying
\cref{def:grammar}, constructible with polynomial encoded overhead, polynomially many
types and rules, and polynomial-size witnesses and local verification. If its live-arc
bound is $b=O(\log(n+2))$, the search above is polynomial. If
$b=O((\log(n+2))^2)$, it runs in
\[
                         \exp(O((\log(n+2))^2))=n^{O(\log n)}
\]
for $n\ge2$. If terminal success is a properly embedded essential-boundary disk in
a source-certified knot exterior and the grammar is complete for those disks, this
yields a recognition algorithm within the same bound.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the bounds into \cref{thm:complexity}. The last assertion additionally
uses the compressing-disc characterization discussed in \cref{sec:native}, and the
assumed completeness of the grammar.
\end{proof}

The corollary isolates a genuine improvement in the allowable frontier scale. Direct
Bell-state enumeration pays $2^{\Theta(b\log b)}$, giving the sufficient scale
$b=O((\log n)^2/\log\log n)$ for a similar quasi-polynomial bound. Here the
$\log b$ loss disappears for the disk-completion language. This comparison does
not imply that the predecessor's more general coning language can be reduced by the
same rule. Nor have we proved the stated width or completeness assumptions for all
knot diagrams.

\section{Connecting the kernel to the native compressing-disc route}\label{sec:native}
\subsection{What the reviewed native modules already provide}
The maintained synthesis \cite{proveit-weighted} describes three relevant entry points:
\begin{quote}\small
\path{normal_surface_components.normal_component_census}\\
\path{normal_component_verify.verify_normal_component_certificate}\\
\path{normal_disk_kernel.normal_compressing_disk_count}.
\end{quote}
Their purpose is to analyze a \emph{supplied} admissible normal vector in a validated
compact triangulation. Binary multiplicities remain compressed. The source-checked
exterior construction \cite{proveit-exterior} binds one canonical triangulation to a
validated input knot diagram. Those facts make a constructive disk search more useful
than an isolated partition calculation, but they do not supply the assembly grammar
assumed in \cref{cor:qp}.

The native essential-disc criterion is componentwise: a connected component with
Euler characteristic one and a nonzero pair of torus-boundary parity evaluations is
a compressing disk. A closed projective plane also has Euler characteristic one but
has zero boundary values, so it is excluded. Taking only the total boundary parity of
a whole normal surface is insufficient; parallel meridian disks can cancel in the
aggregate. Our search instead assembles a single disk and must retain its own boundary
class. This is an application of the existing criterion, not a replacement for the
native component census.

\subsection{Two homology bits do not destroy the state bound}
Let $M$ be a source-certified knot exterior, with boundary torus $\mathbb T$.
Fix a certified basis of $H^1(\mathbb T;\F)$. Suppose a geometric grammar assigns
to each piece its external boundary chain on $\mathbb T$ and ensures that all internal
arc identifications cancel the appropriate chain contributions. The two cocycle
evaluations of the final boundary are then the sum of the piece contributions,
including any certified local correction terms. This is a \emph{chain-level}
requirement: naming two arbitrary stored bits ``homology'' does not meet it.

\begin{proposition}[Essential-boundary refinement]\label[proposition]{prop:essential}
Under the preceding certified chain contract, reducing separately for the four possible
boundary parity labels preserves minimum-cost disk completions with any prescribed
final parity. It uses at most $4\cdot2^{r-1}$ representatives per other fixed type
and active interface. A terminal properly embedded disk with nonzero parity is a
compressing disk in $M$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed complementary object, the required partial parity is determined by the
specified final parity and the complementary contribution. The representative theorem
applies within that parity class, so a no-more-expensive replacement leaves the final
parity unchanged. There are four classes.

The boundary of a properly embedded disk is an embedded circle in the torus. An
inessential embedded circle has zero homology class. An essential embedded circle on
a torus represents a primitive integral class $(a,b)$; a primitive pair cannot have
both entries even, and hence has nonzero reduction modulo two. Thus nonzero parity
is precisely essentiality for this circle. The disk is therefore a compressing disk.
\end{proof}

For a genuine knot exterior in $S^3$, the existence of a compressing disk for its
boundary torus characterizes the unknot: the exterior is irreducible, boundary
compression gives the solid-torus case, and the knot is unknotted. This is the
geometric endpoint already used in the native source theorem \cite{proveit-exterior}.
The source binding matters. A disk in an unrelated solid torus says nothing about
the input knot.

\subsection{The integration gate}
A native use of this reducer needs four independently identifiable layers. First, a
producer must export actual disk pieces, their labelled boundary intervals, and their
embedded regions or other sufficient compatibility types from the current geometric
representation. Second, the search must account for all rule generation and all type
and width growth. Third, each rank reduction must be checked against the actual
family it prunes. Finally, an accepted assembly must replay to a properly embedded
essential-boundary disk in the \emph{same} source-certified exterior.

The package supplies the third layer and an independent \emph{abstract-surface} form
of the final replay, together with a complete solver for its supplied finite grammar.
It does not supply the first layer, a complete normal-vector search, a general hierarchy
compiler, or a native embedded-disk certificate exporter. The canonical exterior
producer itself is \emph{not} missing at the reviewed pin. The remaining gap is the
complete, type-controlled disk-assembly search attached to it.

The intended integration is an isolated experimental report or library module. No
maintained dispatch should infer \texttt{KNOTTED} from the absence of a disk in an
incomplete grammar. A successful abstract polygon gluing should not infer
\texttt{UNKNOT} without its ambient embedding and source certificate. Resource-limit
exceptions are inconclusive and must preserve access to the existing exact fallback.

\section{Safety boundaries and explicit counterexamples}\label{sec:barriers}
\subsection{A same-component guard is not preserved}
Take the three candidates of \cref{ex:r3} with costs $0,0,1$, in the order
$001,010,011$. The reducer keeps the first two and discards the third, because its
root vector is their sum. It still preserves the minimum cost for \emph{every}
disk-completion query.

However, the predicate ``labels $1$ and $2$ lie in the same block'' holds only for
$011$ among these candidates. A subsequent rule enabled only by that predicate
would lose its only admissible source. Therefore the reducer is not a valid
optimization of arbitrary partition-dependent transition systems. This is an executable
negative test in the delivery. The correct repair is to prove an appropriate context
contract or split types by the relevant predicate, and then count the extra types.

In particular, homogeneity guards for arbitrary interval attachments in the preceding
port report may depend on component information not preserved here. Neither the
Bell-state lower bound nor that report's guard semantics is contradicted by our result.
We preserve a narrower terminal language rather than reconstruct the entire state.

\subsection{Compressed point incidence is not geometric arc incidence}
The sparse component census counts components meeting specified point sets. One row
can describe many components and a marked interval can represent many normal arcs.
Our width $r$ counts actual boundary intervals in a surface gluing. The example of
two disks glued along two arcs, rather than one, proves that simply using the number
of histogram rows or named point ports as $r$ is unsound.

A future compressed version could attach a binary multiplicity to a family of parallel
arcs, but it would need to track the resulting Euler correction, attachment cycles,
possible orientation effects, and geometric compatibility. The present theorem does
not turn an exponential number of actual arcs into a logarithmic interface by naming
their bundle once.

\subsection{Boundary constraints, hidden types, and circles}
A partition does not encode every ambient embedding constraint. Different cyclic
orders, different positions on an incompressible boundary pattern, or different
quadrilateral choices can be incompatible with the same future region. The type
contract must expose whatever data the continuation can inspect. If there are
$2^{\Theta(b\log b)}$ such types, a $2^b$ representative count inside each type
has not eliminated the overall Bell-scale obstruction.

Gluing along a whole boundary circle is also not an arc gluing. Its Euler correction
is zero rather than one, and capping by a disk can remove boundary in a way excluded
by the defect monotonicity argument. Likewise, compressing an annulus by an actual
surgery is not an attachment of surfaces along disjoint boundary intervals. The
persistence theorem must not be extended to these operations without a new proof.

\subsection{Optimization is not counting, and certificates have scope}
A representative subset preserves minima and existence for the specified completion
predicates. It does not preserve the number of successful candidates, their distribution
of witness topologies beyond the recorded type, or a full Pareto frontier of several
costs. Linear dependencies may eliminate many distinct valid witnesses. This is
intentional for decision and scalar optimization, but it is unsuitable as a counting
algorithm without additional machinery.

The algebraic checker certifies source-bound row identities and cost dominance.
The PL replay certifies the topology of the returned abstract glued surface. Neither
checks an embedding in a 3-manifold, normal matching equations, diagram-to-exterior
provenance, or the completeness of a producer's rule set. Those are separate proof
obligations, even when all local code checks pass.

\section{Implementation, validation, and paired measurements}\label{sec:validation}
\subsection{The independent triangulated-surface oracle}
For each partition block containing $k$ labels, \path{code/surface_model.py}
constructs a polygonal disk with $4k$ boundary vertices, triangulated from a new central
vertex. It marks $k$ separated boundary edges as the attachment arcs, leaving gaps
between their endpoints. For two frontiers or a whole grammar witness, it glues the
selected boundary edges with the prescribed endpoint maps.

The oracle then constructs the quotient simplicial complex. It checks triangle and
edge incidence and checks every vertex link is a connected path or circle, thereby
rejecting nonmanifold identifications instead of merely counting cells. It computes
connected components, Euler characteristic, boundary-circle count, and orientability
by propagating triangle orientations. These computations do not call the root-vector
or transition routines. The result is a genuine finite surface calculation, not the
partition predicate restated as an expected value.

This oracle intentionally expands small polygonal fixtures. It is not a compressed
normal-surface algorithm, and its triangle count is part of its runtime. An abstract
surface can be checked this way without claiming to know where it embeds in a knot
exterior.

The only algorithmic difference is representative reduction between stages.

\subsection{A negative timing result: do not compress a lone query by default}
A width-eight control uses the same initial family and cap but no intermediate layer.
The direct solver takes \ControlExact{} seconds, while the representative version
takes \ControlReduced{} seconds, approximately \ControlSlowdown{} times longer.
Both return cost $-97$. This control is a single recorded timing per mode, not a
three-repeat median. Its purpose is to show a real overhead case, not to estimate
stable hardware performance from one sample.

A single cap can be checked directly in $\poly(r)$ work per candidate. Constructing
and reducing $2^{r-1}$-bit vectors before that one query is often wasteful. The
benefit appears when enough subsequent candidate generation or cap queries reuse the
smaller family. An eventual native scheduler should retain a direct path and compare
amortized savings against construction and checking costs. The present measurements
justify neither enabling this kernel everywhere nor advertising a whole-recognizer
speed factor.

benchmark was performed. The repository sources and incoming directory were inspected
through the available read interfaces; the incoming binary archive collection was not
fully unpacked and audited. The predecessor port report was reviewed through its
available Library text. The source-audit file records this coverage rather than
claiming an exhaustive review of every incoming manuscript.

\section{Further research questions and concrete next milestones}\label{sec:future}
\subsection{Geometric construction and completeness}
\paragraph{1. A native arc-interface exporter.}
Can the current source-checked compact exterior and normal-component modules export a
supplied disk decomposition with genuine separated boundary arcs, explicit collars,
and an independently replayable embedding? The first target is correctness on supplied
witnesses, not discovery. A successful exporter should distinguish point-incidence ports
from the arc labels used in this article and verify all chain-level boundary parities.

\paragraph{2. A complete disk-assembly grammar.}
Which normal or hierarchy decompositions guarantee that every essential disk has an
assembly generated by polynomially described local rules? The proof must show that
intermediate cuts satisfy the boundary-arc contract, that no omitted surgery is needed,
and that every required normal-coordinate choice can actually be generated within the
claimed bound. A small successful witness is not a bound on the complete search.

\paragraph{3. Controlling geometric types.}
Can cyclic order, normal matching, quadrilateral compatibility, ambient region data,
and external-boundary constraints be organized into only $2^{O(b)}\poly(n)$ types?
If a stronger polynomial type bound is false, isolate the smallest datum producing
super-single-exponential growth. This is as important as reducing partition counts.

\paragraph{4. A width theorem tied to a hierarchy.}
Does a complete compressed hierarchy admit disk-search frontiers with
$b=O((\log n)^2)$ and polynomially bounded witness encodings? A width estimate for
a projection graph alone does not bound the number of actual surface arcs carried
through that frontier. A proof needs a global encoded potential paying for arc births,
refinement, coordinate growth, and all visited geometric types.

\subsection{Sharper algebra and compressed geometry}
\paragraph{5. Multiplicity-aware arc bundles.}
Find a compact completion algebra for many parallel boundary arcs that preserves the
cycle defect and orientation data. A desirable theorem would use logarithms of binary
multiplicities rather than expanding all arcs, while still reproducing the annulus and
M\"obius negative controls. The one-port surrogate without correction is ruled out
by \cref{sec:barriers}.

\paragraph{6. Faster graded reduction and joins.}
The implementation uses conservative dense bit vectors. Can the rooted-forest minors
be streamed, factored, or kept in sparse graded form to avoid $d^2$ storage in common
cases? Representative product-family methods \cite{products} suggest reducing joins
without first constructing every child pair. Any improved bound should include
certificate generation, integer weight comparisons, and witness recovery, not only
field-operation counts.

\paragraph{7. Persistent and changing-interface families.}
Can verified bases be reused under rollback, changes of costs, or insertion and deletion
of active labels? Cost monotonicity in the present certificate is tied to one input
ordering, and arbitrary future weight changes invalidate it. A useful persistent
scheme should maintain checkable source binding and charge its work across the actual
search DAG, not just one successful branch.

\paragraph{8. Bounded-defect completion.}
For targets of genus at most $g$ or a prescribed boundary pattern, determine the rank
of the completion predicate after simultaneously recording defect, component count,
and the signed consistency data of \cref{thm:signed}. The disk case has the special
zero-defect obstruction. Simply accepting connected signed states does not control
annuli or handles. It would be useful to identify parameter ranges admitting
$2^{O(b+g)}$ rather than Bell-scale kernels.

\subsection{Verification, scheduling, and limitations}
\paragraph{9. Multiobjective costs and actual geometric weight.}
Normal weight, encoded complexity, boundary data, and future width may be competing
objectives. Does a useful restricted Pareto order permit small representative families,
or do unavoidable trade-offs force many representatives? For scalar cost, determine
which geometric weights remain exactly additive under the chosen local rules and
which need correction terms.

\paragraph{10. Whole-pipeline ablation.}
After the native exporter exists, benchmark complete recognition with and without
representative reduction on the same knot inputs and budgets. Include direct cap
queries, already-small families, failed grammar searches, and large-type cases.
Record candidate generation, geometry validation, certificate replay, and fallback
work separately. The negative one-query control should remain a required regression.

\paragraph{11. Formal proof and checker hardening.}
A compact formalization can begin with the defect identity, the rooted-forest minor
lemma, the exact matrix factorization, and cost-dominating row expansions. The
geometric type/context replacement theorem is a separate formalization task, not a
consequence of verifying Gaussian elimination. On the implementation side, bounded
allocation, adversarial certificate size, serialization, and cancellation throughout
a long grammar evaluation need production-level engineering.

\paragraph{12. Sharp families inside knot geometry.}
The coordinate partitions prove optimality in the abstract interface model. Which of
them can coexist with one common boundary type in a natural decomposition of a knot
exterior? A realizability theorem would clarify whether the sharp bound is relevant
in practice; an obstruction could yield a still smaller kernel. Conversely, construct
families where the partition kernel is small but type growth or actual arc width defeats
the desired global bound. Such examples would identify the true next bottleneck.

\section{Conclusion}
Bell-many distinguishable physical states do not force Bell-many representatives for
minimum disk completion. The reason is not that topology can be ignored. The exact
defect identity first shows which topology is relevant: all pieces must be disks and
their attachment graph must be a tree. That specific predicate has an explicit
root-transversal factorization, exact rank $2^{r-1}$, and sharp graded representative
bounds. Weighted reduction retains actual witnesses and admits source-bound independent
checking. The typed replacement theorem then justifies reuse inside a finite assembly
search, rather than merely optimizing an isolated matrix calculation.

The verified standalone implementation and its complete-grammar comparisons demonstrate
both the potential benefit and the overhead. The current ProveIt exterior and component
certificates provide a concrete destination for the next geometric integration, but the
complete bounded-width disk-assembly producer is still an unproved obligation. The
present result removes a Bell-number factor from a precisely specified search layer;
it does not disguise the remaining search and geometry costs as constant-time operations.

\section{A complete small reduction example}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
from disk_kernel import Candidate, reduce_family
from certificate_check import verify_certificate

items = [Candidate((0, 0, 1), 0),
         Candidate((0, 1, 0), 0),
         Candidate((0, 1, 1), 1)]
answer = reduce_family(items)
assert len(answer.retained) == 2
assert verify_certificate([x.partition for x in items],
                          [x.cost for x in items],
                          answer.certificate)
# For the third row the expansion is [0, 1].
# This certifies every disk-completion optimum, not arbitrary guards.
\end{lstlisting}

The default root-vector cap is 20 labels. It is an allocation guard, not a mathematical
bound on the theorem. The layered prototype also validates the combined incoming and
outgoing partition of each piece under its current width guard. Its intended role is
small exact experimentation and independent replay, not an uncapped production engine.
The reducer accepts a cooperative callback; an exception propagates rather than
publishing a partial reduction as a complete result. The top-level grammar solver is
finite but does not yet implement a global cooperative cancellation budget. A native
integration must add and test that resource contract.
