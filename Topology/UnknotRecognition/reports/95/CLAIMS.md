# Claims and boundaries

## Proved and implemented

1. **Saturated residual incidence.** Under the actual original source product charts, a saturated bordered interval bundle has one residual two-handle whose original-core unsigned incidences are sums of its annulus incidences. Each own private column initially has incidence two.
2. **Private-column omission.** Columns initially containing only zero or values at least two never acquire a unit under the positive pivot rule. They can be omitted from pivot search, while their exact count remains in the genus calculation.
3. **Whole-cut census.** Verified elimination of all original and residual rows gives the complete unmarked handlebody genus census, with the connector correction `sum(relative_one_handles) - number_of_original_cores + 1`.
4. **Complete full-row peeling.** Full positive cancellation is equivalent to a permanent-one minor on all rows and to empty unit-column peeling. The residual is the greatest stopping subset. This follows from the matching/closure theory already present in report 56.
5. **Early core refusal.** A stopping subset of original core rows remains an obstruction after saturated rows are appended. The optional precheck is a sound shortcut for this raw certificate method.

## Proved, conditional, and not implemented as general geometric operators

- **Observer-family compression:** identical private column families can carry exact observer incidences with polynomial bit growth under task column peeling. Exact uncapped donor entries and authenticated observer-family coordinates are required.
- **Ambient-block repair:** for authenticated embedded surfaces with individually nonzero relative homology classes, the specified seam-free ordinary repairs retain all other ambient surfaces. If `C0` is total initial nonnegative pattern complexity and `I0` the pairwise intersection-component count, there are at most `floor(C0/4) + I0` nonterminal repairs, and possibly one further terminal surgery. The independent-class variant also retains homological independence.

## Established by the preserved experiments

- 85/106 complete native cut censuses versus 78/106 at the pinned baseline; no old success lost.
- 96 independently replayed compiled conclusions; 78 positive agreements with literal Regina cuts; seven positive binary cases beyond the expansion cap.
- 21 remaining inconclusive cases, of which 15 are unions of handlebodies according to the small explicit oracle.
- Six early core proofs, five cases avoiding two actual boundary orbit queries each, ten queries avoided in total.
- All 37 extension test methods pass; the separately tested pinned source passes 1,663 native regression tests.
- Quadratic-to-linear incidence work on the chain/fan matrix families, with raw timings and A/A controls preserved.
- Verified common-success native calls are mostly unchanged and show small regressions on two selected cases; early obstruction calls show substantial task-specific reductions.

## Not established

- A general quasi-polynomial unknot-recognition algorithm.
- A complete handlebody classifier: eight raw obstructions and seven three-handle cases in the audit are positive cut examples.
- A knot verdict from a handlebody cut alone.
- Arbitrary boundary-pattern transport, source addresses for every private cocore, or a complete compressed hierarchy transition.
- Polynomial surface/disc discovery, logarithmic hierarchy depth, or a global bound on all intermediate encodings.
- Preservation of old suffix certificates after ambient repair; simultaneous normalization with controlled intersections; or a polynomial bit-complexity bound from the numerical seam potential.
- Improvement in successful cases merely from replacing the old minimum-fill planner: that old planner already decides full-row completion correctly.

The February and March 2021 Lackenby slide targets differ. The article records both, and distinguishes their announced asymptotics from the iteration and hierarchy-length statements proved in the July 2026 paper.
