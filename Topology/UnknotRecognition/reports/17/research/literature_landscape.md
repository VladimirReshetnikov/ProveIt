# Research landscape and the status of the quasi-polynomial objective

*Source audit: 8 October 2026. Citation keys refer to research_references.bib.*

Three levels of progress must be kept distinct: an announced general recognition theorem, published algorithms for constituent problems and restricted inputs, and the implemented ProveIt recognizer. Lackenby's March 2021 Oxford slides announce a deterministic algorithm for an n-crossing diagram with running time \(2^{O((\log n)^3)}=n^{O((\log n)^2)}\). This is quasi-polynomial, but is not the stronger \(n^{O(\log n)}\) target used in some project documents. The slides attribute the improvement to controlled-complexity surfaces, compressed hierarchy encodings, simultaneous surfaces, and Heegaard/Cheeger-region methods. They are an announcement and explanation, not a complete implementation specification. [lackenby2021](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/lackenby/quasipolynomial-talk-oxford-compressed.pdf)

The July 2026 preprint *Incompressible surfaces, hierarchies and unknot recognition* supplies a new hierarchy algorithm and a new NP-certification argument. It does not establish the announced quasi-polynomial running time in that paper. Section 9 explicitly separates iteration counting from estimating the cost of individual operations; Proposition 9.1 bounds iterations by \(L(g+1)^L\). Consequently, supplying short numerical profiles or a fast terminal-pattern checker does not settle the total bit complexity. One also needs a proved strategy controlling the profiles and exact compressed implementations of construction, cutting, simplification, and restarts. This assessment concerns the reviewed author sources; it does not assert that every unpublished argument is unavailable. [lackenby2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23350v1)

There is substantive published progress on a necessary geometric component. Lackenby's *Some Fast Algorithms for Curves in Surfaces*, now published in *Discrete & Computational Geometry* 76 (2026), 539–588, computes geometric intersection numbers of normal 1-manifolds on compact orientable surfaces in time polynomial in triangulation size and the logarithms of normal weights. Its isotopy theorem additionally assumes essential 1-manifolds. These results offer a credible foundation for compressed boundary-pattern operations, while their integration into a fully costed hierarchy search remains a separate obligation. [lackenbycurves2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00454-026-00845-7)

The algebraic route has different strengths and obstructions. Kronheimer–Mrowka prove that reduced Khovanov rank one characterizes the unknot, giving a complete mathematical endpoint. Bar-Natan's local cancellation algorithm is a practical way to reach that endpoint, but its paper supplies no general small-complex bound. The 2026 Kelomäki–Schütz preprint proves exponential scanning behavior even on positive or alternating 3-braids, alongside polynomial algorithms for compressed homology of all 3-braids and for fixed homological windows at fixed strand count. Thus bounded frontier controls interface geometry without controlling the number of explicit homology generators. [kronheimer2011](https://pmihes.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.1007/s10240-010-0030-y/), [barnatan2007](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/~drorbn/papers/FastKh/), [kelomaki2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02119v1)

Efficient width-dependent Jones computation is established prior art. The signed Tait/Potts correspondence is classical; Makowsky proves bounded-treewidth tractability; Maria proves single exponential carving-width bounds for quantum invariants. Meichanetzidis–Kourtis explicitly use and benchmark the same Q=5 specialization developed into exact integer-pair code here. Accordingly, this continuation should claim a concrete repository improvement with proved transfer bounds, audited arithmetic, reproducible validation, and explicit limits. It should claim neither a new Potts–Jones correspondence nor the first single exponential width algorithm. [makowsky2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2004.01.016), [maria2021](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2021.53), [meichanetzidis2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02119)

At the pinned ProveIt snapshot, the general implementation still has an exponential worst-case fallback. Reports 07–12 already address component sharing, complete specialized braid recognition, factorization, sparse elimination, twist compression, and succinct algebraic cancellation; their restricted or conditional bounds must remain distinct from unrestricted recognition. The new Jones obstruction backend is sound when it proves inequality with the unknot value. Agreement remains inconclusive; in particular, the exact Q=5 weaving failure family proved in this article rules out completeness of that specialization. The global research goal therefore remains to connect compressed operations to a proved global search bound, rather than to infer quasi-polynomial recognition from improved arithmetic or favorable benchmarks.
