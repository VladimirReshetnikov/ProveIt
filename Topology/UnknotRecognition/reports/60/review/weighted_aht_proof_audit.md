# Independent mathematical audit: weighted AHT and normal component profiles

Research notes for the ProveIt continuation, 9 October 2026. These are intermediate audit notes, not a claim of a new general unknot-recognition complexity bound.

## 1. What is established and what is being added

Weighted interval-orbit counting and recovery of normal component vectors are established results of Agol--Hass--Thurston (AHT), Section 6, Theorem 16 and Corollary 17. The repository contribution is an independently checkable implementation over the maintained interval-reduction trace, a stronger bound for the particular rightmost-range transfer schedule, explicit normal-coordinate extraction, and useful consequences for sparse output and disk selection. The statements below separate elementary deductions from implementation claims.

## 2. Weighted replay invariant

Let the original integer universe be [0,N), with equivalence relation generated by k interval isometries. Let w:[0,N) -> Z^d be constant on m0 maximal integer intervals. During replay, the current relation represents the unresolved original orbits. Every original scalar weight is attached to exactly one current representative, unless its entire orbit has already been emitted. Consequently, for each unresolved original orbit O, the sum of weights over its current representatives equals the original sum over O. Emitted records contain the exact original sum and the number of distinct orbits with that sum.

This stronger invariant implies conservation of the global total but cannot be replaced by global conservation. It is also the reason source-vector equality alone does not certify a proposed normal component decomposition: a compatible normal sum can reconnect its summands.

For delete, trim, periodic merge and transmission, the current equivalence relation on the unchanged universe is exactly the same. Leave weights unchanged. In particular, a sharp Fine--Wilf merger is harmless: it preserves the whole equivalence relation. It need not preserve the orbit partition of each individual old generator, and no weighted proof should assume that it does.

For a static contraction, every uncovered point is a singleton orbit in the current relation. Intersect the static gaps with constant-weight runs and emit each intersection as (multiplicity = length, weight = run value). Then apply the same order-preserving deletion map to both pairings and surviving runs. Adjacent equal runs are coalesced. A contraction does not increase the number of live runs.

For a truncation, transfer the entire rightmost carrier range [c,N_current), not only the suffix actually being removed. Then truncate the zero-weight suffix. This distinction is essential for the additive complexity argument.

## 3. Explicit full-range transfer

The repository pairing convention is inclusive: g:[a,b] -> [c,d], with a<=c and b-a=d-c. At a truncation d=N_current-1. The weight runs are more conveniently written half-open.

### Translation

For g(x)=x+p, p=c-a>0, define B=[a,c). Transfer every weight at x in [c,N_current) to

    rho(x) = a + ((x-a) mod p).

The target is in the same orbit by repeated inverse carrier applications. When domain and range are disjoint, this formula reduces to one inverse application and leaves the gap untouched.

For each source run [l,r) of value z, write L=r-l=hp+s with 0<=s<p. Add h*z to all of B, and add z on the cyclic interval of length s beginning at a+((l-a) mod p). The cyclic interval splits into at most two ordinary intervals. Clear the entire source range. This formula performs O(1) integer operations per source run, independent of its expanded length.

### Reflection

The maintained trace trims overlapping reflections before selecting the carrier. Hence domain and range are disjoint. Put A=a+d. A source run [l,r) maps by reflection to

    [A-r+1, A-l+1).

Add its value to that target and clear the full source range. Any reflection-fixed midpoint was removed from the generator during trimming, not from the universe; its weight remains and is either joined by other generators or later emitted as static.

### Truncation

The target of transfer lies below c. The count trace chooses N_new>=c, so all transferred mass survives. The actual removed suffix [N_new,N_current) has weight zero. The unweighted trace establishes that the retained representatives describe exactly the surviving orbits. Together these facts preserve the weighted invariant.

## 4. Additive run count, including the sharper actual-schedule bound

The generic AHT transfer bound is an increase by at most four constant runs. The implementation's rightmost full-range transfer followed by truncation has the stronger bound of at most three; this has been independently checked against the argument in geometry_research/weighted_theorems.tex.

Let J be the old set of internal jump positions and let s=|J intersect (c,N_current)|. Clearing the entire rightmost range removes these s jumps. Original jumps strictly below c remain possible, and need not be charged as new.

For a translation, extend the source-restricted function by zero. Its discrete derivative has possible support at the s old internal jumps and at the two cutoffs c,N_current. Under folding modulo p, c maps to a, the boundary of the target interval. Thus only the s old jumps and the terminal cutoff N_current can create internal jumps in B: at most s+1 possible new internal locations. The only other possible new internal universe boundaries are a and c. Added locations are therefore at most s+3, while s locations were removed. The net run increase is at most three. This argument counts jumps of the full vector, so it is independent of the weight dimension and survives cancellation of individual coordinates.

For a trimmed reflection, source jumps are reflected into the domain: at most s new internal domain jumps. The only further possible boundaries are a,b+1,c, again at most three. The removed s source jumps cancel their contribution to the bound. The same argument applies to a disjoint translation without cyclic notation.

Truncation afterward cannot increase the number of runs. If a=0, one potential internal boundary disappears; if N_new=c, the c boundary disappears. Either condition improves the bound to two. Both conditions together improve it to one, when the corresponding case has nonempty remaining target.

The constant three is sharp for a valid trace. Start with N=7 and constant scalar weight 1, and pairings

    [1,2] -> [5,6], [3,4] -> [4,5], [0,0] -> [3,3].

There are no static gaps. The first pairing is the unique carrier reaching 6; the second pairing's range is not wholly inside its range, so the cut is N_new=6. Full-range transfer yields weights 1 on [0,1), 2 on [1,3), 1 on [3,5), and 0 on [5,6). Thus one run becomes four.

For a general transfer range [c,d+1) not extending to the universe endpoint, the extra possible cutoff d+1 gives the classical bound four. The sharper three therefore depends on the actual schedule and should not be stated for arbitrary transfers.

The key point is additivity. Charging two residual intervals independently for every old run would conceal cancellation of adjacent endpoint events and could incorrectly suggest multiplicative growth.

## 5. Precise parameter and output accounting

Let E be the number of local operations in a complete supplied count trace, R<=E its number of truncations, and B a bound such that every original scalar weight has absolute value below 2^B. Set

    L = ceil(log2(N+1)),
    beta = B + L + O(1),
    M_live = m0 + 3R <= m0 + 3E.

Every live scalar weight is a sum of disjoint original scalar weights, with no duplication. It is therefore bounded in absolute value by N*2^B, even for signed weights. Endpoint-difference events may introduce an extra constant number of bits, but not a new factor depending on expanded length. Multiplicities and coordinates of points require O(L) bits.

The operation count E is part of the certificate-size parameter for arbitrary supplied traces. An adversarial or padded trace must not be assigned a polynomial-in-input verification bound independent of its own length. For the producer's maintained AHT schedule, the established unweighted polynomial trace bound in k and L yields a polynomial-in-compressed-input weighted algorithm. The Fine--Wilf replacement preserves correctness; its scheduler complexity should be cited to the repository's previous proof rather than silently inferred only from semantic equivalence.

For a straightforward implementation using dense d-vectors and sorted endpoint sweeps, a safe arithmetic-work bound for replay is

    O(d * E * (m0+E+k) * log(m0+E+k+2) * M(beta)),

plus the cost of independently validating the unweighted trace. Here M(beta) bounds beta-bit multiplication; schoolbook M(beta)=O(beta^2) is sufficient. Reading, validating and canonicalizing the initial run encoding should also be charged. Different data structures can reduce this bound, but no optimized exponent is needed for the polynomial conclusion.

A static contraction emits at most O(M_live+k) run-gap intersections. Therefore the number of raw emitted records is

    H_raw = O(E*(m0+E+k)).

The final coalesced histogram can only be smaller. Its bit length is at most

    O(H_raw * (d*beta + L)),

apart from structural delimiters. Dense output of one record per original orbit is not promised and may be exponentially larger than the input. A record (mu,z) means mu distinct original orbits, each with weight z; it does not identify all their original points.

For normal component weights, d=7t, initial weights in each scalar coordinate are 0 or 1, and m0=O(t). The total point count is the normal surface's intersection number with the global 1-skeleton, at most four times the total number of elementary disks. Thus L=O(B_normal+log(t+1)). The next theorem gives a stronger linear bound on the number of distinct final normal-coordinate records.

## 6. A support-sensitive linear bound on component types

### Theorem

Let T be a finite triangulation of a compact 3-manifold, allowing the usual face identifications, with t tetrahedra and v manifold vertices. Let F be an embedded normal surface. Let q be the number of its nonzero quadrilateral coordinates, so q<=t. Let H be the number of distinct standard normal-coordinate vectors among the connected components of F, with multiplicity ignored. Then

    H <= v + 2q.

If every component of F is two-sided, then H<=v+q. No incompressibility, minimality, or fundamental-surface assumption is needed.

Here “type” means standard normal-coordinate type, hence normal isotopy class, not merely homeomorphism type. The theorem does not bound the number of components with repetitions, which can be arbitrarily large even for fixed T.

### Lemma 1: disjoint two-sided vectors are independent

Let S_1,...,S_h be mutually disjoint connected two-sided normal surfaces whose standard vectors y_i are pairwise distinct. Then y_1,...,y_h are linearly independent over R.

Proof. A real dependence among integral vectors gives a rational dependence, hence an integral one. Since every y_i is nonzero and coordinatewise nonnegative, the coefficients cannot all have one sign. After separating positive and negative coefficients, obtain

    sum_{i in P} a_i y_i = sum_{j in Q} b_j y_j,

where P,Q are nonempty disjoint index sets and the integers a_i,b_j are positive. Choose mutually disjoint sufficiently small normal product neighborhoods of the two-sided S_i. Each coefficient can be realized by that many disjoint parallel normal copies in its own neighborhood. The two sides are therefore standard vectors of normal surfaces whose connected component multisets are exactly the indicated copies. Normal-coordinate uniqueness gives a normal isotopy between the two unions. It sends a connected component on one side to a connected component on the other, preserving its standard vector. This would identify y_i with y_j for i in P, j in Q, contradicting pairwise distinctness. QED.

Two-sidedness is indispensable: two copies of a one-sided surface do not form two disjoint parallel copies of that surface. Instead they form its connected normal double. The proof below treats this explicitly.

### Lemma 2: allowed-support dimension

Let V be the real vector space of solutions to the standard matching equations with every quadrilateral coordinate outside the q active positions of F constrained to zero. The projection from V to those q quadrilateral coordinates has kernel of dimension v, spanned by the v vertex-link vectors. Thus dim V<=v+q.

Proof. With all quadrilateral coordinates zero, a face matching equation identifies the triangle coefficients on the two corners identified through that face. The corner triangles belonging to a given manifold vertex have connected adjacency graph, since the vertex link is a connected disk or sphere. Hence all their coefficients agree. Coefficients at distinct vertices are independent. Their characteristic vectors are precisely the standard vertex-link vectors. Rank-nullity proves the claim. This argument concerns a real matching space, so there is no need to assume the vector itself is nonnegative. QED.

### Proof of the theorem

First consider all distinct connected component types of F with nonzero quadrilateral part. For each two-sided type, retain one component. For each one-sided type represented by S, replace S by the horizontal frontier of a sufficiently small normal interval neighborhood. This frontier is connected and two-sided, with standard vector 2*v(S): it is the double cover associated to the nontrivial normal line bundle. All replacements and retained surfaces can be chosen mutually disjoint, because the original components are disjoint. Delete repeated resulting normal-coordinate vectors.

Let r be the number of distinct remaining vectors. Every one has nonzero quadrilateral part. Adjoin a sufficiently small vertex link at each of the v vertices, chosen disjoint from this family. These are connected, two-sided normal surfaces, with distinct triangle-only vectors; none duplicates a nonvertex vector. By Lemma 1, the resulting r+v vectors are independent. They all lie in V, so Lemma 2 gives

    r+v <= dim V <= v+q,

and therefore r<=q.

Each resulting nonvertex vector z has at most two original component types mapping to it: a two-sided type with vector z, and a one-sided type with vector z/2. No other possibilities occur, and two different one-sided types cannot map to the same doubled vector. Thus the original number of nonvertex component types is at most 2r<=2q. Every triangle-only connected normal surface is a vertex link, so there are at most v remaining types. This proves H<=v+2q.

If every component is two-sided, replacement is unnecessary and each image has one preimage. The same argument gives H<=v+q. QED.

### Checks on subtle cases

* Several normally isotopic components have equal standard vectors and count once from the start. Arbitrary multiplicity is harmless.
* A one-sided type y and its disjoint two-sided normal double 2y can both occur as components, for example in the normal surface with vector 3y. They collide after the replacement map, and the factor two in the theorem accounts for exactly this phenomenon.
* Two one-sided types cannot collide after doubling unless their original vectors were equal.
* The product-neighborhood construction remains valid for a properly embedded surface with boundary: use the normal interval bundle with its vertical part in the manifold boundary and its horizontal frontier as the double.
* Tiny vertex links exist disjointly because there are finitely many compact normal pieces, all away from the triangulation vertices.
* In an orientable ambient manifold, one-sidedness agrees with nonorientability for an embedded surface. The proof is phrased with the normal line bundle so it does not need that equivalence.
* The finite compact-manifold hypothesis ensures each vertex link is a disk or sphere. Do not silently generalize the statement to nonmanifold vertices, immersed normal surfaces, or arbitrary singular matching systems.

### Stronger optional observation about rays

On a projective ray containing a connected normal surface, a primitive admissible integral vector represents a connected surface. If that surface is two-sided, only its primitive vector is connected on the ray. If it is one-sided, exactly its primitive vector and its double are connected. This follows by applying the ordered-sheet multiplicity rule already established in synthesis/normal_multiplicity.tex. Thus each ray has at most two connected normal types, and a connected standard vector has coordinate gcd at most two. This observation is consistent with the proof above, but is not needed for it.

### Output consequence

For a source vector whose coordinates use at most B_normal bits, each component coordinate is bounded by the corresponding source coordinate. Each multiplicity is bounded by the total number of normal disks. A coalesced component profile therefore has O((v+2q)*(7t*B_normal+B_normal+log(t+1))) bits, hence O(t^2*(B_normal+log(t+1))) in the usual finite triangulation size model. This is stronger than the generic polynomial raw-output bound and justifies examining every distinct component type without iterating over its possibly exponential multiplicity.

This is an elementary deduction from normal-coordinate uniqueness, normal neighborhood doubling, and the matching-space kernel. No literature-priority or optimality claim is made. Any claim that the bound is attained should use an explicit verified triangulation and normal vector; a one-sided Möbius component plus its double and the vertex link in a one-vertex one-quad example is a plausible test, not evidence until checked.

## 7. Disk extraction without extra topology queries

Suppose weighted orbit replay certifies that y is the vector of one connected component of the supplied embedded normal surface. Evaluate the existing linear Euler characteristic and boundary-arc-count functionals on y.

If chi(y)=1 and the boundary arc count is positive, the component is a disk. Indeed an orientable connected compact surface has chi=2-2g-b; with b>=1, equality chi=1 forces g=0 and b=1. A nonorientable connected compact surface with boundary has chi=2-h-b<=0 for h>=1,b>=1. The only closed connected chi=1 possibility is the projective plane, which the positive boundary test excludes.

No separate boundary-component counting or orientation-double orbit calculation is needed for this disk decision. Those calculations remain useful for full topology classification or other component predicates.

If the validated ambient boundary is a torus, an embedded simple closed curve is essential precisely when its mod-2 homology class is nonzero. An essential torus slope is primitive in integral homology, so it cannot be zero modulo 2. Equivalently, its mod-2 intersection cocycle is not a coboundary; a graph cycle with odd evaluation is a short witness. Apply this test to y, not to the source vector: the boundary classes of an even collection of essential disks cancel in the aggregate.

Scope is exact: this selects an essential disk among the components of a supplied normal surface. It does not find the source normal vector, prove that a supplied torus-boundary manifold is the exterior of the input diagram, or make failure to find such a component into a global knottedness certificate.

## 8. Sparse inventory for many marked ports

Let A_1,...,A_r be marked subsets of [0,N), each supplied as a union of intervals, with s marked intervals in total after optional per-mark normalization. Give coordinate i the nonnegative weight 1_{A_i}. Their joint endpoint partition has at most 2s+1 runs and weight dimension r. Weighted orbit replay gives, for each orbit O, its vector

    (|O intersect A_1|,...,|O intersect A_r|).

Threshold coordinates at zero and aggregate equal resulting bitmasks. This yields a complete sparse histogram of which marked sets each orbit meets. Runtime and output are polynomial in k,s,r,log(N+1), with the trace parameter charged as above. There is no 2^r search over subsets. A requested dense array of all 2^r signatures still has that output cost; the improvement is an interface returning only signatures that occur.

This is a direct use of weighted AHT, not a new theorem replacing it. It is nevertheless a concrete replacement for repeated mark-deletion counts or inclusion--exclusion over a dense family of subsets.

Singleton mark coordinates can identify the unique orbit containing a named original point. Since the total mass of such a coordinate is one, any output record with that coordinate equal to one must have multiplicity one. Multiple anchors can therefore certify connectivity of supplied endpoints without naming every point in the orbit.

The inventory is commutative. It loses cyclic order of attachments, edge-to-edge transport, and relative positions along a boundary circle. Four distinct marked points occurring around a circle in orders abcd and acbd have the same incidence counts. Nonabelian words, cyclic order or suitable compressed attachment maps require additional structure. This is the exact boundary between an incidence improvement and a complete hierarchy-decomposition primitive.

For a relevant modern application, A. He, E. Sedgwick and J. Spreer, *A practical algorithm for knot factorisation*, SoCG 2025, article 55, Section 4.1, describe using weighted AHT to track ideal and surviving edge segments during normal-surface crushing: https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol332-socg2025/html/LIPIcs.SoCG.2025.55/LIPIcs.SoCG.2025.55.html . Their compressed theoretical tracking result should not be confused with their simpler practical implementation.

## 9. Research directions that follow concretely

1. Replace dense multi-port subset queries with one sparse weighted profile, and measure improvements on many-port fixtures while preserving exact multiplicity and empty-signature handling.
2. Recover compressed boundary attachment maps or cyclic orders in addition to additive inventories; identify a class where their description length is polynomial, and construct obstructions outside it.
3. Combine component profiles with common-divisor normalization. A two-sided component scales to copies of itself; a one-sided component scales to copies of its double and possibly one central copy. Merge collisions between a preexisting double and a newly doubled type.
4. Use chi=1 plus nonempty boundary to scan candidates cheaply, invoking the existing boundary cocycle only for disk candidates. Establish the performance effect separately from the orbit-extraction cost.
5. Explore the support-sensitive component bound experimentally, characterize extremizers, and search the classical normal-finiteness literature for the best existing constants before making novelty claims.
6. Extend local proof records to decomposition and crushing operations while maintaining marked peripheral data. Distinguish theorem hypotheses for essential boundary patterns from arbitrary candidate branches.
7. Study normal-vector generation on restricted support, genus, handle-pattern or width parameters. Component extraction is polynomial once a vector is supplied; the main global complexity obstruction is obtaining a complete small family of useful vectors or hierarchy choices.
8. Develop full bit-length potentials for intermediate hierarchy descriptions, branching, repairs and output attachment maps. A polynomial orbit primitive alone does not provide the logarithmic depth or bounded branching required for a quasi-polynomial theorem.
