\begin{abstract}
We develop a compressed local optimization kernel for the group-certificate
branch of the ProveIt unknot recognizer. The existing producer chooses a
length-reducing Whitehead direction and then minimizes along its powers.
We instead optimize simultaneously over every integral left and right power
of a fixed multiplier. The non-multiplier letters cannot cancel, so total
cyclic length is an exact weighted integer $L^1$ tension objective on a graph
with only linearly many compressed gap records. Its dual is a capacitated
circulation. We give a bit-polynomial solver, a small integer primal--dual
witness, exact balanced-graph and forest shortcuts, and an independent verifier.

A direct reduced-image construction is the key representation result: retaining
binary-encoded repetition rules, each phase increases grammar size by at most
a constant factor, without general compressed free reduction. Thus $q$
strictly shortening phases, including certificates and replay, cost
$\poly(\sigma)2^{O(q)}$ for initial encoded size $\sigma$. This yields a
quasi-polynomial bound when a polynomial-size knot presentation reaches a
verified terminal class in $O(\log^2 n)$ phases. Neither that phase bound nor
terminal completeness is established for arbitrary diagrams; an infinite
family of already automorphically minimal infinite-cyclic presentations
exposes the latter obstruction.

The package includes 51 passing tests, 1,200 independently checked flow
instances, 360 braid-derived algebraic stages, and reproducible paired
measurements. Synthetic multi-run presentations improve, whereas all 21
one-step advantages on the small raw braid corpus disappear after singleton
elimination, and the ordinary-stage aggregate shows overhead. These are
research-kernel results, not a measured improvement to the complete maintained
recognizer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem, baseline, and precise contribution}
\label{sec:context}

\subsection{Why another compressed group kernel?}

The maintained ProveIt code has already addressed several distinct costs:
explicit word expansion, unit Whitehead descent, long relator powers, and
compressed overlap discovery. In particular, its power optimizer does not
naively repeat an automorphism $N$ times on a word containing $a^N$.
For a selected type-II Whitehead automorphism it computes the exact first
minimum by a weighted-median formula on a shared straight-line program.
The relevant source is \path{fast/fastunknot/whitehead_power.py}; the
producer chooses its direction earlier, in \path{compressed_search.py}.
The maintained proof is \path{synthesis/whitehead_powers.tex}
\cite{proveit,powerrepo}.

These observations rule out claiming the selected-direction median or simple
power batching as a new contribution. The remaining local issue is that a
single cut chooses a common exponent for many different oriented generators.
Different gaps may want different exponents. Profiling one chosen direction
does not optimize that larger family, even if the profile itself is exact.

Our replacement query is: for each multiplier $a$, choose all integral
left/right exponents jointly, then choose the best multiplier. This is a
well-defined infinite search space. It admits a finite, bit-polynomial
optimization and a separately checkable optimality proof. The resulting
operation strictly contains a powered Whitehead move, but is still only a
local family inside the full automorphism group.

\subsection{Relation to existing complexity results}

Roig, Ventura, and Weil give polynomial-time Whitehead minimization in the
\emph{expanded} input length and the free-group rank \cite{rvw}. Their
cut-based approach is part of the background here, not a new result of this
article. Expanded length can be exponential in compressed description size.
Schleimer's exposition supplies polynomial compressed word and reduction
algorithms \cite{schleimer}; the direct constructor below avoids needing
those more general routines for this particular family.

Kapovich's July 2026 preprint establishes fixed-rank compressed primitivity
results, including a polynomial rank-two algorithm, and distinguishes testing
compressed automorphic minimality from constructing a minimum. Its Open
Problem 1.5 explicitly retains the latter distinction \cite{kapovich}.
We do not resolve that general minimization problem. We optimize over a
single commuting shear family at a time and leave the number of changes of
multiplier uncontrolled.

Polynomial min-cost circulation is classical; for example, Goldberg and
Tarjan develop a cost-scaling approach \cite{goldberg}. Our capacity-bit
implementation is described and proved directly so that its iteration bound
does not silently depend on a large capacity's numeric value. We make no
claim that integer tension duality or scaling algorithms themselves are new.

Finally, Lackenby's July 2026 paper treats incompressible surfaces and
hierarchies but states in Section 9 that the implementation detail supplied
there is insufficient for a running-time estimate \cite{lackenby}. The
present algebraic kernel neither fills in that geometric algorithm nor
imports an unverified quasi-polynomial cost assertion from it.

\subsection{Results and their scope}

The principal results proved here are the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item An exact compressed tension formula and a polynomial-size primal--dual
certificate for the best integral shear, uniformly in rank.
\item A direct, literally reduced output grammar of at most constant-factor
larger size, together with an independently implemented decorated-word replay.
\item A $\poly(\sigma)2^{O(q)}$ bound for $q$ successful phases, and the
corresponding restricted quasi-polynomial recognition corollary.
\item Explicit separating and obstructing families: a one-step gain ratio
unbounded in expanded length, and infinite-cyclic presentations on which no
strict automorphic shortening is possible.
\end{enumerate}

These are extensions of the inspected repository mechanisms and are fully
proved in this draft. Establishing priority over every related algebraic or
optimization formulation would require a separate literature investigation.
The experimental conclusions are deliberately narrower than the mathematical
ones: a stronger local query need not be a faster default recognition stage.

\section{Encoding, contracts, and notation}
\label{sec:encoding}

Let $F(X)$ be the free group on $X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_r\}$. We represent a signed
letter by a nonzero integer $x$, with $-x$ denoting its inverse. The set of
\emph{alive generators} is supplied explicitly; a generator absent from all
current relators must not be silently deleted. Such a generator contributes
a free factor to the presented group.

The input is a tuple $\calW=(w_1,\ldots,w_m)$ of literally freely and cyclically
reduced words, including possible empty words and duplicate roots. Relator
slots are retained. Write
\[
 N=\sum_{i=1}^m |w_i|,\qquad
 \calP=\langle X\mid w_1,\ldots,w_m\rangle.
\]
For reduced cyclic words, $|w_i|=\len{w_i}$. A cyclic rotation of a relator
preserves its normal closure, but can change the meaning of a later
\emph{positional} overlap record. This distinction is important in
Section~\ref{sec:integration}.

\begin{definition}[Repetition grammar]
A repetition grammar is a topologically ordered acyclic list of rules of the
forms
\[
 0\to\epsilon,\qquad T\to x,\qquad C\to UV,
 \qquad P\to U^k,
\]
where $k\geq 0$ is written in binary. Empty, zero-power, and unit-power rules
are simplified by the supplied implementation. A root list specifies the
relators. A binary SLP is the special case without repetition rules.
\end{definition}

Repetition rules do not add a stronger compression model for polynomial-time
purposes: binary powering replaces $U^k$ with $O(\bl(k))$ concatenation rules.
They do matter for the sharper \emph{per-phase} representation bound. Expanding
all of them after every phase introduces a bit-length factor that our direct
bound intentionally avoids.

A bottom-up pass stores literal length, first letter, last letter, and a flag
for free reduction. A concatenation is freely reduced precisely when its
children are and its seam does not contain inverse letters. A nontrivial
power is freely reduced precisely when its base is and its cyclic seam is
reduced. Thus the input contract is checked without expansion. Since each
reachable nonempty rule produces a literal subword of a root, reduced roots
also ensure reduced reachable subwords.

Let $s$ be the number of nonempty rules in the supplied representation,
$G=s+m+r+2$, and $\sigma$ its complete binary encoding size, including root
indices, exponent encodings, and generator identifiers. A convenient
arithmetic bit bound is
\begin{equation}
 B=2+\lceil\log_2(N+m+2)\rceil+\lceil\log_2(G+2)\rceil
       +\max_{x\in X}\bl(x),
 \label{eq:bits}
\end{equation}
where a maximum over an empty set is zero. Products of multiplicities and
lengths may need a constant multiple of $B$ bits, which does not change the
bounds. Initially $\log(N+2)=O(\sigma)$: concatenation adds at most one bit
along a path, and repetition adds at most the encoded exponent length.

We use ordinary binary integer arithmetic and charge its bit costs. The
conservative global bounds permit quadratic-cost multiplication and
comparison-based dictionaries. The Python implementation uses dictionaries;
even worst-case quadratic collision overhead is absorbed in the stated
polynomial global bound. Faster balanced/forest counts stated in comparison
operations refer to standard comparison-map implementations, not a claim of
constant-time arithmetic on arbitrarily large integers.

\section{The commuting integral shear family}
\label{sec:shear}

Fix a positive generator $a\in X$. For every signed letter $x$ with
$|x|\ne a$, choose an independent integer $z_x$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{a,z}(a)=a,\qquad
 \Phi_{a,z}(x)=a^{-z_{-x}}x a^{z_x} \quad (|x|\ne a).
 \label{eq:shear}
\end{equation}
The formula on inverse letters agrees with inversion, so it defines an
endomorphism of the free group.

\begin{lemma}[Commuting automorphisms]
For fixed $a$,
\[
 \Phi_{a,z}\Phi_{a,t}=\Phi_{a,z+t},\qquad
 \Phi_{a,z}^{-1}=\Phi_{a,-z}.
\]
In particular every integral potential vector defines an automorphism.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The multiplier and all its powers are fixed. Substituting the image of $x$
into $a^{-t_{-x}}x a^{t_x}$ adds the left exponents and the right exponents.
This gives the first identity on each generator, and the second follows by
setting $t=-z$.
\end{proof}

The family includes powers of the repository's type-II Whitehead convention.
For $a\in A$, $-a\notin A$, put $z_x=k[x\in A]$ for all other signed letters.
Equation~\eqref{eq:shear} is precisely the $k$th power of that Whitehead
map. Conversely, any $z$ factors into at most $2(r-1)$ powered singleton
Whitehead maps. If $z_x>0$, use multiplier $a$, subset $\{a,x\}$, and power
$z_x$. If $z_x<0$, use multiplier $-a$, subset $\{-a,x\}$, and power $-z_x$.
All factors commute. The factors need not each decrease length.

Allowing signed $z$ makes negative multipliers redundant for the joint query:
$\Phi_{-a,z}=\Phi_{a,-z}$. In cyclic length calculations, adding the same
constant to every potential is also irrelevant; it changes only a common
conjugation. Additional independent gauge shifts can occur in disconnected
gap graphs.

\subsection{Why retained letters never cancel}

For a non-pure cyclic word retain its cyclic sequence of letters different
from $a^{\pm1}$. Between two successive retained letters $u,y$ is a signed
run $a^e$, possibly empty. Set $v=-y$. Applying the shear changes that gap to
\begin{equation}
 e+z_u-z_v.
 \label{eq:gapchange}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Skeleton invariance]
After applying any integral shear and taking cyclic free reduction, the
cyclic sequence of retained non-multiplier letters is unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a first cancellation of retained letters were to occur. The two
letters would have to be inverse, so $y=-u$ and $v=u$. Their intervening
exponent in \eqref{eq:gapchange} is then exactly $e$, independently of $z$.
Since the original cyclic word is reduced, this exponent was nonzero.
Those letters cannot become adjacent and cancel, a contradiction. The same
argument applies at the cyclic seam. A word containing no retained letter
is a pure multiplier power and is fixed.
\end{proof}

The proof does not require nonnegative exponents, a common power, small rank,
or any special property of a knot presentation. It is the reason a direct
reduced-image constructor is possible rather than merely a length estimate.

\subsection{The exact objective}

Aggregate equal gap records $(u,v,e)$ over every relator, with positive integer
multiplicity $w_{uve}$. Let $C_a$ be the number of retained letters, counted
with multiplicity, plus the total lengths of all pure multiplier roots.
Then skeleton invariance gives
\begin{equation}
 L_a(z)=C_a+\sum_{(u,v,e)}w_{uve}|e+z_u-z_v|.
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Self-loops $u=v$ are allowed. Their cost is a constant $w|e|$, not a constraint
to be discarded. Empty roots contribute nothing, and a duplicate root
contributes a duplicate seam and all its multiplicities.

\begin{corollary}[Domination and idempotence]
The minimum of \eqref{eq:objective}, followed by minimization over positive
multipliers $a$, is no worse in total cyclic length than any selected-direction
power step. Once an exact $a$-shear minimum has been reached, a second
$a$-shear cannot strictly improve it.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every selected-direction power is in the feasible family. For idempotence,
the orbit of a point under the fixed-$a$ family is unchanged after applying
one member, by the additive composition law. Its minimum has already been
attained.
\end{proof}

Idempotence concerns exact local minima, not equality of returned encodings
or uniqueness of potentials. Every successful later shear phase must change
multiplier, but this fact alone does not bound how often multipliers alternate.

\section{Extracting all gaps without expanding the words}
\label{sec:histogram}

For each reachable node $U$ store four values
\[
 (f_U,\ell_U,p_U,s_U):
 \quad\text{first/last retained letter and signed prefix/suffix $a$-run.}
\]
When $U$ is a pure multiplier word, $f_U=\ell_U=0$ and
$p_U=s_U$ is its signed exponent. For a non-pure word, the prefix and suffix
runs lie outside its first and last retained letters.

A terminal supplies this information immediately. For $U=VW$,
\begin{align*}
 f_U&=f_V\text{ if }f_V\ne0,\text{ and }f_W\text{ otherwise},\\
 \ell_U&=\ell_W\text{ if }\ell_W\ne0,\text{ and }\ell_V\text{ otherwise},\\
 p_U&=p_V\text{ if }f_V\ne0,\text{ and }p_V+p_W\text{ otherwise},\\
 s_U&=s_W\text{ if }\ell_W\ne0,\text{ and }s_V+s_W\text{ otherwise}.
\end{align*}
If both children contain retained letters, this concatenation introduces the
single internal record
\[
 (\ell_V,-f_W,s_V+p_W).
\]
For $U=V^k$ with non-pure $V$, the four endpoint values are unchanged and there
are $k-1$ additional copies of the seam record
$(\ell_V,-f_V,s_V+p_V)$. For pure $V$, multiply its exponent by $k$.

Initialize a node multiplicity for each root occurrence, including duplicate
roots. Traverse reachable rules in reverse topological order. A concatenation
passes its multiplicity to both children, even when the children coincide.
A repetition passes $k$ times its multiplicity to its base and contributes
$k-1$ times its multiplicity at the seam. Finally, add one cyclic seam for each
non-pure root. Retained terminals give $C_a$ through their propagated counts;
pure roots contribute their original lengths separately.

\begin{theorem}[Compressed gap extraction]
The resulting histogram is exactly the multiset of expanded cyclic gaps.
There are at most $s+m$ distinct gap events before equal keys are combined.
Metadata, reverse multiplicities, and histogram construction require
polynomial bit time and $O(s+m)$ integer records, apart from the input.
With comparison dictionaries a bound of
$O((s+m)\log(G+2)B^2)$ bit operations is sufficient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every internal gap of an expanded parse is created either at a concatenation
of two non-pure subwords or at one boundary between copies of a repeated
non-pure subword. Such a creation belongs to a unique occurrence of a grammar
rule. Reverse multiplicities count those occurrences exactly. Gaps not
internal to a root are exactly its one cyclic seam. Pure roots have no such
gaps, and their length is included separately.

There is at most one event key per concatenation rule, one per repetition
rule, and one per root. Counts can be large, but a nonempty node's occurrences
are disjoint in the expanded parse for that node, so their number is at most
$N$; the bounds in \eqref{eq:bits} cover their binary encodings. Each metadata
or multiplicity update uses a bounded number of additions or multiplications
on $O(B)$-bit integers. Dictionary and sorting costs give the stated bound.
\end{proof}

The certificate verifier does not call this reverse propagation routine.
It independently builds a bottom-up histogram for each node: concatenate
child histograms and add one seam; multiply a repeated child's histogram by
$k$ and add $k-1$ seams. This costs at most $O(s(s+m))$ histogram records in
the straightforward implementation. The extra polynomial factor is useful
for implementation independence and does not affect the bounded-phase class.

\section{Integer tension duality and exact witnesses}
\label{sec:dual}

Write $E$ for the gap records and $w_e$ for their weights. The nonconstant
primal objective is
\[
 P=\min_{z\in\Z^V}\sum_{e=(u,v,\eta)\in E}
                 w_e|\eta+z_u-z_v|.
\]
Here $\eta$ denotes a gap's offset; the notation separates it from its record
index. A dual witness assigns an integer $f_e$ to each record, with
\begin{equation}
 |f_e|\le w_e,\qquad
 \divg f(v)=\sum_{e:\,\text{tail}=v}f_e-
            \sum_{e:\,\text{head}=v}f_e=0.
 \label{eq:dualfeasible}
\end{equation}
A self-loop contributes once with each sign and hence zero divergence.

\begin{lemma}[Weak duality with an exact certificate]
For every integer potential $z$ and every feasible $f$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_e w_e|\eta_e+z_{u_e}-z_{v_e}|
       \ \ge\ \sum_e \eta_e f_e.
 \label{eq:weakdual}
\end{equation}
If equality holds, $z$ is a global integer minimum and $f$ is a matching
optimal lower-bound witness.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Termwise, $w|t|\ge ft$ whenever $|f|\le w$. Summing and collecting potential
coefficients gives
\[
 \sum_e f_e(\eta_e+z_{u_e}-z_{v_e})
   =\sum_e \eta_e f_e+\sum_v z_v\divg f(v)
   =\sum_e \eta_e f_e.
\]
The right side is a lower bound for every feasible $z$, so equality proves
optimality.
\end{proof}

This is the complete logical check performed by the small dual verifier.
It checks integer types, exact record support, capacity bounds, divergence,
and equality of the two integer objectives. It does not trust a floating-point
solver, an approximate residual, a fingerprint equality, or a claimed optimal
cut. In particular, a nonzero optimal objective is perfectly compatible with
a zero \emph{primal--dual gap}.

\subsection{Reduction to min-cost circulation}

For each record $e=(u,v,\eta)$ introduce two directed arcs:
\[
 u\longrightarrow v\text{ with capacity }w_e\text{ and cost }-\eta,
 \qquad
 v\longrightarrow u\text{ with capacity }w_e\text{ and cost }\eta.
\]
Let their flows be $g_e^+,g_e^-$ and set $f_e=g_e^+-g_e^-$. Every feasible
circulation gives \eqref{eq:dualfeasible}. Its cost is $-\sum_e\eta f_e$.
Conversely any feasible $f$ is realized by its positive and negative parts,
so there is no relaxation gap from this transformation.

For an arc of cost $c$, use the reduced-cost convention
\[
 c^\pi(u,v)=c+\pi_u-\pi_v.
\]
Suppose a feasible circulation has potentials making all residual reduced
costs nonnegative. Put $z_v=-\pi_v$. For the forward arc associated with a gap,
its reduced cost is
$-\eta+\pi_u-\pi_v=-(\eta+z_u-z_v)$.
If the gap value is positive, that forward arc is saturated and the opposing
original arc carries no flow; hence $f_e=w_e$. If it is negative,
$f_e=-w_e$. If it is zero, every allowed $f_e$ satisfies the equality for that
term. Thus the circulation and its potential give equality in
\eqref{eq:weakdual}.

\begin{theorem}[Integral optimum and bounded potentials]
Every integer gap instance has an integer optimum and an integer dual witness.
One can choose the potential of every component so that
\[
 |z_v|\le (V-1)\max_e|\eta_e|
\]
when $V\ge1$, with the zero instance interpreted separately.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The solver in Section~\ref{sec:flow} constructs an integral feasible circulation
and residual-feasible potentials. At termination choose distances from an
auxiliary source with zero-cost arcs to every vertex. There is no negative
residual cycle. A shortest path may be taken simple, so its cost lies between
$-(V-1)\max|\eta|$ and zero. The distance inequalities give nonnegative
residual reduced costs. Their negatives are the required integral potentials,
and the preceding complementary-slackness argument supplies equality.
\end{proof}

The bound depends on the \emph{bit length} of the offsets, not a search through
all integer vectors in this coordinate box. Enumerating the box would undo
the compression advantage.

\section{A bit-polynomial circulation implementation}
\label{sec:flow}

The reference implementation is \path{shear_kernel/flow.py}. This section
proves the invariants of the actual algorithm rather than appealing to
unit-capacity augmentation. Let $A$ be the number of original directed arcs,
$V$ the number of vertices, and
\[
 \beta=\max_i\bl(U_i),\qquad
 \gamma=1+\max_i\bl(c_i),
\]
for capacities $U_i$ and costs $c_i$. Zero-arc instances return immediately.
A constant offset in these bit-length definitions is harmless.

\subsection{Capacity bits, saturation, and imbalance repair}

Initially all partial capacities and all flows are zero, and all vertex
potentials are zero. Read capacity bits from most significant to least
significant. At a new phase replace each partial capacity and flow by
\[
 U_i'\leftarrow 2U_i'+\text{next bit of }U_i,
 \qquad g_i\leftarrow 2g_i.
\]
Before the new bit, doubling preserves feasibility and the support of every
residual arc. The potential therefore still certifies their nonnegative
reduced costs.

If a newly available forward residual arc has negative reduced cost, saturate
it. The extra residual capacity is at most one: a negative reduced cost meant
that its predecessor was saturated, so only the newly read bit can open it.
Its reverse residual arc has positive reduced cost. After all such saturations
every residual reduced cost is nonnegative, but flow conservation may fail.
Let
\[
 b(v)=\text{inflow}(v)-\text{outflow}(v)
\]
be the resulting imbalance. The total positive imbalance is at most $A$.
Self-loop saturations create no imbalance.

To repair conservation, run multisource Dijkstra from all vertices with
$b(v)>0$, on nonnegative residual reduced costs. Choose a reachable deficit
vertex $t$, $b(t)<0$, of minimum distance $D$. Let $d(v)$ be its computed
distance, with infinity for unreachable vertices. Update
\begin{equation}
 \pi_v\leftarrow\pi_v+\min\{d(v),D\},
 \qquad \min\{\infty,D\}=D.
 \label{eq:truncateddistance}
\end{equation}
Augment along the shortest path to $t$ by the minimum of its residual
capacities, its source surplus, and its target deficit magnitude. Repeat
until all imbalances vanish.

\begin{lemma}[Every repair exists and reduces the imbalance]
If some positive imbalance remains, a positive vertex has a residual path to
a negative vertex. Every repair reduces the total positive imbalance by a
positive integer. Hence a capacity phase uses at most $A$ repairs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The doubled old circulation is a feasible circulation under the new capacities.
Subtract the current capacity-feasible pseudoflow from that circulation.
Positive and negative differences are residual arcs. Their divergence corrects
the current imbalance, so decomposition into paths and cycles gives a path
from some surplus to some deficit. Every path capacity and nonzero endpoint
imbalance is integral and positive. The augmentation changes only its two
endpoint imbalances and reduces their magnitudes without reversing their
signs. Thus the total positive imbalance falls by at least one.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Residual-potential invariant]
After \eqref{eq:truncateddistance}, all residual reduced costs are nonnegative
and each edge on the selected path has zero reduced cost. Augmenting along
that path preserves residual nonnegativity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\widehat d(v)=\min\{d(v),D\}$. For a residual arc with nonnegative reduced
cost $c^\pi(u,v)$, shortest-path inequalities imply
\[
 \widehat d(v)\le\widehat d(u)+c^\pi(u,v).
\]
This also holds when a distance is infinite: a reachable vertex cannot point
to an unreachable vertex along a residual arc, and the other cases follow
from truncation and nonnegativity. The new reduced cost is
$c^\pi(u,v)+\widehat d(u)-\widehat d(v)\ge0$.
Every prefix distance on the chosen shortest path is at most $D$, so those
edges have equality. Augmentation can only create reverse edges of that
path; their reduced cost is also zero.
\end{proof}

At the end of every repair the code recalibrates the potential by
Bellman--Ford from a zero-edge auxiliary source. The invariant proves there
is no negative residual cycle. Recalibration is not required for correctness,
but gives a direct bit-size bound rather than accumulating unconstrained
potential shifts across many phases.

\subsection{Correctness and bit complexity}

\begin{theorem}[Certified circulation solver]
The capacity-bit algorithm terminates after $\beta$ phases and at most
$\beta A$ repairs. It returns an integral feasible circulation and integral
residual-feasible potentials, and hence an optimum tension witness. A
conservative bit-time bound is
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left((\beta+1)(V+1)(A+1)^2
       \bigl(\beta+\gamma+\log(A+V+2)\bigr)\right),
 \label{eq:flowbound}
\end{equation}
with a polynomial space bound. In particular the iteration count is not
proportional to a capacity or an offset's numeric value.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The initialization satisfies the invariant. Doubling, negative-arc saturation,
and the preceding lemmas establish it phase by phase. At the last phase the
partial capacities equal the supplied capacities, all imbalances are zero,
and residual potentials are feasible. Section~\ref{sec:dual} then proves
optimality and constructs the integer tension solution.

Residual graph construction uses $O(A)$ operations. One Dijkstra run uses
$O((A+V)\log(V+2))$ comparisons and additions. Recalibration uses $O(VA)$
relaxations. The latter bound, with harmless degenerate-instance adjustments,
absorbs the former for a graph whose vertices are incident to input arcs.
There are at most $A$ repairs per phase and $\beta$ phases. Saturations and
capacity doubling are lower-order scans.

Capacities and flows have $O(\beta)$ bits. Recalibrated potentials have
$O(\gamma+\log(V+2))$ bits because shortest paths are simple. Dijkstra
reduced costs and distances obey the same kind of bound. The indicated
additions, comparisons, shifts, and residual updates therefore have the
bit cost in \eqref{eq:flowbound}. Computing the final primal and dual
objectives adds polynomially many integer products; in the gap application
these are absorbed by the common $B^2$ bound below.
\end{proof}

The proof also explains two tempting incorrect implementations. Repeatedly
sending one unit of flow can take exponentially many iterations on a binary
capacity. Repeatedly canceling an arbitrary negative cycle does not inherit
the repair count above. Neither is the implementation supplied here.

\begin{corollary}[Complete local shear query]
Given the repetition grammar and alive generators, one can find the best
integral shear over every multiplier, construct its reduced image, and produce
and verify witnesses for every multiplier in
\begin{equation}
 O\bigl((r+1)^2G^2B^2\bigr)
 \label{eq:globalbound}
\end{equation}
bit operations, up to harmless logarithmic factors from the chosen dictionary
and bit model. The certificate has $O((r+1)GB)$ bits. The computation is
polynomial in compressed input size even when rank is part of the input.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For one multiplier, $V\le2(r-1)$ and $A\le2(s+m)$. Gap multiplicities and
offsets have $O(B)$ bits, so substitute these bounds into
\eqref{eq:flowbound} and solve at most $r$ instances. Histogram construction,
objective products, the independent quadratic histogram verifier, and the
direct image construction of Section~\ref{sec:output} are absorbed in the
same conservative polynomial bound. Each multiplier contributes linearly
many record and vertex witnesses of $O(B)$ bits.
\end{proof}

We deliberately use a conservative bound rather than asserting that every
Python dictionary access or large integer operation costs one step. The
main asymptotic point is polynomial dependence on encoded size, not an
optimized exponent for this reference solver.
The bounds describe completed computations with resource allowances chosen
large enough; a configured work cap may instead interrupt the implementation,
which returns no recognition conclusion from such an interruption.

\section{Exact shortcuts: balanced graphs and median forests}
\label{sec:shortcuts}

\subsection{Balanced offset graphs}

A weighted gap graph is \emph{balanced} when there exist integers $z$ such that
\[
 z_v-z_u=\eta \qquad\text{for every record }(u,v,\eta).
\]
A depth-first traversal chooses a root potential zero in each component,
propagates the required differences, and checks every already visited edge.
This also checks parallel-record consistency and self-loop offsets.

\begin{proposition}[Balanced fast path]
The graph is balanced if and only if the signed sum of offsets around every
undirected closed walk is zero. In that case the primal optimum is zero,
$L_a^{\min}=C_a$, and $f=0$ is an exact dual certificate. A spanning-forest
traversal and edge checks decide this using linearly many integer operations
apart from dictionary access.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A potential telescopes around any closed walk, proving necessity. Conversely,
assign potentials by path sums along a spanning forest. Every other edge
closes a walk, so its zero offset sum makes it consistent with the assignment.
The objective is nonnegative and the assignment makes each term zero.
The all-zero dual then has the same value.
\end{proof}

Large multiplicities do not affect this test. Thus binary exponents with
hundreds of bits need not trigger hundreds of circulation phases when the
underlying offset constraints are exactly compatible.

\subsection{Forests of parallel-edge bundles}

A self-loop contributes a fixed objective $w|\eta|$ and the independent dual
value $f=w\sgn(\eta)$. Remove those loops temporarily. Group all remaining
records by their unordered endpoint pair. Suppose the resulting simple graph
is a forest. Orient each bundle from a chosen endpoint $u$ to $v$ and put
$d=z_v-z_u$. Its objective is
\[
 \sum_j w_j|d-t_j|,
\]
where $t_j=\eta_j$ for a record oriented $u\to v$, and
$t_j=-\eta_j$ for a record oriented $v\to u$.

\begin{theorem}[Forest median solution]
Choose a lower weighted median $d_{uv}$ of the $t_j$ in every bundle.
Because the simple graph is a forest, all equations
$z_v-z_u=d_{uv}$ can be satisfied simultaneously. The resulting potential is
a global integer optimum. It has a dual certificate obtained independently
inside each bundle, and can be found with $O((s+m)\log(G+2))$ comparison-map
operations on the gap data, plus integer arithmetic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each bundle's weighted absolute-deviation sum is minimized by a weighted
median. On a forest its independently optimal differences integrate to a
potential, so the sum of all separate lower bounds is attained.

For the dual, orient net flow $g_j$ in the common bundle direction. When
$t_j\ne d$, set $g_j=w_j\sgn(t_j-d)$. At $t_j=d$ choose
$g_j\in[-w_j,w_j]\cap\Z$ so that $\sum_jg_j=0$. This is possible because a
weighted median has at most half the weight strictly on either side, so the
absolute required correction is no greater than the total median weight.
Distribute the integer correction greedily within those capacities. Convert
$g_j$ back to each record's orientation. Every bundle has zero net divergence
at each endpoint, and every term satisfies complementary equality. Restore
the self-loop witnesses. Weak duality proves global optimality.
\end{proof}

Every rank-two multiplier problem has at most two port vertices, so this
shortcut solves it after self-loops are separated. Higher-rank forest instances
are also covered. A graph can fail the balanced test because two parallel
records demand incompatible offsets while still be solved immediately by
medians. Conversely, a balanced graph with cycles is handled by the first
shortcut even though it is not a forest. Only the remaining instances need
the general circulation solver.

These shortcuts change the implementation cost, not the optimization problem
or the certificate checker. The benchmark's \emph{forced-flow} arm disables
both and therefore offers a same-objective control.

\section{Direct reduced images and independent replay}
\label{sec:output}

A length optimizer alone would not settle the representation problem: generic
substitution followed by compressed free reduction might repeatedly multiply
grammar size by a polynomial factor. Skeleton invariance yields something
more specific and stronger for the present maps.

\subsection{Producer construction}

For a non-pure node $U$ build a core $M_U$ starting at its first retained
letter and ending at its last. The core contains exactly its transformed
internal gaps, but not the prefix or suffix multiplier runs. A pure node has
empty core. For a terminal different from the multiplier, the core is that
terminal.

For $U=VW$ with both children non-pure, set
\begin{equation}
 M_U=M_V\,a^{s_V+p_W+z_{\ell_V}-z_{-f_W}}\,M_W.
 \label{eq:coreconcat}
\end{equation}
If just one child is non-pure, use that child's core; if neither is, use the
empty core. For $U=V^k$ with non-pure $V$, put
\begin{equation}
 h=s_V+p_V+z_{\ell_V}-z_{-f_V},\qquad
 M_U=(M_Va^h)^{k-1}M_V.
 \label{eq:corepower}
\end{equation}
For a non-pure root $R$, output
\begin{equation}
 M_R a^{s_R+p_R+z_{\ell_R}-z_{-f_R}}.
 \label{eq:rootimage}
\end{equation}
For a pure root, output its unchanged signed multiplier power. A negative
power $a^h$ is encoded as a nonnegative repetition of $a^{-1}$.

\begin{theorem}[Direct image theorem]
Equations~\eqref{eq:coreconcat}--\eqref{eq:rootimage} give a cyclically reduced
representative of every $\Phi_{a,z}(w_i)$. They use no general word-equality,
prefix, free-reduction, or expansion query. If $G=s+m+r+2$, the fresh output
representation satisfies
\begin{equation}
 G'\le 8G.
 \label{eq:linear-growth}
\end{equation}
The construction takes polynomial bit time, and its output cyclic length is
exactly \eqref{eq:objective}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct over the grammar. Concatenation joins the two retained cores through
exactly the gap in \eqref{eq:gapchange}. A repeated non-pure core has $k-1$
internal copy seams, giving \eqref{eq:corepower}. At a root, moving its
initial multiplier run to its end produces \eqref{eq:rootimage}. Thus every
output has the correct cyclic sequence of retained letters and all the exact
transformed gap exponents.

Only opposite retained letters could cancel after a gap becomes zero.
Skeleton invariance rules this out, including at the last-to-first seam.
Hence the displayed output is already freely and cyclically reduced.
Counting retained letters and runs gives exactly \eqref{eq:objective}.

For the rule count, a terminal creates at most one terminal. A non-pure
concatenation creates at most one run-power and two concatenations, apart
from the two shared multiplier-sign terminals. A repetition creates at most
one run-power, one block concatenation, one repetition, and one final
concatenation. A root creates at most one run-power and one concatenation.
Consequently $s'\le4s+2m+2$ is a sufficient count for the fresh constructor;
the looser \eqref{eq:linear-growth} also covers the fixed root/generator
bookkeeping and empty cases. Each exponent calculation uses a bounded number
of operations on the bounded integers.
\end{proof}

The representative is not promised to be the same literal cyclic rotation as
an unrelated reduction routine would return. The theorem proves the exact
cyclic word and normal closure, not equality of all positional encodings.

\subsection{A distinct verifier recurrence}

The independent replay module \path{shear_kernel/replay.py} does not call the
producer's core constructor or endpoint summaries. It stores a decorated
reduced image
\[
 \Phi(U)=a^{P_U}\,H_U\,a^{S_U},
\]
where $H_U$ starts and ends in a retained letter; a pure node is represented
by its single signed exponent. For a retained terminal $x$,
\[
 (P,H,S)=(-z_{-x},x,z_x).
\]
For two non-pure children, the decorated concatenation is
\[
 (P_V,\ H_Va^{S_V+P_W}H_W,\ S_W).
\]
Pure children add to the appropriate outer exponent. For a non-pure repeated
child the middle is constructed in the \emph{other} association
\[
 H_V\bigl(a^{S_V+P_V}H_V\bigr)^{k-1},
\]
with outer exponents $P_V,S_V$. Finally a root becomes
$H_Ra^{S_R+P_R}$. This gives the same literal cyclic representative as the
producer's formulas, but can produce a different DAG shape.

Each recurrence follows by substitution and multiplier-run combination.
The same non-cancellation proof makes it a valid independent replay without
compressed free reduction. Its rule count is again bounded by a constant
factor. The tests compare both constructors with each other and with literal
substitution where expansion is small.

\subsection{Source binding without hash-based mathematics}

A standalone optimization certificate stores a SHA256 value of the canonical
source DAG serialization. This field detects accidental source mixups; it
is not a word-equality oracle. The verifier rebuilds the actual source's
histograms and checks every primal--dual witness exactly.

For a multi-phase trace, the first source binding is checked. Later producer
hashes refer to a DAG shape different from the independent decorated replay,
so they are advisory there. Each later certificate is instead checked against
the histograms of the \emph{actual independently replayed words}; its
potentials define the next exact automorphism image. Thus no hash collision
or unverified serialized intermediate word is used to establish the terminal
group claim.

\section{A bounded-phase complexity theorem}
\label{sec:phases}

A successful phase means: solve all multiplier queries exactly, choose a
minimum with deterministic tie-breaking, and apply it only when total cyclic
length strictly decreases. The generator and relator lists remain fixed
during the phase sequence. Any preprocessing or interleaved relator operation
must be charged separately.

\begin{lemma}[Bit lengths during monotone phases]
Let the initial total expanded length be $N_0$. During a sequence of successful
phases, all current root lengths are at most $N_0$, all reachable nonempty
rule lengths are at most $N_0$, and a chosen potential has magnitude
$O(rN_0)$. At phase $j$, all relevant integers have
$O(\sigma+j)$ bits, including growing rule indices.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Total cyclic length decreases, and all roots and reachable subwords are
literal reduced words, so none is longer than $N_0$. Gap offsets and
multiplicities are bounded by this length, up to root bookkeeping. The
bounded-potential theorem gives the factor $O(r)$.

The direct output has no hidden cancellation. Its nonempty constituent cores,
runs, and repeated blocks occur as subwords of a root, apart from at most
constant unused multiplier terminals; their encoded lengths are therefore
bounded as well. Since $\log(N_0+2)=O(\sigma)$ and
$G_j\le8^jG_0$, rule indices add at most $O(j+\log G_0)$ bits. The same
argument applies to the independent replay representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-phase search and replay]
\label{thm:phase}
For initial encoding size $\sigma$, at most $q$ successful integral-shear
phases, their complete local optimality certificates, and independent replay
can be computed in
\begin{equation}
 \poly(\sigma)\,2^{O(q)}
 \label{eq:phasebound}
\end{equation}
bit time and space. A final unsuccessful local query and a polynomial-time
terminal predicate on the compressed state obey the same bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The direct theorem gives $G_j\le8^jG_0$. The rank is unchanged, and the bit
lemma gives $B_j=O(\sigma+j)$. Substitute these into the polynomial local
bound and sum over $j\le q$:
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{q} O\!\left((r+1)^2G_0^2\,64^j(\sigma+j)^2\right)
   \le \poly(\sigma+q)\,2^{O(q)}.
\]
Polynomial factors in $q$ are absorbed into $2^{O(q)}$; when $q=0$ the bound
is simply polynomial in $\sigma$. Source serialization, all multiplier
witnesses, and the independent histogram and decorated-word replay have the
same type of polynomial dependence on $G_j$ and $B_j$. Summing their costs
and output sizes proves \eqref{eq:phasebound}.
\end{proof}

This is a representation-sensitive theorem. Merely knowing that each
individual generic reduction is polynomial would not suffice: recursively
applying an unspecified polynomial-size transformation can give a bound like
$\sigma^{d^q}$. The constant-factor direct rule construction avoids that
problem for this sequence of operations.

\begin{corollary}[Quasi-polynomial bounded-phase class]
Suppose an input with size parameter $n$ yields, with independently checked
provenance, a presentation of encoding size $\sigma\le n^{O(1)}$. If the
specified shear descent reaches a sound, complete terminal decision for that
input after $q=O(\log^2(n+2))$ successful phases, the algebraic computation and
its replay take
\[
 2^{O(\log^2(n+2))}=n^{O(\log(n+2))}
\]
bit time. With $q=O(\log(n+2))$ the same part is polynomial-time.
\end{corollary}

This corollary is a theorem about the class satisfying its explicit
hypotheses. It does not assert that arbitrary unknot diagrams have such
presentations, phase counts, or terminal states. The next section gives one
concrete terminal predicate and proves an obstruction to its universality.

\section{Terminal recognition and a genuine completeness obstruction}
\label{sec:terminal}

\subsection{Pure-power presentations}

Suppose every nonempty root is a pure signed power of one generator.
For each alive generator $x_j$ let $d_j$ be the gcd of all its nonzero
relator exponents in absolute value, or $d_j=0$ if it has no such relator.
Then
\begin{equation}
 \calP\cong \mathop{*}_{j=1}^r C_{d_j},
 \qquad C_0=\Z,\quad C_1=\{1\}.
 \label{eq:freeproduct}
\end{equation}
A bottom-up uniform-letter test recognizes the roots, and binary Euclidean
arithmetic computes the $d_j$ without expanding their powers.

\begin{proposition}[Complete pure-power terminal test]
The group in \eqref{eq:freeproduct} is infinite cyclic if and only if exactly
one $d_j$ is zero and every other $d_j$ is one. This is decidable in polynomial
bit time in the compressed state.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Relations involving only one generator present its cyclic factor; the
universal property of free products gives \eqref{eq:freeproduct}. If the
stated condition holds, the only nontrivial factor is $\Z$. With no
nontrivial factor the group is trivial. With one finite nontrivial factor
it is finite cyclic. With two or more nontrivial factors, reduced free-product
normal forms show that nonidentity elements from distinct factors do not
commute, so the group is not $\Z$. These cases exhaust the possibilities.
Uniform-letter metadata and gcd arithmetic have polynomial bit cost.
\end{proof}

For a validated classical knot in $S^3$, the full knot group is infinite
cyclic exactly when the knot is the unknot. To recall the topological input:
if the group is $\Z$, the longitude is null-homotopic. Dehn's lemma gives an
embedded compressing disk with that boundary, and an annulus in the tubular
neighborhood extends it to a spanning disk for the knot. The converse follows
from the solid-torus exterior of the unknot. This is the classical
Dehn-lemma/loop-theorem consequence used by the repository's group verifier
\cite{papa,proveit}.

Thus the phase theorem and this terminal predicate give a sound two-sided
recognition algorithm \emph{on the class of sourced knot presentations that
reach this terminal predicate within the stated phase budget}. The artifact's
standalone CLI deliberately reports only an abstract presentation result:
it does not itself prove that a user-supplied presentation is the full group
of the claimed diagram. A quotient, an omitted relator, or a mislabeled
multi-component link cannot be substituted for that provenance contract.

\subsection{Why unlimited strict shear descent is not complete}

\begin{theorem}[Automorphically minimal infinite-cyclic plateau]
\label{thm:plateau}
For every $N\ge2$, the presentation
\begin{equation}
 \calQ_N=\langle a,b\mid a^N,a^{N+1},[a,b]\rangle,
 \qquad [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1},
 \label{eq:plateau}
\end{equation}
presents $\Z$, has no generator occurring exactly once in any relator,
and is already globally minimum in total cyclic length under \emph{all}
free-group automorphisms. Its minimum is $2N+5$. It is not a pure-power
terminal state.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first two relations imply $a=1$, and the commutator then becomes redundant;
$b$ is unconstrained. This proves the group is $\Z$. The occurrence assertion
is immediate for $N\ge2$.

For any automorphism $\psi$, a nontrivial element $\psi(a)$ has positive
cyclic length. Cyclic lengths of its powers therefore give
\[
 \len{\psi(a^N)}+\len{\psi(a^{N+1})}
    =(2N+1)\len{\psi(a)}\ge2N+1.
\]
The element $\psi([a,b])$ is nontrivial and belongs to the commutator subgroup.
A nontrivial cyclically reduced word with zero abelianization has length at
least four: length one or three cannot have every exponent sum zero, and a
length-two zero-sum word cancels. Hence its cyclic length is at least four.
The initial tuple has total length $2N+5$, so it attains this universal lower
bound. Its commutator root is not a pure power.
\end{proof}

This is a completeness obstruction to \emph{strict automorphic shortening
alone}, not a lower bound for unknot recognition. It does not obstruct
relator combination, quotient deletion, or Tietze elimination. Indeed the
repository's existing relator-power mechanisms are precisely relevant to
such an example. It also is not a family of hard knot diagrams: it is a
transparent family of supplied presentations of an infinite-cyclic group.

At $N=2^{500}$ the implementation confirms the exact local minimum and returns
an inconclusive stall, not a knottedness verdict. The example shows why
proving only a phase bound would still not establish terminal completeness.
One must understand how non-automorphic presentation operations interact with
the representation and search bounds.

\section{Separating families and exact phase compression}
\label{sec:families}

\subsection{Unbounded one-step gain over a selected direction}

\begin{proposition}[A selected median can choose the wrong scale]
For $N\ge3$, consider the tuple
\[
 \calW_N=((ba^N)^2,(ca)^3),\qquad L_0=2N+8.
\]
The best unit Whitehead direction has gain five. Exact line minimization
along any such best direction uses exponent one and leaves length $2N+3$.
An integral $a$-shear reaches length five in one step. Thus the ratio of
one-step gains is $(2N+3)/5$, unbounded in expanded length.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For multiplier $a$, the gap objective is
\[
 5+2|N+z_b-z_{-b}|+3|1+z_c-z_{-c}|.
\]
A unit direction can decrease each positive gap by at most one; choosing both
differences $-1$ realizes gain $2+3=5$. A unit map with multiplier $b$ can
remove at most the two occurrences of $b$, and one with multiplier $c$ can
remove at most the three occurrences of $c$. Words without that multiplier
cannot shorten because all their corresponding initial gaps are zero.
Hence $a$, or its equivalent signed direction, is best.

Along a best direction the length is $5+2|N-k|+3|1-k|$. Its first minimum is
$k=1$, the lower weighted median. Independently setting the two differences
to $-N$ and $-1$ gives length five. Finally every automorphic image of these
two nontrivial powers has length at least two plus three, so five is even a
minimum over the full automorphism group for this tuple.
\end{proof}

The example does \emph{not} give an exponential running-time advantage over
the already batched repository method. A subsequent selected-direction power
can remove the remaining long run; this simple fixture separates one step
from two, not one step from $N$ unbatched steps. Its purpose is to establish
strictly stronger local optimization without mischaracterizing the baseline.

\subsection{Many independently weighted scales}

For $k,h\ge1$ put
\begin{equation}
 N_j=j2^h,\qquad w_j=2^{h+2(k-j+1)},\qquad
 u_j=b_ja^{N_j}\quad(1\le j\le k),
 \label{eq:starparameters}
\end{equation}
and consider
\begin{equation}
 \calS_{k,h}=\langle a,b_1,\ldots,b_k\mid
       u_j^{w_j},u_j^{w_j+1}\ (1\le j\le k)\rangle.
 \label{eq:star}
\end{equation}
There are only $O(k)$ repetition rules, with encoding size
$O(k(h+k+\log(k+2)))$. The combined weight at the $j$th scale is
$v_j=2w_j+1$, and these weights satisfy
\[
 v_j>\sum_{\ell>j}v_\ell.
\]

\begin{theorem}[One phase versus $k$ selected-line phases]
The group \eqref{eq:star} is infinite cyclic. Joint integral-shear optimization
reaches a pure-power terminal state in one phase, at the globally smallest
possible total cyclic length $\sum_jv_j$. A greedy best-unit direction
followed by an eager first line minimum takes exactly $k$ positive phases,
with multiplier $a$ preferred in ties.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Setting $z_{b_j}-z_{-b_j}=-N_j$ maps every $u_j$ to the cyclic word $b_j$.
The pair of resulting exponents $w_j,w_j+1$ has gcd one, so all $b_j$ are
killed and $a$ is free. Each original nontrivial power has every automorphic
cyclic image at least as long as its exponent; consequently
$\sum_j(2w_j+1)$ is also the global automorphic lower bound, attained here.

At an intermediate selected-line state, each unfinished scale has a positive
$a$-run, whereas finished scales have zero run. The best unit $a$-direction
reduces every unfinished gap by one and changes no finished gap, gaining the
sum of the unfinished $v_j$. A unit direction with any other multiplier
$b_j$ can remove at most its own $v_j$ occurrences; all other roots have no
$b_j$ and cannot shorten. If its scale is finished, its own roots are pure
$b_j$ powers and are fixed. Thus $a$ is a best unit multiplier, strictly so
when at least two scales remain.

Along the selected direction, the least unfinished run has weight greater
than all later weights combined. Its length is the unique weighted-median
scale. The first line minimum therefore removes exactly this least run and
subtracts the same amount from all later runs. Repeating removes the scales
in increasing order, one per phase. There are exactly $k$ of them.
\end{proof}

This is an exact, rank-dependent phase compression, not a hard-diagram
construction. It provides a useful capacity and scaling test for the kernel
and explains why the synthetic benchmark can show larger gains than the
ordinary braid-stage corpus. The theorem also holds for many other increasing
run sequences and superincreasing combined weights.

\subsection{Braid-derived stages, and why the negative result matters}

For a braid generator $\sigma_i$, the independent Artin convention sends
\[
 (x_i,x_{i+1})\longmapsto
 (x_ix_{i+1}x_i^{-1},x_i),
\]
with its explicitly implemented inverse. Composing the braid word gives
closure relators $\beta(x_i)x_i^{-1}$. The induced permutation checks that
the closure has one component. Inverse-pair and braid-relation tests check
the convention. This constructs a separate source corpus; it does not call
the production PD-to-Wirtinger constructor.

The audit retains 180 connected closures on two through five strands with
one through twelve input crossings, obtained in 627 generation attempts.
For each it checks the raw presentation and the presentation after exact
singleton Tietze elimination. Both tuples, all elimination records, and
length results are retained. These two stages of the same input are not
statistically independent samples.

A stronger joint query is never worse in resulting length, as the theorem
predicts. It is strictly better on 21 raw stages, but on \emph{zero}
post-singleton-elimination stages. For example, the four-braid
$(-3,1,3,2,3)$ has raw length $12$; a selected-line step leaves $10$, whereas
a joint step leaves $6$. The singleton prelude already eliminates all
relators on this example. Reporting its raw gain as a production recognition
speedup would therefore be misleading.

\subsection{Paired kernel timings}

\path{experiments/benchmark.py} uses one excluded warm-up for each arm,
then five measured rounds with shuffled arm order for each case. Inputs are
preconstructed and immutable; output construction is timed. Joint arms
include the independent optimality witness check. The selected-line arm
independently checks its output-length formula, but does not produce a

No production file should be replaced automatically on the evidence here.

\subsection{Legacy and atomic integration are different projects}

The bridge supplies \code{prepare\_step}, which imports the arena's current
roots and prepares a checked joint solution without replacing the source-root
list. It can translate the solution into the existing
\code{whitehead}/\code{whitehead\_power} records using singleton factors.

For legacy certificates, \code{replay\_in\_arena} applies those factors using
the host's own substitution and cyclic reduction. This preserves the host's
representative convention. Its returned roots and move records must be
committed together only after complete success and independent full-source
replay. This route does not inherit the direct rule-growth bound, and its
intermediate steps can increase length.

In contrast, \code{install\_prepared} imports the direct core output. It is
reserved for a \emph{new atomic-shear protocol}. Installing these roots while
recording only legacy positional moves can make a later overlap certificate
refer to the wrong positions. A new protocol must specify the cyclic root
convention, signed potential map, source identities, replay recurrence,
resource handling, and schema version explicitly.

The source-pinned gateway checks blob hashes, imports the real WordArena,
compares legacy replay with the joint result on small inputs, and checks the
existing power-profile formula. It is supplied for maintainer execution and
is explicitly marked \textbf{not executed} in this delivery. Passing it would
still not be an end-to-end integration benchmark.

\subsection{Resources, failure, and a conservative scheduler}

The circulation \code{Budget} counts cooperative flow work. It is not a
whole-operation wall-time or memory limit. Histogram extraction, structured
shortcuts, serialization, large integer operations, and host allocations
need a shared outer deadline and allocation accounting before service use.
Arena allocations made before a failed tentative step are not rolled back;
a scratch arena or explicit accounting of abandoned nodes is required.

A budget failure, a non-pure terminal, or a shear stall is inconclusive.
The existing complete exact backend must remain available. The current
small-corpus evidence supports an opt-in probe for highly compressed or
multi-scale states, not a new default. Even a perfect local optimizer can
choose a different long-term path than a less aggressive heuristic.

A production comparison should measure original PD construction, unchanged
filters, search, independent source replay, resource exhaustion, and the final
verdict in paired shuffled runs. It should retain no-change controls and
negative results. A smaller algebraic state or a better local length objective
is not by itself a completed recognition speedup.

\section{Further research questions and concrete next targets}
\label{sec:future}

\subsection{Control changes of multiplier, not only powers}

The fixed-multiplier orbit is solved exactly and is idempotent. The next
structural question is whether a natural class of knot-derived presentations
admits a bound on the number of successful \emph{changes} of multiplier.
A useful target is $O(\log^2 n)$ phases with a polynomial-size certified
prelude. An initial theorem could restrict rank, a graph decomposition, or a
geometric presentation parameter. Any claimed bound must permit arbitrarily
large encoded gap exponents rather than reducing to a bound in expanded
length.

A concrete experimental target is to retain the whole sequence of optimizing
multipliers, dual circulations, state sizes, and length gains on adversarial
rather than only random presentations. Seek either a monotone potential
controlling changes of multiplier or an explicit long alternating family.
The mere fact that each step shortens length is not enough.

\subsection{Compress the genuinely frustrated part of the gap graph}

Balanced graphs and forests are solved without general flow. In a general
graph, bridges connect otherwise independent blocks and should be removable
by compatible one-variable optimizations. Can one formulate a certified
block decomposition whose expensive part depends only on the size of the
cyclic, offset-inconsistent core? A useful theorem would bound the cost by a
small frustration parameter plus near-linear processing of the remaining
records, still with binary capacities.

A further target is incremental optimization: after a local relator operation,
reuse an existing dual circulation and repair only the changed gap records.
The reused witness is valuable because exact primal--dual equality can certify
success without trusting the incremental search procedure.

\subsection{Interleave relator moves without losing the phase theorem}

The plateau family proves that automorphisms alone are insufficient for the
pure-power terminal strategy. The repository already has useful compressed
relator-power and overlap operations. The key next theorem is not merely
soundness of one more move; it is a combined representation bound for a
sequence mixing shear phases, generator elimination, quotient deletion, and
partial relator replacement.

One precise goal is an additive or constant-factor grammar bound for a
certified round consisting of one shear phase and a maximal batch of a
restricted relator operation. A phase potential must then count both rounds
and their effect on rank and root identities. An unrestricted appeal to a
polynomial compressed reduction primitive does not establish the desired
end-to-end bound.

\subsection{Broaden terminal states with explicit verification}

Pure powers are a deliberately transparent terminal class. A more useful
terminal could recognize signed generator identifications, free-product
splittings, or a bounded number of residual mixed relators after gcd
elimination. Each extension should return its own replayable proof that the
full presented group is, or is not, infinite cyclic.

On sourced knot inputs, topology may rule out some abstract free-product
possibilities. Such facts should sharpen a verified decision rule rather than
justify silently deleting relations or inferring a knot verdict from an
unsourced group. A complete diagram-to-presentation chain remains essential.

\subsection{Find geometric instances where the local advantage survives}

All observed raw-stage gains in the current small braid corpus are preempted
by singleton elimination. The next meaningful corpus should therefore
condition on surviving the existing cheap stages, include larger source
braids and difficult PD diagrams, and retain the source construction and all
censored runs. This is a concrete test of relevance, not an invitation to
inflate arbitrary group presentations and call them difficult knots.

The synthetic families establish encoded capacity and phase compression. A
separate mathematical target is an explicit diagram family for which the
maintained preprocessing produces a comparable multi-scale tension structure
with polynomial overhead. Such a family would connect the local theorem to
an actual recognition-performance class.

\subsection{A proof-carrying atomic protocol and formalization}

The direct constructor is where the strongest representation theorem lives.
Formalize its signed-letter, seam, repetition, and normal-closure semantics,
then specify an atomic certificate version that does not confuse cyclic
representatives with positional records. The independent decorated recurrence
provides a small, tractable formalization target.

The arithmetic certificate is another suitable target: formalize the identity
\[
 \sum_e f_e(z_{u_e}-z_{v_e})=\sum_vz_v\divg f(v)
\]
and deduce integer optimality from primal--dual equality. This verification
lemma is considerably smaller than formalizing the optimizer. Afterwards,
formalizing the scaling invariant and bounded-phase recurrence would connect
executable witnesses to a machine-checked cost argument.

\section{Conclusion}

This work replaces a selected powered Whitehead direction with an exact
optimization over a whole commuting family of integral multiplier shears.
The important local fact is that non-multiplier letters never cancel; the
important algorithmic fact is that the resulting integer tension has an exact
circulation witness; the important representation fact is that the reduced
output is constructed with constant-factor rule growth.

Together these give an unconditional polynomial compressed local query and
a $\poly(\sigma)2^{O(q)}$ theorem for bounded numbers of successful phases.
They do not supply the missing geometric phase bound or a complete global
presentation strategy. The explicit plateau and the negative post-preprocessing
benchmark result make those boundaries concrete. The delivered code, proofs,
independent checks, and integration safeguards provide a basis for the next
step without upgrading a local improvement into an unsupported general
quasi-polynomial claim.

\section{Certificate checks and failure semantics}

An optimization certificate contains the alive generator list, initial and
optimal total lengths, selected multiplier, a source binding, and one solution
record per positive multiplier. Every solution record contains integer
potentials and signed bounded flows on the reconstructed gap records.
Omitting a multiplier prevents certification of the all-multiplier minimum.
Duplicate witness entries, non-integer objective data, invalid supports,
capacity violations, nonzero divergence, and nonzero primal--dual gap are
rejected. Solver diagnostics such as repair counts are not mathematical proof
premises.

The standalone verifier reconstructs the source histogram independently and
checks all witnesses. A trace verifier then uses the separate decorated-word
recurrence for each selected automorphism, preserving generator and relator
identities. A terminal positive or negative group result is accepted only
when recomputation of the terminal predicate agrees. An inconclusive record
certifies no knot verdict, regardless of whether its diagnostic reason was
a stall or a phase cap.

This is research code, not a hardened remote certificate service. External
callers must enforce whole-input, integer-size, elapsed-time, and allocation
budgets. Those requirements are implementation-safety obligations distinct
from the asymptotic theorem for valid encoded inputs.
