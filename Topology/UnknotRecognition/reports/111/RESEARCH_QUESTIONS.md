# Further research questions

The article contains the full motivation, precise hypotheses and proposed milestones. The following list identifies the intended experiments and proof obligations.

1. **Resolve the two remaining disk-base word stalls.** Retain source-bound presentations, introduce verified basis changes, and demand either a new handlebody certificate or an exact repeatable obstruction.
2. **Strengthen the bounded algebraic policy.** Handle automorphic Fibonacci presentations through certified Nielsen changes, relator multiplication or controlled temporary growth, with discovery and intermediate bit-size bounds.
3. **Export the early homology obstruction.** Turn a verified prefix with `z > h3` into a compact negative certificate for the chosen whole-cut terminal predicate, preserving inconclusive knot semantics.
4. **Share source work across the portfolio.** Reuse authenticated geometry and ordered boundaries and unify the work allowance without changing legacy outputs or cancellation behaviour.
5. **Reduce exact substitution cost.** Investigate occurrence indexes, lazy substitutions and compressed words while preserving the selected minimum-length move and independently replayable traces.
6. **Compile nondisk parallelity bases.** Retain genuine base-boundary words and cyclic edge groups; establish a bit-size bound that includes encoded genus and active ports.
7. **Bound the residual terminal kernel geometrically.** After permitted source-certified normalizations, prove or refute a logarithmic residual-size bound on a meaningful hierarchy class.
8. **Recover source-coordinate meridian disks.** Start with the figure-eight genus-two component; produce embedded disks with exact carriers and controlled compressed size.
9. **Certify essential boundary patterns at terminals.** Retain cyclic order, side information and peripheral data, and independently rule out the violating disks relevant to the hierarchy.
10. **Export geometric basis-obstruction families.** Realize arbitrary algebraic examples through actual native face pairings and admissible normal coordinates, or identify geometric constraints that exclude them.
11. **Find an amortized geometric branch potential.** Pay for `log b` at each genuine branching node through a squared-log mass drop, while allowing bounded deterministic repairs.
12. **Construct a small marked interface quotient.** Prove exact continuation equivalence and source lifting; actively seek equal-signature states with different valid continuations.
13. **Bound complete candidate-surface generation.** Specify a family covering all successful hierarchies and charge unsuccessful search, coordinate generation and admissibility checks.
14. **Control marking and proof growth.** Bound the entire successor encoding in the original diagram size, including endpoints, boundary words, patterns and transport certificates.
15. **Integrate at diagram level.** Record diagram-to-exterior provenance, all candidate costs, retries and final proof replay; measure actual added knot decisions and total latency.
16. **Formalize the smallest trusted interface.** Begin with the finite graph-of-spaces presentation, signed Tietze trace and Euler ledger, then connect them to explicit topological source assumptions.

## Proposed sequence

First integrate the eleven-file opt-in patch with permanent regressions for the three new current-default successes and five exact Euler defects. Next address the signed residual policy and duplicated source work, while developing marked meridian data and the nondisk compiler in parallel. The decisive theorem is a complete marked balanced-cut mechanism or a complete small marked-state quotient, together with polynomial encoding and proof-lifting bounds.

The current singleton policy is provably incomplete. A short positive trace is useful evidence; neither a cached refusal nor a large finite corpus is a completeness theorem. The exact terminal cache omits isolated balls and unused free factors, but cannot serve as a marked hierarchy-state key.
