\begin{abstract}
The maintained ProveIt unknot-recognition project already contains exact
weighted interval-orbit computations, sparse component incidence, and
componentwise normal-surface statistics. We develop a different continuation:
compile a supplied interval relation once into the multiplicities of its
source-atom profiles, then reuse this census for additive observations and
monotone full-port identifications. A capacity-aware scalar encoding recovers
the complete profile census through one weighted orbit call. A
multiplicity-aware incidence graph computes subsequent quotients without
expanding components, and its incremental implementation scans each support
edge at most once during a monotone phase.

The central structural result is a sharp height bound: on $m$ fixed nonempty
source atoms, at most $2m-1$ full-port coning operations can change the
component partition. These operations form a commutative idempotent action.
Consequently a straight-line program containing binary repetitions admits
compressed final-state and prefix evaluation, a first-threshold search with
at most $g+1$ state trials for $g$ grammar nodes, and enumeration of every
count-changing step in $O((h+1)g)$ trials, where $h\le 2m-1$. Large step indices
remain binary integers; their bit costs are included.

We also prove an exact observational-equivalence characterization and give
a four-point obstruction to extending the cache to arbitrary pointwise
attachments. The package includes independent graph and grammar replay,
25 passing test methods, 9,338 exhaustive finite coning cases, randomized
checks, and controlled compressed-program measurements. Native weighted-AHT
integration is supplied as an adapter and an explicit unrun gate, not claimed
as an executed production benchmark. These are local algorithmic results;
no general quasi-polynomial unknot-recognition bound is established.
\end{abstract}
