\section{Further research questions and proposed next experiments}
\label{sec:research}

The most useful follow-up is not to enlarge the benchmark exponents. It is
to prove that a concrete geometric consumer uses the fixed-source interface
and that the number of recompilations is controlled. The following questions
separate those obligations from local engineering work.

\subsection{A native source-to-observer contract}

For one maintained normal-surface consumer, identify the exact source points,
atom cuts, orbit relation, and ownership charges. Prove that every point
belongs to the surface component represented by its orbit and that every
requested weight is constant on the chosen atoms. Then compare a compiled
profile query against the existing direct weighted query, including source
validation and independent replay. The success criterion is an actual
source-bound equality theorem and measured reuse benefit, not agreement
only on a table constructed directly by the benchmark.

\subsection{Which geometric operations are genuine full-port cones?}

Find a hierarchy subroutine whose aggregate connectivity question really
identifies all points in specified port unions. Coning is already a useful
auxiliary counting operation, but actual cutting and gluing can preserve
pointwise correspondence instead. A precise lemma should state the consumer
observation being computed, prove that the coned relation has exactly that
meaning, and list all geometric information discarded. A single verified
consumer is more valuable than a general assertion that ``attachments are
unions''.

\subsection{A refinement budget}

Let a phase introduce new port endpoints. Can those endpoints be charged
to a bounded combinatorial resource, so that the total number of distinct
atoms and the number of recompilations remain polynomial or
quasi-polynomial? The local height proof immediately gives an effective
step budget after the last refinement; it gives no such bound beforehand.
A useful theorem would bound
$\sum_i m_i$ or a sharper persistent-refinement measure across all phases,
not merely the largest $m_i$ encountered.

\subsection{A richer interface for pointwise attachments}

The four-point obstruction identifies missing correlations between point
positions inside atoms. What is the weakest efficiently maintainable
augmentation that resolves it for interval isometries? Candidates include
ordered occurrence maps, compressed permutations, or a profile table
augmented with a finite transport action. A proposed augmentation should be
tested first on the four-point pair and then on its large repeated analogues.
Its description length and composition cost must be bounded independently
of expanded multiplicities.

\subsection{Program languages with changing labels}

The present power rule is sound only for repeated identical cone actions.
A natural extension is a grammar node that applies a fixed permutation of
atom names between copies. For a supplied finite permutation, one can ask
whether its orbit decomposition allows the join of all conjugated cone
summaries to be computed by binary powering. The challenge is to preserve
exact prefix positions and first-event guarantees when the action is no
longer idempotent after one copy. This is a finite algebra question with a
clear transition from the current theorem, not an assumed property of
geometric monodromy.

\subsection{Packing versus direct vector replay}

Measure both source compilation modes and direct vector weights on actual
native orbit traces. Vary the number of atoms, endpoint bits, support
density, and capacity imbalance. Include workloads with no reuse, several
low-dimensional queries, and many correlated queries. A possible adaptive
policy would use measured arithmetic work to choose between direct replay,
trace reuse, and full profile compilation, but it should never substitute
a heuristic hash for the exact packing proof. Negative results are useful:
large packed integers can be the wrong representation on some traces.

\subsection{Sparse profile storage and selected observations}

The current reference stores every coordinate in every profile row. Can a
sparse row representation reduce $sm$ work while preserving cheap equality,
source validation, and quotient aggregation? Conversely, can a requested
family of observations be compiled into a smaller exact sufficient statistic
than the full atom profile? \Cref{thm:observational} rules out lossless
compression for \emph{all} observations beyond equivalence-preserving
encoding, but a restricted observation family may have a much coarser
complete quotient. Its closure under the chosen cone action must be proved,
not presumed from marginal counts.

\subsection{Orientation and additional geometric charges}

A fixed signed cover can be compiled separately, but an attachment can
introduce new parity constraints. Determine an explicit attachment contract
under which cover profiles can be updated without reconstructing the signed
relation. Likewise, for Euler or boundary data after a topological move,
identify the added and removed cell charges and prove their ownership.
The current old-point quotient sum is not a substitute for this topological
calculation.

\subsection{Formalization and adversarial replay}

The finite proofs here are suitable for formalization: mixed-radix decoding,
color-preserving orbit equivalence, the quotient graph theorem, the potential
$2a-b$, and the prefix/plateau certificate lemmas. A useful formal target
would isolate an abstract finite equivalence-relation interface from the
encoded AHT layer. On the software side, further fuzzing should target huge
serialized inputs, aliases between result wrappers and certificates, and
cancellation during native proof construction. Resource exhaustion must
remain inconclusive, not become a false topology verdict.

\subsection{The global amortization problem}

Finally, establish a replacement for the unbounded terms in
\eqref{eq:global-ledger}. One promising shape is a decomposition into
quasi-polynomially many source-stable phases, each with a succinct operation
grammar and a certified interpretation. The necessary theorem must jointly
bound phase count, atom refinement, new geometric descriptions and normal
vector search. The present $2m-1$ bound would then remove one repeated local
cost automatically. Until those structural bounds are available, it should
be used as a proved component of the programme rather than a label for the
whole recognizer.

\section{Conclusion}

A source-atom profile census is more than a collection of marginal counts:
it is the exact invariant of an equivalence relation under arbitrary
atom-preserving point permutations. That makes it a complete reusable
observer for additive atom-constant weights and full-port cones. The
multiplicity-aware graph action preserves compressed size, and the active
subpartition potential limits a fixed-atom phase to $2m-1$ effective
component changes. This finite-height structure supports exact traversal of
binary-repeated programs without traversing their expanded instructions.

The resulting improvement is a rigorous local acceleration with executable
reference code, independent replay, and a sharp structural bound. The
article also identifies where the abstraction fails and what must be proved
before it can accelerate a complete unknot-recognition hierarchy. This
combination---an exact improvement and an explicit boundary of validity---is
the intended contribution to the repository.
