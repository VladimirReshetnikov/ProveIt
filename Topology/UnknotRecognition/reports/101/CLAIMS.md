# Claim and evidence ledger

## Proved in the article

- Exact symbolic validation for coherent affine polytopal atlases in dimension
  at most three, with polynomial bit cost under the stated encoding.
- Occurrence-preserving flag construction, regularity after one subdivision,
  abstract simplicial structure after two, and source/marked-subcomplex replay.
  The underlying topological subdivision facts are classical and cited.
- Linear family/chart output for bounded local prototypes; no rank endpoint or
  offset growth caused by the compiler.
- Canonical native normal-cut specialization: at most 4,607 T regular cell
  families and O(T(B + log(T+1))) encoded bits, with a constructive local export
  proof. The unrestricted native exporter is not implemented in this package.
- Exponential atom-refinement barrier under the explicitly stated requirement
  that both endpoint maps bijectively map partition atoms to atoms.
- Repeated-subdivision accounting, marked carrier transport, independent
  flag-count replay, and the controlled convex-cone Morse bridge.
- Conditional global complexity transfer with every geometric and hierarchy
  assumption stated. No unrestricted quasi-polynomial recognition theorem.

These are research-draft proofs, not peer-reviewed results or proof-assistant
certificates. Literature-wide novelty and priority are not certified.

## Implemented and executed

Generic affine atlas compiler and independent replay; exact convex prototypes;
finite tetrahedral source importer; capped independent literal oracles; actual
bridge to the preceding Morse arithmetic code; source-bound demos; 32 passing
unit tests; the retained audit's 448 counted symbolic replays; paired local
construction/checking/serialization benchmarks with all completed raw trials.
The 16,385-bit binary cases do not enumerate their represented cells.

The source-level validation includes the exact one-tetrahedron zero-cut fixture
from the pinned repository, all its 24 relabellings, and the stated small
face-pairing enumeration. It is not an invocation of the native constructor.
