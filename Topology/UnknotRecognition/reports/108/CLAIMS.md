1. Ordered nonlinear-event forest selection has parameter factor
   `3^k k! Bell(2k)` for triple-unit columns.
2. A distinguished undemanded block removes the order search and gives
   `3^k Bell(2k+1) poly(N)`.
3. Free closure may be removed from premises while preserving original graph
   edges for completion; the nonlinear parameter is measured afterwards.
4. Arbitrary exceptional supports give
   `product(unit targets) Bell(total premises+1) poly(N)`.
5. The reduction extends to an external matroid with polynomial independence
   testing; it never assumes that the activation closure itself is matroidal.
6. Signed binary edge weights admit exact minimum-weight successful-tree
   optimization by weighted matroid intersection and dummy completion.
7. Full uncapped endpoint parity makes every activation preserve graphic span;
   a successful independent seed is a basis. The output algorithm needs only
   `Stirling(q+1,r-p+1)` distinguished partitions per output assignment. A
   uniform deficit bound `r-p <= Delta` sharpens the factor to
   `3^k (Delta+1)^(2k)`, single-exponential for fixed Delta.
8. A separately certified matroid-closure regime reduces to ordinary matroid
   intersection. This is a corollary of established matroid theory.
9. A K4 triangle-subsystem example satisfies parity but violates closure exchange.
10. A polynomial amount of incumbent work followed by the exact FPT fallback
    retains the parameterized bound.
11. Uniform polylogarithmic premise budget, polynomial encoded input size, and
    quasi-polynomial total hierarchy calls/other work imply a quasi-polynomial
    global cost. This conditional accounting result does not prove its premises.

## Experimental findings

All counts and timings in the article are taken from the distributed results.
They concern supplied finite graph/matrix operations. Correlated native
variants and relabelings are not distinct knot-type samples. The new methods
do not increase the maintained 92/106 full-boundary success count. All capped
outcomes are retained. The existing stopping search is faster on most small
inputs, and the new output strategy still caps on the largest stellar input
that the incumbent solves quickly.
