## Conditional recognition theorem

Correct positive-Euler/crushing recognition becomes quasi-polynomial when source
and geometric operations, decomposition production (including failed attempts),
and the sum of local query budgets meet the displayed bounds. The global
existence of such decompositions for all required knot queries is not proved.

## Implemented and executed

The package includes exact source compilation and independent source replay,
local ray construction, scalar decisions, positive witness reconstruction,
independent positive-support negative replay, coordinate-face reuse, a generic
minimal-support fallback, source fixtures, and reproducible experiments.

The frozen evidence records 27 unit test methods, 768 original plus 768
recompiled random-model queries, 1,536 packet replays, 455 source-extremality
checks, two actual matching fixtures, paired algebraic timings, paired complete
source-kernel timings, and larger binary-arithmetic scaling cases.

## Not claimed

- An unrestricted quasi-polynomial unknot recognizer or a proof completing
  Lackenby's announced complexity target.
- A universal small scalar-cut width theorem or a supplied-tree discovery
  algorithm with the desired cost.
- A new invention of matroid 2-sums, LP branching, strict complementarity,
  dynamic programming, or active-set ray enumeration.
- Full historical priority or an exhaustive review of the latest incoming ZIPs.
- A native ProveIt production speedup, native suite execution, or a knot-corpus
  performance distribution.
- A standalone validator of arbitrary knot-exterior triangulations, a diagram
  provenance constructor, essential-boundary test, or crushing implementation.
- Formal verification in Lean or complete arithmetic implementation independence.

These limitations are not failures of the stated conic theorems. They are the
separate obligations needed to use those theorems as an end-to-end recognizer.
