\begin{abstract}
The maintained ProveIt recognizer supports exact primitive-pair quotients and
unit-coordinate forest contractions on compressed group presentations. Its
current analysis bounds consecutive raw phases by recurrences exponential in
the phase count. We replace that accounting by a global invariant of a frozen
source presentation. Selected original exponent-sum rows span the complete
annihilator of the cumulative monomial quotient; each quotient column is a
primitive integer kernel vector with disjoint support. Cofactors and Hadamard's
inequality then bound every cumulative exponent by
$(t-1)^{(t-1)/2}L^{t-1}$ on a block of $t\ge2$ original generators;
singleton columns have unit entries. All exponent bit
lengths are consequently $O(r(B+\log(r+1)))$, independently of raw phase depth.
A reachable-versus-allocated circuit analysis shows that even the existing raw
updater has polynomial total cost throughout such an epoch.

We also implement a source-anchored kernel which evaluates signed counts and
primitive-width profiles on one immutable source circuit, rather than repeatedly
substituting into historical circuits. It includes independent source-bound
replay, bounded certificate parsing, an adapter seam, 38 passing tests, and
reproducible exact audits. Completed audits include 26,037 raw-word comparisons
and a 64,513-bit cumulative exponent. Paired measurements isolate maintenance
of supplied schedules; eager materialization is a substantial regression.
The results remove a raw-depth obligation, not the need to expose useful
relations or control representation resets. They do not establish general
quasi-polynomial unknot recognition, and no complete native recognizer
integration or difficult-knot speedup is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research objective and contribution boundary}

The objective is to advance the actual complexity analysis and implementation
of the ProveIt unknot-recognition program, rather than replace it with a
fast but incomplete invariant test. The particular bottleneck addressed here
is the repeated manipulation of compressed group relators after verified
primitive-factor quotients. The repository already has these algebraic moves,
source-bound replay, and an optional producer; the new contribution is a
stronger global size argument and a representation that exploits it.

The preceding maintained analyses, in
\path{synthesis/primitive_projection.tex} and
\path{synthesis/primitive_forest.tex}, are careful to separate raw phases from
normalization and other search operations \cite{repo-projection,repo-forest}.
Their bounds are valid upper bounds, but they need not be sharp. In particular,
an inequality of the form $B_{j+1}\le 2B_j+O(1)$ does not show that a valid
execution can double its exponent bit length at every step. The successive
exponents are constrained by a single initial matrix, and that constraint
is absent from the local recurrence.

\subsection{What is strengthened}

The main theorem proves a polynomial bit-size bound for an entire
\emph{frozen-source raw epoch}. The permitted operations are consecutive
primitive-pair contractions, including disjoint batches and the leaf-first
linearization of unit-coordinate forests. Donors are always the current
images of fixed original relator slots; no arbitrary relator replacement is
allowed inside the epoch. Rank falls at every contraction, but no logarithmic
or polylogarithmic bound on the number of phases is needed.

There are two distinct consequences. First, a sharper analysis applies to the
existing raw circuit updater: current reachable nodes need not double when
historical allocated nodes do. Second, a new source-anchored representation
avoids much of the remaining historical rebuilding. The first is a theorem
about a maintained mechanism. The second is implemented here as an isolated
research kernel, not silently installed into the full recognizer.

\subsection{Relationship to the general target}

A quasi-polynomial unknot-recognition algorithm was announced in 2021,
following Lackenby's UC Davis lecture \cite{lackenby-talk}. His July 2026 manuscript develops
hierarchies and an unknot algorithm; Section 9 does not give running-time
bounds for all of its operations, and the discussion still requires control
of hierarchy parameters \cite{lackenby-2026}. The maintained ProveIt program
likewise does not claim an implemented general quasi-polynomial bound
\cite{repo-root}. This note addresses a specific algebraic component of that
implementation program, not a replacement proof of the announced geometric
algorithm.

Compressed power-word algorithms provide a broader context for manipulating
very large powers without expansion \cite{lohrey-weiss}. We do not import an
unproved cost bound for normalization from that literature. The raw kernel
uses integer arithmetic, fixed-source circuit scans, and exact prefix extrema;
it needs no compressed word-equality oracle. Primitive words in rank two have
classical structure \cite{osborne-zieschang}; the repository's coherent-width
criterion is reused and proved in the form needed here.

\section{The frozen-source epoch model}
\label{sec:model}

Let $F(X)$ be the free group on $X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_r\}$, where $r\ge1$.
Actual implementation labels may be any distinct positive integers. An epoch
starts with a fixed presentation
\[
 G=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\mid R_1,\ldots,R_m\rangle.
\]
Each $R_j$ is supplied by a binary straight-line program (SLP): an acyclic
circuit whose leaves are signed generators and whose internal gates
concatenate their two inputs. Gate zero represents the empty word. A root
may be repeated, and slots may initially be empty. An SLP represents a
\emph{literal word}, not its freely reduced equivalence class.

Write $M$ for the number of frozen source gates, including zero, and
\[
 L=\max\{1,|R_1|,\ldots,|R_m|\}.
\]
For processing bounds let $B\ge1$ dominate the bit length of the length of
\emph{every scanned source gate}. If the source is trimmed to the union of
its root-reachable gates, one may take
$B=\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil$. The supplied snapshot operation performs this
trimming. Arbitrary serialized sources may contain unused gates; then their
length metadata must also be charged. Since a binary circuit with $M$ gates
has represented-length bit size $O(M)$, this qualification does not destroy
polynomial input-bit complexity.

\begin{definition}[Raw epoch]
A raw epoch retains the original relator slots and modifies them only by a
cumulative homomorphism induced by the permitted primitive contractions.
A slot used as a donor is marked deleted and represented by the empty root.
Every other slot remains the current literal image of its original root.
There is no intervening arbitrary basis change, nonmonomial substitution,
relator recombination, normalization, or source reset.
\end{definition}

This definition is intentionally narrow. At a normalization or exposure
boundary, the producer may freeze a new source and begin a new epoch, but
its construction and size must be charged separately. Treating a changed
source as though it were still the old one would invalidate the matrix
argument below.

\subsection{A permitted primitive-pair contraction}

Suppose a current relator is $w^e$, where $e\ge1$, $w$ is primitive in
$F(a,b)$, and its signed exponent-sum vector is $(u,v)$ with $uv\ne0$.
Primitivity implies $\gcd(|u|,|v|)=1$. We use the convention $a<b$ and
reuse the name $a$ for a \emph{new} quotient generator:
\begin{equation}
 a\longmapsto a^{|v|},\qquad
 b\longmapsto a^{-u\sgn(v)}.
 \label{eq:localmap}
\end{equation}
The right-hand occurrence of $a$ is not recursively substituted into itself.
All other live generators are unchanged.

\begin{lemma}[The local quotient]
\label{lem:quotient}
The quotient $F(a,b)/\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle$ is infinite cyclic,
and \eqref{eq:localmap} realizes it. Substitution into every retained relator
therefore computes the quotient of the complete presentation by $w=1$.
If $e=1$, this preserves the presented group. If $e>1$, it preserves the
presented group whenever that group is torsion-free.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $w$ belongs to a free basis of $F(a,b)$, killing it leaves a free group
of rank one. The character in \eqref{eq:localmap} annihilates $w$ and is onto,
because its two image exponents are coprime. It induces a surjection between
two infinite cyclic groups and hence an isomorphism. For $e>1$, the already
imposed relation $w^e=1$ implies $w=1$ in a torsion-free group. In both cases
adding $w=1$ changes no group element's equality class; rewriting the quotient
free group and every remaining relator is a presentation transformation.
\end{proof}

For a validated classical knot-group source, torsion-freeness is the same
source-derived premise used by the maintained primitive modules
\cite{repo-projection,repo-forest}. The research API does not infer it from
an arbitrary presentation. Its verifier reports whether proper powers
occurred; a caller-supplied Boolean is not a topological proof.

Disjoint pairs can be validated against the same pre-batch state and applied
simultaneously, because they affect disjoint free factors. Selected source
slots are then tombstoned. Although a selected donor's raw image may contain
opposite letters, its one-generator exponent is zero, so deleting precisely
that slot is justified. Unselected slots are never dropped by inference.

\subsection{Forests and their linearization}

A unit-coordinate primitive donor has an equation of the form
$c=p^k$ with nonzero integer $k$. Select at most one parent for each child,
and require the dependency graph to be a forest. The maintained forest
operation can substitute all dependencies in one batch \cite{repo-forest}.

\begin{lemma}[Forest linearization]
\label{lem:forest}
A valid unit-coordinate forest batch has a sequential realization by
primitive-pair eliminations that use the same original donor slots, retain
the intended anchors, and yield the same monomial image of each initial
generator. Consequently all results below apply to arbitrary mixtures of
pair batches and forest batches within a raw epoch.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Eliminate a leaf child $c$ first, keeping its parent $p$. No remaining
selected donor has $c$ as its parent, so none of those donor words changes
at that step. Continue leaf-first. Each later selected donor is therefore
still its pre-batch word when used. The image of a vertex is the anchor
raised to the product of edge powers on its path, exactly as in simultaneous
forest substitution. Orienting a cyclic quotient to keep the parent instead
of the smaller numerical label changes only generator naming and possibly
its sign; neither affects the bounds.
\end{proof}

The acyclic and unit-coordinate hypotheses matter. Overlapping arbitrary
primitive pairs need not present a free factor after simultaneous killing,
and cycles are not covered by this argument.

\section{A global lattice invariant}
\label{sec:lattice}

Index rows by original generators and columns by current live generators.
At every raw state the cumulative map is monomial:
\begin{equation}
 x_i\longmapsto z_{\pi(i)}^{k_i},\qquad k_i\in\Z\setminus\{0\}.
 \label{eq:monomial}
\end{equation}
Let $K\in\Z^{r\times s}$ record this map: row $i$ has its sole nonzero entry
$k_i$ in column $\pi(i)$. For a live generator $z$, let
$I_z=\{i:\pi(i)=z\}$ be its block. All blocks are nonempty and partition $X$.

Let $A\in\Z^{m\times r}$ be the source exponent-sum matrix:
\[
 A_{ji}=\text{signed exponent sum of }x_i\text{ in }R_j.
\]
In particular $|A_{ji}|\le L$. Let $J$ be the set of original slots selected
as donors so far, and $A_J$ its row submatrix. This matrix is an analysis
object; the producer need not construct it.

\begin{lemma}[Primitive disjoint columns]
\label{lem:primitivecolumns}
For each live block $I_z$,
\[
 \gcd\{|k_i|:i\in I_z\}=1.
\]
In particular, the lattice $\im_{\Z}K$ is a direct summand of $\Z^r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Initially $K$ is the identity. Suppose a contraction merges two primitive
columns supported on disjoint blocks. Formula \eqref{eq:localmap} multiplies
one block by $|v|$ and the other by $-u\sgn(v)$. The gcd in their union is
$\gcd(|u|,|v|)=1$; other columns are unchanged. Induction proves the first
claim. A primitive vector on a coordinate block extends to an integral basis
of that block. Taking those extensions independently for all disjoint blocks
proves the direct-summand statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Independence of selected source rows]
\label{lem:rowrank}
After $q$ individual contractions, $s=r-q$ and
\[
 A_JK=0,\qquad |J|=q,\qquad \rank_{\Q}A_J=q.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each selected source word is killed by its contraction, so its exponent row
annihilates the resulting $K$ and every later cumulative map. This gives
$A_JK=0$.

For independence, consider the next donor row $a$ and the previous map
$K_{\mathrm{old}}$. The vector $aK_{\mathrm{old}}$ is the current donor's
exponent vector: its two nonzero coordinates are $eu$ and $ev$. It is
therefore nonzero. Every previous selected row annihilates
$K_{\mathrm{old}}$, so $a$ cannot be in their rational span. Induction gives
rank $q$. Disjoint batches can be linearized, and forests are covered by
Lemma~\ref{lem:forest}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Saturated kernel identity]
\label{thm:saturated}
At every state in a raw epoch,
\begin{equation}
 \ker_{\Q}A_J=\im_{\Q}K,
 \qquad
 \ker_{\Z}A_J=\im_{\Z}K.
 \label{eq:saturated}
\end{equation}
Moreover the restricted matrix $A_J[:,I_z]$ has a one-dimensional rational
kernel, spanned by the primitive column $(k_i)_{i\in I_z}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The columns of $K$ are independent because they are nonzero and have disjoint
support. Lemma~\ref{lem:rowrank} gives
$\dim\ker_{\Q}A_J=r-(r-s)=s$, proving the rational identity.

For the integral identity, take $y\in\ker_{\Z}A_J$ and write $y=Kc$ with
$c\in\Q^s$. On a block $I_z$, $y_i=c_zk_i$. A Bezout combination of the
$k_i$ equals one by Lemma~\ref{lem:primitivecolumns}; the same combination
of the integer $y_i$ equals $c_z$. Thus $c_z\in\Z$, proving integrality.

Finally, a vector in the kernel of $A_J[:,I_z]$ can be extended by zero
outside that block. The rational identity expresses this extension using
the columns of $K$. Disjoint support forces every other column coefficient
to vanish, so the restricted kernel is exactly the indicated line.
\end{proof}

This is the key source constraint missing from a stepwise length recurrence.
It is essential that the selected rows belong to one fixed source. The
argument says substantially more than preservation of abelianization, but
it does \emph{not} replace the nonabelian primitive-word checks that made
the contractions legitimate in the first place.

\section{Cofactor bounds and their consequences}
\label{sec:cofactor}

Define
\[
 \Delta_t(L)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&t=1,\\
 (t-1)^{(t-1)/2}L^{t-1},&t\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Phase-independent exponent bound]
\label{thm:main}
For every raw state and every block $I_z$ of size $t$,
\begin{equation}
 |k_i|\le\Delta_t(L)\qquad(i\in I_z).
 \label{eq:cofactorbound}
\end{equation}
Consequently every cumulative exponent has
\begin{equation}
 O\!\left(r(B+\log(r+1))\right)
 \label{eq:bitbound}
\end{equation}
bits throughout the entire raw epoch. There is no dependence exponential
in the number of raw phases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $t=1$, primitivity gives $|k_i|=1$. For $t\ge2$, choose $t-1$
independent rows of $A_J[:,I_z]$ and call the resulting matrix $C$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:saturated} shows this is possible and that $\ker C$ is
one-dimensional. Let
\[
 c_i=(-1)^{i+1}\det C_{\widehat{i}}
\]
be its vector of alternating maximal minors. Laplace expansion gives
$Cc=0$, and row rank $t-1$ ensures $c\ne0$. Dividing $c$ by the gcd of its
entries gives the unique primitive integral kernel vector up to sign.
Therefore $|k_i|\le |c_i|$.

Each row of a $(t-1)\times(t-1)$ minor has Euclidean norm at most
$\sqrt{t-1}\,L$. Hadamard's inequality bounds its determinant by the product
of these norms, giving \eqref{eq:cofactorbound}. Taking binary logarithms
and using $t\le r$ and $\log_2 L\le B$ proves \eqref{eq:bitbound}.
\end{proof}

For later bookkeeping fix the convenient common bound
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda=8(r+1)\bigl(B+\lceil\log_2(r+1)\rceil+2\bigr).
 \label{eq:Lambda}
\end{equation}
Constants here are deliberately loose. Cumulative exponents, lengths of
substituted source gates, primitive donor coordinates, and intermediate
prefix-height quantities have $O(\Lambda)$ bits. For example, a mapped
root has length at most $L\max_i|k_i|$. A donor's primitive coordinates
are bounded by its occurrence counts, and its height extrema multiply
counts by such coordinates; this only multiplies the bit bound by a constant.

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial raw depth is harmless]
\label{cor:depth}
Any valid raw epoch has at most $r-1$ individual contractions, and all
integer data needed for its source-based primitive checks and monomial
updates have polynomial size in the initial encoded parameters. This remains
true for $r-1$ sequential one-pair phases or arbitrarily deep valid forests.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Why primitivity, rather than arbitrary scaling, matters}

The cofactor argument bounds the \emph{primitive} kernel vector, not arbitrary
multiples of it. If a procedure were allowed to replace a live generator by
an arbitrary power without maintaining surjectivity, the same rational kernel
line could be represented by arbitrarily large integer vectors. The gcd
condition is therefore not cosmetic. It is the exact property that prevents
spurious scale growth.

Similarly, independent source rows are necessary. Merely accumulating many
relations that vanish after projection gives no determinant bound on the map
unless they have the full required rank. The nonzero current donor exponent
vector supplies that rank increase at each contraction.

\subsection{A family requiring linear growth of exponent bit length}

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness of the $rB$ dependence]
\label{prop:sharp}
For $q=2^b$ and $r\ge2$, the source presentation
\begin{equation}
 \langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\mid x_i^q x_{i+1}^{-1},\ 1\le i<r\rangle
 \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
is infinite cyclic. A valid sequential raw epoch produces
$x_i\mapsto x_1^{q^{i-1}}$, and the largest exponent has exactly
$(r-1)b+1$ bits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each source relation eliminates $x_{i+1}$ as $x_i^q$ by an ordinary Tietze
move. Applying these eliminations in increasing order gives the stated powers
and leaves the free generator $x_1$. The bit count follows from
$q^{r-1}=2^{b(r-1)}$.
\end{proof}

Thus a logarithmic bound in $r$ would be false in general. Nevertheless the
bit growth is linear in $rB$, not exponential in the number of contractions.
These are abstract presentations of $\Z$, not claimed difficult knot diagrams.
The distinction is especially important for the capacity experiments below.

\subsection{A stronger bound from a known nonzero character}

\begin{theorem}[Character-controlled columns]
\label{thm:character}
Let $h\in\Z^r$ satisfy $Ah=0$. On any block $I_z$ for which $h$ is not
identically zero, let $g_z=\gcd\{|h_i|:i\in I_z\}$. Then $h_i\ne0$ for
every $i\in I_z$, and
\begin{equation}
 k_i=\varepsilon_z\frac{h_i}{g_z},\qquad \varepsilon_z\in\{1,-1\}.
 \label{eq:character}
\end{equation}
In particular $|k_i|\le\max_{i\in I_z}|h_i|$ on such a block.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $A_Jh=0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:saturated} gives $h=Kc$ with $c\in\Z^s$.
On $I_z$, $h_i=c_zk_i$. If the restriction is nonzero, $c_z\ne0$ and
all $k_i\ne0$, so all $h_i\ne0$. Their gcd is $|c_z|$, because the $k_i$
are primitive. Solving for $k_i$ proves \eqref{eq:character}.
\end{proof}

For consistently oriented Wirtinger meridians the usual character sends every
generator to one. A raw epoch frozen at that presentation therefore has
$|k_i|=1$ throughout. Large powers arise only after a change of source
coordinates, or on other supplied presentations. This gives both a useful
optimization target and a warning: algebraic huge-power fixtures are not
automatically representative of direct meridional inputs. Blocks on which a
known character is identically zero still require the general cofactor bound.

\section{Reachable circuits versus historical allocation}
\label{sec:native}

The preceding result also sharpens the analysis of the existing updater;
a new representation is not necessary merely to obtain a polynomial raw-epoch
bound. This observation prevents an incorrect exponential-speedup narrative.

Let $S_j$ denote the number of nodes reachable from the current relator roots
before batch $j$, and let $N_j$ be all arena nodes allocated so far. They need
not be comparable by a fixed constant: old versions can remain allocated
without being current. Let $P_j$ conservatively count all gates needed for
signed local power images in that batch, including reused gates participating
in those image circuits.

\begin{lemma}[Reachable-node recurrence]
\label{lem:reachable}
For the raw mapper that substitutes terminal images and maps each current
reachable concatenation once,
\begin{align}
 S_{j+1}&\le S_j+O(P_j),\label{eq:reachable}\\
 N_{j+1}-N_j&\le S_j+O(P_j).\label{eq:allocated}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each old reachable concatenation has at most one mapped concatenation in the
new current graph. Its children are either other mapped nodes or nodes in the
chosen terminal-image circuits. Unchanged terminals can remain as before;
changed terminals are accounted for among image gates. The new current graph
therefore contains at most one mapped representative for each old reachable
node, together with the image circuits. Old historical versions do not add a
second copy to \emph{current reachability}. Structural interning and deletion
of donor roots can only lower the count. The same construction allocates at
most that many new nodes, giving the second inequality.
\end{proof}

The pinned \code{apply\_projection} implementation uses exactly this pattern:
it constructs binary powers and performs one snapshot traversal of
\code{arena.\_reachable(roots)} \cite{repo-code}. The inspected word-arena
primitives use raw concatenation and binary powering; they do not call
normalization in these operations \cite{repo-arena}. Negative images are
constructed from oppositely signed letters. The forest variant constructs
shared parent powers with the same total local-power accounting
\cite{repo-forest}.

There are at most $r-1$ contractions. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} bounds every local
power exponent by $O(\Lambda)$ bits, so binary powering gives
\[
 \sum_{j<d}P_j=O(r\Lambda).
\]
Combining this with Lemma~\ref{lem:reachable} yields the following result.

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial cost for the existing raw updater]
\label{thm:native}
Throughout an epoch of $d\le r-1$ successful raw batches,
\begin{align}
 S_j&=O(S_0+r\Lambda),\label{eq:smallcurrent}\\
 N_d&\le N_0+O\bigl(dS_0+(d+1)r\Lambda\bigr)\\
    &=N_0+O(rS_0+r^2\Lambda).\label{eq:smallallocated}
\end{align}
For the inspected raw primitives and polynomial arithmetic donor checks,
planning, updating, and replaying the entire epoch have polynomial total bit
cost in the initial arena size, initial numeric metadata, $m$, $r$, and label
bit sizes. Normalization and legacy search are excluded.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sum \eqref{eq:reachable} to obtain \eqref{eq:smallcurrent}, and then sum
\eqref{eq:allocated}. Every scan and arithmetic primitive-width check is
polynomial in the current reachable or allocated data. There are at most $r$
full candidate scans, counting the final unsuccessful one, and at most $r-1$
contractions. Exact additions, multiplications, gcd computations, sorting of
reachable identifiers, and cached metadata updates all have polynomial cost
on the bounded integers. If the epoch starts in an arena containing old unused
nodes, their existing size and maximum metadata bit length are part of the
initial input cost; the theorem does not erase that history.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The correction to the earlier accounting}

The prior inequality $N_{j+1}\le2N_j+O(rB_j)$ remains true. Its use as the
only recurrence is unnecessarily pessimistic because it treats every
historically allocated node as though it were copied into every current graph.
The preceding two-counter analysis repairs this. Separately, the cofactor
bound replaces a pessimistic recurrence for $B_j$ by a single uniform bound.
Both improvements are required to obtain the clean epoch statement.

Accordingly, the old $2^{O(d)}$ and $2^{O(d\log r)}$ raw bounds should be
\emph{sharpened}, not described as refuted execution lower bounds. A general
short-depth producer was never established; this note shows that short raw
depth is not the remaining issue under the epoch model. It does not show that
a useful raw epoch is easy to expose or that a search uses few source resets.

\section{Source-anchored evaluation without historical substitution}
\label{sec:implementation}

The new representation stores the source circuit once and maintains only
\[
 \bigl(\pi(i),k_i\bigr)_{i=1}^r,\quad
 \text{live labels},\quad\text{deleted source slots},\quad\text{move records}.
\]
A source terminal $x_i^{\epsilon}$ is interpreted as a homogeneous run of
$|k_i|$ letters, with current label $\pi(i)$ and sign $\epsilon\sgn(k_i)$.
No power circuit is built for that run while the epoch remains raw.

\subsection{Signed counts and eligibility}

A bottom-up scan assigns a gate either a map from at most two current labels
to positive and negative occurrence counts, or a marker meaning that more than
two labels occur. Concatenation adds counts and unions supports. Since these
are literal words, opposite occurrences are not cancelled. A relator is a
candidate precisely when its current support has two labels, each occurring
with a single sign.

The scan must be recomputed for a new image table. An old source gate that
once used three distinct live generators may use only two after contractions.
Caching its ``more than two'' marker independently of the image state would
be unsound. The native updater avoids that issue by assigning new node
identities to changed words; a fixed-source implementation must instead use
state-sensitive summaries. The reference kernel deliberately recomputes them.

Let a coherent candidate have signed counts $U,V\ne0$. Put
\[
 e=\gcd(|U|,|V|),\quad u=U/e,\quad v=V/e,\quad p=|u|,\quad q=|v|.
\]
Rename or invert its two generators so its word is positive. The primitive
width test uses an increment $q$ on its first letter and $-p$ on its second.

\subsection{Prefix extrema on a fixed circuit}

For a word segment $W$, store the triple $(D,\ell,h)$ of total height change,
minimum prefix height, and maximum prefix height, including the empty prefix.
For two segments the exact composition law is
\begin{equation}
 (D,\ell,h)\star(E,\ell',h')=
 \bigl(D+E,\min(\ell,D+\ell'),\max(h,D+h')\bigr).
 \label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
A homogeneous source-leaf run with total increment $d$ has profile
$(d,\min(0,d),\max(0,d))$. Thus internal points in a huge power require no
iteration: a constant-slope segment attains its extrema at its endpoints.

\begin{proposition}[Coherent-width certificate]
\label{prop:width}
Let $W$ be a coherent two-letter word with counts $ep,eq$, where $p,q>0$
and $\gcd(p,q)=1$. If the profile with increments $q,-p$ has width
$p+q-1$, then $W$ is the $e$th power of a cyclically shifted primitive word.
The condition can be checked exactly by \eqref{eq:profile} on the source SLP.
\end{proposition}

Appendix~\ref{app:width} gives a self-contained proof of the sufficient
criterion. This is the criterion already used by the maintained primitive
module \cite{repo-primitive}; the present work changes its evaluation
representation and global complexity accounting, not its logical role.
When one total occurrence count is one, the word is automatically primitive:
it is cyclically a power of one generator followed by a single occurrence of
the other. The producer uses this shortcut; the independent verifier does not.

\subsection{Updates and a finite producer}

For a disjoint batch, form the local table in \eqref{eq:localmap}. Compose it
with the cumulative table once, across all original generators. If the current
image of $x_i$ is $a^{k_i}$ and the local map sends $a$ to $z^f$, its new image
is $z^{k_if}$. Mark the selected source slots deleted and remove the eliminated
live labels. All donor checks precede any table mutation.

The reference producer scans source slots in fixed order, greedily choosing
disjoint eligible pairs. An optional limit on pairs per batch supports
controlled sequential schedules. On failure to find a pair it stops with a
nondecisive endpoint; it does not call normalization or arbitrary search.
At rank one it computes the signed exponent of every retained source root
under the cumulative table. It reports the infinite-cyclic quotient endpoint
only when all these exponents are zero.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Raw source-anchored producer; local operations are exact.}]
freeze source circuit, live labels, and original relator slots
images[i] := (i, 1); deleted := empty set
while number of live labels > 1:
    summaries := signed counts on source under current images
    batch := greedy coherent primitive-width donors in undeleted slots
    if batch is empty: return STALLED with the recorded prefix
    validate all batch witnesses against the pre-batch source images
    images := compose all original images with the disjoint local table
    delete precisely the chosen source slots; update live labels
check every remaining relator exponent at the one-generator endpoint
return recorded prefix and exact endpoint classification
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial source-anchored epoch]
\label{thm:anchored}
The reference producer completes one frozen-source epoch in polynomial bit
time, including candidate discovery, witness construction, and the endpoint.
Independent replay has a polynomial bound as well. The working word
representation needs no new SLP nodes inside the epoch.

For example, with
$D=\Lambda+\lceil\log_2(M+m+\max X+2)\rceil$, a deliberately loose
schoolbook bound for production and replay is
\begin{equation}
 O\bigl(r(m+1)(M+r+m)D^3\bigr).
 \label{eq:timebound}
\end{equation}
Up to certificate storage, working data use
$O((M+r+m)D)$ bits, with temporary candidate profiles discarded between scans.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are at most $r-1$ contractions and $r$ candidate scans, including a
possible final failed scan. A shared signed-count scan takes $O(M)$
arithmetic operations. Checking all $m$ candidates separately by a full source
profile scan takes $O(mM)$ operations; sharing these candidate-specific scans
is not assumed. Updating the cumulative table takes $O(r)$ products per
batch, and the terminal exponent pass takes $O(M+m)$ additions and tests.
All integers involved have $O(D)$ bits by Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and the
source-length discussion. Quadratic multiplication and conservative cubic gcd
accounting imply \eqref{eq:timebound}, with ample slack for loop and identifier
costs. Replay checks at most $r-1$ witnesses and has no more expensive task.

The count or profile assigned to each source node has constant arity, and
only one candidate profile need be retained at a time. Images and slot
metadata are linear in $r+m$. A certificate contains at most $r-1$ witnesses,
so its scalar and label payload is also polynomial. Structural dictionaries
can be replaced by ordered maps if a deterministic comparison-model bound is
preferred; the Python implementation uses ordinary dictionaries.
\end{proof}

The bound is not advertised as an optimized exponent of input size. It
establishes that raw phase count is no longer an exponential parameter.
Practical dispatch, caching, and multi-candidate evaluation can be investigated
without changing the proof obligation. External cancellation callbacks are
assumed constant-time in these bounds; their actual costs must otherwise be
added. The default standalone run has no such callback.

\subsection{Materialize only at a real representation boundary}

A fresh ordinary binary SLP for the current presentation can be built by
constructing signed powers for the $r$ cumulative images, then mapping each
source concatenation once. This uses
\[
 O(M+r\Lambda)
\]
gates and polynomial bit work. An export may be necessary before calling an
existing normalizer or another backend. It is unnecessary for the source-based
primitive checks, and performing it after every raw batch defeats the main
representation saving. The benchmark includes this eager policy as an explicit
negative control.

Appending exports into an old arena rather than creating a fresh one retains
history and must be charged accordingly. The supplied adapter does not free
or rewrite the caller's arena, certify an arbitrary image table, or silently
change the existing source-provenance contract.

\section{Independent replay and the nonabelian safety boundary}
\label{sec:verification}

The package contains a producer and a separate verifier. They share the
immutable source grammar definition, but the verifier imports neither the
producer's planner, state class, width routine, nor update helper. For each
witness it traverses only nodes reachable from that source donor and computes
signed counts and prefix extrema together. The producer instead performs full
source scans and uses a unit-occurrence shortcut. This separates important
algorithmic failure modes; it is not a claim of formal verification in a proof
assistant.

\subsection{Certificate contract}

The top-level certificate contains exactly a format identifier, a source
SHA-256 identifier, a list of disjoint batches, and an endpoint classification.
Each witness records its original slot, ordered pair of live labels, primitive
signed vector, positive power exponent, and width. Scalar arithmetic fields
use canonical hexadecimal strings, avoiding decimal conversion limits on
very large exact integers. Identifiers must be strict integers, not Booleans.

Before converting a supplied hexadecimal scalar to an integer, the checker
bounds its string length using a conservative version of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. It then requires canonical spelling and checks its bit
length. The checker rejects reused or invalid slots, nonlive labels,
nonprimitive vectors, noncoherent words, foreign letters, incorrect widths,
extra fields, and overlapping pairs. All witnesses in a batch are verified
against the intact pre-batch state, before any update is applied.

The source hash is a bookkeeping binding, not a probabilistic word-equality
oracle. The checker receives the caller's actual source and recomputes every
mathematical assertion from its content; no positive algebraic conclusion is
obtained by comparing two hashes alone. Source serialization and incoming
payload sizes still need an outer input-byte allowance in a production host.
The kernel's cooperative work allowance is not a hard process-memory limit.

At rank one, the verifier independently recomputes every retained source
relator's exponent. A deleted slot is exempt only because its own donor
witness was verified. A nonempty raw circuit with exponent zero is accepted
at this endpoint, since a word in one generator is determined by its exponent.
No reduction or expansion is required. A \code{stalled} record is merely a
verified prefix with no cyclic endpoint; replay does not certify that every
possible donor has been searched.

\subsection{Why the lattice audit cannot authorize a quotient}

\begin{example}[A false positive for abelianization-only reasoning]
\label{ex:torus}
Consider
\[
 H=\langle a,b\mid a^2b^3\rangle.
\]
Its source row is $(2,3)$, with primitive integer kernel vector $(3,-2)$.
The row rank, annihilation, primitive gcd, and cofactor identities all hold.
Nevertheless $H$ is not infinite cyclic: sending $a$ to a transposition and
$b$ to a three-cycle gives a surjection onto the nonabelian group $S_3$.
The word $a^2b^3$ is therefore not primitive. Its profile with increments
$3,-2$ has width $6$, not the required $2+3-1=4$, and the implemented
primitive checker rejects it.
\end{example}

This example rules out a tempting but invalid shortcut: selecting rows of
$A$ with a convenient integer kernel and treating their quotient as a free
group. The matrix argument controls the size of \emph{already valid}
nonabelian operations. It does not discover valid operations from homology
alone.

\subsection{Proper powers and topological provenance}

For a proper-power donor $w^e$ with $e>1$, replay proves the local primitive
structure and records a torsion-freeness obligation. Without that obligation,
it certifies the quotient obtained by additionally killing $w$, not an
isomorphism with the supplied arbitrary presentation. The returned
\code{needs\_torsion\_freeness} field cannot be ignored.

A full recognizer must independently reconstruct the group presentation from
the validated classical knot source, replay every preceding presentation
move, authorize proper-power elimination from the resulting invariant, and
retain its established terminal interpretation. The new research format has
no planar-diagram field or complete presentation-prefix language. It is
therefore \emph{not} a standalone knot certificate. In particular, presenting
an abstract algebraic fixture to this module is not a test of unknot
recognition on a diagram.

\section{Integration plan for the maintained repository}
\label{sec:integration}

The implementation is packaged for review without mutating the user's
repository. A suggested destination is a new research directory under
\path{Topology/UnknotRecognition/fast/}, followed by a separately reviewed
integration into the primitive-projection route. Existing defaults should not
change on the basis of the algebraic timings above.

\subsection{The minimal host interface}

At a host-selected raw boundary, snapshot the reachable source circuit, its
fixed relator slots, and live labels. Retain the source's complete verified
presentation prefix in the existing host certificate; the research snapshot
alone does not replace it. Run source-based checks and table updates while
all permitted moves remain monomial and source-slot preserving. Independently
replay the arithmetic prefix before accepting any algebraic endpoint. Export
once when a normalizer or another circuit consumer genuinely needs a standard
SLP, then freeze a new source after the host's verified transition.

The included \path{native_adapter.py} supplies snapshot and export functions
using the inspected arena protocol. It does not expose a new native CLI flag,
modify existing version-six or version-seven certificates, or prove a
cross-version compatibility claim. An implementation can initially run it in
shadow mode: compare current raw words and verdict-neutral metadata while
leaving the old path authoritative.

\subsection{Suggested changes to the maintained exposition}

The earlier raw-phase bounds can be retained as elementary coarse bounds and
followed by Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:native}. The text should then
replace ``a short raw schedule is needed to control repeated exponent growth''
by ``raw epochs have polynomial encoded cost; discovery, representation resets,
and decisive completion require separate control.'' The forest discussion can
invoke Lemma~\ref{lem:forest} rather than compounding an $r$ factor per forest
phase indefinitely.

This is a refinement to the analysis, not evidence that the old code was
incorrect. Nor does a polynomial raw epoch justify enabling primitive
projection or forests by default: the existing measurements report ordinary
corpus overhead in some configurations \cite{repo-projection,repo-forest}.
The new experiment does not supersede those whole-call results.

\section{A more precise route to quasi-polynomial recognition}
\label{sec:qp}

Write $\QP$ for time $\exp((\log(n+2))^{O(1)})$ on explicit input size $n$.
The new local theorem supports a cleaner conditional composition statement.

\begin{theorem}[Epoch-search composition]
\label{thm:qp}
Suppose a source-correct recognition procedure on an $n$-bit knot input
explores at most $\exp(O(\log^{\alpha}(n+2)))$ states, for a fixed
$\alpha\ge1$. Assume each state has a frozen-source presentation, initial
numeric metadata, and live-label encoding of size at most
$\exp(O(\log^{\beta}(n+2)))$, for a fixed $\beta\ge1$. Suppose every
nonraw transition, including source reconstruction, exposure, normalization,
and bookkeeping, costs at most
$\exp(O(\log^{\gamma}(n+2)))$, for a fixed $\gamma\ge1$.

If the search is sound and complete with correctly verified terminal tests,
then replacing each raw epoch by either the analyzed maintained mechanism or
the source-anchored algorithm gives total time
\[
 \exp\!\left(O\!\left(\log^{\max\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}}(n+2)\right)\right).
\]
No separate logarithmic raw-phase-depth hypothesis is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each raw epoch costs a fixed polynomial in its initial encoded parameters by
Theorem~\ref{thm:native} or~\ref{thm:anchored}. A polynomial of
$\exp(O(\log^{\beta}(n+2)))$ has the same asymptotic form. Multiply the maximum
per-state processing and transition cost by the state bound. Adding the
corresponding logarithmic exponents yields the claimed maximum-degree bound.
Soundness and completeness are supplied by the hypothesized search, not by
this running-time multiplication.
\end{proof}

The theorem isolates, rather than proves, the global obligations. In
particular, it does not bound the number of states at which no coherent donor
is exposed, the size of a new source after a nonmonomial move, or the cost of
choosing successful moves. A raw producer can stall on a complicated
presentation of $\Z$ without contradicting any local result. An exact
exponential fallback still preserves completeness, but invoking it does not
establish a quasi-polynomial worst-case bound.

A useful conceptual shift is to distinguish three quantities: current
algebraic rank, encoded presentation size at epoch boundaries, and the size of
the explored transition graph. Rank reduction controls the first inside a
raw epoch. The cofactor argument controls intermediate monomial coefficients.
Neither alone controls the other two quantities across arbitrary search.

\section{Further research questions and concrete targets}
\label{sec:questions}

The following problems prioritize missing global mechanisms and testable native
improvements. None is presented as already resolved by the experiments.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \textbf{Bounded-cost exposure from knot provenance.}
For source presentations actually reached from validated knot diagrams, can
one expose a useful coherent primitive donor with a bounded family of
nonmonomial moves? A meaningful theorem must bound both the number of tried
moves and the encoded size of every resulting source, rather than merely
showing that some sequence exists.

\item \textbf{An amortized potential across source resets.}
Find a potential combining source grammar size, character height, selected-row
rank, and presentation complexity that cannot increase too often under the
host's normalization/exposure policy. The target is a bound on total reset
cost, not another bound on contractions within a fixed epoch.

\item \textbf{Native source-anchored replay and complete-call measurement.}
Implement a shadow backend preserving the existing source prefix and old
certificate versions. Require exact equality of the full raw relator slots
and paired timings including producer discovery, independent replay, export,
and earlier pipeline stages on actual diagrams. Report regressions as well as
speedups; do not use the supplied-schedule ratios as substitutes.

\item \textbf{Character-aware dispatch.}
Use Theorem~\ref{thm:character} to distinguish unit-image meridional epochs,
nonzero-character blocks with small exponents, and zero-character blocks.
Can a validated character predict whether source anchoring will save work
before performing an expensive scan? The native cost model must include the
cost of updating that character through the verified prefix.

\item \textbf{State-sensitive summaries with sublinear updates.}
The reference implementation rescans the source when live labels merge.
Can capped supports and occurrence counts be maintained incrementally with a
provable amortized bound while allowing previously large supports to become
small? Caching an old ineligibility marker is not a valid answer. Union-by-size
or dependency indexing should be evaluated against full-scan costs and memory.

\item \textbf{Shared evaluation of many primitive widths.}
Candidate profiles have different weights $(q,-p)$. Develop an exact
multi-query representation for prefix extrema that avoids one full scan per
candidate without producing an exponentially large parametric envelope.
A negative envelope-size example would also clarify the right restricted
source classes.

\item \textbf{Direct source-anchored forest certificates.}
The theorem covers forests through linearization, but the research format
contains only disjoint pair batches. Add direct forest replay with independently
checked acyclicity, unit coordinates, and parent-first table composition.
Compare certificate length and replay work against sequential linearization,
including tall chains and shared-anchor stars.

\item \textbf{Sharper determinant bounds from knot-derived matrices.}
The bound uses only entry size. Can sparsity, total unimodularity of particular
prefix matrices, or controlled minors from the verified topological source
replace $L^{r-1}r^{O(r)}$ by a smaller parameter? The meridional character
already forces unit images before coordinate changes; the interesting question
is what survives the actual exposure operations.

\item \textbf{Controlled extensions beyond monomial images.}
Can a bounded-support generalization allow an original generator to map to a
product of a few live powers while retaining a useful primitive lattice
normalization and a polynomial representation invariant? A rational kernel
bound alone will not control noncommutative word structure. Any extension
needs new nonabelian arguments and explicit circuit-size bounds.

\item \textbf{A complete terminal class with a bounded transition graph.}
Identify a diagram-derived class for which the exact native procedure reaches
a verified terminal after quasi-polynomially many controlled exposure states.
The class should be specified by checkable input properties, not by the
existence of an unknown successful certificate of the desired size.

\item \textbf{Formalize the saturation and reachability lemmas.}
The proof-assistant target can be small and precise: disjoint primitive
columns, row-rank growth, the integer kernel identity, maximal-minor bounds,
and the raw mapper's reachable-node injection. Formalization should expose
which claims need source provenance and which are pure integer linear algebra.

\item \textbf{Geometric interfaces to hierarchy compression.}
Determine whether the polynomial raw-epoch component can serve as an exact
local operation in a quantified handle- or hierarchy-based recognizer. A
successful connection must construct the algebraic source from the geometric
state and control the transition graph and attachment data; analogy alone is
not an end-to-end complexity proof.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

The source exponent-sum matrix constrains a whole sequence of primitive
projections much more strongly than a recurrence on the latest word length.
Selected source rows have exactly the rank lost by the quotient, and the
cumulative monomial columns form their saturated integer kernel. This yields
a phase-independent cofactor bound and, together with reachable-node
accounting, a polynomial bound for complete raw epochs, even for the existing
updater. The new source-anchored implementation further reduces unnecessary
representation maintenance while preserving exact independent arithmetic replay.

The result changes the location of the remaining complexity problem. A shallow
raw schedule is no longer needed to prevent exponential encoded growth inside
a fixed epoch. The unresolved general work is to control exposure, new source

terminal. The supplied proofs, counterexamples, tests, and retained negative

\section{Proof of the coherent-width certificate}
\label{app:width}

We prove Proposition~\ref{prop:width} without invoking word expansion in the
algorithm. Expansion is used only as a mathematical object in this proof.
Invert the two generators independently so that the word is positive in
letters $a,b$. Let its counts be $ep,eq$ with $\gcd(p,q)=1$, and set
$N=p+q$. The height increments are $q$ on $a$ and $-p$ on $b$.

\subsection{The minimum strip forces a repeated period}

Both increments are congruent to $q$ modulo $N$. Thus after $j$ letters the
height is congruent to the initial height plus $jq$ modulo $N$. Since
$\gcd(q,N)=1$, the first $N$ heights have distinct residues. A strip of width
$N-1$ contains exactly $N$ integer heights, so it contains exactly one
representative of each residue.

Translate the strip to $\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}$. From height $h$, an $a$ step
stays in the strip exactly when $h\le p-1$; a $b$ step stays in the strip
exactly when $h\ge p$. These alternatives partition the strip. Hence the
next letter is uniquely determined by the current height. After $N$ steps
the residue returns to its start, so the actual height and then the letter
sequence repeat. The word is a literal $e$th power of a period of length $N$.
That period has zero net height, hence exactly $p$ letters $a$ and $q$
letters $b$.

\subsection{The forced period is primitive}

Use induction on $p+q$. The base $p=q=1$ gives a cyclic shift of $ab$,
which is primitive. If $p>q$, every $b$ is followed by an $a$: after a $b$
the height lies in $\{0,\ldots,q-1\}$, below $p$. Cyclically, every $b$
is therefore preceded by an $a$ as well, because two $b$'s cannot be
adjacent. Group each such preceding $a$ with its $b$, replacing $ab$ by
one new symbol $c$.

The vertices removed by this replacement are precisely those immediately
after these $a$ steps, at heights $p,\ldots,p+q-1$. Every strip height is
visited once in the period, so the remaining heights are exactly
$0,\ldots,p-1$. An unpaired $a$ step still has increment $q$, while a new
$c$ step has increment $q-p=-(p-q)$. The new cyclic word has counts
$p-q,q$, coprime and positive, and width
$p-1=(p-q)+q-1$. It is primitive in the free group on $a,c$ by induction.
The assignment $a\mapsto a$, $c\mapsto ab$ sends a free basis to a free
basis of $F(a,b)$, so its original period is primitive as well.

If $q>p$, interchange the letters and reverse the sign of height, reducing
to the preceding case. Cyclic rotations and independent generator inversions
preserve primitivity. This proves the certificate in its signed form.

\subsection{Why circuit extrema suffice}

A repeated homogeneous run has monotone height; only its endpoints can be
extrema. For a concatenation, all prefixes lie either in the left part or
in the translated right part, giving \eqref{eq:profile}. Induction over the
source DAG computes exactly the same extrema as the conceptual expanded
word. No approximation, probabilistic fingerprint, or truncation enters the
certificate.

\section{A worked three-generator epoch}
\label{app:example}

Consider
\[
 G=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3\mid x_1^2x_2^{-1},\ x_2^3x_3^{-1},\
 x_3x_3^{-1}x_1x_1^{-1}\rangle.
\]
The source matrix is
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0\\0&3&-1\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The first contraction sends $x_2$ to $x_1^2$; the current second source slot
is literally $x_1^6x_3^{-1}$. Its contraction sends $x_3$ to $x_1^6$.
The final cumulative column is $K=(1,2,6)^T$.

The two selected rows have alternating maximal minors $(1,2,6)$, so their
primitive kernel vector is exactly the cumulative image. The third source
slot is never deleted by a redundancy guess. Its final raw word is
$x_1^6x_1^{-6}x_1x_1^{-1}$, whose exponent is checked to be zero at rank one.
This small example displays the three separate mechanisms: nonabelian donor
verification, global integer size control, and all-relator terminal checking.
