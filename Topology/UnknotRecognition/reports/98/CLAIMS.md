# Claim and implementation ledger

## Established in the article

- Component-gauge semantics with exact cost, component-relative edge tags, and hidden actual flags.
- Exact composition under fresh-copy, edge-owned, terminal-only identification and forgetting.
- No exponential component-count factor: degree-`d` row bound and degree-`k` shared-budget candidate bound.
- Polynomial encoded evaluation and complete replay for every fixed threshold, regardless of intermediate disconnection or grammar depth.
- Polynomial equivariant coloring recipes, random-access source-occurrence colors, at most `k` cut-edge addresses, and two mark addresses.
- Contextual sufficiency for the specified marked response.
- Semantic correctness of the accepted report-91 graph-schema translation.

These are proved mathematical results for the stated model, supplied for independent review. Literature-wide novelty priority is not asserted.

## Implemented and executed

The producer, separately implemented complete checker, separately checked symbolic coloring recipe, capped literal oracle, exact explicit-response benchmark baseline, and report-91 adapter. Forty-eight unit-test methods pass. The data files record the larger deterministic audit and paired timings. A proof checker written in Python is not a Lean formalization or a proof of the Python runtime.

## Proved, not implemented

Precompiled XOR consistency/transport plans and minimum-only dominance. Exact-cost certificate mode is the only delivered mode; deleting higher-cost rows would invalidate its completeness contract.

## Recalled rather than claimed as new

The terminal spanning-tree skeleton argument, positive-capacity counting, XOR consistency, and the threshold-three cubic ball-pattern criterion. The article attributes ProveIt report 91, other relevant repository work, Lackenby's handout/preprint, and primary small-cut sparsification literature.

## Not delivered

A source-complete native geometric grammar exporter; authentication of a spherical embedding or a 3-ball; geometric noose/disc construction and backward transport; proof of globally bounded source production or hierarchy repairs; an unrestricted quasi-polynomial unknot recognizer; native knot benchmarks; an upstream commit or production patch.

## Corrections and cautions

The old explicit-response `2^C` lower bound remains correct for explicit boundary assignments. The new atlas changes the representation; it does not refute that bound. Root connectedness alone was not enough for the older parse, but no connectedness hypothesis is needed for the new supplied-grammar theorem.

Relative terminal parity without component-relative marks is not enough. Hidden components cannot be discarded. Fresh copies must not be aliased. Zero-capacity edges do not satisfy the proved row bound. A cheap local graph query does not imply a short geometric hierarchy. The maintained source already has a bounded inherited core; that is prior work, not this delivery's contribution.

Positive recipe verification proves existence only. Absence/minimality requires complete replay. Resource exhaustion is inconclusive. Benchmark gains and regressions are both retained, and the explicit-state baseline is newly written for the same exact-response contract, not misreported as the entire native implementation.
