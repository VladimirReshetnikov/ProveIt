# Claims and verification boundary

## Written proofs supplied

- Homogeneous unit cancellations preserve the erased grading; units in a genuine homogeneous differential are literal scalar identities.
- Quantum potentials are recoverable and unique up to a constant on a connected component. Existing complete ungraded component keys are sufficient for shift-invariant residue norms.
- Retained-mark completion preserves relative chain homotopies and direct sums. This does not rely on exactness or flatness of a quotient functor.
- The reduced residue-four component sum is a sound rank lower bound and is monotone under genuine refinement with exact multiplicities.
- Its invisible-rank deficit is exactly twice the sum of overlapping even/odd homology dimensions in each quantum residue.
- Saturated multiplicities at cap at least two preserve the threshold-capped rejection value. They do not guarantee monotonicity of an uncapped numeric sum.
- The exact signed Tait phase and the q=1 evaluation reconstruct a matching completion's complete residue-four vector in polynomial bit time.
- Singular-safe terminal compression, its rank gate, and the critical square law hold for arbitrary symmetric integer matrices. The corank threshold is sharp for planar signed graphs.
- The stopping-prefix bound is quasi-polynomial under explicit bounds on actual physical size and visited width.

These are manuscript proofs. No claim of independent peer review or exhaustive global priority checking is made. Classical ingredients are cited in the article.

## Implemented and executed locally

- Exact signed integer and rational matrix arithmetic, singular terminal kernels, critical-query shortcut, independent block-identity verifier.
- Classical PD checks, exact matching closure, phase-correct residue oracle.
- Quantum potential recovery and a read-only scanner-state observer, with local interface-model tests.
- A quotient-to-actual-PD cofactor verifier, with correct-case and tamper tests.
- An independent exponential reduced Khovanov cube and d-squared checks.
- 35 unit-test methods; 701 diagram/state-sum comparisons; 83 homology calculations; 4,000 graph quotient comparisons.
- Three-round paired exact determinant-kernel benchmarks with A/A controls and full raw inputs.
- LaTeX compilation and PDF rendering/layout inspection, recorded separately in the release checks.

## Not established or not executed

- No unrestricted quasi-polynomial unknot-recognition algorithm is proved.
- No general early-witness exposure or physical-multiplicity bound is proved.
- No realization theorem says the abstract strictness example is a knot tangle.
- No automatic terminal-partition/phase producer for arbitrary PD frontiers is implemented.
- No full upstream checkout regression suite or end-to-end observer speed benchmark was run.
- The supplied upstream diagnostic was not executed against the remote/current checkout.
- No Rust port, Lean formalization, integral torsion computation, or independent theorem review is supplied.
- The reference observer does not yet interrupt a determinant in the middle of a hard time budget.

## Safe use

A determinant certificate is not a certificate that an arbitrary supplied chain complex represents the input knot. A differential satisfying d-squared zero is not such a certificate either. Preserve relative provenance, retained-mark geometry, and exact algebra as separate obligations. A lower bound above one may reject a validated knot; a lower bound at most one is inconclusive. Resource exhaustion never certifies an unknot.
