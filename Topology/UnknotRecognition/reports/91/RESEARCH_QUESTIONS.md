# Further research

The article develops these questions in detail.

1. Produce faithful edge-owned graph grammars from the native raw-cut and marked-parallelity charts, with an explicit input family and encoded-size theorem.
2. Control the maximum number of terminal-bearing components at every rule, not only root connectedness; preserve useful DAG sharing when reorganizing connected assemblies.
3. Combine flat four-copy reduction with sparse functional summaries and prove a dispatch or parse-selection guarantee.
4. Introduce newly marked source points by local grammar refinement without expanding long paths or repeatedly duplicating all ancestors.
5. Convert the at-most-three cut-edge addresses into source-certified embedded nooses and violating discs, then transport them through hierarchy ancestors.
6. Factor large disconnected zero-cost relations instead of listing all 2^c colorings, while retaining exact witness flags under positive-cost perturbations.
7. Use other finite commutative edge-local witness monoids; the support and pair factors generalize from 4 and 16 to |M| and |M|^2.
8. Build an independently implemented cost-indexed complete checker and an incremental source-digest scheme for unchanged sub-DAGs.
9. Determine sharper support constants for geometric/cubic interface classes; the general polynomial degree k is sharp.
10. Prove grammar size, query count, admissibility repair, hierarchy reset, surface discovery, and disc transport bounds together on a source-certified recognition class.
