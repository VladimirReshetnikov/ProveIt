\section{Contracting unit-coordinate primitive forests in one raw batch}
\label{sec:primitive-forests}

Disjointness is a sufficient condition for simultaneous primitive-pair
projection, but it excludes a useful class of overlapping donors. A
primitive root whose exponent vector has a unit coordinate solves that
generator as an integer power of the other. If the resulting directed
dependencies form a forest, all such equations can be composed and
substituted in one raw batch. The new version-seven certificate checks
this larger operation independently. It is a separate optional mode,
so the preceding version-five and version-six search policies remain
available without enabling the forest planner.

\subsection{A scheduling obstruction that matching cannot repair}

Fix $q\ge1$ and consider generators $a,b_1,\ldots,b_k$ with the two
relations $(a^q b_i)^2$ and $(a^q b_i)^3$ for each $i$. The coprime powers
imply $a^q b_i=1$, so this is an abstract presentation of $\mathbb Z$.
Every eligible two-generator donor meets the common generator $a$.
A disjoint-pair round can therefore choose at most one pair. Projecting
that pair replaces its leaf by $a^{-q}$ and leaves all other donors in
the same star form. The surviving cube slot of that leaf becomes a raw
one-generator zero-exponent circuit, not a new two-generator donor.
Inductively, \emph{every} schedule restricted to these current-relator
pair operations needs $k$ rounds to reach rank one, or $k-1$ rounds
before the rank-two terminal can finish. Choosing a better matching does
not repair this example.

This is an obstruction for the abstract algebraic operation model. It is
not a lower bound for all recognition methods, nor a counterexample to a
short-depth claim specifically about planar Wirtinger presentations.
The family motivates allowing donors to share an anchor when their exact
quotient equations can be composed safely. For this family the forest
operation removes all $k$ leaves in one batch.

\subsection{Why an acyclic unit-coordinate batch preserves the group}

Use the source-provenance invariant from
Section~\ref{sec:primitive-projections}. For a checked donor $w^d$ supported
on distinct generators $p,c$, suppose the primitive root has signed
exponent coordinates $(u,\epsilon)$, where $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$ is
its coordinate at the chosen child $c$. Our arithmetic witnesses require
coherent signs. Hence the primitive root contains exactly one occurrence
of $c^{\epsilon}$ and is a cyclic conjugate of
$p^u c^{\epsilon}$. Killing $w$ is exactly the substitution
\[
 c=p^{-u/\epsilon}=p^{-u\epsilon}.
\]
Knot-group torsion-freeness justifies replacing $w^d=1$ by $w=1$ when
$d>1$. For $d=1$ this is an ordinary generator elimination. Both primitive
coordinates must be nonzero, so no zero image exponent is introduced.

Each selected child has exactly one parent, and selected directed edges
must have no cycle. Donors may share parents, and a parent may itself be
another edge's child. Following parents eventually reaches an uneliminated
anchor $z$. The image of a vertex $x$ is
\[
 x\longmapsto z^{K_x},\qquad
 K_x=\prod_{e\text{ on the path from }x\text{ to }z} k_e,
 \qquad k_e=-u_e\epsilon_e.
\]
The empty path has product one. Leaf-first Tietze elimination proves that
the quotient by all selected primitive roots is the free group on the
anchors and untouched generators. Substituting these images into every
other relator therefore preserves the full presented knot group. Each
selected donor has zero exponent in its own anchor after substitution,
so precisely those donor slots may be emptied. All other original slots
are retained and checked later.

The unit condition is essential. Overlapping arbitrary primitive pairs
do not automatically give such a power forest: sharing a non-unit power
of a newly introduced cyclic generator can produce an amalgamated group
rather than a free cyclic factor. The checker never infers the forest
rule from primitivity alone. Cycles are also excluded, even when particular
cyclic equations might happen to be consistent. This is a sufficient,
independently checkable operation with a clear cost contract.

\subsection{Selection and shared construction of all quotient images}

The planner reuses immutable capped-support and signed-count summaries.
It scans original relator slots, excludes noncoherent roots and primitive
vectors without a unit coordinate, and uses the existing arithmetic witness
for proper powers. When the exponent gcd is one and one signed count is
one, the coherent word is immediately a primitive conjugate of $p^u c$;
no extra producer profile pass is necessary. The independent checker still
recomputes its complete arithmetic profile.

For each eligible donor, the planner chooses an unused unit-coordinate
child, preferring the larger identifier when both orientations are available.
An undirected union-find structure rejects edges joining vertices already
in the same component. Together with at most one parent per child, this
maintains a rooted forest. A component's sole vertex without a parent is
its root, so an allowed new edge attaches one rooted component to another.
The choice is deterministic, but it is not asserted to maximize the number
of eliminations over all possible orientations and later states.

At least two selected edges are required for the producer to choose a
forest batch. Otherwise it falls back to the existing disjoint-pair planner.
After either operation, raw search and the explicit normalization boundary
behave as in the preceding section. A single checked forest edge is valid
for replay, even though the producer uses the older operation for that case.

A first implementation materialized every $K_x$ and rebuilt the power
$z^{K_x}$ separately. This was polynomial but repeated power-construction
loops along a long chain. The final producer follows dependency paths once,
then constructs child images from already constructed parent images:
\[
 I_c=I_p^{k_e}.
\]
Both signed parent images are retained, so a negative edge uses the opposite
parent image without a normalization step. Binary powering sees only the
\emph{local} edge exponent. The result is a shared straight-line program
for all images, followed by one raw mapping of the original reachable
circuit. This change reduces abstract search work on the 64-generator,
128-bit chain from the prototype's 1,723,368 to 223,340; it is a work-counter
comparison, not a timing or whole-recognizer ratio. Exact prototype source
snapshots and its initial passing test/audit records are retained.

\subsection{Independent version-seven replay and resource behavior}

A \texttt{primitive\_forest} move has exactly its kind and an edge list.
Each edge contains exactly a child identifier and a primitive-power proof
with its original donor slot. The checker requires strict integer types,
live distinct pair members, a unit coordinate at the child, a nonzero other
coordinate, distinct children and distinct donor slots. It validates every
arithmetic donor against the intact pre-batch roots before modifying any
root or live-generator set.

The verifier uses a different graph algorithm from the producer. It builds
parent-to-child adjacency, starts a queue at every surviving anchor, and
propagates along edges. Every live generator must be reached exactly once.
An isolated directed cycle cannot be reached from an anchor and is rejected.
The traversal also derives signed exponent products for literal replay.
Compressed replay constructs signed image circuits in this independently
obtained parent-before-child order, then uses a fresh postorder traversal
of the original grammar. It calls neither the forest planner nor its
application helper. Literal replay independently substitutes repeated
letters, checking the complete projected allocation and charged output work
before constructing any repeated list.

Version seven permits the earlier prefix operations, explicit normalization
handoffs and either the raw rank-one exponent endpoint or the rank-two
primitive terminal. Versions one through six retain their existing contracts;
a forest move disguised under version six is rejected. The source PD and
entire presentation prefix remain mandatory. Neither the local producer nor
the local replayer is an arbitrary-presentation knot-verdict API.

Both \texttt{compressed\_certificate} and \texttt{group\_decide} accept
the option \texttt{primitive\_forest=True}.

For \texttt{recognize}, set \texttt{group\_primitive\_forest=True}
and \texttt{use\_group=True}.

The CLI flag is \texttt{--group-primitive-forest}. Forest mode implies the projection route
and mandatory compressed proof replay. It is incompatible with the older
explicit-to-compressed adaptive-search flag. The mode defaults to false.
All graph, grammar and arithmetic passes charge cooperative work and
propagate external cancellation. As before, node limits do not cap all
Python auxiliary memory, and the work allowance is not unit-cost bit time.

\subsection{Polynomial cost of a batch, independent of forest depth}

Let $r$ be the initial live rank, $m$ the retained slot count, $M$ the allocated
SLP-node count and $B\ge1$ the maximum represented-length bit size. A donor's
local exponent has at most $B$ bits. A dependency path has at most $r-1$
edges, so a path product has $O(rB)$ bits. Word lengths after substitution
have $O(rB+B)$ bits. This controls lengths without expanding them.

Shared construction uses $O(B)$ new power nodes per selected edge, not
$O(rB)$ per path endpoint. Mapping the original circuit creates at most one
new concatenation per original reachable node. Thus a conservative bound
for the new total grammar size is
\[
 M'\le 2M+O(rB),\qquad B'=O((r+1)(B+1)).
\]
Candidate arithmetic checks, union-find selection, path processing,
independent graph replay, exact integer products and postorder mapping
are all polynomial in the initial encoded parameters. A loose bound may
classify all $m$ donors separately over all $M$ nodes; no shared-profile
linear-time claim is needed. Standard multiplication/gcd bounds and the
usual dictionary-access implementation qualification suffice. The compressed
operation has this bound regardless of the depth of the selected forest.
Literal replay instead pays for expanded output and may exhaust its budget.

This is stronger than performing one disjoint-pair round per leaf on a star,
but it is a local theorem. If $d$ raw phases mix these batches with ordinary
pair projections, a conservative composition bound is
\[
 \operatorname{poly}(\text{initial parameters})\,
       2^{O(d\log(r_0+1))}.
\]
The extra logarithm reflects the possible multiplicative bit-length growth
of an entire forest; the preceding $2^{O(d)}$ statement counted disjoint-pair
rounds and must not silently be relabeled as a forest-phase bound.
Polylogarithmically many raw phases would still give a quasipolynomial
bound from polynomial-size input data. No such general producer, endpoint
or exposure bound is proved here. Normalization and legacy discovery work
remain outside this raw-phase accounting.

\subsection{Audits and measurements}

The audit checks eighty actual diagrams against the complete package at \texttt{7bf6f01026a1}. Current default and current disjoint-only
certificates are exactly equal to their respective prior versions. Forest
mode produces 34 version-seven certificates with 203 forest edges, all of
whose complete proofs pass both literal and compressed replay. No input
is newly solved or loses a positive result; no donor in this diagram sample
is a proper power. Three separate actual five-braid sources carry powered
Whitehead prefixes of exponents $2^4$, $2^{128}$ and $2^{1024}$ and pass
compressed replay of their version-seven proofs. The small prefix also
passes literal replay.

Seven new test methods cover one hundred random signed trees with unrelated
retained relations, raw star/chain/balanced families, cycles, duplicate children
and slots, non-unit forgeries, strict fields, source rebinding, legacy proofs,
independence from producer helpers, allocation/work exhaustion, cancellation
and CLI behavior. The full maintained suite passes 988 tests in 207.503 seconds with Regina;
the log is \path{data/primitive-forest-full-tests.txt}.

The complete-recognition experiment uses nineteen fresh validated diagrams,
five measured rounds and one warm-up per arm. Eight shuffled arms compare
old default, old disjoint projections, current default and forest mode, each
with an A/A control. Full source reconstruction and mandatory independent
replay are inside the timed recognition call. The separate group-stage
experiment uses actual stabilized-circle diagrams with 8 through 128 crossings
and six shuffled arms. It bypasses earlier diagram simplification, so its
ratios are group-stage measurements, not whole-recognizer speedups.
All 760 whole-recognition calls and 150 group-stage calls complete, with
152 and 30 respective warm-ups. The runs take 83.853 and 12.067 seconds.
All samples, nondecisions, work counters, proof sizes and frozen source hashes
are retained by the driver \path{primitive_power_research/forests.py}
under \path{../fast}. Table ratios are medians of paired times; a value
above one favors forests. They need not equal ratios of separate time medians.

On ordinary survivor/mirror unknots other than survivor 06, old-default/forest
ratios range from 0.911 to 0.940. Survivor 06 has ratio 0.806 and forest
A/A ratio 1.112, so substantial local timing noise accompanies its observed
overhead. Gordian's old-default/forest and old-pair/forest ratios both round
to 0.964. Its search work increases from 1,215,145 in the default and
1,265,669 with disjoint projections to 1,305,143 with forests: 141 forest
attempts expose just one two-edge batch. Its forest proof is 10,060 bytes,
compared with 9,663 and 10,020 bytes respectively. Across the whole corpus,
old-default, old-pair, current-default and forest A/A ranges are
0.986--1.050, 0.979--1.019, 0.959--1.051 and 0.986--1.112. Current default
versus the pinned default ranges from 0.975 to 1.021. These observations
support retaining the optional policy; they do not establish a gain from
running the planner on every ordinary search state.

The restricted actual-diagram group stage does benefit. At 128 crossings,
old default, disjoint projections and forests take median 365.596, 194.214
and 130.191 milliseconds; paired speed ratios are 2.808 and 1.524 relative
to the first two arms. Search work falls from 493,856 to 263,317 to 185,764.
Allocated node counts are 24,891, 1,017 and 1,017. Forest mode uses 63
two-edge batches and 63 normalization handoffs rather than 126 disjoint
rounds, so even these easy actual inputs do not realize the one-batch
abstract-star example. Proof bytes are 8,640, 24,517 and 24,286: a faster
checked group stage need not have a shorter proof than the old default. Across the
five stage inputs, default A/A ratios are 0.986--1.006, pair A/A
0.980--1.001 and forest A/A 0.967--1.007. Earlier diagram simplification
would make these circles easy for the full recognizer; the stage improvement
must not be presented as a difficult-unknot or whole-pipeline result.

The new operation addresses one specific scheduling obstruction. It does
not establish that useful forests become exposed cheaply, nor that the
raw schedule reaches an endpoint for every unknot. The opt-in policy permits
further work on exposure and progress-sensitive dispatch without imposing
unproved performance benefits on the ordinary recognition route.
