\begin{proposition}[Disjoint stopping-set packing obstruction]
Let $A$ be a nonnegative source matrix. Suppose it contains $q$ pairwise
disjoint nonempty row sets $S_i$, each a stopping set: every column sum
on $S_i$ is zero or at least two. If a top reduction deletes at most $h$
rows and $q>h$, no subsequent deletion of lower-forest columns and complete
raw positive singleton cancellation can remove all remaining rows.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each deleted row belongs to at most one of the $S_i$. Some $S_i$ therefore
survives intact after at most $h$ deletions. Deleting columns preserves its
stopping-set property. The raw stopping-set obstruction excludes complete
positive cancellation. The conclusion holds for every allowed top tree
and lower forest; it does not assert any negative topological verdict.
\end{proof}

For \path{nested_1_4_6/interior_links}, seven disjoint stopping sets exceed
the six available top cancellations. For \path{interior_loop/interior_links},
two exceed the one available top cancellation. The probe discovers these
sets from source components of a two-row implication graph, then checks
disjointness and every original column sum directly. Thus the displayed
witnesses, not the graph-discovery code or the finite solver's negative
status, prove the obstruction. The sets remain obstructions upon adding
annular rows and auxiliary columns that vanish on the source rows. Changing
this raw certificate model requires additional geometric operations, not
another enumeration of its forests.
