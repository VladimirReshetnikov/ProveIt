\section{Batching powers of a Whitehead automorphism}
\label{sec:whitehead-powers}

\subsection{The remaining cost of elementary descent}

Compressed words remove the obligation to materialize every letter, but
not the obligation to make every search move. For example, the abstract
presentation
\[
 \langle a,b\mid (ab^N)^2,(ab^N)^3\rangle,\qquad N=2^h,
\]
has an $O(h)$-size straight-line program (SLP). Neither generator occurs
once in either relator. A unit Whitehead substitution $a\mapsto ab^{-1}$,
$b\mapsto b$ reduces total cyclic length by five, and the same direction
remains available for $N$ iterations. Our recorded producer takes these
unit steps before attempting an overlap in rank two. The presentation is
infinite cyclic: writing $u=ab^N$, the two relations imply $u=u^3(u^2)^{-1}=1$,
so $a=b^{-N}$. This example is a supplied group presentation, not a family
of hard knot diagrams. It nevertheless isolates an exponential iteration
cost in the previous compressed search.

The implementation now minimizes cyclic length along a selected Whitehead
power in one exact operation when a cheap adaptive trigger justifies it.
The minimization below is elementary and is proved here; it is not an
assumption imported from an unverified complexity claim in a research report.

\subsection{An exact convex length formula}

Use our established convention for a type-II Whitehead automorphism
$\alpha=\alpha_{a,A}$, where $a\in A$ and $-a\notin A$. Signed letters
$x$ with $|x|\ne |a|$ satisfy
\[
 \alpha^k(x)=a^{-k[-x\in A]}x a^{k[x\in A]},
 \qquad \alpha^k(a)=a.
\]
Here brackets denote indicators, and $k$ is a nonnegative integer.
For a cyclically freely reduced relator, retain its cyclic sequence of
letters other than $a^{\pm1}$. Between successive retained letters $x,y$
there is a possibly empty run $a^e$, where the sign of $e$ is measured
relative to the signed multiplier $a$. Its transformed exponent is
\[
 e+kd,\qquad d=[x\in A]-[-y\in A]\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[The retained letters do not cancel]
For every nonnegative integer $k$, the retained cyclic sequence is the same
before and after applying $\alpha^k$ and cyclic free reduction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Only a gap that becomes zero can bring two retained letters together.
If these letters are inverses, $y=-x$, then $d=0$. Their initial intervening
run cannot be empty because the input is cyclically freely reduced, so
that run remains nonempty for every $k$. Thus no first cancellation of
retained letters is possible, including at the cyclic seam. Words with
no retained letters are powers of $a$ and are fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Weighted-median line minimization]
For a tuple of cyclically freely reduced relators, total cyclic length
under $\alpha^k$ has the form
\[
 F(k)=C+\sum_t w_t|k-t|,\qquad w_t\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
Each gap with $d\ne0$ contributes one occurrence of $t=-ed$;
all other contributions belong to $C$. If the histogram is nonempty,
the smallest nonnegative minimizer is $k_* =\max(0,t_*)$, where $t_*$ is
the lower weighted median: the first sorted key whose cumulative weight
is at least half the total. For an empty histogram, $k_*=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lemma gives the exact contribution $|e+kd|$ from each gap. For
$d=\pm1$ this is $|k-(-ed)|$. The integer forward difference of a term
$|k-t|$ is $-1$ before $t$ and $+1$ at and after $t$. Summing these
nondecreasing differences gives a convex function. The first nonnegative
forward difference occurs at the lower weighted median; restricting to
$k\ge0$ clips that point at zero.
\end{proof}

Consequently, the exact unit change and optimal change are
\[
 F(1)-F(0)=\sum_t w_t\begin{cases}1&t\le0,\\-1&t>0,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 F(k_*)-F(0)=\sum_t w_t(|k_*-t|-|t|).
\]
When the selected min-cut has negative unit change, $k_*\ge1$ and every
unit step before the first minimum strictly decreases length. The macro
therefore never passes through a length-increasing intermediate cyclic
word. It may choose a different overall search path from recomputing the
best cut after every unit step; the procedure remains a bounded heuristic
for finding a certificate.

\subsection{Computing the histogram on a shared grammar}

For each reachable SLP node store four values: first and last retained
letters (zero if absent), and signed lengths of its prefix and suffix
$a$-runs. A terminal supplies these immediately. For a concatenation $UV$,
the first retained letter is that of $U$, if present, and otherwise that
of $V$; the last letter is determined symmetrically. A prefix run extends
into $V$ exactly when $U$ has no retained letter, and similarly for a suffix.
If both children contain retained letters, their concatenation introduces
one internal gap with exponent
\[
 e=\operatorname{suffix}(U)+\operatorname{prefix}(V).
\]
Initialize root multiplicities from the entire relator list and propagate
them downward in reverse topological order. An internal gap contributes
its node's multiplicity to the histogram. If both child identifiers are
the same, both occurrences are still counted. Finally, add one seam gap
per root having a retained letter. Duplicate relators have separate seams.
Every internal gap is created at exactly one concatenation occurrence in
the expanded parse, so the weighted count is exact.

Let $s$ be the number of reachable nonempty grammar rules and $m$ the
number of roots, counting duplicates. There are at most $s+m$ gap events;
seam gaps for duplicate roots have the same key, so there are at most
$2s$ distinct histogram keys. The metadata, multiplicities and histogram
use $O(s)$ integer records plus the root list. Traversal, sorting and median
selection use $O(s\log(s+1)+m)$ arithmetic operations. Rule lengths are
at most $2^s$, multiplicities at most $m2^s$, and the run-length and count integers
have $O(s+\log(m+1))$ bits. Thus this is polynomial bit complexity in the
compressed input size, with no expanded-word traversal.

The powered images share a binary construction of $a^{k_*}$ and its
inverse. Before reduction, substitution creates only polynomially many
new grammar nodes. Exact compressed free and cyclic reduction use the
previously established word kernel. The producer checks both that the
profile's unit change equals the independent min-cut prediction and that
the actual reduced output length equals the profile's predicted gain.
These checks supplement the proof; the certificate verifier does not trust
any optimization assertion.

\subsection{Adaptive triggering and independently checked certificates}

Computing the full profile is unnecessary when the chosen power is one.
The producer profiles when either the immediately preceding move used the
same multiplier and subset, or current expanded total length exceeds four
times the number of allocated arena rules (including the empty rule and
unreachable historical rules). Both triggers are cheap and exact; the
factor four is a policy choice, not a theorem about optimal scheduling.
When neither trigger fires, it performs the original unit move. Thus the
large-compression example above is batched immediately, while an ordinary
run of repeated moves triggers profiling on its second step. Counters
record profile calls, actual power moves, and maximum exponent bit length.
The explicit producer remains unchanged except that a compressed handoff
can return the new certificate format.

Version 3 adds a \code{whitehead\_power} move with the existing multiplier
and signed subset plus an integer exponent at least two. Booleans, floats,
missing or extra fields, and use in older certificate versions are rejected.
A power of one retains the old \code{whitehead} representation. Versions
1 and 2 remain supported; version 3 also permits version-2 relator moves.
The producer selects the lowest version needed by its trace.

The compressed verifier reconstructs the Wirtinger presentation independently,
validates the automorphism, and independently builds its powered substitution.
It never calls the gap profiler or the producer's image builder. The literal
verifier instead repeats the elementary automorphism, checking resources on
every iteration. A gigantic exponent is rejected by the literal work budget
before attempting that many iterations. Such a resource failure is
inconclusive, not a proof of knottedness or invalidity. Both verifiers check
all surviving relators and the final single generator. Powers can represent
length-increasing moves in externally supplied certificates; the optimizer's
shortening condition is not a prerequisite for a valid automorphism.

\subsection{Validation and the remaining global problem}

The profile is checked against literal elementary substitutions for every
cyclically reduced word of length at most three over three generators, for
all 96 valid multiplier/subset choices. Duplicate roots, empty roots,
all-multiplier words, signed multipliers and the seam are included. A further
400 random tuples check mixed gap distributions. The abstract presentation
above completes in three moves for $h=10,100,500$: one power, one relator
overlap and one generator elimination. Expansion is permitted only after
the relators have total length five. A separate artificially inflated trace
on a small genuine unknot is replayed independently with exponent $2^{100}$;
this tests the verifier and is not evidence of a hard knot family.

These results eliminate one exponential elementary-descent mechanism. They
do not bound the number of changes of Whitehead direction, grammar growth
across such changes, the number of relator moves, or the completeness of
presentation simplification. In particular, the implementation still has
no general subexponential or quasi-polynomial recognition guarantee.
The next useful theoretical target is a global potential controlling
alternating directions and representation growth.

\subsection{Paired measurements and an unchanged knot-corpus regime}

The reproducible driver \code{benchmark\_whitehead\_power.py} loads the
trusted historical producer and verifier from commit
\code{6773a9b6e2ab50cdae07a627397054ce4a522b6d} without changing the checkout.
It compares that baseline, an identical A/A control, and the power-enabled
implementation in shuffled order with five measured repetitions following
one excluded warmup. Each of the fifteen knot inputs is reconstructed
from PD and passed through complete recognition with compressed group
search and independent replay: 225 measured queries, all successful.
A separate thirty measured group probes use adaptive search on Gordian
with letter caps 4096 and 8192; all complete. Group time is capped at
15 seconds, whole recognition at 18 seconds, search work at twenty million
units and arena storage at 100000 nodes.
