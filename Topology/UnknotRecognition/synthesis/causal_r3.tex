\section{Causal RIII search: review and unambiguous certificates}
\label{sec:causal-r3}

The next intake placed three archives in arrival order as reports 26--28:
determinant continuations, causal RIII search, and classical closure resets.
The second archive calls itself ``Report 25'' internally; our collection
number is 27 to avoid colliding with the earlier Garside archive.
Original checksum manifests were verified in scratch before placement,
then omitted under the repository intake rules.  Delivered source and
results remain unchanged apart from the mandated line-ending normalization
of report 28's benchmark CSV.  The retained intake audit records all
original hashes, normalized hashes and source pins.  Local unit reruns pass
35, 7 and 29 tests respectively; these counts do not establish mathematical
soundness or integration with the current recognizer.

\subsection{What the causal argument can give}
Report 27 considers bounded-local reversible moves on a fixed set of sites.
Each move has a footprint containing everything it reads and changes.
Actions on disjoint footprints commute in both directions and preserve
each other's legality.  Terminal reductions also have local footprints.
A move is a \emph{birth} when its footprint is disjoint from the union of
all preceding footprints.  That union is accumulated support, not merely
the footprint of the last move.

Every birth can commute to the front of the trace: it is disjoint from
every earlier action.  The births are pairwise disjoint and can be ordered
canonically.  Remaining actions retain an intersection with the preceding
support.  A reduction may become available sooner after this rearrangement,
in which case the trace is truncated.  Thus one can search a normal form
with a canonical sequence of births followed by connected extensions.
Connectedness of the full dependency graph does not imply that every
chronological prefix was connected.

For RIII on a fixed input diagram, let $D(T)$ be the twelve darts at the
three crossings of a triangular face $T$, and let $\alpha$ be the edge
pairing.  The conservative footprint
\[
 F(T)=D(T)\cup\alpha(D(T)),\qquad |F(T)|\le24
\]
contains every modified pairing.  Inverting the face leaves this footprint
unchanged.  Globally there are $O(N)$ triangular faces in a connected
$N$-crossing spherical diagram.  The number whose footprint meets a set
of $O(k)$ darts is $O(k)$, since only incident crossings and their partners
need inspection.  The report consequently obtains a conditional search
bound
\[
 N^{b+O(1)}(Ck)^k
\]
for traces of length at most $k$ and birth number at most $b$, with an
absolute constant $C$.  A depth-first implementation without a global
memo table uses polynomial workspace in $N,k$.  The delivered abstract
prototype does retain a memo table, so its memory usage should not be
identified with the latter claim.

For example, fixed $b$ and $k=O(\log N/\log\log N)$ give polynomial time;
$b,k=O(\log N)$ give quasi-polynomial time.  These are parameterized
statements.  No argument bounds the parameters uniformly for arbitrary
unknots.  Repeated preprocessing also requires the bound at each episode
selected by the actual deterministic reduction policy; existence of a
different successful sequence of choices is insufficient.

We reran the report's independent breadth-first comparisons in scratch.
The generated audit covers 5,000 six-site systems, with 1,660 witnessed
instances, 3,209 already reducible and 131 without a depth-six witness.
The exhaustive three-site catalog covers 608,400 instances through depth
four, with 171,968 witnessed, 163,800 already reducible and 272,632 without
a witness.  Both reproduce zero discrepancies in the recorded normal-form
checks.  These are abstract Boolean systems, not knot-diagram benchmarks;
they exercise the prototype, not the proposed production adapter.

\subsection{Why immediate inverse pruning needs care}
The proposed RIII adapter suppresses the inverse of the preceding move.
The abstract prototype deliberately does not.  For unrestricted shortest
paths an adjacent inverse pair is removable, but this need not preserve
the birth bound.  Here is an explicit four-site example under precisely
the abstract locality assumptions.  Start with all bits zero:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Move & Footprint & Toggled bits & Guard\\
\midrule
$A$ & $\{0,1,2,3\}$ & $\{0,1\}$ & $x_2=x_3=0$\\
$B$ & $\{0,2\}$ & $\{2\}$ & $x_0=0$\\
$C$ & $\{1,3\}$ & $\{3\}$ & $x_1=0$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each guard avoids the toggled bits, so every legal move is an involution.
The terminal predicate is $x_2=x_3=1$.  The trace $BC$ succeeds but has
two births.  The trace $AABC$ succeeds with one birth: after $AA$ the
physical state is again zero, while accumulated support contains all four
sites.  After the first $A$, both $B$ and $C$ are disabled, so the inverse
$A$ is essential for this one-birth trace.

Exhaustion of all words through length four gives exactly $AABC$ and
$AACB$ as first-unlocking traces with at most one birth.  Suppressing
adjacent inverses loses both.  The script
\path{data/causal_inverse_audit.py} compares this independent enumeration
with the delivered prototype and retains the source hashes and results.
This is an abstract counterexample to the pruning justification; it is
not an actual RIII counterexample.  It shows that locality alone cannot
justify transplanting that extra pruning rule into the claimed complete
parameterized search.  Physical-state memoization likewise cannot ignore
support and search phase.  The existing bounded local simplifier remains
a heuristic; its failure is not a knottedness certificate.

\subsection{Integrated prerequisite: identify the actual triangular face}
Review also exposed a separate defect in the existing production trace
format.  Recording only the three crossing indices does not uniquely
identify an RIII move.  The two-strand braid $(1,1,-1)$ has the PD code
\[
 ((5,4,0,1),(1,0,2,3),(2,4,5,3)).
\]
Both $(11,4,0)$ and $(9,2,6)$ are legal triangular faces on crossings
$\{0,1,2\}$.  Their inversions give distinct labeled PD codes:
\[
\begin{split}
 ((0,1,2,0),(3,4,1,3),(2,4,5,5)),\\
 ((0,1,1,2),(3,4,4,0),(5,5,3,2)).
\end{split}
\]
The old replay routine selected a face according to traversal order and
therefore could reconstruct the wrong intermediate diagram.  Both moves
preserve knot type; the defect concerns reproducibility of the recorded
sequence, including the legality of its subsequent moves.

New RIII records include a \code{triangle} field containing the face's
three input dart indices.  These indices remain fixed after earlier
crossing deletions.  Replay checks distinct, present crossing indices,
literal integer types, range, face membership and RIII legality.  It
recovers cyclic order from the current pairing, so reordering the three
supplied dart indices cannot accidentally request a different reconnection.
Legacy crossing-only RIII records are accepted when they identify a unique
face, and rejected with an ambiguity error otherwise.  R1/R2 serialization
is unchanged.  This is a certificate correctness fix, not a new recognition
criterion or an asymptotic improvement.

Five new regression tests cover the explicit two-face example with all
six dart orders, invalid certificates, every candidate RIII face in
deterministically generated knot diagrams, legacy unambiguous replay, and
JSON round trips after crossing deletions.  The full integrated suite passes
539 tests in 109.018 seconds at that checkpoint; the log is
\path{data/reidemeister-trace-integrated-tests.txt}.

\subsection{Integrated search and adaptive dispatch}
The production module \path{fastunknot/causal_r3.py} now implements the
support-aware normal-form search.  It uses an explicit depth-first stack
with no global memo table.  Each frame owns a journal of at most 24 pairing
entries.  Failure, local trial exhaustion and exceptions restore those
entries and the path; successful first-unlocking traces remain applied.
The retained footprint unions, per-frame candidate sets and pairing journals
use polynomial workspace in $N,k$.  Candidate enumeration polls an optional
resource callback, as do terminal tests and the main search loop.

An additional pruning rule is justified under a narrower hypothesis than
in the delivered adapter.  Suppose an action with footprint $F$ is applied
when the earlier accumulated support is $S$.  Its immediate inverse can be
skipped if $F\subseteq S$.  In that case deleting the pair preserves the
physical state, accumulated support, birth count and last birth key.  It
can only delay the start of the connected phase; all subsequent connected
actions remain legal in the normal form.  The resulting trace is shorter.
If $F\not\subseteq S$, the inverse is retained, including inverses of
initial births.  This distinction preserves the preceding counterexample
and the parameterized completeness argument.

The \code{r3\_search} option accepts \code{last}, \code{clustered}, and
\code{adaptive}; the last-move neighborhood implementation remains the
default.  The clustered implementation searches with a caller-specified
\code{r3\_births} bound.  Adaptive dispatch first gives the last-move search
half the remaining trial allowance.  If it fails to make progress, the
clustered search uses the remaining shared allowance.  When the total trial
limit is disabled, the first phase is still limited to $5N$ trials before
switching.  Each phase uses iterative deepening up to \code{r3\_depth}.
At a depth of at least two, the first move is not filtered by the
last-step face shortcut.  If a completed search at that depth tried zero
nondegenerate moves, there is no legal first RIII move at all.  Both search
implementations stop deepening in that case, and adaptive dispatch also
skips its second phase.  This is a proved dead end for RIII-only search;
it says nothing about crossing-increasing moves or knot type.  In particular,
failure of the face-filtered depth-one search alone does not justify stopping.

Successful reductions trigger another episode, with the same accumulated
trial counter.  Only paths ending in a legal RI/RII reduction are committed.
The independent starting moves and retained support are local to each search
episode, not retained across changes of crossing count.

The API and CLI expose the depth, births and trial allowance.  The existing
\code{use\_r3=False} switch still disables the whole stage.  Global time
exhaustion interrupts either implementation and yields \code{UNKNOWN}; local
trial exhaustion is inconclusive and continues the exact recognition
pipeline.  These options are preserved through certified source-reduction
restarts and passed to each summand.  Recognition records per-implementation
trial counts and switch counts for the new modes.

Eleven new production tests compare bounded searches against unrestricted
all-face enumeration on actual diagrams, verify disjoint births commute,
check exact trace replay, retain inverse moves that add support, and exercise
nested cancellation and injected mutation failures.  They also check the
no-first-move shortcut and deeper recognition of a stored hard unknot.
The full integrated suite passes 550 tests in 98.849 seconds.  Its log is
\path{data/causal-r3-integrated-tests.txt}; the separate focused log is
\path{data/causal-r3-production-tests.txt}.

\subsection{Actual-diagram evidence and limits}
The reproducible discovery run in \path{benchmark_causal_r3.py} uses seed
2703 and tests 12,000 random braid words on four to six strands.  Of these,
2,296 close to knots and 1,537 remain above six crossings after the historical
search at depth four and ten trials per input crossing.  On those 1,537,
each new mode ties the resulting crossing count in 1,529 cases and leaves
more crossings in eight; neither improves it.  This is a negative practical
result for the proposed dispatch at those settings, even though the
unlimited clustered search has a stronger parameterized completeness claim.
Finite budgets and the choice of committed reductions matter.

Another 1,200 generated unknots use the existing conjugation-and-scrambling
constructor with 800 rewriting steps.  All already reduce below seven
crossings with the historical simplifier; none enters the stalled-diagram
comparison.  These are easy examples for existing RIII preprocessing and
supply no evidence of a gain from clustered search.  The data and exact
source hashes are in \path{results/causal_r3_discovery_20261008.json}.
For all 3,496 validated diagrams, the current default simplifier's complete
trace and output PD code also agree exactly with pinned revision
\code{28c974650}; the new no-first-move shortcut changes work, not choices.

The stored hard unknot corpus gives a separate lead: two examples admit
further reductions when the historical search depth is raised from four to
six, using fewer than forty trials.  This benefit comes from greater depth,
not from the new support representation.  Full-pipeline paired measurements
are maintained separately from the discovery counts.  The final run covers
21 inputs, seven shuffled paired rounds and three fresh constructions per
arm, after one untimed warmup.  The baseline loads both the simplifier and
recognizer from revision \code{28c974650}, sharing all other modules;
an identical control arm measures variation.  All recognition results finish
and agree, with every default invariant and structural filter enabled and
a five-second global limit.

At depth six, the historical search improves full recognition time by
$1.71\times$ on stored survivor 0 (sixteen crossings reduced to twelve)
and $3.61\times$ on survivor 1 (fifteen crossings reduced to zero).
The adaptive depth-six arm gives $1.48\times$ and $3.65\times$ respectively;
the second example finishes in its historical phase without switching.
Across all inputs, the depth-six historical arm ranges from $0.907\times$
to $3.605\times$, with median $0.957\times$.  Consequently the default
depth remains four; selective examples do not justify a universal increase.

The final default arm, including stricter option validation, cooperative
deadline checks and the no-first-move shortcut, ranges from $0.945\times$
to $1.061\times$ in full recognition, with median $0.989\times$.
The recognition control ratios range from $0.983\times$ to $1.054\times$.
For survivor 8 the no-first-move shortcut reduces isolated simplification
from about $391$ to $325$ microseconds (paired ratio $1.20\times$), with
little effect on full recognition.  On the empty diagram, validation adds
about $1.34$ microseconds to isolated simplification and $0.56$ microseconds
to recognition.  These costs are retained in the reported measurements.

At depth four, the clustered and adaptive full-recognition median ratios
are $0.942\times$ and $0.931\times$; neither supplies evidence for a default
switch.  Raw simplifier ratios must not be confused with end-to-end speedups:
for example, spending more on simplification can save a larger subsequent
homology computation on the depth-six witnesses.  Inputs, individual samples,
verdicts, methods, per-mode trial counts and exact source hashes are retained
in \path{results/causal_r3_integration_20261008.json}.  These are finite
measurements, not asymptotic evidence.

A local search budget may stop preprocessing inconclusively; it cannot
replace the exact fallback.  The conditional bound still requires bounded
unlocking parameters at each episode selected by the actual reduction
policy.  Neither this integration nor adaptive switching proves a general
sub-exponential or quasi-polynomial recognition theorem.
