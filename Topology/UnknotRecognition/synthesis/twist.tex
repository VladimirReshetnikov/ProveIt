\section{Twist compression and a restricted quasi-polynomial family}
\label{sec:twist-integration}

Report 11 supplies a different way to reduce the exponential dependence:
simplify an entire two-strand twist before constructing the closed chain
complex. We have integrated this backend as an explicit option, after
checking its local model, degree convention, and production comparisons.
Unlike component contraction, which changes one morphism product, this
replacement reduces the number of terms in the selected complex.
The parameter is the number of signed runs in a supplied braid word.
It is neither the minimal braid index nor an invariant of an arbitrary
PD presentation.

\subsection{Local replacement and exact assembly}
Write the braid on $b$ strands as
\[
 \beta=\sigma_{i_1}^{\epsilon_1m_1}\cdots
        \sigma_{i_t}^{\epsilon_tm_t},
 \qquad m_j>0,\quad \epsilon_j\in\{-1,1\},\quad n=\sum_jm_j.
\]
The production adapter groups maximal equal signed letters. It does not
perform a hidden search for a shorter braid presentation. The standalone
run API also accepts binary-encoded exponents, but every complexity bound
below is in the expanded crossing count $n$, not their encoding length.

Let $I$ be the identity smoothing and $E$ the turnback. A positive
$m$-crossing twist has the local representative
\[
 I\xrightarrow{s}E\xrightarrow{D}E\xrightarrow{D}\cdots
 \xrightarrow{D}E,\qquad D=X_{\mathrm{top}}+X_{\mathrm{bottom}},
\]
with terms in degrees $0,\ldots,m$. The negative twist is its dual,
in degrees $-m,\ldots,0$, with the saddle entering the final $I$.
This is the characteristic-two, quantum-ungraded specialization of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.07525}{Thompson's Proposition 4.1}
(version 2, December 2018), using Bar-Natan's local delooping and
cancellation. It is a homotopy equivalence relative to the boundary,
so inserting it into the surrounding braid preserves homology.
The integer dot sums and differences become the same map over
$\mathbb F_2$. This locality theorem, not merely a $d^2=0$ check,
justifies replacing the crossing cube.

A macro state chooses $k_j\in\{0,\ldots,m_j\}$ in each block.
Its homological degree is $\sum_j\epsilon_j k_j$, and its geometry
depends only on the support $S=\{j:k_j>0\}$. The implementation closes
the corresponding $I/E$ picture on a $(t+1)$-by-$b$ grid and caches
its circle partition by $S$. A marked closing arc fixes one circle's
label to $X$ in $\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^2)$; the other circles carry binary
labels. A state with $c(S)$ circles therefore contributes $2^{c(S)-1}$
reduced basis elements.

Transitions at the end of a run use the multiplication or
comultiplication saddle. Internal transitions apply the two dot maps.
If the top and bottom arcs lie on one circle their dot contributions
cancel; a dot on the marked circle vanishes. Different blocks commute,
so cross terms cancel in characteristic two. Exact bit-column
elimination computes each boundary rank and hence each homology degree.
The existing rank-one reduced Khovanov detector then decides the knot.

\subsection{Counting the selected complex}
For each support $S$, precisely $\prod_{j\in S}m_j$ states have that
support. Thus the full reduced chain dimension is exactly
\begin{equation}
 N=\sum_{S\subseteq[t]}2^{c(S)-1}\prod_{j\in S}m_j.
 \label{eq:twist-exact-N}
\end{equation}
This is a chain dimension, not a Betti number. The same formula with
$m_j$ replaced by $z^{\epsilon_j}+\cdots+z^{m_j\epsilon_j}$ gives
the dimension in every degree. The Temperley--Lieb trace of
$\prod_j(I+m_jE_{i_j})$ at loop value two, divided by two for the mark,
computes $N$ without expanding the run lengths. This provides the
adapter's exact size preflight. A separate polynomial transfer computes
the degree profile using sliding windows for these consecutive powers.

Here is the useful structural bound from report 11. If $d(S)$ counts
distinct generator indices active in $S$, then
\[
 c(S)\le b+|S|-2d(S).
\]
Begin with $b$ vertical circles and activate turnbacks in word order.
At the first use of index $i$, no prior connection crosses the cut
between positions $i$ and $i+1$. Its two arcs are therefore in different
circles, and that saddle must merge them. Every other activation can
increase the circle count by at most one. Counting the $d(S)$ forced
merges proves the inequality; its slack is an even nonnegative integer.

Set $Q_i=\prod_{j:i_j=i}(1+2m_j)$. Inserting the bound into
\eqref{eq:twist-exact-N} and grouping subsets by their generator gives
\[
 N\le 2^{b-1}\prod_{i=1}^{b-1}
       \left(1+\frac{Q_i-1}{4}\right)
   =U:=\prod_{i=1}^{b-1}\frac{Q_i+3}{2}.
\]
A knot closure uses every generator index: otherwise its strand
permutation preserves the two nonempty sides of an unused cut.
Consequently $Q_i\ge3$, $b\le t+1$, and, for $t>0$,
\begin{equation}
 N\le U\le\prod_j(1+2m_j)
       \le\left(1+\frac{2n}{t}\right)^t.
 \label{eq:twist-run-bound}
\end{equation}
The last step is arithmetic--geometric mean. The bound is sharp for
two-strand presentations and when each generator appears in just one
block. It is not a bound on the smallest possible presentation of a
given knot.

There are at most $N$ macro states, each with polynomial bookkeeping
in $n+b$. Differential matrices have at most $N$ rows and columns,
and their bit elimination costs at most a fixed polynomial times
$N^3$, with storage at most a fixed polynomial times $N^2$. Therefore
the uncapped algorithm satisfies
\[
 T\le\operatorname{poly}(n+b)N^3
   =\operatorname{poly}(n)2^{O(t\log(2+n/t))}.
\]
In particular, for every fixed $c$, supplied knot braids with
$t\le c\log(n+2)$ admit a uniform $2^{O(\log^2(n+2))}$ bound.
Fixed $t$ gives a polynomial whose exponent may depend on $t$; this
is not an FPT claim. Finite allocation and time limits can still cause
an invocation to return \code{UNKNOWN}. No algorithm here constructs
such a short-run braid for every knot diagram.

\subsection{A sharper degree-sensitive bound}
The actual implementation eliminates each homological degree separately.
Writing $d_h=\dim C^h$, define
\[
 M=\sum_h d_hd_{h+1},\qquad
 W=\sum_h d_hd_{h+1}\min(d_h,d_{h+1}).
\]
There are at most $d_h\min(d_h,d_{h+1})$ column reductions for the
$h$th differential, each touching at most $d_{h+1}$ bits. Thus $W$
bounds their total XOR bit work; reading columns and bookkeeping add
at least $O(N)$ work and polynomial factors in index bit lengths.
$M$ is the dense coefficient-bit storage bound. These more informative
quantities are reported by both the assembly and degree-profile
preflight as \code{matrix\_bit\_upper\_bound} and
\code{rank\_xor\_bit\_upper\_bound}. They are analytical bounds,
not sampled CPU times or process-memory measurements.

This gives an additional consequence for a single lengthening block.
Hold the strand count, block positions, signs, and all $m_j$ except
$m_r$ fixed. For any assignment of the other local indices and any
degree $h$, the equation $\sum_j\epsilon_jk_j=h$ determines at most
one value of $k_r$. Every state has dimension at most $2^{b+t-1}$.
Hence
\[
 d_h\le 2^{b+t-1}\prod_{j\ne r}(m_j+1),
\]
independent of $m_r$. The number of degrees and macro states is
$O(m_r+1)$, while the finite list of support geometries and local maps
is unchanged. It follows that $N,M,W=O(m_r+1)$, with constants depending
on the fixed surrounding word. The code therefore has linear word-level
work in this family, with logarithmic factors for index arithmetic in
a conservative bit model. This conclusion is substantially sharper
than substituting a linear $N$ into the generic cubic bound.

For a single positive two-strand twist, explicitly $d_0=2$ and
$d_1=\cdots=d_m=1$. Thus $N=m+2$, the peak degree dimension is two,
and $M=W=m+1$. Tests compare these closed formulas to independently
computed profiles and assembled complexes at lengths 3, 31, and 301.
This is a homology-computation improvement; recognition already has
the faster elementary two-strand certificate.

\subsection{Integration, provenance, and failure semantics}
\code{backend="twist"} or \code{--backend twist} selects this backend
after the existing reduction, factorization, and invariant stages.
The direct rank CLI uses \code{khovanov --twist}. The adapter accepts
only retained, checked source-braid provenance. If the entire diagram
was simplified, its original braid still represents the same knot.
If factorization produced several diagrams, that braid is never passed
to an individual factor. Cheap tests may still decide a factor and thus
the sum. Remaining factors are recorded as inconclusive with
\code{twist\_deferred}; if necessary the backend then decides the
original whole braid once, with its rank recorded only at the top level.
This avoids losing the restricted run-count bound by sending factors
without braid provenance to an unrestricted scanner. Ordinary PD or grid
input has no source word at all and uses the standard scanner, recording
\code{twist\_skipped}. The raw twist-rank API instead
rejects input without provenance.

The macro degree convention differs from the scanner's cube degree:
\[
 h_{\mathrm{macro}}=n_+-h_{\mathrm{cube}},\qquad
 \dim H_{\mathrm{unred}}^{h_{\mathrm{cube}}}
       =2\dim H_{\mathrm{red}}^{h_{\mathrm{macro}}}.
\]
Here $n_+$ counts positive letters of the \emph{original source braid}.
The adapter converts its output to that original cube grading and
labels the evidence explicitly, even when the current PD has fewer
crossings. A guessed shift or a factor's crossing count would be wrong.
Normalized macro degrees are also retained for inspection.

The adapter first rejects a macro-state count exceeding its budget,
then computes the exact reduced basis count by Temperley--Lieb transfer.
Only a successful count within the basis ceiling permits matrix assembly.
Preflight and assembly consume one common time budget. The preflight has
separate limits of 4096 matchings and 256 strands; exhausting a guard
raises a resource limit, not a verdict. The all-$I$ state's dimension
also provides an immediate lower bound before any large geometry is
allocated. The state-count guard uses bounded multiplication, avoiding
construction and decimal formatting of enormous products for succinct
run inputs.

The production recognition option \code{twist\_max\_basis} defaults
to one million, independently of the standard scanner's object ceiling.
The raw \code{Budget} also controls macro states, matrix bits, XORs,
and time. Matrix-bit limits concern coefficient capacity rather than
Python resident memory. Allocation/time exhaustion yields
\code{UNKNOWN} in recognition and the CLI, and \code{ScanLimit} or
\code{MemoryError} in the raw production adapter. The separate
\code{fastunknot.twist} module retains the advanced signed-run,
homology, and preflight interfaces from the report.

For context, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02119}{Kelom\"aki and
Sch\"utz (2026)} give a polynomial homology algorithm for all
three-braids and show exponential behavior of ordinary scanning on
simple three-braid families. The run-count bound above is a different
restriction and does not subsume their result. Our existing three-braid
recognition shortcut already decides that knot class without computing
its homology; it remains ahead of this optional backend.

\subsection{Production checks and observed scaling}
The full production suite passes 209 tests, including the report's 62
local algebra/topology tests and new production integration regressions.
A seeded corpus of 100 knot braid closures on two through five strands
agrees with the existing scanner in every raw cube degree, with
$d^2=0$ checked in both engines. The earlier 200-case production
cross-check remains recorded separately. These are comparisons of
presentations, not counts of distinct knots. New tests also exercise
mirrors, whole-diagram simplification, proper-factor deferral, cheap
factor obstructions, missing provenance, huge encoded counts, exact
preflight rejection, shared time accounting, and CLI resource handling.

Seven randomized paired rounds compare fresh raw homology computations,
with a second standard-scanner arm as an A/A control. Diagram creation
and input loading are outside the timed interval; scanner ordering and
twist size preflight are inside. Every timed computation must agree in
rank and every original cube degree. The results are:

\toprule
Input family & Size parameter & Scanner/twist & A/A\\
\midrule
Single two-strand twist & 51 & 31.61 & 1.013\\
 & 201 & 134.74 & 0.973\\
 & 801 & 574.45 & 1.004\\
Fixed four-strand context & 3 & 0.19 & 1.012\\
 & 15 & 0.30 & 1.002\\
 & 51 & 0.48 & 0.995\\
 & 201 & 1.26 & 1.016\\
 & 801 & 4.29 & 1.024\\
Alternating three-braid, no run compression & 8 crossings & 0.22 & 0.995\\
Morton's four-strand unknot & 11 crossings & 0.06 & 0.972\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The four-strand word is
$\sigma_1^m\sigma_2\sigma_3^{-1}\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1}$
with odd $m$. Its measured chain dimension is $N=177m+234$,
verified by exact assembly and the independent preflight in each sample.
Its peak degree dimension is 177 for the listed $m\ge15$, while the
small $m=3$ example peaks at 160. Thus the code exhibits the fixed-context
bound above; this finite sample is not a proof of the general bound.
The larger context measurements were run separately after the first
corpus, with the same paired protocol.

The raw scanner took approximately 2.302 seconds on the 801-crossing
two-strand case, compared with 0.004 seconds for the macro adapter.
These are \emph{homology} timings: the default recognition pipeline
already decides all the long-twist examples by its braid obstruction.
The alternating control is about 4.5 times slower under the macro
backend, and the Morton example about 15.8 times slower. These regressions
preclude an unconditional default switch. In particular, a small run
count gives a useful asymptotic theorem but need not beat the scanner's
cancellations at a particular finite size.
