\section{Polynomial projectors, certified local algebras, and static reuse}
\label{sec:primary-continuation}

\subsection{What the new algebraic path changes}
The projector-and-seam delivery extends the existing scalar Fitting search.
For a component $C$ of the live cobordism complex, let $R$ be its complete
algebra of scalar chain endomorphisms over $\mathbb F_2$. Scalar blocks mix
only objects with the same matching, homological degree and recovered quantum
shift. The solver imposes $Qd=dQ$ separately for every morphism coefficient,
including every attachment in the component. It does not discard the
non-scalar coefficients of $d$ when constructing those equations.

The old search tries a bounded deterministic list of elements $Q\in R$.
A sufficiently large power gives the Fitting decomposition
$C=\ker Q^k\oplus\operatorname{im}Q^k$. This discovers a proper summand when
both parts are nonzero, but every invertible candidate has trivial kernel.
An invertible candidate can nevertheless have several distinct irreducible
primary factors. The optional \code{primary} backend tests for the associated
polynomial projectors after the ordinary Fitting test fails. A nilpotent or
identity candidate cannot supply such a projector and skips this extra work.
The component, variable and candidate caps remain unchanged.

\subsection{A complete polynomial test for one candidate}
Let $D$ be the number of scalar coordinates and $\mu_Q$ the minimal polynomial
of the direct sum of the typed blocks. The first linear dependence among
$I,Q,\ldots,Q^D$ determines $\mu_Q$ exactly. Flattening the blocks preserves
all matrix entries, so an earlier annihilating polynomial would have caused
an earlier dependence. The evaluation map
\[
 \mathbb F_2[t]/(\mu_Q)\longrightarrow R,\qquad p\longmapsto p(Q)
\]
is injective. Write $\mu_Q=\prod_{i=1}^s f_i^{e_i}$ with distinct irreducible
$f_i$. Each Chinese-remainder factor $\mathbb F_2[t]/(f_i^{e_i})$ is local.
Its only idempotents are zero and one: for an idempotent $e$, either $e$ or
$1-e$ is a unit, giving $e=1$ or $e=0$ respectively. Therefore
\[
 \mathcal B_Q=\{p\bmod\mu_Q:p^2=p\}
 \cong\mathbb F_2^s.
\]
This is Berlekamp's classical fixed-algebra construction~\cite{berlekamp-primary},
including repeated irreducible factors. In characteristic two, squaring is
linear in this commutative quotient. The implementation finds the nullspace
of the columns $t^{2j}+t^j\bmod\mu_Q$. A basis element other than $0,1$ gives
an actual nonzero, nonidentity idempotent $p(Q)$.

Discovery uses retained packed powers. A separate positive checker uses
Horner evaluation, checks an annihilator, verifies the displayed projector
polynomial and squares the actual matrices. A positive witness does not need
to establish minimality of the displayed annihilator. The scanner additionally
checks the full commutator, the invertibility of the splitting bases and every
transformed cross term. Thus the accepted decomposition preserves all typed
attachments. Elementary methods take $O(D^4)$ binary operations and $O(D^3)$
bits for one polynomial-projector query; building the complete commutant and
trying multiple candidates are separate costs.

For example, $f=t^3+t+1$ and $g=t^3+t^2+1$ are irreducible. The invertible
matrix $C_f\oplus C_g$ has no ordinary stable-kernel split, while
$p=t^4+t^2+t$ satisfies $p\equiv0\pmod f$, $p\equiv1\pmod g$ and projects
onto the second summand. This example explains the stronger test without
assuming that such a matrix occurs in every difficult knot scan.

\subsection{A new stopping certificate for the entire scalar algebra}
A failed polynomial test usually says only that this particular $Q$ offers
no polynomial projector. The maintained continuation adds an exception that
can be proved complete for the current scalar algebra. Let
$a=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}R$. If
\[
 \deg\mu_Q=a\quad\hbox{and}\quad\dim\mathcal B_Q=1,
\]
then the injective evaluation map above is surjective by dimension equality.
Consequently $R=\mathbb F_2[Q]$ is a local algebra. It contains no nontrivial
idempotents, so no subsequent scalar candidate can split this component.
The remaining candidate trials can be skipped exactly.

The implementation uses the complete commutant dimension, not the number of
candidates sampled. It rechecks that the generator commutes with the complete
differential, and records the minimal polynomial, degree, candidate matrices
and algebra dimension in the search metrics. A repeated irreducible factor
is allowed: the algebra can have nilpotents and still be local. Conversely,
two copies of a field admit a primary candidate of small degree while their
full commutant is a matrix algebra containing projections. The degree equality
is essential and is tested explicitly.

This certificate concerns the specified scalar endomorphism algebra of the
current complex. It is not a claim that no more general cobordism operation,
homotopy reduction or future crossing extension can simplify the complex.
Unlike the positive projector checker, replay of this negative certificate
must justify completeness of the commutant and minimality of $\mu_Q$.
The current code obtains both from exact linear solves; the saved numerical
dimensions alone would not suffice as an external proof.

\subsection{Transporting complete child spaces without changing the search}
The delivery proposes, but does not implement, static reuse of corner algebras.
We implement it behind \code{fitting\_reuse\_commutant=True}. Suppose a verified
split gives $C=\bigoplus_i C_i$, with inclusion $u_i$, projection $v_i$, and
$e_i=u_iv_i$. Every scalar endomorphism of $C_i$ extends by zero to $C$;
conversely $r\in R$ restricts to $v_i r u_i$. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{End}_{\rm scalar}(C_i)\cong e_i R e_i,
 \qquad \sum_i\dim(e_iRe_i)\le\dim R.
\]
Conjugating a complete parent basis by the accepted change of coordinates and
restricting it to a child therefore spans the entire child commutant.
No new equations against the differential need be solved for that child.

A basis change in the commutant could otherwise change the first sampled
candidates. To avoid this confounder, the transported spanning set is reduced
to the unique basis with increasing least-bit pivots and zero coefficients
at the other pivot positions. That is precisely the existing nullspace
solver's basis in the same variable ordering. Direct tests compare the whole
basis, candidate count, every split witness and final complex against fresh
solving. The two implementations consequently follow the same search, apart
from the common certified stopping rule.

Transport is lazy: size skips, exhausted split allowances and cache hits do
not construct a child space. A cached positive witness does not contain a
complete parent basis, so its children use fresh solves. Basis reuse stays
inside one static compression call; adding a crossing or changing the
differential starts from fresh spaces. Failed transport leaves the current
exact differential intact. The option remains off by default because avoiding
a solve is not itself a runtime advantage: transporting and canonicalizing a
large basis can cost more than solving a small child's equations.

The sum-of-dimensions bound gives at most $\min(a,D)$ nonzero leaves in a
proper static refinement tree. A straightforward simultaneous transport has
$O(aD^3)$ cost per level and $O(aD^4)$ over such a tree, before candidate
search, dependency elimination and differential transport. These bounds are
in the explicit live dimension, which remains uncontrolled in terms of the
original crossing count. They provide no general quasi-polynomial theorem.

\subsection{Validation and measurement protocol}
The transferred kernel passes exhaustive binary-polynomial tests through
degree six, independently evaluated small matrix minimal polynomials,
repeated-primary-factor cases, corrupted-witness rejection and full-differential
conjugation checks. Public API and CLI tests exercise exact ranks, capped
recognition, incompatible options and fallback. Additional maintained tests
compare 200 canonical spans against fresh nullspaces and audit transported
child spaces on multiple algebraic families and genuine knot scans. They
cover cache hits, split-budget ordering and interruption inside transport.
An exhaustive small-commutant test enumerates every endomorphism for each
accepted local-algebra stop and confirms that its only idempotents are zero
and identity. A repeated-field control has a primary nongenerator but still
splits, excluding the unsound shortcut of stopping after any primary candidate.

The maintained \path{benchmark_primary_continuation.py} pins the original
Fitting module at \code{437b48c7f} and the transferred primary module at
\code{74446633a}. The five arms are original Fitting, an identical A/A control,
delivered primary search, maintained primary with the local-algebra stop,
and maintained primary with both the stop and static corner reuse. Every
source hash is checked before and after timing. Five shuffled rounds with
two calls per batch follow one excluded warmup per arm; the seed is 26100879.
Raw knot scans use one common prepared order and compare complete graded
ranks with the ordinary Khovanov scanner. Ordering and diagram construction
are outside those timers. Algebraic search includes the commutant solve,
all candidate work and the verified basis change; complete compression also
includes recursive splitting and sharing. Fixture construction is outside
both timers. The latter inputs are graded algebraic complexes, not established
classical-knot prefixes. Identical final primary witnesses and representations
are checked across the delivered and two maintained primary arms.

The final integrated gate passes all 748 tests in 179.037 seconds with Regina
available. The benchmark completes 950 measured calls and excludes 95 warmup
calls. There are 250 raw knot scans and 350 calls each for algebraic search and
recursive compression. The supplementary field audit checks 320 exact candidate
pairs and 240 field elements by an independent Frobenius-orbit calculation.
Its uniform-sampling probabilities are not attributed to the deterministic
production candidate policy.

On the dense degree-eight family, original Fitting exhausts 32 candidates
without splitting. The delivered primary kernel finds a parent split after two
candidates, but its recursive pass still examines 46 candidates in total.
The maintained local-algebra certificate reduces that total to six, including
three polynomial-projector calls and two certified stops. Complete-compression
medians fall from 18.685 milliseconds for delivered primary to 9.836 for the
maintained continuation, with median paired ratio 1.905. Both retain exactly
the same final complex with 75 differential entries, compared with 142 before
the parent split. This is a gain in complete recursive compression, beyond
merely the already faster search for its first split.

The degree-ten family likewise reduces 52 total candidates to six and retains
102 entries instead of the original 221. The degree-twelve family reduces
58 candidates to six and has delivered/maintained medians 66.129/32.529
milliseconds, with median paired ratio 2.033. Degree twelve raises the scalar
variable allowance above the production default, which must remain explicit
when interpreting the result. The degree-eight bridge fixture has paired ratio
2.849. Timing variation is substantial in the degree-ten samples, including
the identical Fitting control, so its raw samples and paired statistics are
retained rather than turning a ratio of medians into a precision claim.

Corner transport lowers fresh commutant builds from three to one on each
split two-field fixture, but slows every measured complete-compression family.
For dense degree eight it takes 15.048 milliseconds rather than 9.836; its
median paired primary/corner ratio is 0.655. The canonical-basis checks show
that this is transport cost, not a different search outcome. Thus the
polynomial-projector backend enables the local-algebra stop, while corner
reuse remains a separately selectable experimental option. Raw measurements
