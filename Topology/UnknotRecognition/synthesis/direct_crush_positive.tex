\section{Direct crushing of positive rays with classified point repair}
\label{sec:direct-crush-positive}

The positive outputs of the maintained Euler LP, tree and transversal
searches already have the extremality needed by the crushing constructor.
The driver now tries that constructor directly.  It performs the more
general positive-point extraction only when the constructor identifies
nonprimitive scale or a higher-dimensional positive support.  The original
matching equations and the independent quotient checker still decide
whether a step may enter the diagram-bound chain.

This changes the unconditional standard-coordinate extraction described in
Section~\ref{sec:euler-lp-crushing}.  The generic Q-coordinate extraction
inside the polynomial LP fallback remains necessary and unchanged.  So does
the extraction used to obtain a ray from an expanded-edge vertex link in
Section~\ref{sec:vertex-reduction}.  The change removes a redundant algebraic
pass on native positive rays; it supplies neither a new discovery theorem
nor a bound on the number of admissibility branches.

\subsection{Canonical Q rays lift to full matching-cone rays}

Let $A$ be the original standard matching matrix of a validated finite
triangulation with $t$ tetrahedra.  Write
\[
 K_{\mathrm{std}}=\{x\in\mathbb R^{7t}:Ax=0,\ x\ge0\},
 \qquad K_Q=\{q\in\mathbb R^{3t}:Cq=0,\ q\ge0\},
\]
where $C$ is obtained by eliminating triangle coordinates, and let $\pi$
denote projection to quadrilateral coordinates.  These cones do not impose
the quadrilateral compatibility condition.  Compatibility is checked
separately before interpreting a vector as an embedded normal surface.

For each global vertex $v$, let $L_v$ be its triangle-only vertex-link
vector.  Triangle-only matching vectors are precisely the linear
combinations of these vectors: equality of triangle counts propagates
across every face identification of a corner at the same global vertex.
The supports of the different $L_v$ are disjoint.  The canonical lift
$X(q)$ subtracts the minimum triangle potential independently at each
vertex.  It is nonnegative and has a zero triangle coordinate at every
global vertex.  In particular $\pi(K_{\mathrm{std}})=K_Q$.

For a mask $J$ of retained quadrilateral types, define
\[
 K_J=\{q\in K_Q:q_j=0\text{ for }j\notin J\}.
\]
It is a coordinate face of $K_Q$, even when $J$ retains several types in
one tetrahedron.  If $q=u+w$ in $K_Q$ and $q\in K_J$, nonnegativity forces
both summands to vanish outside $J$.  Therefore an extreme ray of $K_J$
is an extreme ray of $K_Q$.  This applies to masks reached by branching,
matching-support deletion, and transversal candidate restrictions.

\begin{proposition}[Canonical lifting with any finite number of vertices]
Let $q\ne0$ generate an extreme ray of $K_Q$.  Its canonical lift $X(q)$
generates an extreme ray of $K_{\mathrm{std}}$.  If $q$ is primitive
integral, then $X(q)$ is integral and primitive.  No one-vertex hypothesis
is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $X(q)=y+z$ with $y,z\in K_{\mathrm{std}}$.  Projecting gives
$q=\pi(y)+\pi(z)$, so Q extremality gives $\pi(y)=\alpha q$ and
$\pi(z)=(1-\alpha)q$ for some $0\le\alpha\le1$.  The signed matching
vector $y-\alpha X(q)$ is triangle-only, hence
\[
 y-\alpha X(q)=\sum_v a_v L_v.
\]
At each vertex choose a zero triangle coordinate of $X(q)$.  Nonnegativity
of $y$ and $z$ makes both coordinates zero there.  Since only $L_v$ has a
nonzero entry at that corner, $a_v=0$.  Thus all coefficients vanish and
the original decomposition is proportional.

For integrality, each triangle matching equation equates two triangle
coordinates up to an integral linear combination of Q coordinates.
Choose a root potential zero in each corner-identification component and
propagate along a spanning forest.  Integral $q$ gives integral potentials;
the equations $Cq=0$ ensure consistency around the remaining edges.
Subtracting an integral minimum at each vertex gives an integral canonical
lift.  Finally, any common divisor of all entries of $X(q)$ divides all
entries of $q$.  Primitive $q$ therefore makes $X(q)$ primitive.
\end{proof}

The zero at \emph{every} vertex is essential to the argument.  A zero at
one vertex cannot eliminate a vertex-link summand at another.  Conversely,
a nonnegative full-standard extreme ray with nonzero quadrilateral content
must have a triangle zero at every vertex: if all corners at $v$ were
positive, a positive multiple of $L_v$ could be subtracted, leaving a
nonnegative matching vector with nonzero quadrilateral content.  Those two
summands would contradict extremality.

\subsection{Why the native positive outputs satisfy the stronger invariant}

For the simplex policy, the Q problem uses the normalized cone
$q\ge0$, $Cq=0$, $\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}q\le1$.  The paired matching
inequalities and their slack variables determine the slacks uniquely from
$q$.  Thus a basic feasible solution projects to a vertex of that
normalized cone.  A nonzero vertex lies on an extreme Q ray.  Stopping at
the first strictly positive objective does not change this basis property;
primitive rescaling then gives an integral Q ray.

The polynomial policy has an additional obligation because its auxiliary
box can create vertices unrelated to Q extremality.  As proved in
Section~\ref{sec:exact-polynomial-lp}, the fallback performs original-kernel
support descent before returning, and checks support nullity one and its
legacy basis data.  This Q extraction remains part of the LP solver.
The Euler oracle subsequently evaluates the actual canonical Euler value
and accepts a normal-surface result only after quadrilateral compatibility
has been checked.  A positive envelope objective alone is insufficient.

Tree and transversal calls solve on coordinate masks of the same source
cone.  The face argument above transfers their returned Q extremality to
the full source.  Their canonical compatible positive lifts therefore
meet the full-standard extremality theorem.  This is an invariant of the
maintained producers, not an extra assertion trusted from their packets.
The public positive Euler certificate deliberately permits a canonical
positive point that is not extreme.  Its verifier continues to establish
matching, compatibility, canonicality, primitive Q coordinates and
positive Euler, without claiming connectedness or extremality.

\subsection{Original-support validation and the narrow repair branch}

The constructor \path{crush_normal_ray} first validates the finite source,
exact nonnegative coordinates, every original matching equation,
quadrilateral compatibility, positive Euler and nonzero quadrilateral
content.  It then checks the gcd and the original positive-support rank.
For $S=\{j:x_j>0\}$, the required equality is
\[
 \operatorname{rank}A_{[:,S]}=|S|-1.
\]
Indeed $x_S$ is strictly positive, so it lies in the relative interior of
the nonnegative cone in $\ker A_{[:,S]}$.  Nullity one gives a ray; larger
nullity allows small nonproportional perturbations in both directions.
No objective equality, normalization row or extra restriction is inserted
into this rank check.  A nonzero matching vector also proves that the rank
cannot exceed $|S|-1$.

Two private subclasses of the existing geometry validation error identify
the repairable stages.  The constructor raises
\path{_NonPrimitiveNormalPoint} at the gcd check and
\path{_NonExtremeNormalPoint} only for support nullity greater than one.
An impossible rank greater than $|S|-1$ raises an arithmetic invariant
error.  Both classifications occur before quotient construction.  Neither
classification establishes canonicality; the first does not establish
extremality either.

The driver's local \path{apply_crush} permits repair only for positive
results from LP, tree and transversal discovery.  Its protected exception
scope contains just the first constructor call.  If one of the two
classified errors occurs, \path{extract_positive_ray} revalidates the
point, including canonical zeros at every vertex, and obtains a primitive
positive ray by support descent.  A single constructor retry uses that
ray.  Exceptions from extraction or the retry propagate; they do not
trigger further repair.  Malformed coordinates, failed matching,
nonpositive Euler, triangle-only input, and quotient failures are not
classified as recoverable.

This retains the previous ability to accept a canonical positive
nonextreme point or a positive integral rescaling.  It also retains the
rejection of vertex-link contamination that removes a required canonical
zero.  It does not narrow the standalone positive Euler certificate
language or relax the standalone crushing constructor.

After either successful constructor call, the independent crushing checker
reconstructs the full original-support system and verifies primitivity,
membership and extremality using its separate right-pivot kernel code.
It reconstructs the labelled quotient and component census independently.
The driver checks strict successor size descent before appending a step;
all of this occurs outside the repair catch.  Final diagram-bound replay
is unchanged, including the authenticated knot-exterior premise needed
to interpret loss of the torus.  No certificate schema changes.

\subsection{Cancellation, shared budgets and retained extraction}

The outer driver wraps every caller callback exception in its private
\texttt{BaseException} carrier.  Consequently even a caller-raised instance
of a repair-classification exception cannot enter the repair branch.
The outer wrapper restores the original exception object and traceback.
The same guarded callback is passed through extraction, retry and
independent verification.  No generic validation-error catch is added.

The shared reduction allowance is checked before the attempted crush or
repair.  If it is exhausted, the returned partial chain contains only
previously accepted steps.  The candidate has supplied no new topological
claim.  Moving this check before the former unconditional extraction can
reduce callback work and extraction statistics on that inconclusive path;
it preserves neither an exact callback count nor validation of the
unaccepted candidate.  Discovery work already performed remains charged,
and no unappended crush is charged as a successful reduction.
The existing \path{extraction_steps} statistic remains present for these
search modes and counts actual fallback support steps.  On genuine native
primitive ray outputs it is zero.

Corner enumeration already supplied rays directly and keeps that path.
Expanded-edge vertex reduction keeps its own positive-point extraction:
the raw or peeled link can have higher-dimensional support.  Its extracted
ray has a full-standard extremality guarantee and need not be Q extreme.
The lifting theorem therefore licenses no removal of that extraction.

\subsection{The precise cost improvement and its limits}

For a genuine native positive ray, the previous standard-coordinate
extraction had zero support-descent steps.  It nevertheless prepared the
source, performed input and output coordinate censuses, compiled the Euler
functional, built a source kernel chart, and normalized the result.  Since
the input was already primitive, the output was the same coordinate vector.
The subsequent crushing constructor repeated left-pivot elimination on
the same original positive-support matrix.  The direct path removes the
first of these passes; it removes no LP solve.

There are $O(t)$ matching rows and at most $7t$ columns, or at most $5t$
positive columns for compatible data.  The dense elimination used here has
an $O(t^3)$ rational-arithmetic upper bound.  Before the common final-chain
replay, the old native-ray path performed three principal support
eliminations: the extractor's chart, the constructor's rank check, and the
independent checker's chart.  The new path performs the last two.  Their
coefficient and coordinate bit bounds, established for the original
matching systems, are unchanged.  The old native-ray path already had
zero tangent descent, so comparing a generic higher-dimensional extraction
bound against this direct path would overstate the improvement.

Thus this is removal of additive polynomial work, not a proved smaller
per-ray time or space exponent.  A genuinely nonextreme fallback pays for
its initial failed rank check as well as the extraction and retry.  A
nonprimitive fallback pays for the failed scale check before extraction.
Neither exceptional path has an improved worst-case bound.  The number
of LP queries, branching masks, transversal candidates and authenticated
crushing stages is unaffected on the native successful path.  No general
quasi-polynomial recognition bound, resolving-parameter monotonicity,
empirical speedup, or timing result is asserted by this argument.

\subsection{Regression controls and reproducible publication}

Seven focused constructor tests distinguish recoverable scale and support
failures from invalid matching data, incompatible types, malformed literal
coordinates, nonpositive input and an impossible computed rank.  The genuine
canonical point
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&2&2&1&0&0\\
 1&1&0&0&3&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}
\]
on the boundary-capped one-tetrahedron torus has support nullity two.
Its extraction takes one support-descent step and yields an independently
replayable crush.  Adding a positive vertex-link summand still fails the
extractor's canonicality check.  Primitive rays scaled by $2$ or $2^{20000}$
exercise normalization without decimal expansion of large integers.
Caller exception identity is checked through the constructor, including
instances of the new classification errors.

Twelve driver controls exercise the shortcut and its repair boundary.
Genuine circle recognition under Bland with anchors or the envelope shortcut,
and under tree and transversal search, succeeds with driver extraction
disabled.  Actual polynomial fallback under both LP strategies is tested on the
tiny supplied torus source.  Each of these six configurations gives exactly
the same full returned result as a comparison wrapper that performs the
removed real extraction before every crush.  Its extracted vector equals
the supplied primitive vector and its descent count is zero.  These are
functional comparisons, not timings.

The nonextreme two-tetrahedron point above is repaired exactly once in each
of the LP, tree and transversal paths.  These controls deliberately substitute
the point after a successful native query.  In the LP control its independently
accepted positive Euler packet explicitly contains the nonextreme point.
Huge rescalings normalize before one retry.  Other tests reject a constructed
false quotient at independent replay, preserve ordinary geometry failures,
and show that a classification error from the verifier or from a retry
cannot reenter repair.  Callback tests cover the initial constructor,
extraction, retry and independent verification, including caller-raised
instances of both classification errors.  Exhausting the shared reduction
allowance suppresses both extraction and construction while retaining a
replayable partial chain.  Genuine factor controls charge only accepted
crushes.  Genuine trefoil preprocessing still calls the vertex-link
extractor and returns an authenticated one-vertex partial chain; this
control claims no nonzero support-descent count for those particular links.

The supplied-source harness replaces diagram provenance and initial source
setup; native LP, geometry, support extraction and
quotient replay remain active.  Their full diagram proofs fail independent
replay outside that harness.  Thus they establish the conditional source
and driver contracts, not new genuine diagram verdicts.  The separate
circle, factor and trefoil controls use ordinary diagram authentication.
