\section{Sharing structural candidates between primitive planners}
\label{sec:primitive-planner}

The preceding forest operation improves selected algebraic families, but
its ordinary-corpus overhead comes largely from looking for donors that
are absent. Previously, each search state ran a complete forest planner
and, when fewer than two edges were selected, a second disjoint-pair
planner. Both rebuilt structural eligibility from the same immutable
words. We now prepare one current candidate list, cache word-content
classification, and dispatch only the planners whose elementary
preconditions can hold. The selected certificate is preserved; this is
an implementation improvement rather than a new knot theorem.

\subsection{What can be cached, and what must remain current}

A word arena has immutable rules and never reuses an allocated node identifier.
For a root, raw support, signed occurrence counts and sign coherence therefore
do not depend on the current relation slot or on the previously applied
presentation moves. The planner stores either an ineligible marker or the
ordered two-generator support together with its two signed counts. A word
with more than two generators, mixed signs for either generator, or fewer
than two generators is outside the supported candidate class.

The underlying capped-support metadata still visits each allocated node at
most once. The new root descriptor is also computed at most once per distinct
root. Repeated roots share the descriptor; empty root zero is ineligible
from initialization. Each search state deduplicates the roots whose descriptors
are missing, computes only that metadata, and then constructs a candidate
list in original slot order. It checks both generator labels against the
\emph{current} live set. Neither a donor's slot nor its live membership is
stored in the immutable descriptor.

This distinction preserves three important cases. Reordering or duplicating
roots changes the selected slot while reusing their content summaries.
Removing a live generator makes a descriptor ineligible for the present
state without invalidating its stored word content. Normalization or
substitution that changes the word creates a different root and receives
a new descriptor. If an arena reuses an already interned root, its exact word
content is unchanged, so the earlier descriptor is valid. Raw cancellation
is never inferred merely from a smaller live set.

The fresh list is a producer-internal snapshot of the current roots and
live set. The forest planner and its possible pair fallback consume that
same snapshot before any presentation mutation. They still create fresh
witness records carrying the actual donor slot. Independent source replay
continues to reconstruct the whole presentation and derive the evidence
from its own words; it does not trust these caches or snapshots.

\subsection{An exact dispatch bound and sparse graph setup}

The producer uses a forest batch only when it selects at least two edges,
with distinct donor slots. Consequently fewer than two structurally eligible
current slots make a forest batch impossible. In that case it skips forest
setup and tries the pair planner directly. This cannot hide a batch that
the preceding forest policy would select. It is an exact upper bound on
the possible number of selected edges, not a guess based on the last few
failed searches or a timeout that could suppress an available move.

When a forest is possible, its union-find maps are created lazily for the
generators encountered by eligible unit-coordinate candidates. Isolated live
generators do not require graph entries. The orientation choice, original-slot
scan order and cycle tests are unchanged. The pair fallback consumes the
same candidate list instead of scanning all relators again. A negative
primitive-power classification remains cached by its exact word root.

For forest candidates, the gcd-normalized signed vector and the result of
the unit-coordinate test are cached separately. This avoids repeating big
integer gcd work on an unchanged donor. A coherent word whose raw count of
one generator is one is already a cyclic conjugate of $a^u b^{\pm1}$,
so the pair planner can construct the same exponent-one primitive witness
without running a prefix-profile query. The forest planner already used
this theorem. The independent checker still verifies the full arithmetic
profile. Proper powers and non-unit primitive vectors continue to use the
existing exact classification routine.

On the Gordian source audit, the old forest policy made 141 forest attempts.
Only three candidate-slot occurrences exist across those states. The new
policy skips graph setup on 140 states and runs the forest planner once,
selecting the same two-edge batch as before. All 141 states still rebuild
current slot/live membership; the optimization does not claim to eliminate
that remaining scan.

\subsection{Cost, resource accounting and unchanged proof contracts}

Let $p$ be the number of queried presentation states, $m$ the retained slot
count, $M$ the total allocated grammar nodes, $B$ a maximum length/count bit
size and $q_j$ the candidate-slot count at state $j$. Descriptors cover at
most $M$ roots and take a constant number of signed count operations each.
The underlying grammar summaries also cover at most $M$ nodes. Using ordinary
integer arithmetic, a conservative accumulated structural/gcd cost is
\[
 O\!\left(p(m+1)+MB^2+\sum_{j=1}^{p}q_j\right)
\]
up to generator/node identifier encoding and dictionary-access qualifications.
This is for preparing and filtering candidates. Forest selection additionally
pays its usual union-find cost over candidate vertices. Uncached primitive
profile queries, applying moves, normalization and the rest of recognition retain
their separately stated costs. Lazy forest storage at a state uses only
$O(q_j)$ vertex entries, bounded also by the live rank. Persistent summaries
remain proportional to the allocated grammar and its encoded count sizes.

The implementation charges both complete slot scans before allocating the
fresh-root and current-candidate lists. It additionally charges fresh
summaries, donor processing and graph traversal. List-comprehension work is
charged in aggregate, while grammar and arithmetic passes continue polling
cancellation. These cooperative counters are conservative accounting units,
not elapsed-time or per-bit CPU measurements. In fact, the new pair-only
path can record slightly \emph{more} charged work because new descriptor
accounting is explicit, even where its Python execution gets faster.

No certificate version, schema, checker, word operation, source reconstruction
or public option changes. The entire previous verifier/host/word modules are
checked byte-for-byte against the pinned baseline in the audit. Default,
disjoint-projection and forest modes retain the same choices when both runs
complete without exhausting resources. A finite work or wall allowance can
change which run finishes because accounting and execution costs change;
that is not an unconditional equality claim for all budgeted results.
The general bound on finding a short successful schedule remains open.

\subsection{Equivalence audits and complete-call evidence}

The pinned package is \texttt{b5b0ae50c4e4}. Eighty actual diagrams are run
in all three modes, giving 240 baseline/current comparisons. Every certificate
and nondecision is identical. All 159 positive mode results pass both
independent source-reconstructing replay paths. Another 2,000 planner
comparisons cover one thousand raw-word states with repeated roots, changed
slot orders and varying live sets. Those are planning tests on arbitrary
words, not knot verdicts.

Four new test methods exercise cached negative structural/profile results,
changing slots and live labels, normalization that exposes a newly eligible
root, shared current snapshots, strict resource exhaustion and caller
cancellation. A donor of the form $a^{2^{4096}}b^{-1}$ is selected and
independently replayed with expansion and the producer's general profile
query disabled. A one-candidate search skips the forest planner and returns
the same pair witness. The earlier primitive tests also pass unchanged.
The full maintained suite passes 992 tests in 234.493 seconds with Regina;
the log is \path{data/primitive-planner-full-tests.txt}.

The whole-recognition benchmark compares previous/current disjoint and forest
modes on nineteen fresh validated diagrams, with five measured rounds, one
warm-up per arm and an A/A control for each of the four implementations.
It includes source reconstruction and mandatory proof replay. The separate
checked group-stage experiment uses actual stabilized circles, bypassing
earlier simplification; its ratios are not whole-recognition speedups.
Both runs preserve all samples and incomplete outcomes, if any, and pin
the complete prior Python package. Their tables report medians of paired
old/current times, so values above one favor the new planner and need not
equal ratios of the displayed separate time medians.

All 760 whole-recognition calls and 200 checked-stage calls complete,
with 152 and 40 respective warm-ups. The runs take 96.775 and 11.452
seconds. Whole-call pair-mode ratios range from 0.983 to 1.018, and
forest-mode ratios from 0.986 to 1.036. The old/new pair A/A ranges are
0.986--1.023 and 0.942--1.035; the old/new forest A/A ranges are
0.977--1.018 and 0.981--1.034. These controls limit attribution of the
small whole-call differences to the planner alone. Gordian's forest ratio
is 1.026, while its forest A/A ratio is 1.034. Its old/new pair ratio is
0.999 despite separate medians 1,636.125 and 1,565.059 milliseconds.
Survivor 00 similarly has pair ratio 1.018 despite old/new separate medians
22.165 and 28.465 milliseconds. Within-run variation explains why neither
ratio of separate medians should replace the retained paired observations.

The larger checked group stages show a clearer effect. At 128 crossings,
pair medians are 200.361/185.550 milliseconds and forest medians are
131.456/119.081 milliseconds, with paired ratios 1.072 and 1.132.
The forest planner skips 63 unsuccessful graph setups and makes 63 productive
attempts instead of 126 attempts. Charged forest work falls from 185,764
to 170,833. Pair work increases slightly from 263,317 to 264,451 under the
new accounting, despite its elapsed improvement. Both modes retain 1,017
allocated nodes; their respective proof sizes remain exactly 24,517 and
24,286 bytes. The words, proofs, round counts and normalization handoffs
are unchanged.

This benefit is not universal: the eight-crossing stage has pair/forest
ratios 0.948/0.974. At sixteen crossings they are 0.970/1.037, increasing
to 1.064/1.110 at sixty-four. Across the stage inputs, old/new pair A/A
ranges are 0.980--0.998 and 0.968--1.032; old/new forest A/A ranges are
0.979--1.025 and 1.007--1.037. Forest controls show some systematic variation,
but their largest paired shift is smaller than the 128-crossing result.
The measured evidence supports cheaper planning on larger exposed stages,
not a universal whole-recognition gain. Both modes remain optional.

The driver is \path{primitive_power_research/planner.py} under
\path{../fast}; its \texttt{audit}, \texttt{benchmark} and \texttt{stages}
modes save full proofs, work counters, source hashes and shuffled sample
orders. These measurements concern the same certified move choices under
cheaper planning, not an enlarged class of solved inputs or a general
quasipolynomial recognition result.
