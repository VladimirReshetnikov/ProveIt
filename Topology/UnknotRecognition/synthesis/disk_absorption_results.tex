\paragraph{Source-cut audit.}
The refreshed 106-case audit certifies 63 whole cuts, compared with 30 before
absorption. Every new success is among the 33 algebraically eliminable
candidates identified by the earlier probe, and all previous successes are
preserved. All 63 source certificates replay independently. Regina's actual
cut construction and component recognition finish for 99 cases and agree
exactly with every one of the 56 native successes within the expansion
limit. Seven large-coordinate successes exceed the 4,096-disk literal cap
and are independently replayed without expansion. No oracle times out.
The source pins and per-case results are retained in
\path{data/disk-absorption-audit.json}.

The 43 remaining refusals comprise 26 nondisk annular cases, seven stalled
augmented cancellations (three detected by the square leaf obstruction),
and ten cores with three-handles. These categories are not perfectly
comparable with the earlier refusal ordering: the boundary and absorption
checks now precede cancellation, so four previously stalled core cases
are classified first by their nondisk annuli. They are not lost successes.

The focused checks cover actual same-tetrahedron and same-zero-handle
self-pairings, source relabellings, signed endpoint charts, malformed and
omitted absorption records, an omitted cancellation, disabled producers,
exact hexadecimal transport, resource caps, nondisk refusals and stalled
augmented cores. Multiplying the two-tetrahedron annular cut by $2^{1024}$
produces and independently verifies exactly $2^{1024}+1$ solid-torus
components without enumerating them. The tests also retain the pre-existing
raw cancellation and disk-gluing checks. These are source-bound whole-cut
certificates, not an enlargement of the complete useful-surface search.

\paragraph{Paired timings and capacity.}
After the regression suite finished, eight finite cuts were measured in
five shuffled paired rounds after warmup. Four use the newly supported
two-tetrahedron annular family and four use a previously supported
three-tetrahedron v1 control. All explicit and native histograms agree.
Input parsing and output serialization are excluded, while the native timer
includes the complete independent replay. The casewise medians of identical
explicit-code controls range from 0.976 to 1.073. The driver, full samples,
source pins and controls are retained in
\path{data/disk-absorption-benchmark.py} and its companion JSON. Run the driver
from the repository root with Regina available.

\paragraph{Diagnostic before the primitive-annulus extension.}
A source-pinned profile diagnostic finds that 15 of the 26 remaining annular
refusals have fewer fully attached boundary circles than boundary components
on every nondisk base involved. There are 19 such base profiles altogether:
13 annuli with one attached circle, four orientable three-holed spheres with
two, one orientable five-holed sphere with four, and one nonorientable surface
with three boundary components and two attached circles. The other profiles
include nine M\"obius bands attached along their sole boundary, one annulus
attached along both boundaries, and one nonorientable surface attached along
both of its boundary components. The diagnostic is retained in
\path{data/annular-profile-diagnostic.json}.

The first group suggests a simultaneous primitive-annulus rule: arcs joining
the attached base circles to a remaining boundary component lift to disjoint
cocore disks in the interval bundle. Their endpoints must avoid every other
marked attaching interval. Turning that observation into an independently
checked gluing rule and accounting for all component joins is further work;
the 15 candidates were not additional certified cuts in the absorption
checkpoint. Section~\ref{marked-cut:primitive-annuli} records the subsequent
implementation and its separate evidence.
