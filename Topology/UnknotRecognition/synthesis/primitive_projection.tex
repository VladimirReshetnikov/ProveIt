\section{Independently replayed disjoint primitive-pair projections}
\label{sec:primitive-projections}

The version-five terminal of Section~\ref{sec:primitive-power-terminal}
uses a primitive-power relation only after the whole presentation reaches
rank two. Report 45 also proposes contracting disjoint generator pairs
inside a larger presentation, while retaining raw word circuits between
rounds. We now implement that operation, including an independent checker
for every complete round. This closes the report's whole-round replay gap.
It does not supply the missing short-depth producer for arbitrary diagrams.
The new search policy is explicitly optional: ordinary-corpus measurements
below show overhead rather than a practical speedup.

\subsection{The quotient map and the invariant preserved by a round}

Let a source-reconstructed knot-group presentation have free generators
$X$, and let one current relation be $w^d$, where $w$ is primitive in the
free factor $F(a,b)$, $d>0$, and its exponent vector $(u,v)$ has both
coordinates nonzero. Knot-group torsion-freeness implies that adjoining
$w=1$ preserves the presented group. Because $w$ belongs to a free basis
of $F(a,b)$, the quotient by its normal closure is infinite cyclic.
The primitive character
\[
 a\longmapsto t^v,\qquad b\longmapsto t^{-u}
\]
is surjective and kills $w$. It therefore realizes that cyclic quotient:
$\gcd(|u|,|v|)=1$, and a surjection between two infinite cyclic groups is
an isomorphism. The remaining free generators are unaffected. Substituting
this character into \emph{every} relator gives a presentation of the same
knot group, with one fewer generator.

The implementation orders $a<b$, reuses the name $a$ for $t$, and orients
the character so that the image of the old $a$ is positive:
\[
 a\longmapsto a^{|v|},\qquad
 b\longmapsto a^{-u\,\operatorname{sign}(v)}.
\]
Here the $a$ on the right is the new quotient generator; the substitution
is simultaneous, so this image is not recursively substituted into itself.
Negative letters receive inverse images. The new generator need not be a
meridian, and no later step assumes that it is one.

For pairwise disjoint pairs, these operations act on different free
factors. The checker validates all donor witnesses against the
\emph{pre-round} presentation, constructs all images, and performs one
simultaneous substitution across all relator roots. It then deletes the
second generator of each pair. A chosen donor becomes a word in its own
new cyclic generator with exponent
$du|v|-dv\,u\operatorname{sign}(v)=0$, so that donor's original slot can
be replaced by the empty word. All other slots are retained, including
empty, repeated and unreduced roots. No claim that other relators are
redundant is needed or accepted.

Each round thus preserves the original knot group. Torsion-freeness remains
available in subsequent rounds because the group remains isomorphic to the
source knot group. For exponent-one donors the local step needs no
torsion-freeness hypothesis, but proper-power donors do. The public verdict
path obtains this hypothesis from a validated classical knot diagram and
complete prefix replay; an arbitrary presentation plus a caller-supplied
flag cannot authorize it.

\subsection{Raw circuits, cached eligibility and the normalization boundary}

The producer scans relator slots in their original order and greedily
chooses disjoint eligible pairs. Eligibility requires exactly two live
generators, one sign per generator, and the minimum-width primitive-power
witness established in the preceding section. Sign coherence implies free
and cyclic reduction without performing normalization. Pure single-generator
powers are excluded from a pair round. A root outside this sufficient raw
class is skipped; its reduced representative might still be eligible.
The greedy choice is deterministic but is not asserted to minimize depth.

Occurrence summaries are cached by immutable arena node identifier. A
terminal contributes one signed occurrence, and a concatenation adds its
children's counts. A support with more than two distinct generators is
stored as an ineligible marker: later concatenation cannot remove letters
from a raw circuit. The traversal stops at cached nodes, so each allocated
node's capped support summary is constructed at most once over the search.
Positive and negative arithmetic classification results are also cached by
root. The selected relation slot is attached to a fresh witness copy; it is
not cached with the immutable word, since repeated roots may occupy
different slots. Normalization or substitution creates other roots and
therefore cannot invalidate these word-content summaries.

A round builds binary power circuits for the images, then maps each
reachable original node once using raw concatenation. It does not call
compressed substitution helpers that automatically reduce words. Inverse
letters and zero-exponent circuits may remain physically present after
substitution. On reaching one live generator, an independent postorder
sum verifies that every retained root uses that generator and has exponent
zero. A word in one generator with zero exponent is the identity; the
resulting presentation is exactly infinite cyclic. No expansion,
equality query or cancellation search is required at this endpoint.
The existing rank-two primitive-power terminal may also close a raw state,
because its selected donor is checked for sign coherence independently.

When the next projection round cannot be found and more than one generator
remains, the producer records an explicit \texttt{normalize\_relators}
move, cyclically reduces all roots, and retries. If no pair becomes eligible,
the earlier singleton, Whitehead and overlap search resumes on the normalized
state. This is an adaptive transition between representations, with an
explicit proof boundary. The cost of that normalization and subsequent
search is separate from the normalization-free round bound below.
Even simple stabilized-circle inputs can alternate projections and
normalizations under the present slot-order policy; a balanced schedule
on an abstract family is not evidence that this policy discovers one on
all diagram presentations.

\subsection{Version six and independent bounded replay}

Version six retains the source PD, method, status, complete move prefix and
terminal record. A \texttt{primitive\_projection} move contains exactly
its kind and a nonempty list of primitive-power witnesses. Each witness
identifies its original donor slot, ordered pair, primitive signed vector,
positive exponent and width. Pairs must be disjoint subsets of the current
live generators, donor slots must be distinct, and both coordinates must
be nonzero. Every witness uses the strict arithmetic checks of version five,
including bounds on claimed integers before expensive arithmetic.
The checker accepts no extra root-dropping or torsion-freeness fields.

Literal replay constructs the images and substitutes into each literal
word; compressed replay independently constructs signed image circuits
and uses a fresh postorder mapping of each original root. Neither invokes
the producer's planner, application routine, cache or rank-one query.
All witnesses are checked before any state mutation. The final
\texttt{rank\_one\_exponent\_zero} record names exactly the one live
generator, and both replay routes check every retained relator. A version-six
proof can instead use the previous rank-two terminal after its checked
prefix. Versions one through five retain their prior meaning.

The literal checker computes the entire projected letter count and checks
its letter and work allowances \emph{before} constructing repeated lists.
The compressed checker constructs binary powers under the existing node
and work budgets. Both propagate caller cancellation. Cached summaries and
other Python allocations are auxiliary memory, so a grammar-node cap is
not a hard process-memory bound. Work counters are cooperative structural
allowances, not a claim that a Python big-integer operation has unit bit cost.

Both \texttt{compressed\_certificate} and \texttt{group\_decide}
accept \texttt{primitive\_projection=True}. The \texttt{recognize} API
accepts \texttt{use\_group=True} together with
\texttt{group\_primitive\_projection=True}.
The CLI flag is \texttt{--group-primitive-projection}. At the group host it
implies compressed search and mandatory compressed replay; combining it
with the older explicit-to-compressed adaptive-search flag is rejected.
It defaults to false. Disabling projections reproduces the previous
version-five producer exactly in the source audit. Disabling both primitive
options retains the earlier rank-one search contract.

\subsection{A bound for consecutive raw rounds, and its limitations}

Let $r_0$ be the initial rank, $m$ the number of retained relator slots,
$M_j$ all allocated grammar nodes after round $j$, and $B_j$ the largest
bit length of any allocated word length, including image intermediates.
Use $B_j\ge1$ and let $d$ count consecutive raw projection rounds with no
intervening normalization or legacy search. Every image exponent is at
most a donor's word length. Thus substituting images increases a word's
length by at most a factor of the previous maximum length. Binary power
construction introduces at most $O(r_0(B_j+1))$ image nodes, and mapping the
old reachable circuit introduces at most one node per old node. Hence
\[
 B_{j+1}\le 2B_j+O(1),\qquad
 M_{j+1}\le 2M_j+O(r_0(B_j+1)).
\]
Consequently $B_j\le O((B_0+1)2^j)$ and
$M_j\le 2^j O(M_0+r_0(B_0+1)(j+1))$. Counts and profile heights have
$O(B_j)$ bits; generator and node identifiers have their usual encoding
costs. There are at most $r_0-1$ pair contractions in the full run.

The maintained implementation uses a sorted reachable-node pass for raw
application and for some arithmetic queries. It may classify several
candidate roots separately, so its bound should not be confused with one
shared linear profile scan over all donors. Nevertheless, scanning all
$m$ roots, independently classifying each eligible candidate and replaying
all chosen witnesses is polynomial in $M_j,B_j,m,r_0$ and identifier bit
sizes. Ordinary quadratic integer multiplication/gcd bounds suffice;
expected dictionary access is the same implementation-model qualification
as in the preceding section. Composing any fixed such polynomial over $d$
rounds yields
\[
 \operatorname{poly}(M_0,B_0,m,r_0,\text{identifier sizes})\,2^{O(d)}.
\]
The independent raw replay and rank-one endpoint have the same form of
bound. This statement does not count a general normalization as a raw
round or give an unproved polynomial bound to the existing heuristic search.

Section~\ref{sec:incoming-2c7f} supersedes the depth-dependent raw-epoch
estimate with a polynomial bound using the fixed source's saturated kernel.
The short-depth condition below was sufficient in the original analysis;
it is no longer needed for an epoch consisting only of these raw moves.
Endpoint discovery and preprocessing remain separate obligations.

If a controlled producer on encoded input size $g$ supplied a polynomial-size
initial presentation, reached an accepted endpoint in $O((\log g)^2)$ raw
rounds, and had quasipolynomial preprocessing cost, this part of recognition
would be quasipolynomial. None of the short-depth, endpoint or preprocessing
hypotheses is established for all diagrams. Rank merely decreases by at
least one per round, which permits $d$ linear in $r_0$. Success of a
greedy batch on a finite family is not a general subexponential algorithm.

\subsection{Source audits, capacity checks and complete-call measurements}

The maintained suite passes 981 tests in 229.282 seconds with Regina.
Seven new test methods cover 120 random simultaneous-substitution comparisons,
independent literal and compressed replay, cached failed queries and changing
root slots, strict schemas and numerical forgeries, source rebinding,
all-relator endpoint checks, actual-diagram proof prefixes, API/CLI behavior,
cancellation and exhaustion. An initial prototype enabled projections by
default and broke the old \texttt{primitive\_power=False} behavior; the
retained exploratory failure log records this. The final implementation
keeps projections optional and passes the unchanged earlier terminal tests.

The pinned baseline is \texttt{e2cf6faa562b}. The research driver loads its
entire Python package into a separate namespace, including the previous
recognizer and diagram class. The 79-diagram audit consists of the earlier
nineteen-case corpus, 55 valid single-component braids from the fixed
240-attempt sample, and five stabilized circles. Current default certificates
and nondecisions equal the pinned baseline exactly. Enabling projections
produces 34 version-six certificates containing 158 pair contractions;
every positive passes both complete source-reconstructing replay paths.
There are no newly solved inputs, lost positives or proper-power donors in
that diagram sample. The audit takes 4.695 seconds including baseline setup.

Separate actual five-braid sources have verified powered Whitehead prefixes
with exponents $2^4$, $2^{128}$ and $2^{1024}$. They test compressed source
replay and the explicit normalization handoff. Separately, abstract balanced
presentations use both $(a^{2^b}b)^2$ and $(a^{2^b}b)^3$ for each tree edge.
The coprime powers imply the edge word is trivial, and leaf-to-root
elimination proves these presentations are $\mathbb Z$ without assuming
torsion-freeness. They are algebraic capacity fixtures, not diagram inputs.
Query and independent replay complete with reduction, cyclic reduction,
expansion and equality disabled. At rank 256 and $b=32$, eight rounds
contract 255 pairs; allocated nodes grow from 5,118 to 18,235, all 510
relator slots survive, and 255 remaining roots are nonempty raw circuits
with zero exponent. This tests the intended representation contract rather
than practical difficulty of an unknot diagram.

The complete-call benchmark uses nineteen fresh validated diagrams, five
measured rounds and one warm-up per arm. Six shuffled arms compare the
pinned baseline, current default and enabled projections, with separate
A/A controls for all three. The timed call includes diagram validation,
recognition, source reconstruction and mandatory independent proof replay.
All 570 measured calls complete, with 114 additional warm-ups; the run
takes 71.320 seconds. Table ratios are medians of paired old/projection
times, so values below one mean a slower projection call. They need not
equal the ratio of the two separately reported time medians. Production, fixture and driver source hashes are frozen
before and after execution. The baseline package is pinned independently;
no adapter that bypasses a new option check is inserted into the timer.

On the ordinary survivor and mirror unknots, paired ratios range from
0.924 to 0.953. Survivor 06 uses one projection and one normalization;
its search work rises from 8,670 to 9,197, nodes from 162 to 166, and
certificate bytes from 848 to 989. Gordian uses two projection rounds,
three pairs and two normalization handoffs. Its search work rises from
1,215,145 to 1,265,669, nodes from 9,025 to 9,084, and proof bytes from
9,663 to 10,020. Its paired ratio is 1.002 despite separate time medians
of 1,989.772 and 1,584.861 milliseconds: substantial within-run variation
makes the ratio of these medians misleading. The baseline/current-default
paired ratio on this same input is 1.071 despite equivalent search work,
further limiting an attribution to projections.

Across all nineteen inputs, old A/A ratios range from 0.991 to 1.024,
current-default A/A from 0.980 to 1.018, and projection A/A from 0.985 to
1.017. Current default versus the pinned baseline ranges from 0.983 to
1.071, with the upper endpoint occurring on Gordian. No extra timing run
is used to replace these retained observations or turn them into a positive
speedup claim.

Projection metadata and unsuccessful eligibility scans can cost more than
the work saved by an occasional contraction. These timings justify the
opt-in default and give no whole-pipeline speedup claim for this corpus.
The useful advance is a complete, independently replayed raw-round
implementation on which a better schedule can now be tested. The next
asymptotic obligation remains a controlled schedule reaching a decisive
endpoint, including the cost of discovering and exposing its donors.
