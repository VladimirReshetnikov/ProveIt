\section{Survivor prediction and adaptive cancellation}
\label{sec:radical-adaptive}

The incoming package \code{unknot\_radical\_transfer\_20261007.zip}
analyzes minimal complexes before performing cobordism-valued elimination.
We reviewed an isolated extraction of the delivered archive, reran its
checks, and integrated a narrower operation into the production scanner:
exact prediction of the surviving matching multiplicities, followed by a
certified shortcut when no differential can remain. An adaptive variant
uses this operation only after ordinary cancellation becomes expensive.
The delivered report and the production measurements are separate artifacts.

\subsection{What binary residue homology determines}
At a fixed frontier with $2k$ endpoints, write $c(a,b)$ for the number of
circles in the overlay of two matching objects. A dotted basis morphism
$x_S:a\to b$ has nonnegative intrinsic weight
\[
  \omega(x_S)=k-c(a,b)+2|S|.
\]
Weight zero means exactly the undotted identity of one matching.
The Euler-characteristic calculation in the composition engine makes
weights additive on every nonzero term of a valid cobordism composition.
More explicitly, for input dot counts $u,v$ and output dot count $q$,
the nonzero genus-zero terms obey
\[
  2q=2u+2v+k-c(a,b)-c(b,c)+c(a,c),
\]
which gives the stated additivity after substitution. Consequently the
operation $\rho$ that retains only the scalar identity coefficient
between equal matchings is multiplicative. An undotted coefficient
between \emph{different} matchings is discarded. Forgetting this typing
would give an incorrect binary complex.

Let $m_{a,h}$ count copies of matching $a$ in degree $h$ of a valid
partial complex $C$. The residue differential $d_0=\rho(d)$ is a direct
sum, over $a$, of ordinary binary complexes. Put
\[
 r_{a,h}=\operatorname{rank}_{\F}(d_0:C_{a,h}\to C_{a,h+1}),\qquad
 \mu_{a,h}=m_{a,h}-r_{a,h}-r_{a,h-1}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact survivor profile]
Complete scalar-unit cancellation leaves exactly $\mu_{a,h}$ copies
of each matching in each degree. This profile is independent of pivot
order at the fixed processed tangle.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An allowed pivot is an endomorphism $\phi$ with scalar coefficient one,
so $\rho(\phi)=\rho(\phi^{-1})=1$. Applying $\rho$ to its cobordism
Schur update gives the ordinary binary pivot update. Hence each
cancellation preserves residue homology. Cancellation terminates,
removing two objects at each step. Its final residue differential is
zero: a remaining nonzero scalar coefficient would be another allowed
pivot. The final multiplicities are therefore the dimensions of the
original residue homology, namely $\mu_{a,h}$.
\end{proof}

This argument uses only the valid compositions already required by the
scanner. It does not require a universal Catalan count, the full
classification of minimal complexes, or the incoming report's sharp
nilpotence exponent. In particular, pivot heuristics can improve fill,
composition work, and intermediate memory, but cannot decrease the
final matching multiplicities of a fixed prefix. Changing the scan
order changes the prefix and is a different operation.

\subsection{An exact shortcut, and an essential non-shortcut}
The profile is not itself the surviving differential. For example,
$a\xrightarrow{x}a$ in degrees zero and one has zero residue
differential, yet its dotted map is nonzero. Cancelling other scalar
pivots can also create such maps between surviving objects.

\begin{proposition}[Degree-gap shortcut]
Suppose the occupied degrees of $\mu$ contain no consecutive integers.
Then $C$ is homotopy equivalent, relative to its frontier, to the direct
sum specified by $\mu$, with zero differential. The same conclusion
holds at a closed frontier without the degree-gap hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Perform unit cancellation conceptually and apply the survivor
proposition. A differential increases homological degree by one, so
the degree-gap hypothesis forbids every remaining entry, even between
unequal matchings. At a closed frontier $k=0$, all coefficients are
scalars and a fully cancelled differential is zero. The usual local
cancellation equivalences therefore identify $C$ with exactly the
stated zero-differential complex.
\end{proof}

The implementation need not carry out that conceptual sequence: it
computes binary ranks and constructs the resulting objects directly.
There are no cobordism compositions in the shortcut. Because the
equivalence is relative to the frontier, subsequent crossing gluing
preserves it. Zero survivors are allowed. Nonconsecutive integer
degrees and previously cancelled object slots are handled explicitly.
The raw helper assumes a valid complex, including $d^2=0$; a nonnegative
profile alone is not a validity certificate.

\subsection{Switching implementations in response to progress}
Eager residue prediction can be slower than the existing sparse
cancellation loop. The adaptive mode follows an introsort-style
policy, with a mathematical distinction: the alternative operation
is a conditional shortcut, not a universal asymptotic fallback.

At the beginning of each crossing stage let $N$ be the live object
count. Start the existing minimum-fill cancellation with an allowance
\[
 B=\max(256,4N)
\]
for Schur update pairs. Before cancelling a pivot from $b$ to $c$,
its candidate work is
\[
  (|\operatorname{out}(b)|-1)(|\operatorname{in}(c)|-1).
\]
These are the Markowitz costs already available to the queue. They
bound touched pairs, not actual expensive compositions: scalar
identities, zero products, and caches may make a pair cheaper.
When the next pivot would exceed $B$, pause before that pivot,
finish the counters, and discard the candidate queues. All completed
pivots, live maps, and composition caches are retained.

Compute the residue profile of this partially reduced complex. If
the degree-gap or closed-boundary certificate applies, finish the
stage using the shortcut. Otherwise resume ordinary cancellation
on the same partial result. There is at most one prediction attempt
per crossing stage, so a failed attempt cannot cause oscillation
between implementations. A later crossing starts a fresh allowance.
The constants are a transparent cost heuristic, not a proved optimal
threshold. Deadline and object limits keep their existing meaning;
neither slow progress nor a failed shortcut decides whether a knot
is trivial.

The shared state is what makes this switch inexpensive and sound.
Every pause lies between complete homotopy equivalences; a partially
applied Schur update is never exposed to another engine. Rebuilding
the queue on fallback costs a fresh pass over its remaining entries,
while the completed algebra is reused. The additional binary rank
work is not bounded by elapsed sparse work, so this policy does not
claim the constant-factor competitive guarantee of a time-sliced
portfolio. In particular, it does not acquire introsort's global
worst-case theorem merely by adopting its switching principle.

\paragraph{Interfaces and evidence.}
Both recognition and raw homology accept the Python option
\code{reduction="adaptive"} or \code{reduction="residue"}; the latter
predicts at every reduced stage and is an ablation/control. The CLI
uses \code{--reduction adaptive|residue|standard}. This controls
chain cancellation and is distinct from the existing
\code{--no-reduction} switch for Reidemeister simplification.
The default remains \code{standard}. The new modes require the
standard backend, bit coefficients, minimum-fill pivots, and no race;
they compose with component contraction and the existing tail option.
Unsupported combinations are rejected before early recognition filters.
Counters expose prediction attempts, successful shortcuts, switches,
fallbacks, and Schur update pairs. Earlier recognition certificates
remain enabled, so raw homology measurements are not pipeline speedups.

\subsection{The stronger report and its boundary assumptions}
For noncrossing matchings in a \emph{prescribed common cyclic order},
the incoming report proves the sharp arc-algebra radical exponent
$J^{2k}=0\ne J^{2k-1}$ for $k\ge1$. Its dot derivative and central
marked dot also yield a two-pass transfer formula. Given a scalar
contraction $(i,p,h)$ and $\delta=d-d_0$, the induced differential is
\[
  D=\sum_{r=0}^{2k-2}p\delta(h\delta)^ri.
\]
This is a finite specialization of the
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0403266}{homological perturbation lemma},
whose inverse formula and special contraction conditions are stated
in Crainic's Section 2. The report supplies sharp-depth examples,
so truncating this general expression after the first correction
would be unsound.

Those stronger formulas are not installed into the arbitrary PD
scanner here. Its frontier pairing representation has no global
common-disk certificate. As a simple warning, the three pairings
$(01)(23),(02)(13),(03)(12)$ are not simultaneously noncrossing in
one cyclic order; blindly composing the associated abstract triple
in the planar gluing engine yields an impossible surface component.
The narrow production proof above needs only its existing valid
geometric compositions. A full transfer adapter must establish
its category and geometry assumptions separately. The report's
provided profile adapter targets the older dictionary-based scanner,
not the current interned-list implementation; our adapter is new.

The survivor profile also exposes the main asymptotic obstruction.
A small number of matching \emph{types} does not bound their
\emph{multiplicities}. Even fixed-width scans can have exponentially
many surviving copies. The report's parameterized transfer estimate
still depends on both boundary half-width and the largest minimal
survivor count. No bound forcing these parameters into a
quasi-polynomial regime for every input has been established.

\subsection{Validation and measured limits}
The isolated report rerun passed 18 unit tests, 2,244 exhaustive
composition/derivation comparisons, 500 full transfer certificates,
500 minimal-profile comparisons, 308 marked-transfer certificates,
306 braid closures against an independent crossing cube, and 1,365
intermediate-stage contraction certificates. Sharp radical witnesses
through $k=12$ and transfer-depth witnesses through $k=8$ also passed.
These checks cover the report's model; they do not prove a global
runtime bound or establish compatibility with arbitrary scanner geometry.

Production tests compare each predicted profile to actual complete
cancellation at every stage of 120 seeded knot closures on two to
five strands, using both greedy and shuffled crossing orders. Every
final homological degree agrees for eager, adaptive, and ordinary
scans; twenty cases also use the independent older set-based engine.
Additional tests check a nontrivial unit, nonzero maps after a declined
shortcut, pause/resume validity, both adaptive outcomes, budget
exhaustion, CLI and pipeline evidence, and incompatible options.
All 222 integrated tests passed. The integrated run and the report validation summaries are retained
under \code{synthesis/data/}.
