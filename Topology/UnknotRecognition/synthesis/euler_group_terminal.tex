\section{A polynomial signed-group terminal with an essential Euler gate}
\label{sec:euler-group-terminal}

The 12 October continuation integrates report~111's source-bound free-group
terminal into the maintained whole-cut portfolio.  It can certify a cut even
when the geometric private-column cancellation attempts stop.  Its output is
an \emph{unmarked} handlebody census.  The certificate proves an actual
fundamental-group presentation is free and checks the Euler characteristic
of the original three-dimensional cut.  It does not infer freeness from
abelianization, and its word substitutions do not supply marked geometric
slides.  This section gives the theorem, the exact discovery bound, the
integration contract and fresh validation evidence.  It leaves the general
quasi-polynomial unknot-recognition goal open.

\subsection{Why a free group needs the original Euler characteristic}

\begin{proposition}[Euler-checked free-group criterion]
\label{euler-group:criterion}
Let $W$ be a finite union of compact connected orientable PL
three-manifolds, every component having nonempty boundary.  Let $c$ be its
component count.  Suppose the free product of its component fundamental
groups is $F_r$.  Then all components are handlebodies if and only if
$\chi(W)=c-r$.  The genus of each component is the rank of its free group.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each component group injects into $F_r$ and is therefore free, of finite
rank $r_i$.  Abelianizing the free product gives $\sum_i r_i=r$.
Since every component has boundary, $H_3(W_i;\mathbb Q)=0$, and hence
\[
 \chi(W)=c-r+\sum_i b_2(W_i).
\]
The Euler equality is equivalent to the vanishing of every $b_2(W_i)$.
The orientation class and the exact sequence of $(W_i,\partial W_i)$ then
show that $\partial W_i$ is connected: the map from
$H_3(W_i,\partial W_i;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q$ must surject onto
$H_2(\partial W_i;\mathbb Q)$, whose dimension counts boundary components.
The boundary Euler identity $\chi(\partial W_i)=2\chi(W_i)$ now says its
genus is $r_i$.

For completeness, a compact orientable three-manifold with connected
boundary of genus $g$ and group $F_g$ is a handlebody, without a separate
irreducibility assumption.  If $g>0$, the positive-genus closed surface
group cannot inject into a free group: subgroups of free groups are free,
whereas a closed orientable positive-genus surface group is not free.
The Loop Theorem gives an essential boundary compression disk.  After
cutting along it, van Kampen expresses the old group as a free product of
the new component groups, with an extra free generator for a
nonseparating cut.  The new groups are free as well.  Repeat boundary
compressions.  The sum of $2h-1$ over positive-genus boundary components
strictly decreases, so this ends with spherical boundaries.

Let there be $d$ disks, $v$ final pieces and $b$ final boundary spheres;
write $s_j$ for the final free ranks.  Boundary Euler characteristic and
van Kampen give respectively
\[
 d=b+g-1,\qquad g=\sum_j s_j+d-v+1.
\]
Every piece has boundary, so $b\geq v$.  Combining the equalities gives
$0=\sum_j s_j+b-v$.  Thus every piece is simply connected and has one
sphere boundary.  Capping it by a ball and applying the
three-dimensional Poincare theorem and PL Schoenflies theorem shows it
is a ball.  Reattaching the disk neighborhoods is attaching one-handles
to balls, which yields a handlebody.  The converse follows from the usual
handlebody spine and Euler characteristic.
\end{proof}

The classical inputs are the Loop Theorem in Hatcher's notes and the
Poincare theorem; Morgan and Tian give a detailed proof of the latter
\cite{euler-hatcher-three,euler-morgan-tian}.  The argument above derives
the precise criterion used here.  It is not a claim that a chosen
presentation or an arbitrary boundary pattern has a geometric realization
by the recorded algebraic moves.

The boundary hypothesis holds for these source cuts.  The source is
connected, compact and orientable with one torus boundary.  A component
of a cut disjoint from the old boundary has a new boundary from the cut
surface; a component with neither old nor new boundary would already be
a separate closed component of the source.  This also covers closed and
one-sided supplied normal surfaces.

The Euler condition cannot be omitted.  For example $S^2\times I$ has
trivial fundamental group but two sphere boundaries and $b_2=1$.
Likewise the interior connected sum of a handlebody with $S^1\times S^2$
has a free group but a nonzero second Betti number.  Checking only a
reduced presentation's Euler characteristic would lose precisely this
obstruction.  The implementation always retains the original source
three-handles in the Euler ledger.

\subsection{The source presentation and its size}

The terminal currently supports parallelity profiles that are orientable
product bundles with disk bases.  Ported profiles have multiplicity one;
unported profiles contribute their binary number of isolated balls.
Unsupported profiles cause a method refusal.  The compiler receives the
independently authenticated finite core, base profiles and ordered
attachment words.

Collapse a spanning forest of the core zero/one-handle graph.  Each
remaining one-handle is a generator.  Each original two-handle supplies
its actual signed cyclic attaching word, read from the source-face flags.
Those flags, not just the endpoints, distinguish the two directions of a
loop one-handle.  A complete annular attachment of a disk bundle adds
one two-handle relation.  Its signed midpoint word is recovered from the
ordered ports and the face-sector endpoint charts; forest crossings are
erased.  Every such word must close in the authenticated core graph.

For a disk bundle attached along proper intervals, use one ball vertex
and one edge per attaching disk.  Contract a forest of this graph of
spaces.  Its remaining edges add free generators.  Connected components
of this graph specify the component presentations.  Complete port
ownership and the partition into interval and circle attachments are
checked, so no attachment can be silently dropped or used twice.
Original three-handles have no effect on the fundamental group, but
remain in the source Euler characteristic.

Write $h_j$ for the original core handle counts, $a_\circ$ for full
annular disk bundles, $d$ for ported disk bundles with proper intervals,
$j$ for the number of proper interval attachments, $\kappa$ for the
number of core graph components, $c_f$ for the number of joined graph
components, and $b$ for the binary count of isolated disk bundles.
The graph-of-spaces cycle rank is $\beta=j-\kappa-d+c_f$.  If $a,m$ are
the total generator and relation counts, then
\[
 a=h_1-h_0+\kappa+\beta,\qquad m=h_2+a_\circ,\qquad c=c_f+b,
\]
and the compiler checks the exact ledger
\begin{equation}
 \chi(W)=c-a+m-h_3.
 \label{euler-group:ledger}
\end{equation}
Indeed the direct source expression is
$h_0-h_1+h_2-h_3+a_\circ+d+b-j$, which gives this identity by substitution.
All additions involving $b$ use binary arithmetic; the isolated balls
are never expanded.

For $t$ source tetrahedra, the existing finite constructor gives
$h_0\leq6t$, $h_1\leq8t$, $h_2\leq12t$, $h_3\leq4t$ and at most $66t$
core ports.  Interior edge-link wedge lists have total length at most
$6t$, and at most two endpoint ranks are retained per list.  The total
one-handle passage length in original two-handle words is therefore at
most $12t$.  The boundary words partition the ports, so full annular
words add at most $66t$ letters.  Forest erasure cannot increase length.
Consequently this presentation satisfies
\[
 a\leq74t,\qquad m\leq78t,\qquad L:=\sum_\rho|\rho|\leq78t.
\]
These bounds concern the finite presentation, not the bit size of every
profile or of all source orbit proofs.  With $f$ supplied profile
records and $E$ their total encoded bit mass together with the finite
source, the elementary forest and ordering implementation takes a
conservative $O(t^2+f)$ structural steps plus its arithmetic cost on $E$.
Source preparation and authentication are separately charged.

\subsection{A nonincreasing Tietze policy with a polynomial bound}

Freely and cyclically reduce each relator.  If a generator $x$ appears
exactly once, counting both signs, in a donor relator, cyclically rotate
it to $x^\epsilon u$.  The relation defines $x=u^{-\epsilon}$.  Substitute
this word, or its inverse, at every occurrence in the other relators;
delete the donor and $x$; freely and cyclically reduce again.  Record
relators that become empty.  Each step is an elementary Tietze
elimination, so it preserves the group.

The producer first chooses a private generator column if one exists.
Otherwise it tries all singleton donor/generator pairs and accepts only
candidates whose total reduced relator length does not exceed the
current length.  It minimizes that new length, breaking ties by row and
generator labels.  If every relator disappears, the remaining generators
are a free basis.  If no permitted move remains, it returns
\code{INCONCLUSIVE}.  Generators absent from every relator stay in the
ledger and contribute their ranks.

\begin{proposition}[Local discovery and replay cost]
\label{euler-group:cost}
For compact indexed labels, put $N=a+m+L+1$.  The implemented policy uses
$O(N^4)$ indexed word operations for discovery, $O(N^3)$ for independent
replay, and $O(N^2)$ temporary letter storage.  The recorded elimination
pairs require $O(m\log(N+1))$ bits, in addition to the source and other
certificate fields.  The bounds exclude the cost of user callbacks.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Accepted states have total word length at most $L$.  A stage has at most
$L$ singleton donor/generator candidates.  Trying one substitutes a word
of length at most $L$ at at most $L$ positions, producing $O(L^2)$
temporary letters.  Stack free reduction and trimming inverse endpoints
are linear in this temporary length.  Including row and generator
ledgers costs $O(N^2)$ per candidate.  Every accepted move deletes one
row and one generator, so there are at most $\min(a,m)$ stages.  This
proves the discovery bound.  A checker receives the chosen pair and
performs one substitution per stage, proving the replay bound.  Keeping
one candidate trial at a time proves the storage bound.  Each recorded
pair consists of two stable indexed labels.
\end{proof}

In a bit model, indexed-label operations can conservatively be charged
an additional $O(\log(N+1))$ factor.  For the finite source presentation,
$N=O(t)$.  Thus the algebraic terminal is polynomial in $t$; the complete
source query still includes its separate orbit, profile and encoded
arithmetic costs.  The nonincreasing policy is essential to the stated
discovery bound, even though an individual Tietze move remains sound
without it.

This procedure is incomplete even for free groups.  The automorphism
$\varphi(a)=ab$, $\varphi(b)=a$ has inverse $a\mapsto b$,
$b\mapsto b^{-1}a$.  Its fourth iterates are $abaababa$ and $abaab$.
They form a free basis, so
\[
 \langle a,b\mid abaababa,abaab\rangle
\]
presents the trivial group.  Both relators are positive and neither
contains a generator exactly once.  The policy stops immediately.
Adding an unused generator gives the same refusal for a rank-one free
group.  No useful-surface completeness result follows from this terminal.

Suppose $k$ generator eliminations finish the presentation and $z$
relators were discarded as trivial.  Then $m=k+z$ and $r=a-k$.
Equation~\eqref{euler-group:ledger} gives the exact diagnostic
\[
 \chi(W)-c+r=z-h_3=\sum_i b_2(W_i).
\]
The checker independently requires both $\chi(W)=c-r$ and $z=h_3$.
Positive defect is a refusal of the handlebody criterion, with the exact
second-Betti sum available as a diagnostic.  It is not a knottedness
verdict.

\subsection{Integration, budgets and independent certificates}

The public whole-cut API keeps its source, maximum-passage and deferred
geometric attempts in their existing order.  Only after all applicable
attempts refuse does the default group fallback try the signed-group
terminal (\path{group_fallback=True}).  Setting the option to false retains the earlier
geometric-only policy.  Existing geometric successes return immediately,
without altering their certificates.

The source parallelity packet and lazy boundary packet are shared by all
attempts.  The group terminal always uses the \emph{original} source
core, including its three-handle count, even when a geometric attempt
used a top-reduced core.  Unsupported base profiles are screened before
spending boundary orbit allowance.  Both endpoint-mask and moment
boundary backends are supported.  The four source discoveries and the
selected boundary discoveries share one \code{max\_cycles} allowance;
the fallback never starts a second source query with a fresh allowance.
The direct \path{normal_cut_group_handlebodies} API uses the moment
boundary backend.

The \path{normal-cut-euler-free-v1} certificate contains the source
parallelity and boundary proofs, the complete presentation, per-component
Tietze traces, and the census.  The independent dispatcher authenticates
source and boundary, reconstructs the signed presentation using separate
code, replays every substitution and the nonincrease gate, and recomputes
the free ranks and Euler census.  Its operation cap counts the recorded
source and boundary orbit operations \emph{plus} all Tietze pivots.  This
is an operation-record cap, not a machine-instruction bound.  Callback
exceptions propagate, including falsey callback objects.  Recordless
production has the same census but does not attach a certificate.

The implementation lives in six new modules:
\path{free_group_elimination.py}, its independent verifier,
\path{source_cut_group.py}, its independent verifier,
\path{normal_cut_groups.py}, and its independent verifier.
The existing whole-cut producer and dispatcher supply the shared
portfolio integration.  Original delivered files remain immutable in
\path{reports/111}; the integration is adapted to the maintained API,
not an overwrite by the older delivered wrapper.

\subsection{Fresh validation and remaining work}

The exact delivered patch applied cleanly in a scratch copy at its pinned
base, and its 33 focused tests passed.  In the maintained integration,
37 focused tests pass, including four new tests for single discovery per
backend, exact cycle and replay-operation boundaries, replay with
producer helpers disabled, malformed nested boundary proofs, recordless
agreement and cooperative cancellation.  Logs and reproducible drivers
are retained under \path{synthesis/data/euler-group-*}.

The delivered packet runner initially accepted 64 of 76 saved packets.
It rejected the remaining twelve before replay because their source
records include a \path{source_three_handles} annotation.  The separate
maintained replay driver admits exactly that extra annotation and checks
it against the authenticated core.  It does not change the saved
certificates or loosen their source verifiers.  The original failed run
is retained alongside the corrected replay evidence.

The fresh separate-process audit compares the maintained producer with
commit \code{02f95a64a} on all 120 native cuts.  The old 111 positive
certificates are byte-identical, and all 112 new positives independently
replay.  The added case is \path{pilot/finite_figureEight/0}: two handlebodies
of genera one and two, with $\chi=-1$ and total free rank three.  Its source
presentation has 25 generators, 22 relators and 53 letters.  A fresh literal
Regina cut with 319 tetrahedra confirms the same histogram.  This is a
recognition-coverage gain, not a before/after speed comparison for the old
inconclusive case.  The corrected saved-packet run independently verifies
all 76 unchanged delivered certificates with producer helpers disabled.

The broad run executed 1774 tests in 685.763 seconds under Python 3.13.14
with Regina available: 1772 passed, and two historical assertions failed
because they expected the old fixed-forest geometric policy to refuse.
Both tests now explicitly set \path{group_fallback=False} for those
historical comparisons.  Their default-policy positive checks and
independent replay assertions remain intact.  The two corrected tests
pass in the targeted rerun.  There were no skips or dependency errors.
All 1774 tests are therefore covered across the broad run and the two-test
correction run; this is not reported as a clean single full-suite run.
The original failures and both the first one-test and final two-test
rerun logs are retained.

The source hash recorded by the corpus audit precedes three docstring-only
clarifications in the wrapper.  The publication check retains that exact
tested wrapper, checks its hash against the audit, and compares executable
ASTs with docstrings removed against the maintained wrapper.  All other
runtime hashes match exactly.  The timing evidence binds the final runtime
hashes.  This documentation-only difference is not hidden by rewriting the
original audit results.
