\section{Structural certificates and compressed complexes: October continuation}
\label{sec:structural}

\subsection{Intake, implementation, and scope}
Report 07~\cite{structural07} extends the Python recognizer at a pinned
repository snapshot. Its patch was reviewed and integrated on 7 October 2026
as version 0.3.0. The delivered archive stays unchanged; this section is the
maintained account of the implementation. The Rust implementation still uses
the earlier pipeline. The complete integrated Python suite passes all 69
tests on CPython 3.13.14, including randomized comparisons with the inherited
scanner, generic set algebra, independent cube calculations in the inherited
suite, certificate replay, and stagewise checks that $d^2=0$.
These checks support the implementation; they are not formal proofs of its
algebra or of the topological theorems it imports.

The new default first tries a signed Seifert-graph certificate. If it is
inconclusive, the previous pipeline runs. The ordinary scanner remains the
default backend, because compression adds overhead on complexes that do not
split. Three optional backends provide exact component sharing, saturated
decision weights, and early Euler obstructions. They preserve the distinction
between a proved decision and resource exhaustion. No universal
quasi-polynomial running-time guarantee follows from this integration.

\subsection{A linear structural decision}
Let $D$ be a validated, oriented, one-component classical diagram, with $n$
crossings, writhe $r$, and $s$ Seifert circles. The Seifert graph has one
vertex per circle and one signed edge per crossing. Let $c_+$ and $c_-$ count
the connected components of its positive and negative spanning subgraphs,
including isolated vertices in both counts. Define
\[
 g_D=(n-s+1)/2,\qquad \delta=s+1-c_+-c_-.
\]
The Seifert surface consists of $s$ discs and $n$ bands and has one boundary
component; its Euler characteristic is $s-n=1-2g_D$.
Abe's criterion identifies $\delta=0$ with homogeneity. For a homogeneous
diagram, the canonical Seifert surface realizes knot genus
\cite[Theorems 2.3 and 3.4]{abe}. The Kawamura--Lobb bounds give
\[
 r-s+2c_+-1\ \le\ s(K)\ \le\ r+s-2c_-+1.
\]
Here $s(K)$ is Rasmussen's invariant, distinct from the circle count $s$;
the upper bound follows by applying the lower bound to the mirror
\cite{lobb,abe}. The interval width is $2\delta$.

Consequently the implementation certifies the unknot when $g_D=0$, a
nontrivial knot when $\delta=0$ and $g_D>0$, and a nontrivial knot when the
interval excludes zero. The first condition gives a spanning disc. The
second gives positive knot genus. The third uses $s(U)=0$. In every other
case the stage is inconclusive. In particular, the interval $[0,0]$ alone
does not certify triviality: the figure-eight knot is a homogeneous
positive-genus example with that interval.

The implementation follows the oriented smoothing and edge-pairing
involutions to label circles, then traverses each signed subgraph. This
costs $O(n)$ word operations after input validation and normalization.
Each word holds an $O(\log n)$-bit index; this statement does not include
reading arbitrarily long external edge labels. The separate certificate
verifier rebuilds orientation from the PD code and uses union by size with
path compression, costing $O(n\alpha(n))$ word operations. It checks all
counts and the decision criterion rather than trusting cached production
data. Both implementations share the classical one-component input promise
and the cited topology.

\subsection{What exact sharing represents}
At each scan frontier the inherited Bar--Natan complex has matching objects
and differential entries in the finite dot-mask algebra over $\F$.
Connect two objects whenever a differential entry between them is nonzero.
The connected components of this graph are literal direct summands.
The shared scanner stores
\[
 C\simeq\bigoplus_a\bigoplus_h T_a[h]^{\oplus p_{a,h}},
 \qquad p_a(z)=\sum_h p_{a,h}z^h\in\mathbb N[z].
\]
A template $T_a$ records a complete component with its minimum homological
degree subtracted. Its key includes the ordered matching identifiers,
relative degrees, and every differential target and coefficient. Equality
of complete keys exhibits a chain isomorphism. A hash collision cannot
identify unequal keys; a missed isomorphism merely loses a saving.
Multiplicity coefficients are nonnegative integers, never scalars in $\F$.
Two identical isolated generators have rank two, not rank zero.

Crossing extension is additive and commutes with shifts. Unit elimination
preserves direct sums: the Schur-complement correction
$\gamma\phi^{-1}\eta$ remains in the pivot's parent block. Thus it suffices
to extend and contract one representative, split its resulting differential
graph, and attach each child's normalization shift to its parent's weight.
Merging equal child keys adds their shifted weight polynomials. Induction
proves preservation of the represented chain-homotopy type. At closure all
nonzero differential entries are units and have been eliminated; summing
weights gives the exact unreduced rank and its raw homological-degree
counts. The inherited characteristic-two splitting yields the reduced rank
by dividing by two. The method does not output a homology basis, quantum
grading, or integral torsion.

\subsection{Safe decision compression and early obstructions}
The map $q(m)=\min(3,m)$ is a semiring homomorphism from nonnegative integer
bookkeeping to saturated addition and multiplication. Evaluating each
$p_a$ at one and applying $q$ therefore preserves the final total rank
capped at three. It discards absolute grading but does not modify the
differential algebra. For a validated knot, unreduced $\F$ rank two
identifies the unknot, while a capped rank of three identifies a nontrivial
knot, using the inherited rank criterion and~\cite{km}. No decision is
made from large multiplicities at an incomplete scan stage.

An early decision needs information about the actual remaining crossings.
Let $F_i$ extend through that suffix and close the diagram. The completed
component bound is
\[
 B_i=\sum_a p_a(1)\,|\chi(F_i(T_a))|\le\dim_{\F}H(C).
\]
This follows by additivity of homology and $|\chi(X)|\le\dim H(X)$ for
each completed summand. When a component splits, the triangle inequality
implies $B_i\le B_{i+1}$; at complete closure the bound equals the rank.
The multiplicity uses $p_a(1)$, not $p_a(-1)$, since shifts do not change
the absolute Euler characteristic of an individual copy.

The memoized suffix functional on a matching is
\[
 E_i(m)=2^{\ell_0}E_{i+1}(m_0)-2^{\ell_1}E_{i+1}(m_1),
 \qquad E_n(\varnothing)=1,
\]
where smoothing $j$ creates $\ell_j$ closed circles and matching $m_j$.
The terminal empty object here is the monoidal unit; the separate empty
input-PD convention returns one unknot of rank two. A component's completed
Euler value is $\sum_v(-1)^{h_v}E_i(m_v)$. Signed intermediate values must
remain exact: saturating them before subtraction would invalidate the
bound. Only the final nonnegative bound and multiplicities may be capped.
The implementation uses an explicit dependency stack, avoiding recursion
depth failures on long narrow diagrams.

The default Euler budget registers at most 4096 suffix states. Exhaustion
disables this optional inference and continues the saturated scanner.
Object or time exhaustion instead propagates to an \code{UNKNOWN} result.
An early result exposes \code{rank\_lower\_bound\_capped}; a completed
decision exposes \code{rank\_capped}. Neither is reported as an exact rank
when it equals three.

\subsection{The conditional complexity statement}
Fix the complete scan order. Let $w$ bound every frontier, including those
in an unvisited suffix after an early decision, and let $b$ bound the size
of every differential component after elimination and before sharing.
These are execution parameters, not knot invariants or conjecturally
optimal decompositions. Write $M(0)=1$ and $M(w)=(w-1)!!$ for positive even
$w$. The number of complete template keys of size at most $b$ is at most
\[
 K(b,w)=\sum_{t=1}^{b}t^t M(w)^t 2^{t^2 2^{w/2}}.
\]
For a numbered connected component with $t$ vertices, relative degrees
lie in $\{0,\ldots,t-1\}$, giving $t^t$ assignments. There are at most
$M(w)$ boundary matchings per vertex. A morphism has at most $2^{w/2}$
coefficient bits, so it has up to $2^{2^{w/2}}$ possible values. Allowing
all $t^2$ entries gives the last factor. Confusing coefficient bits with
the number of morphisms would incorrectly remove an exponential here.

At a compressed stage there are at most $bK(b,w)$ physical objects. A
crossing creates at most eight objects per parent before elimination:
each of its two smoothings can create at most two circles. Matrix
elimination is polynomial in physical size; coefficient operations cost
$2^{O(w)}$ times polynomial factors. After $j$ crossings the virtual
expanded state has at most $8^j$ objects, so each multiplicity coefficient
has $O(j)$ bits and each exact weight has at most $j+1$ degree positions.
The conservative bit bound, including exact multiplicity arithmetic, is
\[
 T(n;b,w)\le n^{O(1)}(bK(b,w))^{O(1)}2^{O(w)}.
\]
The suffix Euler table has at most $(n+1)M(w)$ states with $O(n)$-bit
values and fits within the same conservative bound.

In particular $b^2 2^{w/2}=O(\log^2(n+2))$ suffices for
$n^{O(\log n)}$ time. This includes fixed $w$ with $b=O(\log n)$, or
fixed $b$ with $w\le4\log_2\log(n+2)+O(1)$. The order must be supplied
or efficiently constructed. Neither the existence of such orders for
every knot nor the required component-size bound has been established.
Small experimental values do not prove either statement. Moreover,
report 07 supplies RI/RII-reduced unknot diagrams of homogeneity defect
one with arbitrarily large canonical genus; defect alone cannot close
the missing bound. The hierarchy obligations in Section~\ref{sec:missing}
remain a separate unresolved route.

\subsection{Local measurements and reproducibility}
The integrated benchmark \code{fast/benchmark\_structural.py} records
seven randomized paired rounds, with five fresh Diagram records per arm
and an identical-baseline A/A control. Parsing, validation, imports, and
serialization are excluded from every arm. The baseline disables the
Seifert stage; the treatment enables it. These are recognition-function
timings, not end-to-end command-line times. The raw record is
\code{fast/results/structural\_integration\_20261007.json}.

On this host the median paired baseline/new ratios were 27.10 for a
1000-crossing weaving braid, 26.34 for a 1000-crossing positive three-braid,
and 50.56 for a 1004-crossing positive five-braid. Their median A/A ratios
were 0.993, 1.002, and 1.008. Inconclusive controls show the added cost:
Conway 0.97, hard unknot 0.95, and the tiny scrambled-grid unknot 0.60.
Ratios below one mean the added stage slowed the recognition call.
These are descriptive measurements with host-dependent variability, not
fitted complexity exponents or hardware-independent speed guarantees.

Separate raw-scanner checks bypass all geometric factorization and
recognition filters. Exact sharing agrees with the standard rank and
degree counts on one and two Conway summands; eight summands give
$2\cdot33^8=2{,}812{,}817{,}236{,}482$ without expanding that many
generators. This demonstrates representation compression. It does not
claim a corresponding recognition speedup: the existing recognition
pipeline already factors visible connected sums. The archive's original
timing observations remain separate from these local checks.
