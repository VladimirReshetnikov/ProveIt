\section{Fraction-free terminal kernels and the cost of switching}
\label{sec:integer-terminal}

Section~\ref{sec:dynamic-terminal-review} found that direct integer quotient
elimination beat the rational observer on small native graphs. This does not
mean every query should repeat the interior elimination. We now retain the
terminal-kernel structure while preparing and querying it with integers.
The validated fraction-free kernel becomes the boundary research observer's
default; \texttt{arithmetic='rational'} retains the earlier reference. Neither
observer is dispatched by ordinary knot recognition. The improvement changes
exact arithmetic cost, not the meaning or sufficiency of a boundary observation.

\subsection{An integer border with a determinant scale}

Ground terminal zero, and order the remaining coordinates as $h$ interior
vertices followed by $t=b-1$ terminals. Perform fraction-free elimination
while a nonzero pivot remains in the interior block. Row and column swaps
are allowed independently within that block. With previous pivot
$\Delta_{k-1}$, initially one, and current pivot $p$, update trailing entries by
\[
 a'_{ij}=\frac{p a_{ij}-a_{ik}a_{kj}}{\Delta_{k-1}}.
\]
Every division is checked for zero remainder. The bordered-minor identity
underlying Bareiss elimination guarantees exactness \cite{bareiss-terminal}.
A zero diagonal is insufficient to stop: off-diagonal interior pivots are
searched too, and both permutation signs are recorded. Only a wholly zero
remaining interior block ends preparation.

After $\rho$ pivots put $r=h-\rho$, let $\Delta\ne0$ be the last pivot
(one if none were taken), and let $\epsilon$ be the accumulated swap sign.
The retained integer matrix $K$ equals $\Delta$ times the remaining rational
Schur complement. It has a zero $r\times r$ interior corner. Independent
row and column pivots can destroy symmetry, so its two coupling blocks are
not assumed to be transposes.

For a partition with $q$ non-root terminal blocks, sum rows and columns of
$K$ within those blocks and discard terminals in the root block, retaining
the null directions. Write the resulting integer border as $K_\pi$ and set
$d=r+q$. Its exact quotient cofactor is
\[
 \tau_\pi=\begin{cases}
 0,&r>q,\\
 \epsilon\Delta,&d=0,\\
 \displaystyle\epsilon\frac{\det K_\pi}{\Delta^{d-1}},&d>0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The final division is checked as well. The empty-border case is essential:
eliminating every coordinate leaves the eliminated determinant, not an empty
determinant divided by an integer power with negative exponent.

\begin{proof}
The selected interior rows and columns form a nonsingular $\rho\times\rho$
block of the permuted source, of determinant $\Delta$. Terminal identification
does not affect these coordinates. Schur elimination therefore factors each
quotient determinant as $\epsilon\Delta$ times its remaining Schur determinant.
The integer border scales all $d$ remaining rows by $\Delta$, so its
determinant acquires a factor $\Delta^d$. This proves the formula for $d>0$;
the empty Schur determinant gives the $d=0$ case. If $r>q$, the $r$ null rows
couple to only $q$ terminal columns, forcing zero. A failed exact division
is consequently an arithmetic failure, never evidence about knot type.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Independent replay and resource bounds}

\path{../fast/fastunknot/terminal_determinant.py} stores immutable border rows,
the scale, sign, source labels and interior pivot positions. Its independent
diagnostic verifier replays those positions with rational Schur subtraction,
checks nonzero pivots, multiplies the rational pivots, and compares the scaled
residual entry by entry. It checks that the claimed null interior is zero.
It never calls the constructor or its fraction-free recurrence. This binds
the kernel to its supplied matrix; no new serialized knot-certificate
protocol is claimed.

Preparation costs $O(v^3)$ integer operations and $O(v^2)$ space. If
$r\le b-1$, a query uses $O(b^2+(r+q)^3)$ integer operations on a border of
size at most $2(b-1)$; if $r>b-1$, every partition returns zero immediately.
This matches the earlier static kernel's arithmetic bound while avoiding
repeated fraction normalization. It is not a uniform bit-complexity speedup.
For source entries of at most $\tau$ bits, bordered minors have
$O(v(\tau+\log(v+1)))$ bits. Aggregation adds logarithmic factors, and the
final numerator and denominator can acquire another factor $O(b)$ in their
bit lengths before exact cancellation. These bounds are polynomial for
explicit input, but individually reduced fractions can be more economical
than a common scale on some matrices.

Construction, query elimination and diagnostic replay accept a cooperative
callback. Interrupted construction publishes no kernel. Queries use fresh
mutable border storage and preserve the prepared kernel. The boundary adapter
keeps its geometry checks, phases, component counts and normalized partition
cache. Its existing whole-adapter deadline limitations remain; an algebra
callback alone does not establish a production resource contract.

Four new tests compare 8,000 random signed-graph quotients with independently
built full integer cofactors, and replay each kernel independently. They
exercise off-diagonal pivots, singular interiors, empty quotients, nonunit
scales, malformed input, corrupted kernels and interruption. Twenty-five
actual random knot diagrams compare all boundary values and metadata against
explicit rational mode. Before the default change, all 782 maintained tests
passed with Regina in 167.115 seconds; the new default is checked separately
against the full suite and native completion audit. The final default passes
all 782 tests with Regina in 182.267 seconds. Across 78 diagrams and 582
stages, all 4,503 native queries agree with independently rebuilt completions,
including 15 singular rational-interior cases. Of 3,468 additional arbitrary
pairings, 745 are accepted and independently agree, 472 classical pairings
are declined by the inherited-color contract, and 2,251 nonclassical pairings
are rejected. These totals match the earlier geometry audit. Every prepared
kernel in this audit also passes independent rational source replay.

\subsection{Larger native stages and a switching experiment}

The benchmark pins the rational observer at \texttt{7951ae852} and includes
an identical control. Other arms use direct full integer quotient elimination,
the reusable integer kernel, or a candidate policy that handles four distinct
connected partitions directly and prepares the integer kernel on the fifth.
All share the same normalized partition cache. Cache hits do not consume
another distinct-query allowance. The four-query threshold is an experiment,
not an optimal stopping theorem.

In addition to the previous 4,503 native observations, we extend an actual
five-strand scanner prefix by copies of the identity braid word
$\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{-1}$. This preserves its closure
while enlarging both face shadings, producing selected graphs with 17, 41
and 73 vertices and nine terminals. Repeating only one generator's cancelling
pair would keep the selected graph at nine vertices. We measure one connected
query, the full 200-query stream, and 200 repeats of one connected query.
The completions are actual maintained prefix states; these constructed
workloads separate setup, reuse and caching, rather than represent hard knots.

Fresh PD validation, geometry, preparation, queries and cache activity are
timed. Input generation, output hashing and independent verification are
excluded. Seed 261008119 shuffles one warmup round and five measured rounds
for five arms and eight workloads: 40 warmups and 200 measured samples. The
smallest one-query case uses ten fresh replays per sample. All complete values
and metadata agree across every arm and round. Raw samples are in
\path{../fast/results/integer_terminal_20261008.json}.
The measured integer implementation is frozen at \texttt{fc8b7da1c}, before
changing the research constructor's default; the benchmark selected integer
mode explicitly. The later default change and explicit rational-reference
test are covered by the separate final validation above.

Across the original native corpus, the reusable integer kernel has median
271.8 ms against 340.2 ms for the rational observer, with paired speedup 1.229.
On the full 73-vertex stage it takes 22.8 ms against 439.0 ms, with paired
speedup 19.223. Direct integer evaluation still takes 355.3 ms there because
it repeats the interior work on 25 distinct partitions. The four-query policy
takes 77.1 ms: paying for four large full quotients before setup costs more
than preparing immediately. With only one distinct query, the adaptive arm
correctly avoids setup even when that query is repeated 200 times. On the
small native corpus it reduces kernel preparations from 582 to 128, but its
275.2 ms median does not beat the reusable kernel's 271.8 ms. Identical-control
paired ratios range from 0.985 to 1.019 across the workloads.

These results justify the integer kernel as the research observer's default,
but not the candidate switching rule. A future adaptive policy should account
for the actual cost of discarded direct-query work, especially as the interior
grows. The measured improvement is at the observer boundary, not a full-knot
recognition speedup, and leaves the global quasi-polynomial problem open.
