\section{A source-bound primitive-power terminal for compressed knot groups}
\label{sec:primitive-power-terminal}

Report 45's arithmetic kernel was previously reproduced but deliberately
kept outside the knot-verdict path. The missing step was a terminal contract:
the old compressed search succeeded only after reaching one generator and
no nonempty relators. A primitive-power relation at rank two does not meet
that syntactic condition. We now add a distinct version-five terminal,
replay its entire presentation prefix, and independently verify its
arithmetic evidence on the reconstructed relator. This adds a positive
stopping rule to the existing optional compressed group route.

\subsection{The minimum-width arithmetic test}

For a nonempty positive two-letter word, let $P,Q$ count its letters,
$d=\gcd(P,Q)$, and $(p,q)=(P/d,Q/d)$. The prefix-height walk assigns
weight $q$ to the first letter and $-p$ to the second. Its total height is
zero. Write $\Omega$ for the difference between its maximum and minimum
prefix heights, including the empty prefix.

When both counts are positive, each step changes height by $q$ modulo
$\ell=p+q$. Coprimality makes the first $\ell$ prefixes visit all residues,
so $\Omega\ge\ell-1$. If equality holds, all heights lie in an interval
of exactly $\ell$ consecutive integers. The two possible next heights,
$h+q$ and $h-p$, differ by $\ell$, and exactly one remains in that interval.
Thus the next letter is forced by the present height. The walk repeats
the same length-$\ell$ rotation cycle $d$ times. That cycle is a cyclic
conjugate of the Christoffel word with counts $(p,q)$.

Euclidean division of $(p,q)$ expresses its Christoffel word through
Nielsen maps $a\mapsto a$, $b\mapsto a^k b$, or
$a\mapsto ab^k$, $b\mapsto b$, until one generator remains. Hence the
cycle is primitive in $F(a,b)$. Conversely, the rank-two primitive
conjugacy classification used in report 45 identifies every cyclically
reduced primitive word, after independent generator inversions, with
such a cycle. Therefore a nonempty cyclically reduced word is a power of
a primitive element exactly when each generator occurs with only one sign
and its normalized width is $|u|+|v|-1$. Here
\[
 (A,B)=d(u,v),\qquad d=\gcd(|A|,|B|),
\]
where $A,B$ are signed exponent sums. The signed-letter weights are
$(v,-v,-u,u)$ on $(a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1})$.
A one-letter power has width zero and primitive vector $(\pm1,0)$ or
$(0,\pm1)$, so it is included.

For a binary grammar, prefix profiles form a small exact concatenation
summary. If $U$ has total, minimum and maximum $(s,l,h)$ and $V$ has
$(t,L,H)$, then
\[
 \operatorname{profile}(UV)
   =(s+t,\min(l,s+L),\max(h,s+H)).
\]
Four signed occurrence counts also add at concatenations. These recurrences
use binary integers and never enumerate represented letters. The native
query uses the maintained \texttt{WordArena} directly, preserving its
original generator names and relator slots. It creates no word nodes and
calls no equality, longest-prefix or normalization routine.

\subsection{One shared slope for the intact rank-two state}

At a two-generator presentation of a knot group, the relator exponent rows
span one rational line: the abelianization is $\mathbb Z$. The producer
computes a primitive direction $(u,v)$ from the first nonzero row, checks
collinearity of every row by $vA-uB=0$, and performs one shared profile
pass over the reachable grammar. It keeps every root slot, including empty
and repeated roots. A nonzero, sign-coherent root with the required width
supplies the terminal witness. Its exponent and signed vector are derived
from its own counts, so inverse-oriented roots are handled correctly.

All sign-incoherent roots are skipped. This makes the positive test safe
even if a caller provides a raw grammar: sign coherence itself implies
free and cyclic reduction, since inverse letters cannot occur. Absence
of a hit on an arbitrary raw root is not a complete primitivity decision;
normalizing a general compressed input remains a separate cost. On the
cyclically reduced states maintained by the knot search, the report's
criterion applies directly. Noncollinear input is outside the shared-slope
query's supported family and yields no terminal evidence.

The query runs when the compressed search reaches two live generators,
before building a general elimination plan or searching for further
Whitehead and overlap moves. This position matters in the actual corpus:
a prototype placed after singleton detection had no positive activations,
because all tested successful states already contained an eliminable
relation. Moving the exact stopping rule before that work permits avoiding
the final substitution and normalization across all relator slots. The
measurements below determine the resulting whole-call effect.

\subsection{Why the source-bound terminal proves an unknot}

The certificate checker begins with a validated classical one-component
planar diagram. It independently reconstructs the full Wirtinger
presentation and replays every elimination, Whitehead and relator move
in the supplied prefix. Those moves preserve the presented group, and
no unaccounted relator slot is discarded. Suppose that the resulting
presentation has exactly two live generators and contains a relator
$w^d$, with $d>0$ and $w$ primitive.

A classical knot group is torsion-free, so $w^d=1$ implies $w=1$ in that
group. Since $w$ belongs to a free basis of the rank-two free group, its
normal quotient is infinite cyclic. The full presented group is therefore
a quotient of a cyclic group. Its abelianization is still $\mathbb Z$,
so it must itself be infinite cyclic. The classical knot-group unknot
criterion then implies that the original diagram represents the unknot.
For exponent one the root-killing step does not require torsion-freeness.
These are imported topological facts, as in the report's source-bound
argument, not new claims about arbitrary presentations.

Other relator slots need not be individually shown redundant after this
terminal is established, but all of them must have been retained in the
verified presentation prefix. The distinction is essential: the abstract
presentation $\langle a,b\mid a^d\rangle$ for $d>1$ also has a local
primitive-power witness and is not an infinite cyclic knot group.
Neither the arithmetic producer nor its arithmetic checker alone returns
a knot verdict or accepts a caller's torsion-free flag.

\subsection{Version five, independent replay and bounded execution}

A successful terminal proof retains the usual method, status, original PD
and move prefix. Version five replaces \texttt{remaining\_generator} by
\texttt{terminal}, containing its kind, original current relation slot,
ordered live generator pair, two signed primitive coordinates, positive
exponent and width. The outer checker rejects extra or missing fields,
unknown versions and mismatched diagrams. Versions one through four
continue to require the original rank-one terminal.

After independent prefix replay, the explicit checker walks the selected
literal relator and recomputes signed counts and prefix extrema. The
compressed checker instead performs a fresh postorder traversal of that
root's grammar, distinct from the producer's sorted reachable-node scan.
Both check exactly two live generators, the selected slot, strict integer
types, primitive gcd, exponent products, sign coherence and minimum width.
The proof's numerical fields are bounded by the reconstructed word length
before gcd or profile arithmetic, so forged oversized metadata cannot
inflate the replay calculation. Neither route calls the query producer,
selects a gcd direction for search, or runs Whitehead discovery.

Every arithmetic pass charges the existing work budget and propagates
external cancellation. The kernel allocates no new SLP nodes; its temporary
summaries are proportional to the reachable grammar, already bounded by
the enclosing node cap. These are cooperative abstract allowances, not
hard bounds on Python integer-instruction time or process memory. Search
exhaustion remains inconclusive. An unaffordable explicit replay can fail
its resource allowance even where compressed replay succeeds; it never
silently expands beyond that allowance.

The low-level \texttt{compressed\_certificate} option
\texttt{primitive\_power} defaults to true and can be disabled for the old
search path. The ordinary PD dispatcher still requires the existing
compressed-group opt-in. The explicit search and its representation-switching
handoff retain their old terminal contract. Internally, the compressed
search enables rank-two success only when its caller supplies a distinct
terminal-output record; the old call form still means one generator and
no relators. Thus an abstract internal success cannot accidentally be
encoded with a fabricated remaining generator.

\subsection{Local cost and what remains unproved}

Let $S$ count reachable grammar nodes, $M$ all allocated node identifiers,
$m$ current relator slots and $B$ the largest represented-length bit size.
The native query uses $O(S\log(S+1)+m)$ structural operations and a
constant number of additions/comparisons per profile node, plus gcd and
collinearity arithmetic. The sorting term comes from the maintained
reachable-node ordering; it is absent from the report's already ordered
standalone grammar scan. Prefix heights have $O(B)$ bits, since their
absolute value is at most the product of word length and a slope component.

With conventional dictionary access, a conservative arithmetic/identifier
bit accounting is
\[
 O\bigl(SB+S\log(S+1)\log(M+1)+Sb
        +m(B^2+\log(M+1))\bigr),
\]
where $b$ bounds generator-label encoding. The quadratic term permits
ordinary integer multiplication and gcd bounds. Replay needs only the
selected root's postorder summaries and the claimed primitive vector;
it does not repeat the all-relator slope search. Python hash tables retain
the usual implementation-model qualification; these counts are not an
unconditional adversarial dictionary-time theorem.

This polynomial local test removes a potentially long sequence of
normalizations or substitutions once a suitable rank-two state exists.
It bounds neither the cost of reaching that state nor aggregate grammar
growth during the preceding presentation search. Higher-rank disjoint-pair
contractions from report 45 still need a complete independently replayed
projection contract and a controlled schedule. No general quasipolynomial
unknot-recognition bound follows from the new terminal.

\subsection{Native audits and whole-recognition evidence}

The pinned maintained baseline is \texttt{cd77d1bee8fa}. Its actual group
host, compressed search and compressed replay are loaded in a separate
package. Relevant downstream modules are checked byte-for-byte against
that baseline. The algebraic audit compares all 29,540 cyclically reduced
words through length nine with the independent literal KMP-root/Whitehead
oracle, report 45's delivered arithmetic producer and checker, and native
arithmetic replay. All results agree, including 844 primitive-power
positives.

The source audit checks 74 actual diagrams: the nineteen-case research
corpus and 55 valid single-component braids from a fixed 240-attempt random
sample. Disabling the new terminal yields exactly the old producer's
certificate or nondecision on every input. Every new positive certificate
passes both full source-reconstructing replay paths. Any newly closed
state would additionally be checked with the complete independent
recognizer; none occurred in this sample. Recorded data do not treat a
failed positive group search as proof of knottedness.

Eight focused tests cover short exhaustive words, 300 random parses,
large generator identifiers, repeated and empty slots, noncollinear and
sign-incoherent inputs, forged numerical fields, schema/source mutations,
caller cancellation and shared exhaustion. A nonuniform Fibonacci grammar
raised to exponent $2^{5000}+1$ passes query and replay with expansion,
equality and longest-prefix methods disabled. A separate actual-diagram
proof has a powered Whitehead prefix of exponent $2^{1024}$; compressed
source replay succeeds with query discovery and expansion disabled, while
explicit replay correctly exceeds its finite allowance. These are
capacity and verifier tests, not difficult-diagram speedup claims.
The full maintained suite passes 974 tests with Regina in 210.163 seconds.
The diagram audit contains 44 version-five terminal hits, all with exponent
one. There are no observed proper-power hits and no newly solved diagrams.
The more general torsion-free rule is therefore supported by its proof
and algebraic tests, not by a claim of proper-power activity in this corpus.
Raw audit results are in \path{data/primitive-power-native-audit.json}.

The complete-recognition benchmark uses nineteen actual diagrams, four
shuffled arms, five measured rounds and one excluded warmup: 380 measured
calls and 76 warmups. Every measured call completes with the expected
verdict. Each starts with fresh PD validation and includes the compressed
group search, source reconstruction and independent proof replay when
that stage is reached. The old host and its old compressed search/replay
modules are pinned together; the comparison does not silently run old
search through the new terminal checker. Identical old and new arms
measure timing variation. The 43.282-second run retains all inputs,
certificates, orders, elapsed samples, charged work and arena statistics
in \path{../fast/results/primitive_power_pipeline_20261008.json}.

The elapsed results are mostly close to unchanged. Survivor 00 has
old/new medians 16.892/16.588 ms and paired ratio 1.015. Its charged search
work falls from 17,998 to 17,879, but the terminal record makes its compact
certificate 1,216 bytes instead of 1,160. Mirror 03 has ratio 0.985,
with arena nodes decreasing from 617 to 615 and charged work from
25,558 to 25,431. Mirror 08 has ratio 1.052; this is a small measured
improvement, not the large literal-baseline ratio advertised for the
standalone delivered arithmetic kernel.

On Gordian, search work decreases from 1,215,916 to 1,215,145, and one
longest-prefix call is avoided. The arena still has 9,025 nodes and
largest word bit length thirteen. Certificate size increases from 9,607
to 9,663 bytes. Whole-call medians are 1,500.813 and 1,512.295 ms, with
paired ratio 0.998; no speedup is established. The expensive prefix
leading to rank two dominates this input. The new certificate replaces
one final elimination; it does not accelerate that prefix.

The old identical-arm ratios across the corpus range from 0.974 to 1.016;
the new controls range from 0.978 to 1.046. Survivor 02 has paired ratio
0.964 and new-control ratio 1.046. Nontrivial-knot examples resolved before
the group stage also exhibit small variations. These controls preclude
interpreting every few-percent difference as an algorithmic effect.
Arena node counts and exact certificate bytes are retained as storage
measures; process peak memory was not measured. No result is censored in
this run, but the driver retains incomplete calls and excludes them from
completed medians and paired ratios.

This integration supplies a sound compressed stopping rule and removes
some exact work at the observed terminal states. Its current whole-diagram
timing benefit is small or absent. The next research question is whether
certified transformations expose primitive-power relators earlier, or
support independently replayed higher-rank pair contractions with bounded
aggregate grammar growth. Merely repeating a polynomial local query does
not establish the required global bound.
