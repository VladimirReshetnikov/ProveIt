\section{Exact compressed-word replay without expanding relators}
\label{sec:compressed-words}

\subsection{Separating word representation from proof search}

The group-search implementations above store their relators as explicit
letter lists.  This makes free reduction straightforward, but ties their
operation counts and storage ceilings to expanded length.  The new
\code{compressed\_words.py} implements a deterministic compressed-word
kernel, and \code{compressed\_group.py} uses it to replay the same group
certificates.  The kernel represents finite words by binary straight-line
programs (SLPs), retaining concatenation and repeated subwords as a DAG.
It supports exact equality, substrings, inversion, free reduction, cyclic
reduction, simultaneous letter substitution and occurrence queries.

The equality and reduction algorithms follow the constructive account of
Plandowski's and Lohrey's results in Schleimer
\cite{schleimer-compressed}, especially Sections~3, 7 and~8.  These are
existing polynomial algorithms; the contribution here is an exact,
resource-bounded implementation and its integration into certificate replay.
No probabilistic fingerprint or assumed hash-collision bound decides word
equality.  Python dictionaries index exact rule tuples and assertions,
with ordinary collision-resolving equality checks.

The initial integration was a representation improvement for verification.
The default producer still uses explicit relators.  The optional compressed
search developed below extends the representation to discovery.  The compressed
checker does not by itself construct a short proof, control all grammar
growth over a long search, or prove general quasi-polynomial recognition.
Its present practical cost is also higher on most measured knot queries,
so it is separately opt-in.

\subsection{The immutable word arena}

Node zero represents the empty word.  Other nodes either contain one
signed generator or concatenate two earlier nonempty nodes.  Identical
rules share a node.  Each node caches its length and endpoint letters;
lengths and substring coordinates use exact integers.  A binary program
with $N$ nonempty nodes represents words of length at most $2^N$, so
these coordinates have $O(N)$ bits, apart from the separate bit cost of
generator names.  Repeated squaring constructs powers using logarithmically
many concatenations.

A substring operation descends only at the two interval boundaries,
reusing complete covered subtrees.  It creates $O(h)$ nodes for input
height $h$ without increasing that height.  Explicit stacks avoid Python
recursion limits.  Inversion reverses concatenation order and negates
terminal letters, sharing memoized inverse nodes.  Occurrence counting
uses a topological pass: a generator count at a concatenation is the sum
of its child counts.  Following the child with a positive count locates
the first occurrence and its sign without reading preceding letters.

The arena has separate limits on work and allocated nodes.  The node
allowance also caps live equality assertions and the equality-result
cache.  Exceeding any limit raises an inconclusive resource exception,
never a guessed equality result.  Cached nodes are immutable; an equality
result cannot become stale after a later operation.  Diagnostic expansion
is explicitly bounded and is not called by compressed equality, reduction
or certificate replay.

\subsection{Equality by splitting and periodicity compaction}

For clarity, treat the two compared programs as disjoint tagged copies,
even when they share arena nodes.  An assertion $(U,V,i)$ aligns $V$ at
offset $i$ in $U$ and requires equality on the intersecting interval.
If $|U|\le i+|V|$, it compares a suffix of $U$ with a prefix of $V$;
otherwise it asserts that $V$ occurs inside $U$.  A proper prefix inclusion
at offset zero is written with the two symbols interchanged.  Root
length and endpoint mismatches are immediate rejections.  Equal root
identities are immediate exact acceptances.

Start with $(A,B,0)$.  At each round choose a mentioned nonterminal of
maximum expanded length, and replace its assertions by assertions involving
its two children.  The new assertions are exactly the intersections of the
old comparison interval with the child intervals.  Terminal comparisons
are checked directly.  Every split preserves the conjunction of all
assertions.  Each selected nonterminal disappears permanently, because
nonempty children have strictly smaller length.

Splitting alone can create too many overlapping comparisons.  For an
ordered pair $(U,V)$, consider three suffix--prefix assertions at offsets
$i<j<k$.  If
\[
 (j-i)+(k-i)\le |U|-i,
\]
replace those three assertions by the assertions at offsets
\[
 i,\qquad i+\gcd(j-i,k-i).
\]
Two overlapping copies imply a period of the relevant suffix.  The displayed
inequality allows the periodicity lemma to replace the two periods by their
greatest common divisor.  Thus this compaction is logically equivalent,
including when the compared words are unequal.  It is not merely a way of
sampling a few positions.

The code groups assertions by ordered symbol pair and repeatedly checks
consecutive triples of sorted offsets.  This suffices: if any triple
starting at $i$ meets the inequality, the next two offsets after $i$ are
no larger and also meet it.  When no triple can be compacted, every two
successive gaps advance more than half the remaining suffix length.
There are consequently $O(\log |U|)=O(N)$ overlap assertions per pair.
Across $O(N^2)$ symbol pairs there are $O(N^3)$ overlap assertions; at most
$O(N)$ split rounds accumulate $O(N^4)$ subword assertions.  Our elementary
sorting-and-compaction implementation therefore has a polynomial bound in
the grammar size and coordinate bit lengths.  Its high worst-case degree
is not claimed to match the best compressed-string algorithms.

Tests compare the implementation with literal strings on 400 random
30-rule grammars.  They also force compaction on distinct parses of periodic
words and on a same-length interior mutation with identical endpoints.
The latter exercises rejection through the actual equality procedure,
rather than through an endpoint or length shortcut.

\subsection{Free reduction from exact longest-prefix queries}

Binary search on the prefix length, using SLP substring construction and
exact equality, computes the longest common prefix.  It uses
$O(\log L)$ equality calls for expanded length $L$; since the SLP length is
binary-encoded, this number is polynomial in the grammar size.

If $u$ and $v$ are freely reduced, all cancellations in their product occur
at their common boundary.  Let
\[
 c=\operatorname{lcp}(u^{-1},v).
\]
Then its freely reduced form is
\[
 u[0:|u|-c]\;v[c:|v|].
\]
A bottom-up traversal applies this formula at every concatenation node.
When the boundary letters are not inverse, no prefix query is necessary.
The reduced children and their slices remain compressed and shared.
A second prefix comparison against the inverse word, capped at half its
length, removes the conjugating prefix and suffix for cyclic reduction.

For a single input SLP, reduction does not increase expanded length or
parse height.  Boundary slicing and the logarithmic prefix search create
only polynomially many additional rules over the original nodes.
Inversion, occurrence counting and one simultaneous substitution followed
by reduction are likewise polynomial operations in their encoded inputs.
However, repeatedly applying polynomial transformations need not maintain
polynomial-size intermediate grammars in the original knot size.  A bound
on the total constructed grammar size over the search remains a separate
obligation.

One exact test compares the two differently parsed words
\[
 (ab)^{2^{100}}a=a(ba)^{2^{100}}
\]
and freely reduces their quotient to the empty word.  Neither word is
expanded.  Another test uses a 1,500-level repeated-power grammar to check
substring and inversion operations without recursive-stack overflow.

\subsection{Replaying the existing knot certificates}

The public checker accepts \code{compressed=True}; the optional pipeline
flag is \code{--group-compressed}, and Python recognition uses
\code{use\_group=True, group\_compressed=True}.  Existing certificate
versions~1 and~2 are unchanged.  The independent PD reconstruction remains
the same as in the explicit checker, and only its small initial relators
are materialized.  The compressed engine then replays every move.

For a generator elimination, an exact DAG occurrence count must equal one.
Its position splits the defining relator into prefix, signed generator
and suffix; the derived replacement and its inverse are substituted into
all other relators with shared reduction.  For a Whitehead move, validated
letter images are substituted simultaneously.  For a relator-overlap
move, compressed rotations and inversions are formed, the stated prefix
equality is checked exactly, and the target is replaced using the same
normal-closure identity as before.  A distinct donor must remain present.
Terminal acceptance still requires one generator and no nonempty relators.

The original \code{max\_letters} bound applies to the reconstructed initial
presentation in compressed mode.  Intermediate expanded lengths are not
subject to that limit; \code{max\_nodes} and the work allowance instead
bound compressed replay.  The default node/assertion allowance is 100,000.
Local exhaustion propagates as \code{GroupLimit} and resumes recognition
when called through the optional stage.  Global cancellation propagates
unchanged.  Search and replay still share the same local wall allowance.
Evidence records whether replay used explicit letters or compressed SLPs.

The initial presentation construction and classical cyclic-knot-group
criterion are shared by the two checkers.  The compressed and explicit
word algorithms are distinct, but this is not two entirely unrelated
proof systems.  The regression suite checks every maintained survivor,
the two overlap-dependent mirrors and Gordian, along with forged moves,
resource exhaustion and actual CLI dispatch.

\subsection{Capacity gains, measured overhead, and their scope}

\code{benchmark\_compressed\_words.py} records whole-query, replay-only,
constructed-trace and word-kernel measurements separately.  Each query
starts from fresh validated PD data.  The 15 knot cases comprise twelve
original survivors, the two previously stalled mirrors and Gordian.
There are five measured randomized A/A/B rounds after one excluded warmup,
with explicit verification, a repeated explicit control, and compressed
verification.  Search settings are identical: relator moves enabled,
two local seconds, ten million work units and four global seconds.

On the fourteen small cases, the sums of median whole-query costs are
$51.02$ ms, $50.89$ ms and $137.53$ ms respectively.  On Gordian the medians
are $1.236$, $1.235$ and $1.417$ seconds.  All queries complete, but the
compressed verifier adds overhead on these inputs.  The default stays
explicit.  We do not present compression as a current general speedup.

To test representation capacity separately, construct valid but deliberately
inflated proof traces for a three-crossing unknot.  Apply alternating
Whitehead shears repeatedly, undo them, and append an ordinary certificate.
These are artificial verification stresses, not hard unknot-recognition
instances; a trace simplifier could cancel inverse moves.  At eight rounds,
both checkers finish, at medians $8.84$ ms explicit and $2.12$ ms compressed.
At sixteen, thirty-two and sixty-four rounds, the explicit verifier exhausts
its 200,000-letter allowance.  The compressed verifier finishes all three;
its medians are $5.69$, $18.28$ and $64.06$ ms.  The sixty-four-round trace
uses 904 SLP nodes and represents an intermediate node length with 90 bits.
The explicit limit observations are not completed verification times.

Finally, the noncanonical equality-and-cancellation kernel above, with
exponent $2^{100}$, completes in a median $0.676$ seconds using 10,614
allocated nodes and 835,846 charged work units.  This includes grammar
construction, equality and reduction.  Raw samples, actual synthetic
certificates, environment metadata and separate timing scopes are in
\path{fast/results/compressed_words_20261008.json}.

The resulting advance is exact access to words far larger than available
expanded storage.  The following extension adds compressed search
operations; a quantitative bound on the grammars and moves needed for
arbitrary knot diagrams remains open.  The complete recognition fallback remains
exponential; this implementation does not establish general sub-exponential
or quasi-polynomial unknot recognition.

\subsection{Compressed discovery: multiplicities and cyclic adjacency}
\label{sec:compressed-search}

The separately selectable \code{compressed\_search.py} now keeps relators
as SLP roots throughout elimination and Whitehead search.  The key
additional operation extracts the weighted Whitehead graph without
examining expanded letters.  Let $N$ be the number of reachable grammar
rules, $m$ the number of relator slots and $r$ the surviving generator
rank.  Seed each nonempty root with multiplicity one, adding multiplicities
when several relator slots share that root.  Visit rules in reverse
topological order.  At $X=UV$, add the multiplicity of $X$ to both children,
even when $U=V$.  At a terminal, add its multiplicity to the absolute
generator's total occurrence count.

This computes exact counts: the coefficient of a node is the number of its
occurrences in the fully unfolded derivation forest.  Distributing a
coefficient through a concatenation partitions these occurrences into its
two child occurrences.  Induction down the DAG proves the terminal counts.
It also proves the following adjacency construction.  At each $X=UV$ with
multiplicity $c_X$, insert weight $c_X$ on the undirected edge
\[
 \{\operatorname{last}(U),-\operatorname{first}(V)\}.
\]
For each nonempty relator root, insert its closing edge with weight one.
Every internal adjacent pair in an unfolded word has exactly one lowest
concatenation separating its letters, so this counts each pair exactly once.
Singleton relators receive just their closing edge. Empty relators contribute
nothing. As with the explicit implementation, both signed vertices are
retained, and the edge convention is $\{x,-y\}$.

The traversal takes $O(N\log N+m)$ abstract operations, including the sort
used to obtain a topological order, with $O(N+m+r^2)$ storage as a loose
bound. These are integer-operation counts, not constant-time claims for
unbounded integers: multiplicities have $O(N+\log(m+1))$ bits. In
particular, a power containing $2^{100}$ copies of a subword contributes
a 101-bit weight, not $2^{100}$ edge visits. The existing exact integer
minimum-cut routine can therefore operate on the compressed graph. Its
Edmonds--Karp augmentation bound is independent of the numeric capacity
values; arithmetic still pays their bit cost.

\subsection{Unique occurrences and elimination without expansion}

Elimination needs to know which generators occur exactly once in each
individual relator. Repeating full occurrence counting independently for
every generator and every relator wastes sharing. Instead store two masks
for every reachable node: $P_X$ records presence and $R_X$ records at least
two occurrences. For a terminal $g$, set $P_X=\{ |g| \}$ and $R_X=\varnothing$.
For a concatenation,
\[
 P_{UV}=P_U\cup P_V,\qquad
 R_{UV}=R_U\cup R_V\cup(P_U\cap P_V).
\]
Thus the unique generators at a root are precisely $P_X\setminus R_X$.
The implementation uses dense generator indices and integer bit masks;
the cost of a mask operation grows with its $r$ bits. The transient mask
storage is $O(Nr)$ bits. This is polynomial in grammar size and rank, with
no dependence on expanded word length except its encoded bit length.

Combine these unique-generator sets with aggregate counts to retain the
explicit producer's elimination priority
\[
 ((|w|-2)\,\operatorname{count}(g),\ |w|,\ \text{relation index},\ g).
\]
For the chosen occurrence $w=u g^{\epsilon}v$, obtain its position using
the existing compressed occurrence query. Form $vu$ by two slices and a
concatenation. Substitute $(vu)^{-1}$ when $\epsilon=1$, or $vu$ when
$\epsilon=-1$, into all other relators; retain the eliminated relator's
empty slot. Exact compressed free and cyclic reduction finishes the move.
The usual Tietze transformation proves preservation of the group.

When no generator can be eliminated, a negative Whitehead cut supplies
the same length-reducing automorphism used by the explicit producer.
Its generator images are short words, but substitution and cancellation
remain compressed. The implementation checks that the resulting total
length change equals the cut prediction. All lengths and heuristic scores
use exact integers. A zero or positive best cut is only a stalled search,
not a nontrivial-knot certificate.

\subsection{The bounded overlap bridge and verification boundary}

The cyclic overlap matcher is still a suffix automaton on explicit letters.
The new producer retains the rank-adaptive policy from the previous section,
but enters this matcher only when the total current expanded length is at
most \code{max\_letters}. It charges expansion and matching to the same
search work allowance. If the length exceeds the cap, it skips overlap
matching and continues to the compressed Whitehead test. It does not
report that no overlap exists. Consequently the new backend is a hybrid,
and its search paths need not cover all paths available with a larger
explicit overlap allowance.

The CLI flag \code{--group-compressed-search} enables compressed discovery
and compressed replay; Python callers combine
\code{group\_compressed\_search=True} with \code{use\_group=True}.
The earlier \code{--group-compressed} flag continues to select replay only.
Standalone \code{compressed\_certificate} returns an untrusted candidate
trace. Before a recognition verdict, the checker reconstructs the full
Wirtinger presentation independently from the input PD and replays the
trace. It shares the deterministic word kernel, but does not call the new
summary, eligibility, cut-selection or producer move routines. On the
finite knot corpus, the independently implemented literal checker also
accepts all the produced traces. This corroborates these examples; it is
not a proof that two implementations sharing a kernel cannot share a bug.

\code{max\_nodes} bounds allocated grammar nodes and equality assertions;
\code{max\_work} bounds cooperative search operations. The independent
replay has its own work allowance, and both share the local wall deadline.
The explicit-letter cap bounds initial PD reconstruction and optional
overlap expansion, rather than all intermediate represented lengths.
Exhaustion remains inconclusive and caller cancellation propagates.

\subsection{What the extension does and does not bound}

For a supplied grammar, counts, unique-occurrence masks and Whitehead
graphs are polynomial-time constructions. So are the individual compressed
word operations established above. These statements do not bound the
number of Whitehead moves in terms of the original crossing count, or
the accumulated grammar size after repeated substitutions. Even a
strictly length-decreasing search may have exponentially many steps when
its initial length is exponentially compressed. Generator elimination can
also increase subsequent relator lengths. A proof of a general
quasi-polynomial recognizer must address these global quantities.

The practical policy therefore keeps explicit search as the default.
Representation adaptivity should be justified by measured progress and
resource use, with independent verification retained after any transition.
Switching implementations alone supplies no improved worst-case exponent.

\subsection{Discovery audit and measured limitations}

The 8 October 2026 discovery audit repeats the same fifteen input diagrams:
twelve original Khovanov survivors, their two previously stalled mirrors,
and Gordian. For each input it executes a randomized A/A/B ordering of
explicit search, an identical explicit control, and compressed search.
Every arm includes fresh PD validation, the complete recognition query
and independent certificate replay. One warm-up round is excluded and
five rounds are measured, giving 225 measured queries. The local group
allowance is ten seconds, the global allowance twelve seconds, and each
search/replay receives ten million abstract work units. The larger time
allowance deliberately permits comparison of completed compressed searches;
it differs from the earlier two-second replay-only experiment.

All 225 queries return UNKNOT with group evidence. The sum of median
times over the fourteen small cases is $73.52$ ms for explicit search,
$74.00$ ms for its control and $391.33$ ms for compressed search. Gordian's
respective medians are $1.247$, $1.252$ and $7.076$ seconds. Thus this
implementation increases representation capacity but substantially slows
the present corpus. A profile of Gordian identifies exact compressed
equality inside cancellation as the main cost; the shared eligibility
masks do not remove that bottleneck. Its producer allocates 11,529 grammar
nodes and uses one bounded overlap expansion. These are observed values,
not worst-case complexity bounds.

A separate synthetic audit builds $(ab)^{2^k}$, computes counts and the
cyclic graph for two copies, checks its unique-occurrence masks, then
constructs the abstract presentation $\langle a,b\mid ab^{2^k}\rangle$
and eliminates $a$ without expanding. This presentation is explicitly
known to be infinite cyclic; it is not a new family of hard knot diagrams.
