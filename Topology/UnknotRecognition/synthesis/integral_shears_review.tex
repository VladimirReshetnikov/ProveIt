\subsection{From powered Whitehead moves to integer tensions}

Fix a positive alive generator $a$. For each signed letter $x$ with
$|x|\ne a$, choose an integer $z_x$ and define
\[
 \Phi_{a,z}(a)=a,\qquad
 \Phi_{a,z}(x)=a^{-z_{-x}}x a^{z_x}.
\]
The inverse-letter formula is consistent, and substitution gives
$\Phi_{a,z}\Phi_{a,t}=\Phi_{a,z+t}$. Thus every potential vector defines
an automorphism, with inverse $\Phi_{a,-z}$. A powered Whitehead direction
is the special case $z_x=k[x\in A]$. Conversely, the integral shear factors
into at most $2(r-1)$ powered singleton directions at rank $r$. Negative
potentials use the inverse multiplier. These factors commute, although
individual factors can temporarily increase length.

For a literally cyclically reduced relator, retain the cyclic sequence of
non-$a^{\pm1}$ letters. If consecutive retained letters are $u,y$, with
intervening run $a^e$, write $v=-y$. The transformed gap exponent is
\[
 e+z_u-z_v.
\]
No retained letters cancel. Indeed, a first such cancellation would require
$y=-u$, hence $v=u$; its exponent remains $e$, which was nonzero by the
input reduction contract. This argument includes the cyclic seam. Pure
multiplier powers are fixed. Consequently the exact total cyclic length is
\begin{equation}
 L_a(z)=C_a+\sum_{(u,v,e)}w_{uve}|e+z_u-z_v|,
 \label{eq:shear-review-objective}
\end{equation}
where $C_a$ counts retained letters and pure-root lengths. Duplicate relator
slots contribute with multiplicity. Self-loop records contribute constant
costs and must be retained.

The gap histogram can be computed on a repetition grammar without expanding
its words. Each rule stores its first and last retained letters and its
signed outer multiplier runs. A concatenation creates at most one new gap;
a repetition creates a copy-seam gap with multiplicity $k-1$. Reverse
propagation of root multiplicities counts all rule occurrences, and a
separate root seam closes each non-pure relator. There are $O(s+m)$ gap
records for $s$ rules and $m$ roots. Large multiplicities and exponents are
binary integers, not iteration counts. The verifier instead builds
bottom-up histograms, providing a distinct polynomial recurrence.

\subsection{An exact certificate and a polynomial solver}

For each gap choose an integer $f_{uve}$ satisfying
\[
 |f_{uve}|\le w_{uve},\qquad \operatorname{div}f=0.
\]
For any potentials $z$, termwise comparison and cancellation of the vertex
potentials give
\[
 \sum w_{uve}|e+z_u-z_v|\ \ge\
 \sum f_{uve}(e+z_u-z_v)=\sum e f_{uve}.
\]
Equality certifies a global integer minimum. The checker needs only exact
support, integer types, capacities, conservation and equality of these two
objectives. It does not rerun the optimizer or rely on hashes for mathematical
word equality.

The delivered optimizer replaces a gap by opposite arcs of capacity $w$ and
costs $-e,e$. A circulation on these arcs yields $f=g^+-g^-$. Residual-feasible
vertex potentials provide the primal solution through $z=-\pi$. Its
capacity-scaling implementation processes bits from most to least significant:
it doubles old capacities and flows, installs the next capacity bits, saturates
new negative-reduced-cost arcs, then repairs the resulting imbalances along
shortest residual paths. Each newly opened negative arc has capacity at most
one, so the total imbalance is at most the number of arcs. Each integral
repair reduces this total. There are at most $A$ repairs per capacity bit,
not one repair per unit of the original large capacities.

Truncated shortest-path distance updates preserve nonnegative residual
reduced costs, including unreachable vertices. Bellman--Ford recalibration
after repairs bounds potential bit lengths by simple-path costs. This gives
a polynomial bit-time algorithm in graph size and the binary input lengths.
The zero-imbalance self-loop case is included. Balanced offset graphs can
solve every gap exactly by graph traversal. Forests of parallel-edge bundles
admit weighted-median differences on each bundle; acyclicity makes those
independent choices compatible. The same primal--dual checker validates the
shortcut results. These observations explain why forcing the general flow
solver on every structured input is unnecessary.

The archive's work budget charges circulation operations. It is not a global
service allowance: histogram construction, balanced/forest shortcuts,
certificate checking and grammar allocation also need host accounting before
any bounded production probe is introduced.

\subsection{Why the retained representation matters}

For each non-pure rule $U$, construct a transformed core $M_U$ between its
first and last retained letters. Concatenation joins child cores with the
one transformed multiplier run. If $U=V^k$ is non-pure and $h$ is the
transformed copy-seam exponent, use
\[
 M_U=(M_Va^h)^{k-1}M_V.
\]
Append the transformed cyclic seam at each root. Skeleton invariance proves
that the output is already freely and cyclically reduced. This construction
uses no generic free-reduction or equality queries. Each old rule introduces
a bounded number of new rules, provided repetition rules with binary
exponents remain part of the representation.

Writing $G=s+m+r+2$, the report's conservative bound is $G'\le8G$.
A different verifier recurrence stores decorated images with outer multiplier
runs and associates the repeated core in the opposite order. It has the same
constant-factor growth property. During strictly shortening phases, expanded
length is bounded by its initial value and integer bit lengths grow only
with the initial encoding size and the logarithm of the rule count.
Accordingly $q$ such phases, their local optimality certificates and their
independent replay cost
\[
 \operatorname{poly}(\sigma)\,2^{O(q)}
\]
in the initial binary encoding size $\sigma$. Polynomial factors in $q$
are absorbed into the exponential; the zero-phase case remains polynomial.
For $\sigma\le n^{O(1)}$ and $q=O(\log^2(n+2))$, this is
$2^{O(\log^2(n+2))}$, a quasi-polynomial bound. With logarithmically many
phases the same computation is polynomial.

This is a conditional class bound, not a general unknot theorem. A knot
algorithm must also provide checked full-presentation provenance, a phase
bound, a sufficient terminal predicate, and bounds for interleaved relator
operations. In particular, the maintained \code{WordArena} represents powers
by binary concatenation. Exporting every new repetition rule to that arena
adds a bit-length factor per phase and does not inherit the displayed
constant-factor growth theorem. Legacy substitution and reduction have their
own costs as well.

\subsection{A concrete limit of automorphic descent}

The pure-power terminal predicate has a simple complete algebraic meaning.
If each nonempty relator is a power of a single alive generator, take the gcd
$d_j$ of its nonzero exponents, with $d_j=0$ when that generator has no
relation. The group is the free product of the cyclic factors $C_{d_j}$,
where $C_0=\mathbb Z$ and $C_1$ is trivial. It is infinite cyclic precisely
when exactly one $d_j$ is zero and all others are one. Absent generators
must therefore remain in the alive set.

Even unlimited strictly shortening automorphisms do not always reach this
terminal class. For $N\ge2$, consider
\[
 \langle a,b\mid a^N,a^{N+1},[a,b]\rangle.
\]
The first two relators kill $a$, so this is an infinite-cyclic group, but it
has no singleton relation and the commutator is not a pure-power root.
Under any free-group automorphism the first two relators have combined
cyclic length at least $2N+1$. The commutator image is a nontrivial element
with zero abelianization, hence cyclic length at least four. The original
total $2N+5$ already attains that lower bound. This is a plateau under all
free-group automorphisms, not merely a poor choice of multiplier.

Relator combinations or other Tietze operations can escape this example;
our maintained overlap and relator-power machinery is relevant to that
separate task. The example is an abstract presentation, not a family of hard
knot diagrams. It establishes that a phase-count result by itself would
still leave a terminal-completeness obligation.

\subsection{Reproduced delivery and maintained-source experiments}

All 51 delivered unit tests passed in 3.325 seconds. The deterministic audit
also reproduced 1,200 circulation instances, 360 stages from 180 connected
braid closures, 1,012 individual relator-image comparisons and three large
serialized presentation certificates. It completed in 5.220 seconds. These
are correctness checks, not comparative performance measurements.

The delivery's source-pinned gateway passed all 160 comparisons against the
actual maintained \code{WordArena}, selected-power formulas and legacy
replay. All four pinned Git blobs match; no unpinned override was required.
The independently implemented Artin-presentation corpus in the delivery is
useful, but it does not exercise our actual PD presentation constructor or
singleton scheduler. We therefore added
\path{data/audit_integral_shears.py}.

This audit reconstructs full native Wirtinger presentations from 30 validated
one-component diagrams already recorded in the port-register audit. For
each diagram it checks the raw state and the state after the maintained
\code{\_search} performs its singleton prelude. The exact selected-power
comparator minimizes the power along the maintained cut direction, even
where the production heuristic would skip that extra query. It is a local
length comparator, not a timing of the production policy.

On all 60 states the delivered legacy macro replay agrees, up to cyclic
rotation, with both the direct constructor and explicit letter substitution.
This gives 234 relator-image checks. The joint query strictly improves the
selected-power length on 12 of the 30 raw states and on none of the 30
post-singleton states. These are paired stages of the same diagrams, not
60 independent knot samples.

We then continue the maintained search from the legacy representatives and
the corresponding recorded moves. Forty-six traces finish and pass both
explicit and compressed independent full-source certificate verification;
14 stall and none hits the continuation cap. A stall remains inconclusive.
These checks establish compatibility of successful composed traces, not a
speedup or a larger recognized class.

The experiment also finds 19 literal representative differences between
direct construction and legacy replay. Their cyclic words agree, but later
overlap records refer to positions in a particular representative. Thus
installing direct roots while recording only legacy moves can invalidate
subsequent positional replay. An atomic-shear certificate would need its
own specified representative convention and independently tested verifier.
Our audit deliberately continues from the actual legacy roots.

The delivery likewise reports 21 improved raw Artin presentations and zero
improvements after its singleton elimination. Its supplied small-stage
measurements show overhead; we have not rerun those comparative timings.
The reported gains on large synthetic presentations concern an independently
implemented selected-line baseline, not our maintained recognizer. Combined
with the native-stage experiment, this is insufficient evidence to change
runtime defaults or add an unconditional joint query.

\subsection{Integration decision and next proof obligation}

We retain the source-pinned gateway result, reproducible native adapter audit,
mathematical construction and explicit counterexample in the maintained
synthesis. The producer remains unchanged. A future runtime experiment should
first identify highly compressed post-singleton states where joint optimization
changes the search, then compare complete verified runs with the same resource
allowances. If direct construction is needed for asymptotics, that experiment
also requires a retained repetition representation and an atomic certificate
protocol. A bound on multiplier changes and on intervening relator operations
would be the next substantive step toward the quasi-polynomial target.
