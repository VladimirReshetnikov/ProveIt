\section{Native lazy lexicographic pivot selection}
\label{sec:native-lex-prefix}

The polynomial LP policy now uses Dantzig entering and full original-slack
lexicographic leaving in its capped simplex prefix.  Explicit
\path{policy='bland'} retains the preceding Bland implementation.  The
finite evidence of Section~\ref{sec:whole-call-prefix} motivated this change:
the candidate completed the W34 call within the shared pivot allowance,
while also increasing the number of LP queries and, on some inputs, search
nodes.  This section describes the maintained implementation and its fresh
validation.  It does not reinterpret the frozen pilot as a test of newer
native sources.

\subsection{An exact comparison without materialized ratio tuples}

Dantzig entering scans all reduced costs, selects the largest strictly
positive rational value and retains the least column index on equality.
It includes slack variables.  For leaving, write $m$ for the tableau row
count, $n$ for the original variable count, and $a_i=T_{ij}>0$ for the
entering coefficient of a candidate row.  The mathematical key remains
\[
 \left(d_i/a_i,T_{i,n}/a_i,\ldots,T_{i,n+m-1}/a_i\right).
\]
The implementation compares one candidate with the current best row, starting
with the right-hand side and then visiting every original slack column in
order until the first unequal component.  It constructs neither the complete
tuple nor a slice of the slack block.

For two components and their positive divisors, let
\[
 x=p/q,\quad a=u/v,\qquad y=r/s,\quad b=w/z,
 \qquad q,s,u,v,w,z>0.
\]
Then the exact comparison is
\[
            x/a<y/b \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
            pvsw<rzqu.
\]
The numerators $p,r$ may be negative.  All factors cleared from denominators
are positive, so no sign reversal occurs.  The code forms these two integer
products directly, avoiding intermediate Fraction construction, reduction
and division.  It replaces the incumbent only on strict less and stops on
strict greater.  If every component is equal it retains the earlier candidate,
matching the stable literal tuple minimum even on artificial helper inputs.
Distinct rows of a genuine invertible original slack block cannot have equal
complete keys, as proved in Proposition~\ref{prop:capped-pivot-lex}.

Skipping later components after a difference is an exact implementation of
lexicographic order.  It does not omit any original slack column from that
order: every column remains available when preceding components tie.
In particular currently basic original slacks remain essential.  Comparing
the unscaled components would be incorrect when the entering coefficients
differ.

\subsection{Call-local dispatch and preserved contracts}

The private tableau helper receives a call-local rule.  Its default remains
Bland; the common polynomial controller supplies the lexicographic rule
explicitly.  The explicit Bland branch retains its old invocation and its
literal entering/leaving expressions.  The public and prepared entry-point
signatures, strict validation, immutable prepared matrix, original-row
selection and returned dictionary schemas are unchanged.

There is no module-global selector replacement, shared mutable tableau,
warm-start basis, or selector state stored in the prepared context.
Each objective retains a fresh tableau, right-hand side and basis.  A
callback can invoke either policy through either entry point without changing
the suspended call's rule.  The candidate and each actually compared
component provide cancellation polls, and callback exceptions propagate
without interception or substitution.  Poll counts intentionally differ
from both the old polynomial prefix and the tuple-based experiment; the
equivalence concerns the exact pivot choices and results when callbacks do
not interrupt or mutate external inputs.

Positive and nonpositive terminal checks still precede the hard pivot-cap
check.  A conclusion reached on the last allowed pivot is accepted.  An
exhausted user allowance prevents fallback; reaching only the internal
prefix limit permits the unchanged exact fallback if the user allowance
remains.  Zero internal prefix and zero user allowance therefore retain
different meanings.  Ellipsoid updates and feasibility calls keep their
separate counters and caps.  Selector comparisons and callback polls are
not relabelled as pivots or tableau updates.

\subsection{Correctness and bit cost}

The complete original slack block is $B^{-1}$.  The leaving comparison is
therefore exactly the fixed symbolic right-hand-side perturbation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:capped-pivot-lex}.  Dantzig's chosen real reduced cost
is strictly positive.  The proof of formal feasibility and no repeated
basis consequently applies unchanged.  Only the unperturbed positive
objective causes a positive return; the original-row primal, primitive-ray
and homogeneous-dual meanings remain unchanged.  The exact fallback and
its support and basis recovery are not modified.

Suppose the numerators and denominators of tableau entries have at most
$B$ bits.  Each four-factor comparison product has at most $4B+O(1)$ bits.
There are at most $O(m^2)$ component comparisons per pivot, and the entering
scan takes $O(n+m)$ comparisons.  Beyond the pre-existing candidate list,
the selector retains only a constant number of row indices and $O(B)$-bit
integers.  Early differences can reduce work, but do not improve the
worst-case comparison bound.  This cost fits the existing dense tableau
scale $O(m(n+m))$ with polynomial-size exact arithmetic.

All feasible bases belong to the same fixed augmented input matrix, so the
determinant bounds on tableau bit size do not depend on the selector.
The default $(n+r+1)^2$-pivot limit, with $r$ retained independent rows,
therefore preserves the polynomial prefix and the conservative complete
$O(U^{19})$ kernel bound of Section~\ref{sec:exact-polynomial-lp}.
As there, arbitrary work performed inside caller callbacks is not part of
the solver's own operation bound.  Anti-cycling alone would not give a
polynomial pivot theorem; the prefix cap and polynomial fallback remain
necessary to the stated bound.

The source-search bound involving the resolving parameter remains
$O(A(3\delta)^h\operatorname{poly}(t,L))$ under its existing hypotheses.
Changing a pivot rule supplies no uniform bound on $h$, no balanced-separator
theorem and no general quasi-polynomial recognition result.

\subsection{Fresh regression and adoption evidence}

The focused run passes $32$ tests in the new selector, prepared-matrix and
polynomial-LP modules.  The complete regression then passes $408$ tests
across $45$ modules with Regina and no skips.  All $363$ files in its exact
pre-run native, test, fixture and command closure remain unchanged afterward.
The old $394$-test snapshot remains a record of the preceding implementation.

The $14$ new methods cover largest-cost entering and stable ties, every
potentially decisive slack component, signed rational and large-bit entries,
genuine dependent zero-right-hand-side degeneracy, and complete result
agreement with the literal divided-Fraction tuple rule.  Five retained
pre-change Bland controls check complete public and prepared results.
Independent small-cone vertex enumeration and original slack-basis solves
check positive outputs; every original-row dual inequality is checked for
negative outputs.  Real two-tetrahedron tree/transversal cases have independent
negative-tree or normal-crushing replay.

Cap and fallback controls include the last allowed pivot and zero internal
versus user limits.  Forty-eight nested policy, entry-point and exit-type
combinations exercise call-local state and exception identity.  Cancellation
is checked at every visited poll of small positive and negative prefixes,
at distinct fallback sites, and through a final-slack selector comparison.
No existing test expectation was weakened to accommodate a changed ray.

A separate fresh call starts from the original W34 planar diagram using the
maintained native implementation and the archived candidate's exact options.
It makes one recognition call, with no injected source, selector replacement,
archived native import or wall-clock cutoff.  It returns \texttt{KNOTTED}
after $62$ LP calls, $6{,}606$ pivots and $33{,}094{,}980$ tableau updates,
with no fallback.  The terminal search visits one node and tests $124$
anchors, reusing $63$.  The full typed result, statistics and labelled source
ledger equal the archived candidate's after both proofs have been checked.
This is one new whole-call observation; the earlier fourteen-call comparison
and its $449$ retained queries remain historical evidence.

The reconstructed chain starts with $160$ tetrahedra, shells to $148$, and
accepts $32$ vertex-reduction crushes, three strict $3$--$2$ moves and three
up--down--down macros.  Its endpoint has $31$ tetrahedra, one vertex and two
boundary faces.  The saved negative certificate and every original-diagram
preprocessing step pass independent verification.  A second, separate process
replays the saved result and the one archived comparison proof with discovery
disabled, starting no recognition calls.  All $302$ current native, runner,
accessor and archive pins remain unchanged through both executions.
The proof checks validate the returned certificate and work counters; they
do not reconstruct a fresh per-query LP operation trace or measure runtime.

Commands, pre-run source pins, complete logs and the publication binding are
retained under \path{data/native-lex-prefix-*}.  The publication helper runs
no tests, discovery, timing experiment or PDF build.  The earlier fixed-query
and whole-call archives retain their original sources and results.  No
elapsed-time or universal speedup is claimed for this native change.
