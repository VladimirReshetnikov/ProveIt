\section{Carry-free encoding of source port ownership}
\label{sec:prism-port-encoding}

Section~\ref{sec:prism-interfaces} assigned each core-facing port its own
coordinate in every weight vector. With $P\le24T$ ports, each vector had
dimension $P+2$, even when it carried only one port indicator. The new
encoding keeps the cell and band counts and replaces all port coordinates
by one nonnegative binary integer. Port $i$ contributes $2^i$ at its
unique prism endpoint. The weighted operation still uses ordinary exact
integer addition, including when several ports meet the same point.

\begin{proposition}[Exact additive ownership bitsets]
Suppose each of $P$ labelled ports supplies exactly one singleton unit
indicator in the source universe. For every orbit, the sum of their
$2^i$ contributions is the bitset of precisely its port indices.
Three-coordinate weighted discovery and independent replay therefore
recover exactly the original attached-region partition and all cell/band
profiles. Every port-bearing histogram row has multiplicity one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each port belongs to exactly one source point and thus exactly one orbit.
For a given orbit let $I$ be the set of its port indices. Its last weight
is $\sum_{i\in I}2^i$. No exponent occurs twice in this sum, so its binary
digits are exactly the indicators of $I$, with no carries. Uniqueness of
binary expansion makes this representation injective on port subsets.
Distinct nonempty subsets are disjoint across orbits and cannot be equal,
so a port-bearing row cannot describe several orbits. The first two weight
coordinates retain exactly their former definitions. Decoding the subsets
and sorting the resulting profiles yields the same geometric structure.
\end{proof}

The restriction to unique unit source indicators is essential. This is
not an injective encoding of arbitrary integer vectors: two copies of
$2^i$ would be indistinguishable from one copy of $2^{i+1}$. Here source
clipping independently reconstructs each labelled port once; weighted
replay binds the proof to those exact singleton contributions. Decoding
alone is never used to authenticate ownership. The final summary also
checks the complete port partition, rejects out-of-range bits and requires
multiplicity one on port-bearing rows.

All weights are nonnegative. Exact transport conserves their total mass,
whose port coordinate is $2^P-1$. Therefore every transported port weight
has at most $P$ bits. The reduction from $P+2$ Python integer coordinates
to three does not mean constant bit cost: shifts, additions, multiplication,
hashing and serialization charge these $O(P)$-bit integers. For $O(T)$
indicator intervals their worst-case total bit footprint remains
$O(TP)=O(T^2)$, while the number of coefficient objects and coordinate-loop
iterations is reduced. This improves representation and practical costs
within the existing polynomial encoded-input bound; it does not alter
the global recognition exponent or prove a hierarchy depth bound.

The API's \code{port\_encoding} accepts \code{vector}, \code{bitset} and
\code{auto}. The default chooses a bitset when $P>1$, and otherwise retains
the vector whose dimension is no larger. Vector proofs retain
\code{normal-prism-interfaces-v1}; bitset proofs use
\code{normal-prism-interfaces-v2}. Both versions independently reconstruct
source-face events and weights before weighted replay. The checker's
binary-digit decoder is separate from the producer's least-set-bit decoder.
It first bounds the mask length by $P$, then converts it once and visits
at most $P$ binary digits per profile. This avoids repeatedly right-shifting
a large integer through its absent low-order ports.
The geometric structure and ribbon-only interpretation are unchanged.

Tests compare both encodings with pointwise source-face geometry, exercise
more than one hundred ports with a 501-bit multiplicity, reject altered
source masks and ownership summaries, and replay all 58 saved version-one
proofs with producers disabled. General core topology, edge/vertex handles,
ordered parallelity boundaries and later hierarchy stages remain unresolved.

\subsection{Exact source structures and complete-query timing}

All 5,289 supplied source structures agree with explicit face recovery,
including 48 mixed cases and seven large binary cases. Previous whole-piece
counts are preserved. The 58 new proofs and all 58 legacy proofs independently
replay with producers disabled. The retained audit authenticates 672 source
and evidence hashes. The final full suite passes 1,579 tests with eight skips;
all 21 focused tests also pass after the independent decoder improvement.
The test and audit logs remain under \code{data/prism-port-encoding-*}.

Both measured encodings use the same linear contraction kernel. Each timed
call includes fresh source validation, clipping, discovery, weighted
transport, independent replay and complete result serialization. Input
preparation and structure-digest comparison are outside timing. The output
structures agree in all arms, while their certificate encodings and sizes
can differ. Five shuffled paired rounds follow one warmup round; an
identical vector arm supplies the A/A control. The ratio is the median
paired vector/auto ratio, with values above one favouring auto.
Prior results are released and garbage is collected before each arm's
timer. This prevents small cases from inheriting cleanup of a preceding
large workload; those cleanup costs are excluded equally in all arms.

The primitive $T=16,32,64$ cases have paired vector/auto ratios
1.654, 2.791 and 5.034, with corresponding identical-vector controls
1.010, 1.003 and 0.985. At $T=64$ the weight dimension drops from 128 to
three and the complete serialized result shrinks from 417,245 to 325,587
bytes. The 501-bit multiplicity case has ratio 1.527 with control 0.981.
The $T=4$ case stays near parity, at 1.008 with control 0.992. With no
ports, the $T=1$ auto and vector paths are identical; its observed ratio
1.041 and control 1.083 illustrate small-call timing variability.

These are standalone source-geometry queries. Their gains do not establish
a full-recognizer improvement or a quasi-polynomial recognition theorem.
