\section{Escaping a coherent family and eliminating vacuous optimization}
\label{sec:coherent-escape}

Section~\ref{sec:coherent-obstruction} rules out completeness of the integral
coherent-height family on arbitrary solid-torus triangulations. It does not
make this obstruction invariant under retriangulation. In fact, a single
checked $3$--$2$ move on that example changes the primitive coherent surface
from genus two to a disc. This motivates testing changes of triangulation,
while also identifying an avoidable cost: on a one-vertex triangulation,
optimizing vertex potentials cannot change any height span. The maintained
span solver now detects this case and constructs a linear arithmetic
optimality certificate without a flow network.

These are two separate improvements with different roles. The $3$--$2$
operation extends the explicit, independently checked geometry API. Its
greedy search remains an experiment. The one-vertex branch is an automatic
optimization of an existing arithmetic API and preserves its proof schema.
Neither result supplies a general discovery bound.

\subsection{An independently verified 3--2 replacement}

The input site is a tetrahedron and two distinct local vertices specifying
an edge. The producer reconstructs its signed global edge class and requires
exactly three local occurrences in three different tetrahedra. All faces
containing this edge must be paired within the selected region. Give the
edge endpoints formal labels $3,4$ and the other two corners of the selected
tetrahedron labels $0,1$. Propagating the labels through the interior faces
assigns the third belt label $2$ and must give exactly the three formal
tetrahedral vertex sets
\[
 \{3,4,0,1\},\quad \{3,4,1,2\},\quad \{3,4,2,0\}.
\]
The consistency test includes all interior face maps, not merely edge degree.
Replace this filling by tetrahedra $(0,1,2,3)$ and $(0,1,2,4)$ joined across
their common $(0,1,2)$ face, and transfer the six formal boundary gluings.
As in the $2$--$3$ direction, globally identified vertices and pairings among
formal boundary facets are permitted. An inverse relative-boundary PL
homeomorphism gives the topology proof.

The certificate lists the three original tetrahedra and their four formal
labels. The independent checker reconstructs both finite manifolds, validates
the three distinct indices and formal sets, checks the unchanged exterior,
and compares the six boundary signatures. Interior faces are checked
separately. It reuses only the independent boundary-signature routine from
the $2$--$3$ checker; no move producer is imported. A reordered valid output
that does not follow the certificate's prescribed labels is rejected.

After manifold preparation, site discovery, face rewriting and replay take
$O(N)$ indexed operations for $N$ tetrahedra. The explicit API performs only
one supplied move. A simple repeated scan for an eligible $3$--$2$ edge
terminates after fewer than $N$ successful moves because the tetrahedron
count strictly decreases. Rebuilding native geometry on every pass gives
$O(N^2\alpha(N))$ union--find and indexed work under the existing word-cost
model, excluding the research driver's additional Regina isomorphism audits.
This is a bound for finding
a local minimum in tetrahedron count, not for finding a disc or for reaching
a globally smallest triangulation. A plateau may require temporary increases.

\subsection{The explicit escape and the coverage experiment}

On the eight-tetrahedron obstruction, select tetrahedron $5$ and its local
edge $\{0,1\}$. The new triangulation has seven tetrahedra and one global
vertex. Its primitive coherent surface has $36$ normal pieces and is a
compressing disc. The retained record includes the entire before/after
triangulations, the independently checked replacement and the independent
normal-disc-count certificate. Together with the previous six-move record
it has a fixed solid-torus source. This does not claim correspondence with
a supplied planar diagram.

For broader move validation, four length-100 paths start from layered solid
tori with two and eight tetrahedra and canonical one-crossing and trefoil
exteriors. At each step, Regina selects an eligible upward or downward
site; the native producer performs the supplied move, the independent native
checker replays it, and the result is compared with Regina's separate
replacement up to triangulation isomorphism. All $400$ comparisons pass:
$210$ are $2$--$3$ moves and $190$ are $3$--$2$ moves. Additional tests cover
reversed local edge order, relabelled one-vertex inputs, malformed certificates,
forged boundary maps, resource limits, cancellation and immutable inputs.

The discovery experiment uses the $82$ maintained source diagrams with at
most sixteen crossings. It repeatedly chooses the first eligible edge in
local incidence order, and then evaluates the raw cocycle and up to four
alternative tree gauges. One arm begins at the canonical pulling exterior;
the other first performs the existing boundary shellings. Every successful
$3$--$2$ replacement is independently replayed and compared with Regina.
The arms perform $144$ and $73$ moves respectively, and yield nonnegative-Euler
coherent candidates on $20$ and $26$ source labels. Regina checks connectivity,
orientability and Euler characteristic of these supplied candidates, and
checks compressibility when the candidate is a disc.

Neither arm adds a source label beyond the earlier extended-tree and planar
capping search. This experiment does not run the minimum-span stage and is
not a complete-recognition timing comparison. In particular, its candidate
counts must not be compared as though they were full default-pipeline success
counts. The negative coverage result does not justify adding this greedy
simplifier to the recognition schedule; the explicit move API remains useful
for testing more selective geometric strategies.

\subsection{A direct dual certificate when there is one vertex}

The arithmetic objective is
\[
 D(p)=\sum_{t=1}^N\left(\max_i(h_{t,i}+p_{v_{t,i}})
                         -\min_i(h_{t,i}+p_{v_{t,i}})\right).
\]
If all global vertex labels agree, every local corner receives the same
potential $p$. Therefore
\[
 D(p)=D(0)=\sum_t\bigl(\max_i h_{t,i}-\min_i h_{t,i}\bigr)
 \quad\text{for every real or integral }p.
\]
Choose a minimum corner $i_t$ and maximum corner $j_t$ in each tetrahedron.
The existing certificate format permits the matching entries
$(t,i_t,t,j_t)$. Each tetrahedron is used exactly once on each side and the
matched corners share their global vertex, so all potential terms cancel.
The matching's dual value is exactly $D(0)$. The unchanged weak-duality
checker proves optimality, independently reconstructing the normal vector
from its six local edge weights. This includes tied extrema and constant
height rows, where the two corners may coincide.

The new branch returns potential zero, these diagonal matching entries and
the unchanged local normal coordinates. It still invokes the independent
checker before returning. It allocates no flow network, performs no
augmentations or shortest-path searches, and keeps the previous schema and
statistics fields. Matching certificates need not be identical to those
chosen by the generic solver. The coordinate vector and optimal objective
are identical. Multiple-vertex inputs continue through the previous solver.
The branch establishes no primitivity, connectedness, genus or knot verdict;
those require their existing geometric and source checks.

There are $O(N)$ integer arithmetic operations and $O(N)$ stored integers,
including the internal proof replay. If input integers have at most $B$ bits,
the work is $O(N(B+\log N))$ in the elementary bit-cost model. No integer
height or normal-sheet multiplicity is expanded. The implemented deterministic
guard count is exactly $5N+3$.

This improves an actual quadratic case of the old implementation. Take one
global vertex and rows $(0,0,0,t)$ for $t=0,\ldots,N-1$. The previous flow
solver performs $N-1$ shortest-path searches after its initial unit, with a
full $2N+3$-node potential update on each search. These updates alone take
$\Omega(N^2)$ indexed operations, even though the answer is independent of
the potential. The direct primal/dual construction removes that cost rather
than merely changing the blocking-flow schedule.

\subsection{Compatibility, measurements and remaining scope}

The arithmetic audit compares the old solver at revision \code{a6cadc5d6}
with the new one on $300$ random one-vertex instances and $300$ random
multiple-vertex instances. In the former, coordinates agree and both
certificates pass the unchanged checker; in the latter, the entire result,
including all work statistics and certificates, agrees exactly. Binary
tests use scaled heights and independent tetrahedron offsets exceeding
$20{,}000$ bits without increasing the guard count.

The current native source pipeline also reproduces all $252$ saved records
on the $84$-diagram corpus: shellings off, on, and on with planar capping.
All $47$ distinct positive proofs are identical and replay successfully.
Thus the new arithmetic branch changes no candidate, verdict, deterministic
work cap or proof in this maintained diagram workload. That compatibility
is not evidence of a recognition speedup on these diagrams.
