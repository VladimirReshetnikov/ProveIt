\section{Exact long-run recurrences and streamed macro homology}
\label{sec:twist-continuation}

Report 18 extends the existing twist macro complex in two directions:
an exact compressed homological profile for one sufficiently long run, and
a separate backend that streams degree layers to reduce retained storage.
The module \path{fastunknot.twist.continuation} exposes both on signed
run-encoded braid input, with structural recognition before homology in its
recognition mode. Its homology mode always computes the requested invariant.
The existing production defaults and \code{backend="twist"} adapter retain
their original macro implementation. Ordinary recognition additionally offers
the opt-in \code{--braid-profile} structural evaluation on checked source words.

\subsection{The repeated differential, not just repeated dimensions}
Select a run $\sigma_i^{\epsilon m}$ and freeze the remaining context.
Let its macro homological support be $[a,b]$, where
$a$ is the sum of negative context exponents and $b$ the sum of positive
ones. Write $W=b-a$ and $m_0=W+2$.
When the selected run is in its turnback sector $E$, the direct sum of all
context groups is a fixed vector space $C$ of dimension $M$. Let $D$ be
the context differential and $L$ the sum of the dots on the cap and cup.
The repeated interior differential is the \emph{same} square-zero operator
\[
 F=D+L,\qquad \eta=M-2\operatorname{rank}F.
\]
Indeed $D^2=0$, each dot squares to zero, and distinct dot actions commute.
The Frobenius identities make exterior dots commute with every context
saddle, giving $DL=LD$ and $F^2=0$ over $\F$. The argument restricts to
the reduced marked-circle subcomplex. This is an ungraded endomorphism
statement about $C$; $D$ and $L$ need not have the same original bidegree.

For a positive run, complete copies of $C$ occur in total macro degrees
$b+1\le h\le a+m$. At $m=m_0$ there are exactly two adjacent complete
copies, in degrees $b+1,b+2$, with boundary map $F$ between them.
For a negative run the complete copies lie in $b-m\le h\le a-1$,
and the reference map is the boundary in degree $a-2$.
Geometry depends on whether the selected sector is $I$ or $E$, not on its
positive internal index, which identifies these interior maps exactly.

Let $P_{m_0}(z)$ be the reference reduced homological polynomial and
$\Delta=m-m_0\ge0$. Split its positive-run profile into
$A_+$ in degrees at most $b+1$ and $B_+$ in degrees at least $b+2$.
For the negative run split into $A_-$ at most $a-2$ and $B_-$ at least
$a-1$. Then
\begin{align*}
 P_m(z)&=A_+(z)+z^\Delta B_+(z)
             +\eta\sum_{h=b+2}^{a+m-1}z^h,\\
 P_{-m}(z)&=z^{-\Delta}A_-(z)+B_-(z)
             +\eta\sum_{h=b-m+1}^{a-2}z^h.
\end{align*}
The lower and upper end pieces retain both incident boundary maps, with
the displayed shift where needed. Every inserted degree has $F$ as both
incoming and outgoing differential and therefore has dimension $\eta$.
There are exactly $\Delta$ inserted degrees, so
\[
 R(m)=R(m_0)+\Delta\eta.
\]
The implementation extracts $\eta$ from the reference \emph{boundary rank},
not from either end-piece Betti number. Those Betti numbers need not equal
the interior dimension. These formulas are proved identities of the macro
complex; they are not extrapolations fitted to sampled ranks.

\subsection{Why the slope is positive}
Let $J_E$ be the link obtained by replacing the selected run with its cap
and cup. Coherent braid orientation forces the two outside paths at that
box to join top ends to bottom ends. Adding the cap and cup therefore puts
both marked arcs on the same actual component, regardless of the other
components of the closure.

Jaeger's weighted-dot sliding theorem states that the characteristic-two
twisted complex depends on the sum of weights on each actual component
\cite{jaeger-twisted}. Two unit weights on that component cancel. In the
report's explicit specialization, sliding across one crossing uses a reverse
saddle $H$ with $H^2=0$ and $DH+HD=X_p+X_{p'}$; the change of basis
$1+H$ conjugates the corresponding dot-deformed differentials. Thus the
ordinary reduced cube with the two-dot deformation has the homology of
$J_E$. Relative local reductions in the other run boxes commute with the
exterior dots, so this identification passes to the macro context as well.
Consequently
\[
 \eta=\dim_{\F}\widetilde{Kh}(J_E;\F)>0.
\]
Positivity follows from the nonzero reduced Jones Euler polynomial of a
nonempty link: its value at one has absolute value $2^{\ell-1}$.
A free integral homology summand therefore survives over $\F$.
This argument does not use the generally false assertion that adding an
arbitrary null-homotopic operator preserves homology.

The dot argument identifies a total dimension after collapsing grading.
The original run's homological profile follows instead from the separate
identification of the complete groups and both incident maps above.
Quantum grading is not returned. The raw continuation API retains macro
degrees. Converting to the original production cube convention requires
$h_{\rm cube}=n_+^{\rm original}-h_{\rm macro}$ and, for a knot, doubling
reduced dimensions. The shortened reference's positive crossing count is
not the original shift. Also, shortening can change component parity:
recognition checks the original run list, and the reference may be a link.

\subsection{Compressed output and a fixed-context bound}
The tail algorithm keeps two finite sets of exceptional degree--dimension
pairs and one constant interval, with inclusive endpoints. Exceptions and
the interval are disjoint. Queries and total rank use integer arithmetic;
explicit expansion is optional and capped. The tests include exponents with
100,001 bits while the two-strand reference still has three macro states
and four reduced basis elements. This is an in-memory API test; the JSON
frontend retains Python's decimal-integer conversion limit.

The reference has $2W+2$ expanded crossings. At $s$ strands a conservative
basis bound is $2^{s-1}3^{2W+2}$, so even elementary assembly and elimination
give $2^{O(s+W)}$ reference work, with polynomial dependence on the binary
length of $m$ for reconstruction. For fixed context and strand count this
removes enumeration of the long exponent. For growing context the reference
can still be exponential. On a knot closure the context must touch all
other strand separators, so $s\le W+2$; a context of
$W=O(\log^2 n)$ yields a quasi-polynomial bound in the expanded crossing
count $n$. This remains a restricted homology statement.

Since $\eta>0$, an explicit list of the inserted nonzero degrees has at
least $\Delta$ entries. No algorithm returning that expanded list can be
polynomial in $\log m$ for fixed context. The compact output contract is
essential, rather than an optional presentation improvement. The largest
run is the default candidate. Two runs cannot both strictly dominate their
complement, so this one-run recurrence cannot simply be iterated to compress
every large exponent independently.

\subsection{Direct structural evaluation on run-encoded braids}
For each generator index let $p_i,n_i$ count its positive and negative
occurrences, and set $P=\sum p_i$, $N=\sum n_i$, $e=P-N$,
$u_+=|\{i:p_i>0\}|$, $u_-=|\{i:n_i>0\}|$. The braid's signed Seifert
graph is a path multigraph on $s$ vertices. On a one-component closure,
the existing structural formulas become
\[
 e+s-2u_+-1\le s(K)\le e-s+2u_-+1,
 \qquad 2g=P+N-s+1.
\]
Its homogeneity defect is the number of indices occurring in both signs.
Genus zero certifies the unknot; positive homogeneous genus or an interval
excluding zero certifies knottedness. A zero-containing interval alone is
inconclusive. Sparse permutation tracking uses each exponent's parity to
check the original component count without expanding the word or allocating
an array for untouched strands. Missing a strand separator rejects a knot
candidate immediately; otherwise $s-1$ is bounded by the number of runs.
These checks are polynomial in the encoded input length.

The production PD convention reverses the Artin crossing sign. Public
Seifert fields therefore use writhe $-e$ and interval $[-U,-L]$ when the
Artin interval is $[L,U]$. Separate Artin fields make that conversion
explicit. Tests compare the entire dictionary with the independent PD
Seifert graph, rather than comparing only verdicts.

Every one-component dominant-run input is already structurally knotted.
For a positive run of magnitude $m\ge W+2$, the lower bound equals
$2(P-u_+)-d$, with $d=m+W-s+1$, and
\[
 2(P-u_+)-d\ge 2(m-1)-(m+W-s+1)
             =m-W+s-3\ge s-1>0.
\]
The negative case is symmetric. Thus tail timings do not represent a newly
solved recognition family. The report's signature-packing diagnostic also
adds no cases: its selected definite rank is at most $P-u_+$ or $N-u_-$,
so its successful inertia bound is dominated by the same interval. Its
diagnostic and verifier are retained for reproducibility, but recognition
uses the cheaper dominating calculation.

\subsection{Streaming changes storage, with an explicit time tradeoff}
The separate streamed backend enumerates bounded compositions of degree
totals. Each macro state is visited once, without repeatedly scanning the
whole Cartesian product for each degree. A depth-first enumerator prunes
prefixes using suffix capacities and avoids recursion limits. Its total
enumeration work is $O(t\prod_j(|e_j|+1))$ for $t$ runs.

Only two adjacent state indexes are live. Local Frobenius maps retain circle
indexes instead of exponentially large label tables; generated columns feed
directly into binary elimination. Geometry and map caches have bounded entry
and weighted-slot capacities. If square-zero checking is requested, two
adjacent matrices are additionally retained. The limits distinguish total
enumerated states and basis from live states, layer basis, matrix bit payload,
column references, geometry vertices, and map slots. They are mathematical
storage envelopes, not allocator or RSS reservations. The returned degree,
chain-dimension, and boundary-rank dictionaries still grow with the number
of degrees; two live state layers do not imply constant total memory.

After computing ranks of $d_{h-1}$ and $d_h$, the value
$\beta_h=\dim C^h-\operatorname{rank}d_{h-1}-\operatorname{rank}d_h$
is final. On a knot, a cumulative finalized reduced rank greater than one
certifies \code{KNOTTED} and permits early exit. Such a result explicitly
has \code{reduced\_rank=null}, a lower bound, and
\code{homology\_complete=false}. Agreement with rank one before completion
cannot certify the unknot. Full homology mode never uses this early exit.

The combined RLE frontend checks structural certificates first, then uses
the selected \code{tail}, \code{streaming}, or \code{macro} backend. It is
an explicit interface, not an unmeasured automatic storage switch. Resource
exhaustion returns \code{UNKNOWN}; CLI exit codes are 0 for completion, 2
for invalid input or configuration, and 3 for exhaustion. Tail's finite
reference and reconstruction share one cooperative deadline in the production
integration, and mutable streaming budgets are copied and revalidated.
Tail's square-zero flag refers to the finite reference, not to an unbuilt
enormous matrix. The ordinary source-word profile stage is opt-in and never
assigns the whole source braid to an individual connected-sum factor.

\subsection{Validation and measurements}
The archive passed 254 tests unchanged. The production suite now passes 391,
including 400 tail/macro profile comparisons, 80 independent cube comparisons,
both signs and boundary thresholds, original component parity, huge binary
exponents, streamed enumeration/maps/storage limits, partial-rank semantics,
and deadline/budget regressions. Separate reruns produced 832 independently
formulated recurrence comparisons, 220 cube comparisons, and 500 checks
identifying the slope with a separately extracted ordinary turnback
subcomplex. Their records are in \path{synthesis/data/twist-*-audit.json}.

Seven paired A/B/A rounds compare complete homology contracts, with tail's
compact profile expanded only for validation outside timing. At exponent
1025, the two-strand, figure-eight-context, and four-strand-context cases
gained 38.70, 58.59, and 46.84 times respectively. Their reduced reference
bases were 4, 93, and 126, versus 1027, 15393, and 18486 for full macro
assembly. The no-compression control ratio was 0.974. Raw samples and the
separate A/A control are retained in
\path{fast/results/twist_tail_20261008.json}.

The separate eight-case streaming benchmark includes fresh A/A controls,
seven shuffled rounds, and independent traced-allocation runs. Streaming
was slower in every full-homology case: macro/stream ratios ranged from
0.229 to 0.694. Peak traced allocation ratios ranged from 1.020 to 3.064
in streaming's favor. These are Python allocation peaks, not process RSS;
early-decision arms have a different output contract and are recorded
separately. Data are in
\path{fast/results/twist_streaming_20261008.json}.
This supports streaming as a storage option, with no default change.
The structural benchmark separately measures encoded input versus expansion
and recognition on fresh prebuilt diagrams; these scopes must not be
combined into a general recognition-speedup claim.
For the four-strand family with runs $(1,m),(2,-(2m-1)),(3,m)$ and odd
$m=17,129,1025,4097$, the source-profile option gained 2.296, 2.824,
2.905, and 3.334 times on fresh prebuilt diagrams. Its A/A ratios ranged
from 0.997 to 1.022. These inputs have homogeneous positive genus despite
an Artin Rasmussen interval $[0,0]$, so the homogeneous criterion, not
interval equality, supplies the verdict. The separate encoded-input
comparison includes expansion, PD construction, and Seifert evaluation
in its baseline; its ratios range from 94.4 to 29687.0. These large numbers
primarily measure the benefit of preserving compressed input. Morton's
11-crossing case remains a structural-only inconclusive control, with no
full-pipeline timing in this experiment. All raw data are in
\path{fast/results/braid_profile_20261008.json}.
