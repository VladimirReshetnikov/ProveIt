\section{Exact pivot selection within a capped polynomial prefix}
\label{sec:capped-pivot-pilot}

The fixed-source LP can spend many pivots at degenerate feasible bases.
The polynomial controller of Section~\ref{sec:exact-polynomial-lp} already
bounds this work by capping its simplex prefix and retaining exact fallback.
This permits a separate question: can another exact pivot rule finish
useful queries earlier within the same cap?  The present section proves
the relevant invariants and records a complete fixed-query pilot.  Its
alternate selectors were research code; at that checkpoint the polynomial
prefix and explicit \path{policy='bland'} both used their existing Bland rule.
Section~\ref{sec:native-lex-prefix} records the subsequent native adoption.

\subsection{A fixed symbolic perturbation of the right-hand side}

For $n$ Q variables and $r$ retained original equality rows, write
\[
 G=\begin{bmatrix}R\\-R\\\mathbf1^T\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 b=(0,\ldots,0,1)^T,\qquad m=2r+1.
\]
The initial system is $Gx+s=b$ with $x,s\ge0$, augmented matrix
$H=[G\mid I_m]$ and the slack basis.  At a later feasible basis $B$,
the tableau is $T=B^{-1}H$ and its right-hand side is $d=B^{-1}b$.
The block of \emph{all original slack columns} is exactly $B^{-1}$,
even when some original slack variables have left the basis.

Dantzig entering chooses a column of largest strictly positive reduced
cost, with least column index on an exact tie.  Ordinary minimum-ratio
leaving, with least basic-variable index on a tie, need not prevent
cycling under this entering rule.  The pilot therefore also tests an
exact lexicographic leaving rule.  If column $j$ enters, compare
\[
 \left(\frac{d_i}{T_{ij}},
       \frac{T_{i,n}}{T_{ij}},\ldots,
       \frac{T_{i,n+m-1}}{T_{ij}}\right)
       \quad\text{over }i\text{ with }T_{ij}>0
\]
in lexicographic order.  All divisions and comparisons are rational and
exact.  The full original slack block is required; selecting only currently
nonbasic slacks would change the argument.

\begin{proposition}[Lexicographic leaving for the degenerate kernel LP]
\label{prop:capped-pivot-lex}
Starting at the slack basis, the displayed leaving rule maintains
lexicographic feasibility for the fixed formal right-hand side
\[
         b(\varepsilon)=b+\varepsilon e_0+
             \varepsilon^2e_1+\cdots+\varepsilon^m e_{m-1}.
\]
With any entering column of strictly positive real reduced cost, it never
repeats a basis.  Projecting each tableau to its constant term retains a
feasible basis of the original, unperturbed LP.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Order polynomials in the positive infinitesimal $\varepsilon$ by the sign
of their first nonzero coefficient.  Initially every component of
$b(\varepsilon)$ is strictly positive: matching rows have their assigned
positive infinitesimal and the normalization row has constant term one.
At basis $B$, the ordered coefficient vector of its $i$th basic value is
\[
                    (d_i,(B^{-1})_{i0},\ldots,(B^{-1})_{i,m-1}).
\]
Thus the displayed tuple is exactly its perturbed ratio for column $j$.
Choosing the least ratio keeps every updated basic value nonnegative in
this order.  Distinct candidate tuples cannot be equal, since equality
would make two rows of the invertible matrix $B^{-1}$ proportional.
The entering variable obtains a strictly positive perturbed value; all
remaining basic values are strictly positive as well.  Inductively the
perturbed basis remains feasible and nondegenerate.

Reduced costs depend on $B,H,c$, not on the right-hand side.  A strictly
positive reduced cost therefore gives a strict increase in the formal
objective at every pivot.  Returning to an earlier basis would recover
the same formal objective, a contradiction.  This also applies when a
slack variable enters.  Finally, a lexicographically nonnegative basic
value has a nonnegative constant term, so every original basic solution
remains feasible, although it may be degenerate.
\end{proof}

This is a finiteness argument, not a polynomial bound on the number of
bases.  The implementation need not store infinitesimals: the existing
slack block and rational comparison implement their ordered coefficients.
Early positive termination uses only the \emph{unperturbed} objective and
point.  It still returns a feasible nonzero basic point of the normalized
kernel cone and therefore an extreme ray.  If no reduced cost is positive,
the existing original-row dual check remains valid.  A formal improvement
with zero real objective is not a positive Euler witness.

\subsection{The capped bit bound and unchanged fallback contract}

Every tableau entry and right-hand-side entry is a ratio of minors of
the same input augmented matrix.  Clearing rational input denominators
and applying determinant bounds gives the polynomial bit bounds used in
Section~\ref{sec:exact-polynomial-lp}, regardless of the entering rule.
Dividing two such entries to form a ratio preserves polynomial bit size;
exact comparison uses cross-products of polynomial-size integers.
Dantzig entering scans $O(n+m)$ coefficients, and forming all complete
lexicographic ratio tuples takes $O(m^2)$ rational operations per pivot.
Both remain polynomial within the dense tableau cost model.

Consequently any of these exact selectors can be used inside the existing
$(n+r+1)^2$-pivot prefix without losing its polynomial bit-time guarantee.
The hard user allowance can shorten that prefix.  Terminal checks still
precede cap checks, so a conclusion obtained on the last allowed pivot is
accepted.  Exhausting a user allowance returns \path{INCONCLUSIVE} without
fallback; reaching only the internal prefix limit retains the ordinary
exact fallback if the user has remaining allowance.  Ellipsoid iterations
and feasibility calls keep their separate units and caps.

Changing a selector does not authorize reuse of a prior objective's
tableau, budget or radius bounds.  Independent primal and dual checks,
original-support extremality, primitive scaling and feasible slack-basis
replay retain their existing meanings.  More selector callback polls do
not change exception identity or count as extra simplex pivots.  Neither
lexicographic anti-cycling nor the polynomial LP policy bounds the number
of admissibility masks in the complete unknot search.

\subsection{Replay, preservation and the next implementation obligation}

Each completed call, capped result and diagnostic is saved before the next
call.  All $24$ arm/query records are present.  Independent rational replay
checks every original matching row and dual inequality, primitive positive
scaling, support nullity one, and a nonsingular original slack basis
representing the feasible point.  Source-model reconstruction additionally
binds each matrix and objective to its labelled endpoint, mask and
anchor/envelope context.  A final replay disables LP discovery and accepts
the complete controls, source capture and comparison.  The immutable
snapshot, scripts, protocol, full results and logs are retained in
\path{data/lp-pivot-pilot-973c6fdee.tar.gz};
\path{data/lp_pivot_pilot_replay.py} replays them in an isolated temporary
directory using the frozen sources.

Pivot counts and tableau updates are diagnostics, not elapsed-time or
complete bit-operation measurements.  Lexicographic comparisons themselves
have a cost.  The baseline's uninstrumented selector fields do not mean
its selector performs zero work.  This experiment supports testing the
lexicographic rule in a fresh complete call, with all outcomes retained;
it does not establish a universal speedup or a general quasi-polynomial
recognition algorithm.

Any future native adoption must port the selectors into the current
tuple-safe, prepared-matrix implementation, preserve explicit Bland
comparison behavior, and retain all cap and verification contracts above.
The frozen pilot's older tableau helper must not replace the newer matrix
ownership code.  The remaining algorithmic question is the behavior of
the complete search under the changed witnesses, followed by the separate
global resolving-parameter obligation.
