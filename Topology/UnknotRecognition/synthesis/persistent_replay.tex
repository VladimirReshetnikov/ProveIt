\section{Polynomial replay of an entire raw singleton block}
\label{sec:persistent-replay}

The persistent-elimination prototype in
\path{ProveIt_UnknotRecognition_Research_2026-10-09.zip}, delivered in
commit \texttt{92efcbaed}, gives a stronger representation bound than the
per-batch estimate of Section~\ref{sec:ordered-batch}. A sequence of raw
singleton eliminations can retain all historical word contexts in one
signed circuit, with one binding per eliminated generator. Balanced context
assembly makes the \emph{whole block} polynomial in its initial encoded
parameters. This removes a depth hypothesis for that block; it does not
bound unrestricted normalization, relator exposure or complete knot search.

The native integration applies this representation to independent replay of
consecutive version-eight \texttt{elimination\_batch} moves. It changes neither
the certificate schema nor producer selection. The new checker reconstructs
all contexts from the already independently recovered presentation, imports
no producer routines, and exports once at the next different move or terminal.
The separately implemented literal verifier is unchanged. The producer still
uses its existing immutable arena and needs a later representation integration
before the whole-block bound applies to its search.

\subsection{Bindings and raw word semantics}

A positive handle refers to a terminal $g$ or an immutable concatenation
$(u,v)$ of signed handles; a negative handle denotes the formal inverse.
Thus inversion reverses child order and changes both signs without allocating
an inverse grammar. A live terminal $g$ may receive exactly one signed binding
$g\mapsto w$. Every old occurrence then follows that binding, including
occurrences in older images. Concatenations themselves never change.

For a donor $U g^\epsilon V$, where $g$ has exactly one raw occurrence,
the relation gives
\[
                  g=(VU)^{-\epsilon}.
\]
The checker descends the unique occurrence path through concatenations and
bindings. Negative handles reverse the two children and their signs.
Siblings before the occurrence are collected in traversal order; siblings
after it are collected in reverse nesting order. Reversing the latter list
and appending the former gives exactly $VU$. Pairwise balanced assembly
creates the image with no expanded-word positions, slicing, free reduction
or equality test. Empty images are allowed. In particular, a cancelling pair
in a raw donor remains two occurrences rather than disappearing.

Every off-path sibling is $g$-free in the current binding graph. Installing
the new binding therefore cannot create a directed cycle. Subsequent
eliminations act on all old occurrences through their new terminal bindings,
so induction gives exactly the ordinary raw Tietze substitutions in every
retained relator. Only the donor's original slot becomes zero; all other slots
are retained. This argument requires the raw-word condition: a reduction
oracle that hid occurrences by cancellation would invalidate the path test.

\subsection{Independent batch validation and legacy witnesses}

Allocation order ceases to be a dependency order once terminal bindings can
point forward. Each batch therefore starts with Kahn's algorithm over all
historical circuit nodes. A repeated child contributes two incidence edges;
both edges are removed when that child is processed. Failure to obtain every
node rejects the circuit.

Assign ranks $1,\ldots,b$ to the batch's selected generators in their claimed
dependency order and rank zero to survivors. In dependency order, compute
the maximum rank in each current word and its multiplicity capped at two.
Bound terminals inherit their target's summary; negative handles have the
same summary as their positive counterparts. A donor is valid when its
selected generator is its unique largest-rank letter. This simultaneously
proves the singleton condition and that all its selected dependencies have
strictly smaller rank. Unbound terminals must belong to the current live set.

If the supplied order fails, the legacy path computes a selected-generator
support bitset for every node, derives the donor dependency graph, and runs
Kahn's algorithm on that graph. It then repeats the rank check in the recovered
order. A cyclic graph, missing pivot, repeated pivot, invalid source slot,
reused donor or eliminated generator rejects the witness. Thus unordered
legacy evidence remains supported without exporting the intermediate grammar.
The bitsets add a polynomial factor in the number of selected generators;
they are not treated as constant-size machine words.

All donor contexts in one batch are derived against the same pre-batch state.
Their bindings are installed together only after validation and construction
succeed. The strict rank inequalities prove the resulting binding graph
acyclic. Equivalently, install the bindings in increasing dependency rank:
each right-hand side can refer only to already installed selected definitions.
This has the same raw word values as the old simultaneous substitution.
Unused definitions are checked as well as those reached by surviving relators.

The public roots and live set remain unchanged while the entire block is
processed privately. The final export walks signed dependencies and emits
ordinary immutable arena terminals and concatenations. Each signed state is
compiled at most once; a binding becomes an alias. With $S$ circuit nodes,
at most $2S$ ordinary nodes are added, often fewer because only retained roots
are exported and interning can reuse old nodes. No mutable binding is inserted
into the ordinary arena, so its cached inverses, reductions and metadata remain
valid. A malformed later batch rejects the whole private block. Cancellation
or export exhaustion can leave historical arena allocations, but publish no
new relator roots or live-generator set.
If the ordinary arena initially retains $N_0$ historical nodes, the combined
node count during replay and export is at most $N_0+3S$, excluding ancillary
arrays, bitsets and integer storage.

\subsection{Whole-block size and bit bounds}

Let $S_0$ be the initial number of nonempty signed-circuit nodes, $D_0$ the
maximum concatenation depth with bindings ignored, and $k$ the number of
distinct generators eliminated in the block. No move in this block creates
a generator, so $k$ is at most the initial live rank minus one. Let $D_i$
be the historical maximum binding-free depth after $i$ eliminations and
$H_i$ the maximum full dependency height.

Every dependency path contains at most $i$ binding edges. Between successive
binding edges it follows at most $D_i$ immutable concatenations. Therefore
\[
        H_i\le(i+1)D_i+i < (i+1)(D_i+1)=A_i.
\]
A singleton context contributes at most $H_i$ siblings. Balanced assembly of
$q$ siblings adds at most $\max(q-1,0)$ nodes and at most
$\lceil\log_2\max(1,q)\rceil$ concatenation levels above their roots. Hence
\[
 D_{i+1}\le D_i+\lceil\log_2\max(2,A_i)\rceil.
\]
For simultaneous batches, first fix all sibling lists in the pre-batch graph,
then conceptually construct and install images in dependency order. Those
lists are no longer than the later historical height, and their original
concatenation depths are no larger than the later $D_i$. Thus the same
one-generator recurrence upper-bounds the implemented batch construction.

Put $T_i=D_0+i+2\ge2$. Induction yields
\[
                 D_i\le D_0+6i\log_2 T_i.
\]
Indeed, this hypothesis implies
$A_i\le T_i^2(1+6\log_2 T_i)\le T_i^5$:
$\log_2 t\le t-1$ and $6t-5\le t^3$ for $t\ge2$.
Consequently $\lceil\log_2\max(2,A_i)\rceil\le6\log_2T_i$,
which gives the next induction step because $T_{i+1}\ge T_i$.
Summing the bounds on newly created contexts gives the explicit conservative
historical-size estimate
\[
 S_k\le S_0+\frac{k(k+1)}2(D_0+1)
       +2k(k-1)(k+1)\log_2(D_0+k+2)=S_*.
\]
Only $k$ additional binding entries are needed. Initial import has at most
the reachable ordinary source size; signed terminals and interning can reduce it.

A binary circuit of full height $H$ unfolds to at most $2^H$ letters.
Thus all exact length integers computed during export need at most
\[
 B_*=(k+1)\bigl(D_0+6k\log_2(D_0+k+2)+1\bigr)
\]
bits. Rank summaries themselves need only $O(\log(k+1))$-bit ranks and
counts in $\{0,1,2\}$. There are at most $k$ batches; their topological and
rank passes cost $O(kS_*)$ graph operations. Context traversal and balanced
assembly are bounded by the same historical-height accounting. Unordered
legacy batches add selected-support bit operations and dependency-graph work,
still polynomial in $S_0,D_0,k$. Source import, sorting, generator identifiers,
interning, the retained relator-slot list and $B_*$-bit export arithmetic must
also be charged. These observations prove polynomial encoded replay for the
whole raw block, rather than composing a separate constant-factor grammar
bound once per batch.

The native node allowance charges private circuit nodes and all historical
ordinary arena nodes together, including nodes produced during export.
All graph scans, context steps and export work use the caller's existing
cooperative budget. This is not a hard byte-memory or wall-clock bound;
Python containers, integers and temporary arrays have additional costs.
The different charged operations can change finite-budget outcomes.

\subsection{Remaining integration and global obligations}

The new module is \path{../fast/fastunknot/persistent_elimination_verify.py};
the existing compressed source verifier groups consecutive batches and calls
it. An isolated batch keeps the preceding ordered checker. Any different
move ends the group before its own semantics are replayed. No normalization
is silently inserted or removed, and all existing certificate versions remain
subject to their original source and terminal contracts.

This is a real strengthening of replay for an entire raw block. It is not
yet a persistent producer or an exhaustive compressed donor-discovery theorem.
The existing producer may still materialize intermediate grammars. Arbitrary
alternations with normalization, Whitehead moves and new exposures have not
received a comparable global bound. Nor does a supplied block guarantee an
unknot terminal. Integrating a compatible persistent planner and controlling
those remaining operations are subsequent steps toward the original general
quasipolynomial goal, which remains open.
