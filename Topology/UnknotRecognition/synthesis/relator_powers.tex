\section{Quotient deletion and exact uniform-word summaries}
\label{sec:relator-powers}

\subsection{From repeated subtraction to a compressed quotient}

The occurrence matcher of Section~\ref{sec:compressed-matching} supplies an
exact place to delete a whole donor, but one deletion can make very little
progress. Consider the supplied presentation
\[
 \langle a,b\mid a^N,\ a^{N^2+1}\rangle,\qquad N=2^h.
\]
Both relators have binary straight-line programs of size $O(h)$. There is
no singleton generator until one exponent becomes one, and the cyclic
Whitehead graph has only the edge $\{a,a^{-1}\}$, with weight $N^2+N+1$.
A cut separating its endpoints has weight equal to the multiplier degree,
so it gives no strict shortening. With the explicit overlap bridge disabled,
the previous whole-donor fallback subtracts $N$ from $N^2+1$ once per move:
it needs $N$ deletions followed by an elimination. Compression alone has
not removed this exponential number of iterations.

The new move performs the quotient in one step. At the occurrence selected
by the existing matcher, rotate the target to a word $R$ beginning with
$D$, where $D$ is the donor's recorded spelling or its inverse. Put
\[
 Q=\left\lfloor\frac{|R|}{|D|}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad \ell=\operatorname{LCP}(R,D^Q),
 \qquad q=\left\lfloor\frac{\ell}{|D|}\right\rfloor.
\]
Binary powering constructs $D^Q$ without expanding it. Since the occurrence
was checked, $q\ge1$. This is the maximal number of consecutive complete
copies at that chosen occurrence, restricted to one turn of the target.
It is not the maximum gain over every occurrence or cyclic donor spelling.
If $q=1$ the producer emits the existing \code{relator} move. Otherwise it
emits \code{relator\_power}, replaces $R=D^qW$ by the cyclic reduction of
$W$, and retains the donor. An incomplete final copy is retained.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of quotient deletion]
Replacing a target $D^qW$ by $W$ while retaining the donor relation $D$
preserves the normal closure of the relators, for every positive integer
$q$. Inverting or cyclically conjugating either relator before applying
this operation does not change this conclusion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Modulo the relation $D=1$, the equations $D^qW=1$ and $W=1$ are equivalent.
More explicitly, $W=D^{-q}(D^qW)$ lies in the normal closure of the old
pair, and $D^qW$ lies in the normal closure of the new pair. The two normal
closures therefore coincide. Inversion and conjugation preserve the normal
closure generated by a word. Free and cyclic reduction preserve its relation
as well.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A versioned certificate with independent replay}

Version 4 adds the move fields
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Field & Meaning \\
\midrule
\code{kind} & \code{relator\_power} \\
\code{target}, \code{donor} & Distinct nonempty relator slots \\
\code{target\_rotation}, \code{donor\_rotation} & Valid cyclic offsets \\
\code{inverse} & Whether to invert the donor before rotation \\
\code{overlap} & The entire donor length \\
\code{copies} & Integer $q\ge2$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The record must have exactly these fields. Booleans are not accepted as
integer copy counts, and the count must not exceed the target length
divided by the donor length. The producer currently uses donor offset zero;
replay supports all valid donor offsets. The verifier does not require the
count to be maximal: any fully checked positive quotient is sound.

The literal verifier compares each letter of the removed target prefix
with the corresponding donor letter modulo $|D|$. It does not allocate
$q$ explicit copies of the donor. It then normalizes the existing target
suffix. The compressed verifier independently constructs a binary power
$D^q$ and checks exact equality with the target prefix of length $q|D|$,
then cyclically reduces the remaining suffix. Neither verifier calls the
producer's occurrence or application helper. A public knot verdict still
requires reconstructing the presentation from the original planar diagram
and checking the complete trace. Internal success on supplied relators is
not by itself a knot-recognition verdict.

Version 4 retains the earlier elimination, elementary Whitehead, ordinary
relator and Whitehead-power moves. The latter is valid in versions 3 and 4;
older certificate formats remain accepted. New quotient moves require
version 4, and unknown versions are rejected. Invalid claims return false;
work, time or storage exhaustion propagates to an inconclusive search,
not a negative knot verdict.

\subsection{Why batching also needs a better local query}

Initially, batching alone still exhausted the twenty-million-work allowance
at $h=500$. The target prefix and binary donor power describe the same
long constant word with different parses. A general compressed equality
algorithm can prove that fact, but invoking it repeatedly during LCP search
wastes substantial work. The arena now stores an exact summary $u(X)$:
\[
 u(\epsilon)=0,\qquad u(x)=x,\qquad
 u(XY)=\begin{cases}
 u(X),&u(X)=u(Y)\ne0,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here $x$ is a signed generator, and zero denotes either the empty word or
a mixed word. Concatenation already removes empty children, so each new
binary rule combines two nonempty words. Induction on the grammar proves
that $u(X)=x\ne0$ exactly when $X$ consists entirely of copies of $x$.
This costs constant additional work and one stored label per new rule.
It is an exact summary, not a hash or a probabilistic fingerprint.

If two nonempty nodes have the same nonzero summary, their LCP is the
minimum of their lengths and the optional cap. They are equal precisely
when their lengths agree. These queries require constant charged work
and create no grammar nodes, even for unrelated parses of words with
exponential length. Their integer operations still have the bit cost of
the stored lengths. Cap validation and budget checks precede the shortcut.
Words with mixed interiors continue through the general deterministic
engine; equal endpoints alone do not justify the shortcut.

There is also a separate allocation improvement. Before constructing the
inverse of a candidate donor, the search filters its possible targets by
distinct slot, length and absolute-generator multiplicities. If no target
could contain the donor, neither spelling is searched and no inverse is
built. For the family $a^N,ba^Nb^{-1}a$, the largest candidate cannot fit
in the shorter relator; previously its unused inverse consumed roughly
another copy of the large grammar. The new order succeeds with a
600-node allowance at $N=2^{500}$. This pruning changes allocation costs,
not which exact occurrences are eligible.

\subsection{A polynomial bound for the pure-power subproblem}

\begin{proposition}[Restricted Euclidean descent]
Let $m,n\ge1$ be supplied in binary, and initialize the internal compressed
search with the standard binary-power grammars for
$\langle a,b\mid a^m,a^n\rangle$. Enable relator moves, disable explicit
overlap expansion by setting its allowance to zero, and allow sufficient
compressed resources. The search succeeds exactly when $\gcd(m,n)=1$.
It takes $O(\log\max(m,n))$ quotient or ordinary donor steps, plus at most
one elimination, and has polynomial bit complexity in the binary input
length. On $m=N,n=N^2+1$ with $N\ge2$, it takes exactly two moves.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If either exponent is one, eliminating $a$ removes both relators and leaves
$b$ free. Otherwise there are no singleton generators, and the Whitehead
graph described above has no shortening cut. A larger donor cannot fit the
smaller target. The smaller donor matches the larger target at position
zero, and the LCP quotient removes exactly
$\lfloor\max(m,n)/\min(m,n)\rfloor$ copies. The surviving exponent is the
Euclidean remainder. Equal exponents leave one empty relator. Continuing
therefore reaches the gcd; gcd one permits elimination, whereas a remaining
single power $a^g$ with $g>1$ has no eligible donor target or shortening move
and the search stalls. Stalling does not emit a general nontriviality
certificate.

Every two nonterminal Euclidean divisions reduce the larger positive
exponent by at least a factor of two. Thus there are $O(H)$ divisions,
where $H$ is the binary length of the larger initial exponent. The initial
power grammars have height and size $O(H)$. A suffix operation in the
implemented grammar rebuilds only boundary paths, preserving or reducing
the height: this follows by induction at each retained binary rule.
All residual relators are suffixes of earlier positive powers, so their
height remains $O(H)$. Each binary quotient power and each slice adds
$O(H)$ rules. Positive words have no free or cyclic cancellation, so their
reduction does not increase height or allocate a larger normal form.
Doubled targets contribute only constantly many further rules per step.
Consequently the accumulated arena has $O(H^2)$ rules. The uniform summary
answers every needed positive-power equality and LCP query directly; the
first occurrence is a prefix, so no general occurrence table is built.
Summary traversals, sorting, cuts on this fixed-rank graph and terminal
elimination all require polynomial work on this arena. Stored lengths and
multiplicities have $O(H)$ bits. Including integer arithmetic gives a
polynomial bit bound, rather than merely a bound on move count.

For $(N,N^2+1)$ the first quotient is $N$, the remainder is one, and the
second move eliminates $a$. The old one-copy search needed $N+1$ moves.
\end{proof}

This proposition concerns an explicitly specified internal presentation
family and search policy. It does not assert that arbitrary knot diagrams
reach such a family cheaply. Nor does it bound the number of changing
nonuniform donor moves, general grammar growth, or the cost of finding all
partial cyclic overlaps. The proof therefore removes one concrete
exponential descent example without establishing a general subexponential
or quasi-polynomial recognition algorithm.

\subsection{Validation and controlled measurements}

The six new tests include 400 randomly parsed words for the uniform
invariant, 300 cyclic donor-copy examples checked against literal deletion,
100 small random Euclidean pairs, a long Fibonacci pair and its noncoprime
multiple, and quadratic exponent families through $N=2^{500}$. Expansion
is patched to fail in the compressed search tests. The uniform tests use
distinct parses, capped queries, opposite signs, a changed interior letter,
large cancellation and exhausted budgets. The allocation test forbids
inverse construction before the positive useful match.

A 142-move alternative trace for the genuine Gordian diagram includes a
checked two-copy donor move after 132 prior moves. Both public replayers
accept it with producer helpers disabled. Mutations of its count, offsets,
overlap, fields and version are rejected. This is a legal alternative
trace, not evidence that the default search discovers that macro or speeds
up this knot. A separate internal trace starts with small supplied relators,
uses a Whitehead power to create an exponential word, and checks deletion
of $2^{500}+1$ copies without expansion. It tests compressed replay,
not a public knot verdict on an invented presentation.

The integrated suite passes 672 tests with Regina 7.4.1 in 146.146 seconds.
The benchmark compares archived word-arena and donor-helper components from
commit \code{10bc9bb754} with the new components inside a common current
recognition and replay harness. Thus it is a component-controlled historical
comparison, not a run of the entire old executable. The A/A arm repeats the
archived components. Each case has one excluded warmup and five measured
rounds, with randomized arm order using seed 2709. All full knot queries
include fresh PD construction and independent certificate replay.
