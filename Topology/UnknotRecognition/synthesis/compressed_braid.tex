\section{Native compressed braid recognition and singleton forests}
\label{sec:compressed-braid}

The compressed-braid delivery arriving at commit \texttt{36f1dd8ef}
provides a useful complete algorithm on a restricted input domain.
We integrate its three-braid normal forms, singleton decompositions and local
proofs with the maintained exact string kernel. The result decides a supplied
binary grammar for a three-braid in polynomial bit time, given sufficient
resources, even when its expanded word is exponentially long. It is a separate
source-bound interface, not a conversion from arbitrary planar diagrams.
The earlier explicit three-braid criterion is unchanged; the contribution is
compressed discovery with bounded intermediate representation growth and replay.

\subsection{Input and exact finite summaries}

An input contains a strand count $m$, an ordered rule list and a root index.
Rule zero is empty. Subsequent rules are either a signed Artin generator
$\sigma_j^{\pm1}$, $1\le j<m$, or concatenation of two strictly earlier rules.
The represented object is the ordinary closure of the root word. Builder
operations for powering and inversion produce these same binary rules:
binary powering needs $O(\log(q+1))$ rules for exponent $q$, and inversion
reverses concatenations with memoization. Neither is an extra input operation.

Let $g$ denote input bit size, $s$ the number of nonempty rules, and
\[
 N_* = \max_i |W_i|,\qquad
 B=\lceil\log_2(N_*+2)\rceil.
\]
All supplied rules count, including unreachable ones, since validation examines
them. Strict back references give $N_*\le 2^s$ and $B=O(s+1)$.
Length and exponent sum are additive at concatenations; strand permutations
compose. For three strands this costs $O(s)$ operations on $O(B)$-bit integers
and constant-size permutations. The root permutation's cycles are exactly the
closure components. A non-knot closure returns \texttt{LINK}, separately from
\texttt{KNOTTED}. Boolean aliases for integer fields are rejected.

\subsection{The complete three-braid test}

Write $a=\sigma_1$, $b=\sigma_2$, $x=aba$, $y=ab$, and $z=y^2$.
Then
\[
 B_3=\langle x,y\mid x^2=y^3\rangle,\qquad
 B_3/Z(B_3)=C_2*C_3.
\]
The central generator has exponent sum six. Thus quotient element and exact
exponent sum together determine a braid: an element trivial in both is a
central power with zero exponent. The same argument lifts quotient conjugacy
when the exponent sums agree. In the quotient,
\[
 a\mapsto zx,\quad a^{-1}\mapsto xy,\quad
 b\mapsto xz,\quad b^{-1}\mapsto yx.
\]
Reduced syllables alternate between the $x$ factor and the $\{y,z\}$ factor.
For a one-component closure with exponent $e$ and cyclically reduced quotient
core $C$, the existing complete criterion is
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 e & \text{unknot core, up to cyclic rotation}\\\hline
 2 & y\\
 -2 & z\\
 0 & \text{four syllables: two }x\text{'s, one }y\text{ and one }z.
\end{array}
\]
Only cores of length at most four need explicit inspection.

Here is the algebraic reason the criterion is complete. Bennequin's inequality
applied to an unknot and its mirror gives $|e|\le2$. For the reduced Burau
matrix $M$ at $t=-1$, the three-braid Alexander formula gives
$\det K=|2-\operatorname{tr}M|$. An unknot therefore requires trace one or
three. A cyclically reduced quotient word of length greater than one rotates
to a product of $xy$ and $xz$. Up to overall sign and a fixed conjugation,
its matrix is a positive product of
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If it contains $p>0$ copies of $L$ and $q>0$ copies of $R$, expansion of
these matrices as identity plus a nonnegative elementary matrix gives trace
at least $2+pq$: each choice of one upper and one lower elementary term
contributes one to trace. Absolute trace three forces $p=q=1$; a pure
power has trace two. The remaining cores $1,x,y,z$ have absolute traces
$2,0,1,1$. Their exponent residues modulo six, together with $|e|\le2$,
give precisely the three rows above. Conversely these rows are quotient
conjugate to $ab$, $b^{-1}a^{-1}$ and $ab^{-1}$ with the same exponent.
The central ambiguity vanishes, and each canonical closure destabilizes
to an unknot. This restricted argument does not make determinant one a
general unknot certificate.

\subsection{One prefix query replaces expanded reduction}

The native \texttt{WordArena} supplies exact concatenation, substring,
equality and longest common prefix (LCP) on straight-line programs.
Syllable inversion here reverses strings, fixes $x$ and exchanges $y,z$;
it does not use free-group integer sign negation. For reduced strings $U,V$,
compute
\[
 k=\operatorname{LCP}(U^{-1},V),\qquad
 U'=U[0:|U|-k],\quad V'=V[k:|V|].
\]
If either remainder is empty, the other is the product. If the surviving
endpoints have different factors, concatenate them. Otherwise maximality
excludes inverse endpoints, leaving only $yy$ or $zz$; replace these by $z$
or $y$. The neighbors of this new syllable, if present, are $x$, so reduction
cannot cascade. Each input concatenation requires one LCP and at most one
additional factor multiplication, regardless of expanded length.

For cyclic reduction of a reduced $W$, take
\[
 t=\operatorname{LCP}(W,W^{-1},\lfloor |W|/2\rfloor),\qquad
 P=W[0:t],\quad R=W[t:|W|-t].
\]
If $R$ has at most one syllable or endpoints in different factors, its core
is $R$ and conjugator $P$. Otherwise maximality forces $R=fMf$ with
$f\in\{y,z\}$. Set $C=Mf^2$ and $Q=Pf$. Then
$QCQ^{-1}=PfMfP^{-1}=W$, and $C$ is cyclically reduced.
All trimming positions are binary integers; the operation never iterates over
removed syllables.

\subsection{Representation size and bit complexity}

A substring of a height-$h$ binary string grammar requires $O(h)$ new nodes:
only its two boundary paths are partly retained, and complete subtrees are
reused. Reassembling in the original tree shape does not increase height.
Inversion preserves height. A reduced input rule at dependency depth $d$
therefore has height at most $2d+2$: a terminal image has height two, and
product reassembly adds at most two levels above sliced children.

The maintained LCP routine first tries a bounded direct DAG comparison, then
uses exact equality and bisection. Its worst-case equality query count is
$O(B+1)$. There are $O(s)$ product/cyclic prefix queries, and every scratch
substring adds $O(s)$ nodes. Memoized inversion adds only a constant number
of rules per newly visited rule. Consequently the total string allocation is
\[
 S=O(s^2(B+1)),\qquad
 \text{equality calls}=O(s(B+1)).
\]
If $E(S,B)$ is the deterministic polynomial bit bound for the maintained
exact SLP equality procedure, discovery costs
\[
 O\!\left(s(B+1)E(Cs^2(B+1),B+O(1))\right)
 +\operatorname{poly}(g)
\]
for a fixed constant $C$. Exact compressed equality is the essential
primitive~\cite{schleimer-compressed}; a bounded heuristic alone would not
justify this theorem. Since $B=O(s+1)$, this is polynomial in input bit size.
The cubic specialization of the allocation bound is \emph{not} a cubic
running-time claim. Dictionary and integer costs also belong in the polynomial
term. The finite default budgets are operational limits; increasing all
allowances geometrically recovers the uncapped decision procedure.

\subsection{Independent replay and the host contract}

A proof binds the original grammar by exact canonical JSON, not merely a
hash. It records finite summaries, the string-rule DAG, one reduced root and
cancellation length per source rule, and the cyclic reduction witness.
The verifier reconstructs the supplied string DAG in a fresh native arena.
It checks reducedness, exact inverse cancellation equality and residual
endpoints; these endpoints establish maximality without searching for an
LCP. It then checks the cyclic witness and finite unknot criterion.
It imports neither the reducer nor its prefix-search procedure. The trusted
base still includes exact string equality, integer arithmetic, input parsing
and the topological criterion. Independent structure does not mean absence
of shared primitives.

Certificates have
$O(g+S\log(S+2)+sB)$ bits and replay uses $O(s)$ exact equality calls plus
polynomial work. Arbitrary supplied proofs are verified in time polynomial
in their combined input and proof sizes. The public \texttt{recognize}
function publishes a proof-bearing result only after independent replay.
Standalone \texttt{verify} performs the same source binding with fresh budgets.

The host shares one work allowance and one cumulative string-node allowance
across metadata, every factor, discovery and replay. It also caps input rules,
canonical input bytes, proof bytes and optional elapsed time. Defaults are
100,000 input rules, 100,000 allocated string nodes, ten million abstract work
units, 16 MB of canonical input and 64 MB of proof. Exhaustion returns
\texttt{INCONCLUSIVE} without a partial certificate. External cancellation
and unexpected implementation errors propagate; they are not converted into
mathematical verdicts. These are cooperative allowances, not hard CPU or
resident-memory guarantees. CLI input-byte checking precedes JSON parsing;
the API time allowance does not include that CLI parse.

\subsection{Singleton cuts on a compressed braid}

For general $m$, a reverse DAG pass propagates root multiplicities and sums
unsigned generator occurrences. A repeated child receives both contributions.
This counts occurrences in the root, rather than counting distinct terminal
rules. A missing generator separates two nonempty strand blocks with no
crossing between them, so the closure is a link. This test precedes allocating
an $m$-entry permutation. Otherwise all $m-1$ generators occur and
$m\le s+1$, bounding subsequent permutation work polynomially in $g$ even
when the declared strand count was supplied in binary.

A generator $\sigma_j$ occurring exactly once gives a connected-sum cut.
Rotate the braid to put that crossing at the closure seam. Every other
crossing lies entirely within the first $j$ or last $m-j$ strands, and
crossings in these two groups commute. A sphere through the single seam
separates the two projected closures, joined by the one crossing, of either
sign. The closure component count is the sum of the two projected component
counts minus one; a one-component parent therefore has knot factors.
Doing all such cuts yields ordered strand intervals. Projecting to an
interval removes other generators, shifts retained indices, and suppresses
empty concatenations in one pass through the input DAG. There are at most
$m$ factors and $O(s)$ projected rules per factor.

The verifier recomputes occurrence counts, exact cut locations and projected
grammars, then checks each leaf proof. Leaves with at most two strands use
their complete exponent criterion; three-strand leaves use compressed quotient
replay. Wider leaves may be rejected by $|e|>m-1$. In the initial integration an
unresolved wider leaf stayed explicitly \texttt{INCONCLUSIVE};
Section~\ref{sec:exceptional-cube} adds its complete bounded fallback.
A replayed forest certificate can
certify that residual description without certifying unknotness. For a forest
of knots, all trivial factors imply an unknot, and a nontrivial factor implies
a nontrivial connected sum.

\subsection{A conditional exceptional-factor bound}

After all singleton cuts, every retained generator in an unresolved factor
occurs at least twice. For its expanded crossing count $n_i$, strand count
$m_i$ and minority-sign count $\kappa_i$, this gives
\[
 m_i-1\le n_i/2,\qquad |e_i|=n_i-2\kappa_i.
\]
If its Bennequin obstruction does not already decide the factor, then
$n_i-2\kappa_i\le m_i-1$, hence
\[
 n_i\le4\kappa_i,\qquad m_i\le2\kappa_i+1.
\]
A complete exponential decision fallback on just these expanded exceptional
factors would therefore yield
\[
 \operatorname{poly}(g)+\sum_{i\text{ unresolved}}2^{O(\kappa_i)}.
\]
The solved compressed three-braid factors are excluded from the sum even when
their minority counts are exponentially large. With polynomially many factors
and $\max_i\kappa_i=O((\log g)^2)$, this bound is quasi-polynomial in $g$.
This was a conditional composition theorem at the delivery's initial
integration. Section~\ref{sec:exceptional-cube} connects the complete fallback
and its source-projection certificate contract, making the bound an implemented
restricted-input guarantee. No theorem bounds the exceptional parameters for
every knot diagram.

\subsection{Why matrix evaluation can destroy compression}

Let $u=ab^{-1}$ and $\beta_m=u^m(ab)u^{-m}$. Its grammar has
$O(\log(m+1))$ rules and its closure is an unknot. Under Burau at $-1$,
$u$ maps to $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Writing $b=F_{2m}$ and $d=F_{2m-1}$, one entry of the conjugated matrix is
$3b^2+3bd+d^2$. It has $\Omega(m)$ bits despite trace one. Taking $m=2^k$
therefore makes explicit integer matrix evaluation exponentially costly in
$k$, while the string proof remains polynomial. This is an obstruction to
materializing those integers, not to symbolic matrix circuits or modular
methods.

\subsection{Native validation and measurements}

All 53 files in the delivery manifest match their hashes. The original 42
tests pass, and the adapted 42 tests plus 12 host tests pass with the actual
maintained \texttt{WordArena}. The full maintained suite passes 885 tests with
Regina in 204.549 seconds. Host tests cover shared budget boundaries across
factor production and replay, cancellation during validation and replay,
Boolean field substitutions, source rebinding, legacy proofs and CLI limits.
Replay succeeds with producer reduction and LCP disabled. Huge inputs are
checked with the debug expansion method disabled.

The broader native audit checks all 87,381 words through length eight against
the maintained explicit gateway, an explicit free-product oracle and a separate
Burau computation. It finds 41,005 links, 22,208 unknots and 24,168 nontrivial
knots, with no disagreement. Four hundred shared random grammars agree with
the explicit controls; another 400 quotient checks force the polynomial
fallback by disabling fast prefix/equality probes. Capacity cases include
conjugating powers $2^{4096}$, differently associated conjugators, and forests
with 96 strands. Public discovery and standalone replay both pass. The
36.061-second audit and its exact source hashes are retained in
\path{data/compressed-braid-native-audit.json}.

The paired benchmark starts each arm with the same grammar. Its explicit
arm validates, expands under a $2^{20}$-letter cap, and calls the maintained
explicit braid gateway. Its native arm includes input/proof limits, discovery
and independent replay. Thus the native arm pays for a stronger proof output;
we do not subtract replay time to improve its comparison. Each algorithm has
an identical A/A control, one shuffled warmup and five shuffled measured
rounds. Small cases are batched ten times. Oversized explicit inputs are
omitted by preflight, not reported as timeouts or assigned speedup ratios.
Forest cases have no matched complete wider explicit gateway and report only
native timings. All raw samples, orders, budgets and source hashes are in
\path{../fast/results/compressed_braid_20261008.json}.

For the unknot sleeve with conjugating exponent $2^{16}$ (262,146 expanded
letters), the explicit median is 305.270 ms and native discovery plus replay
is 2.580 ms, with paired ratio 118.476. The nontrivial-core sleeve at the same
exponent improves from 254.164 to 17.952 ms, with paired ratio 14.169.
At exponent $2^8$, the explicit unknot path is still faster (1.198 versus
1.641 ms). Random explicit conjugators of length 500 likewise favor the
explicit gateway: 1.651 versus 48.793 ms. These regressions are retained.
The $2^{4096}$ unknot sleeve takes 434.503 ms including replay and emits
349,628 canonical proof bytes. The 48-strand forest of 16 compressed factors
takes 393.375 ms. These last two have no explicit comparison. Identical-arm
ratios show a few percent variation, so the measurements support the large
family-specific gains, not fine timing distinctions.

The ordinary explicit recognizer remains the default for explicit braid words.
This separate native grammar interface removes an exponential expansion cost
on a useful input class; it does not demonstrate an improvement for arbitrary
planar diagrams. An adaptive composition should use the explicit path when
expansion is cheap, retain compression for large shared words, and send only
unsupported residual factors to a complete fallback. A crossover observed on
one family is insufficient to install a universal timing threshold.
