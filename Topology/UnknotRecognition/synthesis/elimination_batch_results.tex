The complete-recognition experiment uses nineteen actual diagrams with
six shuffled arms: prior default, current default and optional portfolio,
each with an A/A control. Five measured rounds and one warm-up per arm
give 570 measurements and 114 warm-ups; all complete. The run takes
61.994 seconds. Displayed ratios are medians of paired old/new times,
not ratios of the displayed separate medians. Values above one favor
the new mode.

Current-default ratios range from 0.983 to 1.043; old/current default
A/A ranges are 0.934--1.053 and 0.988--1.029. The new optional portfolio
has a much wider range, 0.261--3.844, while its A/A controls span
0.983--1.024. These are real tradeoffs in search order, not a uniform
speed improvement. Mirror 03 improves from 23.779 to 6.198 milliseconds,
with paired ratio 3.844. It drops from 19 moves, 615 nodes and 25,431
work units to two moves, 212 nodes and 6,352 work units; its proof shrinks
from 1,491 to 774 bytes.

The corresponding survivor 03 moves in the opposite direction. Its
10.952/42.003-millisecond medians give paired ratio 0.261. One batch
eliminates ten generators, but subsequent search grows the complete proof
from 12 to 18 moves, nodes from 267 to 1,145, and work from 11,980 to
36,797. Proof bytes rise from 970 to 1,709. Survivor 01 and survivor 04
also regress, with ratios 0.490 and 0.313. A successful bounded trial
can still be slower than the untried baseline, and the work quota is not
a theorem of competitive elapsed time.

Gordian takes fallback and retains exactly the old 140-move, 9,663-byte
certificate and 9,025-node successful search. The failed trial is charged
50,001 work units, raising total producer work from 1,215,145 to
1,265,146. Complete-call medians are 1,476.434/1,490.813 milliseconds
with paired ratio 0.991. The guard prevents the direct batch schedule's
loss of this positive, but adds work; no broader coverage claim follows.

The separate checked group-stage experiment uses six actual stabilized
circles from eight through 256 crossings. Eight shuffled arms compare
prior default, prior primitive forest, direct batch search and the capped
portfolio, each with an A/A control. All 240 measured calls and 48 warm-ups
complete in 53.353 seconds. Every timing includes a fresh validated PD,
certificate production and mandatory full source replay. Earlier diagram
simplification is deliberately bypassed; these circles are not difficult
whole-recognition instances.

Direct batching uses one move on every circle. At 128 crossings, it
eliminates 127 generators in one batch, replacing 126 earlier moves.
Old/forest/direct medians are 351.086/110.656/30.048 milliseconds;
paired direct ratios are 11.684 against the prior default and 3.665
against the primitive forest. Producer work falls from 493,856 to 62,591
and nodes from 24,891 to 1,264. The direct proof has 6,247 bytes versus
8,640 for the old default and 24,286 for the forest.

At 256 crossings the corresponding medians are
1,422.297/390.203/74.867 milliseconds, with paired direct ratios
19.228 and 5.178. Direct work is 157,823 versus 1,905,056, and nodes
are 2,544 versus 98,939. The 255-generator batch retains a 12,775-byte
proof, compared with 17,600 bytes for 254 old moves. Across the stages,
old, forest, direct and portfolio A/A ranges are respectively
0.989--1.017, 0.990--1.012, 0.991--1.043 and 0.993--1.012.

The conservative host cap sacrifices those larger-stage gains. The
128- and 256-crossing direct producers require more than 50,000 work
units, so the portfolio abandons them and repeats the prior policy.
At 128 crossings its paired ratio is 0.971 and work is 543,857.
At 256 crossings its ratio is 1.001 despite increased work of 1,955,056;
timing variation does not remove that charged overhead. At 64 crossings
the direct producer fits the cap, and the portfolio has paired ratio
7.275 over the old stage. Both the successful smaller trials and the
lost larger opportunities are retained. The cap is an engineering policy,
not a scale-independent characterization of when batching is advantageous.
All new recognition options remain disabled by default.
