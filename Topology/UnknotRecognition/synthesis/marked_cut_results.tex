\subsection{Initial disk-interface audit and evidence boundaries}

This subsection preserves the first checkpoint before annulus absorption.
Its source pins and timings describe that earlier implementation; the
subsequent extension is recorded in Section~\ref{marked-cut:disk-absorption}.

The integrated audit replays the 106 source cuts supplied with report~85.
It produces 30 complete certificates, all accepted by the independent
source and genus checkers. Regina constructs the actual cut triangulation
and recognizes all its connected components for 99 cases; all 23 completed
native cases among those agree exactly in genus and multiplicity. Seven
large-coordinate cuts are excluded from literal expansion by the explicit
4,096-normal-disk guard, and their native certificates remain independently
replayed. No oracle timeout occurs.

The 76 unresolved native attempts consist of 60 full-circle interfaces,
six stalled raw cancellations, and ten cores containing three-handles.
These are refusal categories, not assertions that those cuts fail to be
handlebodies. In particular, the disk-base annulus absorption theorem
identifies further geometric work rather than grounds to silently accept
the current refusals.

The 22 focused tests include 1,200 saturated-versus-exact incidence schedules,
750 square matrices checked against exact permanents, literal boundary walks,
source relabellings, one-sided bases, forged proofs, shared resource caps,
and multiplicity $2^{16384}$. They also disable producers during replay and
exercise exact hexadecimal transport before the graph calculation.
Fresh audit data and source pins are in
\path{data/marked-cut-audit.json}. These checks concern supplied-cut topology,
not completeness of surface discovery or a global knot-recognition bound.

The full Regina-enabled regression suite passes all 1,648 tests with no
skips in 501.552 seconds on this host. Its existing expanded-sheet and
marked-cover audits also complete. The runtime source pins remain identical
to the independent 106-case cut audit. The replay-specific hexadecimal
regression accepts valid signed/uppercase encodings and rejects malformed
strings and booleans.

A separate algebraic feasibility probe examines the 60 annular refusals.
Exactly 38 have only disk-base circle attachments. Appending the proposed
unsigned two-handle rows permits complete saturated cancellation in 33 of
those 38 cases. This is a prioritization diagnostic, not 33 additional
topological certificates: the source-chart absorption constructor and its
independent checker are still absent. The probe and its source pins are
retained in \path{data/marked-cut-annulus-probe.py} and the corresponding
JSON. Its command is run from the repository root; it never supplies a knot
verdict or changes the accepted census.

\subsection{Complete-cut timings and binary capacity}

After the full regression run finished, ten finite cuts were measured in
five shuffled paired rounds after warmup. Both arms start with the same
face pairings and normal coordinates. The native arm constructs its entire
certificate and independently verifies it; the explicit arm builds Regina's
cut triangulation and recognizes every connected component. Input parsing
and output serialization are outside these timing intervals. Separate
identical-explicit controls have casewise median ratios between 0.982 and
1.089. The raw rounds and source pins are retained in
\path{data/marked-cut-benchmark.json}.

Three of the ten finite cases favor the native implementation; seven favor
the explicit reference. The 4,096-copy one-tetrahedron example improves by
55.702 in median paired time, while the twelve-tetrahedron single meridian
improves by 2.707. The small four-tetrahedron single-meridian example has a
ratio of only 0.032: proof construction and replay dominate. These measurements
justify neither a universal speedup nor a new default recognition stage.
They demonstrate the intended benefit of avoiding literal cut expansion
while preserving the complete component query and independent evidence.

The separate capacity runs use multiplicity $2^{16384}$. Production plus
replay takes 0.024 seconds for one tetrahedron, 2.406 seconds for four, and
12.652 seconds for eight. Their serialized certificates contain 1,701,021,
30,078,776 and 71,291,795 bytes respectively. At four tetrahedra and
multiplicity $2^{1024}$, the corresponding figures are 0.783 seconds and
2,510,984 bytes. These are single-run capacity observations; no expanded
reference was attempted. Large binary topology can therefore be certified
without enumerating its pieces, but the present proof representation still
has a substantial size cost. The eight-tetrahedron case retains 47 ports
and three maximal disk attachments rather than expanding its multiplicity.
