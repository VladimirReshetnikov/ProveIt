\section{Acyclic elimination with nonmonomial word images}
\label{sec:elimination-batch}

Primitive forests replace generators by powers of individual surviving
generators. A more general batch can replace a generator by an arbitrary
word, provided each defining relator contains that generator exactly once
and the chosen definitions have no cyclic dependencies. The resulting
implementation compiles a linked word circuit once, rather than expanding
definitions or repeatedly normalizing after individual eliminations.
This broadens the available raw batch operation. It does not prove that
arbitrary knot diagrams admit a short sequence of such batches.

\subsection{The simultaneous Tietze transformation}

Let a selected relator be $Lx^\varepsilon R$, where $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$
and neither $L$ nor $R$ contains $x$ or $x^{-1}$. Its equation is equivalent to
\[
 x=W_x,\qquad W_x=
 \begin{cases}
 L^{-1}R^{-1},&\varepsilon=1,\\
 RL,&\varepsilon=-1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Different chosen generators must use different original relator slots.
Draw an edge from $x$ to each other chosen generator occurring in $W_x$.
Assume this graph is acyclic. In dependency order, replace every chosen
letter in $W_x$ by its already resolved word. This defines a homomorphism
$\phi$ from the old free group to the free group on the surviving generators.
It fixes every survivor.

Repeated elementary Tietze elimination in that dependency order proves
\[
 \langle X\mid\mathcal R\rangle\cong
 \left\langle X\setminus E\ \middle|\
 \phi(r),\ r\text{ in an unselected original slot}\right\rangle.
\]
Each selected equation becomes trivial under $\phi$, which justifies
clearing exactly its selected slot. Every other slot, including empty or
repeated roots, is retained. A cycle is not allowed: simultaneous equations
$x=y$, $y=x$ do not justify deleting both generators. The source-replay
checker therefore validates the complete dependency graph before applying
the quotient.

This theorem is valid for arbitrary presentations and uses no
torsion-freeness hypothesis. A knot verdict still requires the validated
source diagram and replay of the entire presentation prefix. The existing
rank-one exponent endpoint and rank-two primitive-power endpoint retain
their source-dependent interpretation. The local batch helper alone is
not a knot-recognition API.

\subsection{Planning and compiling the raw circuit}

The producer caches presence and repetition bit masks at immutable word
nodes. At a concatenation, presence is union, and repetition is the union
of child repetitions and their common presence. Thus a present but
unrepeated generator occurs exactly once, without inspecting expanded
letters. Generator bit positions remain fixed throughout a search; current
slots and live membership are checked afresh.

Candidates retain the earlier singleton cost ordering: relation length
and total generator occurrence count determine the primary score, with
original slot and generator order breaking ties. The greedy planner picks
at most one definition per generator and one per donor slot. Before adding
the outgoing dependencies of a new chosen generator, it checks whether
any dependency already reaches that generator. This rejects cycles even
when their other edges came from earlier selections. It leaves at least
one generator alive. Search uses a batch only when at least two generators
are selected and therefore skips planning below rank three. No optimality
or general rank-halving property is asserted for this greedy selection.

For each selected donor the producer finds its unique occurrence and
constructs $W_x$ by exact substring and inverse operations. These are raw
circuits; no free reduction is required. It then links terminal $x$ to
the circuit for $W_x$, and terminal $x^{-1}$ to its inverse. Original word
concatenations and these new definition links form an acyclic graph.
An iterative postorder evaluation maps each encountered source node once,
building shared output concatenations. The word circuit may have forward
links relative to its old allocation order, so merely scanning old node
identifiers would be incorrect. The dependency graph supplies the missing
topological justification.

If another batch is available, search continues on raw roots. Otherwise
it records the existing explicit normalization boundary before returning
to positional overlap and Whitehead moves. A terminal can instead close
the raw state. Nonmonomial definitions can create many power runs, so the
bounded-run closure theorem of Section~\ref{sec:syllable-normalization}
does not automatically extend to these normalized phases.

\subsection{Version eight and independent replay}

A version-eight \texttt{elimination\_batch} move contains a nonempty
\texttt{entries} list. Each entry has exactly its original relation slot
and eliminated generator. The checker accepts no caller-provided word
images, occurrence summaries, dependency graph or topological order.
It checks strict integer types, live membership, valid distinct slots,
distinct generators and preservation of at least one live generator.

Compressed replay independently derives every unique occurrence and its
donor support from the reconstructed words. It builds its own definitions
and checks their dependency graph with a queue-based topological traversal.
It then evaluates the linked word circuit with a second Kahn traversal,
using incoming-edge counts and reverse adjacency, rather than the
producer's postorder mapping. No producer planner, metadata or application
routine is imported. Both evaluators implement the same proved quotient
while preserving separate word-processing code.

Literal replay derives the equations directly from lists. Before expanding
any substituted definition, it computes all resolved image lengths in
dependency order and the total retained-output length. It checks and charges
the total storage for positive and negative images and retained words before
allocating them. Large compressed certificates can therefore exhaust literal
replay while remaining inexpensive to check with word circuits. Failure
does not turn into a verdict. Both replay paths propagate external
cancellation and retain all existing resource limits. Legacy versions
remain accepted, and a version-eight move is rejected in an older wrapper.

\subsection{Encoded cost of a batch and conditional depth bounds}

The following conservative bounds describe the original analysis.
Section~\ref{sec:incoming-2c7f} strengthens the singleton-only bound to
linear grammar growth per batch and $2^{O(d)}$ composition, using the
disjointness of selected occurrence paths. It separately proves polynomial
cost for complete fixed-source monomial epochs.

Let $k$ be the number of chosen definitions, $M$ the historical grammar
size, $H\le M$ its maximum reachable height, $r$ the live rank and $B\ge1$
the maximum represented-length bit size. Each donor image is a substring
rotation with a possible inverse. Exact slices add $O(H)$ nodes per donor;
shared inverses add at most a constant multiple of the nodes they invert.
The linked-circuit evaluation then allocates at most one concatenation
per needed source concatenation. A conservative resulting storage bound is
\[
 M'=O(M+kH+k)=O((k+1)(M+1)).
\]
Occurrence scans, dense support masks, greedy cycle checks, slicing,
topological replay and integer length arithmetic are polynomial in the
encoded input parameters. Separate donor scans may revisit source nodes;
the implementation does not claim one linear metadata pass for all replay
checks. Identifier encoding and dictionary-access qualifications are the
same as for the preceding compressed operations.

If $L$ bounds an old expanded length, a resolved definition along a chain
of at most $k$ selected generators has length at most
$\max(2,L)^k$. A retained relator has length at most
$\max(2,L)^{k+1}$. Hence
\[
 B'=O((k+1)(B+1)).
\]
Together these estimates give polynomial encoded cost for an entire batch,
independent of the expanded output size and with polynomial dependence on
dependency depth. For $d$ raw phases, allowing both these and the earlier
monomial batches, a conservative composition bound is
\[
 \operatorname{poly}(\text{initial encoded parameters})\,
       2^{O(d\log(r_0+1))}.
\]
If each successful phase removed a fixed fraction of the remaining rank,
there would be $O(\log r_0)$ phases and this part would be quasipolynomial.
That rank-reduction hypothesis, arrival at a checked endpoint, and the
cost of arbitrary intervening normalization or exposure remain unproved
for general diagrams. The new batch closes an encoded-operation gap, not
the global discovery gap.

The capacity family is explicitly nonmonomial. With two surviving letters
$a,b$, define each successive generator by
\[
 x_i=(x_{i-1}a x_{i-1}^{-1}b)^{2^s},\qquad x_0=a.
\]
The raw length recurrence is $\ell_i=2^s(2\ell_{i-1}+2)$, $\ell_0=1$.
Eight levels with $s=128$ give 1,034-bit output lengths and grow the arena
from 1,076 to 3,959 nodes. Sixty-four levels with $s=64$ grow 4,484 nodes
to 17,215 and give 4,162-bit lengths. At sixty-four levels and $s=1024$,
65,924 nodes grow to 261,055, while the length needs 65,602 bits.
These node counts include the empty node. Separately initialized producer
and checker arenas give identical structural root digests and exact lengths.
These are presentation-capacity checks with two surviving generators,
not difficult knot instances or whole-recognition timing claims.

\subsection{A capped adaptive trial protects the established search}

Changing elimination order can make later exposure harder even when the
batch itself is cheap and valid. On the Gordian source, direct batch search
eliminates 127 generators in two batches but exhausts a two-million-work
allowance. The prior search succeeds. Thus soundness and fewer elimination
rounds do not imply faster complete recognition.

The first public host treated this new schedule as a bounded trial. Its
original policy, measured below, is refined in
Section~\ref{sec:elimination-reach}. With producer
allowance $W$, it gives the trial $\min(\lfloor W/4\rfloor,50000)$ work
units. On a nondecision or local resource exhaustion it reconstructs the
original diagram's presentation and invokes the preceding compressed
policy. The fallback receives $W$ minus the work charged or reserved for
the trial. On an exception the entire trial quota is reserved at minimum,
because initial presentation recovery can fail before arena counters are
updated. A final aggregate charge can cross the nominal allowance; its
reported cost is also deducted. The host retains the trial statistics even
if fallback later exhausts its remaining allowance.

Both attempts share the existing wall deadline, and caller cancellation
propagates rather than triggering a restart. Mandatory independent replay
still checks any returned certificate under its existing verifier allowance.
The restart uses the original source, not the modified batch state whose
subsequent search stalled. This preserves the useful alternative search
order at a bounded cooperative-work cost; it does not promise identical
coverage under every finite work or time limit, nor constant bit-time per
charged operation.

The \texttt{group\_decide} API accepts \texttt{elimination\_batch=True}.

For \texttt{recognize}, enable \texttt{use\_group=True} and
\texttt{group\_elimination\_batch=True}.

The CLI flag is \texttt{--group-elimination-batch}. The option implies
compressed search and replay and is incompatible with the older
explicit-to-compressed adaptive-search flag. It defaults to false and can
be combined with primitive projection/forest options. The lower-level
\texttt{compressed\_certificate} API also accepts
\texttt{elimination\_batch=True}. It exposes direct batch search with
the caller's full allowance; its result is a
certificate, not an independently verified verdict.

\subsection{Validation and measurements}

Eight focused tests cover one hundred random dependency DAGs with exact
literal homomorphism oracles; positive and negative occurrences; cyclic
donor rotations; empty definitions; unrelated and repeated retained slots;
deep nonmonomial circuits; strict fields, cycles, duplicate donors and
generators; source rebinding; independent replay; preflight allocation;
CLI dispatch; cancellation; and original-source fallback accounting.
The full maintained suite passes 1,008 tests with Regina in 213.793 seconds.

Against the full pinned package at \texttt{3346396cd135}, eighty actual
diagrams preserve all 240 results in the three prior modes. Every positive
proof passes both literal and compressed source reconstruction and replay.
Direct batch search returns 52 positive certificates, 47 of them version
eight; it loses Gordian relative to the old 53-positive set. The capped host
returns all 53 prior positives, with no new positive in this sample.
The audit, including the capacity checks, takes 13.718 seconds.
