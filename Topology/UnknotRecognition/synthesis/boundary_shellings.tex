\section{Certified boundary shellings before surface discovery}
\label{sec:boundary-shellings}

Optimizing a cocycle on one fixed triangulation does not search all useful
normal surfaces of the knot exterior. The new optional branch changes that
triangulation first: it removes a sequence of embedded boundary tetrahedra,
then runs the existing raw, tree-gauge and minimum-span candidate schedule.
Every removal preserves the exterior, and every positive answer carries both
the removal trace and its connected surface witness. This changes the family
of surfaces reached by a bounded search. It does not establish completeness
of that family or a general subexponential recognition bound.

\subsection{A deliberately restricted elementary move}

Let $M$ be the verified finite triangulated knot exterior, and let $T$ be a
tetrahedron whose four local vertices belong to four distinct global vertex
classes. This makes the closed tetrahedron embedded: identification of two
of its local edges or faces would identify their endpoint sets, and no such
sets coincide. Require that $T$ have $b\in\{1,2,3\}$ boundary facets and that
its entire intersection with $\partial M$ be exactly their union. In
particular, lower-dimensional boundary intersections must also be checked.
With one boundary facet opposite vertex $f$, vertex $f$ must be interior.
With two boundary facets opposite vertices $f_0,f_1$, the edge $f_0f_1$ must
be interior. With three boundary facets, all vertices and edges already lie
in their union, so there is no additional lower-dimensional condition.

The union of the boundary facets is an embedded disc in $\partial T$; the
complementary union is another embedded disc. Removing the open tetrahedron
and the boundary faces that have no remaining incidence replaces one of these
discs by the other. A collar of the attachment disc identifies this operation
with removing a boundary ball. It therefore preserves the PL homeomorphism
type of $M$. This is the embedded special case of the standard boundary
shelling move. Regina's documented move also checks lower-dimensional
boundary membership and face identifications; its admissible family is
broader than our four-distinct-vertices restriction
\cite{regina-boundary-shelling}.

Counting boundary facets alone is unsafe. After the first legal shelling of
the one-crossing-circle exterior, tetrahedron 3 of the densely renumbered remainder has four distinct
global vertices and just one boundary facet, opposite local vertex zero.
That vertex nevertheless lies on another boundary face of the exterior.
Deleting this tetrahedron fails our replay check. The retained regression
constructs the induced face pairing after this forbidden removal and checks
that the verifier rejects the trace, rather than relying on a producer's
eligibility flag. Generalized tetrahedra with repeated global vertices are
also excluded, even when a more general shelling test might permit them.

\subsection{Stable indices and bounded incidence updates}

The trace names tetrahedra by their original indices. Removed rows become
tombstones; surviving neighbors have only the appropriate reciprocal face
record changed to a boundary face. Dense reindexing is performed once at the
end, preserving the original order of survivors. A trace has fewer than $N$
entries for an initial $N$-tetrahedron torus-boundary manifold, and each entry
needs $O(\log(N+1))$ bits. There is no sequence of repeatedly copied full
triangulations in the certificate.

Initial global vertex and edge labels can be retained during these moves.
For a surviving vertex, the move deletes a boundary triangle or a boundary
disc from its link, or opens a spherical link into a disc. For a surviving
edge it removes an endpoint interval from its interval link, or opens a
circle link into an interval. The surviving links stay connected. Classes
therefore neither split nor merge; some disappear entirely. This justifies
using the initial labels to maintain counts of incident boundary triangles.
Both producer and checker update all contributions of one move before using
the new boundary status.

A surviving interior class can become boundary, but a boundary class cannot
become interior under this monotone deletion process; it can only disappear.
Consequently, the boolean boundary status of each original vertex or edge
changes at most twice. The producer keeps original incidence lists and
reconsiders their tetrahedra only when that status changes. It also rechecks
the at most four neighbors whose face records change. Across the whole
sequence these lists contain $O(N)$ entries and produce $O(N)$ affected
visits, including visits to tombstones. A heap selects the eligible tetrahedron
with the most boundary facets, breaking ties by stable index; version numbers
discard obsolete heap entries. Sorting affected indices and maintaining the
heap cost $O(N\log(N+1))$ indexed comparison operations and $O(N)$ storage,
after the initial geometry preparation. Counts, indices and versions have
$O(\log(N+1))$ bits. These are bounds on the added shelling stage, not a new
bound on all geometry validation or the later cocycle solver. Python hashing
retains the conservative collision-cost qualifications used earlier.

The independent replay needs no heap or incidence-to-tetrahedron schedule.
It tests the one-vertex or one-edge obstruction directly, exposes actual
neighbor faces, and compares every final face record against the prescribed
stable renumbering. Its additional work after initial preparation is linear
in $N$ in the indexed graph model. The producer instead tests equality of
boundary subcomplexes and updates counts in the removed tetrahedron's local
labels. These separate implementations are compared against Regina's legal
move checker and final triangulation signatures in the retained audit.

\subsection{Authenticating the simplified exterior and its surface}

The new outer schema \code{diagram-shelling-witness-v1} stores the canonical
source triangulation, stable removal indices and an inner surface certificate.
The inner certificate uses the existing disc, annulus-cap or planar-cap
format on the reduced triangulation. Its source PD still names the supplied
original diagram. Verification first independently reconstructs the canonical
source relation, then replays all shellings and checks the exact final face
pairing. Only after this authenticated homeomorphism chain does the surface
checker accept the reduced exterior as its source.

The connectedness, primitivity, matching, Euler and boundary-capping checks
are the same as before. A private checker helper accepts an internally chosen
source predicate; public legacy checkers always select the canonical-exterior
predicate. The shelling checker supplies the canonical-plus-replay predicate.
No public checker accepts a caller's assertion that a triangulation is already
authenticated, or accepts a producer's cached geometric analysis. Stripping
the outer shelling proof from these new examples makes the old source check
fail, as it should. A proof for a different knot also fails source binding.

An essential disc on the reduced exterior transports to an essential disc
on the original exterior through the verified homeomorphism. The same holds
for the disc obtained by annulus or planar boundary capping. No meridian or
longitude coordinate transport is required for this recognition conclusion:
essentialness of a boundary curve is a topological property. The certificate
need not expand the transported disc into a normal vector on the original
triangulation. Its explicit triangulation and trace overhead is
$O(N\log(N+1))$ bits in addition to the inner binary surface proof.

The API switches and CLI flag are:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Native search & \code{shellings=True} \\
Recognizer & \code{normal\_seed\_shellings=True} \\
CLI & \code{--normal-seed-shellings}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This branch shares the existing work allowance and cancellation callback.
It defaults to false. A bounded miss continues through the existing
recognition fallback and never certifies knottedness. Simplifying the
triangulation need not preserve which surfaces the original bounded candidate
schedule reaches; any deployment decision must measure that tradeoff as well
as tetrahedron counts.
