\section{Cycle overlap, rooted components and local trace search}
\label{sec:incoming-contexts}

Arrival \code{8188525b7} supplies six more manuscripts, now preserved as
reports 70--75 with their full PDFs, sources, code and evidence. Placement
commit \code{7dff9ca69} removes only their wrappers and placed archives;
existing Git attributes and ignore rules apply, without intake checksum or
manual line-ending processing. The subsequent mathematical review and
native integration are part of the authorized performance programme. The
original report directories remain immutable. Their results are preserved
independently of which implementation is selected.

Three themes improve the available theoretical interfaces. Affine profiles
bound distinct observed weights without expanding sheets. Cycle envelopes
replace total seam count by a smaller two-sided overlap parameter. Rooted
completion asks for one useful component rather than requiring an entire
surface to be a disc. The geometric reports add exact batch selection and a
constructive bounded local trace search, while proving an obstruction to
unrestricted active-support parameterization. Report 74 supplies the native
minimum-envelope improvement of the next section.

\subsection{Sharp weighted affine profiles}

Report 70 assumes an explicit base graph with uniform fibres of size $W$
and full-fibre maps $x\mapsto\pm x+a\pmod W$. A spanning-tree gauge reduces
each connected block to its root monodromy. The translation subgroup is
described by a divisor $d\mid W$; an optional reflection pairs residues by
$r\mapsto c-r\pmod d$. This gcd classification is inherited from earlier
cover work. The new results concern interval weights and exact attachments.

For $K$ transported signed vector interval records, write their lengths as
$\ell_j=q_jd+t_j$, $0\le t_j<d$, and let $I_j$ be their cyclic residual
intervals. A translation orbit has weight
\[
 F(r)=\sum_j(q_j+\mathbf1_{I_j}(r))z_j.
\]
Its endpoint sweep has at most $2K+1$ linear cells. In the reflected case
put $G(r)=F(r)+F(c-r)$. A nonfixed residue pair has weight $G(r)$; a fixed
residue has $F(r)$, not $2F(r)$. Reflection has at most two fixed residues.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp affine profile support]
The histogram has at most $2K+1$ distinct keys without reflection and
$2K+3$ with reflection. Across $b$ blocks and $h$ additional pointwise
attachments with additive payloads, its support is at most $2K+3b+h$.
The reflected bound is attained for every $K\ge1$, even with scalar
nonnegative weights.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The indicator intervals give at most $4K$ changing edges for $G$ on the
residue cycle. Its reflection quotient is a path, up to endpoint loops.
A reflection-fixed edge has exchanged endpoints, so an invariant function
cannot change there. Changing edges therefore come in pairs, giving at most
$2K$ changes along the quotient and at most $2K+1$ generic values. Correcting
the at most two fixed residues adds at most two keys. Small moduli one and
two satisfy the bound directly. Block supports add. An attachment touches
old orbit vertices; each new affected quotient component contains an
attachment, so at most $h$ new keys appear after replacing its old entries.

For sharpness take $d=4K+4$, $W=5d$, root maps $x\mapsto x+d$ and
$x\mapsto-x$, and weight-one intervals
$[d-j,4d+K+j+1)$ for $1\le j\le K$. The two fixed weights are $3K,4K$.
The nonfixed weights are every integer from $6K$ through $8K$, each once.
They are disjoint from the fixed weights, giving exactly $2K+3$ keys.
\end{proof}

The implementation may retain up to $4K+1$ sweep cells; the sharper theorem
is about distinct output keys. Equal weights never identify equal attachment
positions. The sparse overlay retains original endpoints and recomputes their
actual orbit labels after a bulk change.

During a fixed-base insertion-only epoch, a root translation updates
$d$ to $\gcd(d,a)$; a later reflection updates it to $\gcd(d,a-c)$.
A first reflection occurs at most once. Every changed divisor is a proper
divisor, hence at most half its previous value. There are therefore at most
\[
 \sum_i\bigl(\lfloor\log_2d_i^0\rfloor+
                      \mathbf1_{\text{no initial reflection in }i}\bigr)
\]
bulk rebuild triggers. Redundant maps need no rebuild, although reading their
input records still costs time. The forest, blocks, weights and attachment
coordinates are fixed during this epoch. Cross-block changes require another
contract. Full-fibre structure must be authenticated before applying this
kernel to a normal interval system; an arbitrary restricted pairing is not
a full-fibre map. The manuscript does not supply that geometric extraction.

\subsection{Exact ranks from the two-sided cycle envelope}

Report 71 assumes candidate partitions refine $\sigma$ and future partitions
refine $\rho$ on $r$ individually labelled seam intervals. Form their coarse
bipartite multigraph $H$, retaining parallel seams. A disconnected $H$ cannot
become a whole-disc completion by vertex splitting and is rejected. For
connected $H$, put $s=|\sigma|$, $t=|\rho|$ and
\[
 \lambda=r+1-s-t=\dim Z(H;\mathbb F_2).
\]
This is two-sided cycle overlap, rather than total interface size.

Choose a fundamental cycle basis $z_1,\ldots,z_\lambda$. Splitting a
$\sigma$-vertex according to $p$ imposes even-incidence constraints on all
but one of its subblocks. Their matrix $A_p$ has $j=|p|-s$ rows; similarly
$B_q$ has $k=|q|-t$ rows on the other side. Summing subblock equations gives
the omitted coarse equation, so
\[
 Z(G(p,q))=\ker\begin{pmatrix}A_p\\B_q\end{pmatrix}
               \quad\text{in cycle-basis coordinates}.
\]
For $j+k=\lambda$, the split graph has $r$ edges and $r+1$ vertices. It
is a tree exactly when that square stacked matrix is nonsingular. For any
other grade sum it cannot be a tree. The separated-interval gluing theorem
then gives the disc predicate, under its disc-fragment and geometric contracts.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp cycle-envelope dimensions]
The binary completion matrix has rank exactly $2^\lambda$, with
grade-$j$ rank $\binom\lambda j$ for complementary future grade.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The maximal minors of $A_p$ give a feature supported on $j$-subsets of
$[\lambda]$. Laplace expansion of the stacked determinant pairs it with
complementary minors of $B_q$. This gives the stated upper bounds.
For the lower bounds choose a spanning tree of $H$ and its $\lambda$ chords.
Detach the left endpoints of any chord subset $S$ into singleton subblocks,
leaving core blocks nonempty because each retains a tree edge. Its constraint
rows are the corresponding coordinate units, giving exterior feature $e_S$.
Detaching right endpoints of $R$ gives $e_R$. The split graph is a tree
exactly when $R=S^c$: each chord is then a pendant edge on the original
tree; a chord detached at both ends is a separate component. These states
give a complement-permutation submatrix of size $2^\lambda$, and its
grade-$j$ restriction has size $\binom\lambda j$.
\end{proof}

A cost-ordered original-witness basis retains at most $2^\lambda$ candidates
and preserves all admitted completion minima. The chord witnesses also force
every representative-subset method to retain that many on a universal family.
This sharpness is for the specified abstract continuation class, not every
embedded knot-region class. A feature row vanishes exactly when
$p\vee\rho$ is disconnected, providing a cheap shared viability filter.

The delivered complete finite-grammar compiler derives safe candidate and
future envelopes in polynomial time. Its elementary search cost is
$\operatorname{poly}(I,B)2^{3\lambda_{\max}}$, including explicit input size
$I$ and cost-bit bound $B$. A 256-arc example has $\lambda=3$ and needs eight
representatives. A general low-cycle geometric producer remains unproved.
Actual seams, fixed-type substitution and essential-boundary/source binding
remain obligations; a binary multiplicity is not one physical seam.

\subsection{One distinguished disc component: a ternary interface}

Whole-disc completion can reject a useful disc accompanied by unrelated
components. Report 72 distinguishes a root seam, records whether each
fragment component is a disc, and permits other components to remain bad.
Component-local charges lie in $H=(\mathbb F_2)^k$, with $q=|H|$.
The root component succeeds exactly when all its fragments are good and its
incidence graph is a tree, with the requested total charge.

Its feature coordinates are $(X,A,\gamma)$, where $0\in X$,
$A\subseteq X\setminus\{0\}$, and $\gamma\in H$. A coordinate is one when
$X$ is a union of good blocks, $X\setminus A$ omits exactly one point from
every block, and those blocks' charges sum to $\gamma$. Complementary
exterior coordinates pair the two sides, with charges adding to the target.
Saturation under both full partitions ensures that a connected restricted
graph containing seam zero must be exactly the actual root component. Thus
there is at most one contributing $X$, avoiding an uncontrolled parity sum
over several possible components. The feature dimension is
\[
 q\sum_{X\ni0}2^{|X|-1}=q3^{r-1}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact rooted ranks]
The uncharged binary rank is $3^{r-1}$. Every exact-charge kernel has rank
$q3^{r-1}$. For $q\ge2$, the nonzero-charge kernel has the same rank.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At each non-root label take three fragment choices: join the good root
block, be a good singleton, or be a bad singleton. Their compatibility matrix is
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&1&1\\0&1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det M=-1.
\]
Labels act independently, giving $M^{\otimes(r-1)}$. Arbitrary root charges
add a permutation factor for an exact target. For a nonzero target predicate
the charge factor is $J+I$ over $\mathbb F_2$; since $q$ is even,
$(J+I)^2=I$. These full-rank submatrices attain the feature upper bound.
\end{proof}

Cost-ordered bases preserve every exact/nonzero completion minimum using
original witnesses, even for large signed costs. For exact charges a
weighted universal family forces all $q3^{r-1}$ witnesses: use local costs
zero, two and one respectively for root/good/bad choices; appropriate
complementary choices uniquely expose each candidate as cheapest. There is
no claimed matching weighted lower bound for the nonzero-only predicate.
Rank alone does not supply such a lower bound. Already with one seam and
many root charges, the cheapest two distinct charges suffice for all
nonzero-charge queries in that simple family, despite full charge-matrix rank.

Two properly owned peripheral bits suffice for an actual disc on a boundary
torus: its simple boundary is essential exactly when its mod-two class is
nonzero. Aggregate charge cannot replace component-local charge; two parallel
essential discs can sum to zero. A permanent root marker is represented by
an algebraic dummy cap, not a physical cap added to the output.

The complete supplied layered-language algorithm costs
$\operatorname{poly}(I,B,b)q^3 27^b$ for frontier width $b$ excluding the
root marker. Portless non-root components can be forgotten while retaining
their costs and witness material; the objective is total assembly cost,
not root-only cost. Cyclic rotation of a site order increases frontier width
by at most a factor two, giving a root-selection theorem. The delivered
executable is a layered-language solver, not a general order compiler.
Mesh replay realizes bad pieces as annuli with formal charges; it does not
construct their three-dimensional embedding or actual boundary cocycles.

\subsection{A tree envelope does not compress every rooted query}

The whole-disc cycle bound and the rooted bound cannot be combined by simply
replacing $r$ with $\lambda$. A small counterfamily makes the missing
root-reachability information explicit.

\begin{proposition}[Rooted tree-envelope obstruction]
There are connected coarse envelopes with $\lambda=0$ whose uncharged rooted
compatibility matrix has binary rank $2^{r-1}$. A universal weighted
original-witness reducer needs $2^{r-1}$ candidates on a corresponding family,
even with nonnegative costs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $\sigma$ to be one block and $\rho$ to be all singletons. Their graph
is a star with $r$ edges and $r+1$ vertices, hence $\lambda=0$. For every
$X\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r-1\}$, the left candidate has a good root block
$\{0\}\cup X$ and good singleton blocks elsewhere. The right cap is the
singleton partition with root zero good and bad annuli at a subset $B$ of
the other labels. Its remaining pieces are discs.

The root component is always a star and is a disc exactly when
$X\cap B=\varnothing$. The matrix on these two families is the disjointness
matrix, a tensor power of
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, whose determinant
is $-1$. Its binary rank is $2^{r-1}$. Every other rooted row in this star
envelope is zero or depends only on its good root block, so this is also
the exact uncharged rank for that envelope.

Give candidate $X$ total cost $(r-1)-|X|$, counting its non-root singleton
pieces. Against cap $B=X^c$, the feasible candidates are precisely subsets
of $X$. Candidate $X$ is their unique cheapest member. Deleting any candidate
therefore changes one completion optimum, proving the weighted lower bound.
\end{proof}

This is an abstract surface-fragment result, realized by discs and annuli
with separated seams. It is not a lower bound for all knot algorithms or for
one fixed three-dimensional region. It does not force an exponential
subfamily for mere feasibility: in this displayed family the root-only
candidate succeeds against every cap. The lower bound concerns universal
bilinear compatibility and weighted minima. It identifies an additional obligation:
control the root-reachable good/bad frontier as well as cycle overlap. Fresh
checks compare 1,365 direct/feature entries, 341 literal mesh pairs and 63
uniquely exposed optima. The companion proof and audit are preserved even
though the result warns against a tempting performance extrapolation.

\subsection{Commuting moves and exact peeled batch selection}

Report 73 proves that tetrahedron-disjoint formal $3$--$2$ bipyramids commute
as relative-boundary replacements, even with shared global vertices or
paired facets. Their raw Euler and piece changes add. Every region has only
ten formal edge types; any overlapping collapse is centred at one of these,
so the deduplicated conflict graph has maximum degree nine. Greedy packing
of $M$ candidates therefore supplies at least $\lceil M/10\rceil$ moves.

Vertex-link-peeled Euler changes are not additive: the retained genuine
32-tetrahedron counterexample has singleton gains one and one but joint gain
zero. For a fixed disjoint family $E$, let $r_i=2g_i$ be raw gain and
$c_{vi}$ the reward for omitting move $i$ at global vertex $v$. Coherent
minima prove at most two positive endpoints per move and
$c_{vi}\le r_i$, with the sharper boundary bound $c_{vi}\le g_i$.
The exact omission objective is
\[
 F(E\setminus R)=F(E)+
 \sum_v\max(\{c_{vi}:i\in R\}\cup\{0\})-\sum_{i\in R}r_i.
\]
Among maximizers choose $R$ of minimum size. Removing any move uniquely
maximizing at at most one endpoint loses reward at most its cost, contradicting
minimality. Every retained omission therefore uniquely maximizes at two
endpoints, and two omissions cannot share an endpoint. Thus the optimum
omission set is a maximum-weight matching with edge weight
$c_{ui}+c_{vi}-r_i>0$. Integer transformation $(|E|+1)w_i-1$ selects the
fewest omissions among equal weights. This solves the fixed-family problem;
it does not optimize the initial packing or authenticate an essential disc.

Every positive edge has an interior endpoint, since two boundary rewards
sum to at most $2g_i=r_i$. Hence at most one omission per relevant interior
vertex is needed. The maintained pulling source has two interior poles,
which form a vertex cover of the reward graph; a linear scan solves its
matching problem. It retains at least
$\max(0,\lceil M/10\rceil-2)$ packed moves with nondecreasing peeled score.
The total number of boundary vertices is not two. Geometry preparation and
packing costs are charged separately.

Deterministic AVL corner trees retain all records and cache thirteen-key
prefixes. A hypothetical singleton deletion removes at most twelve old
corners, so its new minimum is among those prefixes and new corners.
Committed edits replenish them in logarithmic tree work. The complete
endpoint checker reconstructs the full geometry rather than trusting this
dynamic index. The delivered large batch/scoring gains are local fixed-workload
comparisons, not complete recognition measurements.

\subsection{Complete active support and a linear root obstruction}

For $C=\{x\ge0:Ax=0\}$ and positive objective $c$, report 73 gives one
primal--dual active-support certificate:
\[
 Ax=0,\quad x\ge0,\quad c^Tx\ge1,\quad
 s=A^{\mathsf T}y\ge0,\quad x+s\ge\mathbf1.
\]
Orthogonality $x^Ts=0$ proves that exactly one of $x_j,s_j$ is positive
at every coordinate. A positive $x_j$ witnesses activity; a positive $s_j$
forces that coordinate zero throughout $C$. Conversely summing active
primal witnesses and inactive-coordinate Farkas multipliers constructs
such a pair when the positive-objective part is nonempty. The negative
alternative is the original homogeneous dual $A^{\mathsf T}h\ge c$.
A lifted homogeneous system with $3N+2m+2$ variables realizes the pair,
but its exact Bland implementation has no polynomial pivot bound. In the
delivered adverse audit it reduces 130 unary calls to 17 lifted calls while
time rises from 1.4183 to 22.8305 seconds. It remains an optional interface.

Let $J(C)$ be active coordinates in still-conflicting type groups and define
$\rho(C)$ as the rank of their coordinate functionals on $\operatorname{span}C$.
Branching on an incompatible active pair $i,j$ into faces $x_i=0$ and
$x_j=0$ preserves admissible coverage and drops this rank by at least one
on each positive child. With a polynomial exact LP policy the binary tree
has at most $2^{\rho+1}-1$ nodes. Complete anchor enumeration is still needed.

For an unrestricted normal root with one zero triangle anchor per global
vertex, all quadrilateral types are active. The vector $r$ with triangles
zero and all quads one is matching. At any tetrahedron with no chosen anchor,
the local vector $w_a$ with four triangle entries $+1$ and three quad entries
$-1$ changes every face arc by zero. Both $r$ and $r+w_a$ belong to $C$.
Their disjoint quadrilateral images prove
\[
 \rho(C)\ge t-u\ge t-v,
\]
where $u$ is the number of anchored tetrahedra and $v$ the total global
vertex count. On pulling sources $t=20n'$, $v=4n'+2$, so
$\rho\ge16n'-2$. This rules out a polylogarithmic unrestricted-root ambiguity
rank on these actual sources; it is not a runtime lower bound or an
obstruction to other hierarchy algorithms.

\subsection{A constructive positive relaxation at every anchor}

The same local relation gives a further consequence, made explicit here.
Let $p$ count interior vertices of a finite compact manifold triangulation,
and let $\chi_{\rm lin}$ be the usual normal Euler functional extended
linearly to matching vectors without quadrilateral compatibility.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform positive anchored relaxation]
For every choice of one zero triangle anchor per global vertex, there is
an integral nonnegative matching vector $z$ satisfying all anchors with
\[
 \chi_{\rm lin}(z)=v+p-u\ge p.
\]
In particular every unrestricted anchor root with $p>0$ has positive Euler
LP feasibility. On the pulling diagram sources it has value at least two.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $z=r+\sum_{a\text{ unanchored}}w_a$. In an anchored tetrahedron it
has three quads and no triangles; in an unanchored tetrahedron it has four
triangles and no quads. Every face has one arc of each type, every global
edge has weight two, and every chosen anchor is zero. Thus it is a
nonnegative matching vector.

Write $e$ for global edges and $b$ for boundary facets. There are $4t-u$
pieces, $12t$ local sides and $3b$ unpaired boundary arcs, so
\[
 \chi_{\rm lin}(z)=2e-(12t+3b)/2+(4t-u).
\]
The manifold has $f=2t+b/2$ faces and
$e=v+t+b/2-\chi(M)$. Its boundary has $v_\partial=v-p$ vertices,
$3b/2$ edges and $b$ triangles. Doubling, or mod-two duality, gives
$2\chi(M)=\chi(\partial M)=v_\partial-b/2$.
Substitution yields $2v-u-v_\partial=v+p-u$. Since $u\le v$, the bound
follows. Pulling sources have $p=2$.
\end{proof}

This vector is deliberately incompatible: every anchored tetrahedron has
all three quad types. It proves LP feasibility and never an embedded normal
surface or a knot verdict. Thus an initial negative Euler dual cannot settle
these unrestricted pulling roots, whether their diagrams are knotted or
unknotted. The formal relative $2$--$3$ and $3$--$2$ moves preserve vertex classes
and the external boundary, so this interior-vertex feasibility consequence
also persists along those moves. The original linear rank bound is not
asserted unchanged after tetrahedra are removed. Type-restricted descendants
and other positive-witness tests remain meaningful. Adding a sufficiently
small multiple of $r$ also proves all
quadrilateral types active in the positive-objective part. Fresh controls
check 374 anchor patterns on eleven genuine sources, including both knot
and unknot diagrams; every vector matches, has the predicted formal Euler
value, and is rejected by the actual admissible-normal validator. The proof
is uniform and does not rely on these finite controls.

\subsection{Commitments, local regions and positive LP witness reuse}

Report 75 gives a constant-alphabet encoding for formal Pachner traces with
at most $U$ total upward $2$--$3$ moves. A tetrahedron incarnation chooses
idle, one of four upward faces, or one of six downward edges. Two different
ready committed events cannot compete for an incarnation: its selected
port identifies a unique legal event. Disjoint simultaneously enabled
replacements commute relative to their boundary and transported cocycle.
An intended ready event can be moved ahead of earlier disjoint events,
giving a deterministic scheduler with the same endpoint.

If there are $u\le U$ upward and $d$ downward moves, $d\le t+u$ and the
number of original/new incarnations is
$t+3u+2d\le3t+5U$. Padding commitments with idle symbols therefore bounds
commuting trace classes, including all finite prefixes, by $11^{3t+5U}$.
With $U=0$ the alphabet has seven symbols. Exact sleep-set exploration
inherits the bound using persistent incarnation/event identities and the
specific geometric commutation relation. This is not a theorem for arbitrary
state-dependent independence rules or only for maximal traces.

Protect originals outside an allowed region $F$ of size $i$. Then
$d\le i+u$ and only $3i+5U$ incarnations need free commitments. The dual
graph has degree at most four. A rooted depth-first tree traversal bounds
connected $i$-vertex regions by $t16^{i-1}$; distributing components gives
at most $(r+1)(c+1)t^c32^r$ regions of size at most $r$ with at most $c$
components. Canonical-parent reverse search constructs them with polynomial
delay rather than enumerating all $t^r$ subsets. The resulting complete
bounded search costs
\[
 t^c2^{O(r+U)}\operatorname{poly}(t+U+B)
\]
when its endpoint predicate is complete and polynomial; otherwise its cost
is charged explicitly. The region is a cover of the actual consumed
original cells, which need not have only $c$ components. Neither maximum
excess size nor upward-burst count can replace total $U$. Upward cocycle
transport adds at most one coefficient bit per move; downward removal adds
none. Source-complete successful regions with $c=O(\log t)$ and
$r+U=O(\log^2t)$ would give a constructive QP class. No such general coverage
theorem is supplied.

The other interface reuses exact positive kernel witnesses for deletion LPs.
If a checked witness already has the queried coordinate zero, it certifies
feasibility of that deletion without solving again. Local reuse works even
with global cache capacity zero; a source/anchor-bound support antichain
extends reuse across masks. Only positive deletion queries whose witness
content does not affect the fixed propagation policy are skipped, preserving
every completed deterministic certificate. The delivered algebraic control
has LP calls $3s^2+2s+1$ versus $2s+1$ and Bland pivots
$6s^3+3s^2+s$ versus $3s^2+s$. It is not a claimed triangulation realization.
The source adapter's four surveyed positives all use zero moves, validating
provenance rather than adding move-search coverage. Bounded exhaustion stays
inconclusive as a knot question.

\subsection{Fresh evidence and integration decisions}

Scratch validation of reports 70--72 passes 34, 42 and 42 test methods.
Fresh audits reproduce the sharp-profile tests; 633,531 cycle-envelope
entries over 3,162 matrices, 16,904 weighted queries and 1,412 literal grammar
assignments; and the rooted charged/mesh/language audit with 10,000 weighted
queries and 3,200 assignments. Their complete original theorem ledgers and
proofs remain available. Report 73 passes 51 standard-library tests plus
five optional matching tests in an isolated NetworkX 3.7 environment, with
independent active and geometric replay. Report 75 passes 28 standard-library
tests plus its optional native-topology test, and fresh geometric, region,
LP and Regina replays. Detailed fresh counts and reproduction records are
retained in \code{data/incoming-818-*}; the new relaxation check is
\code{data/anchored-relaxation.json} and its companion script.

These finite checks support source-level review, not proof-assistant
formalization or an originality claim beyond the reports' attributions.
The affine, cycle-envelope, rooted-language, active-face and trace/cache
overlays remain separate research interfaces: their geometric admission and
complete-source promises are not supplied by their algebraic features.
Minimum envelopes are integrated and tested on the maintained native sector
API in the next section. All six manuscripts remain preserved, including
the structural obstructions and other results without a measured native
recognition gain. None establishes a general subexponential or QP recognizer.
