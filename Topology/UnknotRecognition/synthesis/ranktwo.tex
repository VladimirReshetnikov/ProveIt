\section{Verified rank-two braid preprocessing}
\label{sec:ranktwo-integration}

The incoming archive \path{unknot_rank_two_kernels_20261007.zip}
is now preserved as report 19. It contributes an exact braid-word compressor. It finds an optimal set of
disjoint two-index subwords to replace by the empty word or a single
Artin letter. We integrate the search and its independent replay verifier
as an optional bounded preprocessing stage, after the inexpensive existing
certificates and before the more expensive braid descent and diagram
fallbacks. Unlike the closed-three-braid gateway, this procedure proves
braid equality inside the surrounding word.

\subsection{Faithful local states without copying prefixes}
For adjacent indices write $a=\sigma_i$, $b=\sigma_{i+1}$.
The local group is
\[
 B_3=\langle a,b\mid aba=bab\rangle
     =\langle x,y\mid x^2=y^3\rangle,
 \qquad x=aba,\quad y=ab.
\]
The common element $z=x^2=y^3$ is central and has Artin exponent sum six.
The quotient by $z$ is $C_2*C_3$. Thus a local braid is uniquely determined
by its reduced quotient word together with its exponent sum: equal
quotients differ by $z^k$, and equal exponents force $6k=0$.

The quotient normal form must not be cyclically reduced. The conjugate
letters $a$ and $b$ are not equal inside an arbitrary context. Nor may the
central exponent guard be omitted: $(aba)^2$ has empty quotient word but
is nontrivial. For distant indices the local subgroup is $\mathbb Z^2$,
so two exponent counts suffice. A single index uses one count.

In the quotient, the four signed letters have images
$y^2x,xy,xy^2,yx$. Each appends two tokens to an alternating normal-form
stack. The search stores these stacks persistently in a trie with three
child slots per node. Push creates or reuses a child, cancellation follows
a parent pointer, and combination of order-three tokens uses a child of
the parent. Node identity is an exact canonical word identity; no hash
fingerprint substitutes for group equality. The prefix tables themselves
use deterministic AVL trees in the integrated recognition stage.

\subsection{An optimal pass over bounded-overlap intervals}
Compress maximal runs of a common absolute generator index. Label each
edge between consecutive runs by its unordered pair of indices. A maximal
string of equal edge labels specifies a maximal two-index interval,
called a corridor in the source. Consecutive corridors overlap in exactly
one run; nonneighbors are disjoint. Every permitted subinterval lies in a
corridor, each letter belongs to at most two, and the sum of their lengths
is at most $2n$.

Let $F[j]$ be the maximum number of letters removable from the first $j$
letters by disjoint permitted replacements. Inside one active corridor,
write $p_j$ for its local prefix product. Maintain an exact table
\[
 H(q)=\max_{i:p_i=q}(F[i]-i),
\]
with an attaining start position. For a target $v$ of length zero or one,
\[
 w[i:j]=v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_i=p_jv^{-1}.
\]
Therefore the next optimum is
\[
 F[j]=\max\left(F[j-1],\,
       \max_{\text{active corridors},\,v}\{j-|v|+H(p_jv^{-1})\}\right).
\]
Queries precede insertion of the current endpoint. Backtracking the
attaining starts recovers disjoint replacements. The last replacement of
any optimal prefix plan is represented in this recurrence, proving
optimality by induction. This is optimality for one pass and this explicit
rule set, not global minimality among braid presentations or Markov moves.

There are at most five targets per corridor and at most two active
corridors. Each adjacent-group update takes two trie-token operations.
There are $O(n)$ exact states, queries, and backpointers. AVL table access
costs $O(\log(n+2))$ comparisons, giving
\[
 O(n\log(n+2))\ \text{word-RAM time},\qquad O(n)\ \text{words}.
\]
State identifiers, exponent counts, and indices have
$O(\log(n+s+2))$ bits for $s$ strands, giving the conservative bit bound
$O(n\log^2(n+s+2))$. The low-level package also retains an optional hash
backend whose dictionary-time bound is expected rather than deterministic.
Recognition uses one AVL pass. Repeated passes remain available through
the low-level API, but their conservative total bound is quadratic up to
logarithmic factors.

\subsection{Independent replay and evidence association}
The verifier does not import the optimizer or its quotient trie. It uses
a central normal form $z^k f_1\cdots f_r$ with alternating $x,y,y^2$
factors. Each signed Artin letter expands into $z^{-1}$ times the same two
positive factors used above; reductions $xx=z$ and $y^3=z$ update the
central integer explicitly. A replacement is accepted only if its source
and target differ by the identity in this form, or by equal exponent
counts in the commuting case.

Stable linked-list node identifiers describe interval endpoints across
passes. Inserted target letters get fresh identifiers. Replay checks that
endpoints remain live and correctly ordered, that the interval uses at
most two indices, that the target is permitted, that equality holds, and
that the word strictly shortens. The final output is reconstructed and
compared exactly with the certificate. Its input digest binds the
transcript to the supplied word; it is not an authenticity signature.

Braid equality preserves exponent parity, so each successful replacement
saves at least two letters. Across all passes there are at most $n/2$
insertions and $3n/2$ removed nodes. Thus valid-certificate replay takes
linear word-RAM work and memory, even when search used multiple passes.
The verifier establishes soundness of the replacements; it does not
certify the optimizer's separate optimality or saturation claims.

The pipeline requires checked source-braid provenance. It compresses that
source, independently replays the transcript, and constructs a new validated
PD only if the word is shorter than the current simplified diagram. An
accepted replacement restarts recognition on the new source with compression
disabled, preserving all other options and the remaining global time.
Earlier PD reductions and later reductions lie on distinct branches of
the evidence, named \code{before\_ranktwo} and \code{after\_ranktwo}; the
source word and braid-equality certificate connect the original knot to
the restarted input. They must not be concatenated as one PD move trace.
The existing factor and twist provenance rules then apply to the restarted
recognition without modification.

The stage is enabled by \code{use\_ranktwo=True} or \code{--ranktwo}.
It has one shared local allowance for search and replay, defaulting to
$0.1$ seconds, configurable through \code{ranktwo\_seconds} or
\code{--ranktwo-seconds}. Budgets are cooperative. Local time or allocation
failure skips the probe and continues the existing complete route;
global exhaustion returns \code{UNKNOWN}. A failed certificate is an error,
never a knot verdict. Already decisive cheap certificates retain priority.
Inputs without checked braid provenance bypass this stage.

\subsection{A constructive quasi-polynomial input class and its barrier}
Let $\kappa_1(w)$ be the smallest output length under the one-pass rules.
The optimizer finds this number without receiving a decomposition. A
\emph{flat rank-two inflation} of a $k$-letter core replaces disjoint
letters by equal rank-two words and inserts disjoint rank-two identity
blocks. Those blocks themselves define an admissible plan, so
$\kappa_1(w)\le k$. Following the verified pass by an uncapped exact
exponential homology computation takes
\[
 O(n\log^2(n+s+2))+
 \operatorname{poly}(\kappa_1(w))2^{O(\kappa_1(w))}.
\]
For a knot closure the reduced strand count satisfies
$s\le\kappa_1(w)+1$: a word on $s$ strands whose permutation is one cycle
needs at least $s-1$ adjacent transpositions. This removes a hidden strand
factor. Flat inflations of $O(\log^2 n)$-letter cores consequently admit
quasi-polynomial recognition without a supplied inflation decomposition.
The statement concerns the uncapped route, including
\code{ranktwo\_seconds=None} in the integrated API. A local cutoff can
miss the compression and invoke an exponential fallback on the larger
input even when that fallback eventually completes; a finite global budget
can return \code{UNKNOWN}. The integrated earlier simplifications have
polynomial overhead and do not invalidate this conditional bound.

No universal small-core theorem follows. An explicit obstruction is
\[
 U=abcaba,
 \quad V=cbacbc,
 \quad q_h=(UV^{-1})^h abc\quad\text{in }B_4.
\]
The positive words $U,V$ are equal half-twists, so $q_h$ closes to the
unknot and has length $12h+3$. Within a uniform-sign block, exponent sum
proves every subword geodesic. Any two-index subword crossing a sign
boundary is contained in $ac^{-1}$ or $c^{-1}a$, flanked by index $b$;
its commuting exponent vector has norm two, proving geodesicity there
as well. Thus no strictly shortening rank-two substitution of any target
length can begin on this family. Escaping it requires, for example,
length-preserving relations, a larger support, or separately certified
closed-knot operations. Increasing the number of identical shortening
passes cannot solve that obstruction.

\subsection{Production validation and end-to-end measurements}
The archive's 33 tests pass locally. Its previously unrun real-upstream
smoke test now passes all 16 sleeve cases against the current braid gateway:
the gateway alone is inconclusive before compression and certifies the
unknot after replay. This gateway result does not by itself establish an
end-to-end speedup; the current diagram simplifier may already decide them.

All 272 integrated tests pass. New tests compare 200 random words with an
independent exhaustive interval oracle, both dictionaries, both target
radii, and the Artin action on a free group. Sixty knot inputs compare full
normalized homological profiles before and after shortening, checking
$d^2=0$. Other regressions cover central and conjugacy guards, certificate
corruption, the higher-rank barrier, original input counts, evidence scopes,
local and global budgets, absent provenance, preservation of cheap verdicts,
and restart into the standard, shared, twist, barcode, and Fitting backends.
Logs and the actual gateway smoke-test output are retained in
\path{synthesis/data/}.

The benchmark uses seven shuffled paired rounds with an identical A/A
control. It includes PD construction, all default certificates and
simplifications, compression, replay, and the complete fallback; imports
are excluded. It uses a five-second global budget and the default
$0.1$-second compression budget. The source inputs, verdicts, methods,
transcripts, and timings are recorded. Separate traced-allocation runs
record both their peak and the path actually taken, since instrumentation
can affect a time-limited branch. These are measurements of this machine,
not asymptotic guarantees or a broadly sampled knot distribution.

The sleeve cases with parameters $1,16,64$ improve by approximately
$3.4$, $12.5$, and $33.8$ times respectively. Their baseline already
certifies the unknot through diagram reduction; compression reaches the
short source braid and its genus-zero certificate sooner. The stress
five-braid instead runs about $1.8$ times slower: its probe cannot supply
a shorter input than the current simplified diagram, and Alexander still
provides the verdict. The barrier fixture is settled by the descending
diagram certificate before compression, and the positive braid by
Bennequin's obstruction; neither timing measures a compressor success.
The two PD-only named knots bypass the stage. These contrasting outcomes
support keeping the new stage optional.

In the separate traced-allocation run, the largest sleeve baseline hit the
five-second global deadline. Its recorded peak is censored and must not be
compared as a peak of a completed recognition. All ordinary timed arms
completed with agreeing verdicts.
