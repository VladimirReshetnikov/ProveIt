\section{An obstruction to the entire coherent-height family}
\label{sec:coherent-obstruction}

The tree, minimum-span and Euler-optimization searches vary representatives
of a primitive integral cocycle. Their failure on an input previously left
two possibilities: the search had missed a useful representative, or the
entire coherent-height family was unsuitable. An explicit solid-torus
triangulation now separates these possibilities. It has eight tetrahedra and
one global vertex. Every primitive integral coherent-height surface on it
has the same normal coordinates, describing a connected orientable surface
of genus two with one boundary component. A separate admissible vector is
a compressing disc. Thus even exhaustive gauge optimization cannot make
this restricted family complete on arbitrary solid-torus triangulations.

This is a limitation of the candidate representation, not a negative knot
certificate. In particular, it does not establish failure on the canonical
diagram exteriors used by the maintained recognizer. Those exteriors have
additional structure and vertices. It also does not rule out a method that
changes triangulations, compresses surfaces, or searches other normal vectors.
The general quasipolynomial recognition question is unchanged.

\subsection{Certifying a change of triangulation}

The new local operation \code{pachner\_23} replaces two distinct tetrahedra
joined along a chosen face by three tetrahedra. Number the three corners of
the shared face $0,1,2$, in ascending local order on the first tetrahedron;
call its opposite apex $3$ and the second apex $4$. These are \emph{formal}
labels, not global vertex classes. The new tetrahedra, with the displayed
local orders, are
\[
 (3,4,0,1),\qquad (3,4,1,2),\qquad (3,4,2,0).
\]
Pair their repeated triangular facets by the identity on formal labels.
The remaining six facets are exactly the boundary facets of the old
bipyramid. Transfer every old boundary gluing, including gluings between
these six facets, through their formal labels. Surviving old tetrahedra
retain their order and are renumbered densely; the three new ones follow.

Both fillings are balls with the same labelled boundary. A PL
homeomorphism relative to that boundary descends through its prescribed
identifications to the ambient triangulation. The five formal vertices need
not be five different global vertices: all coincide in the obstruction
below. The selected old tetrahedra must be distinct, and their common face
must be paired. This is the usual bipyramid description of a $2$--$3$
Pachner move; Regina documents the more general attaching-face construction
\cite{regina-pachner}.

The independent checker imports no replacement producer. It validates both
finite manifolds, checks the unchanged outside gluings, and constructs a
signature of the six formal boundary facets in each filling. A signature
records a free boundary face, a pairing to another formal boundary face, or
an attachment to a stable outside tetrahedron, together with the full
three-vertex map. It separately verifies the interior face pairings.
Equality of the signatures proves the relative-boundary replacement.
A valid output manifold of the right size is not by itself sufficient.

For a supplied triangulation of $N$ tetrahedra, face rewriting and signature
comparison use $O(N)$ indexed operations and storage, in addition to finite
manifold preparation. The certificate records only the chosen tetrahedron
and face, but this experiment retains every intermediate triangulation.
A $K$-move record therefore uses
$O((KN+K^2)\log(N+K))$ bits for these snapshots. No compressed move-sequence
bound or bound on finding a useful sequence is asserted. The operation is
an explicit geometry API and is not added to the default recognition search.

\subsection{A reproducible eight-tetrahedron example}

Start with the standard two-tetrahedron layered solid torus returned by
\code{layered\_torus(2)}. The certificate includes its complete face pairing;
replay requires exact equality with this fixed root. Apply the following
six moves, using zero-based tetrahedron and opposite-face indices in each
successive canonical output:
\[
 (1,2),\ (0,1),\ (2,2),\ (3,1),\ (3,2),\ (6,0).
\]
Every relative-boundary replacement is checked independently. The final
triangulation has one vertex and ten edges. Regina independently recognizes
it as a solid torus, with isomorphism signature
\code{iHvrQAkbgfeffhhasssvcaj}. The native argument uses the fixed layered
solid-torus root and the six checked moves; no topology-engine verdict is
an input to the move checker.

\[
 |x|_1=41,\qquad \chi(x)=-3,\qquad

These are valid normal coordinates, but the following rank argument excludes
them from the coherent family, regardless of the chosen heights.

\subsection{An exact certificate for all primitive coherent vectors}

Let $E$ be the global edge set, with the representative orientations
reconstructed by the geometry validator. Build the integral face matrix
$A$ with one row per global triangular face. For ascending local face
corners $a,b,c$, the equation is
\[
 c_{ab}+c_{bc}-c_{ac}=0,
\]
with signs changed as required by the chosen global edge orientations.
Repeated occurrences of an edge contribute to the same matrix entry.
The space of integral cocycles is $\ker(A)\cap\mathbb Z^E$.

The checker first verifies that every local height difference agrees with
one signed global cochain $c$, that $Ac=0$, and that
$\gcd\{c_e:e\in E\}=1$. It then verifies a nonzero minor of size $E-1$.
For this example $E=10$ and the supplied $9\times9$ minor has determinant
$1$. Thus $\operatorname{rank} A\ge9$, while the nonzero kernel vector gives
$\operatorname{rank} A\le9$. The rational kernel is exactly $\mathbb Q c$.
If $q c$ is integral, Bezout's identity for the coordinates of $c$ makes
$q$ integral. Hence
\[
 \ker(A)\cap\mathbb Z^{10}=\mathbb Zc.
\]
This avoids trusting the cocycle producer's elimination or a supplied
homology rank. The certificate checker independently reconstructs $A$ and
evaluates the selected minor by fraction-free elimination.

There is only one global vertex, so every vertex coboundary is zero.
Consequently the only primitive integral cocycles are $c$ and $-c$;
there is no nontrivial vertex gauge to optimize. Heights realizing either
cochain differ from the displayed heights only by a constant on each
tetrahedron and possibly a common sign. Constants leave the normal level
gaps unchanged, and negation reverses their order without changing the
normal coordinates. Equivalently, their six local edge weights are
$|c_e|$, which uniquely determine an admissible local normal vector.
The checker verifies these weights against the supplied $x$.

It follows that \emph{every primitive coherent-height surface on this
triangulation is normally isotopic to $x$}. None is a disc, an annulus, or a
planar surface suitable for the current boundary-capping witness. Allowing
nonprimitive integral cocycles does not help here: $kc$ gives the coordinate
vector $|k|x$, represented by $|k|$ disjoint parallel copies of the two-sided
surface $x$, and zero gives the empty vector. This conclusion concerns the
coherent construction, not arbitrary embedded normal surfaces.

\subsection{Verification cost, scope and retained evidence}

There are $O(N)$ global edges and faces. The current family checker builds
a dense $O(N^2)$ matrix and evaluates its minor in $O(N^3)$ integer
arithmetic operations. Each original row has absolute sum at most three;
Hadamard's inequality bounds the bit lengths of its minors by $O(N)$.
Together with reading and checking the supplied binary heights and normal
coordinates, this gives polynomial bit complexity in the encoded input.
This is a certificate for a restricted family on one supplied triangulation,
not a certificate of knottedness and not a candidate-discovery algorithm.
Its return value makes that scope explicit.

The retained research driver reproduces the complete example without Regina,
including the six moves, rank minor, topology proof and disc-count proof.
Its replay mode uses the independent checkers and no search. An optional
Regina audit confirms both final surfaces and checks 240 additional randomly
selected $2$--$3$ moves, arranged as twelve length-20 paths starting from
layered solid tori of sizes two, three and eight and a canonical one-crossing
diagram exterior. Every native output is isomorphic to Regina's independent
replacement. The seed and all selected moves and isomorphism signatures are
retained, along with hashes of the native source files.

Focused tests check twenty random relabellings of the entire family,
certificate mutations, malformed and same-tetrahedron move sites,
nonprimitive cochains, extra global vertices, sign changes and independent
tetrahedron offsets of more than 20,000 bits. They also check producer-free
replay, exact work limits, cancellation and input immutability.

the optional Regina cross-check. The evidence files are
\path{data/coherent-obstruction-*}. No whole-recognizer speedup is claimed:
the maintained candidate schedule and default settings are unchanged.
The practical consequence is to direct completeness work toward changes of
triangulation, surface compression or a broader normal-vector family, rather
than assuming that a better potential optimizer alone will suffice.
