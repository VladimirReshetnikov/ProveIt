\section{Compressed common-basis certificates for primitive annuli}
\label{sec:signed-primitive-annuli}

The next 12 October continuation integrates the signed primitive-annulus
method of report~114.  This is a different terminal from
Section~\ref{sec:euler-group-terminal}: it starts with a geometrically
certified handlebody core and interprets all remaining annular interfaces
on that core.  It transports their actual signed words through the core
cancellations in a shared grammar, then finds one basis change for the
whole family.  Small generator-support blocks admit a complete polynomial
algebraic test.  The maintained whole-cut portfolio uses this test after
its earlier geometric and Euler/group methods refuse.

The objective is to avoid expanding exponentially long transported curves
or searching elementary basis moves one at a time.  The result is an
unmarked handlebody census.  Neither the algebraic basis certificate nor
the existence of dual disks provides the marked successor geometry needed
for a complete hierarchy.  The general quasi-polynomial goal remains open.

\subsection{The geometric hypothesis: one primitive family}

Let $H$ be an orientable handlebody, and let $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_q$ be
pairwise disjoint embedded simple closed curves in its boundary.  Their
conjugacy classes are a \emph{conjugate primitive family} if one
automorphism $\Phi$ of $\pi_1(H)=F_r$ takes them to conjugates of distinct
basis letters, with signs.  Different curves may have different
conjugators; they must share the same automorphism.

\begin{proposition}[Primitive family relative to disk interfaces]
\label{signed-annuli:bridge}
The classes form a conjugate primitive family if and only if there are
pairwise disjoint properly embedded disks $D_1,\ldots,D_q$ in $H$ with
$|\partial D_i\cap\beta_j|=\delta_{ij}$.  The disks can be chosen to avoid
any prescribed finite collection of disjoint disk attachment regions in
$\partial H$ disjoint from the curves.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any subset $J$ of the curves, attach two-handles to $H$ along those
curves.  The result is connected, compact and orientable with nonempty
boundary.  Its fundamental group is the quotient by their normal
closures, namely $F_{r-|J|}$, since one common basis witness identifies
all the classes.  Each two-handle increases Euler characteristic by one,
so its Euler characteristic is $1-r+|J|$.  Proposition~\ref{euler-group:criterion}
therefore says it is a handlebody.  This holds for every subset, so
Gordon's primitive-set theorem supplies simultaneous dual disks
\cite{signed-gordon}.  The proof quantifies over subsets; the algorithm
never enumerates them.  The single common basis certificate establishes
the group statement for all of them at once.

Conversely, in the graph dual to cutting along the given dual disks,
$\beta_i$ traverses its distinguished edge exactly once and no other
edge.  That edge is a loop.  The dual graph is connected and all edges
are loops, so its complement is connected.  Extend these disks to a
complete meridian system.  In the resulting basis each curve contains
its distinguished generator once and every other distinguished generator
zero times; all other letters belong to the complementary generators.
Simultaneous Nielsen changes fixing those complementary generators send
the curves to distinct basis classes.

Finally isotope the entire disjoint meridian-boundary family off the
prescribed disk regions.  Successive outermost arcs can be pushed to
boundary collars disjoint from the $\beta_i$.  No essential meridian
boundary lies entirely inside a disk region.  Isotopy extension carries
the boundary isotopy to the disks.  Thin annular neighborhoods of the
curves then meet their dual disks in exactly one spanning arc each.
\end{proof}

The argument includes \emph{every} pending annulus in a core component.
It does not claim that the dual disks can also avoid omitted annular
interfaces.  Testing curves separately is insufficient: two primitive
curves can compete for the same basis generator.  Likewise, unimodular
exponent-sum data are necessary but do not establish a common primitive
family.  The actual compressed words must pass the common automorphism
test below.

Attaching all these primitive annuli to the remaining bordered bundle
pieces gives the previously established disk-gluing census.  In each
core component, the number of incident annuli is subtracted from its
handlebody genus.  Distinct target generators ensure this number does
not exceed the core rank.  The graph of disk attachments then adds its
cycle ranks, and unported bundle profiles retain their binary
multiplicities.  The original source-cut Euler identity remains a
consistency check.  The geometric primitive-family theorem supplies the
topological conclusion.

\subsection{Authenticating signs and transporting passive curves}

The source compiler receives the authenticated parallelity charts,
ordered boundary words, disk-bundle absorption and a complete geometric
core proof.  It reads signs from oriented source-face flags.  An edge
with equal endpoints still has distinct source and target face flags,
so endpoint order alone would lose essential information.  Original
core two-handles and absorbed disk-bundle circles give active signed
relators.  Every non-disk annular circle gives a passive signed word.
Source forests erase their crossings; no free cancellation is used to
justify a geometric pivot.

For a column-private geometric cancellation, its pivot generator occurs
once in its donor row and zero times in all other remaining real rows,
counting unsigned passages.  The source proofs authenticate that stronger
geometric condition.  Write the signed donor cyclically as
$x^\epsilon u$.  In the quotient its pivot satisfies $x=u^{-\epsilon}$.
Since an earlier pivot cannot appear in any later surviving row, the
dependencies are triangular.  Process the cancellation list backwards:
surviving generators are letters, and each eliminated generator becomes
a shared concatenation of images already constructed.  Build all passive
words from these same images.  For the geometric row-leaf method the
successively eliminated generators map to the identity; the compiler
checks that every other letter in the donor was eliminated earlier.

\begin{proposition}[Shared signed transport]
If the original active and passive words have total length $L$, the
component has $r$ generators, the complete geometric schedule has $p$
cancellations, and there are $q$ passive roots, their quotient words have
a shared straight-line representation with $O(L+r+p+q)$ productions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each donor contributes concatenations for its literal letters only once.
References to generator images reuse existing nodes rather than copy
expanded words.  Inverse images are cached and share the reversed
productions.  Each passive word contributes its literal concatenations
once.  Surviving letters, pivot entries and output roots supply the
remaining linear terms.  No represented word length enters the count.
\end{proof}

This is a grammar-size statement, not a claim of linear bit time.
Lengths can be exponential in the number of productions, and storing and
operating on their binary values has an additional polynomial cost.
The established deterministic compressed free and cyclic reduction
kernel from Section~\ref{sec:compressed-words} is used when reduction is
needed.  The source compiler itself preserves signs and cyclic order.

A certified two/three-handle reduction is compatible with this adapter:
it retains every zero/one-handle, all ports, and the actual cycles of
retained two-handles.  After independently replaying that geometric
reduction, the same signed source construction applies.  An abstract
Euler/group handlebody certificate is not substituted for the required
geometric core cancellation proof.

\subsection{A supplied common automorphism stays compressed}

Allowed basis moves are inversion, interchange, and a powered Nielsen
move $x_i\mapsto x_j^k x_i$ or $x_i x_j^k$, with $i\ne j$ and nonzero
binary integer $k$.  Let $B$ count move records and their exponent bit
lengths, and let $S$ be the source grammar size.

Process the move list backwards, maintaining the images under the suffix
already processed.  An inversion uses the inverse of one suffix image;
a swap exchanges two references; a multiplication concatenates one
suffix image with a binary power of another.  Binary powering creates
$O(\log(|k|+1))$ productions.  Once the suffix images are complete, map
the original grammar once, preserving its shared productions.  Thus all
transformed roots together need $O(S+r+B)$ grammar productions before
free reduction.  Their length bit sizes are polynomial in $S+B+r$.

The checker independently constructs suffix layers, uses a different
binary powering traversal and evaluates the source DAG separately.
Its layer dictionaries can cost $O(rm)$ references for $m$ moves; that
is still polynomial, and is not described as linear storage.  Producer
and checker share the existing exact compressed-word kernel, not the
Euclidean selector or the source compiler.  An independent literal
forward-substitution audit tests this shared-kernel boundary.

\subsection{Complete polynomial discovery for rank-two support blocks}

\begin{proposition}[A common lift for two conjugacy classes]
Let $u,v\in F(a,b)$ have exponent-sum columns forming a matrix $A$.
They are a conjugate primitive pair if and only if $\det A=\pm1$ and,
for any automorphism $\Phi$ with abelianization $A^{-1}$, the cyclically
reduced words $\Phi(u)$ and $\Phi(v)$ are respectively $a$ and $b$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A primitive pair admits a common witness, which can be composed with a
signed permutation so that its ordered target classes are $[a],[b]$.
The witness has abelianization $A^{-1}$.  Since
$\operatorname{Out}(F_2)\cong\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, any two
such lifts differ by an inner automorphism, hence agree on conjugacy
classes.  Conversely the displayed tests themselves give a witness.
\end{proof}

Exponent sums are computed by exact DAG arithmetic.  Euclidean row
operations factor the inverse unimodular matrix into swaps, inversions
and integer shears of polynomial encoded length.  These lift to powered
Nielsen moves, whose common action is evaluated by the preceding shared
construction.  Compressed cyclic reduction completes the test.
For a single class, use coprime exponent sums, a Euclidean lift taking
that vector to $(1,0)$, and the final one-letter test.  The deterministic
polynomial rank-two compressed primitivity algorithm and the distinction
from general fixed-rank nondeterministic certification are established
in Kapovich's work \cite{signed-kapovich}.  The simultaneous pair test
above is its elementary common-lift consequence, with the order and
signs fixed explicitly.

The choice of a fast rank-two routine does not by itself justify ignoring
other generators.  The following inheritance observation does.

\begin{lemma}[Inheritance by containing subgroups]
If a tuple in a subgroup $A\leq F$ is conjugate primitive in $F$, it is
conjugate primitive in $A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose an ambient basis with distinct letters $z_i$ such that each tuple
entry is $h_i z_i^{\epsilon_i}h_i^{-1}$.  In the covering graph of the
basis rose corresponding to $A$, the lift of $h_i$ ends at a vertex
where the $z_i$ edge is a loop, by uniqueness of path lifting.  These
loop edges are distinct.  A spanning tree contains none of them, so the
associated free basis of $A$ contains the corresponding tree-based
loops.  The original entries are their conjugates by the loops
$h_i t_i^{-1}$ in $A$, where $t_i$ is the tree path to that vertex.
\end{proof}

Cyclically reduce every input word and join generators that appear in
one support.  If every resulting support component contains at most two
generators, run the exact single-class or pair test in each block.
Rank-one blocks allow only one class of exponent $\pm1$, rank-two blocks
allow at most two classes, and trivial classes fail.  Successful block
automorphisms extend by the identity and commute.  Conversely the
inheritance lemma shows that a primitive family in the ambient group
must be primitive in each support block.  This proves a complete
polynomial algebraic decision method on the stated restricted class,
uniformly in the total rank.  The support restriction is measured in the
surviving basis supplied by the geometric proof; no search over all such
bases is claimed.

Larger support blocks are a method refusal.  The verifier can still
accept a supplied common automorphism in arbitrary rank.  Its ability
to replay such a certificate is not a completeness theorem for the
producer.  The maintained finite node and work allowances can also
cause refusal within the restricted class; the polynomial theorem
concerns sufficient resource allowances, not success at a fixed ceiling.
A repeated strict Whitehead descent is not an adequate replacement:
compressed input can represent words requiring exponentially many
individual decreasing elementary moves.

\subsection{Maintained integration and resource contracts}

The default \path{annulus_method='signed_rank_two'} adds a late phase.
The existing source, maximum-passage and deferred forest attempts, then
the Euler/group terminal, retain priority.  Earlier positive packets
are returned without alteration.  A refusing geometric attempt retains
its authenticated candidate for the late phase.  On the unreduced path,
the augmented core and its successful certificate are reused directly.
On the reduced path, the cached top reduction and prepared core are
reused; disk absorption and an augmented core proof are computed only
if needed.  Deferred forest selection is also passed to that augmented
core computation.

All attempts share the same source and lazy boundary packet.  The signed
phase starts no new orbit query.  The \path{counts} option disables the
new phase.  At most three cached candidates arise from the adaptive
forest portfolio.  Each candidate uses one word arena for all its
components, with the existing limits of one million nodes and ten
million work units.  These are per-candidate word-kernel limits; the
orbit cycle allowance remains shared across the whole query.
Resource exhaustion means no conclusion.  No partial primitive family
is used to certify a cut.

The two source-bound signed certificate schemas distinguish the original
core and the independently replayed top-reduced core.  Replay authenticates
source, boundary, reduction if present, disk absorption, geometric core,
signed source words, the shared quotient and each component's common
basis certificate.  The whole-cut operation cap counts the recorded
orbit operations plus powered Nielsen move records; it is not a count
of machine instructions or exponent magnitudes.

The integration fixes a delivered callback issue: a callback's
\path{ValueError}, \path{TypeError}, arithmetic exception or
\path{CompressedLimit} must propagate, even when an internal verifier
would ordinarily catch that exception class as malformed input or a
resource refusal.  Explicit callback guards distinguish the cases.
The algebra checker restores the arena's original callback in a
\path{finally} block.  Falsey callback objects are used normally.
