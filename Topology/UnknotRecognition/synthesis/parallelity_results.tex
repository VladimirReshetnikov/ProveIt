The completed audit covers 1,755 finite cases: 1,635 pilot surfaces,
48 compatible mixed surfaces, 36 analytic multiples and 36 relabellings.
It checks 32,437 literal base polygons and 119,490 vertex links. All native
profiles agree with the finite expansion, and all source certificates replay
with producer entry points disabled. The artifact retains 106 complete
source proofs and hashes every tested finite proof. Seven binary cases
include multipliers $2^{128}$ and $2^{500}$ on one, four and sixteen tetrahedra and
$2^{16384}$ copies on one tetrahedron. Their core sizes are respectively
7, 43 and 187 handles, independent of scaling; the corresponding polygon
family counts are 5, 29 and 125. The audit completed in 209.58 seconds on
this machine. These measurements validate the tested cases and the binary
representation; they are not a worst-case recognition theorem.

The full Regina-enabled regression suite passes all 1,611 tests with no
skips, including twelve new selector and parallelity tests.

The complete supplied-surface task was timed in five shuffled paired rounds
after warmup. Both arms validate the source, construct bundle and core charts,
classify the base surfaces and serialize the result. The native arm also
performs independent source and orbit-certificate replay; the literal arm
reconstructs geometry from raw-cell incidences and explicitly checks all
polygon vertex links. Their representations and proof obligations differ.
Input preparation outside these tasks and garbage collection are excluded.

Binary compression wins for large multiplicity: the measured ratio at
4,096 copies is 57.22. The one-tetrahedron native times from 16 to 4,096
copies remain near 11 milliseconds. Small examples pay substantial orbit
and proof overhead: the four-tetrahedron primitive case is about 16.7 times
slower than expansion, and the sixteen-tetrahedron primitive case about
4.95 times slower. Identical literal-control ratios range from 0.983 to
1.020. These are supplied-surface operator measurements; they establish
neither an end-to-end recognizer speedup nor the desired general bound.
