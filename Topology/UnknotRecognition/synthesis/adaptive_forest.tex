\section{Adaptive factor scheduling and checked pre-cube reduction}
\label{sec:adaptive-forest}

The complete exceptional-factor fallback exposes two avoidable costs.
A small wider braid may simplify to three strands, yet a direct cube pays
exponential cost before using that structure. A nontrivial connected-sum
factor may already have an inexpensive proof, yet an earlier expensive factor
can consume the shared allowance first. We address both costs while retaining
the same complete fallback and exceptional-parameter bound.

\subsection{Prioritize decisions before exponential work}

The producer validates the original grammar, computes exact singleton cuts,
and prepares each projected leaf and its finite summaries. It then assigns
three priority classes. The first contains factors with at most two strands
and factors already excluded by the exponent obstruction $|e|>m-1$.
The second contains the remaining three-braid factors, which have a complete
compressed polynomial algorithm. The third contains wider residual factors.
Within a class, shorter projected grammars precede longer ones; original
factor index breaks ties. Preparing these projections remains polynomial in
the input size and does not expand any word.

This is a deterministic structural policy, not a timing threshold fitted to
the benchmark. It promotes cases whose available decision method is cheap
and complete. It does not promise to minimize running time on every input:
a particularly simple wider factor might finish before an awkward compressed
three-braid. All attempted operations still share the host's resource budget.
The returned \texttt{factor\_order} is execution metadata, not an extra
mathematical assertion accepted by the verifier.

As soon as a factor is proved nontrivial, the producer stops. It need not
finish unvisited factors or retain proofs for earlier trivial factors.
This changes bounded behavior as well as runtime: an exact negative result
can now survive a zero cube-generator allowance when a finite obstruction
appears later in strand order. Without priority, the first wider factor
could exhaust that allowance and leave the whole input inconclusive.

An unknot verdict still requires every factor to be proved trivial. Complete
split proofs retain all factor records in their original strand order, even
when discovery visits them in a different order. An unsupported factor may
remain inconclusive while later factors are examined; any subsequent exact
negative result is sufficient to decide the whole sum.

\subsection{A proof for one nontrivial summand}

The new forest format is version three. Its \texttt{knotted-factor} phase
contains the original input, the exact complete singleton-cut list, a factor
index and precisely one leaf record. The verifier recomputes the source
summaries and cut list, verifies that the original closure is a knot, and
checks that the index names one of the resulting consecutive strand intervals.
It reconstructs that interval's projected grammar and checks its metadata and
child proof. It accepts this phase only when the child proves
\texttt{KNOTTED} and the parent asserts \texttt{KNOTTED}.

Soundness uses the connected-sum decomposition from
Section~\ref{sec:compressed-braid}: an unknot has only trivial knot summands.
Thus one nontrivial summand disproves unknotness of the original source.
The unvisited factors require no certificate. The original one-component
check matters, since the connected-sum-of-knots argument is not a statement
about arbitrary links. A single positive factor cannot prove the whole input
trivial; the verifier explicitly rejects that substitution. It also rejects
an index rebound to a different interval, Boolean index aliases, an omitted
leaf in a complete split proof, and downgrades of the selected-factor phase
to an older version.

This selective proof can be much smaller than the former complete forest
proof. It still includes the whole source and exact cut data, plus the
selected factor's projection and certificate. Preparation currently constructs
all projections before scheduling, so early termination saves decision and
replay work, not that polynomial preparation cost. Versions one and two remain
accepted with their original complete-list semantics.

\subsection{A checked reduction before building a cube}

A wider factor that passes its expanded-length preflight is converted to an
explicit word under the existing cap. Before building its reduced Khovanov
cube, the producer invokes the maintained singleton endpoint Markov descent
from Section~\ref{sec:linear-braid-descent}. This routine freely and cyclically
cancels adjacent inverse letters, then deletes singleton endpoint generators
by a cyclic rotation and destabilization, stopping on reaching three strands
or when no endpoint move applies. Every operation preserves the isotopy type
of the closure and never increases word length or strand count.

If both the final word and strand count are unchanged, the original direct
cube certificate is used. No witness of an unsuccessful simplification search
is required. If there is progress, an \texttt{exceptional-reduction-v1} child
contains the checked Markov trace followed by a terminal proof. Three-strand
residuals use the compressed quotient recognizer on a deterministically built
balanced grammar for the explicit residual word. Wider residuals still use
the complete cube, now on the shorter word. Reaching three strands, rather
than guessing minimal braid index, is sufficient for the complete terminal
criterion. Failure to reach three strands remains eligible for homology.

Replay starts from the original factor's projected grammar, expands it under
the same cap, and invokes the independent maintained endpoint verifier on the
supplied trace. It reconstructs the actual resulting strand count and word.
The terminal grammar is rebuilt from that word, then the corresponding
three-braid or cube proof is checked against it. The declared final word is
never used as a substitute for replay. Version three is required for this
nested reduction proof; an older forest cannot silently acquire new terminal
semantics.

The endpoint verifier checks recorded choices without searching for an
endpoint. Its legacy version-one path shares deterministic free/cyclic
cancellation with discovery, while its version-two path replays local moves
on original positions. That existing trusted boundary is retained; we do not
claim two independent implementations of every simplification primitive.
Both maintained Markov schemas contain no Boolean fields, so the adapter
rejects Boolean values throughout the nested witness before legacy replay.
This prevents Python integer equality from accepting an aliased signed
generator or metadata field. External exceptions, including a user callback's
\texttt{ValueError} subtype, propagate without reclassification as a knot
verdict.

\subsection{Resources and asymptotic accounting}

The public producer enables this reduction by default. The control
\path{use_fallback_reduction=False}, or CLI flag
\texttt{--no-fallback-reduction}, provides an ablation of only the new
pre-cube stage. It retains factor priority and selective negative proofs.
Verification accepts every supported proof independently of the producer's
choice of this control. Existing crossing preflight, input and proof bytes,
shared work, time, string nodes and cumulative cube-generator allowances
remain in force. A solved reduced three-braid consumes no cube generators,
but its string production and replay still consume the shared string budget.

Let $f$ be the number of factors and $g$ the input bit size. The new ordering
adds $O(f\log(f+1))$ comparisons after the existing polynomial projections.
For an admitted wider factor, explicit endpoint descent costs $O(n+m)$ word
operations on this one-component domain. The residual has no more letters or
strands than its source. Constructing a balanced residual grammar and applying
the complete three-braid procedure therefore adds polynomial work in that
explicit factor size; a remaining cube retains the previous exponential upper
bound. Consequently the implemented uncapped bound remains
\[
 \operatorname{poly}(g)+\sum_{i\in I}2^{O(\kappa_i)},
\]
where $I$ and $\kappa_i$ refer to the exceptional factors \emph{before} this
new elementary reduction. We do not replace them by residual minority counts
without proving a new singleton bound for those residuals. Early negative
termination can remove summands from the actual work, but a worst-case unknot
still requires all factors. No new general quasi-polynomial claim follows.

\subsection{Validation and measurements}

Twelve new tests exercise a reducible wider unknot with zero cube allowance,
a stalled nontrivial four-braid, and a shorter residual that still needs the
cube. They verify that later finite and compressed negative factors run before
an earlier wider factor; that selective proofs cannot prove unknotness or be
rebound to another interval; and that all positive factors remain necessary.
They mutate reduction output, terminal source grammars and versions, replay
with reduction/rank discovery disabled, and exercise exact shared-work
boundaries and external cancellation. The earlier direct-cube tests explicitly
disable the new reduction, preserving coverage of the fallback rather than
accidentally testing only a simplification shortcut.

All 78 focused tests pass, followed by all 909 maintained tests with Regina in
212.426 seconds. The paired experiments load the previous maintained package
from commit \texttt{666a62f6a} under a separate package name, sharing the
unchanged native string kernel. Baseline module hashes are retained, rather
than approximating the old behavior by disabling one new option.

A 160-case cross-version audit reuses the preceding section's independently
checked signed braid inputs. Old and new public verdicts agree in all cases:
108 unknots and 52 nontrivial knots. The new verifier accepts every old proof
and every newly produced proof. Public cube use falls from 17 to 12 cases.
The audit also fixes the operational difference at a zero generator allowance:
a wider trivial factor followed by a two-strand trefoil was previously
inconclusive and now has a checked nontriviality proof for the later factor.
The 5.581-second audit records source hashes and the prior input audit's hash
in \path{data/adaptive-forest-audit.json}. An initial baseline-loader path
failure is retained separately; it occurred before any algorithm comparison.

The performance experiment uses seed 261008494, one shuffled warmup and five
measured rounds on nine inputs. It compares the actual previous maintained
host, an identical old control, the current host, an identical current control,
and the current host with only elementary fallback reduction disabled.
Every arm includes its normal proof production and independent replay.
The last arm retains priority and selective negative proofs, separating their
benefit from pre-cube simplification. All signed inputs come from the checked
fixture constructors, and all arms must agree on the verdict. Source hashes,
raw samples, execution orders, proof sizes and resource counts are retained in
\path{../fast/results/adaptive_forest_20261008.json}.

The reducible nine-crossing four-braid improves from a 265.060 ms median to
2.039 ms, with median paired ratio 128.769. Its cumulative cube-generator
count falls from 4,638 to zero, and its proof shrinks from 68,785 to 1,395
canonical bytes. Disabling reduction restores the expensive cube. For the
11-crossing word whose shorter residual remains four-stranded, the median
improves from 1,203.179 to 138.640 ms, with paired ratio 8.889; generator
reservations fall from 28,470 to 3,774. This case checks that useful progress
need not reach a polynomial terminal to reduce complete-fallback cost.

When the nine-crossing wider unknot precedes a two-strand trefoil, priority
and the selected-factor proof reduce 163.016 ms to 0.696 ms, with paired ratio
234.638. The proof shrinks from 69,095 to 656 bytes. The no-reduction arm is
similarly fast, isolating scheduling and selective replay as the mechanism.
A later compressed nontrivial three-braid gives a paired ratio of 9.539 by
the same mechanism. The original shared budget would have spent work on the
irrelevant earlier cube in both cases.

For a positive mixed forest containing a $2^{512}$ conjugation sleeve, all
factors remain necessary, but the wider factor no longer needs a cube.
The median falls from 238.687 to 79.079 ms, with paired ratio 2.980.
A smaller positive sum improves by paired ratio 35.050. On the stalled
four-braid, the current 135.774 ms median is close to the old 134.800 ms;
its complete cube remains necessary. The positive all-three-braid forest
and direct three-braid control likewise show no meaningful gain. Their
small differences are retained, not used to claim an improvement.

Identical-arm paired ratios range from 0.961 to 1.077. The short reducible
case has the widest control variation, so the large ratios should be read
as approximate family-specific improvements, not exact machine-independent
constants. The experiment has 225 measured calls and 45 warmup calls.
It does not measure arbitrary-diagram recognition or bound the general
exceptional minority parameter. The next opportunities include avoiding
unneeded projection preparation on negative forests and integrating the
pending sparse two-meridian seed search from report 47; neither is included
in these timings.
