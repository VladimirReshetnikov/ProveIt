\section{Direct geometric first descent without cochain bookkeeping}
\label{sec:geometric-descent-crushing}

The fixed one-upward policy of
Section~\ref{sec:pachner-descent-crushing} uses an identically zero
cochain to reuse a general transported-surface search.  Its acceptance
claim is geometric: the endpoint triangulates the same compact manifold
with one fewer tetrahedron.  The new source adapter
\path{reduce_geometric_descent_once} implements that fixed geometric
search directly.  It retains independent elementary move verification
and removes cochain transport, cover completion, incarnation interning
and global marked-endpoint caches from this discovery path.

The published cochain producer and its packet language remain available.
The new method has a distinct proof schema because its evidence consists
of elementary relative-boundary replacements, not a transported cochain
and a common original cover.  The methods need not choose the same
labelled endpoint.  A shorter discovery implementation by itself gives
no measured speed claim or bound on subsequent normal-surface search.

\subsection{Exhausting the complete fixed family directly}

Let the initial finite source contain $t$ tetrahedra.  Write $U$ for an
expansion $2\to3$ and $D$ for a contraction $3\to2$.  An intermediate
source reached by $u$ expansions and $d$ contractions has $t+u-d$
tetrahedra.  If at most one expansion is allowed in total, every proper
prefix of a first strict descent satisfies
\[
                              d\le u\le1.
\]
Hence its complete word is $D$ or $UDD$.  This elementary count proves
that an exhaustive search over those two words is sufficient; no
connected-cover locality theorem is needed for this specialization.

The implemented search first lists all candidate contractions of the
initial source.  If one independently verifies, it returns that $D$.
Otherwise it visits every candidate expansion, every candidate first
contraction of that expanded source, and every candidate final
contraction of the resulting $UD$ source.  It stops at the first fully
verified smaller endpoint.  Inverse moves are not skipped.  The search
is depth first and discards a completed branch before advancing to the
next; it retains neither all attempted sources nor an attempt log.

Keeping every first contraction after each expansion is necessary.
Two different $D$ choices can give different $UD$ sources, only one of
which admits a final contraction.  Thus a policy that takes just the
first $D$, fails to find another $D$, and proceeds to the next $U$
would not exhaust the claimed family.  Once a final $D$ verifies,
however, any such final contraction is a sufficient witness.

The upward order is increasing local $(\text{tetrahedron},\text{face})$
among paired faces joining two distinct tetrahedra.  Both reciprocal
orientations are retained.  This redundant constant factor avoids
depending on an isomorphism quotient to justify the enumerated choices.
Different paired faces between the same two tetrahedra remain distinct
candidates.

For downward moves, source preparation reconstructs global edge classes.
An eligible class is interior and has three local incidences in three
distinct tetrahedra.  Each is represented by its least local
$(\text{tetrahedron},a,b)$ with $a<b$, and representatives are tried
in increasing order.  Formal apex and belt labels are reconstructed
by the existing $3\to2$ constructor.  A legal formal three-tetrahedron
bipyramid occurs among these candidates; exchanging apex labels or
permuting the three belt labels changes its presentation, not the
relative-boundary replacement it represents.

Eligibility is only a discovery filter.  Every constructed successor
must pass the independent elementary verifier before becoming a search
prefix.  Expected local constructor rejection advances the scan;
failure of independent replay for a returned construction raises an
error rather than contributing to an exhaustion assertion.  The
uncapped public adapter returns \path{REDUCED} or \path{UNCHANGED}.
There is no node, upward, work or cover-limit option that could truncate
the family while returning unchanged.  Caller cancellation propagates.
An unchanged result excludes only the maintained formal $D/UDD$ family,
not more general simplification or an essential normal disc.

\subsection{A purely geometric source packet}

The packet has exactly four fields: \path{schema},
\path{source_sha256}, \path{steps} and \path{successor}.
Its schema is \path{normal-geometric-descent-v1}.  The source digest
is SHA-256 of the complete labelled input in canonical JSON with sorted
keys and compact separators.  Each step has exactly
\path{triangulation} and \path{certificate}.  These hold the full
labelled intermediate source and its elementary proof, using schema
\path{pachner-23-v1} or \path{pachner-32-v1}.  There are no height, normal
coordinate, cover or disc fields in this outer proof language.

The independent entry point \path{verify_normal_geometric_descent}
receives the source explicitly and reconstructs the sequence from it.
It accepts only the schema words
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{Elementary word} & \text{Successive sizes}\\
 \hline
 D   & t-1\\
 UDD & t+1,\ t,\ t-1.
 \end{array}
\]
The final source must be nonempty.  The checker enforces literal field
and index types, replays every elementary proof from its actual
predecessor, and compares the declared successor with the replayed
endpoint by type-sensitive equality.  A Boolean or floating-point
replacement for an integer label cannot supply an equal source.

The elementary checkers independently validate each complete finite
connected orientable compact source with one torus boundary.  They
compare every survivor record, every formal internal facet pairing,
and all six formal boundary attachment maps.  This includes
selfattachments between boundary facets of the chosen region.
The common bipyramid homeomorphism fixes its formal boundary and
therefore descends through these full quotient relations, as proved
in Proposition~\ref{prop:strict-pachner32-relative}.  Composing one or
three such maps proves that the endpoint is homeomorphic to the
supplied source relative to the corresponding ambient boundary.
No embedded image of the closed formal ball is assumed.

All vertices of the two formal bipyramid triangulations lie on their
common boundary.  Thus each elementary move preserves ambient vertex
equivalence classes.  The source interface permits multiple vertices
and preserves a one-vertex source when given one.  It requires no
cohomology generator, peripheral slope or normal-cone transport.
Diagram provenance, when needed for a recognition verdict, is supplied
by the outer authenticated chain.

The independent checker imports no discovery producer.  Both the
producer and checker guard caller callbacks with private
\path{BaseException} carriers.  In particular a caller-raised
\path{NormalOrbitError} must not be caught as malformed input inside
an elementary verifier, and a caller-raised search-limit exception
must not become ordinary geometric exhaustion.  The original exception
object and traceback are preserved.  A discovered endpoint is exposed
only after the complete source-bound packet passes independent replay.

\subsection{Explicit time, storage and certificate bounds}

Let $L=\log(t+2)$.  A live source has at most $t+1$ tetrahedra.
There are at most $4(t+1)$ paired local-face ports, including both
orientations, and $O(t)$ eligible edge representatives among its
$6(t+1)$ local edge incidences.  At most $O(t)$ expansions and
$O(t^2)$ $UD$ prefixes are visited.  Their final contraction scans
therefore test at most $O(t^3)$ candidates altogether.  This count
allows unsuccessful formal-region attempts and repeated geometries;
it assumes no cache hit or commuting-trace reduction.

\begin{proposition}[Streaming geometric first-descent cost]
\label{prop:geometric-descent-crushing-cost}
For a validated source with $t$ tetrahedra, one uncapped direct search
has the conservative bit bounds
\[
 \text{discovery time }O(t^5L^2),\qquad
 \text{working space }O(tL).
\]
An accepted packet has $O(tL)$ bits and independent replay costs
$O(t^2L^2)$.  Arbitrary work performed by a caller callback is charged
separately.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the local bound
\[
                         P(s)=O(s^2\log^2(s+2))
\]
from Section~\ref{sec:strict-pachner32} for source preparation,
candidate construction and independent relative-boundary replay.
It includes dense source copying and relabelling, sparse matching-row
construction performed by the validator, complete finite-manifold
checks, source hashing and exact comparisons.  All indices and
incidence counts have logarithmic bit length.  Even pessimistic linear
scans for local associative lookup fit this bound.  The actual
producer and elementary verifier may prepare the same source more
than once; the fixed number of such preparations per candidate is
included in $P$.

There are $O(t^3)$ candidate attempts.  Preparing and sorting the
$O(t)$ candidates at each of the $O(t^2)$ possible nonterminal
prefixes is also covered by this bound.  Thus total discovery is
$O(t^3P(t+1))=O(t^5L^2)$.  Final source hashing, packet construction
and a second complete replay of its at most three elementary moves
fit the same bound.  There is no global endpoint, incarnation or
cover dictionary whose accumulated size needs an additional lookup
factor.

For storage, inspect the local representations rather than bounding
space by the running time.  Complete source preparation retains
$O(s)$ face, vertex, edge, adjacency, union-find, link and boundary
records.  It creates $O(s)$ sparse matching rows, each with at most
four entries, and performs no dense elimination.  The constructor and
elementary checker retain $O(s)$ source and survivor records and
constant-size formal-region dictionaries.  Hence each local call uses
$O(s\log(s+2))$ bits.  The call stack has fixed depth; candidate lists
at its finitely many levels have $O(t)$ local ports.  A fixed number
of full sources and elementary proofs remain live while exploring a
branch.  Counters have $O(L)$ bits since their values are polynomial
in $t$.  Old source preparations, completed branches and unused
attempt records are not accumulated.  This proves $O(tL)$ working
storage for the implemented search.

A packet has one or three full intermediate sources plus its final
successor, each encoded in $O(tL)$ bits.  Its local proofs contain
only a constant number of source indices and formal labels.  The
whole packet therefore has $O(tL)$ bits, even when serialization
repeats its last source.  Its independent verifier makes constantly
many local replays and exact comparisons, costing $O(P(t+1))$.
\end{proof}

Repeatedly accepting this strict macro permits fewer than $t$
successes, with at most $3(t-1)$ elementary moves in their proofs.
Including terminal unsuccessful scans gives conservative discovery
cost $O(t^6L^2)$, peak search working space $O(tL)$, and retained
accepted-chain size $O(t^2L)$.  Replaying that whole accepted chain
costs $O(t^3L^2)$.  The same polynomial overhead applies when other
strictly decreasing preprocessors are interleaved; normal Euler
search remains a separate cost.
Keeping the accepted chain in memory as well as the current search
uses $O(t^2L)$ bits in total.

Compared with the conservative implementation bounds in
Section~\ref{sec:pachner-descent-crushing}, the per-query estimate
changes from $O(t^6L^2)$ to $O(t^5L^2)$, and the repeated-preprocessing
estimate from $O(t^7L^2)$ to $O(t^6L^2)$.  The working-storage estimate
changes from $O(t^3L)$ to $O(tL)$.  These compare proved upper bounds:
they are not lower bounds on the legacy implementation's actual cost,
and they do not imply a measured speed factor on any given input.

The streaming storage bound is a property of the new native producer.
The earlier scratch prototype retained a list of all candidate-attempt
metadata, which could itself occupy $O(t^3L)$ bits.  That diagnostic
list is absent here.  Neither the prototype's elapsed runtime nor a
comparison between its callback counts and legacy counts establishes
a measured benefit for the native integration.

\subsection{Diagnostic units and recognition integration}

The result preserves the common source-adapter statistics.
The size fields are \path{initial_tetrahedra} and \path{remaining_tetrahedra}.
Discovery uses \path{search_nodes} and \path{search_work}; accepted moves
use \path{macros}, \path{upward_moves} and \path{downward_moves}.
The final flag is \path{search_complete}.  A geometric search node is the root or a
verified $U$ or $UD$ prefix; the selected terminal $D$ or $UDD$
endpoint is not counted as another node.  Work counts all invocations
of the caller callback throughout this adapter, including initial
preparation and final independent packet replay.  Additional candidate,
rejection and scan counters are additive diagnostics.  Accepted
upward and downward counts refer only to the returned macro, not to
discarded branches.

These units differ from the legacy cochain engine's popped-frame and
cooperative-checkpoint counters.  They must not be divided to obtain
a computational speed ratio.  Neither is an independent count of
bit operations.  A success has \path{search_complete=False}; a full
unsuccessful enumeration has \path{search_complete=True}.  Neither
flag is a knot verdict.  Interrupted calls propagate without returning
a purported complete work total.

The direct and factored recognition interfaces select the policy with
\path{pachner_descent_method}, whose values are \path{cochain} and
\path{geometric}.  The default remains \path{cochain}; the existing
\path{reduce_pachner_descent} flag still determines whether this
preprocessing stage runs.  When enabled with the geometric method,
the mixed chain uses \path{diagram-crushing-v5} and accepts the new
geometric packet through its independent checker.  Older chain
versions retain their earlier step languages.  This method selection
does not reinterpret the legacy zero-cochain proof.
Version five also replays all earlier reduction kinds, including the
legacy cochain macro.  Choosing a method while the enabling flag is
false does not introduce a new step kind or change the existing chain
schema.

The scheduling priority remains enabled vertex reduction, boundary
unlayering, strict $3\to2$ scanning, and then first-descent search.
One accepted geometric macro uses one shared reduction-budget unit,
including when its proof has three elementary moves.  Accepted
elementary counts are separate.  Returned discovery work is recorded
even if the reduction allowance prevents appending a discovered macro.
Every changed source restarts preprocessing and normal algebra; no
matching matrix, LP dual or conflict cache is carried to a different
labelled source without a separate proof.

Independent elementary homeomorphisms and decreasing tetrahedron
count do not imply decreasing canonical-Euler search difficulty or
$h(T')\le h(T)$.  The new enumeration can select a different labelled
endpoint from the cochain search, and a returned trace need not carry
that older packet's connected-cover witness.  Endpoint-specific
parameter proofs and timing claims therefore require their own exact
source comparisons and fresh complete-call evidence.  The result of
this section is a complete polynomial specialization of the fixed
geometric family; the missing universal quasi-polynomial recognition
bound remains missing.

\subsection{Focused native verification controls}

The eleven focused producer tests pass without Regina.  Their real
trefoil control starts at the authenticated eight-tetrahedron source
and independently verifies a $UDD$ endpoint with seven tetrahedra.
A separate control puts the inverse expansion first among the
contractions of the expanded source.  That inverse gives a valid $UD$
prefix with no final contraction; the test then requires discovery to
continue to a later first contraction and complete a genuine $UDD$
proof.  This checks the branching obligation directly.

Other controls exercise a direct contraction whose region contains all
source tetrahedra and has formal-boundary selfattachments, preservation
of a six-vertex finite source, and complete exhaustion on a one-tetrahedron
source.  They check that no cover, cochain or normal-disc producer is
used, that replay still succeeds with elementary discovery disabled,
and that input data are not mutated.  Independently rejected constructions
raise instead of becoming unchanged results.  Callback controls cover
source preparation, both elementary constructors, both elementary
verifiers, final packet replay and the last checkpoint of exhaustion.
The reported work counter is also checked against actual callback
invocations.  These are finite regression controls for the stated
contracts, not a timing comparison with the cochain policy.

Eight additional tests target the independent geometric verifier.
They replay both a selfattached direct contraction and a multivertex
$UDD$ packet with discovery, cover and solver routines disabled.
They reject malformed literal fields, altered intermediate sources,
and an outer successor that disagrees with an otherwise valid inner
proof.  A valid legacy sequence of shape $DUD$ is rejected because it
does not satisfy this packet's first-descent contract.  Callback
exception identity is checked within source validation, boundary-map
verification and final successor comparison.

Nine integration tests exercise genuine diagram recognition and the
factored interface.  They check exact equality between the default
legacy result and explicit \path{cochain} selection, replay of an old
macro in a version-five chain, and rejection of the new geometric
packet by versions one through four.  Genuine factored controls check
that a three-move macro consumes one shared reduction-budget unit and
that a completed negative child has a replayable composed proof.
A separate scheduling harness stubs only the diagram-to-source premise
to isolate macro, unlayering and terminal-crushing budget boundaries;
its artificial diagram authentication is not evidence for a genuine
diagram verdict.  Denied acceptance retains the completed discovery
diagnostics, and independently rejected packets fail closed.

The complete regression run passes 364 tests across 41 modules with
Regina available and no skipped tests.  This includes the eleven
producer, eight verifier and nine integration controls above, together
with the earlier reduction and recognition suites.  These finite
checks support the implementation and compatibility contracts; they
provide neither a measured policy speed comparison nor a universal
bound on the normal-search parameter.


\subsection{Frozen whole-diagram comparison and exact endpoints}
\label{subsec:geometric-descent-whole-call}

The subsequent experiment compares the explicit cochain and geometric
methods on the same native implementation.  The implementation and
364-test checkpoint were published at revision \path{2149e2e56}; the
comparison protocol was committed at \path{66817eceb} before this
experiment.  Its package, \path{fast/geometric_descent_research}, pins
all 299 native Python modules and its six own files, including the
protocol README.  All 305 pins remain unchanged throughout discovery,
timing and independent evidence replay.  Generated evidence is stored
outside the frozen package, under \path{synthesis/data}.

Both arms enable vertex reduction, boundary unlayering, strict $3\to2$
preprocessing and first-descent macros; only
\path{pachner_descent_method} differs.  The raw whole-diagram driver
is used even for $TT$, rather than its visible-factor wrapper.
The common transversal/source policy uses polynomial exact LP with
its default bounded fast-path prefix.  Each ordinary call has caps of
256 search nodes, 10,000 pivots, anchors and candidate hitting subsets,
256 sectors, 10,000 bases, and 10,000 ellipsoid iterations and
feasibility calls.  The sector quantum is 64.  The shared reduction
allowance is unlimited, but every accepted reduction decreases source
size.  There is no wall cutoff.  These limits control the recognition
search; the selected fixed-family geometric searches remain uncapped.

\subsection{Fifty complete timing calls, including regressions}

The timing set was fixed before the audit: circle, trefoil,
figure-eight, $TT$ and $T25$.  All five had to have the same conclusive
verdict in both audit arms; otherwise the complete timing experiment
would have been declined.  $T27$ and $W34$ were excluded in advance,
independently of their eventual outcomes.  Each admitted case receives
one warmup per arm, three pairs in alternating arm order, and one
cochain A/A pair: fifty complete calls altogether.  All fifty return
and pass validation, with no failures or omitted samples.

Each timer includes fresh recognition, independent proof replay and
source/operation accounting, full-result canonical serialization and
SHA-256.  The complete timed result, including its diagnostic fields,
must match the new audit result for that case and arm exactly.
Validated input construction, native module initialization, option
preparation, reference serialization and evidence-file writes are
outside the timer.  No additional native module initializes within
a sample, and no Regina call occurs there.  Full results, warmups,
orders and durations are retained, not just summary ratios.

The run uses Python 3.13.14 on a six-logical-CPU Linux host, with CPU5
affinity.  This task runs no competing heavy job during measurement,
but unrelated Lean work remains active; affinity does not reserve a
core.  Before/after process snapshots and the complete command are
retained.  Thus these observations describe one finite shared-host
experiment, with no population or exclusive-machine inference.

\subsection{Saved-evidence rejection and publication scope}

The external script \path{geometric-descent-corruption-controls.py}
checks the frozen audit and benchmark without invoking discovery or
performing new timings.  It accepts three valid controls and rejects
27 reproducible mutations.  Each mutation is retained as a JSON-path
edit program with a digest of the resulting document.  The controls
cover source pins, cases and arm coverage, input PDs, literal counters,
caps, method selection, chain schema, partial-versus-complete status,
macro source binding, elementary accounting, endpoint hashes, timed
payloads, pair order and summary arithmetic.  Altered warmup and paired
payloads remain rejected when their advertised hashes are recomputed.

A stronger policy control reuses a valid cochain proof for the same
diagram inside a version-five chain.  The general native verifier
accepts it, as required for compatibility.  The experiment's geometric
arm rejects it because the selected policy requires geometric macros.
The corresponding altered timed result is rejected even with a fresh
full-result digest and byte count.  This distinguishes a valid knot
proof from evidence for the claimed experimental arm.

For repeated rejection controls only, a transparent cache stores actual
Boolean returns of four native verifier entry points, keyed by the
complete typed source/diagram and proof arguments.  A changed native
argument misses; callbacks bypass this cache and exceptions propagate
uncached.  Unchanged valid controls populate it first.  All research
checks of configuration, coverage, statistics, policy, endpoint
comparison and timing arithmetic rerun for every mutation.  Hits,
misses, bypasses and exceptions are retained.  This is not a claim of
fresh native replay for every duplicate proof, nor an exhaustive
adversarial or execution-trace audit.

The publication helper \path{geometric_descent_evidence_publication.py}
freshly replays the complete saved audit, binds all fifty full timing
outputs, checks the retained rejection records and environment, and
pins the current article, PDF and build logs.  It verifies that native
code and regression dependencies remain those covered by the published
364-test run.  Historical core and cochain source manifests retain
their original meaning; they are not rewritten as current source
records.  The helper runs neither discovery, timing, tests nor a PDF
build.  The resulting evidence establishes this finite comparison
and its proof boundaries.  General quasi-polynomial unknot recognition
remains unproved.
