\section{Source-checked annihilation from plain power relations}
\label{sec:power-pairs}

The delivery \path{unknot_power_conjugacy_20261008.zip} in commit
\texttt{b365341b1} studies graphs of power-conjugacy relations and their
deletion closure. Its two-generator power-minor argument yields a directly
implementable special case. The maintained optional primitive-projection
search now tries this case after its existing primitive donors fail. It can
delete pairs for which neither individual relation is primitive, while keeping
all surviving relator slots and the source-bound cyclic-group endpoint.

\subsection{An annihilating minor, not just an abelianized relation}

Suppose the current full presentation contains relators cyclically spelled
$x^a y^b$ and $x^c y^d$, where a zero exponent permits a pure-power relator.
Their equations give $x^a=y^{-b}$ and $x^c=y^{-d}$. Taking powers and comparing
within a single cyclic subgroup proves
\[
 x^{ad}=y^{-bd}=x^{cb},\qquad
 y^{bc}=x^{-ac}=y^{da}.
\]
Consequently $x^\Delta=y^\Delta=1$ for $\Delta=ad-bc$. If $\Delta\ne0$
and the ambient group is torsion-free, both $x$ and $y$ are trivial.
Substituting the empty word for every occurrence of both generators preserves
the presented group. This is not a commutativity assumption, and it does not
use a unique-root rule for distinct elements.

The hypothesis is supplied by the existing knot host: every accepted move
starts from an independently reconstructed classical knot-group presentation
and a checked prefix of group-preserving moves. Internal algebraic routines
cannot establish that provenance. A nonzero minor does \emph{not} prove
triviality in an arbitrary group; for example $x^2=y^2=1$ permits nontrivial
torsion. No caller Boolean replaces the source reconstruction.

The report also proves an unconditional variant. For a group presented solely
by plain power rows on $x,y$, let $D$ be the gcd of all absolute two-row minors,
with $D=0$ if all minors vanish. Each minor annihilates both elements, so
$D=1$ implies $x=y=1$ in any ambient group. Conversely, for the isolated
two-generator presentation, if the exponent-row lattice has rank below two,
its abelianization has positive rank; at rank two its index is $D$. Thus the
isolated group is trivial exactly when $D=1$. The sufficiency uses the actual
power-word shape, not just the exponent matrix. Three rows can have minors
$6,7,29$, so an unconditional witness may need more than one minor.
The native knot-specific rule needs only one \emph{nonzero} minor, using its
already established torsion-free premise.

\subsection{Compressed donor discovery and independent checking}

The producer recognizes raw cyclic words with at most two signed-letter runs
on distinct absolute generators. It permits a single run as a pure-power
supplement. It neither freely reduces a donor nor infers its shape from
exponent sums alone. A cyclic cut may split one run into a prefix and suffix,
so a linear spelling with three runs can still qualify when its first and
last signed letters agree.

For each immutable word node the producer caches at most three run records.
Concatenation joins the touching equal signed letters and adds their binary
multiplicities. More than three runs is a permanent rejection: concatenation
cannot decrease the number of internal runs, and closing the cyclic boundary
can remove at most one. Cached content remains valid as substitution creates
new immutable nodes. The planner rebuilds slot and live-label associations
on every call.

Two-run donors are grouped by their unordered generator pair, with exponents
oriented to the sorted labels. At most one pure-power donor per generator
is appended to each corresponding group. The first mixed row is nonzero;
all rows are collinear exactly when each has zero determinant with that row.
Thus a linear scan per group finds a nonzero minor whenever that group has
one. Groups are traversed deterministically; each selected pair is disjoint
from earlier pairs, and at least one live generator remains. The present
collector requires a mixed donor for a group: it does not enumerate pairs
consisting solely of two pure-power relators. It is not a complete arbitrary
compressed conjugacy or relator-exposure algorithm.

The existing primitive planner has already prepared every coherent two-generator
candidate when it stalls. Every mixed cyclic two-run donor belongs to this
snapshot. If the snapshot is empty, the host skips the new detector completely:
this is an exact eligibility implication, not a heuristic budget cutoff.
It avoids repeating a whole-root scan on states with no possible mixed donor,
while preserving the detector's supported discovery class. The first native
timing exposed the cost of omitting this check; both runs are retained below.

Version nine adds a \texttt{power\_pair\_delete} move containing the two
generator labels and two original source-slot indices per pair. No claimed
exponents, determinants or proof-produced word summaries are trusted. The
independent compressed checker computes first/last letters, signed occurrence
counts and the number of adjacent signed-letter changes, capped at three.
Closing the cyclic boundary adds its last/first change. One or two signed
labels with distinct absolute generators and at most two cyclic changes are
exactly the permitted donor shapes. This checker does not import the
producer's run-list representation, planner or updater. The literal checker
separately counts changes directly in the supplied words.

Both replayers authenticate all selected donors and nonzero minors before
deleting any letters. Their separately implemented substitutions retain every
slot, including donors, and remove the two labels everywhere. Public roots and
live generators change only after the full substitution succeeds. Exhaustion
can leave historical arena nodes but no partial published move. Strict schema,
integer, distinct-label, live-membership and version checks reject malformed
evidence. Every other certificate move and endpoint keeps its existing rules.
Older version-eight certificates cannot authorize this new inference.

\subsection{A polynomial deletion epoch and a strict local extension}

Let $S_0$ be the initial reachable grammar size, $m$ its slot count, $r$ its
live rank, and $B$ the largest initial raw-length bit size. One raw deletion
maps each old concatenation to at most one new concatenation and maps each
terminal to itself or zero. Therefore reachable size and expanded raw length
do not increase. With $d\le\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor$ rounds,
\[
 S_i\le S_0,\qquad N_d\le N_0+dS_0,
 \qquad B_i\le B.
\]
Each donor pass and substitution costs a polynomial in $S_0,m,r,B$ and
identifier size. Exponents have at most $B$ bits and determinant arithmetic
has polynomial bit cost. Sorting pair keys and scanning groups are charged;
no integer factorization is needed. These bounds prove polynomial encoded
cost for a raw epoch consisting solely of these deletions, including the
implemented restricted discovery and independent replay. Normalization and
changes of generators end that accounting epoch.

For $M\ge2$, consider the supplied presentation
\[
 \left\langle t,x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_k,y_k\ \middle|\
 x_i^M=y_i^{M+1},\quad x_i^{M+1}=y_i^{M+2}\ (1\le i\le k)
 \right\rangle.
\]
Each pair has determinant $M(M+2)-(M+1)^2=-1$, so even the unconditional
argument makes all $x_i,y_i$ trivial and leaves the infinite cyclic generator
$t$. No displayed donor has a singleton. Neither two-run word is primitive:
its one-relator quotient surjects onto $C_M*C_{M+1}$, or respectively
$C_{M+1}*C_{M+2}$, which is nonabelian. Its coprime exponent coordinates also
exclude a proper power. Thus the existing primitive-forest donor conditions
find no move in this spelling, whereas the new detector closes it in one
round. This is a strict extension of the local operations, not a hardness
claim against the old complete search or a hard family of knot diagrams.

\subsection{Source exposure and the unintegrated graph proposal}

Original consistently oriented meridians all have abelianization charge one.
A generator proved trivial must have charge zero. Consequently the new rule
cannot activate directly on unmodified meridians; changes of generators are
needed to expose its targets. A native source fixture starts with the closure
of the four-strand braid $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3$. Its independently recovered
presentation has relators
\[
 x_1x_2^{-1},\quad x_1x_2x_1^{-1}x_3^{-1},\quad x_3x_1^{-1}.
\]
Two explicit Whitehead automorphisms substitute $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$ and
$x_3\mapsto x_1x_3$. After checked cyclic normalization the relators are
$x_2^{-1},x_2x_3^{-1},x_3$. The new move independently authenticates a mixed
row and a pure row, deletes $x_2,x_3$, and reaches the existing rank-one
endpoint. This proves source-bound integration. These deliberately supplied
automorphisms are not claimed to be newly discovered by the automatic search.

The broader delivered graph uses relations $x^a=u y^b u^{-1}$ and classical
knot-group balancedness. The primary statement, Lemma 2.3 of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00621}{Himeno--Motegi--Teragaito}, was checked:
a nontrivial knot-group element conjugate between two powers has equal
absolute exponents. It cannot be replaced by torsion-freeness alone, and
inverse conjugacy is not a valid triviality test. That graph algorithm,
complete compressed conjugator discovery, modular sieves and its delivered
whole-braid audit are not integrated or claimed as native measurements here.
This pair implementation uses the elementary plain-power argument above.
Section~\ref{sec:power-components} subsequently integrates plain signed-cycle
and pure-seed component witnesses; conjugated-power graphs remain unintegrated.
