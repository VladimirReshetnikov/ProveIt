\paragraph{Source-cut audit and prior successes.}
The refreshed 106-case audit certifies 78 cuts and independently replays all
78 proofs. Every one of the previous 63 successes is retained, and the 15
additional successes are exactly the eligible cases in the preceding profile
diagnostic. Regina constructs the actual cut and recognizes its connected
components in 99 cases, with exact agreement on all 71 native successes below
the literal expansion cap. Seven large-coordinate successes use independent
source replay without expansion; there are no oracle timeouts. Records and
runtime pins are retained in \path{data/primitive-annuli-audit.json}.

The 28 remaining refusals are 11 unsupported annular interfaces, seven failed
augmented core cancellations (including three square leaf obstructions), and
ten cores with three-handles. They carry no negative recognition conclusion.
The new nonorientable-base example certifies one ball and two genus-three
handlebodies; the five-tetrahedron multiple-annulus example certifies one ball
and one genus-two handlebody. Both match independently constructed Regina cuts.

The 22 focused tests pass. They cover simultaneous annuli, repeated attachment
graph edges, a nonorientable base, source relabellings, no-spare-boundary
refusals, forged counts and indices, schema downgrades, disabled producers,
hexadecimal transport, shared budget caps, multiplicity $2^{1024}$, and legacy
v1/v2 replay. All low-level family arguments remain conditional on the source
proofs; the whole-cut verifier checks those sources first.

\paragraph{Full regression.}
The complete Regina-enabled suite passes all 1,663 tests with no skips in
515.218 seconds. It includes 13,846 expanded-sheet topology comparisons and
196,712 independent marked-cover comparisons. The log is retained in
\path{data/primitive-annuli-tests.txt}. Source pins remain identical to the
106-cut audit after merging the concurrent repository changes.

\paragraph{Paired timings and binary capacity.}
Two newly supported v3 families were measured at four multiplicities each,
using five shuffled paired rounds after warmup and after the full regression
run completed. All native and explicit histograms agree. Native time includes
production and independent source replay; the explicit path constructs the
cut and recognizes every component. Input parsing and output serialization
are excluded. The driver and all samples are retained in
\path{data/primitive-annuli-benchmark.py} and its companion JSON; run the
driver from the repository root with Regina available.

Two of eight finite cases favor the native path in the measured paired
medians; six favor the explicit path. At 1,024 copies the four-tetrahedron
case has median native time 0.505 seconds, explicit time 2.113 seconds, and
paired ratio 4.184. The five-tetrahedron case has corresponding times
3.188 and 3.813 seconds and ratio 1.215. At eight copies that second family
has a ratio of only 0.010: the full orbit proofs and replay are expensive
relative to the small explicit cut.

The casewise medians of identical-explicit A/A comparisons range from 0.756
to 1.072. This observed variation limits interpretation of small timing
differences; in particular the 1.215 ratio is a modest measured gain, not
strong evidence of a stable speedup. All controls and raw rounds are retained. These are
supplied-cut measurements and do not justify a new default recognition stage.

Single-run capacity checks use coordinate multiplier $2^{16384}$. The
four-tetrahedron cut takes 1.932 seconds for production plus replay and
produces a 27,213,181-byte serialized certificate, with one primitive annulus.
The five-tetrahedron cut takes 17.867 seconds and produces 72,911,111 bytes,
with two primitive annuli and one absorbed disk-base component. Neither
capacity check attempts literal expansion. The component multiplicities stay
binary, while the present proof format still has a substantial serialization
size cost.

\paragraph{Diagnostic for the remaining core-side annuli.}
All eleven remaining annular refusals have handlebody cuts according to the
explicit oracle. A separate passive-incidence probe carries their midpoint
crossing counts through the existing core-cancellation schedule. In five
cases every pending annulus has a surviving cocore column with one crossing
and zero crossings from the other pending annuli. Four cases have no successful
core-cancellation schedule, and two have no complete set of such private
columns. The data are retained in \path{data/core-annulus-probe.json}.
This suggests a core-side primitive-family certificate, but the diagnostic
has no independently authenticated passive transport and adds no accepted
cuts. It must not be substituted for the required geometric proof.
