multiplicity representation than literal direct-sum sharing: small template
complexes repeated over large finite-state domains, with nonzero coupling
between copies through finitely many scalar rank-one channels, called ports.
A differential support graph can be connected even with one such port.
These algebraic ports are not topological boundary circles.

This is a relevant response to the explicit-multiplicity obstruction. The
consumer computes exact homology in polynomial time in its supplied compact
description. It does not construct that description from an arbitrary diagram
or retain the relative chain maps required at an open tangle frontier. The
report's large virtual complexes are abstract algebraic examples; its small
knot examples first expand their cubes. Accordingly, this review adds an
actual maintained-scanner compatibility audit, but no production recognition
branch or new general asymptotic guarantee.

\subsection{Why the small core remains valid when singular}
Work over $\mathbb F_2$ and write
\[
 A=\bigoplus_\nu A_\nu\otimes I_{\Omega_\nu},\qquad D=A+UV,
\]
where every template $A_\nu$ is square-zero and homogeneous, and $U,V$ have
$r$ columns and rows, respectively. Before homology is computed, the full
$D$ must also be proved homogeneous and square-zero.

For any matrix $A$, rectangular if necessary, choose a reflexive generalized
inverse $h$ with $AhA=A$ and $hAh=h$. An invertible rank minor constructs one
by ordinary elimination. Put
\[
 U_0=(I-Ah)U,\qquad V_0=V(I-hA),\qquad E=I_r+VhU.
\]
Let $\overline U$ be a row basis of $U_0$ and $\overline V$ a column basis
of $V_0$, with dimensions $s,t\le r$. The retained core is
\[
 K=\begin{pmatrix}0_{s\times t}&\overline U\\
                   \overline V&E\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}(D)=\operatorname{rank}(A)+\operatorname{rank}(K)-r.
\]
Here is the rank argument. The bordered matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&U\\V&I_r\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has rank
$r+\operatorname{rank}(A+UV)$ by eliminating its bottom identity block.
Alternatively, put $A,h$ into compatible identity-plus-zero bases and
eliminate the rank-$\operatorname{rank}(A)$ block. What remains is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&U_0\\V_0&E\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ in quotient
coordinates. Independent operations on its zero-corner rows and columns
retain precisely the displayed row and column bases. This contributes
$\operatorname{rank}(K)$ to the rank. Equating the two expressions proves
the formula without inverting $E$.

For a valid differential, $\beta(D)=\dim C-2\operatorname{rank}(D)$, giving
\[
 \beta(D)=\beta(A)+2r-2\operatorname{rank}(K).
\]
Singularity is essential: even the scalar update $A=U=V=(1)$ has
$A+UV=0$ and $E=0$. An inverse-based update would fail on this elementary
case, while the bordered formula gives the correct rank zero.

\subsection{Finite-state coordinates supply ranks and contractions}
Each block uses a layered weighted register
\[
 F(x_1,\ldots,x_m)=\alpha T_1^{x_1}\cdots T_m^{x_m}O
\]
whose simultaneous outputs are all local $U,V$ coordinates. All $m$ layers
are explicitly listed; the register length is not an exponentially large
repetition count supplied in binary. At each layer retain an independent
subset of the actual rows obtained by multiplying the previous basis by the
two transition matrices. Linearity proves inductively that these at-most-$B$
rows span all reachable states, where $B$ is the maximum layer width.
Store a predecessor and a bit for every retained witness. This constructs
actual word witnesses in $O(mB)$ records instead of copying all prefixes.
The underlying forward-space method is classical weighted-automaton
linear algebra~\cite{kiefer-register}.

These evaluation rows, followed by template actions, span all quotient rows
and columns needed in $K$. Pairings such as $VhU$ require a separate parity
contraction. For terminal state rows $w(x)$, define
\[
 W_0=\alpha^T\alpha,\qquad
 W_j=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}(T_j^b)^TW_{j-1}T_j^b.
\]
Then $W_m=\sum_xw(x)^Tw(x)$ by induction. Terminal coefficient vectors
$a,b$ give
\[
 \sum_x(w(x)a)(w(x)b)=a^TW_mb.
\]
This is not a rank test: a
nonzero constant function on a positive-length Boolean cube has evaluation
rank one and zero self-pairing in characteristic two. Reachability determines
ranks; the matrices $W_j$ compute contractions only.

The full square-zero condition is likewise finite-state:
\[
 D^2=[\,AU+U(VU)\quad U\,]
       \begin{bmatrix}V\\VA\end{bmatrix}.
\]
After computing $VU$, both factors are linear transforms of the register
outputs. Every pairing between a row-span basis of the first and a
column-span basis of the second must vanish. Bilinearity makes this equivalent
to vanishing of every entry of $D^2$, rather than a sample of entries.
Forbidden homological-degree coordinates are checked on spanning evaluations
in the same way.

A supplied deterministic language can restrict the multiplicity domain.
Tensoring its state transitions with the weighted register masks rejected
words to zero, with effective width at most the product of the two widths.
Integer dynamic programming counts accepted words exactly; counting paths
in an arbitrary nondeterministic automaton would not suffice. If masking is
implemented by padding to the full Boolean domain, subtract the known
uncoupled complement \emph{before} saturating any homology count. Cardinalities
are integers, not parities.

\subsection{Fixed-base minimization and a necessary cancellation budget}
For a fixed base $A$, the fewest scalar ports is
$r_{\min}=\operatorname{rank}(D-A)$. The report obtains it without enumerating
the domain. Within one homogeneous degree group, take row and column bases
$P,Q$ for $U,V$ and suitable one-sided inverses. Then $U=LP$, $V=QR$, with
$L$ full column rank and $R$ full row rank, so
$\operatorname{rank}(UV)=\operatorname{rank}(PQ)$. A rank factorization of
this small product transforms only the terminal register outputs. Transition
matrices, widths and register lengths are unchanged. Summing over disjoint
degree blocks preserves homogeneity and gives the global minimum for this
base. Choosing a better base is a separate problem.

Rank subadditivity gives the sharp budget
\[
 |\beta(D)-\beta(A)|\le2\operatorname{rank}(D-A)\le2r.
\]
Thus $\beta(A)>2r+2$ already rules out rank two, and the sufficient base cap
for this decision is $\min(\beta(A),2r+3)$, rather than a fixed cap of three.
For example, one arrow on four zero-base generators leaves homology two;
one arrow on six leaves homology four. The nontrivial port core is identical,
and capping both base dimensions at three would lose the distinction.
This does not invalidate saturation of genuinely isolated direct sums in the
maintained scanner: those summands have no coupling to cancel their homology.

A useful description of an unknot must therefore have a mostly contractible
base, unless its port count is large. Starting with an enormous zero base
cannot evade this inequality. Moreover, passive multiplicity extension itself
can defeat a fixed-base port bound:
\[
 \operatorname{rank}((D-A)\otimes I_q)
       =q\operatorname{rank}(D-A).
\]
This follows by tensoring a rank-normal form with $I_q$ and is independent of
register width. Appending $t$ passive Boolean factors can force $2^t$ times
as many scalar ports. A native construction must absorb such replicated
updates into new templates, cancel them with valid relative operations, or
use a richer structured coupling representation with its own size theorem.

\subsection{The conditional bound and the next constructive obligation}
For block size $d_\nu$, explicitly listed register length $m_\nu$, effective
width $B_\nu$ and total port count $r$, the report supplies the elementary
field-operation bound
\[
 O\!\left(\sum_\nu\left[d_\nu^3+(m_\nu+1)B_\nu^3+
 d_\nu^2B_\nu r+d_\nu B_\nu^2r+d_\nu B_\nu r^2\right]+r^3\right).
\]
Dimensions and language counts also have polynomial bit cost in the explicit
descriptor. Hence a proved quasi-polynomial-time producer and verifier of a
quasi-polynomial-size description would give quasi-polynomial recognition.
A succinct description found only after an exponential scan does not meet
that premise. Nor does a good description of a closed scalar complex supply
chain-homotopy data for arbitrary open-frontier cobordism gluing.

The next useful target is therefore a producer that selects near-contractible
templates \emph{before} explicit copy allocation, preserves typed relative
maps until closure, and bounds effective widths and minimum port ranks after
every operation. Its measurements must include failed admission attempts,
conversion, exact validity checks and fallback. The tensor rank identity is
an early rejection criterion for a proposed fixed-base construction, not a
reason to assume that all bases are impossible.

\subsection{Reproduced evidence and actual maintained-scanner compatibility}
The delivered 48 test methods pass locally in 1.517 seconds. Its separate
validation script reproduces 4,352 exhaustive rank updates, including 1,728
singular middle matrices, and 404 independent signed braid-cube comparisons.
Those are 210 knot and 194 link presentations, not 404 distinct knot types.
The recorded validation run takes 4.070 seconds; these are correctness-audit
times, not competitive benchmark results.

The maintained audit adds 30 nonempty, validated one-component diagrams:
trefoil, figure-eight and Hard 8 with their mirrors, plus seeded small braids.
For each diagram it checks original, reversed and shuffled crossing orders,
with full cancellation or the final two crossings left uncancelled. All 180
closed scalar complexes agree with an independent expanded reduced cube,
doubled to the unreduced rank, and with ordinary subsequent cancellation.
The adapter rejects all 144 encountered nonclosed first-crossing frontiers.
Port minimization preserves every answer, and all prototype topological
verdict fields remain unset.

This audit includes actual maintained list-based scanner states, rather than
only API-shaped fixtures. Nevertheless, all converted registers have length
zero. The largest input has 354 already allocated scalar generators and needs
209 raw ports; the adapter has not compressed an exponentially large state.
The delivered benchmark also reports slowdowns on all four already-expanded
small knot cubes. No automatic production dispatch follows from these checks.
The singular-safe consumer and the sharp constraints on a future producer are
the useful retained results at this checkpoint.
