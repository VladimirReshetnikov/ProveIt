\section{Ordered bundle interfaces and certified handlebody cuts}
\label{sec:marked-cut-handlebodies}

The source decomposition of Section~\ref{sec:parallelity-core} now has a
native consumer that proves the topological type of a restricted class of
\emph{whole normal cuts}. It recovers the cyclic order of core-facing ports,
certifies geometric cancellations in the inherited core, and glues the
resulting handlebodies along their actual disk interfaces. Its output is a
binary histogram of component genera, including isolated bundles with very
large multiplicities. This integrates the interface and core portions of
report~85~\cite{marked-parallelity-report85}; its separate sector-block
algorithms are not part of this implementation.

The source remains a validated finite, connected, compact, orientable
triangulation with one torus boundary, together with an admissible integral
standard normal vector. The surface may be empty, disconnected or one-sided.
The consumer does not infer a ball from its boundary or homology. Successful
certificates prove a handlebody census; unresolved cases carry no negative
recognition conclusion. A knot conclusion would additionally require the
appropriate connected cutting surface and authenticated diagram exterior.

\subsection{Two source queries recover the marked boundary}

Let $P$ be the number of source ports. The source construction bounds
$P\le66T$ and supplies the complete polygonal interval bundle, including
corner contacts and a separate fibre-reversal bit at every gluing. Number
the two ends of port $p$ by $2p$ and $2p+1$.

The first query imposes only corner identifications and assigns the distinct
weights $2^j$ to endpoint tokens $j$. Its weighted orbit histogram gives the
partition of port endpoints into actual base vertices. At a marked boundary
vertex there are one or two such tokens. The second query doubles the corner
universe by the interval-fibre orientation. It adds the original-boundary
edges but omits all port edges, and assigns distinct weights to the lifted
endpoint tokens. Every component with a marked endpoint is then a lift of
an interval joining two port ends. Each such interval has two lifts; their
endpoint pairing determines the fibre transport bit.

\begin{proposition}[Source-bound boundary skeleton]
\label{marked-cut:skeleton}
The two weighted queries determine each ported base boundary circle as a
signed cyclic port word, together with its intervening gaps, the presence
or absence of original boundary in each gap, and its fibre transport.
They distinguish the maximal attaching intervals from whole attaching
circles. The returned active skeleton has $P$ gaps and $P$ port edges.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Remove the interiors of all port edges from the completed boundary circles.
A circle containing a port becomes a collection of intervals whose ends
are the named tokens. An interval can degenerate to a single common vertex
when two ports are consecutive. The second query gives the endpoint
matching of these intervals. The first query distinguishes a degenerate
interval from a positive original-boundary path: precisely the former has
both tokens in the same vertex orbit. These conclusions use the source
boundary's one-manifold structure; they do not follow from an arbitrary
pairing supplied by a caller.

Alternately cross a port and its matched gap. This traverses each ported
boundary circle exactly once, up to orientation and starting point. The
implementation starts at the least unused port in its positive local
chart. Multiply the fibre bits around the circle; orientability of the
three-dimensional total bundle forces trivial boundary holonomy. Consecutive
ports separated only by zero gaps form one maximal attaching interval.
If a circle has no positive gap, its complete vertical boundary is an
annulus. Otherwise the vertical bundle over each maximal closed interval
is a disk. Unported boundary circles are intentionally omitted here; their
counts and base topology are already in the source profiles.
\end{proof}

The distinction is necessary. Fifteen individual ports in the four-tetrahedron
layered meridian example form only three attaching disks. Treating each
port as an independent disk changes the gluing graph and its genus. Likewise,
a whole attaching circle must not be cut into an arbitrary collection of
disks. No extra peripheral curves or inherited pattern words inside an
original-boundary gap are preserved by this observer.

\subsection{Sparse weights retain a polynomial interface}

The queries use $2P$ and $4P$ singleton atoms. Under the suffix-only weighted
transport of Section~\ref{sec:weighted-transversal}, every atom is either at
one current point or in an already emitted orbit. Thus at most $K$ points
carry atoms when the query started with $K$ atoms. Its canonical piecewise
constant weight function has at most $2K+1$ runs, independently of the
number of truncations. Repeated atom locations simply combine their weights.
With distinct powers of two, every nonzero weight is a disjoint subset mask
of at most $K$ bits and each nonzero output mask has multiplicity one.

Consequently the two interface queries have at most $4P+1$ and $8P+1$
weight runs. The ordinary interval-orbit trace, binary source ranks and
these bounded masks give polynomial encoded work in $T$ and the input
coordinate bit lengths. This is a bound for the supplied-cut interface,
not a bound on the number of surfaces a complete hierarchy must search.
The independent checker reconstructs the polygon geometry from raw cut
cells, builds both queries separately, replays their weighted certificates,
and traverses the resulting endpoint permutation. It imports none of the
interface producer's decoder or query routines.

\subsection{Geometric singleton pivots in the core}

The inherited core has at most $30T$ handles. For this consumer it must have
no three-handles. Contract a spanning forest of its zero/one-handle graph;
the remaining one-handles have a complete cocore disk system $D_h$.
For each two-handle attaching curve $\alpha_i$, retain its actual unsigned
intersection count
\[
 n_{ih}=|\alpha_i\cap\partial D_h|.
\]
The alternating source sectors determine these counts, including repeated
occurrences. Forest contraction deletes only the tree-handle entries.
Signed exponent sums or freely reduced words cannot replace this matrix.

\begin{lemma}[Geometric positive pivot]
\label{marked-cut:pivot}
If $n_{rg}=1$, attaching the two-handle $r$ reduces the handlebody genus by
one and admits a new complete disk system on the other columns with
\[
 n'_{ih}=n_{ih}+n_{ig}n_{rh}\qquad(i\ne r,\ h\ne g).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Keep $D_g$ fixed. Along $\alpha_r$, choose an intersection with another
$\partial D_h$ adjacent to the unique intersection with $\partial D_g$.
Slide $D_h$ over a parallel copy of $D_g$ along a thin band following the
intervening arc. Its interior misses all disk boundaries and all other
attaching curves. The smoothing removes that intersection with $\alpha_r$;
for every other curve $\alpha_i$ the replacement adds exactly $n_{ig}$
intersections. The disks remain an embedded complete system. Repeat until
$\alpha_r$ misses every disk except $D_g$. Disk $D_h$ has been slid exactly
$n_{rh}$ times. The selected one/two-handle pair now cancels by the geometric
one-point cancellation theorem~\cite[Lemma 10.3]{sadykov-cancellation}.
The surviving attaching curves have precisely the displayed incidences.
\end{proof}

This gives a topological existence certificate even when the expanded disk
slides would be very long. It does not yet encode the transported position
of every later boundary-pattern strand. This distinction separates it from
the no-slide source reductions in Section~\ref{sec:source-handles}.

Only the values zero, one and at least two are needed for subsequent pivots.
The map $n\mapsto\min(2,n)$ preserves addition and multiplication into the
saturated nonnegative semiring. Hence the independent checker updates
\[
 c'_{ih}=\min(2,c_{ih}+c_{ig}c_{rh})
\]
on constant-sized entries without losing the singleton predicate. A complete
schedule has $O(T)$ pivots, $O(T\log(T+1))$ bits of source labels, and at most
$O(T^3)$ matrix operations. A connected core with $v$ zero-handles, $e$
one-handles and $f$ two-handles then has genus $e-v+1-f$.

\subsection{A complete source test within the square raw model}

For a square remaining incidence matrix, a successful positive-pivot sequence
exists exactly when source row-leaf deletion removes every row and column.
A leaf is a row with exactly one live nonzero, equal to one. The justification
is the permanent-one criterion proved in report~85 and the earlier immutable
source closure of report~56. Reversing a successful positive pivot preserves
uniqueness of the edge-labelled perfect matching. A nonnegative square
matrix with permanent one has a unique matching with unit matched entries;
orient every other row incidence toward its column. A directed cycle would
produce a second matching, so there is a sink row leaf. Induction removes
all rows. Conversely, a row leaf has no fill term in any surviving column,
so every source leaf deletion is a valid geometric pivot.

The permanent is a proof device and is never computed. The producer maintains
source incidence lists and a heap of eligible row labels, costing
$O(I+T\log(T+1))$ structural operations for $I$ source incidences. The checker
uses deletion times and scans immutable rows. In the rectangular case the
producer first tries this leaf order, then a deterministic saturated pivot
schedule. Section~\ref{marked-cut:column-peeling} strengthens this to an exact
rectangular full-row test and replaces the repeated scans. Failure remains
topologically inconclusive. Even square completeness is only for this
raw cancellation model on the selected forest, not recognition of all balls.

\subsection{Assembling the entire cut}

An orientable interval bundle over a connected bordered surface $B$ is a
handlebody of genus $1-\chi(B)$. Indeed a system of $1-\chi(B)$ proper arcs
cuts $B$ into a disk; their inverse images cut the total bundle into a ball.
This includes the orientable bundle over a nonorientable base. Closed bases
are excluded by the current consumer.

The full source product-intersection theorem identifies the core/bundle
intersection with the vertical bundle over the closed core-labelled base
arcs, including endpoint contacts. After adjacent ports are amalgamated,
distinct proper runs have separated closures and compatible corner collars.
Their vertical bundles are disjoint embedded disks on both sides. This
geometric input is indispensable; a cyclic word alone would not prove it.

\begin{proposition}[Exact binary handlebody census]
\label{marked-cut:census}
Suppose every core component has a verified cancellation certificate, all
bundle bases have nonempty boundary, and every attaching run is proper.
Each component of the cut is a handlebody. For a connected component $C$ of
the disk-attachment multigraph, its genus is
\[
 G_C=\sum_{v\in V(C)}g_v+|E(C)|-|V(C)|+1.
\]
Unported bundle profiles contribute their binary multiplicities of isolated
handlebodies. These contributions determine the complete genus histogram.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Vertices represent the certified core handlebodies and individual ported
bundle components; each maximal disk interface gives one edge. Choose
meridian systems in all pieces disjoint from the marked boundary disks.
This is possible by a boundary isotopy of the finitely many disks away
from a chosen meridian system, extended through a collar. Cut along these
meridians and the gluing disks outside a spanning tree. The remainder is a
tree of balls joined along disks, hence a ball by successively absorbing
leaf balls. The number of cut disks is exactly the displayed genus.
Ported profiles have multiplicity one by their distinct source ownership.
Every unported component is separate from the core and must be counted
with its full multiplicity. No expansion of that multiplicity is needed.
\end{proof}

The producer uses union-find for this graph; the checker reconstructs the
components by graph search. Both verify the redundant Euler identity
$\chi(K)+\chi(\mathcal B)-\chi(K\cap\mathcal B)$. That identity is a
consistency check, not the ball proof. The complete cut is one ball exactly
when the histogram is $\{(0,1)\}$. A union of many balls is distinguished from
one ball even when its multiplicity has thousands of bits.

\subsection{Implementation contracts and the next geometric extension}

Three APIs each have an independently implemented verifier:
\begin{itemize}
\item \path{normal_parallelity_boundary_words} recovers the ordered interface;
\item \path{core_handlebody_certificate} certifies the inherited core;
\item \path{normal_cut_handlebodies} assembles the complete cut census.
\end{itemize}
The low-level core API
assumes authenticated geometric charts; the whole-cut wrapper supplies them.
The six orbit discoveries share the cycle allowance, and replay counts all
six supplied traces against its operation cap. Callback exceptions propagate.
Integer transport uses the existing exact hexadecimal codec; the native
integration also decodes core component quantities before graph arithmetic,
including signed and uppercase hexadecimal forms, while rejecting booleans.

A full attaching circle on a \emph{disk} base can be absorbed as a two-handle,
as proved in report~85. Its actual face-sector ports supply an unsigned
incidence row and source flags distinguish the ends even for self-pairings.
The initial checkpoint below did not implement that extension;
Section~\ref{marked-cut:disk-absorption} records the subsequent certified
implementation. General annular gluing,
three-handle removal in cut cores, explicit pattern transport, useful-surface
coverage and a global bound on hierarchy descriptions remain separate tasks.
The optional initial cocore stage and the full exponential fallback are
unchanged; this API does not dispatch a new knot-recognition verdict.
