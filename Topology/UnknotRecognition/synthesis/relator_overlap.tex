\section{Relator overlaps: a native certificate for Gordian}
\label{sec:relator-overlap}

\subsection{Preserving a normal closure while shortening a relator}

The first group-certificate stage could stall after exhausting generator
elimination and length-reducing Whitehead moves.  An additional exact move
now changes the relators themselves.  Suppose two distinct relator slots,
after cyclic rotation and optional inversion of the donor, read
\[
 R_i=UW,\qquad R_j^{\epsilon}\sim UV,
 \qquad i\ne j,\quad \epsilon\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
Replace the target by $V^{-1}W$ and retain the donor.  This preserves the
normal closure of the relator list, because
\[
 (UV)^{-1}(UW)=V^{-1}W,\qquad
 (UV)(V^{-1}W)=UW.
\]
Both the old and new target therefore lie in the normal closure of the
other presentation.  Cyclic conjugation and inversion preserve a relator's
normal closure, so undoing the displayed rotations does not change the
argument.  The quotient group, and hence the positive unknot conclusion
at terminal rank one, is unchanged.

Requiring different target and donor slots is essential.  A relator cannot
be deleted by using itself as the donor: that would remove a relation
rather than replace it by an equivalent one.  Distinct slots containing
equal relators can legitimately reduce to one copy.  The independent
checker enforces this distinction.

If $u=|U|$ and $d=|R_j|$, the literal target length decreases by
$2u-d$.  The producer proposes only moves with $2u>d$; free and cyclic
cancellation can shorten further.  Among these moves it maximizes the
guaranteed decrease, not the decrease after all incidental cancellations.
This is a Dehn-style presentation simplification.  Regina documents a
related use of relator substitutions and generator elimination
\cite{regina-group-simplify}.  We implement and replay our own individual
moves; a declared external isomorphism is not used as proof evidence.

No small-cancellation hypothesis is required for the correctness of an
individual substitution.  Such hypotheses enter completeness theorems
for Dehn algorithms.  We do not assume that arbitrary knot presentations
satisfy them, and failure to find an overlap has no negative meaning.

\subsection{Finding the best cyclic overlap without listing rotations}

A direct search that builds a long tuple key for each rotation can spend
quadratic space or work on a single long relator.  Small fixed-length
seeds avoid that copying but can still produce many candidates on
repetitive words.  The implementation instead uses an exact suffix
automaton, following the classical linear-size subword-automaton
framework \cite{blumer-subwords}.

For each nonempty donor $v$, and separately for $v^{-1}$, build the
suffix automaton of $v$ followed by all but its last letter.  This word of
length $2|v|-1$ contains every cyclic rotation and every cyclic segment of
length at most $|v|$.  An automaton state stores its maximum represented
length, suffix link, exact letter transitions, and the end position of
one occurrence.  Online extension creates a new state, follows suffix
links to add missing transitions, and splits a state when a transition
would otherwise identify incompatible maximum lengths.  The clone copies
transitions and the occurrence position, while redirecting the necessary
suffix-link transitions.

Scan each other doubled target word through this automaton.  On a missing
transition, follow suffix links and shorten the currently matched suffix.
For a match of length $\ell$ against target $w$, use
\[
 u=\min(\ell,|v|,|w|).
\]
The caps forbid a match from wrapping around either cyclic word more than
once.  The target scan position and stored donor occurrence position give
the two rotation offsets modulo their respective lengths.  Compare
$2u-|v|$ with the best positive decrease found so far.  A target whose
upper bound $2|w|-|v|$ cannot improve the current best can be skipped.
No probabilistic fingerprints or collision assumptions enter equality:
letters are signed generator integers, and a literal prefix comparison
checks the selected witness before application.

Let $m$ be the number of nonempty relators and $L$ their total explicit
length.  Building each automaton and scanning every target costs
$O(mL)$ dictionary operations overall, plus traversal of the original
relator slots.  The auxiliary automaton occupies $O(L)$ space, since only
one donor orientation is indexed at a time.  With the usual amortized
hash-table costs this is the corresponding expected word-operation bound;
the correctness is deterministic.  Even replacing dictionary lookup by a
linear scan over the at most $2n$ letters gives a polynomial worst-case
bound.  The implementation charges construction, clone copies, suffix
links and target scanning against its cooperative work allowance.

The five new tests compare the optimized search with an independent
exhaustive search over all rotations and both donor signs on 2,141 word
lists.  These include every pair of cyclically reduced rank-two words of
length at most three, random signed lists, empty slots and periodic words.
The test checks the maximum guaranteed decrease and the literal selected
substitution, not only whether some move was returned.

\subsection{Certificate format and independent verification}

A trace containing an overlap uses certificate version~2.  The previous
elimination/Whitehead-only traces remain version~1 and are still accepted.
The new move records target and donor indices, their cyclic offsets, the
donor inversion flag, and overlap length.  The checker validates types,
indices, distinct slots, offsets, overlap bounds and letter equality.
It reconstructs the two cyclic words by modular indexing rather than by
the producer's suffix automaton or rotation helper, then directly replaces
the target by the inverse donor remainder followed by the target remainder.
The unchanged donor remains available throughout.

The checker does not require a maximum overlap or even positive length
gain: the normal-closure identity is valid without those search-policy
properties.  Its expansion ceiling and operation allowance still apply.
Forged self-donors, false matching prefixes, invalid offsets, incorrect
versions and malformed fields are rejected.  Acceptance still requires
one surviving generator and every remaining relator empty.  Tests replay
the Gordian trace with the search and application helpers disabled, and
cross-check another 80 random small knot inputs against the full Khovanov
cube whenever a positive certificate is produced.

\subsection{What the larger search establishes}

On the supplied 141-crossing Gordian PD, the native producer finds a trace
with 140 generator eliminations and one relator-overlap move.  Independent
replay proves its full knot group infinite cyclic.  This replaces the
previously missing native positive certificate for this example; it does
not need Regina at runtime.  The two mirrored survivor diagrams that
stalled under the original group search also acquire positive certificates.
The input itself remains the numeric PD fixture documented with the
normal-surface experiments; no claim based on its filename or provenance
is used by the verifier.

The same conditional complexity obstacle remains.  Between generator
eliminations, both overlap moves and chosen Whitehead moves strictly
reduce total relator length.  If every expanded list has length at most
$B$, there are at most $O(n(B+1))$ moves.  Adding a polynomial-time overlap
search to each step preserves a polynomial bound in $n$ and $B$.
Neither a quasi-polynomial bound on $B$ nor completeness of these greedy
moves is proved.  The existing work, wall and expansion caps yield safe
fallback rather than a new general asymptotic guarantee.

Kapovich's August 2026 preprint \cite{kapovich-generic} studies generic
small-cancellation stability and isomorphism rigidity for fixed generator
and relator counts.  Its stated generic quadratic isomorphism algorithm
is for its random-presentation model, not for all finitely presented
groups.  Our Wirtinger generators and relations grow with the crossing
number and are strongly constrained by topology.  Thus its generic
conclusions do not establish an average-case or worst-case bound for this
knot recognizer.  We use exact normal-closure identities for soundness and
measured search behaviour for scheduling, rather than importing that
unverified distributional transfer.

\subsection{Adaptive scheduling rather than unconditional overlap search}

The first implementation tried overlaps whenever direct generator
elimination stalled, before trying Whitehead moves.  A paired 29-input
experiment showed why that order should not be universal.  Although it
certified Gordian, the sum of median query costs on the twelve survivors
and twelve mirrors increased from $113.14$ ms with the previous group
stage to $191.77$ ms with overlaps first.  These are sums of individual
medians, not end-to-end batch timings.  The code and all 580 measured
queries at this checkpoint remain in commit \code{772b24bca} and
\code{results/relator\_overlap\_20261008.json}.

The revised policy observes the surviving generator count after each
elimination.  At rank at most six, it tries a shortening Whitehead move
first and searches for relator overlaps only if none exists.  Above six,
it tries overlaps first, then Whitehead moves.  It reuses the result of
an already completed cut search, rather than performing it twice.
Six is an empirical dispatch threshold: it reflects the different costs
of small graphs and many signed-multiplier flow problems, not a theorem
that low-rank presentations are easy.  The regression test verifies that
all twelve original survivors retain exactly their prior successful
certificate traces, without invoking overlap search.  The stalled
mirrors still gain the new move when they need it.

The new mode is selected by \code{--group-relators}, which also enables
the optional group stage.  Python callers use
\code{use\_group=True, group\_relators=True}.  The previous \code{--group}
behaviour remains available.  Time and work caps are explicit: the Gordian
experiment uses a two-second shared search-and-replay allowance and ten
million work units per producer/checker, while the default group settings
remain 0.05 seconds and two million units.  Python callers set
\code{group\_seconds} and \code{group\_max\_work}; the standalone producer
and checker expose \code{max\_work}.  Raising the time allowance alone
need not overcome the separate work ceiling.

The benchmark uses fresh validated PD inputs, strips braid-source
provenance, randomizes the order of default, previous group, overlap and
Regina arms, and excludes one warmup before five measured rounds.  All
arms share a four-second global cap and a 50,000-object Khovanov ceiling;
both group arms use the same two-second and ten-million-work allowances.
Regina also has a two-second local allowance including process startup.
Certificates are independently replayed inside the measured query.
The initial overlap-first checkpoint completed Gordian in all five rounds
at median $1.452$ seconds, versus $1.957$ seconds for Regina.  Both the
default and previous group arms timed out in every round.  No ratio
against these censored control times is reported as a completion speedup.

A fresh 580-query experiment of the revised dispatcher is stored in
\path{results/relator_adaptive_20261008.json}.  Its 24 survivor/mirror
inputs all finish with independently checked group certificates in every
round.  Their summed median query costs are $120.28$ ms for the default,
$84.17$ ms for the previous group search, $84.86$ ms for adaptive overlap
search, and $120.00$ ms for the Regina arm, which bypasses native startup
on these small inputs.  Thus the additional reach costs less than one
percent against the previous group-stage aggregate in this experiment.
Absolute timings vary between experiments; compare arms within the same
randomized run rather than attributing the entire change between the two
archived runs to scheduling.

Gordian completes in all five adaptive runs at median $1.212$ seconds,
including validation, all prior pipeline work, search and independent
replay.  Regina completes all five at median $1.708$ seconds in the same
run.  The default and previous group arms remain censored at four seconds.
The two formerly stalled mirrors receive group certificates, but are not
faster than the default Khovanov route; this is greater certificate reach,
not universal acceleration.  These observations keep the group extensions
opt-in.  They do not show that relator-overlap search dominates normal
surfaces or Khovanov on other knot families.
