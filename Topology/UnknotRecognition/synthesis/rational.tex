\section{Arithmetic continuations and checked Montesinos sources}
\label{sec:rational-arithmetic}

Report 22 supplies a useful change of representation: some prefixes whose
explicit homotopy complexes are necessarily large have short arithmetic
descriptions sufficient for the actual closure decision.  Production now
implements its supplied-Montesinos classifier and a separate optional local
obstruction.  This is a complete polynomial decision on a specified source
grammar, not an algorithm discovering that grammar in every PD.  The incoming
archive remains unchanged; this section describes the maintained integration.

\subsection{What the source describes}
An input consists of an integer $e$ and separate finite rational tangles
with ordinary continued fractions.  Its knot is
\[
 N\bigl([e]+T(p_1/q_1)+\cdots+T(p_s/q_s)\bigr),
 \qquad \gcd(p_i,q_i)=1,\quad q_i>0.
\]
The summands remain separate throughout.  Reducing their numerical sum
would lose topological information: two $1/2$ summands give uncancelled
numerator $4$, whereas the numerical sum is $1$.  A quarter rotation
sends a slope to $-1/x$; a rotation followed by crossing reversal gives
$1/x$.  These conventions agree with the rational-tangle operations in
\cite{rational-tangles}.  The compiler performs those operations on actual
four-terminal crossing graphs, joins adjacent terminal pairs, and adds the
numerator caps.  A lazy crossing-switch bit avoids repeatedly mirroring
earlier crossings during continued-fraction construction.

For each continued fraction, read coefficients backwards from $(p,q)=(1,0)$:
\[
 (p,q)\longmapsto(ap+q,p).
\]
Every update matrix is unimodular.  Hence no gcd computation is required,
intermediate infinity is harmless, and the final pair is primitive.
A final infinite slope is rejected.  Write $p_i=a_iq_i+\beta_i$ with
$0\le\beta_i<q_i$, add all $a_i$ to $e$, and discard integral summands.
The remaining $r$ pairs $(\beta_i,\alpha_i)$ have
$0<\beta_i<\alpha_i$ and $\alpha_i\ge2$.  Integral-twist transfer through
rational summands preserves the numerator closure.  Set
\begin{equation}
 Q=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_i,\qquad
 D=eQ+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\beta_i Q/\alpha_i.
 \label{eq:rational-determinant}
\end{equation}
The implemented accumulator starts at $(D,Q)=(e,1)$ and updates
$(D,Q)\mapsto(D\alpha+\beta Q,Q\alpha)$, without cancellation.

\begin{lemma}[Component gate]
The numerator closure in this grammar is a knot exactly when $D$ is odd.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A primitive rational column modulo two specifies one of the horizontal,
vertical, and crossed endpoint pairings, respectively $(0,1)$, $(1,0)$,
and $(1,1)$.  Twisting and reciprocal operations preserve this correspondence.
If $k>0$ denominators are even, the open horizontal sum has $k-1$ internal
closed strings and its numerator closure has $k$ components.  If $k=0$,
the number of components is one or two according as
$e+\sum_i\beta_i$ is odd or even.  Formula
\eqref{eq:rational-determinant} has odd parity exactly for $k=1$, or for
$k=0$ with the stated odd sum.  For $k\ge2$ every term is even.
This also covers the empty exceptional list.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Decision on supplied Montesinos knots]
After the component gate, the answer is UNKNOT precisely when
$r\le2$ and $|D|=1$; otherwise it is KNOTTED.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the classical double-cover construction: the cover is a Seifert
manifold whose group, in this convention, is
\[
 \langle h,x_1,\ldots,x_r\mid [h,x_i]=1,
 x_i^{\alpha_i}h^{\beta_i}=1,\ x_1\cdots x_r=h^e\rangle.
\]
Rational tangle balls lift to solid tori; their meridians have slopes
$\alpha_i x_i+\beta_i h$.  Abelianizing this presentation gives homology
order $|D|$ when $D\ne0$.  If $r\ge3$, kill $h$ and all but three of the
$x_i$.  The resulting triangle group with orders $a,b,c\ge2$ is nontrivial.
Here is an explicit verification avoiding an algorithm for triangle groups.
Put $\lambda=e^{\pi i/a}$, $t=2\cos(\pi/b)$, $s=2\cos(\pi/c)$,
\[
 u=\frac{s-\lambda^{-1}t}{\lambda-\lambda^{-1}},\quad
 v=\frac{\lambda t-s}{\lambda-\lambda^{-1}},\quad
 X=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\0&\lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad
 Y=\begin{pmatrix}u&1\\uv-1&v\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both determinants are one and the traces of $Y,XY$ are $t,s$.
Their distinct eigenvalues give $X^a=Y^b=(XY)^c=-I$.
Thus $X,Y,(XY)^{-1}$ define a nontrivial projective representation;
$X$ has projective order $a$.  The cover cannot be $S^3$ and the knot
cannot be the unknot.  This is a symbolic proof, not a numerical subroutine.

For at most two exceptional summands the closure is two-bridge, even though
the open sum need not be rational.  The rational-link classification gives
the unknot exactly when its numerator parameter $|D|$ equals one.
In the two-summand case this is also the rational gluing criterion of
\cite[Theorem 5]{rational-hard}.  The Seifert-cover and local-closure
foundations are discussed in \cite[Theorem 4.9]{rational-local}.
\end{proof}

This detects determinant-one nontrivial knots.  For example, for every odd
$a\ge3$ take slopes $-1/a,1/(a+2),1/r$ with
$r=(a^2+2a-1)/2$.  The signed pretzel expression is
$-a(a+2)+2r=-1$, so the normalized positive-denominator determinant has
absolute value one.  All three denominators exceed one, proving nontriviality.
The usual genus-one Seifert matrix for odd pretzel parameters $(p,q,r)$ is
\[
 \frac12\begin{pmatrix}p+q&q+1\\q-1&q+r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is $(pq+pr+qr+1)/4=0$ here; its Alexander polynomial is
a unit and its symmetrization has signature zero.  Thus these easy arithmetic
decisions include a family on which those two obstructions are inconclusive.

\paragraph{Bit cost and output.}
Let $B$ include all binary coefficients, signs, and list delimiters.  Each
continued-fraction column has bit length bounded by the sum of
$1+\lceil\log_2(1+|a|)\rceil$ along its block.  All final columns together
have $O(B)$ bits.  Multiplying an $s$-bit partial entry by a $b$-bit coefficient
costs $O(sb+s)$ with schoolbook arithmetic; summing over the input costs
$O(B^2)$.  The divisions producing $(a_i,\beta_i)$ and the uncancelled
accumulator have the same bound, by charging products to both operand lengths.
The classifier therefore uses $O(B^2)$ bit operations and $O(B)$ bits of
integer/source data.  This statement does not count Python object overhead
as a single bit.  The certificate has $O(B)$ encoded bits; integers exceeding
1024 bits use exact hexadecimal records.  The independent replay uses forward
matrix products and full denominator-product sums, with a conservative
$O(B^3)$ bound because it also replays prefix size statistics.  The reported
maximum entry size concerns tracked states, not every temporary or peak memory.

\subsection{Provenance, budgets, and integration}
The compressed API is \code{montesinos\_certificate}, taking $e$ and the tangle list.
The Diagram constructor \code{from\_rational} (also \code{from\_montesinos}) builds
all crossings and validates the resulting spherical, one-component PD.
Its default expansion ceiling is 100000 crossings, checked before allocating
crossings.  Input parsing and construction precede the ordinary recognition
timer.  The ordinary JSON grammar uses a \code{montesinos} object with
\code{e} and \code{tangles}; its CLI expands that input, rather than silently
switching to the compressed interface.

Only the checked constructor attaches a detached immutable source.  Bare
PD input has no such assertion.  Mirroring negates all coefficients;
serialized sources are reconstructed and must match the stored PD up to
half-turns of individual rows.  Multiple serialized source types are rejected.
An initialized Diagram cannot be reinitialized with a different PD while
retaining old provenance.  Equality and hashing still depend solely on PD.
These are supported API invariants, not protection against arbitrary mutation
of interpreter internals.

The recognizer checks this source before other certificates;
\code{use\_rational=False} or \code{--no-rational} disables that stage.
Arithmetic polls the same cooperative deadline and the recognizer checks
again after certificate construction.  The source is used only for the
original whole diagram, before factorization.  The rank-two restart option
continues to carry all recognition settings.  PDs without this new source
retain the previous pipeline.  The general quasi-polynomial flag stays false.

\subsection{Literal local obstructions with a checked disk}
For a genuine two-string tangle with $r\ge2$ finite nonintegral summands,
Nogueira--Salgueiro's criterion says it embeds in some unknot exactly when
$r=2$ and
\[
 p_1q_2+p_2q_1\equiv\pm1\pmod{q_1q_2}.
\]
We use only its negative direction.  A verified occurrence failing this
criterion proves the entire host knot nontrivial, regardless of the exterior.
An allowed complement gives no positive verdict for the actual host.
The implemented templates require initial $e=0$, connected crossing shadow,
four distinct ports, and no closed strand component.

The source compiler recomputes fractions and builds the literal crossing
pattern.  Its rotation system must be spherical with every port on one
facial boundary in the declared order.  Opposite-dart tracing must produce
exactly two open strings.  An occurrence maps crossings injectively into
a freshly validated host PD.  Offsets zero and two preserve the cyclic
order and under-pair.  Every internal edge must match and every other dart
must lead outside the selected set, yielding four distinct cut edges.

These checks prove the needed disk condition.  Take a regular neighborhood
of the connected selected graph, ending halfway along its outgoing edges.
Every remaining graph component attaches at a port, since the entire knot
shadow is connected.  All ports lie in one face, so the remaining graph lies
there too.  Fill the other neighborhood boundary components with their empty
complementary disks.  The result is a disk meeting precisely the four cut
edges; thickening it gives a tangle ball.  An abstract four-edge cut alone
would not justify this argument.

Fixing one root image and its half-turn forces the image and rotation of
every neighbor through internal edges.  Connectedness leaves only $2n$
root trials, each costing $O(k)$ for a $k$-crossing template.  The matching
core thus costs $O(nk)$ word operations.  Public host validation also sorts
labels, adding $O(n\log(n+2))$; source reading and compilation must be charged
separately.  A huge zero-coefficient description is not free just because
it expands to few crossings.  Python dictionaries use the usual table-cost
model in this count; indexed arrays give the corresponding deterministic
algorithmic bound.  The six default patterns have slopes $(1/3,1/3)$,
$(1/2,1/5)$, $(1/3,1/4)$ and their mirrors.

\code{recognize\_with\_subtangles} is a separate opt-in wrapper.  No match
continues ordinary recognition with the remaining time; exhaustion yields
UNKNOWN.  The production integration also checks time after successful final
verification, closing a gap in the delivered wrapper.  Time values must be
finite and nonnegative.  Template compilation and PD validation are cooperative
operations, not interruptible real-time allocations.

An abstract extension enumerating at most $h$ coefficients and all separators,
with coefficients in $[-n,n]$ and total expanded size at most $n$, has at most
$\sum_{\ell\le h}(2n+1)^\ell2^{\ell-1}=(n+2)^{O(h)}$ candidates.
Compiling and checking them gives quasi-polynomial discovery when
$h=O(\log n)$.  This is a restricted literal-witness theorem from report 22,
not the implemented fixed catalogue and not a bound for every knot diagram.

\subsection{Why arithmetic helps when a small linear summary cannot}
Let
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 v_w=G_w(1,1)^t,\quad x_w=G_w(3,2)^t
\]
for words $w\in\{A,B\}^m$ and their literal matrix products $G_w$.
Two twists realize each letter on rational tangles.  The associated
prefix and mirrored continuation have at most $2m+1$ and $2m+3$ crossings.
All cross closures are knots: the two columns have parity $(1,1)$ and
$(1,0)$.  The rational gluing test is $|\det(v_u,x_w)|=1$.

For $u=w$ the determinant is $-1$.  For distinct words, their positive-cone
images are disjoint: $A$ sends a positive column into $x>2y$, while $B$ sends
it into $y>2x$; remove the common prefix to prove the assertion inductively.
Consequently $G_w^{-1}v_u=(a,b)^t$ has one nonpositive and one positive
integer coordinate.  The determinant $2a-3b$ has absolute value at least
two, and odd parity raises this to at least three.  Thus the Boolean
continuation matrix is the $2^m$-dimensional identity over every field.
Distinct exact continuation states require at least $m$ bits; a linear
representation of all Boolean answers needs dimension at least $2^m$.
Neither statement is a general running-time lower bound.  The signed
determinant matrix has rank at most two, and its nonlinear absolute-value-one
readout decides the same family using $O(m)$ bits and $O(m^2)$ bit operations.

There is also an explicit full-complex obstruction inside unknots.  Put
$G_k=(AB)^k$, $v_k=(p_k,q_k)^t=G_k(1,1)^t$, and
$x_k=G_k(3,2)^t$.  The whole closure is an unknot with $N_k=8k+4$ displayed
crossings.  Its designated rational prefix has $4k+1$ crossings and
\[
 p_0=1,\quad p_1=7,\quad p_{k+2}=6p_{k+1}-p_k,\quad
 p_k\ge(3+2\sqrt2)^k.
\]
Any full, explicitly delooped four-ended complex homotopy equivalent to that
prefix needs at least $p_k/2$ summands.  Indeed, closing each matching and
reducing on a fixed exterior marked arc yields at most two scalar generators
per summand.  Closing and applying the marked TQFT is additive and preserves
chain-homotopy equations.  Homology dimension is therefore at most twice
the summand count, whereas its graded Euler characteristic at quantum
parameter $i$ gives $\dim\Kh\ge\det=p_k$.

If the quantum grading is preserved, the stronger bound is $p_k+q_k$.
At $i$, a reduced closure with more than one circle contributes zero because
$i+i^{-1}=0$; a one-circle closure contributes absolute value one.
Numerator closure bounds one matching's multiplicity below by $p_k$ and
denominator closure bounds the other by $q_k$.  Both are knots since $p_k,q_k$
are odd.  Orientations may be chosen separately: the orientation-free bracket
complex changes only by global grading shifts, which do not change these
absolute-value estimates.  This obstruction concerns that specified prefix,
full homotopy type, and expanded summands.  It does not cover every order,
binary multiplicity encodings, or the actual arithmetic decision procedure.

For adaptive recognition the consequence is concrete: bounding geometric
frontier alone does not bound full surviving object count.  When checked
arithmetic structure is available, its decision stage should precede a full
complex.  For general PDs, discovering and composing such structures without
expansion remains a research obligation; switching between existing complex
representations alone does not prove the missing bound.

\subsection{Production validation and measured scope}
All 152 delivered tests pass unchanged.  The maintained suite has 411 tests,
including two additional resource/option regressions.  The patch hash and all
81 delivered patched-file hashes agree with the patch manifest; all 82 source
Git blobs agree with the pinned commit.  The package-wide checksum launcher
cannot run because the delivered tree lacks its \code{SHA256SUMS} file;
the narrower checks are recorded explicitly in \code{rational-source-audit.json}.
Independent reruns
against production check 1200 sources: 718 knot-PD Alexander comparisons,
482 link component comparisons, and 1436 mirrored pickle checks.  The local
audit rejects 12 forged certificates and a crossing switch, checks 40
reorder/half-turn/relabel/mirror instances, and finds no false rejection on
31 known unknots.  The identity diagnostic checks 1398101 matrix entries
through $m=10$.  The checkpoint audit constructs 21 whole diagrams and adds
14 Alexander and six independent cube comparisons.  These are finite
implementation checks; the proofs above supply asymptotic conclusions.

\code{fast/rational\_research/benchmark.py} retains seven paired rounds
with fresh prebuilt Diagrams, one-second recognition budgets, and a 20000-object
cap.  Each round measures baseline, five source-enabled samples, then baseline.
The after/before ratio is a drift control, not a separate randomized A/A trial.
Construction plus new recognition is recorded separately; recognition-only
speedups must not be read as construction-inclusive speedups.  Local patterns
use the same before/new/after protocol, with the template cache warm.
Raw data and exact source hashes are in
\code{fast/results/rational\_20261008.json}.
