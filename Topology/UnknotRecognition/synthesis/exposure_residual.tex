\section{Escaping the exposure residual and pruning overlap queries}
\label{sec:exposure-residual}

The stalled native presentation in Section~\ref{sec:whitehead-exposure-review}
does admit an inexpensive algebraic next step, although the resulting search
policy is slower than our incumbent on this example. This distinction matters:
escaping a restricted policy's obstruction is evidence about which operations
are needed, not evidence of an improved recognition complexity bound.

\subsection{A full-source certified continuation}

The offline driver \path{../fast/exposure_research/residual_bridge.py} loads
the immutable exposure delivery from its arrival commit, checks its archive
digest and all 44 manifest entries, and constructs the maintained Wirtinger
presentation of the 141-crossing Gordian fixture. It reconstructs all 131
recorded prefix moves, preserving generator names and relation slots. The
result has rank 11 and 97,684 letters, with neither a singleton occurrence
nor a unit bridge in any relator graph.

One maintained overlap query selects donor slot 32 and target slot 54,
with donor rotation 294, target rotation 2, positive donor sign and overlap
length 14,722. Applying its normal-closure-preserving replacement leaves
84,400 letters: a decrease of 13,284. There are now two singleton occurrences
and four unit bridges. The maintained compressed continuation reaches rank
one; the full certificate contains 142 moves. Independent explicit and
compressed verifiers both replay it from the original PD. The certificate
is saved in \path{../fast/results/gordian_exposure_overlap_certificate.json}.
The bridges are diagnostics of the new opportunity; the proof of the unknot
is the independently replayed complete group certificate.

This experiment answers the concrete residual question left open by the
delivery. It does not establish that a rank-first prefix is necessary or
beneficial. Existing overlap-enabled group search already solves the fixture,
and uses 141 moves. Nor is the offline reconstruction a hardened public
interface: it accepts the fixed, checked delivery's move format and relies
on final independent replay. It is not used by runtime dispatch.

\subsection{The policy comparison includes reconstruction and replay}

At checkpoint \texttt{875c8565d}, before the pruning below, we compared five
arms on fresh PD input: explicit search, an identical explicit control,
compressed search, adaptive search with a 20,000-letter switch, and the
exposure--overlap handoff. Each search has 20 million charged work units;
each of the two independent verifiers has its own 20 million. One shared
15-second wall allowance covers input construction, search and both replays.
All arms allow 600,000 replay letters. The exposure producer permits its
specified $3M$ temporary Whitehead workspace, while the incumbent explicit
producer retains its existing $M=200,000$ search-letter limit. Thus this is
a comparison of the stated policies, not identical intermediate storage.
Archive import and result serialization are excluded. Reconstruction and
the before/after bridge audits are included in the exposure arm.

One excluded warmup per arm and five shuffled measured rounds, with seed
261008113, give 25 completed positives and no capped observations. Median
times in seconds are 0.866 for explicit, 0.851 for its identical control,
1.326 for compressed, 1.031 for adaptive, and 4.478 for exposure--overlap.
The median paired explicit/control ratio is 1.018; the paired explicit/handoff
ratio is 0.193. The handoff is approximately 5.2 times slower and gains no
recognition coverage here. Its charged search work is 8,857,800, including
prefix reconstruction and graph audits. These results support retaining the
existing dispatch. Raw samples and frozen source hashes are in
\path{../fast/results/exposure_residual_20261008.json}; the production source
hashes refer to that checkpoint, not to the subsequent optimization.

\subsection{An exact upper bound on further overlap improvements}

For a donor of length $d$ and a cyclic match of length $u$, the overlap
producer guarantees shortening
\[
 g=2u-d,\qquad u\le d,\qquad g\le d.
\]
If the best already selected gain is $G$, a donor with $d\le G$ cannot
improve it. The producer replaces its current witness only on a strict
improvement, so such a donor cannot change tie breaking either. If a scan
attains $g=d$, its remaining positions, targets and inverse sign can all be
skipped. A later longer donor is still searched. These bounds are applied
before constructing a suffix automaton and inside the existing loops;
donor, sign, target and scan order are otherwise unchanged.

Consequently, with adequate resources the complete selected witness,
including rotations, is identical to the earlier producer's. Independent
certificate replay is unchanged. Under a work cap the new producer may
finish when the old one exhausts its allowance. Each skipped donor still
charges a work unit and polls cancellation. The general bound remains
$O(n+mL)$ dictionary operations for $n$ slots, $m$ nonempty relators and
total stored length $L$. For $m$ equal length-$d$ donors whose first pair
fully matches, this particular query now uses $O(n+d)$ operations on the
already stored words: one automaton, one successful scan, and slot traversal.
The earlier implementation still built both signed automata for every donor,
costing $\Omega(md)$ even after the optimum was known. No general
quasi-polynomial recognition bound follows from this local pruning.

The pruning audit swaps only the overlap query against its source pinned at
\texttt{875c8565d}; its identical old-function control detects measurement
drift. It first compares 2,000 complete randomly generated witnesses,
including signs, tie choices and cyclic positions. Six workloads then use
one excluded warmup and five measured shuffled rounds per arm (seed
261008114). The repeated-donor cases exercise the new early stop, disjoint
alphabets exercise an unsuccessful full search, and a short mixed-length
case ensures that an early winner does not suppress a later longer donor.
The two native workloads include fresh PD construction and both independent
replay modes; their full certificate hashes must agree across all arms and
rounds. Their resource limits match the earlier policy comparison. The raw
record is \path{../fast/results/overlap_bounds_20261008.json}.

All 90 measured operations and 18 warmups complete, with identical witnesses
or full certificate hashes across arms. For 128 repeated 128-letter donors,
charged query work falls from 179,840 to 1,022 and the median paired speedup
is 159.06. The 16-donor version improves by 22.15. The disjoint control has
unchanged charged work and a paired ratio of 0.986; it provides no evidence
of a speedup on unsuccessful scans. On the actual Gordian explicit search,
including both replayers, median elapsed time falls from 0.856 to 0.572
seconds, with paired speedup 1.51. The exposure handoff falls from 4.338 to
2.892 seconds, also 1.51 times faster, and charged search work decreases from
8,857,800 to 6,455,975. It remains substantially slower than the incumbent.
Identical-control paired ratios range from 0.991 to 1.040. These are local
same-machine observations on the stated workloads, not a universal speedup.

All 778 maintained tests pass with Regina enabled in 185.977 seconds.
New regressions require the repeated-donor query to fit a linear work
allowance, preserve a later longer winner, and honor cancellation and work
limits while skipping donors. Existing overlap tests independently check
maximum gains and forged certificates; the pinned audit additionally checks
the entire deterministic witness. The resulting native improvement changes
neither the recognized input class nor the general worst-case guarantee.
