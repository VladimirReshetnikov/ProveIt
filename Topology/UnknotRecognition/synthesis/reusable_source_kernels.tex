\section{Reusable matching kernels for atlas preparation and source replay}
\label{sec:reusable-source-kernels}

The profile in Section~\ref{sec:predicate-filtered-atlases} identifies repeated
exact elimination in three places: local ray enumeration, recovery of a source
extreme ray from an atlas mixture, and independent extremality replay.  This
section replaces those repeated ambient-coordinate systems by reusable
kernel charts.  The maintained producer is \path{fastunknot/source_kernel.py};
the separately implemented checker is
\path{fastunknot/source_kernel_verify.py}.  The experimental atlas adapter
\path{projective_port_research/kernel_atlas.py} uses both and retains the
complete predicate-filtered query contract.  The preserved report~113 and
the preceding research backend remain unchanged, permitting direct comparison.

\subsection{Kernel coordinates and extreme supports}

Let $A$ be a rational matrix with $n$ columns, $h=\dim\ker A$, and let the rows
of $K\in\mathbb Q^{h\times n}$ be a basis of its kernel.  Choose the basis as
a free-coordinate chart: there is a list $J=(j_1,\ldots,j_h)$ with
$K_{i,j_a}=\delta_{ia}$.  Thus every matching vector is uniquely
$x=K^{\mathsf T}z$, and its parameters are simply $z=x_J$.  Write
$a_j=K_{*,j}$ for the coordinate form, so that $x_j=a_j^{\mathsf T}z$.
For $Z\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ let $E_Z$ have rows $a_j^{\mathsf T}$,
$j\in Z$.  Zero coordinate forms may be discarded.

\begin{proposition}[Extremality in a prepared source chart]
For a nonzero $x\geq0$ with $Ax=0$, put $Z=\{j:x_j=0\}$.  Then $x$ spans
an extreme ray of $\{u\geq0:Au=0\}$ if and only if
\[
 \operatorname{rank}E_Z=h-1.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The injective map $K^{\mathsf T}$ identifies $\ker E_Z$ with matching vectors
supported in $\operatorname{supp}x$.  If this space has dimension greater
than one, choose a matching direction supported there and independent of
$x$.  A sufficiently small displacement in either direction retains
nonnegativity, contradicting extremality.  Conversely, in a decomposition
$x=u+v$ with nonnegative matching vectors, both summands vanish on $Z$.
A one-dimensional supported kernel forces them to be nonnegative multiples
of $x$.  Since $x\ne0$ already belongs to this supported kernel, its dimension
is one exactly at the displayed rank.
\end{proof}

Coordinate incompatibilities are checked on supports after this full-cone
criterion.  They are not treated as linear equations or used to declare a
nonextreme cone vector extreme.

\subsection{Local enumeration with distinct coordinate hyperplanes}

Normalize each nonzero $a_j$ to a primitive integer vector with a canonical
sign, and deduplicate the resulting hyperplanes.  Let their number be $m\leq n$.
For each $(h-1)$-subset, solve its homogeneous equations in $h$ variables.
If the kernel is one-dimensional, reconstruct $x=K^{\mathsf T}z$, orient it
nonnegatively if possible, make it primitive, and test incompatibilities.
Deduplicate the resulting original-coordinate rays.  Nullity zero gives no
rays; nullity one has just the empty candidate subset.

\begin{proposition}[Complete kernel-hyperplane enumeration]
The procedure returns exactly all admissible full-cone extreme rays.  After
one polynomial-time kernel preparation, it solves at most
$\binom{m}{h-1}$ candidate systems in dimension $h$, with polynomial
reconstruction and bit complexity per candidate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At an extreme ray the preceding proposition supplies $h-1$ independent active
coordinate forms.  Deduplication preserves their span, so one enumerated
subset defines that ray.  Conversely, an accepted line has a nonnegative
orientation and $h-1$ independent zero forms, hence is extreme.  Two forms
of opposite sign define the same hyperplane but are still separate source
inequalities: reconstruction checks every original coordinate.  Consequently
the argument also covers cones of dimension smaller than $h$.  Rational
elimination and reconstruction have polynomial bit bounds by the usual
minor estimates, first for $K$ and then for the systems in its coordinates.
\end{proof}

The previous zero-set enumerator chooses from all $n$ coordinate positions
and re-eliminates a restricted ambient matching matrix for every candidate.
The improvement reduces both the number of distinct systems and their
dimension; it does not assert a smaller worst-case exponent when all forms
are distinct.  As an exact operation example, take three independent groups
of four proportional coordinate forms.  Here $n=12$, $h=3$, and $m=3$.  Both algorithms
prepare a $9\times12$ kernel.  The old enumeration additionally solves
$66$ systems of size $9\times10$; the new one solves three $2\times3$
systems, with the same three rays.  Twenty copies per group give $n=60$
and three new candidates versus the old $\binom{60}{2}=1770$.

\subsection{Incremental descent to a positive source extreme ray}

Suppose an atlas query supplies a compatible matching vector $x\geq0$ with
$c^{\mathsf T}x>0$.  Check matching membership by
$x=K^{\mathsf T}x_J$, check the objective, prescribed zeros and conflicts,
then normalize $\sum_jx_j=1$.  Put $s=\sum_ja_j$ and compute once a
free-coordinate row basis $B$ for
\[
 \ker\begin{pmatrix}E_Z\\s^{\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Each row of $B$ parametrizes a matching direction preserving the current
zeros and normalization.  If $B$ is empty, the witness is already extreme.
Otherwise choose its first row $b$, form $d=K^{\mathsf T}b$, clear its
denominators and common divisor, and orient it so that $c^{\mathsf T}d\geq0$.
The nonzero direction has both positive and negative coordinates, because
$\sum_jd_j=0$.  Set
\[
 t=\min_{d_j<0}\frac{x_j}{-d_j},\qquad x'=x+td.
\]
All requested zeros, incompatibilities and support-exclusion clauses survive
the support decrease.  The objective does not decrease, and at least one
positive coordinate vanishes.

For each newly zero coordinate $j$, compute $v_i=a_j^{\mathsf T}B_i$.
If every $v_i$ vanishes, the new restriction is redundant.  Otherwise choose
$p$ with $v_p\ne0$, delete row $p$ and replace every remaining row by
\[
 B_i\longleftarrow B_i-\frac{v_i}{v_p}B_p.
\]
This single elimination intersects the direction space with the new zero
hyperplane.  Several simultaneously killed coordinates may be dependent;
they must not be counted as separate rank increases.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded incremental support descent]
The procedure returns a primitive positive compatible source extreme ray in
the original support.  It performs at most
\[
 h-1-\operatorname{rank}E_{Z_0}
\]
support steps, with polynomial bit complexity in the binary source, witness
and objective sizes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The update formula is a basis of the old direction space intersected with
the new linear equation.  Some killed coordinate has nonzero value on the
chosen direction, so at least one update strictly reduces dimension per step.
The row $s^{\mathsf T}$ is independent of $E_{Z_0}$: normalized feasibility
gives $E_{Z_0}x_J=0$ and $s^{\mathsf T}x_J=1$.  Thus the initial direction
dimension is exactly the asserted step bound.  At termination the supported
matching kernel has dimension one.  Positivity and feasibility were preserved
at every step, proving the claimed output.

For the bit bound, initially $B$ has identity columns at its free parameter
coordinates.  Deleting row $p$ and its identity column preserves the other
identity columns under the displayed update.  Since $K$ is also an identity
chart, the composed rows $B_iK$ have identity entries at surviving original
source coordinates.  They are therefore the unique free-coordinate chart
for the current system consisting of $A$, the all-ones row, and coordinate
zero rows.  Their entries are ratios of minors of this fixed-coefficient
system, so have polynomial bit length independently of the elimination
history.  Only the emitted direction is made primitive; rescaling the chart
rows arbitrarily would lose this direct bound.

Write the current iterate using a common denominator as $x=X/D$.  If $j$
achieves the displayed minimum and $d_j<0$, the next iterate can be written
with denominator $D|d_j|$ and numerators
$X_i|d_j|+X_jd_i$.  Each step therefore adds only polynomially many bits.
There are at most $h-1$ steps, and primitive reduction cannot increase the
encoding length.  Preparation, reconstruction and rank-one updates are all
polynomial exact arithmetic operations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Independent replay and the trust boundary}

The certificate remains the primitive original-coordinate vector.  The
checker snapshots the raw source and constructs its own chart with separate
elimination code and reversed pivot order; it imports no producer algebra.
For every witness it checks exact syntax, nonnegativity, nonzeroness,
primitive integral scale, source membership, conflicts, requested zeros and
positive objective.  Its own zero-coordinate forms then test rank $h-1$.
A bounded cache can reuse this last support-rank result, since it is tied to
one immutable source.  It never substitutes for the per-witness checks.

No kernel or descent trace supplied by the producer is trusted.  Geometry
remains a separate independently replayed predicate.  Resource caps continue
to yield an inconclusive result, and caller callback exceptions propagate,
including exceptions having the same class as an internal cap.  The local
candidate cap counts candidate hyperplane subsets, not kernel preparation,
reconstruction, output sorting or memory; it is not a total work budget.

\subsection{Reproducible correctness and performance evidence}

The focused run in \path{data/kernel-atlas-tests.txt} passes 42 methods:
29 new tests and 13 existing subspace/filter regressions.  The new tests
include 420 independent small-cone ray comparisons, ten delivered fixture
blocks, 30 coordinate/equation transformations, 600 positive-mixture descent
checks, and 1,200 rank-one restrictions checked against freshly computed
constraint ranks and identity-chart invariants.  Verifier tests cover
120 random sources, 223 independently enumerated positive rays and their
223 rejected nonprimitive doubles, and 2,023 objective/zero-face queries.
They also disable producer algebra before constructing and using the checker.
The integrated atlas adds 480 independent restricted-query comparisons and
complete positive-ray exhaustion on 60 random synchronized sources.
Large rational coefficients, simultaneous dependent zeros, malformed inputs,
immutable source snapshots, bounded-cache forgeries, caps and caller
cancellation have explicit regression cases.

The fresh 22-sector native audit makes the same 202 anchor queries as the
preceding section.  All three backends agree on 60 accepted discs and 142
completed negative sector queries.  The new backend retains all nine earlier
recoveries, with 220 linear queries, 18 rejected-query occurrences and 374
mask transitions.  Its 78 inspected query witnesses are replayed from the
original matching equations and native geometric packets, without atlas
discovery; complete comparisons use an independent whole-source enumeration
for the 112 two-tetrahedron queries and the maintained complete sector
enumerator for the larger fixtures.  These are supplied triangulations,
not diagram-authenticated knot exteriors.

\subsection{Scope of the asymptotic improvement}

Replacing the linear algebra does not bound the source nullities, atlas
dimensions, number of sectors, or number of rejected positive witnesses.
The conditional bound of Section~\ref{sec:predicate-filtered-atlases} remains
conditional.  In particular, faster independent source replay does not turn
the retained finite native workload into a complete quasi-polynomial unknot
recognizer.  The default recognition dispatcher is unchanged.

There is a separate geometric route around accumulating rejections on a
fixed source.  Burton--Ozlen~\cite[Lemma~5]{burton-ozlen} show that crushing
a connected positive-Euler non-vertex-link normal surface in a classical
knot exterior with more than one tetrahedron yields a strictly smaller
triangulation of the same exterior if a torus-boundary component survives;
otherwise the knot is trivial.  This can consume a rejected surface and
restart discovery, instead of proving a small rejection count for the
current fixed-source algorithm.  It requires authenticated knot-exterior
provenance and a replayed crushing successor; merely validating an orientable
manifold with torus boundary does not supply those hypotheses.  A bound on
complete useful-surface discovery on every successor would still be needed.
