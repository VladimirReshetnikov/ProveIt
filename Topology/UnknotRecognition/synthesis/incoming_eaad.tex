\section{Binary patch grammars, boundary observers and causal localization}
\label{sec:incoming-eaad}

Reports 76--80 are preserved in full, including their original manuscripts,
PDFs, proofs, executable experiments and proposed patches. Placement
\code{eadb83e0e} precedes the separate review recorded here. Executions use
scratch copies; the delivered source trees are unchanged. Mathematical
results are retained whether or not they improve the native recognizer.
The two native integrations are explained in the following sections.
None of these reports proves general quasi-polynomial unknot recognition.

\subsection{A rectangular algebra for whole-disc patch transfer}

Report 76 uses actual unions of discs with individually labelled, collared
input and output intervals. Every component must meet the external boundary
interface. Gluing is zero-rejecting: a sealed component or an incidence
cycle permanently prevents the whole assembly from being one disc.
This predicate differs from the distinguished-component predicate of
Section~\ref{sec:incoming-contexts}.

Linearize transfers over $\F$ and quotient by the radical of all admitted
cap tests. Substituting a fixed transfer into a cap gives another admitted
cap, so this radical is preserved by left and right composition and tensor
product. This substitution argument is needed before quotient multiplication
can be used; equality under a single chosen closure would not suffice.

\begin{proposition}[Rectangular disk-transfer algebra]
For positive interface sizes $a,b$, the cap quotient satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}(a,b)\cong
 \operatorname{Mat}_{2^{b-1}\times2^{a-1}}
                (\F[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)).
\]
Composition is ordinary rectangular matrix multiplication under this
identification, with the closure pairing given by the corresponding trace.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With one input and one output, the strip is the unit and two separate discs
represent $\varepsilon$. Composing the latter with itself seals the middle
disc, hence $\varepsilon^2=0$. For two ports, actual transfers with partitions
$000,001$ give maps $u_0,u_1:1\to2$; partitions $010,000$ give
$v_0,v_1:2\to1$. Direct cap testing gives
$v_i u_j=\delta_{ij}I_1$ and $u_0v_0+u_1v_1=I_2$ in the quotient.
The latter sum is an algebraic identity, not a physical glued surface.
Iterating the splitting yields $b\cong1^{\oplus2^{b-1}}$.
The composites of the resulting inclusion and projection maps are matrix
units with coefficients in $\operatorname{End}(1)$, proving the formula
and its compatibility with composition. The full report supplies the cap
tables and explicit maps, including the trace normalization.
\end{proof}

For a homogeneous acyclic binary grammar of width $b$, with $q$ XOR-charge
values, the feature bound is $R=q2^{2b-1}$. Cost-ordered elimination retains
original candidates, rather than interpreting an XOR basis row as a surface.
The elementary all-cap min-cost algorithm has bit cost
$\operatorname{poly}(N,b,B)R^4$, including grammar size and signed-cost bits.
A binary power of physical length $W$ takes $O(\log W)$ composition stages;
each copy may choose independently from its language. Raising one selected
witness to the $W$th power would solve a different problem. A witness DAG
of size $O(M+SR)$ preserves the selected original word and its costs.
For a nonnegative free-star objective, an optimum needs at most $R-1$
layers. This shortest-word theorem does not permit shortening a prescribed
physical span. No general geometric patch exporter or ambient/source binding
is supplied by this algebraic compiler.

\subsection{A small boundary quotient, under a checked simplicity promise}

Report 77 concerns a marked closed orientable surface $\Sigma_g$, $g\ge2$.
For a multiple $q$ of $g$, use
\[
 H_g(q)=(\mathbb Z/q)^{2g}\times\mathbb Z/g,\quad
 (x,z)(y,t)=(x+y,z+t+c(x,y)),\quad
 c(x,y)=\sum_i x_{a_i}y_{b_i}.
\]
The inverse is $(-x,-z+c(x,x))$ and a signed power is
$(kx,kz+\binom{k}{2}c(x,x))$. The product of the $g$ standard commutators
has central coordinate $g=0$, so the surface relation is respected.

For an independently source-verified simple curve, nonseparating curves
have a nonzero first coordinate: their integral homology class is primitive.
A separating curve bounding a genus-$h$ side has signature $(0,h)$, up to
orientation. Thus zero detects contractibility under this promise, and a
nonzero central value recovers the unordered genera $\{h,g-h\}$.
The general finite-quotient principle is classical, due to
Livingston and Pikaart~\cite{livingston-boundary,pikaart-boundary}; this review makes no exhaustive novelty claim.

This small observer is not a general word-problem solver. In particular,
\[
 w=[[a_1,b_1],a_1[a_1,b_1]a_1^{-1}]
\]
vanishes in every class-two observer here but is nontrivial in the surface
group. Map $a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2$ to $x,y,y,x$ in a free group and all remaining
generators to one. The surface relator maps to
$[x,y][y,x]=1$, whereas $w$ has nonempty free reduction.
Agreement among these observers cannot certify simplicity. Ordered boundary
words and geometric marking provenance are indispensable; a letter census
loses the central coordinate. Ordinary contractibility also does not certify
admissibility relative to a punctured or marked boundary pattern.

A power SLP with encoded size $S$, including exponent bits and reference
nodes, can be evaluated in $O(Sg\log^2(g+1))$ bit operations using lazy
basis-generator handling. Only $2g+1$ modular coordinates are stored, not
an exponentially large group table. Safe transport under every oriented
marking change requires common coordinate modulus $g$ for odd $g$ and
$2g$ for even $g$. Indeed a generator image must retain order dividing $q$,
which requires $g\mid\binom q2$; a twist sends $a_1^g$ to $(a_1b_1)^g$
with central value $g/2$ when $g$ is even. Thus the narrower even-genus
quotient is not invariant under all marking changes. Checking a supplied
symplectic image tuple makes subsequent cached transport algebraically safe,
at $O(g^2\log^2 g)$ per value. It does not authenticate that tuple as a
geometric change of marking.

\subsection{Exactly which fixed double-cover banks detect every separator}

Identify the character space with the symplectic space
$V=\F^{2g}$. An essential separating curve determines a proper splitting
$V=U\perp W$. In the double cover with nonzero character $\alpha$, either
individual lift has nonzero homology exactly when $\alpha$ restricts
nontrivially to both sides. When both side covers are connected they have
two boundary circles; an individual lifted circle is nonseparating, while
the sum of the two classes is zero. Testing only their sum loses detection.
Every proper symplectic splitting is realizable by a separating simple
curve, by changing a symplectic marking through transvections/Dehn twists.

For a bank $A$, put $X=\langle A\rangle$ and choose a basis $B\subseteq A$.
Join each nonbasis character to all basis vectors in its support; also join
basis pairs with nonzero symplectic pairing. Its components span the unique
finest orthogonal decomposition $X=X_1\perp\cdots\perp X_s$ in which every bank
member lies in one summand. This decomposition is independent of the chosen
basis, although its graph presentation can change.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Universal fixed banks]
A nonempty bank detects every essential separator through individual-lift
homology if and only if this graph is connected and $X$ is coisotropic,
$X^\perp\subseteq X$. The criterion costs $O(mg^2+g^3)$ binary operations
for $m$ characters. The minimum bank size is $g+1$.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
Write $d_i=\dim X_i$, $r_i=\dim\operatorname{rad}X_i$. Split each $X_i$
into a nondegenerate part and its radical. The sum of all radicals is
isotropic in the complement of the nondegenerate parts. Extend its basis
to a symplectic basis there and assign one dual partner per radical vector.
This constructs simultaneous orthogonal nondegenerate envelopes $E_i$
of dimensions $d_i+r_i$.

If the interaction graph is disconnected, one envelope and its orthogonal
complement form a proper splitting in which every character is pure.
If the graph is connected but $X$ is not coisotropic, a single envelope
of dimension $d+\dim\operatorname{rad}X<2g$ gives such a splitting.
Conversely any undetected splitting makes every character pure. Graph
connectedness forces all of $X$ onto one side. Coisotropy forces a
nondegenerate side containing $X$ to have dimension at least $2g$, a
contradiction. Coisotropy is tested by
$2d-\operatorname{rank}(\omega|_X)=2g$.

At most $g$ characters either span a noncoisotropic space, or are an
independent basis of a $g$-dimensional Lagrangian. In the latter case their
interaction graph has $g$ isolated vertices and fails when $g\ge2$.
A Lagrangian basis $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_g$ together with its sum
is coisotropic and connected, proving the upper bound.
\end{proof}

In particular, the full $2g$ standard symplectic basis is not universal:
its graph has $g$ components. The lower bound applies to fixed degree-two
covers and individual-lift homology, not adaptive cover selection,
higher-degree covers, or arbitrary curve algorithms.

\subsection{A quadratic bridge between genus and lifted homology}

The same report preserves a separate cellular/Magnus identity. Degree-two
signatures over $\F$ have multiplication
\[
 (x,M)(y,N)=(x+y,M+N+xy^{\mathsf T}).
\]
A positive generator is $(e_i,0)$, its inverse is
$(e_i,e_ie_i^{\mathsf T})$, and every signature has fourth power one.
Let $J$ have standard symplectic $2\times2$ blocks. The surface relator
is $(0,J)$; changing a word representative with first coordinate zero
can toggle $M$ by $J$.

For a separating simple curve bounding genus $h$,
\[
 \{\operatorname{rank}M,\operatorname{rank}(M+J)\}
       =\{2h,2(g-h)\}.
\]
Indeed each commutator contributes $uv^{\mathsf T}+vu^{\mathsf T}$,
giving the bivector of one symplectic summand; congruence preserves its
rank and conjugation fixes a zero-first-coordinate signature.

More generally, for any word with zero mod-two first coordinate and a
nonzero character $\alpha$, its individual lifted homology satisfies
\[
 [\widetilde w]\ne0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                M^{\mathsf T}\alpha\notin\{0,J\alpha\}.
\]
To see this, traverse its letters in the two-vertex cellular cover. The
prefix exponent vector determines the sheet of each positive edge; a
negative letter adds its own character bit when locating the reversed edge.
These contributions are precisely the contraction of the quadratic
signature. Since total exponent parity is zero, both sheet chains are the
same vector $M^{\mathsf T}\alpha$. The two lifted two-cells have the same
boundary, the duplication of $J\alpha$, so the boundary image is its
one-dimensional span. This proves the formula without expanding the SLP.
It also supplies independent cellular and quadratic replay routes.

\subsection{Causal localization of bounded tetrahedron descent}

Report 79 strengthens report 75's local-region theory for a particular
objective. Moves are relative $2$--$3$ and $3$--$2$ replacements on marked
tetrahedron incarnations, with their source legality and transport checked.
Construct the birth DAG of a trace: a directed arc joins a move creating an
incarnation to a later move consuming it, retaining parallel arcs.
Two simultaneously legal moves with disjoint consumed incarnations commute.
There is also a backward-persistence lemma: if the second move consumes no
incarnation born at the first, all its sources survive the first move.
Its internal facet, or complete three-tetrahedron edge star, is unchanged,
so it was legal before the first move. This justifies swapping those moves.

\begin{proposition}[Connected first descent]
If a descending trace uses at most $U$ upward moves, a descending trace exists
with $u\le U$ upward moves, $d=u+1$ downward moves, length $2u+1$, and a
connected original footprint of size at most $3u+3$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Birth-DAG components project to legal traces by repeated swaps using
backward persistence, and their tetrahedron losses add. Choose a shortest
descending trace within the upward allowance and truncate at its first
descent. Then $d=u+1$. If its birth DAG had several components, one would
have positive loss; its legal projection would be a shorter descending
trace, a contradiction.

For a connected birth component with $u$ upward and $d$ downward moves,
there are $2u+3d$ consumed source incarnations. At least $u+d-1$ are born
within the component, so at most $u+2d+1$ are original. Their original
footprint is connected: maintain ancestor blocks of original cells and
the bijection between current cut facets and original boundary ports.
Each connected source replacement merges adjacent connected ancestor blocks.
At the end these blocks are exactly the birth-component footprints.
Substitution of $d=u+1$ gives $3u+3$.
\end{proof}

Constructive extraction alternates projection to a positive-loss component
and truncation at first descent until stable. Each step reduces the number
of events; truncation alone need not produce a connected footprint.
A six-tetrahedron ball shows the footprint bound is sharp at $U=1$.

Enumerate connected original regions of radius $3U+3$ in the bounded-degree
dual graph, then the constant-alphabet local traces. With source-bit size
$B$, this gives a complete bounded-descent algorithm
$2^{O(U)}\operatorname{poly}(t+B)$, without a separate locality hypothesis.
A negative result exhausts descent under that upward allowance; it is not
a knottedness certificate. The report also proves a multiunit loss-$k$
bound $2^{O(U+k)}\operatorname{poly}(t+B)$ by decomposing independent birth
components and using color coding/perfect hash families. Its deterministic
splitter generator is not implemented; the executable packing routine
solves one supplied coloring.

A shared cover index avoids restarting a trace in overlapping regions:
it filters covers by the actually consumed original footprint. Full
predicate completeness requires invariance under legal projection and
commutation. Marked-endpoint descent predicates qualify; arbitrary
history-dependent or selected-cover callbacks need not. At delivery the preserved implementation remained separate from the
maintained trace/source interfaces. Section~\ref{sec:pachner-native} later
reconciles and integrates its bounded search and independent transport
checkers, including an optional source-bound diagram adapter. No universal small-upward descent or
terminal structure theorem for all unknots follows from this result.

\subsection{Fresh finite evidence and preserved proof scope}

Fresh scratch runs pass all 52, 47, 66 and 36 tests of reports 76--79,
respectively. Report 76 checks 44,168 cap entries, 4,964 rectangular
products, 41,438 trace pairings, 14,400 weighted queries, 39,926 powered
versus explicit cap queries, 855 literal pair meshes and 1,408 complete-word
meshes. Its $W=2^{1024}$ example returns $-3W+4$ with 1,025 compiled rules
and 2,049 reachable witness nodes; this is one compressed-language example.

of the ten splittings, exhausts all banks of size at most $g$ for genera
two and three, and checks 1,100 random splitting cases. The all-genus claims
depend on the proofs, not these finite counts.

Report 78's fresh audit checks 600 flow models against 418,908 boxed
potentials, 80 complete bands against 51,824 potentials, and 3,725 strata.
Its 100 constructed meshes, 800 legal moves, 1,200 surfaces and 1,360
positive-core budgets pass. All 100 old and new outputs have Euler
characteristic one, so this corpus demonstrates no added positive coverage.
Report 79 replays 124 saved covered endpoints and eighteen strict descents,
in addition to its 36 focused tests. Its much larger delivered regression
corpus is preserved, not represented here as freshly rerun.
Report 80's scratch reproduction passes 39 tests and seventeen saved
coverage proofs. Native audits, integration tests and timings follow below.
Fresh logs and structured evidence are retained under \code{data/incoming-eaad-*}.
