\section{Strict relative-boundary \texorpdfstring{$3\to2$}{3-to-2} preprocessing}
\label{sec:strict-pachner32}

A strict $3\to2$ replacement removes one tetrahedron without changing the
underlying compact manifold.  The optional preprocessing policy repeatedly
scans the current source for this particular move and accepts the first
independently checked replacement.  Its termination measure is the number
of tetrahedra.  Its topological justification is equality of two formal
boundary attachment problems, which is stronger than agreement of homology,
boundary genus, or tetrahedron counts.

The source adapter \path{reduce_pachner32_once} combines the maintained
local constructor \path{pachner_32} with source binding and independent
relative-boundary replay.  The optional flag \path{reduce_pachner32=True}
exposes this policy in the direct and factored crushing interfaces.  Its
default is false.  The new mixed chain authenticates every accepted move
before normal search resumes.  This section proves the local topology,
the preprocessing bound, and the scope of that integration.

\subsection{Two triangulations of one formal ball}

Choose five distinct formal labels $0,1,2,3,4$.  Realize $0,1,2$ as the
vertices of a planar triangle and $3,4$ on opposite sides of its plane,
with the segment $34$ crossing the interior of the triangle.  The convex
triangular bipyramid has the following two triangulations:
\[
\begin{split}
 B_- &: [3,4,0,1],\ [3,4,1,2],\ [3,4,2,0],\\
 B_+ &: [0,1,2,3],\ [0,1,2,4].
\end{split}
\]
Within each triangulation, equal formal triangular facets are identified
by their label-preserving maps.  The three internal facets of $B_-$ are
$[3,4,i]$ for $i=0,1,2$; the single internal facet of $B_+$ is $[0,1,2]$.
Both have exactly the same six boundary facets:
\[
 [3,0,1],\ [3,1,2],\ [3,2,0],\quad
 [4,0,1],\ [4,1,2],\ [4,2,0].
\]
The displayed simplices fill the same geometric ball with disjoint
interiors.  The identity of that ball induces a PL homeomorphism
$f:B_-\longrightarrow B_+$ that fixes the entire formal boundary.
A common subdivision of the two triangulations makes the identity
simplicial if an explicit common triangulation is desired.

The five formal labels need not represent five distinct vertices of the
ambient triangulation.  Distinctness here concerns the vertices of the
abstract ball before its six boundary facets are attached.  In particular,
the argument permits all five labels to become a single global vertex.

\begin{proposition}[Descent through arbitrary checked boundary attachments]
\label{prop:strict-pachner32-relative}
Let a finite face-pairing source contain three distinct tetrahedra whose
labelled internal face maps are exactly those of $B_-$.  Replace them by
$B_+$, preserve every surviving tetrahedron and its local vertex labels,
and preserve all six formal boundary attachment maps.  Then the original
and resulting quotient spaces are PL homeomorphic.  This remains true
when some formal boundary facets are attached to other formal boundary
facets of the same region.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $X$ be the disjoint union of the surviving abstract tetrahedra,
retaining their mutual face-pairing relations.  After the prescribed
internal gluing of the selected region, the old source is a quotient of
$B_-\sqcup X$.  Its remaining generating relations consist of
survivor-to-survivor identifications, attachments from a formal boundary
facet to a survivor face, and attachments between two formal boundary
facets.  An unpaired formal facet contributes no identification.  Every
relation involving the region lies on its formal boundary: all other
region facets have already been used in the prescribed internal gluing.

The map $f\sqcup\operatorname{id}_X$ is a homeomorphism before these
remaining quotients are taken.  It preserves each generator of the
equivalence relation, because $f$ fixes every point of each labelled
boundary triangle and the complete attachment maps are unchanged.
Its inverse has the same property.  The universal property of the quotient
therefore gives continuous inverse maps on the two quotient spaces.
The maps are PL after compatible subdivision of the finitely many
identified facets.  This proves the claimed PL homeomorphism.

This proof never requires the image of the closed formal ball in the old
quotient to be embedded.  In particular, identifying two points of its
boundary, two boundary edges, or two boundary facets imposes the same
relation before and after the replacement.  Selfattachments cause no
additional topological hypothesis once those full relations are checked.
\end{proof}

All vertices of both formal balls lie on their common boundary.  Hence
the replacement also preserves the quotient's vertex equivalence classes:
no vertex is introduced into or removed from the formal interior.
It follows that a one-vertex source remains one-vertex.  The natural
homeomorphism fixes the ambient boundary under the corresponding labelled
facet identifications.  A software consumer carrying a peripheral basis
or cochain must nevertheless provide and check its representation under
the new cell labels; the unmarked source packet does not contain
such additional data.

\subsection{What independent local replay establishes}

The local certificate \path{pachner-32-v1} contains exactly a schema tag
and three region records.  Each record gives a source tetrahedron index
and its four formal vertex labels.  The existing verifier checks literal
integer indices, three distinct tetrahedra, distinct labels within each
record, and precisely the three formal vertex sets defining $B_-$.  The
two new tetrahedra have fixed final positions and formal label rows
$(0,1,2,3)$ and $(0,1,2,4)$.  Surviving tetrahedra retain their original
order under dense relabelling.

For every formal facet, the boundary-signature checker counts its
occurrences.  A facet appearing twice must have exactly the indicated
internal pairing and its full label-preserving vertex map.  A facet
appearing once contributes one of three records:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an unpaired boundary facet;
\item a surviving tetrahedron, its face, and the complete map from formal
      labels to surviving local vertex labels;
\item another formal region facet and the complete map of formal labels
      between the two facets.
\end{enumerate}
The old and new signature dictionaries must be equal and contain six
facets.  In addition, the verifier compares every survivor boundary face
and every survivor-to-survivor face pairing literally after relabelling.
Reciprocity of the complete source tables checks the other sides of the
survivor-to-region attachments.  These tests establish exactly the
generating relations used in the proposition, including the case with
no surviving tetrahedra.

Both complete sources are independently passed to the finite-manifold
validator.  It checks the dense literal face records, permutations and
reciprocal maps, excludes a face paired with itself and edge reversal,
and checks connectedness and orientability.  Edge links are circles or
intervals; vertex links are spheres or discs.  The finite boundary is
connected and has Euler characteristic zero, and hence is a torus.
These checks establish the supported input and output manifold class.
They do not establish diagram provenance, and two valid sources of the
same size and boundary genus would not suffice for the replacement theorem.
The independent boundary-signature comparison supplies that theorem's
additional hypothesis.

A degree-three edge count is consequently a discovery filter, not an
acceptance certificate.  The constructor propagates formal labels around
the selected edge, but a returned construction must still pass independent
replay and output-source validation before being accepted or exposed as
partial progress.

\subsection{A complete finite greedy scan and its bit bound}

At a current source with $s$ tetrahedra, reconstruct the equivalence
classes of its $6s$ local edge incidences.  Mark an edge as boundary if
one of its incidences lies in an unpaired triangular face.  Eligible
classes are interior classes with exactly three local incidences, in
three distinct tetrahedra.  List them in the order of their least local
triple $(\text{tetrahedron},\text{endpoint},\text{endpoint})$, with local
endpoints increasing.  Try the corresponding formal replacements in
that order, and accept the first independently verified result.  If
accepted, discard the incidence table and restart on the successor.
If all eligible candidates fail, return that this scan is unchanged.

There are at most $6s$ global edge classes and at most $2s$ eligible
classes, since eligible classes each use three of the $6s$ local
incidences.  This bounds the complete candidate list without assuming
that every eligible edge yields an accepted move.  Exhausting the list
means only that this strict local rule found no accepted replacement.
It proves neither minimum tetrahedron count nor absence of simplification
by other moves, and it is not a knot verdict.

\begin{proposition}[Polynomial strict descent]
\label{prop:strict-pachner32-cost}
For a validated source with $t$ tetrahedra, the preceding greedy policy
makes fewer than $t$ accepted moves.  If $P(s)$ bounds one complete
source check and candidate construction with independent replay at size
$s$, then a conservative bound for discovery is
\[
 O\!\left(\sum_{s=1}^{t}sP(s)\right)
 \subseteq O\bigl(t^2P(t)\bigr)
\]
for a nondecreasing bound $P$.  With explicit labelled successors, the
accepted packet chain occupies $O(t^2\log(t+2))$ bits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every accepted move removes three tetrahedra and inserts two, so its
successor has exactly one fewer tetrahedron and remains nonempty.
Thus fewer than $t$ moves can be accepted.  At each visited size $s$,
there is one complete incidence reconstruction and at most $2s$
candidate attempts.  Reconstructing and sorting the incidence records
is polynomial and can be absorbed in $O(sP(s))$.  Including the terminal
unsuccessful scan gives the displayed sum.

A validated dense face table of size $s$ has $O(s\log(s+2))$ bits:
tetrahedron indices use $O(\log(s+2))$ bits, and local vertex
permutations have constant size.  The formal region proof adds three
tetrahedron indices and a constant number of labels, hence only
$O(\log(s+2))$ bits.  A fixed-length source digest and the full labelled
successor dominate the outer packet size by
$O(s\log(s+2))$.  Summing over all accepted sizes proves the storage
bound.  Retaining every source as well changes it by only a constant
factor.  It does not require storing failed candidates or a full
execution transcript in each accepted packet.
\end{proof}

For a fully explicit conservative bit estimate, take
\[
 P(s)=O\bigl(s^2\log^2(s+2)\bigr).
\]
The source validator processes $O(s)$ local faces, edges, vertices and
sparse matching rows.  Union by size bounds parent-path length by
$O(\log(s+2))$; all indices and incidence counters have logarithmic bit
length and matching coefficients have constant size.  Connectivity,
link and boundary checks add polynomial work on these same records.
The constructor and verifier copy or compare $O(s)$ face records and
handle a constant-size formal region.  Even replacing associative
lookups by linear scans fits this deliberately loose estimate, without
assuming average-case hash-table performance.  Source hashing and dense
relabelled output construction fit the same bound.  Therefore the full
greedy discovery policy has bit bound
\[
 O\bigl(t^4\log^2(t+2)\bigr).
\]
Replaying only the accepted packet chain costs at most
$O(\sum_{s\le t}P(s))=O(t^3\log^2(t+2))$ under the same conservative
accounting.  The stronger discovery bound also covers replay.  These are
bounds for the preprocessing policy, not for subsequent normal search.

\subsection{The source packet and independent acceptance}

The source-only API \path{reduce_pachner32_once(source, check=...)} wraps
the local proof in a four-field packet.  Its \path{schema} value is
\path{normal-pachner-32-v1}.  The other fields are \path{source_sha256},
\path{certificate}, and \path{successor}.  The nested certificate is the existing exact
\path{pachner-32-v1} formal-region proof; the successor is the complete
labelled face table.  The digest is SHA-256 of the complete source's
canonical JSON, with sorted keys and compact separators.

The independent entry point \path{verify_normal_pachner32} checks this
field set and digest against its supplied source.  It requires at least
three source tetrahedra and a successor with exactly one fewer tetrahedron.
It then invokes the independent relative-boundary verifier, which checks
the nested schema, the two complete finite sources, and the formal
replacement.  Neither source is restricted to one vertex.  The source
digest identifies the current labelled input; the checked relative-boundary
replacement proves the homeomorphism.

The source adapter returns \path{REDUCED} with exactly the result fields
\path{status}, \path{certificate}, and \path{stats}.  Its statistics
contain \path{initial_tetrahedra}, \path{remaining_tetrahedra},
\path{candidate_tests}, \path{moves}, and \path{scan_complete}.
An accepted result has one move, one fewer remaining tetrahedron, and
\path{scan_complete=False}: discovery stops at the first independently
verified replacement.  A completed unsuccessful scan returns
\path{UNCHANGED} with fields \path{status}, \path{reason}, and
\path{stats}, no packet, zero moves, unchanged tetrahedron count, and
\path{scan_complete=True}.  There is no work cap on this polynomial
scan; invalid sources raise the native validation error.

Neither local status asserts unknot recognition.  A $3\to2$ step always
has a nonempty successor and cannot supply a terminal unknot certificate.
The scan-completion flag describes candidate exhaustion, not minimum
triangulation size or recognition completeness.

The public adapter and checker guard callbacks with a private exception
carrier, including callbacks inside the legacy constructor and verifier.
This ensures that caller exceptions retain their identity even when their
class is also used for malformed-source errors.  In particular, the
legacy verifier's catch of \path{NormalOrbitError} cannot swallow a
guarded caller exception.  Invalid packets return false, while interrupted
discovery does not masquerade as completed candidate exhaustion.

\subsection{Versioned mixed chains, priority and shared work}

With \path{reduce_pachner32=True}, the driver emits
\path{diagram-crushing-v3}.  Its field set remains exactly
\path{schema}, \path{input_pd}, \path{source_triangulation},
\path{shelling}, \path{steps}, \path{status}, and \path{triangulation}.
The independently checked original exterior and any initial shellings
establish the first source.  Each ordered step is then checked against
the immediately preceding source under these version rules:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Chain version & Permitted step schemas\\
\hline
\path{diagram-crushing-v1} & \path{normal-ray-crushing-v1}\\
\path{diagram-crushing-v2} & The v1 step and \path{normal-boundary-unlayer-v1}\\
\path{diagram-crushing-v3} & The v2 steps and \path{normal-pachner-32-v1}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The chain checker dispatches every permitted step to its independent
verifier and rejects any nonempty successor that fails to shrink strictly.
No step may follow a terminal successor.  Its final status must be
\path{UNKNOT} precisely when the successor is absent, and otherwise
\path{REDUCED} with the exact final labelled source.  A $3\to2$ step
cannot cause the absent-successor case.  Old chain versions retain their
previous permitted step classes and reject the new local packet.

The outer recognition schemas keep their existing meanings: version~1
contains a terminal chain or sector proofs, and version~2 contains a
complete negative Euler tree.  This distinction concerns final exhaustion
data, not which local
reductions appear inside the authenticated chain.  A negative verdict
still needs complete coverage of the final source.  A partial
\path{REDUCED} chain authenticates progress and supplies no knot verdict.
The factored proof schema likewise continues to compose the required
complete child verdicts using their independently checked chains.

At each current source, the driver first attempts enabled vertex reduction,
then enabled inverse boundary layering, then the enabled strict $3\to2$
scan.  Only when none reduces does it start normal search.  Each accepted
reduction is independently replayed before append, sets the new current
source, and restarts this priority order.  In particular, a $3\to2$ move
can expose an inverse layer that is considered on the next iteration.
Every successful branch strictly decreases tetrahedron count, so their
combined successful preprocessing iterations remain linearly bounded by
the starting count.  There are no upward $2\to3$ or neutral moves in
this policy.

The driver enables the new flag only for literal booleans and defaults
it to false.  With the flag false, it selects the previous v1/v2 chain
schema according to the unlayer option.  With the flag true, v3 is used
even if no $3\to2$ move is ultimately accepted.  The enabled option adds
the diagnostics \path{pachner32_calls}, \path{pachner32_candidates}, and
\path{pachner32_moves}; their meanings are source scans returned,
candidate tests, and appended $3\to2$ reductions, respectively.

The shared \path{max_crushes} allowance now charges
\[
 \texttt{crushing\_steps}
 +\texttt{boundary\_unlayerings}
 +\texttt{pachner32\_moves}.
\]
The raw driver enforces it by the current chain length before appending
a move, including a terminal normal crush.  The factored wrapper subtracts
all three counters before each child call, including work from children
that remain inconclusive.  Disabled-option counters contribute zero.
All prior node, pivot, anchor and other search expenditures remain
charged across source restarts and child calls.

This allowance bounds committed reductions.  The source scan may already
have constructed and verified a candidate before the driver discovers
that no move allowance remains; its call and candidate diagnostics are
still retained.  Full-source validation, failed candidate attempts and
independent replay are polynomial preprocessing outside this move count.
A zero move allowance therefore does not assert zero preprocessing work.

\subsection{Fresh normal algebra and the remaining complexity limit}

After a replacement, subsequent normal search constructs its models from
the new current source.  Matching and cycle matrices, triangle potentials,
anchor objectives, ray coordinates, dual caches, learned conflicts and
candidate search states are not transported from the previous source.
Flat sector jobs and exhaustion data also restart for the new source.
The cumulative work counters survive; the source-specific search data do
not.  The relative-boundary homeomorphism concerns the compact manifold
and supplies no coordinate-cone identification.  No monotonicity is
proved for the canonical Euler functional, the resolving parameter $h$,
the conflict-size parameter, or the effort of the next LP search.
An old negative proof cannot be reused on the successor merely because
the manifolds are homeomorphic.

If the incoming source is authenticated as the relevant knot exterior by
a diagram chain, the local theorem preserves that premise for the unmarked
successor.  A standalone finite torus-boundary source does not gain such
provenance from a Pachner packet.  This integration proves polynomial
strict preprocessing with independently checked progress.  A uniform
quasi-polynomial bound for all subsequent recognition work remains open.

\subsection{Implementation controls}

The retained \path{data/strict-pachner32-tests.txt} records $267$ passing
tests across $29$ modules, with Regina available and no dependency skips.
The exact command is retained in
\path{data/strict-pachner32-test-command.txt}.  The new producer, source
verifier and driver integration contribute $25$ test methods.  They
cover genuine circle, trefoil, figure-eight and two-trefoil recognition,
source-bound proof replay with discovery disabled, rejection by older
chain versions, malformed sources and formal-region packets, and callback
identity inside legacy validation.  A six-vertex source is also accepted,
so the local checker does not impose an unnecessary one-vertex restriction.

An explicit three-tetrahedron control consists entirely of the formal
region.  Its six formal boundary facets have two attachment pairs within
the region and two unpaired ambient boundary triangles.  Its $3\to2$
packet is independently accepted.  The successor exposes an inverse
boundary layer, after which a terminal normal crush completes a supplied
solid-torus reduction.  A scheduling harness retains all three real
operations and checks move allowances $0,1,2,3$ exactly.  Only its
diagram-to-source premise is stubbed, so this harness is an accounting
control, not an additional genuine diagram-recognition experiment.

For a genuine two-trefoil input, a shared allowance of $13$ reductions
permits $12$ normal crushes and one $3\to2$ step in the first child.
The second child receives no remaining move allowance; both partial
chains replay and the factored result remains inconclusive.  A separate
complete call supplies the required nontrivial child verdict.  These
checks establish proof composition and accounting, not a timing gain
or a uniform bound for the remaining search.
