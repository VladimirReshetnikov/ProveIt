These are component-level integration decisions, not blanket acceptance or
rejection of the reports. Their prototypes target different snapshots and
internal representations. The current work preserves the later compressed
matcher refinements rather than replacing them with an older bundled tree.

The second tensor implementation is particularly relevant to adaptive
selection. It represents a scalar as a signed mantissa with a separate
valuation, removing large powers from the integer payload. Its report gives
a tree254 comparison of 130177 bits for the shifted scalar versus 1536 bits
for the mantissa. This is supplied experimental evidence, not yet a
measurement of the maintained spin backend. It motivates a direct comparison
and possibly transferring that arithmetic, rather than adding an unexamined
second default path.

The braid and causal reports provide parameterized complete subproblems.
The graded-torus report provides a claimed complete recognition bound for a
syntactically restricted class. None proves that arbitrary diagrams enter
those favorable parameter regimes. The modular observer can miss existing
obstructions with a finite prime palette, and even its completed threshold
comparison does not detect every knot. The complete general recognition
bound therefore remains open after these deliveries.

\subsection{A direct comparison of the two tensor implementations}
The maintained \path{benchmark_tensor_candidates.py} loads the second report's
\path{tensor_jones.py} directly from its original ZIP member, recording both
archive and source hashes. This is a research comparison, not a second
production backend. Four cases use one common certified order per case,
prepared outside timing. Each arm includes fresh PD construction, tensor
preparation, contraction and full polynomial decoding. The four arms are
maintained spin, an identical A/A spin control, and the report's valuation
and global-shift arithmetic. Seed 26100862 shuffles five measured rounds
after one excluded warmup. All 80 measured queries and 16 warmups complete
under the common 4096-state, 200000-transition and three-second allowances.
Every completed polynomial agrees. The weaving case uses the independent
whole-cube oracle; grids have independently checked descending certificates;
the tree case uses agreement between completed arms.

On the 254-crossing tree, the four arm medians are 365.409, 365.028,
68.833 and 278.227 milliseconds respectively. The median paired spin/valuation
ratio is 5.134; within the report's own implementation, the global-shift/
valuation ratio is 4.039. On the 144-crossing grid, spin/valuation medians
are 209.727/118.829 milliseconds; on the 196-crossing grid they are
714.712/338.190, with paired ratios 1.765 and 2.113. The ten-crossing weaving
case slightly favors the report's global-shift mode over its valuation mode,
1.146 versus 1.232 milliseconds, retaining a useful small-input control.

The tree's maintained spin counter includes its large final normalization
integer and reaches 195585 bits. The report's global-shift contraction
counter reaches 130177 bits; its valuation mantissa reaches 1536 bits.
Those are differently represented quantities and not total memory usage.
The two report modes have identical transition counts, so their direct
comparison isolates the arithmetic choice. Spin and the report use different
local tensor aggregation and turn/sign conventions: for example the tree
has 12589 spin transitions versus 15931 in either report mode. Their timing
ratio must not be attributed solely to valuation normalization.

These results justify reviewing and transferring valuation arithmetic next.
They do not yet change the maintained spin implementation. The exact
representation is $z^v m$, with nonzero $m$ not divisible by the power-of-two
base $z$. Addition aligns the smaller valuation, adds signed mantissas, and
removes all common factors of $z$; exact cancellation has its own zero
representation. This can remove large intermediate monomial padding while
preserving the exact evaluated number, including cancellation and carries.
A production transfer must retain the spin backend's phase convention,
normalization checks, budgets and independently tested polynomial output.
