\section{Formalization in Lean}\label{app:lean}
The repository directory \texttt{lean/} contains a Lean~4 library
(\texttt{BCH}, built against Mathlib) that formalizes some of the theorems
of this article. Each formal statement is designed to coincide with the
statement printed here, with no additional hypotheses; where the article's
wording needed to be made precise for this purpose, the article was
adjusted rather than the formal statement weakened. This appendix records
the correspondence and the conventions.

\subsection*{Setting and conventions}
A ``real or complex unital Banach algebra with a submultiplicative norm''
is rendered by the Mathlib hypotheses
\begin{center}
\texttt{[RCLike $\mathbb K$] [NormedRing $\mathbb A$]
[NormedAlgebra $\mathbb K$ $\mathbb A$] [CompleteSpace $\mathbb A$]},
\end{center}
where \texttt{RCLike $\mathbb K$} means $\kk=\R$ or $\kk=\C$,
\texttt{NormedRing} is a unital ring with $\norm{ab}\leq\norm a\norm b$ (no
normalization $\norm1=1$ is assumed, in agreement with
\cref{sec:convergence}), and \texttt{CompleteSpace} is completeness. The
Lean bracket $[\![X,Y]\!]$ is the ring commutator $XY-YX$ of
\cref{sec:scope}, scalars act by $t\bullet X$, and \texttt{exp} is
Mathlib's \texttt{NormedSpace.exp}, the sum of the series $\sum_nX^n/n!$.
Mathlib defines this exponential through the unique $\Q$-algebra structure
of $\mathcal A$; inside proofs that structure, and where needed an
$\R$-algebra structure, is obtained by restriction of scalars from $\kk$,
so that no extra instance hypothesis appears in any statement. When the
scalar field does not occur in a statement (\cref{prop:commuting} and the
group-commutator identity of \cref{thm:central}), it is an explicit
argument of the Lean theorem. The adjoint action $\ad_X$ is the bounded
operator \texttt{BCH.ad K X}, a continuous $\kk$-linear map
$\mathcal A\to\mathcal A$, and $e^{s\ad_X}$ is the exponential in the
Banach algebra of bounded operators.

No proof in the development is left open: the sources contain no
\texttt{sorry}, and for both parts of \cref{thm:bch} the command
\texttt{\#print axioms} reports only Lean's three standard axioms
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}, so in
particular neither statement depends on \texttt{sorryAx}.

\subsection*{Proof method in the formalization}
The article proves the exponential identities of \cref{sec:special} and
\cref{thm:campbell} by uniqueness for linear differential equations. The
formal proofs use an equivalent device that needs only the theorem that a
differentiable function on $\R$ or $\C$ with zero derivative is constant
(Mathlib's \texttt{is\_const\_of\_deriv\_eq\_zero}): an auxiliary
function is exhibited whose derivative vanishes identically and whose
values at $0$ and $1$ are the two sides of the identity. For instance,
\eqref{eq:centralconj} follows from the constancy of
$\sigma\mapsto e^{\sigma X}(Y+(s-\sigma)C)e^{-\sigma X}$,
\eqref{eq:centralt} from that of
$s\mapsto e^{-sY}e^{-sX}e^{s(X+Y)}e^{s^2D}$ with $D+D=C$,
and \eqref{eq:campbell} from that of
$\sigma\mapsto e^{-\sigma\ad_X}\bigl(e^{\sigma X}Ye^{-\sigma X}\bigr)$.
Statements with a general scalar parameter $t$ are deduced from the case
$t=1$ applied to $tX$ and $tY$. The proof of \cref{prop:mercator} in the
article is the one used in the formalization (\texttt{exp\_mlog}); the
chain rule along commuting curves it requires is
\texttt{hasDerivAt\_exp\_comp\_of\_commute}. \Cref{thm:convergence}(i) is
proved as in the article: the majorant of $\norm{Z_n}$ is obtained by running
the recursion \eqref{eq:degreerec} on the real numbers $\norm X,\norm Y$
(the Lean definitions are generic in the algebra, so the majorant is the same
definition instantiated with $\mathcal A=\R$), the generic statement
``the degree parts of $U^k$ sum absolutely to $U^k$'' gives
$\sum_n P_k(n)(\norm X,\norm Y)=(e^{s}-1)^k$, the double series
$\sum_k\sum_n\frac1kP_k(n)$ is summed in either order because its terms are
nonnegative, and the same rearrangement, justified by absolute convergence,
identifies $\sum_nZ_n$ with the Mercator logarithm of \cref{prop:mercator}.
For \eqref{eq:expremainder} the integral \eqref{eq:expdifference} is replaced
by the mean value inequality applied to $u\mapsto e^{(1-u)A}e^{uB}$ on
$[0,1]$, which gives the same bound. The closed forms of $Z_1,\ldots,Z_4$
are obtained by expanding the recursion \eqref{eq:degreerec} and normalizing
the resulting linear combinations of words; the bracket forms
\eqref{eq:z2}--\eqref{eq:z4} then follow by expanding the commutators.

For \cref{thm:ode} the coefficients $\bplus n$ are defined in Lean by the
recursion \eqref{eq:bernrec} (which is the identity $\beta\phi=1$), and
$\beta(\ad_Z)$ is the resulting operator series, convergent for
$\norm{\ad_Z}<1$ because $|\bplus n|\leq1$; the differentiability of
$t\mapsto\log(e^Xe^{tY})$ is obtained from the inverse function theorem
applied to $\exp$ (strictly differentiable, with the invertible differential
of \cref{thm:dexp}) together with the uniqueness of the small logarithm
(\cref{prop:uniquelog}), and the neighborhood of $(0,0)$ is not made
explicit. The converse half of \cref{thm:ode} is proved as in the article:
if $Z$ solves \eqref{eq:ode}, then $e^{Z(t)}e^{-tY}$ has zero derivative by
\cref{thm:dexp} and $\phi(\ad_Z)\beta(\ad_Z)=\id$, so $e^{Z(t)}=e^Xe^{tY}$,
and \cref{prop:uniquelog} identifies $Z(t)$. \Cref{cor:linearY} is
formalized in the analytic form \eqref{eq:linearYderiv}: it is the
differential equation at $t=0$, where $Z(X,0)=X$ gives
$\beta(\ad_{Z(0)})=\beta(\ad_X)$, combined with
\texttt{bplus\_eq\_bernoulli'}; the hyperbolic rewritings in
\eqref{eq:linearY} and the formal reading ``the part of $Z$ linear in $Y$''
are not formalized. \Cref{thm:poincare} is not formalized. The proof of \cref{prop:closedlie} follows the text: the
differential inequality for $\operatorname{dist}(Z(t),\g)$ is fed into
Mathlib's Gronwall inequality for upper right Dini derivatives, and no
solution of the differential equation inside $\g$ has to be constructed.

The hypothesis $\norm I=1$ of \cref{sec:splitting} is the Mathlib class
\texttt{NormOneClass}; \cref{thm:trotter} is stated with a scalar $t\in\kk$
and the product $(e^{tX/n}e^{tY/n})^n$ written with $n$-fold multiplication,
and the uniformity in $t$ is expressed by the explicit bound. The
second-order error of the symmetric splitting, \cref{prop:symmetric}, is
formalized without the multifactor expansion: with $A=tX/(2n)$, $B=tY/n$,
$W=\BCH(A,B)$ and $L=\BCH(W,A)$ one has $e^L=e^Ae^Be^A$, and the identity
$L=2A+B+T+\tfrac12[V,A]+T'$, where $V$, $T$ are the parts of degree
$\geq2$, $\geq3$ of $\BCH(A,B)$ and $T'$ the part of degree $\geq3$ of
$\BCH(W,A)$, exhibits the cancellation $Z_2(A,B)+\tfrac12[A+B,A]=0$; the
bounds of \cref{lem:tail} (with $\norm W\leq2(\norm A+\norm B)$, from
$-\log(2-e^{\sigma})\leq2\sigma$ for $\sigma\leq\tfrac14$) give
$\norm{L-(2A+B)}\leq c_2(\norm A+\norm B)^3$ with $c_2=c_0+28c_0'$
(\texttt{symmetric\_log\_bound}), and the rest of the argument is that of
\cref{thm:trotter}. The oddness of the formal logarithm and the formula for
$E_3$ in \eqref{eq:S2log} are not formalized.

\subsection*{The formal BCH theorem}
The analytic statements are formalized over $\kk=\R$ or $\C$, which is their
setting in the text. The formal statements \cref{thm:bch}(a),
\cref{thm:formalbch} and \cref{thm:dynkin} are formalized over an arbitrary
field of characteristic zero, as in the text, although the Lean proof of the
Lie property passes through the analytic \cref{prop:closedlie} and therefore
first gives only $\kk=\R$. The general case is obtained by base change, using
that all the coefficients involved are rational. The bridge is the operator
form $Z_n=\frac1nR(Z_n)$ of \cref{thm:dynkin}: since $R(a)$ is by definition a
linear combination of right-nested brackets of the generators, $R(a)$ is a Lie
polynomial for \emph{every} $a$ (\texttt{R\_mem\_lieGen}), so that identity
alone yields the theorem. A homomorphism $f$ of coefficient fields induces a
ring homomorphism $\kk_1\langle X,Y\rangle\to\kk_2\langle X,Y\rangle$ acting on
coefficients (\texttt{mapCoeff}) that fixes the generators and commutes with
$Z_n$ and with $R$ (\texttt{mapCoeff\_bchHom}, \texttt{mapCoeff\_R}); applying
it to the injective $f\colon\Q\to\R$ brings the identity down to $\kk=\Q$, and
applying it to the unique $\Q\to\kk$ pushes it up to any field of
characteristic zero. Friedrichs' criterion itself (\cref{thm:friedrichs}) is
not formalized.

The Lie nature of the homogeneous components (\cref{thm:formalbch}) is
formalized in the free algebra $\kk\langle X,Y\rangle$, modelled as the
monoid algebra of the free monoid on two letters (\texttt{FreeTwo}, with
generators \texttt{genX}, \texttt{genY}); the Lie polynomials are the Lie
subalgebra \texttt{lieGen} generated by the two generators, and the formal
component $Z_n$ is \texttt{bchHom} evaluated at the generators, homogeneous of
degree $n$ (\texttt{bchHom\_isHomogeneous}). The theorem
\texttt{bchHom\_mem\_lieGen} states that $Z_n\in\texttt{lieGen}$ for every
$n$. Its proof does not follow \cref{sec:hopf}: instead of Friedrichs'
criterion and the Poincar\'e--Birkhoff--Witt theorem it uses the analytic
Lie-series property of \cref{prop:closedlie}, applied in the
finite-dimensional Banach algebra of operators on the space of words of
length at most $n$, on which $\kk\langle X,Y\rangle$ acts by truncated left
multiplication (\texttt{rho}). The image of the Lie polynomials there is a
closed Lie subalgebra containing the images of $X$ and $Y$, hence the image
of $Z_n$; so $Z_n-L$ lies in the kernel of the truncated representation for
some Lie polynomial $L$. The kernel has no components in degrees $\leq n$
(\texttt{proj\_eq\_zero\_of\_rho\_eq\_zero}), $Z_n$ is homogeneous, and the
Lie polynomials are graded (\texttt{proj\_mem\_lieSpan}), so $Z_n$ is the
degree-$n$ component of $L$ and therefore a Lie polynomial. This route proves
the formal theorem from the analytic one, the reverse of the order in the
text.

The Dynkin--Specht--Wever identity \eqref{eq:dsw} is formalized without the
Hopf-algebraic convolution $D=N*S$: the right Dynkin operator \texttt{R} is
defined directly as the linear extension of the right-nested bracketing of
words, and the identity $R(P)=nP$ is proved for homogeneous Lie
polynomials $P$ of degree $n$ (\texttt{R\_of\_mem\_lieGen}), which by
\cref{thm:friedrichs} is the same class as the homogeneous primitives of
\cref{lem:dsw} (the equivalence itself, Friedrichs' criterion, is not
formalized). The proof is the classical one: the algebra
homomorphism $\phi\colon\kk\langle X,Y\rangle\to\End\kk\langle X,Y\rangle$
with $\phi(X)=\ad_X$, $\phi(Y)=\ad_Y$ (\texttt{adRep}) satisfies
$R(ab)=\phi(a)R(b)+\eps(b)R(a)$ (\texttt{R\_mul}) and agrees with the
adjoint representation on Lie polynomials (\texttt{adRep\_eq\_ad\_of\_mem}),
so for homogeneous Lie polynomials $A,B$ of degrees $p,q\geq1$ with
$R(A)=pA$, $R(B)=qB$ one gets $R([A,B])=q[A,B]-p[B,A]=(p+q)[A,B]$; the
statement is propagated through the Lie span componentwise, using the
grading of the Lie polynomials. Combined with \cref{thm:formalbch} this
gives Dynkin's formula in the form \eqref{eq:dynkinword},
$Z_n=\frac1n\sum_{|w|=n}c(w)R(w)$ (\texttt{bchHom\_eq\_dynkin}), where
$c(w)$ are the coefficients of the formal $Z_n$. The explicit values of
these coefficients, \cref{thm:wordcut}, are formalized in the block form
\eqref{eq:wordblocks}: the $k$-tuples of blocks $(r_i,s_i)$ with
$r_i+s_i>0$ and $\sum_i(r_i+s_i)=n$ form the finite set
\texttt{blockTuples k n}, defined by recursion on $k$ and characterized by
\texttt{mem\_blockTuples}; unfolding the recursion \eqref{eq:degreerec}
gives $P_k(n)=\sum(\prod_ir_i!s_i!)^{-1}X^{r_1}Y^{s_1}\cdots X^{r_k}Y^{s_k}$
over these tuples (\texttt{powBlock\_eq\_sum\_blockTuples}), hence
\eqref{eq:homblocks} in any algebra (\texttt{bchHom\_eq\_sum\_blockTuples}),
and reading off the coefficient of a word $w$ in the free algebra gives
\eqref{eq:wordblocks} (\texttt{coeff\_bchHom}). Applying $R$ termwise then
yields the degree-$n$ part of the all-terms form \eqref{eq:dynkin}
(\texttt{bchHom\_eq\_dynkin\_blocks}). The version of \eqref{eq:wordcut}
indexed by cuts $0=i_0<\dots<i_k=n$ with the weight $a$ of
\eqref{eq:weight} is not separately formalized; it differs from
\eqref{eq:wordblocks} only by the vanishing terms. The trace identity \eqref{eq:trace} follows from these
two theorems exactly as in the proof of \cref{prop:trace}: $Z_n$ for
$n\geq2$ is a homogeneous Lie polynomial, so $Z_n=\frac1n\sum_wc(w)R(w)$
with every $R(w)$ a commutator, and a tracial functional kills commutators
(\texttt{tracial\_bchHom}); the branch statement \eqref{eq:tracebranch},
which needs $\det e^A=e^{\tr A}$, is not formalized.

Parts (iii) and (iv) of \cref{thm:convergence} are formalized together, in
a normed space with an arbitrary bracket satisfying
$\norm{[u,v]}\leq\kappa\norm u\norm v$ with $\kappa>0$ (no Lie axioms are
needed for the bound, and finite-dimensionality is used in the text only
to guarantee the existence of $\kappa$). The right-nested brackets are
evaluated directly on lists of letters (\texttt{rbEval}), the estimate
$\norm{R(a_1\cdots a_n)}\leq\kappa^{n-1}\prod_j\norm{a_j}$ is
\texttt{norm\_rbEval\_le}, and the sum of the norms of the degree-$n$
summands of \eqref{eq:dynkin} is bounded by $\kappa^{-1}$ times the
majorant of \cref{thm:convergence}(i) evaluated at $\kappa\norm X$,
$\kappa\norm Y$ (\texttt{dynkinNormSum\_le}), exactly as in the proof of
(iii); summing gives the bounds, and the tail estimate uses the scaling
\texttt{bchMaj\_mul} of the majorant. In an associative algebra the
evaluated summands coincide with those of \texttt{bchHom\_eq\_dynkin\_blocks}
(\texttt{evalHom\_rbList}), which gives $Z_n=$ \texttt{dynkinTerm}
(\texttt{bchHom\_eq\_dynkinTerm}) and the constant $\kappa=2$ of (iii).
