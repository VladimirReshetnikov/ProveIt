# Lazard paper claim crosswalk

This file is the completion checklist for Daniel Lazard, *Solving Quintics
by Radicals*.  It deliberately distinguishes a proof source from a theorem
accepted by a prover kernel.  A row is complete only after its public import
and audit target build in both Lean and Coq without admissions or extra
axioms.

The paper cannot literally have a proof of every printed assertion: its
displayed `P22` contains two typographical errors, Theorems 3 and 4 are false
under Definition 1, the arbitrary-degree form of Theorem 2 is false under the
paper's global hypothesis `char != 2,5`, and the Section 3 assertion that the
Vandermonde resolvent is always separable because an irreducible polynomial
cannot have zero discriminant is false in positive characteristic.  For
Theorem 2, the regular six-variable action of `C6` in characteristic three
already violates the advertised bounded homogeneous freeness conclusion: in
degree seven its quotient by the
positive-degree symmetric parameters has dimension `17`, whereas the Hilbert
numerator forced by a homogeneous free module has coefficient `16`.  For the
Section 3 assertion, `X^3-t` over `F_3(t)` is irreducible and inseparable, hence
has zero discriminant, while characteristic three satisfies `char != 2,5`.
Both prover developments now state the exact repair: over a field,
`X^2-Delta` is separable if and only if `(2 : F) != 0` and `Delta != 0`;
separability of the source polynomial, rather than irreducibility alone,
supplies the corresponding nonzero derivative resultant.
The Section 4 cubic formula also divides by three, so the printed global
characteristic assumptions do not justify it in characteristic three.  The
literal Section 5 list of “the transitive subgroups of `S5`” also omits `S5`
itself; it is complete only when read as a list of proper transitive
subgroups, up to conjugacy.  Likewise, the converse direction of Theorem 1
gives containment in a conjugate of the displayed subgroup for an arbitrary
root labelling; containment in that fixed subgroup requires choosing the
corresponding resolvent root/relabelling.  The literal Section 5 phrase that
“any invariant of `C` of degree less than five is zero” on `X^5-a` also needs
a constant-term qualification: the invariant `1` is an immediate exception.
Section 5 also prints the incompatible pair `phi(T) = -U`,
`phi(U) = -T`.  Its displayed definitions instead give `phi(T) = -U` and
`phi(U) = T`; equivalently, the later Figure-3 convention uses
`U_formula = -U_printed`, which transforms to `-T`.  The developments keep
the two conventions separate and prove the corrected action instead of
silently adopting the erroneous sign.
The useful corrected statement is that every positive-degree homogeneous
component in degrees one through four vanishes; hence an arbitrary invariant
of degree below five specializes to its constant term and all of its relevant
conjugates have the same value.  Separate right-coset arguments are required
for `S5/C5` and `S5/D5`, and both literal orbit products are inseparable on the
explicit irreducible rational witness `X^5-2`; the displayed five roots are
also proved to give the exact monic factorization and to exhaust the root set.

There is a separate arbitrary-degree scope error already in Section 2.  After
fixing an arbitrary irreducible degree-`d` polynomial over an arbitrary field
of characteristic different from two and five, the paper explicitly says
that a polynomial is solvable by radicals if and only if its Galois group is
solvable.  This is a mathematical assertion, not merely a historical
attribution to Galois.  Ordinary radical towers do not give that converse in
unrestricted positive characteristic.  In the paper's allowed characteristic
three, the Artin--Schreier polynomial `X^3-X-t` over
`overline(F_3)(t)` is irreducible and separable with cyclic Galois group of
order three, but it is not solvable by ordinary radicals.  (The familiar
`F_3(t)` version motivates the example; algebraically closed constants make
the roots-of-unity step in the formal no-radical-tower proof completely
explicit.)  Characteristic zero is a valid uniform repair; so is
the usual separable prime-to-characteristic setting relevant to the solvable
  quintic groups, whose orders divide `20`.  Both prover developments now
  contain literal Artin--Schreier counterexamples, rather than merely reusing
  the unrelated `X^3-t` inseparability example.  They prove the new cubic's
  irreducibility and separability, its cyclic splitting behavior, and a
  prime-radical tower obstruction.  The focused Lean and Coq modules are now
  kernel-checked: Lean's terminal axiom reports list only `propext`,
  `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`, while all seven Coq assumption reports
  are closed under the global context.  Both registered public aggregate and
  audit graphs remain pending.

The paired Theorem 1 developments use equivalent but deliberately different
coset conventions. Lean indexes the orbit by left cosets `aG`, acts by left
multiplication, and uses the formal conjugate obtained by renaming with `a`;
the stabilizer is `aGa^-1`. Coq indexes by right cosets `G :* x`, acts by
right translation, and uses the inverse rename `x^-1 . P`; the stabilizer is
`G :^ x = x^-1 G x`. In either convention an unspecified base-field root
selects an unspecified coset, so the converse conclusion is conjugate
containment rather than containment in the fixed displayed copy of `G`.
The final Lean `paperRootTupleResolvent_hasRoot_iff_image_le_conjugate` and
Coq `lazard_paper_ordered_base_resolvent_has_root_iff_image_sub_conjugate`
wrappers derive ordinary invariance, root permutation/equivariance, and the
specialized orbit factorization from exact stabilizer plus an exact
duplicate-free root presentation; specialized-resolvent separability remains
the intended premise for the converse.
The generic specialization theorem is, necessarily, stated in an ambient
finite Galois extension (a Lean typeclass and Coq's `galois K E` hypothesis);
that is structural scope, not a caller-supplied fixed-field characterization
or root-action certificate. In the Section 5 rational-quintic endpoints the
canonical splitting field and characteristic-zero irreducibility discharge
this ambient condition internally.

The abstract `S5/F20` obstruction is also realized by an actual polynomial in
both provers.  `LazardGeneralResolventRealizedCounterexample` starts from the
explicit irreducible depressed cyclic rational quintic, gives a duplicate-free

and retains a base-field root and separability of the scalar resolvent after an
explicit three-cycle relabelling.  The relabelled image is contained in the
corresponding conjugate `F20` but is not contained in the displayed standard
`F20`.  Thus the conjugacy correction is polynomial-level rather than merely a
finite-group model; these new endpoints remain **source/uncompiled** until the
focused and public kernel builds accept them.

The checklist concerns mathematical propositions.  The historical part of a
sentence is kept separate from any mathematical content it quotes.  Thus the
attributions to Abel, Galois, Cayley, Molien, and Paxton Young, claims about
what the author knew when the paper was written, and descriptions of another
author's implementation are documentary.  The corresponding mathematical
claims used by Lazard -- existence of nonsolvable equations, the resolvent
criterion, specialized separability of Cayley's resolvent, and the displayed
`M5` Molien numerator -- are independent proof obligations and have their own
rows below.  The Section-1 suggestion that the method could in theory be
extended to degree seven is not a precise theorem until an invariant,
resolvent, branch specification, and correctness statement are supplied; the
number `120` in that forecast is only the expected resolvent degree, not a
formalized septic formula.

Section 9 itself contains three different kinds of statements:

- The existence, contents, availability, and translation difficulty of the
  author's Maple file; the behavior of Maple V.3, V.5, and Maple 8; the
  reported 27-example/1400-second/600-MHz run; the three failed symbolic
  examples; and the author's assessment of usefulness are historical,
  empirical, engineering, or evaluative claims.  They depend on an external
  program and historical execution environment and are not kernel theorems.
- The requirement that repeated occurrences of a radical use one coherent
  determination is a semantic specification.  The proof developments realize
  it with shared algebraic witnesses and branch certificates; it is not a
  proposition asserting that a computer algebra system follows that
  specification.
- The algebraic content is already in the checklist: substitution soundness
  and all-root completeness belong to the Section-7 rows; the alleged
  minimality of the extracted roots repeats Theorems 3 and 4 and is refuted
  there.  The explicit primitive-fifth-root modules now literally define all
  four nested-square values in the printed order and prove that they are the
  first, fourth, second, and third powers of the first value.  Lean identifies
  both nested square roots with their positive-imaginary complex
  determinations and proves their coherence equation.  Coq instead defines
  the second nested square root coherently as `(s-1)t/2`, proves internally
  that its square is `-10+2s`, and therefore requires no independent branch
  certificate.  Each of the other three literal values is also proved
  primitive, and all endpoints have direct public checks and assumption
  reports (**source/uncompiled**).  The separate assertion that a particular
  historical Maple output was textually equivalent to the display remains
  documentary, not a kernel theorem.
