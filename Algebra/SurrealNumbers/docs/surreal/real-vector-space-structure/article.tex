\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The surreal numbers form a proper-class vector space over their canonical
copy of \(\R\).  Three manuscripts written independently on the same day
answer the question ``what is its natural basis?'' in the same way and
prove the answer in different ways; this report prints them as one text.
The monomials \(\omega^\gamma\) are a canonical \emph{Hahn} basis (unique
set-supported, reverse-well-ordered strong expansions), the basis of every
one-dimensional layer of the associated graded algebra
\(\gr\No\cong\R[\No]\), and a Hamel basis only of the finite-support
subspace.  No set spans \(\No\); in NBG with global choice a class Hamel
basis exists, may contain every monomial, and must contain a proper class
of infinite-support vectors, with supports of unbounded cardinality.  Every
infinite support window \(A\) has Hamel dimension \(|\R|^{|A|}\), and for
every nonzero set-sized exponent group \(\Gamma\) the finite-monomial part
of \(\R((t^\Gamma))\) has codimension \(|\R((t^\Gamma))|\).

The linear structure that \emph{is} canonical is described in detail:
support projections, convex subspaces (classical), the commutant of the
coordinate projections, positive diagonal operators, finite real linear
systems, valuation bases on finite-dimensional subspaces and their global
failure, strong linear maps as Hahn-compatible column families, the strong
real dual (summation-finite weights), the vanishing of the order-bounded
dual, and the rigidity of set-indexed convergence that keeps normal forms
from being Schauder expansions.  For real exponent groups the Euler operator
\(\EuD(t^\gamma)=\gamma t^\gamma\) makes \(\R((t^\Gamma))\) modulo its
finite-monomial part a continuum-dimensional \(\R(X)\)-vector space; every
real-linear map commuting with \(\EuD\) is a strong coefficient multiplier,
and the finite-monomial part has no \(\EuD\)-invariant complement.
Results that re-prove material of other reports of the collection
(completion of Hahn vector spaces, monomial automorphisms, Euler
derivations) are printed as pointers with the sources' proofs as second
routes.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and the answer}\label{rvs:sec:intro}
\label{rvs:eu:sec:intro}

The surreal numbers \(\No\) contain the real numbers as a canonical ordered
subfield.  Consequently their additive class is naturally a vector class over
\(\R\).  This observation is elementary; the word \emph{basis} is not.  In
ordinary linear algebra a basis means a Hamel basis, so every vector is a
\emph{finite} linear combination.  The defining coordinate theorem for
surreals, however, is Conway normal form, and its support may have any
set-sized ordinal order type.  Treating those two notions as interchangeable
obscures the main structure.

The answer developed in this article is:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{The canonical basis of \(\No\) over \(\R\) is the monomial class
\(\{\omega^\gamma:\gamma\in\No\}\) in the Hahn, strong-summation, and
associated-graded senses.  It is not a Hamel basis.  Hamel bases exist only
after a class-choice principle is specified, are necessarily proper classes,
and have no comparably canonical known description.}
\end{quote}

The distinction can be summarized as follows.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.96\textwidth}{L{0.20\textwidth}Y Y}
\toprule
Notion of basis & Allowed expansion & Verdict for \(\No\) over \(\R\)\\
\midrule
Hamel basis & Finite linear combinations & Exists in NBG with global choice;
necessarily a proper class and noncanonical.\\
Conway--Hahn basis & Set-indexed, reverse-well-ordered, locally finite strong
sums & Canonically \(\{\omega^\gamma\}\); every surreal has one unique
expansion.\\
Associated-graded basis & One coordinate in each dominance grade &
Canonically the same monomials.\\
Schauder basis & Limits of partial sums in a topology & Not in the full fine
order or natural valuation topology: every set-indexed convergent net is
eventually constant.\\
Transcendence basis & Algebraic field generation & Choice-dependent; the
monomials nevertheless contain explicit proper-class algebraically
independent families.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsection{Why the repository makes this question timely}

The ProveIt repository already separates several uses of the word ``vector.''
Its report on vector and tensor fields studies finite tuples with surreal
coefficients.  Its reports on Hahn duality and Hahn spectral theory study
set-sized coefficient spaces \(V((t^\Gamma))\), strong sums, completion, and
several dual notions.  A separate report and the work of Kaplan, Krapp, and
Serra study automorphisms through Conway normal forms.  The present article
asks a more foundational question: view the scalar class \(\No\) itself as
an \(\R\)-vector object and determine which linear-algebraic structures are
canonical.

An informal MathOverflow discussion in 2013 already noticed both sides of
the issue: global choice should produce a class-sized real basis, while the
``homogeneous'' pieces ought to be real multiples of powers of \(\omega\)
\cite{MO2013}.  Conway normal form makes the latter intuition exact, but only
as a Hahn decomposition rather than an algebraic direct sum.

\subsection{Main results at a glance}\label{rvs:subsec:main-results}

Besides the central basis theorem, the article proves the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\Alph*.}]
\item No set of surreals spans \(\No\) over \(\R\)
(\cref{rvs:thm:no-set-spans}).
\item Under global choice a Hamel class basis exists
(\cref{rvs:thm:class-basis}), but every one has a proper class of vectors outside
the finite-support skeleton (\cref{rvs:cor:basis-many-infinite}).
\item Every infinite reverse-well-ordered support window \(A\) has Hamel
dimension \(\lvert\R\rvert^{\lvert A\rvert}\)
(\cref{rvs:thm:window-dimension}).
\item Normal-form truncation gives canonical complementary projections for
every exponent subclass and a one-dimensional graded piece at every surreal
exponent (\cref{rvs:thm:support-splitting,rvs:thm:graded}).
\item Strongly linear maps are exactly Hahn-compatible column matrices
(\cref{rvs:thm:strong-matrix}), and the strong real dual is classified by
summation-finite coefficient weights (\cref{rvs:thm:strong-dual}).
\item The order-bounded real dual is zero
(\cref{rvs:thm:order-dual-zero}), although the strong dual is nonzero and even
contains functionals with proper-class active support.
\item The fine natural valuation topology has only eventually constant
set-indexed convergent nets (\cref{rvs:thm:eventually-constant}).
\item Monomial-preserving strong ordered-field automorphisms are controlled by
ordered exponent automorphisms and positive real characters
(\cref{rvs:thm:monomial-aut}), while additive real-valued characters of the
exponent group yield diagonal strong derivations
(\cref{rvs:thm:diagonal-derivation}).
\item Rational independence of exponents is exactly algebraic independence of
the corresponding monomials (\cref{rvs:thm:monomial-alg-ind}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Source 52's formulation and its summary theorems}\label{rvs:w:subsec:intro52}

\wtag{} Source 52 opens with the same question.  Its text is printed here
in its own convention \(t^\gamma=\omega^{-\gamma}\) (\cref{rvs:w:sec:conventions});
its \(E_\Gamma\) and \(C_\Gamma\) are written \(\FinS\Gamma\) and \(\LfS\Gamma\), its
Euler operator \(D\) is written \(\EuD\), and its \(\mathfrak c\) is written \(\cont\).
Its section references now point to the sections of this report that print
the material.

There are two very different answers to the question ``What is a natural basis of the surreal numbers over $\R$?'' If a basis means that each vector is a \emph{finite} linear combination of basis vectors, the question concerns a Hamel basis. If it means unique coordinates in an admissible generalized series, Conway normal form already supplies the answer:
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:normalintro}
 x=\hSum_{\alpha<\lambda}r_\alpha\omega^{a_\alpha},
 \qquad r_\alpha\in\R\setminus\{0\},\quad
 a_\alpha>a_\beta\quad(\alpha<\beta),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is an ordinal. Thus
\[
 \M=\{\omega^a:a\in\No\}
\]
is the canonical \emph{Hahn coordinate family}. The support is a set, not a proper class, and the sum is a Hahn sum, not an assertion of convergence in the full surreal order topology. Conway normal form is classical; see \cite{Conway,Gonshor,MantovaMatusinski}, especially the statement of Theorem~2.10 in \cite{MantovaMatusinski}.

The interesting research is not to relabel this theorem as a Hamel-basis theorem. It is to understand what fails when infinite coordinates are replaced by finite coordinates, how much of the vector space remains invisible, and which natural operations organize that invisible part.
\subsubsection{A guide to the results}
The article has three levels. Sections~\ref{rvs:eu:sec:foundations}--\ref{rvs:eu:sec:duals} settle the basic basis question, explain size and topology, and establish useful structural results. Sections~\ref{rvs:eu:sec:euler}--\ref{rvs:eu:sec:layers} develop a more substantial operator-theoretic picture. The final sections provide examples, verification boundaries, and a research program.

For a set-sized ordered abelian group $\Gamma$, write
\[
 H_\Gamma=\R((t^\Gamma)),\qquad
 \FinS{\Gamma}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\R t^\gamma,
 \qquad \LfS{\Gamma}=\overline{\FinS{\Gamma}}^{\,v}.
\]
Here $t^\gamma$ is a formal monomial, realized inside $\No$ as $\omega^{-\gamma}$ whenever $\Gamma$ is an ordered subgroup of $\No$. The main conclusions are as follows.

\begin{theorem}[Summary of the basis and dimension results]\label{rvs:eu:thm:summarybasis}
For every nonzero set-sized ordered abelian group $\Gamma$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The monomials form a Hamel basis of $\FinS{\Gamma}$, but not of $H_\Gamma$, and
\[
 \dim_\R(H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma})=\dim_\R H_\Gamma=|H_\Gamma|.
\]
\item Every finite-dimensional real subspace of $H_\Gamma$ has a unique reduced leading-coordinate basis. The whole $H_\Gamma$ has no valuation-independent Hamel basis.
\item $\LfS{\Gamma}$ consists exactly of series whose support below every valuation cutoff is finite. Moreover, $\LfS{\Gamma}=H_\Gamma$ exactly when $\Gamma$ is ordered isomorphic to $\Z$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Proofs appear in Sections~\ref{rvs:eu:sec:dimension}, \ref{rvs:eu:sec:valuation}, and \ref{rvs:eu:sec:topology}. The term ``valuation-independent'' is defined there; ordinary Hamel bases still exist for set-sized spaces under choice.

\begin{theorem}[Summary of the Euler results]\label{rvs:eu:thm:summaryeuler}
Let $0\ne\Gamma\subseteq\R$ be an additive subgroup, and set
\[
 \EuD\left(\hSum_\gamma x_\gamma t^\gamma\right)
 =\hSum_\gamma \gamma x_\gamma t^\gamma.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q_\Gamma=H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma}$ is canonically an $\R(X)$-vector space, with $X$ acting by $\EuD$, and $\dim_{\R(X)}Q_\Gamma=\cont=2^{\aleph_0}$.
\item Every real-linear $T:H_\Gamma\to H_\Gamma$ satisfying $T\EuD=\EuD T$ has the form % ed.: source 52's bare D, its Euler operator, renamed \EuD
\[
 T(x)=\hSum_\gamma a_\gamma x_\gamma t^\gamma
 \quad\text{for a unique }(a_\gamma)\in\R^\Gamma.
\]
In particular, $T$ automatically preserves Hahn summation.
\item The exact sequence $0\to \FinS{\Gamma}\to H_\Gamma\to Q_\Gamma\to0$ does not split as a sequence of $\R[X]$-modules. In ordinary language, no algebraic complement of the finite-monomial span is invariant under $\EuD$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
These statements are proved in Sections~\ref{rvs:eu:sec:euler} and \ref{rvs:eu:sec:commutant}. They sharpen the mere observation that monomials do not span: the missing directions have a canonical scalar calculus, and no basis extension can separate them while preserving the natural Euler operator.

\subsection{Source 53's answer in one page}\label{rvs:w:subsec:intro53}

\wtag{} Source 53 gives the same answer in four levels, and recommends
terminology that this report adopts.

The additive group of $\No$ is canonically an $\R$-vector space: a real scalar
$r$ acts on a surreal $x$ by multiplication inside the surreal field. The hard
part of the question is not whether scalar multiplication exists, but what
``basis'' should mean for a proper class whose canonical expansions may have
arbitrary set-sized ordinal length.

The classical Conway normal-form theorem gives a unique expansion
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:cv:eq:normal-form-preview}
  x=\sum_{i<\lambda} r_i\omega^{a_i},
  \qquad a_0>a_1>\cdots,\quad r_i\in\R\setminus\{0\},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is an ordinal. This looks like a coordinate expansion, and it is
one, but it is not a Hamel expansion unless $\lambda$ is finite. The correct
answer therefore has four levels.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.3em]
\item \textbf{Hamel basis.} A Hamel basis allows only finite sums. The monomials
$\omega^a$ span exactly the finite-normal-form subspace $\Fin$, not all of $\No$.
Any Hamel basis for all surreals is a proper class and is highly noncanonical.
Its existence is a class-set-theoretic statement, not merely an application of
the set-sized basis theorem.

\item \textbf{Hahn or summation basis.} If admissible sums are allowed to have
set-sized reverse-well-ordered support, then the monomials are a canonical basis:
normal form gives existence and uniqueness. This is the most faithful answer to
``what is the natural basis?''

\item \textbf{Graded basis.} At each leading exponent $a$, the quotient of
surreals of leading exponent at most $a$ by those of leading exponent below $a$
is one-dimensional, generated by the class of $\omega^a$. Thus the monomials are
an ordinary basis of the associated graded object.

\item \textbf{Topological basis.} For the full natural valuation topology, the
finite monomial span is closed. Hence the monomials do not become a Schauder basis
by taking valuation limits. Coefficientwise or Hahn summation, not natural-valuation
completion, is the relevant infinitary operation.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{Recommended terminology.}
Call $\{\omega^a:a\in\No\}$ the \emph{canonical monomial Hahn basis} of $\No$
over $\R$. Reserve \emph{Hamel basis} for an algebraic class basis with finite
expansions. The two notions solve different problems and should not be conflated.
\end{resultbox}

The report develops this answer into exact theorems. Especially useful are the
support-slice dimension formula
\[
  \dim_{\R}\Hahn(A)=|\R|^{|A|},
\]
the coordinate-projection calculus, the classification of diagonal positive
operators, the classification of convex $\R$-subspaces by cuts in the exponent
class, and the closure theorem for $\Fin$.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 states its main conclusion on its title page:

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{Main conclusion.}
The monomials
\[
  \M=\{\omega^a:a\in\No\}
\]
are the unique natural coordinate directions supplied by Conway normal form.
They are $\R$-linearly independent, but they are \emph{not} a Hamel basis of
$\No$: finite linear combinations produce only finite normal forms. They are,
however, a canonical \emph{Hahn basis} (a summation basis): every surreal has a
unique set-supported, reverse-well-ordered expansion
\[
  x=\HSum_{a\in\supp(x)} c_a(x)\,\omega^a.
\]
They are also an ordinary basis of every one-dimensional graded layer of the
natural valuation filtration, and their classes form the canonical basis of
\(\gr\No\cong\R[\No]\).

A true Hamel basis exists only after a class-level choice principle is specified;
it is necessarily a proper class, necessarily contains vectors with arbitrarily
large supports, and has no comparable canonicality. In fact, the finite monomial
span is closed in the full natural valuation topology, so the normal-form expansion
is not an ordinary topological completion of finite sums.
\end{resultbox}

\noindent\wtag{} \emph{Reading of ``unique natural coordinate directions''.}
The monomials are canonical relative to the omega map, that is, to the
simplicity structure of \(\No\); they are not determined by the ordered real
vector space alone.  Source 52's shear (\cref{rvs:w:rem:shear}) is a strong,
order-preserving real-linear automorphism fixing \(\R\) that sends
\(t^\gamma\) to \(t^\gamma+t^\delta\).  Read the box's ``unique natural''
as ``canonical, given the omega map''.

\subsection{Class-theoretic convention}\label{rvs:subsec:classes}

We work in von Neumann--Bernays--Godel set theory with global choice (NBG+GC),
as is common in recent work on class-sized automorphism groups of \(\No\)
\cite{KKS}.  A \emph{class} may be a set or a proper class.  A class vector
space over the set field \(\R\) has the usual operations, with quantification
over its vectors understood class-theoretically.  A \emph{Hamel class basis}
is a linearly independent class such that every vector is a finite linear
combination of basis vectors.

The use of NBG+GC has three purposes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It permits direct statements about \(\No\) as one algebraic object.
\item It permits a class-length recursion choosing compatible bases of its
set-sized birthday stages.
\item It keeps every actual infinite sum set-indexed.  No proper-class sum is
ever formed in this article.
\end{enumerate}
Readers preferring ZFC may interpret every construction inside a fixed
set-sized surreal subfield or birthday cutoff; all local arguments are then
ordinary set mathematics.  Only the global basis-existence statement and the
proper-class conclusions require the class framework.

\subsection{Set-sized workspaces and class vector spaces (sources 53 and 52)}\label{rvs:w:subsec:classes53}

\wtag{} Sources 53 and 52 state their class-theoretic conventions as
follows.  Source 53 also allows a universe-relative reading; source 52
fixes ZFC for sets and G\"odel--Bernays class theory with global choice for
classes.  All three readings agree with \cref{rvs:subsec:classes}.

In ordinary ZFC, a vector space is a set. The totality $\No$ of all surreal
numbers is a proper class. Accordingly, a statement such as ``$\No$ has a Hamel
basis'' requires one of two conventions.

\begin{itemize}
\item Work in a class theory such as NBG, GBC, or MK and speak of a
\emph{class vector space}. This is the convention used for global statements in
this report.
\item Fix a Grothendieck universe or a Lean universe level and work with the
set-sized surreal field available at that level. Ordinary basis theorems then apply
inside the ambient metatheory. The \repo\ formalization follows this disciplined
universe-relative approach.
\end{itemize}

The elementary linear identities below are insensitive to this distinction. The
distinction becomes essential only for existence and cardinality statements about
a Hamel basis of the entire class.
\begin{definition}[Class real vector space]
A \emph{class real vector space} is a possibly proper class $V$ with class
operations $+$ and $\R\times V\to V$ satisfying the usual vector-space axioms.
A class family $B\subseteq V$ is \emph{Hamel independent} if every finite linear
relation among distinct members of $B$ is trivial. It is a \emph{class Hamel
basis} if, in addition, every $v\in V$ is a finite linear combination of members
of $B$.
\end{definition}

Since $\R$ is canonically embedded in the surreal field, $r\cdot x$ is defined by
surreal multiplication. Thus $\No$ is a class vector space over $\R$, and its
order is compatible with positive real scalar multiplication.
The entire surreal field $\No$ is a proper class. Accordingly, ``$\No$ is a real vector space'' means a \emph{class vector space}: its addition and real scalar multiplication satisfy the vector-space identities, but its collection of vectors is not a set. We use ordinary ZFC for all set-sized Hahn spaces. Statements explicitly involving a class Hamel basis use G\"odel--Bernays class theory with global choice. No cardinal-valued dimension is assigned to the whole proper class.

A set-sized ordered subgroup $\Gamma\subseteq(\No,+)$ defines the concrete Hahn subfield
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:hahnrealization}
 H_\Gamma=\left\{\hSum_{\gamma\in S}x_\gamma\omega^{-\gamma}:
 S\subseteq\Gamma\text{ well ordered},\ x_\gamma\in\R\right\}.
\end{equation}
Normal form identifies this with the abstract Hahn field $\R((t^\Gamma))$. No divisibility assumption on $\Gamma$ is needed for its field operations. It need not be real closed.

\begin{warning}[The notation $t^\gamma$]
For arbitrary surreal $\gamma$, $t^\gamma=\omega^{-\gamma}$ denotes the Conway monomial map with the sign reversed. It is not an unproved identification with $\exp(\gamma\log t)$ for general surreal exponents. We use only the monomial identity $t^{\gamma+\delta}=t^\gamma t^\delta$. The Euler construction later is restricted to real exponents.
\end{warning}
\subsection{Reverse well ordering and supports}

\begin{definition}[Reverse well ordered]
A linearly ordered set \(S\) is \emph{reverse well ordered} if every nonempty
subset of \(S\) has a largest element.  Equivalently, \(S\) admits a unique
enumeration without repetition
\[
  S=\{\gamma_\beta:\beta<\lambda\},\qquad
  \gamma_0>\gamma_1>\cdots,
\]
for some ordinal \(\lambda\).
\end{definition}

Every subset of a reverse-well-ordered set is reverse well ordered.  This
simple hereditary property is responsible for the existence of arbitrary
support truncations later in the article.

\begin{theorem}[Conway normal form]\label{rvs:thm:cnf}
\label{rvs:cv:thm:CNF}
Every surreal number \(x\) has a unique expansion
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:cnf}
  x=\sum_{\beta<\lambda} r_\beta\,\omega^{\gamma_\beta},
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda\) is an ordinal, the exponents
\(\gamma_\beta\in\No\) are strictly decreasing, and
\(r_\beta\in\R\setminus\{0\}\).  Conversely, every expression of this form
represents a surreal number.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 states the same theorem with the arithmetic
included (addition coefficientwise, multiplication by Hahn convolution with
\(\omega^a\omega^b=\omega^{a+b}\), so that \(\No=\R((\omega^{\No}))\) with the
usual proper-class qualification) and comments:

This is the decisive structural theorem. It is stated in this form in modern
surveys and in the analysis literature \cite{MantovaMatusinski,CostinEhrlich};
Ehrlich derives the same ``Conway names'' from the structure of $\No$ as a
complete simplicity-hierarchical ordered vector space over $\R$
\cite{Ehrlich2011}.
This is the classical Conway--Gonshor normal-form theorem
\cite{Conway,Gonshor,vdDE,MantovaMatusinski}.  The map
\(\gamma\mapsto\omega^\gamma\) here is Conway's \emph{omega map}.  It sends
the additive ordered class \(\No\) isomorphically onto the multiplicative
ordered class of monomials and chooses the simplest positive representative
of each Archimedean equivalence class.  It should not be confused merely with
formal use of ordinary real exponentiation.

For \(x\) in the form \eqref{rvs:eq:cnf}, define
\[
  \supp(x)=\{\gamma_\beta:\beta<\lambda\},
  \qquad
  [\omega^\gamma]x=
  \begin{cases}
  r_\beta,&\gamma=\gamma_\beta,\\
  0,&\gamma\notin\supp(x).
  \end{cases}
\]
The first notation is the \emph{normal-form support}; the second is the
coefficient of \(\omega^\gamma\).  The support is always a set, even though
the exponent class is proper.

\begin{definition}[The Hahn product over the surreal exponent class]
Let
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:hahn-product}
 \Hahn_{\R}(\No)=
 \left\{f:\No\to\R:
 \supp(f)=\{\gamma:f(\gamma)\ne0\}
 \text{ is a reverse-well-ordered set}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The functions are class functions, but each one has set support.  Addition and
real scalar multiplication are coefficientwise.
\end{definition}

The normal-form theorem gives a canonical \(\R\)-linear class isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:no-hahn-linear}
  \No\ \cong\ \Hahn_{\R}(\No),
  \qquad
  x\longmapsto\bigl(\gamma\mapsto[\omega^\gamma]x\bigr).
\end{equation}
Hahn--Neumann support arithmetic further identifies the ordered field
structure with the generalized power-series field
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:no-hahn-field}
  \No=\R((\omega^{\No})).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
 \omega^\gamma\omega^\delta=\omega^{\gamma+\delta},
\]
and multiplication of two normal forms is the convolution of their
coefficients.  The support theorem guarantees that each output coefficient
is a finite sum and that the resulting support is reverse well ordered
\cite{Hahn,Neumann,Gonshor}.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 states the identification
\eqref{rvs:eq:no-hahn-linear} as a theorem with a converse, and draws two
consequences.

\begin{theorem}[Restricted class-product model]\label{rvs:cv:thm:coordinate-model}
The coefficient map
\[
  C:\No\longrightarrow\R^{\No},\qquad C(x)=(c_a(x))_{a\in\No},
\]
is an injective $\R$-linear class map. Its image is exactly the class of functions
$c:\No\to\R$ whose support is a set and is reverse well ordered. Under this
identification, addition and scalar multiplication are pointwise.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normal form gives the support condition, existence, and uniqueness. Coefficientwise
addition and multiplication by a real constant give linearity. Conversely, if
$c$ has set-sized reverse-well-ordered support, enumerate that support in decreasing
order and apply the converse clause of \cref{rvs:cv:thm:CNF}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Coefficients separate points]\label[corollary]{rvs:cv:cor:coeff-separate}
For $x,y\in\No$,
\[
  x=y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c_a(x)=c_a(y)\ \text{for every }a\in\No.
\]
Each $c_a:\No\to\R$ is an $\R$-linear functional.
\end{corollary}

Normal form immediately gives the familiar decomposition

\end{equation}
where $x_{>0}$ is purely infinite and $x_{<0}$ is infinitesimal. In vector-space

\end{equation}

\subsection{Strong Hahn summation}\label{rvs:subsec:strong-sum}

Normal form is not an ordinary finite sum and, as \cref{rvs:sec:topology} will
show, it is not generally a topological limit of finite partial sums.  The
appropriate operation is strong Hahn summation.

\begin{definition}[Hahn-summable family]\label[definition]{rvs:def:hahn-summable}
\label[definition]{rvs:cv:def:strong-sum}
Let \(I\) be a set and \((x_i)_{i\in I}\) a family in \(\No\).  The family is
\emph{Hahn summable}, or \emph{strongly summable}, if
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\bigcup_{i\in I}\supp(x_i)\) is reverse well ordered, and
\item for every exponent \(\gamma\), only finitely many \(i\) have
\([\omega^\gamma]x_i\ne0\).
\end{enumerate}
Its strong sum is
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:strong-sum}
 \sH_{i\in I}x_i
 =\sum_{\gamma}
   \left(\sum_{i\in I}[\omega^\gamma]x_i\right)\omega^\gamma,
\end{equation}
where the inner sum is finite.
\end{definition}

This definition is purely algebraic and support-controlled.  It is invariant
under reindexing of the family and is associative under the usual flattening
hypotheses.  In particular, every normal form is the strong sum of its
monomial terms:
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:normal-strong}
 x=\sH_{\gamma\in\supp(x)}
     [\omega^\gamma]x\,\omega^\gamma.
\end{equation}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 gives the same definition and adds:

This is an algebraic summability notion, not convergence in the natural valuation
topology. It is the notion implemented for actual surreal sign sequences in the
\repo\ strong-summation development; see \cref{rvs:cv:sec:proveit}.

\subsubsection*{The same rule in the \texorpdfstring{\(t\)}{t}-convention (source 52)}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 states \cref{rvs:def:hahn-summable} for Hahn
series in \(t\)-exponents, where joint supports are well ordered, and defines
strong maps (\cref{rvs:def:strong-map}) in the same breath.

\begin{definition}[Strong Hahn summability]\label[definition]{rvs:eu:def:summable}
A set-indexed family $(x_i)_{i\in I}$ of Hahn series is summable if
\[
 S=\bigcup_{i\in I}\supp_t(x_i)
\]
is well ordered and, for every $\gamma$, only finitely many $i$ satisfy $(x_i)_\gamma\ne0$. Its sum is the series with coefficient $\sum_i(x_i)_\gamma$ at $\gamma$. A real-linear map is \emph{strong} if it takes every summable family to a summable family and commutes with its sum.
\end{definition}
This is the standard generalized-series convention underlying strong additivity in surreal differential algebra \cite{BerarducciMantova}. A subfamily is still summable, and a regrouping is legitimate whenever the same joint-support and finite-occurrence conditions hold.

Every vector has the summable monomial decomposition
\[
 x=\hSum_{\gamma\in\supp_t x}x_\gamma t^\gamma.
\]
This statement includes the support restriction. It does not license arbitrary real coefficient functions on the entire exponent group. For example, $\{1/n:n\ge1\}$ is not well ordered in its usual order, so $\sum_{n\ge1}t^{1/n}$ is not a Hahn series. In contrast, $\{-1/n:n\ge1\}$ is well ordered and $\sum_{n\ge1}t^{-1/n}$ is a valid Hahn series.
\begin{example}[A geometric normal form]\label[example]{rvs:ex:geometric}
The reverse-well-ordered set \(\{0,-1,-2,\ldots\}\) supports the identity
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:geometric}
  \frac{1}{1-\omega^{-1}}
  =1+\omega^{-1}+\omega^{-2}+\cdots
  =\sH_{n<\omega}\omega^{-n}.
\end{equation}
The right side is a legitimate surreal normal form.  It is not a finite
linear combination of monomials.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[What is not allowed]
The class-indexed expression \(\sum_{\gamma\in\No}\omega^\gamma\) is not a
surreal number: its support is a proper class.  Likewise, a set-supported
formal expression whose support is not reverse well ordered is not a Conway
normal form.  The word ``transfinite'' therefore does not mean that arbitrary
class sums are permitted.
\end{example}

\subsection{Leading exponent, valuation, and Archimedean scale}

For \(x\ne0\), let
\[
 \lead(x)=\max\supp(x),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{lc}(x)=[\omega^{\lead(x)}]x.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:leading-factor}
 x=\operatorname{lc}(x)\,\omega^{\lead(x)}(1+\varepsilon),
 \qquad \abs{\varepsilon}<1/n\quad(n\in\N_{>0}),
\end{equation}
so \(\varepsilon\) is infinitesimal.  The sign of \(x\) is the sign of its
leading real coefficient, and the monomial \(\omega^{\lead(x)}\) is the
simplest positive representative of its Archimedean magnitude.

We use the additive natural valuation
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:natural-valuation}
 v(x)=-\lead(x)\quad(x\ne0),\qquad v(0)=+\infty.
\end{equation}
Thus larger values mean smaller surreal magnitude.  This sign convention
agrees with writing the formal Hahn variable as \(t^\gamma=\omega^{-\gamma}\).

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 adds: its residue field is \(\R\) and its value
group is the additive ordered class \(\No\).

\subsection{Four basis notions}

For a set-sized vector space, ``basis'' normally means Hamel basis.  In
non-Archimedean analysis and transseries, one also encounters topological,
orthogonal, and strong-summation bases.  The distinctions are essential here.

\begin{definition}[Hamel and Hahn bases]
Let \(V\) be a class vector space over \(\R\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \emph{Hamel class basis} \(B\) is linearly independent and every
\(x\in V\) is a unique finite sum \(\sum_{b\in F}r_b b\), with \(F\subset B\)
finite.
\item A \emph{Hahn basis} indexed by a linearly ordered class \(\Gamma\) is a
family \((e_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\) together with a specified strong
summation rule such that every vector has a unique expansion
\[
 x=\sH_{\gamma\in S}r_\gamma e_\gamma
\]
with \(S\) a set in the admissible support class.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

A Hahn basis is not a weakened Hamel basis.  It is a basis for a richer
algebraic object: a vector space equipped with distinguished infinite sums.
Likewise, a Schauder basis requires a topology and convergence of partial
sums, a condition independent of formal Hahn summability.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 gives the same definition of a Hahn basis (as
``local terminology'') and remarks:

The term is descriptive; closely related notions in the literature include Hahn
groups, Hahn spaces, generalized series, and summable families.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 separates three meanings:

A \emph{Hamel basis} gives unique finite combinations. A \emph{Hahn basis}, as the term is used here, gives unique admissible Hahn expansions. A \emph{valuation basis} is a Hamel basis for which no finite linear combination suffers leading-value cancellation. These requirements are not interchangeable. Neither is a Hahn basis automatically a Schauder basis: that concept requires a specified topology and a convergence statement.

Conway's monomials are natural because each positive Archimedean class has a simplest representative. The simplicity relation is extra structure beyond the ordered real-vector-space reduct. Section~\ref{rvs:eu:sec:naturality} makes this qualification precise.
\begin{theorem}[The canonical Conway--Hahn basis]\label{rvs:thm:hahn-basis}
\label{rvs:cv:thm:hahn-basis}
The monomial class
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:monomial-class}
  \M=\{\omega^\gamma:\gamma\in\No\}
\end{equation}
is a canonical Hahn basis of \(\No\) over \(\R\).  More precisely, the map
\[
 \Hahn_{\R}(\No)\longrightarrow\No,
 \qquad
 f\longmapsto\sH_{\gamma\in\supp(f)}f(\gamma)\omega^\gamma
\]
is an \(\R\)-linear class isomorphism, and its coordinate expansion is
unique.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Existence and uniqueness are exactly \cref{rvs:thm:cnf}.  Linearity follows from
coefficientwise addition and scalar multiplication.  Every support involved
is a reverse-well-ordered set, and no class-indexed sum is taken.
\end{proof}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 proves the same theorem, phrased as: for every
\(x\) the monomial family \((c_a(x)\omega^a)_{a\in\supp(x)}\) is strongly
summable with Hahn sum \(x\), and this representation is unique.  It adds:

\begin{remark}
This theorem is the mathematically natural positive answer to the question in the
title. It says that $\No$ is not merely analogous to a coordinate space: it has
canonical coordinate functionals $c_a$ and canonical coordinate vectors $\omega^a$.
What changes from elementary linear algebra is the admissible summation law.
\end{remark}
The theorem answers the user's basis question in the sense best adapted to
surreal arithmetic.  It also explains why the answer is useful rather than
merely formal: the basis vectors multiply by addition of their indices, order
is read from the first nonzero coordinate, and every truncation of an
expansion is again a surreal.

\subsection{The finite-support skeleton}\label{rvs:subsec:finite-skeleton}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:fin-def}
 \Fin=\{x\in\No:\supp(x)\text{ is finite}\}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:direct-vs-hahn}
 \Fin=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\No}\R\omega^\gamma
 \quad\subsetneq\quad
 \No=\classprod_{\gamma\in\No}\R\omega^\gamma.
\end{equation}
The symbol on the right denotes the Hahn product: set-supported,
reverse-well-ordered coordinate families, not the unrestricted direct
product.


\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 introduces the same subspace as a span:

\begin{definition}[Finite monomial span]
Let
\[
  \M=\{\omega^a:a\in\No\},\qquad
  \Fin=\Span_{\R}(\M).
\]
Thus $\Fin$ consists exactly of surreal numbers with finite support.
\end{definition}

As a class algebra,
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:cv:eq:group-algebra}
  \Fin\cong\R[\No],
\end{equation}
the group algebra of the additive exponent class, interpreted as finite formal
sums. It is a subring but not a field.
\begin{proposition}[Where the monomials are a Hamel basis]
\label[proposition]{rvs:w:prop:monomial-hamel}\label[proposition]{rvs:cv:prop:monomial-independence}
The class \(\M\) is a Hamel class basis of \(\Fin\), and it is real-linearly
independent in \(\No\).  It does not Hamel-span \(\No\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A finite-support normal form is, by definition, a finite real linear
combination of distinct monomials, and uniqueness of normal form proves
linear independence.  Strictness follows from \cref{rvs:ex:geometric}, whose
support is infinite.
\end{proof}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 proves the same proposition with the same
argument, and adds:

\begin{corollary}
No Hamel basis of $\No$ can consist entirely of monomials, or even entirely of
finite-support surreals.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A finite combination of finite-support vectors again has finite support.
\end{proof}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 proves the version for every set-sized Hahn
workspace \(H_\Gamma\):

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness and finite-span obstruction]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:hahnnotHamel}
The monomials are linearly independent over $\R$, and their finite linear span is exactly the finite-support subspace. When $\Gamma\ne0$, this subspace is proper in $H_\Gamma$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In a finite relation between distinct monomials, each coefficient is a distinct normal-form coefficient and must vanish. A finite combination has finite support, and conversely any finite-support series is such a combination. Choose $\delta>0$ in $\Gamma$. The support $\{n\delta:n\ge0\}$ is well ordered, and
\[
 \hSum_{n\ge0}t^{n\delta}=\frac1{1-t^\delta}
\]
has infinitely many nonzero coefficients. The equality follows by multiplying by $1-t^\delta$ and canceling coefficients; the required product and sum are Hahn-admissible.
\end{proof}
In particular, with $t=\omega^{-1}$,
\[
 \frac1{1-\omega^{-1}}=1+\omega^{-1}+\omega^{-2}+\cdots
\]
is already a counterexample to the claim that the Conway monomials are a Hamel basis. Notice how elementary the vector is: rationality as a \emph{field expression} does not imply a finite real-linear monomial expansion.
Thus the most natural algebraic direct sum is not the whole surreal field.
Passing from \(\Fin\) to \(\No\) is analogous to passing from polynomials to a
Hahn-series field, not merely adjoining more basis vectors.

\begin{remark}[Why ``all Archimedean lines'' is not a direct-sum answer]
Each canonical line \(\R\omega^\gamma\) is Archimedean as an ordered additive
group, but \(\No\) is not their algebraic direct sum.  Moreover, there are
many nonhomogeneous one-dimensional real subspaces such as
\(\R(\omega+1)\).  The omega map is what canonically selects one pure line per
Archimedean scale.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Why the monomial basis is natural}\label{rvs:subsec:naturality}

The word ``natural'' can conceal taste.  Here it is supported by five precise
properties.

\begin{principle}[Simplicity normalization]
For every Archimedean class of positive surreals,
\(\omega^\gamma\) is its unique simplest positive representative.  Hence the
basis vectors are selected by the defining simplicity hierarchy of surreal
numbers, not by an external well ordering.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Multiplicative grading]
The index law
\[
 \omega^\gamma\omega^\delta=\omega^{\gamma+\delta}
\]
turns the additive exponent class into the multiplicative monomial class.
The basis therefore interacts exactly with the field multiplication.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Leading-coordinate order]
For nonzero \(x\), the sign and Archimedean scale of \(x\) are determined by
the coefficient and index of its largest supported monomial.  Thus the basis
is adapted to both the total order and the natural valuation.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Truncation stability]
Every subset of a reverse-well-ordered support is reverse well ordered.
Consequently every coordinate truncation of a surreal normal form is again a
surreal number.
\end{principle}

\begin{principle}[Strong uniqueness]
The expansion remains unique even though arbitrary set-sized ordinal lengths
are allowed.  This is substantially stronger than a nonunique asymptotic
expansion.
\end{principle}

No arbitrary Hamel basis supplied by choice is known to enjoy this package.
Indeed, a Hamel basis must somehow encode infinite-support normal forms into
individual basis vectors, destroying the one-monomial-per-scale geometry.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 makes the qualification precise: the simplicity
relation is extra structure beyond the ordered real-vector-space reduct, and
a shear fixing \(\R\) moves monomials while preserving order, leading values
and leading coefficients (\cref{rvs:w:rem:shear}).

\subsection{Changing the scalar field}\label{rvs:subsec:scalar-change}

The special role of \(\R\) can also be characterized by coefficient
normalization.

\begin{proposition}[Hahn bases over smaller coefficient fields]
Let \(k\subseteq\R\) be a subfield and let \(C\) be a Hamel basis of \(\R\)
over \(k\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:k-hahn-basis}
  \{c\omega^\gamma:c\in C,\ \gamma\in\No\}
\end{equation}
is a Hahn basis of \(\No\) over \(k\): every surreal has a unique strongly
summable expansion in these vectors, with finitely many \(C\)-coordinates at
each exponent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write each real normal-form coefficient \(r_\gamma\) uniquely as a finite
\(k\)-linear combination of elements of \(C\).  Replacing
\(r_\gamma\omega^\gamma\) by those finitely many terms preserves the union of
exponent supports and local finiteness.  Conversely, collecting the finitely
many terms at each exponent recovers the unique real coefficient and then the
unique Conway normal form.
\end{proof}

Over \(\R\), the coefficient basis is canonically \(\{1\}\).  Over \(\Q\),
for example, an additional highly noncanonical Hamel basis of \(\R\) is
unavoidable.  This is another sense in which \(\R\) is the natural scalar
field for the monomial coordinates.

\subsection{A first warning about topology}

It is tempting to enumerate the support in decreasing order and call the
normal form the limit of its finite initial sums.  In ordinary Hahn fields
this may work for selected supports and selected valuation topologies, but it
fails globally for \(\No\).  The full reason is proved in
\cref{rvs:sec:topology}; already the geometric series \eqref{rvs:eq:geometric} is not
the limit of its ordinary partial sums in the full natural valuation topology,
because the tail valuations \(1,2,3,\ldots\) never exceed the surreal
threshold \(\omega\).  Strong summation, not sequential convergence, is the
primitive operation.
\section{Hamel bases, class size, and local dimensions}\label{rvs:sec:hamel}
\label{rvs:eu:sec:dimension}

\subsection{No set-sized family can span}\label{rvs:subsec:no-set-span}

The first obstruction to ordinary linear algebra is stronger than
infinite-dimensionality.

\begin{theorem}[No set spans \(\No\)]\label{rvs:thm:no-set-spans}
\label{rvs:cv:thm:no-set-spans}
If \(S\subseteq\No\) is a set, then
\[
  \Span_{\R}(S)\ne\No.
\]
More precisely, there is an exponent \(\gamma\in\No\) such that
\(\omega^\gamma\notin\Span_{\R}(S)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The union
\[
  U=\bigcup_{x\in S}\supp(x)
\]
is a set, by Replacement and Union.  The support of every finite real linear
combination of members of \(S\) is contained in \(U\), since addition and
scalar multiplication create no new exponents.  The class \(\No\) is proper,
so choose \(\gamma\notin U\).  Then \(\omega^\gamma\) has support
\(\{\gamma\}\) and cannot lie in the span.
\end{proof}


\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 53; source 52 gives the same argument in \cref{rvs:eu:prop:classbasis})]

If $B$ is a set, the collection of finite sequences from $B$, finite sequences
from $\R$, and their evaluated linear combinations is a set by Replacement and
Union. Hence $\Span_{\R}(B)$ is a set. It cannot equal the proper class $\No$.
\end{proof}

This argument is stronger than saying merely that the dimension is ``infinite.''
There is no set cardinal that can be the dimension of the entire class.
\begin{corollary}\label[corollary]{rvs:cor:basis-proper}
Every Hamel basis of \(\No\) over \(\R\), if one is available in the chosen
class theory, is a proper class.  In particular, \(\No\) has no cardinal-valued
Hamel dimension in the ordinary set-theoretic sense.
\end{corollary}

The proof is support-theoretic and does not use birthdays, saturation, or
field multiplication.  It applies to many proper-class Hahn spaces.

\subsection{Existence with global choice}\label{rvs:subsec:basis-existence}

The ordinary vector-space basis theorem applies only to sets.  Nevertheless,
NBG+GC supplies a class analogue by a transparent stage construction.

For an ordinal \(\alpha\), let \(\No_{<\alpha}\) be the set of surreal
numbers born before day \(\alpha\), and put
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:Valpha}
  V_\alpha=\Span_{\R}(\No_{<\alpha}).
\end{equation}
Each \(V_\alpha\) is a set-sized real vector space,
\(V_\alpha\subseteq V_\beta\) for \(\alpha<\beta\), and
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:union-Va}
  \No=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\On}V_\alpha.
\end{equation}
At a limit ordinal \(\lambda\), every finite linear combination of elements
born below \(\lambda\) already occurs at some earlier stage, so
\(V_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}V_\alpha\).

\begin{theorem}[Existence of a Hamel class basis]\label{rvs:thm:class-basis}
In NBG with global choice, \(\No\) has a Hamel class basis over \(\R\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Construct bases \(B_\alpha\) by transfinite class recursion.  Start with the
empty basis of \(V_0\).  At a successor stage, use the ordinary basis-extension
theorem inside the set \(V_{\alpha+1}\) to extend \(B_\alpha\) to a basis
\(B_{\alpha+1}\) of \(V_{\alpha+1}\).  Global choice permits these extensions
to be selected uniformly through all ordinals.  At a limit \(\lambda\), set
\(B_\lambda=\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}B_\alpha\); this is a basis of
\(V_\lambda\) because every vector and every finite dependence occurs at an
earlier stage.  Finally let \(B=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\On}B_\alpha\).  Every
surreal lies in some \(V_\alpha\) and has a finite \(B_\alpha\)-expansion,
while every finite dependence among members of \(B\) occurs in one stage.
Thus \(B\) is a Hamel class basis.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[A Hamel basis may contain all monomials]
\label[proposition]{rvs:prop:basis-containing-monomials}
\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:classbasis}\label[proposition]{rvs:cv:prop:class-basis-existence}
In NBG with global choice, the linearly independent class \(\M\) extends to
a Hamel class basis \(B\) of \(\No\).  Every such extension has a proper
class of additional, infinite-support vectors.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Global choice gives a class enumeration
\((x_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\On}\) of \(\No\setminus\M\).  Recursively build a
set \(C_\alpha\subseteq\{x_\beta:\beta<\alpha\}\): at stage \(\alpha\), add
\(x_\alpha\) exactly when it is not in the finite real span of
\(\M\cup C_\alpha\).  Each stage adds at most one vector, so the previously
added part is a set and elementary transfinite recursion applies.  Put
\(C=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\On}C_\alpha\) and \(B=\M\cup C\).

A finite dependence in \(B\) would have a last-added member of \(C\), which
was added only because it was outside the span of all earlier members and
\(\M\); hence \(B\) is independent.  Every enumerated vector was either
added or already in the span at its stage, so \(B\) spans \(\No\).  Finally,
\cref{rvs:cor:basis-many-infinite} shows that \(B\setminus\Fin\), and therefore
also the supplement beyond the monomials, is a proper class.
\end{proof}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 states, as its proposition ``What choice does
and does not provide'':
\begin{quote}

Every set-sized real vector space has a Hamel basis under the axiom of choice, and every linearly independent subset extends to one. No set of surreal numbers spans $\No$ over $\R$. In class theory with global choice, there is a class Hamel basis of $\No$ containing all Conway monomials.
\end{quote}
The first clause is the ordinary basis theorem and the second is
\cref{rvs:thm:no-set-spans}.  Source 52 works in G\"odel--Bernays class theory
with global choice, the same foundation as above, and proves the last clause
differently.
\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 52), by a set-like global well-order]

The set-sized assertion is the usual maximal-independent-set argument. If $S$ is a set, its real linear span is the image of the set of finite lists from $\R\times S$ under finite summation, and is therefore a set. It cannot equal $\No$.

For the last assertion, fix a set-like global well-order of the surreal class: every initial segment is a set. Traverse this order and select a surreal $x$ exactly when
\[
 x\notin\Span_\R(\M\cup C_{<x}),
\]
where $C_{<x}$ is the set of previously selected elements. Membership in this span means existence of a \emph{finite} combination and is an elementary formula with the class parameter $\M$. On each set initial segment, ordinary transfinite recursion produces a unique selection; these constructions agree on overlaps and hence define a class $C$. Each skipped element is spanned, and each selected element enlarges the span. Every finite relation in $\M\cup C$ is contradicted by its last selected member, or by independence of $\M$ if it has none. Thus $\M\cup C$ is a class Hamel basis.
\end{proof}

This construction depends on a global well-order and is not a canonical coordinate formula. It also does not provide an algorithm for computing the finite Hamel expansion of a given surreal. The set-like construction is explained to avoid an invalid application of set-sized Zorn's lemma to a proper class.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 proves the same statement by staged Hahn fields.
It writes:

The set-sized theorem ``every vector space has a basis'' does not automatically
produce a basis for a proper class. A clean positive result is available once the
class-recursion principle is stated explicitly.

\begin{quote}
\emph{Source 53's statement.}

Assume a class theory with global choice and enough elementary transfinite recursion
to make coherent choices through all ordinals (for example, MK with global choice,
or GBC augmented by a suitable ETR principle). Then $\No$ has a class Hamel basis.
Moreover, one can construct such a basis containing every monomial $\omega^a$.
\end{quote}
\noindent\wtag{} \emph{Correction of the hypothesis.}  ``MK with global
choice, or GBC augmented by a suitable ETR principle'' is more than the
construction needs.  In GBC (NBG with global choice) the recursion below is
set-valued with a class parameter: every stage \(B_\alpha\) is a set.  NBG
proves such recursions, and global choice selects the extensions, so
neither MK nor an ETR principle is needed.  This is the foundation of
\cref{rvs:thm:class-basis} and of the two proofs above; the repository
uses the same principle (\path{surcomplex/trigonometry}, its set-valued
class recursion).  Source 53's construction is kept as a third route, and
its question on the weakest principle is re-scoped in
\cref{rvs:w:q:foundations}.
\begin{proof}[Third proof (source 53), by staged Hahn fields]

For each ordinal $\alpha$, let $\Gamma_\alpha$ be the set-sized additive subgroup
of $\No$ generated by the exponents born before $\alpha$, and let
\[
  W_\alpha=\R((\omega^{\Gamma_\alpha}))\subseteq\No.
\]
Each $W_\alpha$ is a set-sized real vector space, the chain is increasing, and
$\bigcup_{\alpha\in\On}W_\alpha=\No$: the support of any one surreal is a set,
so the birthdays of its exponents have an ordinal bound.

Use class recursion to choose nested bases $B_\alpha$ of $W_\alpha$. At successor
stages, first adjoin all newly available monomials; their distinct new exponents
make them independent modulo the previous Hahn field. Extend this independent set
to a basis by the ordinary set-sized basis theorem. At a limit stage, the union of
the earlier bases is independent in $W_\alpha$ and may again be extended to a basis.
The class union $B=\bigcup_\alpha B_\alpha$ is independent and spans every
$W_\alpha$, hence all of $\No$, and contains all monomials.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proposition is an existence statement, not a naturality statement. The extension
steps make vast arbitrary choices. Conway normal form does not single out the added
basis vectors, and two such Hamel bases need not bear any useful relation to the
order, valuation, multiplication, simplicity hierarchy, or exponent symmetries.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}[Existence is not naturality]
The construction makes class-many arbitrary extension choices.  It proves
that a Hamel basis exists under a stated foundation; it does not identify a
basis respected by normal-form truncations, multiplication, order, the omega
map, exponentiation, or the canonical derivations.  The Conway monomials are
canonical precisely because they solve a different, strong-summation basis
problem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The quotient by finite support is already class-large}

The failure of the monomials to Hamel-span \(\No\) is not repaired by adding
only a set of infinite-support vectors.  We give an explicit witness.

For each ordinal \(\alpha\), define the disjoint reverse-well-ordered block
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:block-Ea}
 E_\alpha=
 \{-\!(\omega\alpha+n):n<\omega\}\subseteq\No,
\end{equation}
where \(\omega\alpha\) denotes ordinal multiplication, viewed as a surreal
ordinal, and put
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:block-ua}
 u_\alpha=\sH_{n<\omega}
          \omega^{-(\omega\alpha+n)}.
\end{equation}
The intervals \([\omega\alpha,\omega\alpha+\omega)\) are disjoint, hence so
are the supports \(E_\alpha\).

\begin{theorem}[A proper-class independent quotient family]
\label{rvs:thm:quotient-independent}
The cosets
\[
  \{u_\alpha+\Fin:\alpha\in\On\}
\]
are real-linearly independent in \(\No/\Fin\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take distinct ordinals \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m\) and real numbers
\(c_1,\ldots,c_m\).  Because the supports \(E_{\alpha_j}\) are pairwise
disjoint, no cancellation occurs between different blocks.  If some
\(c_j\ne0\), the support of \(\sum_j c_j u_{\alpha_j}\) contains the entire
infinite block \(E_{\alpha_j}\), and therefore the sum does not belong to
\(\Fin\).  Hence a linear combination represents zero in the quotient only
when every coefficient is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label[corollary]{rvs:cor:basis-many-infinite}
Every Hamel class basis \(B\) of \(\No\) contains a proper class of vectors
outside \(\Fin\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The images in \(\No/\Fin\) of the members of \(B\setminus\Fin\) span the
quotient.  If \(B\setminus\Fin\) were a set, its real span would be a set and
could not contain the proper-class family of distinct independent cosets from
\cref{rvs:thm:quotient-independent}.
\end{proof}

This result sharpens the slogan that a Hamel basis is nonmonomial.  It must
contain class-many genuinely infinite normal forms.

\subsubsection*{No set-sized repair of the monomial family (source 52)}

Write $\Fin=\Span_\R\{t^\gamma:\gamma\in\No\}$. This is a class, so ordinary cosets of it are generally proper classes rather than elements of a set quotient.

\begin{theorem}[No set-sized repair of the monomial family]\label{rvs:eu:thm:classdefect}
For every set $S\subseteq\No$,
\[
 \Span_\R(\M\cup S)\ne\No.
\]
There is also a class-indexed family $(u_\alpha)_{\alpha\in\On}$ such that every nontrivial finite linear combination lies outside $\Fin$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The union $T$ of the valuation supports of the members of $S$ is a set of surreals, hence has an upper bound. Choose a positive ordinal $\eta$ above all its elements. Then
\[
 u=\hSum_{n\ge0}t^{\eta+n}
\]
has infinite support disjoint from $T$. Any vector in $\Span_\R S$ is supported in $T$, so no finite-monomial correction of such a vector equals $u$.

For the family assertion, let $\eta_\alpha=\omega^{\alpha+1}$ and put
\[
 u_\alpha=\hSum_{n\ge0}t^{\eta_\alpha+n}.
\]
For $\alpha\ne\beta$, $\eta_\alpha-\eta_\beta$ is not an integer, so these supports lie in disjoint cosets modulo $\Z$. A nontrivial finite combination retains an infinite support.
\end{proof}
In particular, any class Hamel basis containing $\M$ has a proper class of additional elements. This is a statement about finite relations and set-sized repairs, not an illicit cardinal calculation with a proper-class quotient.
\subsection{Every Hamel basis must contain enormous supports}

\begin{theorem}[Unavoidable support complexity]\label{rvs:cv:thm:support-complexity}
Let $B$ be any class Hamel basis of $\No$ over $\R$. For every infinite cardinal
$\kappa$, some $b\in B$ has
\[
  |\supp(b)|\ge\kappa.
\]
Thus no fixed set cardinal bounds the support sizes of the elements of a Hamel basis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For an infinite initial ordinal $\kappa$, the series
\[
  x_\kappa=\sum_{\alpha<\kappa}\omega^{-\alpha}
\]
is a surreal with support of cardinality $\kappa$. Write
$x_\kappa=\sum_{j=1}^n r_jb_j$ with $b_j\in B$. Coefficientwise addition gives
\[
  \supp(x_\kappa)\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^n\supp(b_j).
\]
A finite union of sets all of cardinality less than the infinite cardinal $\kappa$
has cardinality less than $\kappa$. Hence at least one $b_j$ has support cardinality
at least $\kappa$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
There is no class Hamel basis all of whose elements have countable support, bounded
ordinal support length, or support cardinality below any prescribed infinite
cardinal.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[\wtag{} Three clauses of one phenomenon]\label[remark]{rvs:w:rem:three-clauses}
The three sources prove three strengths of the same obstruction.
\Cref{rvs:cv:thm:support-complexity} (source 53) implies
\cref{rvs:cor:basis-many-infinite} (source 51): if the members of a Hamel
basis outside \(\Fin\) formed a set, their support cardinalities would be
bounded by a set cardinal.  \Cref{rvs:cor:basis-many-infinite} implies the
final sentence of \cref{rvs:eu:thm:classdefect} (source 52), because
\(\M\subseteq\Fin\).  The two explicit \(\On\)-indexed families differ:
source 51 uses the disjoint blocks \(E_\alpha=\{-(\omega\alpha+n)\}\), source
52 the cosets \(\eta_\alpha+\Z\) with \(\eta_\alpha=\omega^{\alpha+1}\), in
\(t\)-exponents.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Set-sized support windows}\label{rvs:subsec:windows}
\label{rvs:cv:sec:slices}

Class size should not hide the familiar cardinal arithmetic visible in every
set-sized coordinate window.  Let \(A\subseteq\No\) be a reverse-well-ordered
set and define
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:H-A}
  \Hahn(A)=\{x\in\No:\supp(x)\subseteq A\}.
\end{equation}
Every subset of \(A\) is reverse well ordered, so coefficient extraction gives
an ordinary vector-space isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:H-A-product}
  \Hahn(A)\cong\R^A.
\end{equation}
The monomials indexed by \(A\) form a Hahn basis of \(\Hahn(A)\), but only a
Hamel basis of its finite-support subspace.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 calls \(\Hahn(A)\) a support slice and also names
its finite part:

Global class dimension has no set cardinal value, but natural set-sized pieces do.
They already show how far a Hamel basis is from the monomial coordinate family.

\begin{definition}[Support slice]
Let $A\subseteq\No$ be a set that is reverse well ordered. Define
\[
  \Hahn(A)=\{x\in\No:\supp(x)\subseteq A\},
  \qquad
  \Hahn(A)^{\mathrm{fin}}=\Hahn(A)\cap\Fin.
\]
\end{definition}

Because every subset of a reverse-well-ordered set is again reverse well ordered,
there is no further support restriction inside $A$.

\begin{theorem}[Full product theorem for a support slice]\label{rvs:cv:thm:slice-product}
The coefficient map restricts to a canonical vector-space isomorphism
\[
  \Hahn(A)\cong\R^A,
  \qquad
  \Hahn(A)^{\mathrm{fin}}\cong\R^{(A)},
\]
where $\R^{(A)}$ denotes the finitely supported functions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any function $f:A\to\R$, the nonzero support of $f$ is a subset of $A$ and
is therefore reverse well ordered. Hence it defines a surreal normal form. The
inverse map is coefficient extraction. The finite-support assertion is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hamel dimension of a support window]
\label{rvs:thm:window-dimension}\label{rvs:cv:thm:slice-dimension}
If \(A\) is an infinite reverse-well-ordered set and \(\kappa=\lvert A\rvert\), then
\begin{align}
  \dim_{\R}\Hahn(A)&=\lvert\R\rvert^{\kappa},\label{rvs:eq:window-dim}\\
  \dim_{\R}\Hahn(A)^{\mathrm{fin}} &= \kappa,\label{rvs:cv:eq:finite-slice-dim}\\
  \dim_{\R}(\Hahn(A)/\Hahn(A)^{\mathrm{fin}}) &= \lvert\R\rvert^\kappa.\label{rvs:cv:eq:quotient-slice-dim}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\noindent\wtag{} \eqref{rvs:eq:window-dim} is source 51's statement;
source 53 (its ``Exact local Hamel dimension'') states all three.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \eqref{rvs:eq:window-dim} (source 51)]

By \eqref{rvs:eq:H-A-product}, this is the Erd\H{o}s--Kaplansky theorem
\(\dim_D D^I=\lvert D^I\rvert\) for an infinite index set \(I\) and division
ring \(D\) \cite{Jacobson,Bergman}.  Taking \(D=\R\) and \(I=A\) gives the
formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of all three (source 53)]

By \cref{rvs:cv:thm:slice-product}, the first equality is the Erd\H{o}s--Kaplansky
theorem
\[
  \dim_K K^I=|K|^{|I|}
\]
for an infinite index set $I$ \cite{Kuba}. The coordinate vectors form a Hamel
basis of the direct sum, giving \eqref{rvs:cv:eq:finite-slice-dim}. Put
$\mu=|\R|^\kappa$. Since $\mu\ge2^\kappa>\kappa$, the dimension identity
\[
  \dim\Hahn(A)=\dim\Hahn(A)^{\mathrm{fin}}
       +\dim(\Hahn(A)/\Hahn(A)^{\mathrm{fin}})
\]
for cardinal dimensions forces the quotient dimension to be $\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A countable window has continuum dimension]
\label[corollary]{rvs:cor:countable-window}
\label[corollary]{rvs:cv:thm:vandermonde-family}
Let \(A=\{\gamma_0>\gamma_1>\cdots\}\) be countably infinite.  Then
\[
  \dim_{\R}\Hahn(A)=2^{\aleph_0}.
\]
An explicit continuum-sized independent family is
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:vandermonde-family}
 x_s=\sH_{n<\omega}s^n\omega^{\gamma_n},
 \qquad s\in\R.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The space has cardinality
\(\lvert\R\rvert^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_0}\), so its dimension is at most the
continuum.  To prove the matching lower bound, take distinct
\(s_1,\ldots,s_m\in\R\) and suppose
\(\sum_{j=1}^m c_jx_{s_j}=0\).  Comparing the first \(m\) coordinates gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m c_jt_j^n=0,
 \qquad n=0,\ldots,m-1.
\]
The Vandermonde matrix \((s_j^n)\) is invertible, hence all \(c_j=0\).
Thus \(\{x_s:s\in\R\}\) is linearly independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Source 53's countable tail]\label[remark]{rvs:cv:cor:countable-tail}
\wtag{} Source 53 states, as a corollary of \cref{rvs:thm:window-dimension}:
for

\[
  A=\{0,-1,-2,\ldots\},
\]
the space of series $\sum_{n\ge0}r_n\omega^{-n}$ has real Hamel dimension
$2^{\aleph_0}$, whereas its monomial span has countable dimension. The quotient
by finite Laurent polynomials also has dimension $2^{\aleph_0}$.

Its theorem ``Geometric-series Vandermonde family'' is the family
\eqref{rvs:eq:vandermonde-family} on this window,
\(g_s=1/(1-s\omega^{-1})=\sum_{n\ge0}s^n\omega^{-n}\) for \(s\in\R\), proved by
the same Vandermonde determinant \(\prod_{i<j}(s_j-s_i)\ne0\).
\end{remark}

This is the exact analogue of the distinction between the sequence space
$\R^{\N}$ and its finitely supported subspace. The surreal order and field
multiplication add structure, but they do not reduce the algebraic dimension.

\begin{remark}[Source 53 on the Vandermonde family]

The same family is used in standard proofs about product-space dimensions. Here it
has a direct surreal interpretation: a one-parameter family of inverses of linear
infinitesimal perturbations already produces continuum many independent vectors.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[The gap between coordinate and Hamel dimensions]
On a countable support window, the canonical monomial Hahn basis has
cardinality \(\aleph_0\), while every Hamel basis has cardinality continuum.
The difference is exactly the information carried by arbitrary infinite
coordinate strings.
\end{example}
\subsection{Product topology versus algebraic dimension}

Via $\Hahn(A)\cong\R^A$, one may put the product topology on a support slice.
Then $\Hahn(A)^{\mathrm{fin}}$ is dense: every basic neighborhood specifies only
finitely many coordinates, which a finite-support vector can match. If $A$ is
uncountable, this density generally requires nets rather than sequences. This
coefficientwise topology is radically different from the natural valuation
topology studied in \cref{rvs:cv:sec:topology}, where the same finite-support subspace is
closed.

\subsection{The full Hahn field of a set exponent group (source 52)}\label{rvs:w:subsec:fullcodim}

\wtag{} Source 52 measures the defect of the monomial span in the whole
Hahn field \(H_\Gamma\), not in a fixed window.  Its Vandermonde family
\(q_r=(1-rt^\delta)^{-1}\) is the family \eqref{rvs:eq:vandermonde-family}
up to a change of window.

\begin{theorem}[The missing part has full dimension]\label{rvs:eu:thm:fullcodim}
Let $\Gamma$ be a nonzero set-sized ordered abelian group. Then
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:fullcodim}
 \dim_\R(H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma})=\dim_\R H_\Gamma=|H_\Gamma|.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\delta>0$. For $r\in\R_{>0}$ put
\[
 q_r=\hSum_{n\ge0}r^n t^{n\delta}=\frac1{1-r t^\delta}.
\]
We first show that the cosets $q_r+\FinS{\Gamma}$ are linearly independent. If $r_1,\ldots,r_m$ are distinct and
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m a_jq_{r_j}\in \FinS{\Gamma},
\]
then $\sum_j a_jr_j^n=0$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Taking $m$ consecutive values $n=N,\ldots,N+m-1$ gives a matrix with determinant
\[
 \left(\prod_{j=1}^m r_j^N\right)
 \prod_{i<j}(r_j-r_i)\ne0.
\]
Thus all $a_j$ vanish, proving $\dim_\R(H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma})\ge\cont$.

If $|\Gamma|>\cont$, choose representatives $A$ for $\Gamma/\Z\delta$. Since each coset is countable, $|A|=|\Gamma|$. The series
\[
 u_a=\hSum_{n\ge0}t^{a+n\delta}\qquad(a\in A)
\]
have pairwise disjoint infinite supports. Every nontrivial finite linear combination has infinite support, so their cosets modulo $\FinS{\Gamma}$ are independent. Together with the previous paragraph this proves
\[
 d:=\dim_\R(H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma})\ge\max(\cont,|\Gamma|).
\]
The dimension formula for a subspace and quotient gives
\[
 \dim_\R H_\Gamma=\dim_\R \FinS{\Gamma}+d=|\Gamma|+d=d.
\]
Finally, an infinite-dimensional vector space over $\R$ with basis cardinal $d$ has cardinality $\max(\cont,d)$. Since $d\ge\cont$, this equals $d$, proving the theorem.
\end{proof}
The Vandermonde argument is the familiar cardinal-linear-algebra mechanism appearing in the Erd\H{o}s--Kaplansky context; compare \cite{Kuba}. The application modulo the finite-support span and the disjoint-coset argument above are given explicitly rather than inferred from cardinality alone.

\begin{corollary}[Countable and real exponent groups]\label[corollary]{rvs:eu:cor:continuum}
If $\Gamma\ne0$ is countable, or if $0\ne\Gamma\subseteq\R$, then
\[
 \dim_\R H_\Gamma=\dim_\R(H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma})=\cont.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For countable $\Gamma$, there are at most $\cont$ coefficient functions. For $\Gamma\subseteq\R$, every well-ordered subset is countable: assign a rational number in the interval between each nonfinal element and its successor. These intervals are disjoint, so there are only countably many nonfinal elements, and at most one final element. There are at most $\cont$ countable supports and $\cont$ coefficient assignments on each. Constants give the reverse cardinal inequality. Apply Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:fullcodim}.
\end{proof}
For $\Gamma=\Z$ the monomials form a countable independent family, but one must add continuum many Hamel directions. For $\Gamma=\R$, there are already continuum many monomials and one must still add continuum many directions. Equal infinite cardinality never proves that an independent family spans.

\subsection{What a ``dimension of \texorpdfstring{\(\No\)}{No}'' can mean}

There is no set cardinal \(\kappa\) with \(\dim_{\R}\No=\kappa\), because no
set spans.  Three replacements are meaningful.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \emph{class Hamel dimension} is represented by any Hamel basis
class, but comparing proper-class sizes requires the chosen class theory and
is too coarse to capture support.
\item The \emph{graded dimension} is one at each exponent
(\cref{rvs:sec:filtration}).
\item The \emph{window dimension profile}
\(A\mapsto\lvert\R\rvert^{\lvert A\rvert}\) records the ordinary Hamel
dimensions of set-sized Hahn blocks.
\end{enumerate}
The second and third are canonical and structurally informative; the first is
mostly an existence invariant.

\section{Canonical projections, flags, and the associated graded}\label{rvs:sec:filtration}
\label{rvs:cv:sec:projections}\label{rvs:cv:sec:graded}

The monomial coordinates provide much more than a list of generators.  They
split \(\No\) functorially by arbitrary exponent classes and resolve its
natural valuation into one-dimensional layers.

\subsection{Support projections}\label{rvs:subsec:support-projections}

For a subclass \(A\subseteq\No\), define
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:PA}
 P_A(x)=\sH_{\gamma\in\supp(x)\cap A}
       [\omega^\gamma]x\,\omega^\gamma.
\end{equation}
This is well defined because \(\supp(x)\cap A\) is a subset of a
reverse-well-ordered set.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical support splitting]\label{rvs:thm:support-splitting}
\label{rvs:cv:thm:projection-calculus}
For every subclass \(A\subseteq\No\), \(P_A\) is a strongly summation-
preserving real-linear idempotent and
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:support-split}
 \No=\No[A]\oplus\No[\No\setminus A],
 \qquad
 \No[A]=\{x:\supp(x)\subseteq A\}.
\end{equation}
The projections satisfy
\begin{align}
 P_AP_B&=P_{A\cap B},\label{rvs:eq:boolean1}\\
 P_{\No\setminus A}&=\id-P_A,\label{rvs:eq:boolean2}\\
 P_{A\cup B}&=P_A+P_B-P_{A\cap B}.\label{rvs:eq:boolean3}
\end{align}
Thus exponent subclasses act as a Boolean algebra of canonical linear
projections.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Coefficientwise linearity and idempotence are immediate.  Every normal form
splits uniquely into the terms with exponent in \(A\) and those outside
\(A\), proving \eqref{rvs:eq:support-split}.  If \((x_i)\) is Hahn summable, the
union of the supports of \((P_Ax_i)\) is a subset of the original
reverse-well-ordered union, and local finiteness at every exponent is
preserved.  Hence \(P_A\) is strong.  The Boolean identities are checked on
each coordinate.
\end{proof}

Important special cases include:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the single-coordinate projection
\(P_{\{\gamma\}}x=[\omega^\gamma]x\,\omega^\gamma\);
\item initial and final truncations at an exponent \(\delta\);
\item the decomposition into positive, zero, and negative exponents;
\item projections onto ordinal exponents, real exponents, or any definable
scale family.
\end{enumerate}
No Hamel-basis choice is needed.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 proves the same calculus coordinatewise, listing
also \(P_\varnothing=0\), \(P_{\No}=\id\) and \(P_{S\cup T}=P_S+P_T\) for disjoint
\(S,T\), and derives:

\begin{corollary}[Cut decomposition]\label[corollary]{rvs:cv:cor:cut-decomposition}
For every exponent $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:cv:eq:cut-decomposition}
  \No=\No_{>a}\oplus\R\omega^a\oplus\No_{<a},
\end{equation}
where the three summands consist of series supported above $a$, at $a$, and below
$a$. The projections are $P_{(a,\infty)}$, $P_{\{a\}}$, and
$P_{(-\infty,a)}$.
\end{corollary}

Setting $a=0$ recovers purely infinite, real, and infinitesimal parts. More generally,
a set-indexed partition $(S_i)_{i\in I}$ of the exponent class gives, for each
$x$, a strongly summable decomposition
\[
  x=\HSum_{i\in I}P_{S_i}x;
\]
only set-many summands can be nonzero because $\supp(x)$ is a set.

\subsubsection*{Coordinate-resolved subspaces of a Hahn workspace (source 52)}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 states the same splitting on \(H_\Gamma\) (in
\(t\)-exponents) and classifies the subspaces it resolves.

For $A\subseteq\Gamma$ let
\[
 H[A]=\{x\in H_\Gamma:\supp_t x\subseteq A\},\qquad
 P_A(x)=\hSum_{\gamma\in A}x_\gamma t^\gamma.
\]
Every $P_A$ is a strong projection, $P_AP_B=P_{A\cap B}$, and
\[
 H_\Gamma=H[A]\oplus H[\Gamma\setminus A].
\]
The subsets need not be subgroups; these are vector-space decompositions, not generally field decompositions.

\begin{proposition}[Classification with all coordinate projections]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:coordinatesubspace}
A real subspace $V\subseteq H_\Gamma$ is closed under all admissible Hahn sums and invariant under every one-coordinate projection if and only if $V=H[A]$ for a unique $A\subseteq\Gamma$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $A=\{\gamma:t^\gamma\in V\}$. If $x\in V$ and $x_\gamma\ne0$, its coordinate projection puts $t^\gamma$ in $V$, so $\supp_t x\subseteq A$. Conversely every series supported in $A$ is an admissible sum of monomials already in $V$. The reverse implication follows immediately from the support definitions.
\end{proof}
This theorem provides an exact sense in which support geometry replaces arbitrary basis selection. Strong closure without coordinate invariance is a different, more difficult subspace problem.
\begin{warning}
Support projections are real-linear, not generally field homomorphisms.  For
example, constant-term extraction is multiplicative on certain support
subrings but not on all of \(\No\), because exponents from different factors
can add to zero.
\end{warning}

\subsection{The dominance flag}

For \(\gamma\in\No\), define
\begin{align}
 F_{\le\gamma}
 &=\{x\in\No:\supp(x)\subseteq(-\infty,\gamma]\},\label{rvs:eq:Fle}\\
 F_{<\gamma}
 &=\{x\in\No:\supp(x)\subseteq(-\infty,\gamma)\}.
 \label{rvs:eq:Fl}
\end{align}
Equivalently, a nonzero \(x\) belongs to \(F_{\le\gamma}\) exactly when
\(\lead(x)\le\gamma\).  These subspaces form an increasing proper-class flag.

\begin{proposition}[One-dimensional graded quotients]\label[proposition]{rvs:prop:grade-one}
For every \(\gamma\in\No\), coefficient extraction induces a canonical
isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:one-grade}
  F_{\le\gamma}/F_{<\gamma}\ \cong\ \R,
  \qquad
  x+F_{<\gamma}\longmapsto[\omega^\gamma]x.
\end{equation}
The class of \(\omega^\gamma\) is the distinguished basis vector of this
quotient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The kernel of the coefficient map on \(F_{\le\gamma}\) is precisely
\(F_{<\gamma}\), and every real coefficient occurs as
\(r\omega^\gamma\).
\end{proof}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 puts the same point this way:

A Hamel basis is noncanonical because it tries to encode an entire infinite tail
by finitely many chosen vectors. The valuation filtration avoids that problem by
retaining one leading scale at a time.

\begin{remark}[Source 53's form, with the canonical splitting]\label[remark]{rvs:cv:thm:one-dimensional-layer}
\wtag{} Source 53 states \cref{rvs:prop:grade-one} as a split exact sequence.

For every $a\in\No$, coefficient extraction induces a short exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:cv:eq:scale-exact}
  0\longrightarrow F_{<a}\longrightarrow F_{\le a}
   \xrightarrow{\ c_a\ }\R\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
It is canonically split by $r\mapsto r\omega^a$. Hence
\[
  F_{\le a}=F_{<a}\oplus\R\omega^a,
  \qquad
  F_{\le a}/F_{<a}\cong\R.
\]
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
The kernel of $c_a$ on $F_{\le a}$ consists precisely of the series with no
$a$-term, hence with leading exponent below $a$. Surjectivity and the splitting
follow from $c_a(r\omega^a)=r$.
\end{proof}
Thus \(\No\) has one real direction at every Archimedean scale, plus the
support-completion needed to combine set-many scales.

\subsection{Associated graded algebra}\label{rvs:subsec:graded}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:gr-def}
  \gr(\No)=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\No}
  F_{\le\gamma}/F_{<\gamma}.
\end{equation}
The direct sum is algebraic: an element has only finitely many nonzero
homogeneous coordinates.  Multiplication of leading terms makes this a graded
\(\R\)-algebra.

\begin{theorem}[The associated graded is the group algebra]
\label{rvs:thm:graded}
\label{rvs:cv:thm:associated-graded}
There is a canonical graded \(\R\)-algebra isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:gr-group-algebra}
  \gr(\No)\ \cong\ \R[\No_{\mathrm{add}}]
  \ \cong\ \Fin,
\end{equation}
where \(\R[\No_{\mathrm{add}}]\) is the finite-support group algebra of the
additive surreal class.  Under this isomorphism the homogeneous symbol of
\(\omega^\gamma\) is the group-algebra element \(e_\gamma\), with
\[
 e_\gamma e_\delta=e_{\gamma+\delta}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{rvs:prop:grade-one}, the grade at \(\gamma\) is one-dimensional with
basis the symbol of \(\omega^\gamma\).  If
\(x=r\omega^\gamma+\text{lower terms}\) and
\(y=s\omega^\delta+\text{lower terms}\), then
\[
 xy=rs\omega^{\gamma+\delta}+\text{terms of exponent below }\gamma+\delta.
\]
Hence multiplication of homogeneous symbols is exactly the group-algebra
law.  The finite-support normal forms identify that group algebra with
\(\Fin\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What completion adds]
The associated graded remembers every individual dominance layer and their
multiplicative law, but it forgets the admissible infinite assembly of those
layers.  Recovering \(\No\) from \(\gr(\No)\) therefore requires the
reverse-well-ordered support rule.  This is the precise sense in which
\(\No\) is a Hahn completion of its graded skeleton, rather than an ordinary
linear span.
\end{remark}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 proves \cref{rvs:thm:graded} from the
multiplicativity of the filtration, writing \(T^a\) for the group-algebra
monomial \(e_a\):

The filtration is multiplicative:
\[
  F_{\le a}F_{\le b}\subseteq F_{\le a+b}.
\]
Therefore it has an associated graded class algebra
\[
  \gr\No=\bigoplus_{a\in\No}F_{\le a}/F_{<a}.
\]
\begin{corollary}[Principal symbol]
For $x\ne0$, the initial form
\[
  \operatorname{in}(x)=\lc(x)T^{\lead(x)}
\]
satisfies
\[
  \operatorname{in}(xy)=\operatorname{in}(x)\operatorname{in}(y).
\]
\end{corollary}

This is the strongest canonical ordinary-basis statement available: one monomial
basis vector survives in each scale quotient, and multiplication of leading terms
is exact.
\subsection{Canonical coefficient exact sequences}

For any exponent subclass \(A\), restriction of coefficients gives a split
exact sequence of real vector classes
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:split-exact-A}
  0\longrightarrow \No[\No\setminus A]
  \longrightarrow \No
  \xrightarrow{\ P_A\ } \No[A]
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
For a singleton \(A=\{\gamma\}\), composition with
\(r\omega^\gamma\mapsto r\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:coord-exact}
  0\longrightarrow\ker[\omega^\gamma]
  \longrightarrow\No
  \xrightarrow{[\omega^\gamma]}\R
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
These canonical splittings will supply the simplest nonzero members of the
strong dual in \cref{rvs:sec:strong-maps}.

\noindent\wtag{} The next subsection is source 53's.  The classification
is classical (Hahn's embedding theory and Conrad's theory of convex
subgroups of ordered groups: convex subgroups of a Hahn group correspond to
initial segments of its value chain); source 53 gives a direct proof and
claims no priority (\cref{rvs:w:subsec:intro53}).

\subsection{Convex subspaces are exactly exponent cuts}

A real subspace $V\subseteq\No$ is \emph{convex} if $x,z\in V$ and
$x\le y\le z$ imply $y\in V$. For an initial (downward-closed) subclass
$C\subseteq\No$, put
\[
  V_C=\{0\}\cup\{x\ne0:\lead(x)\in C\}.
\]
Since $C$ is downward closed, this is equivalently the class of all normal forms
supported in $C$.

\begin{theorem}[Classification of convex real subspaces]\label{rvs:cv:thm:convex-subspaces}
The assignments
\[
  C\longmapsto V_C,
  \qquad
  V\longmapsto C_V:=\{a\in\No:\omega^a\in V\}
\]
are mutually inverse order isomorphisms between
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item downward-closed subclasses of the exponent order $\No$, and
\item convex $\R$-subspaces of the ordered vector space $\No$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First let $C$ be downward closed. If $0\le x\le y$ and $y\in V_C$, comparison of
leading terms gives $\lead(x)\le\lead(y)\in C$, so $x\in V_C$. Thus $V_C$ is
convex; closure under addition and real scalars follows from support containment.

Conversely let $V$ be convex. If $a\in C_V$ and $b<a$, then
$0<\omega^b<\omega^a$, so convexity gives $\omega^b\in V$; hence $C_V$ is
downward closed. Now take $x\ne0$ with leading exponent $a$. Its leading-term
factorization has the form
\[
  x=\omega^a r(1+\varepsilon),
  \qquad r\in\R^\times,
  \qquad |\varepsilon|<1/n\text{ for all }n\ge1.
\]
Thus $|x|$ and $\omega^a$ bound positive real multiples of one another. A convex
real subspace contains $x$ if and only if it contains $\omega^a$. Therefore
$x\in V$ if and only if $a\in C_V$, proving $V=V_{C_V}$ and the inverse identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The principal filtration pieces $F_{<a}$ and $F_{\le a}$ are convex. Nonprincipal
cuts in the exponent class give additional convex subspaces. Hence the lattice of
convex real subspaces is canonically the lattice of cuts of the exponent order.
\end{corollary}

This result explains why the monomials are natural even without a basis theorem:
they parameterize the Archimedean scale layers and completely control convex
linear geometry.


\section{Diagonal operators, commutants, and positivity}\label{rvs:w:sec:diagonal}\label{rvs:cv:sec:diagonal}

\wtag{} Source 51 introduces the diagonal maps alongside strong maps
(\cref{rvs:sec:strong-maps}); source 53 develops them into a spectral
calculus.  Both are printed here; ``strong'' refers to
\cref{rvs:def:strong-map}.

\subsection{Diagonal maps and support projections}

For an arbitrary class function \(a:\No\to\R\), define
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:Da}
  D_a\!\left(\sH_\gamma r_\gamma\omega^\gamma\right)
  =\sH_\gamma a(\gamma)r_\gamma\omega^\gamma.
\end{equation}
The output support is contained in the input support, so \(D_a\) is strong.

\begin{proposition}[Diagonal operator calculus]\label[proposition]{rvs:prop:diagonal}
\label[proposition]{rvs:cv:prop:diagonal-algebra}
For class functions \(a,b:\No\to\R\),
\begin{align}
 D_aD_b&=D_{ab},\\
 D_a+D_b&=D_{a+b}.
\end{align}
Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(D_a\) is injective exactly when \(a(\gamma)\ne0\) for every
\(\gamma\);
\item \(D_a\) is a strong automorphism exactly when every \(a(\gamma)\ne0\),
with inverse \(D_{1/a}\);
\item \(D_a\) is idempotent exactly when
\(a(\gamma)\in\{0,1\}\) for every \(\gamma\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the support projection \(P_A\) is
\(D_{\mathbf{1}_A}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All assertions follow by checking each monomial coordinate.  Over the field
\(\R\), the scalar equation \(a^2=a\) has only the solutions \(0\) and
\(1\).
\end{proof}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 proves the same calculus (its ``Diagonal
algebra'', with \(D_{\one}=\id\)) and writes \(D_\lambda\) for \(D_a\);
its corollary that a diagonal operator is idempotent exactly when it is a
support projection is clause~3 of \cref{rvs:prop:diagonal}.

The coefficient projections behave like a complete family of one-dimensional
spectral projections. This analogy can be made exact without introducing an inner
product or a norm.

\begin{definition}[Diagonal operator]
For a class function $\lambda:\No\to\R$, define
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:cv:eq:diagonal-operator}
  D_\lambda x=\sum_{a\in\supp(x)}\lambda(a)c_a(x)\omega^a.
\end{equation}
The support can only shrink, so this is a well-defined surreal number.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[Commutant theorem]\label{rvs:cv:thm:commutant}
Let $T:\No\to\No$ be an $\R$-linear class map. The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $T$ commutes with every one-coordinate projection $P_{\{a\}}$.
\item There is a unique class function $\lambda:\No\to\R$ such that
$T=D_\lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the commutant of the complete monomial projection family is exactly the
coefficientwise diagonal algebra.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every diagonal operator plainly commutes with every coordinate projection.
Conversely, suppose $T P_{\{a\}}=P_{\{a\}}T$. Since
$P_{\{a\}}\omega^a=\omega^a$, we have
\[
  P_{\{a\}}T(\omega^a)=T(\omega^a),
\]
so $T(\omega^a)$ lies in the one-dimensional range $\R\omega^a$. Write
$T(\omega^a)=\lambda(a)\omega^a$. For arbitrary $x$,
\[
  P_{\{a\}}T(x)=T(P_{\{a\}}x)
   =T(c_a(x)\omega^a)
   =c_a(x)\lambda(a)\omega^a.
\]
Hence the $a$-coefficient of $T(x)$ is $\lambda(a)c_a(x)$ for every $a$.
Coefficients separate points by \cref{rvs:cv:cor:coeff-separate}, so $T=D_\lambda$.
Uniqueness follows by evaluation on $\omega^a$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 proves a different theorem with the same
conclusion: on a real-exponent workspace, every real-linear map commuting
with the single Euler operator \(\EuD\) is a coefficient multiplier
(\cref{rvs:eu:thm:commutant}).

\subsection{Which diagonal maps preserve order?}

A linear map $T$ on an ordered vector space is \emph{positive} if $x\ge0$ implies
$T(x)\ge0$. Because surreal order is determined by the first nonzero coefficient,
positivity of $D_\lambda$ has a sharp and slightly surprising classification.

\begin{theorem}[Positive diagonal operators]\label{rvs:cv:thm:positive-diagonal}
Let $\lambda:\No\to\R$, and put
\[
  U_\lambda=\{a\in\No:\lambda(a)\ne0\}.
\]
Then $D_\lambda$ is positive if and only if
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\lambda(a)\ge0$ for every $a$, and
\item $U_\lambda$ is a final (upward-closed) subclass of the exponent order.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, $D_\lambda$ is an order automorphism if and only if
$\lambda(a)>0$ for every $a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that $D_\lambda$ is positive. Applying it to the positive monomial
$\omega^a$ gives $\lambda(a)\omega^a\ge0$, hence $\lambda(a)\ge0$. If
$b<a$, $\lambda(b)>0$, and $\lambda(a)=0$, then
\[
  x=\omega^a-\omega^b>0
\]
but
\[
  D_\lambda x=-\lambda(b)\omega^b<0,
\]
a contradiction. Thus nonvanishing propagates upward, so $U_\lambda$ is final.

Conversely, let $x>0$ have leading exponent $a$ and positive leading coefficient.
If $a\in U_\lambda$, the leading coefficient of $D_\lambda x$ is multiplied by
the positive number $\lambda(a)$, so $D_\lambda x>0$. If $a\notin U_\lambda$,
finality implies that no exponent below $a$ lies in $U_\lambda$; hence every term
of $x$ is killed and $D_\lambda x=0$. This proves positivity.

If every $\lambda(a)>0$, then the same argument shows that $D_\lambda$ preserves
and reflects the sign of every nonzero $x$, and \cref{rvs:cv:prop:diagonal-algebra} gives
the positive inverse $D_{1/\lambda}$. Conversely an order automorphism cannot kill
or reverse any positive monomial, so all $\lambda(a)>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Positive support projections]\label[corollary]{rvs:cv:cor:positive-projections}
The support projection $P_S$ is positive if and only if $S$ is a final subclass
of $\No$.
\end{corollary}

For example, projection onto exponents $>0$ is positive, whereas projection onto
negative exponents is not: the positive surreal $1-\omega^{-1}$ is sent to the
negative infinitesimal $-\omega^{-1}$.

\subsection{Covariance under monomial symmetries}

Multiplication by a monomial defines an $\R$-linear order automorphism
\[
  S_b(x)=\omega^b x,
\]
which translates every exponent by $b$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:cv:eq:shift-covariance}
  S_bD_\lambda S_{-b}=D_{\lambda_b},
  \qquad
  \lambda_b(a)=\lambda(a-b).
\end{equation}
The diagonal algebra and the shift group form a natural crossed-product-like
operator system.

More generally, every ordered additive automorphism
$e:(\No,+,<)\to(\No,+,<)$ induces the coefficient-reindexing field automorphism
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:cv:eq:exponent-auto}
  \sigma_e\left(\sum_a r_a\omega^a\right)
   =\sum_a r_a\omega^{e(a)}.
\end{equation}
It fixes $\R$, sends $\omega^a$ to $\omega^{e(a)}$, and satisfies
\begin{align}
  \sigma_eP_S\sigma_e^{-1}&=P_{e(S)},\label{rvs:cv:eq:projection-covariance}\\
  \sigma_eD_\lambda\sigma_e^{-1}&=D_{\lambda\circ e^{-1}}.
  \label{rvs:cv:eq:diagonal-covariance}
\end{align}
The \repo\ source contains a Lean implementation of these exponent automorphisms
for the actual sign-sequence surreal field. This covariance is a strong sense in
which the monomial Hahn basis is natural: every exponent symmetry merely permutes
its vectors.

\noindent\wtag{} ``Every exponent symmetry merely permutes its vectors''
holds for the field automorphisms \(\sigma_e\) induced by exponent
automorphisms.  It does not hold for all order-preserving strong linear
automorphisms: source 52's reweightings \(T_{\gamma,a}\) rescale one monomial
(\cref{rvs:eu:thm:naturalobstruction}) and its shear sends \(t^\gamma\) to
\(t^\gamma+t^\delta\) (\cref{rvs:w:rem:shear}).  Monomials are canonical
relative to the omega map.

\begin{corollary}[Projection-and-shift rigidity]\label[corollary]{rvs:cv:cor:projection-shift-rigidity}
If an $\R$-linear map $T:\No\to\No$ commutes with every coordinate projection
$P_{\{a\}}$ and every monomial shift $S_b$, then $T=r\,\id$ for a unique
$r\in\R$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{rvs:cv:thm:commutant}, $T=D_\lambda$. Commutation with $S_b$ and
\eqref{rvs:cv:eq:shift-covariance} give $\lambda(a)=\lambda(a-b)$ for all $a,b$.
Taking $b=a$ shows $\lambda(a)=\lambda(0)$, so $\lambda$ is constant.
\end{proof}
\section{Finite real linear algebra transfers coefficientwise}\label{rvs:cv:sec:linear-systems}

A principal advantage of the coordinate model is that every finite real linear
system over surreals decomposes into the same real system at each exponent.

Let $A=(A_{ij})\in M_{m\times n}(\R)$ and define
\[
  A_{\No}:\No^n\to\No^m,
  \qquad
  (A_{\No}x)_i=\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}x_j.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Coefficientwise linear-system criterion]\label{rvs:cv:thm:coefficientwise-system}
For $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\No^n$,
\[
  A_{\No}x=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  A\big(c_a(x_1),\ldots,c_a(x_n)\big)^{\mathsf T}=0
  \quad\text{for every }a\in\No.
\]
Equivalently, the coefficient vector at every exponent belongs to the ordinary
real nullspace $\ker_{\R}A$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every row $i$ and exponent $a$, linearity of coefficient extraction gives
\[
  c_a\left(\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}x_j\right)
   =\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}c_a(x_j).
\]
The result follows because coefficients separate points.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rank transfer]\label{rvs:cv:thm:rank-transfer}
If $A$ has real rank $r$, then, as class real vector spaces,
\[
  \ker A_{\No}\cong\No^{n-r},
  \qquad
  \im A_{\No}\cong\No^r.
\]
After choosing real change-of-basis matrices, $A_{\No}$ is equivalent to the block
map
\[
  (x_1,\ldots,x_n)\longmapsto(x_1,\ldots,x_r,0,\ldots,0).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose $P\in\operatorname{GL}_m(\R)$ and $Q\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\R)$ with
\[
  PAQ=\begin{pmatrix}I_r&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The same finite matrices act invertibly on $\No^m$ and $\No^n$, since their
inverses also have real entries. Conjugating $A_{\No}$ gives the displayed block
map, from which the kernel and image descriptions follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scale-by-scale solution space]
If $u_1,\ldots,u_{n-r}$ is a real basis of $\ker A$, every surreal solution is
a strongly summable scale decomposition
\[
  x=\HSum_{a\in S}\omega^a
       \left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-r}\rho_{a,k}u_k\right),
\]
where $S$ is reverse well ordered and $\rho_{a,k}\in\R$. Conversely every such
admissible family is a solution.
\end{corollary}

This theorem is the finite-dimensional version of ``tensoring real linear algebra
with the surreal coefficient space.'' It requires no choice of a global Hamel
basis. The \repo\ files on coefficient extraction and module kernel bases formalize
closely related statements for the actual purely infinite omnific ideal.

\section{Valuation bases and the limits of naturality (source 52)}\label{rvs:w:sec:valuation}\label{rvs:eu:sec:valuation}\label{rvs:eu:sec:naturality}

\wtag{} This section is source 52's, in its \(t\)-convention.  Its graded

\(t\)-exponents.  The finite-dimensional reduced basis and the absence of a

\subsection{Leading values and the associated graded space}
For $x\ne0$ define
\[
 v(x)=\min\supp_t x,\qquad \lc(x)=x_{v(x)},\qquad v(0)=\infty.
\]
Then $v(rx)=v(x)$ for $r\ne0$, and $v(x+y)\ge\min(v(x),v(y))$. Let
\[
 H_{\ge\gamma}=\{x:v(x)\ge\gamma\},\qquad
 H_{>\gamma}=\{x:v(x)>\gamma\}.
\]
The coefficient map at $\gamma$ induces a canonical isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:graded}
 H_{\ge\gamma}/H_{>\gamma}\simeq\R.
\end{equation}
Thus the associated graded real vector space is
\[
 \gr_vH_\Gamma\simeq\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\R.
\]
It has a canonical one-dimensional line at each scale. Passing to the associated graded space forgets all tails; it does not identify $H_\Gamma$ itself with that direct sum.

\begin{definition}[Valuation independence]
A family $(b_i)$ is valuation-independent over $\R$ if every nonzero finite combination satisfies
\[
 v\left(\sum_i r_i b_i\right)=\min_{r_i\ne0}v(b_i).
\]
A valuation basis is a Hamel basis with this property. This is the trivially valued ground-field convention of valued-vector-space theory; see \cite{Kuhlmann}.
\end{definition}
In a Hahn space with real coefficients a family is valuation-independent exactly when its nonzero members have pairwise distinct values. Distinct values prevent cancellation at the unique least one. Conversely, two equal values can be canceled by a suitable nonzero real linear combination of their leading coefficients.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical reduced Hahn row echelon form]\label{rvs:eu:thm:echelon}
Let $V\subseteq H_\Gamma$ be a $d$-dimensional real subspace. There are uniquely determined values
\[
 \gamma_1<\cdots<\gamma_d
\]
and a unique ordered basis $b_1,\ldots,b_d$ of $V$ such that
\[
 v(b_i)=\gamma_i,\qquad (b_i)_{\gamma_j}=\delta_{ij}.
\]
The set of values attained by $V\setminus\{0\}$ is exactly $\{\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_d\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take any finite spanning family of $V$. The minimum of its leading values is the minimum value attained in $V$: no finite sum can have a smaller value. Call this minimum $\gamma_1$. The nonzero coefficient functional at $\gamma_1$ has a kernel $V_1$ of dimension $d-1$, and every nonzero vector in $V_1$ has value greater than $\gamma_1$. Repeat on $V_1$, then on the next kernel. The dimension drops by one each time and gives $d$ intrinsic pivots.

The map
\[
 V\longrightarrow\R^d,\qquad x\longmapsto(x_{\gamma_1},\ldots,x_{\gamma_d})
\]
is an isomorphism: its successive kernels are exactly those just constructed. Let $b_i$ be the inverse image of the $i$th coordinate vector. It has value $\gamma_i$ and the required coefficients. This also proves uniqueness. Any nonzero combination of the $b_i$ has value the first pivot with nonzero coefficient.
\end{proof}
This is a useful positive answer to the user's question at finite dimension: the normal-form coordinates determine a canonical basis of each finite-dimensional subspace, even when its vectors have infinite support. No global well-order or arbitrary tie-breaking is required.


\noindent\wtag{} \Cref{rvs:eu:lem:spherical} is the case \(V=\R\) of the
three-duals report's Proposition 5.1 (spherical completeness of the full Hahn
space \(V_\Gamma\)); source 52's proof is printed as a second route.

\subsection{Spherical completeness and the obstruction}
\begin{lemma}[Set-indexed spherical completeness]\label[lemma]{rvs:eu:lem:spherical}
Every nonempty set-sized nest of closed balls
\[
 B(a_i,\gamma_i)=\{x:v(x-a_i)\ge\gamma_i\}
\]
in $H_\Gamma$ has nonempty intersection. The same assertion holds in the full surreal Hahn class for set-sized nests.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 52), direct]
Membership in $B(a_i,\gamma_i)$ prescribes the coefficients at exponents $\delta<\gamma_i$. Nestedness makes these prescriptions compatible: two centers agree below the smaller radius. Let $S$ be the union of the nonzero prescribed supports and give each member its prescribed coefficient, with zero elsewhere.

We verify the one nontrivial point, well-ordering. Given a nonempty $A\subseteq S$, choose $s\in A$ and a prescription with $s<\gamma_i$. Every member of $S$ at most $s$ belongs to the corresponding initial support of $a_i$, by compatibility. Hence the nonempty set $A\cap(-\infty,s]$ has a least member, which is also least in $A$. Thus $S$ is well ordered. The constructed series lies in every ball. For $\No$, the union remains a set because the nest and all individual supports are sets.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No global valuation-independent Hamel basis]\label{rvs:eu:thm:novalbasis}
If $\Gamma\ne0$, $H_\Gamma$ has no valuation basis over $\R$. Neither does the full surreal class have a class valuation basis over $\R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $B$ were such a basis and fix $\delta>0$. Because values of finite combinations are minima of basis values, for each $n\ge1$ there is a basis vector $b_n$ with $v(b_n)=n\delta$. Put $s_n=b_1+\cdots+b_n$. The balls
\[
 B_n=B(s_n,(n+1)\delta)
\]
are nested: $v(s_{n+1}-s_n)=(n+1)\delta$. By Lemma~\ref{rvs:eu:lem:spherical}, choose $x\in\bigcap_nB_n$.

A Hamel expansion of $x$ uses only finitely many members of $B$. But $v(x-s_n)\ge(n+1)\delta$, together with valuation independence, forces the coefficient of every $b_i$, $1\le i\le n$, in $x$ to equal $1$. This holds for every $n$, a contradiction. The same proof uses only a countable nest for $\No$.
\end{proof}
It is important that we did \emph{not} assume the family $(b_n)$ itself is Hahn-summable. Increasing leading values alone do not ensure summability in arbitrary rank. The nested-ball proof avoids that error.

\begin{proposition}[No best finite-monomial approximation]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:nobest}
For $x\notin \FinS{\Gamma}$, the set $\{v(x-e):e\in \FinS{\Gamma}\}$ has no largest member.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given $e$, the difference $z=x-e$ is nonzero. Replace $e$ by $e+\lc(z)t^{v(z)}$. This remains in $\FinS{\Gamma}$ and strictly improves the value of the error.
\end{proof}
The inclusion $\FinS{\Gamma}\subseteq H_\Gamma$ is therefore immediate in the valued-vector-space sense: every leading residue is already represented by a monomial. It is not ``nice'' in the best-approximation sense used in \cite{Kuhlmann}. The quotient in Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:fullcodim} is an \emph{algebraic} quotient; no best-approximation quotient valuation has silently been assumed.
\subsection{A precise symmetry obstruction}
For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ and $a>0$, define a coordinate reweighting
\[
 T_{\gamma,a}(x)=x+(a-1)x_\gamma t^\gamma.
\]
It is a strong, order-preserving, real-linear automorphism. If $\gamma\ne0$, it fixes the entire real subfield. It is generally not a field automorphism.

\begin{theorem}[No symmetry-invariant projective Hamel basis]\label{rvs:eu:thm:naturalobstruction}
For $\Gamma\ne0$, there is no Hamel basis $B$ of $H_\Gamma$ whose set of real lines $\{\R b:b\in B\}$ is invariant under all $T_{\gamma,a}$ with $\gamma\ne0$ and $a>0$. The assertion also holds for the full surreal class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a basis vector $b$ has at least two nonzero coordinates. Choose a nonzero exponent $\gamma$ in its support. As $a$ ranges over three distinct positive reals, the vectors
\[
 b+(a-1)b_\gamma t^\gamma
\]
lie in the two-dimensional span of $b$ and $t^\gamma$ but determine three distinct lines. Invariance would make those three lines basis lines, contradicting linear independence. Hence every basis vector would have one-point support. Such vectors span only $\FinS{\Gamma}$, not $H_\Gamma$.
\end{proof}
This is stronger than observing that there are many Hamel bases, but narrower than claiming that no conceivable enriched structure can define one. It rules out naturality with respect to a specified elementary family of strong ordered-vector-space symmetries, even when bases are regarded only up to rescaling.

\subsection{Why the simplicity relation matters}
\label{rvs:w:rem:shear}
If $\gamma<\delta$ and $\gamma\ne0$, the shear
\[
 S(x)=x+x_\gamma t^\delta
\]
is another strong order-preserving real-linear automorphism fixing $\R$. Its inverse is $x\mapsto x-x_\gamma t^\delta$. It preserves every leading value and leading coefficient, but sends $t^\gamma$ to $t^\gamma+t^\delta$. Thus even the distinguished monomial representative of a value is not recoverable from the ordered-vector-space structure alone.

The monomials are canonical in the \emph{surreal structure with simplicity}, or in a Hahn presentation with a specified cross-section. There is no contradiction between that canonicity and the shear example. They refer to different categories of structure-preserving maps.

\part{Strong maps, duals, and topology}\label{rvs:w:part:strong}

\section{Strong linear maps and the intrinsic real dual}\label{rvs:sec:strong-maps}
\label{rvs:eu:sec:duals}

A Hamel-linear map is determined by a Hamel basis, hence inherits all of its
choice dependence.  A map adapted to Conway normal form should instead
commute with the distinguished strong sums.

\subsection{Strong linearity}

\begin{definition}[Strong real-linear map]\label[definition]{rvs:def:strong-map}
An \(\R\)-linear class map \(T:\No\to\No\) is \emph{strong} if for every
Hahn-summable set-indexed family \((x_i)_{i\in I}\), the family
\((Tx_i)_{i\in I}\) is Hahn summable and
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:strong-map}
  T\!\left(\sH_{i\in I}x_i\right)=\sH_{i\in I}Tx_i.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The definition includes both preservation of admissibility and commutation
with the sum.  Merely writing a coefficientwise formula is not enough unless
its output supports satisfy the Hahn conditions.

\begin{definition}[Hahn-compatible column family]
A class family \((y_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\No}\) in \(\No\) is
\emph{Hahn compatible} if for every reverse-well-ordered set
\(S\subseteq\No\), the family \((y_\gamma)_{\gamma\in S}\) is Hahn summable.
\end{definition}

Write
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:matrix-entry}
  y_\gamma=\sH_{\delta}
  a_{\delta\gamma}\omega^\delta,
  \qquad a_{\delta\gamma}\in\R.
\end{equation}
Then compatibility says exactly that, for every reverse-well-ordered set
\(S\),
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\bigcup_{\gamma\in S}\{\delta:a_{\delta\gamma}\ne0\}\) is reverse
well ordered, and
\item for each row exponent \(\delta\), only finitely many
\(\gamma\in S\) have \(a_{\delta\gamma}\ne0\).
\end{enumerate}
This is the surreal-Hahn analogue of a column-finiteness condition.

\begin{theorem}[Hahn-matrix classification of strong maps]
\label{rvs:thm:strong-matrix}
The assignments
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:column-correspondence}
  T\longmapsto(T\omega^\gamma)_{\gamma\in\No}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:T-from-columns}
  (y_\gamma)\longmapsto
  \left[
  \sH_{\gamma\in S}r_\gamma\omega^\gamma
  \longmapsto
  \sH_{\gamma\in S}r_\gamma y_\gamma
  \right]
\end{equation}
are inverse bijections between strong \(\R\)-linear endomorphisms of \(\No\)
and Hahn-compatible column families.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(T\) be strong.  For a reverse-well-ordered set \(S\), the monomial
family \((\omega^\gamma)_{\gamma\in S}\) is Hahn summable.  Therefore
\((T\omega^\gamma)_{\gamma\in S}\) is Hahn summable, so the columns are
compatible.  Applying strong linearity to a normal form gives
\eqref{rvs:eq:T-from-columns}, establishing uniqueness.

Conversely, suppose \((y_\gamma)\) is compatible and define \(T\) by
\eqref{rvs:eq:T-from-columns}.  The output sum exists by compatibility and is
plainly real linear.  Let \((x_i)_{i\in I}\) be Hahn summable and write
\(x_i=\sH_\gamma r_{i\gamma}\omega^\gamma\).  Put
\(S=\bigcup_i\supp(x_i)\).  Compatibility says that
\((y_\gamma)_{\gamma\in S}\) has reverse-well-ordered union of supports and
is locally finite in every output exponent.  For a fixed \(\gamma\), only
finitely many \(i\) have \(r_{i\gamma}\ne0\).  Hence the flattened family
\((r_{i\gamma}y_\gamma)_{(i,\gamma)}\) is Hahn summable: at each output
exponent only finitely many \(\gamma\)'s contribute, and for each such
\(\gamma\) only finitely many \(i\)'s contribute.  Associativity of finite
coefficient sums now yields
\[
 T\!\left(\sH_i x_i\right)
 =\sH_{i,\gamma}r_{i\gamma}y_\gamma
 =\sH_i Tx_i.
\]
Thus \(T\) is strong.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem is a universal property of the Conway--Hahn basis.  A Hamel basis
determines all algebraic linear maps; the monomial Hahn basis determines all
strong linear maps, with the compatibility condition recording exactly which
images of basis vectors extend to every normal form.
\end{remark}

\Cref{rvs:thm:strong-matrix} is the real-linear, proper-class version for the
scalar class \(\No\) itself.  It answers source 53's question on strongly
linear matrices (\cref{rvs:w:rem:answered-matrices}).

\subsection{The strong real-valued dual}\label{rvs:subsec:strong-dual}

Embed \(\R\) in \(\No\) as the exponent-zero line.  A family of real numbers
is Hahn summable in this subspace exactly when only finitely many members are
nonzero, because all nonzero terms occupy the same exponent \(0\).

\begin{definition}[Summation-finite exponent class]\label[definition]{rvs:def:summation-finite}
A subclass \(A\subseteq\No\) is \emph{summation finite} if
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:summation-finite}
  A\cap S\text{ is finite for every reverse-well-ordered set }
  S\subseteq\No.
\end{equation}
A weight \(a:\No\to\R\) is summation finite if its active class
\(\supp(a)=\{\gamma:a(\gamma)\ne0\}\) is summation finite.
\end{definition}


\begin{proposition}[Sequential characterization]
\label[proposition]{rvs:prop:summation-finite-seq}
A subclass \(A\subseteq\No\) is summation finite if and only if it contains
no strictly decreasing sequence
\(a_0>a_1>a_2>\cdots\) indexed by \(\omega\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A strictly decreasing sequence has reverse-well-ordered range: every nonempty
subset of its range has a largest member, corresponding to the least index
that occurs.  Hence such a sequence violates summation finiteness.
Conversely, if \(A\cap S\) is infinite for a reverse-well-ordered set \(S\),
enumerate that intersection in decreasing order.  Its order type is an
infinite ordinal and its first \(\omega\) members form a strictly decreasing
sequence in \(A\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Classification of the strong real dual]
\label{rvs:thm:strong-dual}
An \(\R\)-linear map \(\Lambda:\No\to\R\) is strong if and only if there is a
summation-finite weight \(a:\No\to\R\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:strong-functional}
  \Lambda(x)=
  \sum_{\gamma\in\supp(x)\cap\supp(a)}
  a(\gamma)[\omega^\gamma]x.
\end{equation}
The sum in \eqref{rvs:eq:strong-functional} is always finite.  The weight is
unique and equals
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:weight-from-Lambda}
  a(\gamma)=\Lambda(\omega^\gamma).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose \(\Lambda\) is strong and define \(a\) by
\eqref{rvs:eq:weight-from-Lambda}.  For every reverse-well-ordered set \(S\), the
family \((\omega^\gamma)_{\gamma\in S}\) is Hahn summable.  Its image family
\((a(\gamma))_{\gamma\in S}\) must be summable in \(\R\), hence only
finitely many of those reals are nonzero.  Thus \(\supp(a)\) is summation
finite.  Applying \(\Lambda\) to the strong normal form of \(x\) yields
\eqref{rvs:eq:strong-functional}.

Conversely, let \(a\) be summation finite and define \(\Lambda\) by
\eqref{rvs:eq:strong-functional}.  It is real linear.  If \((x_i)_{i\in I}\) is
Hahn summable and \(S=\bigcup_i\supp(x_i)\), then
\(F=S\cap\supp(a)\) is finite.  For each \(\gamma\in F\), only finitely many
\(i\) have a nonzero \(\gamma\)-coefficient.  Therefore only finitely many
\(\Lambda(x_i)\) can be nonzero, and finite rearrangement of coefficients
gives
\(\Lambda(\sH_i x_i)=\sum_i\Lambda(x_i)\).  Hence \(\Lambda\) is strong.
Uniqueness follows by evaluation on monomials.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Coordinate functionals]
For every \(\gamma\), the coefficient map
\[
  \pi_\gamma(x)=[\omega^\gamma]x
\]
is strong.  It corresponds to the weight supported on the singleton
\(\{\gamma\}\).
\end{example}

The next example shows that a strong functional need not use only finitely
many coordinates globally.

\begin{lemma}[Well-ordered active classes are summation finite]
\label[lemma]{rvs:lem:well-ordered-active}
If a subclass \(A\subseteq\No\) is well ordered by the inherited order, then
it is summation finite.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a reverse-well-ordered set \(S\), the intersection \(A\cap S\) is both
well ordered and reverse well ordered.  An infinite linear order cannot have
both properties: an infinite ordinal, or any infinite set of ordinal type,
has a subset with no greatest element.  Hence \(A\cap S\) is finite.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The ordinal-coefficient trace]\label[example]{rvs:ex:ordinal-trace}
The class \(\On\) is well ordered, so
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:ordinal-trace}
  \operatorname{Tr}_{\On}(x)
  =\sum_{\gamma\in\supp(x)\cap\On}
    [\omega^\gamma]x
\end{equation}
is a well-defined strong \(\R\)-linear functional.  Its active exponent
class is the proper class \(\On\).  Every individual normal form contains
only finitely many ordinal exponents, even though the functional is active at
all ordinals.
\end{example}


\subsubsection*{The strong dual of a set-sized slice (source 52)}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 proves the same classification for a set-sized
workspace \(H_\Gamma\).  Its weights have reverse-well-ordered support in
\(t\)-exponents, that is well-ordered support in \(\omega\)-exponents; by
\cref{rvs:lem:well-ordered-active,rvs:prop:summation-finite-seq} (and
dependent choice) this is exactly summation-finiteness.  So for
\(\Gamma\subseteq\No\) the theorem is \cref{rvs:thm:strong-dual} restricted
to \(H_\Gamma\); source 52 adds that strong functionals are
valuation-continuous.

\subsection{The strong dual has an explicit support description}
When the target is $\R$ alone, a Hahn-summable family of target vectors means that only finitely many are nonzero. This convention differs from ordinary convergence of a numerical real series.

\begin{theorem}[Strong real dual]\label{rvs:eu:thm:strongdual}
Let $\Gamma$ be set-sized. Strong real-linear maps $f:H_\Gamma\to\R$ correspond exactly to weight functions $a:\Gamma\to\R$ whose nonzero support is reverse well ordered. The formula is
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:strongdual}
 f(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\supp_t x\cap\supp_t a}a_\gamma x_\gamma,
\end{equation}
where the displayed sum is always finite.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $f$ is strong, let $a_\gamma=f(t^\gamma)$. An infinite increasing sequence in $\supp_t a$ would give a summable monomial family with infinitely many nonzero images. Therefore $\supp_t a$ has no such sequence. Under choice, this is equivalent to reverse well-ordering: a nonempty subset with no largest member recursively yields an increasing sequence. Strongness applied to the monomial expansion of $x$ now gives \eqref{rvs:eu:eq:strongdual}.

Conversely, a well-ordered set and a reverse-well-ordered set have finite intersection; an infinite intersection would contain an increasing sequence. For a summable family $(x_i)$, its joint support meets $\supp_t a$ in a finite set, and each of those exponents occurs in only finitely many $x_i$. Thus only finitely many $f(x_i)$ can be nonzero, and all summations in the required identity reduce to a finite calculation.
\end{proof}
Every strong functional is valuation-continuous: the support of $a$, when nonempty, has a largest member, so the functional vanishes on a sufficiently high tail. Coordinate extraction is the simplest example.

\begin{remark}[Three duals already appear]
There are at least three very different notions of real dual for \(\No\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item the algebraic dual determined by a choice of Hamel basis;
\item the intrinsic strong dual classified by \cref{rvs:thm:strong-dual};
\item the order-bounded dual, which is zero by \cref{rvs:thm:order-dual-zero}.
\end{enumerate}
This mirrors, at the scalar-class level, the repository's separation of
represented, strong, and valuation-continuous duals for set-sized Hahn vector
spaces.
\end{remark}

\section{Order duality and the failure of Schauder convergence}\label{rvs:sec:topology}
\label{rvs:cv:sec:topology}

The strong dual is nontrivial because it is adapted to formal support sums.
Demanding compatibility with the total order has the opposite effect: every
real-valued order-bounded linear functional vanishes.

\subsection{The order-bounded real dual is zero}

\begin{definition}[Order-bounded linear map]
An \(\R\)-linear map \(L:\No\to\R\) is \emph{order bounded} if for every
\(y\ge0\) there is a real number \(M_y\) such that
\[
  \abs{x}\le y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\abs{L(x)}\le M_y.
\]
Equivalently, it maps every surreal order interval to a bounded subset of
\(\R\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Collapse of the order-bounded real dual]
\label{rvs:thm:order-dual-zero}
Every order-bounded \(\R\)-linear map \(L:\No\to\R\) is zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(x\in\No\).  If \(x=0\) there is nothing to prove.  Put
\(y=\omega\abs{x}>0\).  For every positive integer \(n\), one has
\(n\abs{x}<\omega\abs{x}=y\), so \(nx\in[-y,y]\).  Order boundedness gives a
real \(M_y\) with
\[
  n\abs{L(x)}=\abs{L(nx)}\le M_y
  \qquad(n\in\N_{>0}).
\]
No positive real number can satisfy these inequalities for every \(n\).
Hence \(L(x)=0\).  Since \(x\) was arbitrary, \(L=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No positive real linear state]\label[corollary]{rvs:cor:no-positive-state}
Every positive \(\R\)-linear map \(L:\No\to\R\) is zero.  In particular,
there is no positive real-linear retraction of \(\No\) onto its real
subfield.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If \(L\) is positive and \(-y\le x\le y\), then
\(-L(y)\le L(x)\le L(y)\).  Thus \(L\) is order bounded and
\cref{rvs:thm:order-dual-zero} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Constant term is strong but not positive]
The coefficient functional
\[
  \ct(x)=[\omega^0]x
\]
is strong and restricts to the identity on \(\R\), but it is not positive:
\(\omega-1>0\) while \(\ct(\omega-1)=-1\).  This example shows exactly why
\cref{rvs:cor:no-positive-state} does not contradict the existence of canonical
coefficient retractions.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[No nonzero order-compatible real seminorm]
\label[corollary]{rvs:cor:no-order-seminorm}
Suppose \(p:\No\to\R_{\ge0}\) is absolutely homogeneous over \(\R\) and
monotone with respect to surreal magnitude:
\[
  \abs{x}\le\abs{y}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p(x)\le p(y).
\]
Then \(p=0\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For \(x\ne0\) and every \(n\in\N_{>0}\),
\(\abs{nx}<\abs{\omega x}\).  Hence
\[
 n p(x)=p(nx)\le p(\omega x),
\]
where the right side is one fixed real number.  Therefore \(p(x)=0\).
\end{proof}

A choice of Hamel basis can of course be declared orthonormal, producing a
noncanonical real-valued norm.  The corollary says that no such norm can
respect the ambient surreal order in the displayed sense.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 proves the vanishing of positive functionals in
every non-Archimedean ordered field containing \(\R\);
\cref{rvs:cor:no-positive-state} is the case \(K=\No\), and
\cref{rvs:thm:order-dual-zero} strengthens it, for \(\No\), from positive to
order-bounded maps.

\begin{proposition}[No global positive real observable]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:positive}
Let $K\supsetneq\R$ be a non-Archimedean ordered field containing $\R$. Every positive real-linear map $L:K\to\R$ is zero. In particular this holds for $H_\Gamma$, $\Gamma\ne0$, and for $\No$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $u>n$ for every positive integer $n$. For $x>0$, positivity gives $L(ux)\ge nL(x)$ for every $n$. Both values are real, and $L(x)\ge0$, so $L(x)=0$. Differences of positive elements give $L=0$.
\end{proof}
On the finite-element valuation ring $\mathcal O=H_{\ge0}$, the coefficient map $x\mapsto x_0$ is instead the unique positive real-linear map fixing $1$. An infinitesimal $\varepsilon$ satisfies $-1/n<\varepsilon<1/n$ for every $n$, so positivity forces its image to zero; the real part is fixed. The same coefficient map extends linearly to all $H_\Gamma$, but not positively: $t^{-\delta}-1>0$ has constant coefficient $-1$.

This explains why a real scalar field alone does not provide a Hilbert-space interpretation of all surreal scales. Arbitrary Hamel-based inner products on set-sized slices can be introduced, but they are extra choices and cannot turn the surreal order into ordinary real norm geometry.
\subsection{The fine valuation topology}

The natural valuation \eqref{rvs:eq:natural-valuation} gives fine balls
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:valuation-ball}
  B_\gamma(0)=\{x\in\No:v(x)>\gamma\},
  \qquad \gamma\in\No.
\end{equation}
Translates of these balls define the full natural valuation topology.  It is
``fine'' because every surreal threshold is allowed, not merely thresholds
from a fixed set-sized subgroup.

The following elementary fact about \(\No\) is decisive.

\begin{lemma}[Every set of surreals is bounded]
\label[lemma]{rvs:lem:set-bounded}
Every set \(A\subseteq\No\) has a surreal upper bound and a surreal lower
bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Conway cut \(\{A\mid\varnothing\}\) is greater than every member of
\(A\).  Applying this to \(-A\) gives a lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Set-indexed convergence is eventually constant]
\label{rvs:thm:eventually-constant}
Let \((x_i)_{i\in I}\) be a net in \(\No\) whose directed index \(I\) is a
set.  If \(x_i\to x\) in the full natural valuation topology, then
\(x_i=x\) eventually.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider the set
\[
 A=\{v(x_i-x):i\in I,\ x_i\ne x\}\subseteq\No.
\]
By \cref{rvs:lem:set-bounded}, choose \(\beta\in\No\) strictly above every member
of \(A\).  Convergence to \(x\) in the neighborhood
\(x+B_\beta(0)\) implies that eventually
\(v(x_i-x)>\beta\).  No nonzero difference in the net has such a value by
the choice of \(\beta\), so eventually \(x_i-x=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Non-discrete but net-rigid]
The topology is not discrete: no single threshold \(\gamma\) excludes every
nonzero infinitesimal, because \(\omega^{-\delta}\in B_\gamma(0)\) for
sufficiently large \(\delta\).  Nevertheless, every \emph{set}-indexed net
has only a set of valuation values, hence a common upper bound.  This is a
proper-class phenomenon: the neighborhood system itself has proper-class
cofinality.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Normal forms are not Schauder expansions]
\label[corollary]{rvs:cor:not-schauder}
If a surreal has infinite normal-form support, no set-indexed net of its
finite support truncations converges to it in the full natural valuation
topology.  Consequently the Conway monomials are not a Schauder basis for
that topology.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A convergent net of finite truncations would be eventually equal to its
limit by \cref{rvs:thm:eventually-constant}, forcing the limit to have finite
support.
\end{proof}

The same argument applies to the fine order topology.

\begin{corollary}[Fine order convergence is also eventually constant]
\label[corollary]{rvs:cor:order-net}
Every set-indexed net convergent in the order topology generated by all
surreal intervals \((x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)\), with
\(\varepsilon>0\), is eventually constant.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the set of nonzero distances
\(D=\{\abs{x_i-x}:x_i\ne x\}\), the cut \(\{0\mid D\}\) gives a positive
surreal \(\varepsilon\) smaller than every member of \(D\).  Eventual
membership in the \(\varepsilon\)-interval around \(x\) therefore forces
equality.
\end{proof}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 proves a stronger form of
\cref{rvs:cor:order-net}: every set is uniformly discrete, and Cauchy nets
are also eventually constant.  It works with interval neighbourhoods only.

We use ``order topology on $\No$'' only through its interval neighborhoods. We do not treat a proper-class collection of open subclasses as an ordinary set topology.

\begin{theorem}[Every set is uniformly discrete in the full surreal line]\label{rvs:eu:thm:classdiscrete}
For every set $A\subseteq\No$, there is $\epsilon>0$ in $\No$ such that
\[
 a\ne b\text{ in }A\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |a-b|>\epsilon.
\]
Every set-indexed convergent net in the full surreal order topology is eventually constant. Every set-indexed Cauchy net is also eventually constant.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The positive pairwise distances form a set $D$. The cut $\{0\mid D\}$ is positive and smaller than every member of $D$; if $D$ is empty choose any positive $\epsilon$. This proves uniform discreteness. For convergence to $x$, apply the result to the range of the net together with $x$, then use the $\epsilon$-neighborhood of $x$. For a Cauchy net, apply it to the range and use the Cauchy condition at this fixed $\epsilon$: all entries in a sufficiently late tail must coincide.
\end{proof}
In particular, every set-sized Hahn subfield has the \emph{discrete} topology induced from the full surreal order topology, although its internal valuation topology is non-discrete. The sequence $\omega^{-n}$ converges to zero in $H_\Z$ internally, but not in the full surreal line, where $\omega^{-\omega}$ is a smaller positive scale than every term.

\begin{corollary}[The full finite-monomial class is closed]\label[corollary]{rvs:eu:cor:classEclosed}
$\Fin$ is closed in the interval sense in $\No$: for every $x\notin \Fin$ there is an interval about $x$ missing $\Fin$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose a valuation exponent $\delta$ above the entire set $\supp_t x$. For any finite-support $e$, at least one exponent of the infinite support of $x$ remains uncanceled. Hence $v(x-e)<\delta$ and $|x-e|>t^\delta$. The interval of radius $t^\delta$ about $x$ works.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}[Not a topological vector space over the usual reals]
For nonzero $\Gamma$, the scalar copy of $\R$ is discrete in the internal valuation topology, and also in the full surreal order topology. The inclusion of the usual Euclidean $\R$ is not continuous: a sufficiently small valuation neighborhood meets it only at zero. Thus scalar multiplication is not continuous when $\R$ has its usual topology. These topologies give vector spaces over the \emph{discrete} real field, not ordinary real locally convex spaces.
\end{warning}

\subsection{Closedness of the finite span and the trichotomy (source 53)}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53 shows that no approximation by finite-support
vectors at all, not only by truncations, reaches an infinite-support
surreal.  Its neighbourhoods are \(U_\gamma=B_{-\gamma}(0)\).

The most important conceptual trap is to treat a Conway normal form as an ordinary
limit of its finite truncations. It is a legitimate Hahn sum, but generally not a
limit in the full natural valuation topology.

For $\gamma\in\No$, let
\[
  U_\gamma=\{0\}\cup\{x\ne0:\lead(x)<\gamma\}.
\]
These classes form the zero-neighborhood basis corresponding to the valuation
$v(x)=-\lead(x)$: a difference is small when all of its exponents lie far below
the chosen threshold.

\begin{theorem}[Closedness of the finite monomial span]\label{rvs:cv:thm:finite-span-closed}
The finite-support subspace $\Fin$ is closed in the full natural valuation
topology on $\No$. Equivalently,
\[
  \overline{\Span_{\R}\{\omega^a:a\in\No\}}^{\,v}=\Fin.
\]
In particular, no infinite-support surreal is the natural-valuation limit of a net
of finite-support surreals.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $x\notin\Fin$ and put $S=\supp(x)$, an infinite set. Every set of surreals has
a strict lower bound: for example, Conway's cut $\{\,\mid S\}$ is less than every
member of $S$. Choose such a $\gamma$, so $\gamma<a$ for all $a\in S$.

For any $y\in\Fin$, the finite set $\supp(y)$ cannot contain $S$. Choose
$a\in S\setminus\supp(y)$. The coefficient of $\omega^a$ in $x-y$ is then
$c_a(x)\ne0$, so
\[
  \lead(x-y)\ge a>\gamma.
\]
Hence $x-y\notin U_\gamma$. The neighborhood $x+U_\gamma$ is disjoint from
$\Fin$, proving that the complement of $\Fin$ is open.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The geometric series does not converge valuation-topologically]
Let
\[
  x=\sum_{n\ge0}\omega^{-n},
  \qquad
  s_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\omega^{-n}.
\]
Then
\[
  \lead(x-s_N)=-(N+1),\qquad v(x-s_N)=N+1.
\]
Although $N+1$ tends to infinity among the natural numbers, it never eventually
exceeds the surreal threshold $\omega$. Thus $(s_N)$ does not converge to $x$ in
the full natural valuation topology. Its Hahn sum is nevertheless perfectly
well defined and equals $(1-\omega^{-1})^{-1}$.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}[No natural-valuation Schauder basis]
The monomial family is not a Schauder basis, transfinite Schauder basis, or net
basis for $\No$ when ``convergence'' is interpreted using the full natural
valuation topology and approximants are finite linear combinations.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Algebraic--topological--summation trichotomy]\label{rvs:cv:thm:trichotomy}
Let $\M=\{\omega^a:a\in\No\}$. Then
\begin{align*}
  \text{algebraic span of }\M
    &=\Fin,\\
  \text{natural-valuation closure of that span}
    &=\Fin,\\
  \text{smallest class subspace containing }\M
     \text{ and closed under strongly summable set-families}
    &=\No.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first line is \cref{rvs:cv:prop:monomial-independence}; the second is
\cref{rvs:cv:thm:finite-span-closed}. For the third, every surreal is the strongly
summable family of its normal-form monomials by \cref{rvs:cv:thm:hahn-basis}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why no contradiction arises]
On a fixed support slice $\Hahn(A)\cong\R^A$, the product topology makes finite-support
vectors dense. The natural valuation topology compares a tail against \emph{every}
surreal scale, including thresholds below the entire set $A$. It is therefore much
finer in the direction relevant to the closure theorem. Coarsened valuations used
in transseries or rank-one Hahn fields may restore ordinary convergence for selected
series; the theorem concerns the full natural valuation with value group $\No$.
\end{remark}
\subsection{How nontrivial convergence can be recovered}

The anti-Schauder theorem concerns the \emph{full} value group \(\No\).
Useful analytic work generally chooses a set-sized Hahn workspace
\(k((t^\Gamma))\), a set-sized cofinal scale family, or a coarser topology.
Within such a workspace, a support can be cofinal in the chosen value group,
and partial sums may converge.  This is why the repository's analytical
reports carefully distinguish set-sized workspaces, strong summation, and
fine surreal topology.  A future theory of ``Schauder bases for \(\No\)''
must specify which scale system has been retained and which proper-class
neighborhoods have been discarded.

\section{Completion of the monomial span on a slice (source 52; the three-duals report)}\label{rvs:w:sec:completion}\label{rvs:eu:sec:topology}

\wtag{} The statements of this section are not new.  They are the diagonal
case of the report \emph{Three Duals at Surreal Scales}
(\path{surcomplex/three-duals-of-hahn-vector-spaces}; ``three-duals''
below), and source 52 calls that report ``an explicit precedent, not a
novelty''.  Source 52's direct proofs are printed as second routes.

\paragraph{The dictionary.}  Let \(V=\R^{(\Gamma)}\) with basis
\((e_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\), and let
\[
 \iota:H_\Gamma\longrightarrow V((t^\Gamma)),\qquad
 \iota\Bigl(\hSum_\gamma x_\gamma t^\gamma\Bigr)=\hSum_\gamma t^\gamma\,(x_\gamma e_\gamma).
\]
\(\iota\) is an \(\R\)-linear embedding preserving supports, valuations and
strong sums, and the coefficients of \(\iota(x)\) below a cut span a space
of dimension equal to the number of support points of \(x\) below that cut.
Hence, by three-duals Theorem 4.2 (finite rank below every cut),
\[
 \FinS\Gamma=\iota^{-1}\bigl(E_\Gamma(V)\bigr),\qquad
 \LfS\Gamma=\iota^{-1}\bigl(C_\Gamma(V)\bigr).
\]
The witnesses in the proofs of three-duals Theorems 4.5 and 4.6 have the
form \(\sH_n t^{\gamma_n}e_n\) with independent \(e_n\); choosing
\(e_n=e_{\gamma_n}\) puts them in the image of \(\iota\).  So
\cref{rvs:eu:thm:completion,rvs:eu:thm:cofinality} are three-duals
Theorems 4.2, 4.5 and 4.6 (with Lemma 4.4) pulled back along \(\iota\), and
\cref{rvs:eu:prop:enlargement} is three-duals Theorem 10.1 (cofinal
preservation and noncofinal collapse) pulled back.  Do not read
\(\FinS\Gamma,\LfS\Gamma\) as three-duals' \(E_\Gamma(\R),C_\Gamma(\R)\), which are
all of \(H_\Gamma\).

The internal valuation topology on $H_\Gamma$ has neighborhoods $H_{>\gamma}$ of zero. We call a support \emph{left-finite} if it has finite intersection with $(-\infty,\gamma]$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact completion of the monomial span]\label{rvs:eu:thm:completion}
For $\Gamma\ne0$,
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:completion}
 \LfS{\Gamma}=\overline{\FinS{\Gamma}}^{\,v}
 =\{x\in H_\Gamma:(\forall\gamma\in\Gamma)\,
 |\supp_t x\cap(-\infty,\gamma]|<\infty\}.
\end{equation}
This space is complete and is a realization of the valuation completion of $\FinS{\Gamma}$. The net of all finite monomial subsums of $x$ converges to $x$ exactly when $x\in \LfS{\Gamma}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 52), direct]
To have $v(x-e)>\gamma$, the finite-support series $e$ must agree with every coefficient of $x$ at exponents at most $\gamma$. Such an $e$ exists exactly when there are finitely many of these coefficients. This proves the closure formula and the finite-subsums criterion.

For completeness, let $(x_i)$ be a Cauchy net in $\LfS{\Gamma}$. Below any fixed cutoff, its coefficients eventually agree exactly with those of one $x_i$ and hence have finite support there. The stabilized coefficients define a function whose support is left-finite. Such a support is well ordered: for a nonempty subset, choose one member $s$ and take the least of the finitely many members at most $s$. The resulting Hahn series is in $\LfS{\Gamma}$ and is the net's limit. Density of $\FinS{\Gamma}$ gives the asserted completion.
\end{proof}
No growth bound on the real coefficients appears in this theorem. The topology regards nonzero real scalars as having value zero; coefficients cannot compensate for a support that fails to escape to higher valuations.
\begin{theorem}[Cofinality and cyclicity classification]\label{rvs:eu:thm:cofinality}
For a nonzero ordered abelian group $\Gamma$:
\begin{align*}
 \LfS{\Gamma}\ne \FinS{\Gamma}&\iff\cf(\Gamma)=\aleph_0,\\
 \LfS{\Gamma}=H_\Gamma&\iff\Gamma\simeq_{\mathrm{ord}}\Z.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 52), direct]
An infinite left-finite support can be listed in increasing order as an $\omega$-sequence: each member has only finitely many predecessors. This sequence must be cofinal in $\Gamma$, or a bound would contain infinitely many support elements below it. Conversely, an increasing cofinal sequence is a left-finite infinite support. This proves the first equivalence.

In $\Z$, every well-ordered subset is bounded below, and has finite intersection with every bounded interval, so every support is left-finite.

Suppose $\Gamma$ is not cyclic. If it has a least positive element $\epsilon$ but is not $\Z\epsilon$, there is a positive $\eta$ greater than every $n\epsilon$. Indeed, any positive element bounded by some $n\epsilon$ must be an integer multiple of $\epsilon$, by minimality of $\epsilon$. Thus $\{n\epsilon:n\ge0\}$ is an infinite bounded well-ordered support.

If there is no least positive element, choose a strictly decreasing positive sequence $\epsilon_n$. Then $\{-\epsilon_n:n\ge1\}$ is an infinite increasing, bounded support. Either construction yields a Hahn series outside $\LfS{\Gamma}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Changing the available precision scales}
\begin{proposition}[Ambient enlargement]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:enlargement}
Let $\Gamma\subseteq\Delta$ be ordered abelian groups. If $\Gamma$ is cofinal in $\Delta$, then $\LfS{\Delta}\cap H_\Gamma=\LfS{\Gamma}$. If it is not cofinal, then
\[
 \LfS{\Delta}\cap H_\Gamma=\FinS{\Gamma}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 52), direct]
In the cofinal case, every $\Delta$-cutoff is bounded above by a $\Gamma$-cutoff, so the two left-finiteness tests agree. Otherwise choose $\delta\in\Delta$ above all of $\Gamma$. The left-finiteness test at $\delta$ requires the entire support of a vector in $H_\Gamma$ to be finite.
\end{proof}
This is the monomial-span version of the noncofinal-enlargement phenomenon emphasized in the repository's completion and tail-span reports \cite{RepoDuals,RepoTail}.

\begin{theorem}[Exactly what monomial-invisible continuous functionals detect]\label{rvs:eu:thm:invisible}

\[
 \bigcap_{f\in\mathcal N}\ker f=\LfS{\Gamma}.
\]
In particular, $\mathcal N=0$ if and only if $\Gamma\simeq_{\mathrm{ord}}\Z$, for $\Gamma\ne0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Continuity forces each such kernel to contain the closure $\LfS{\Gamma}$. Conversely, if $x\notin \LfS{\Gamma}$, there is a $\gamma$ for which
\[
 x\notin \FinS{\Gamma}+H_{>\gamma}.
\]
On the real vector-space quotient by $\FinS{\Gamma}+H_{>\gamma}$ choose a linear functional taking the nonzero class of $x$ to $1$, extending from its one-dimensional span by a Hamel basis. Its pullback annihilates $\FinS{\Gamma}$ and the open subspace $H_{>\gamma}$, so is continuous. It detects $x$.

\end{proof}

\section{The Euler quotient: canonical extra scalars}\label{rvs:eu:sec:euler}
Throughout this section and the next two, $\Gamma$ is a nonzero additive subgroup of $\R$. This restriction is essential: the exponent itself will be used as a \emph{real} coefficient.

\subsection{A strong derivation diagonal in Hahn coordinates}
\begin{definition}[Euler derivation]
Define
\[
 \EuD(x)=\hSum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma x_\gamma t^\gamma.
\]
\end{definition}
It is real-linear and strong, since it does not enlarge supports. It is a derivation: in a product coefficient at exponent $\eta=\gamma+\delta$, the identity $\eta=\gamma+\delta$ gives the Leibniz rule term by term. Hahn convolution has only finitely many contributions at each exponent, so this computation is legitimate.

No global surreal derivative is being redefined. On an appropriate rational-exponent slice, this is the familiar Euler operator $t\,d/dt$. The repository already uses such operators \cite{RepoTail}. Here the object of study is their action on real vector-space quotients.

\begin{proposition}[All algebraic spectral torsion is finite-monomial]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:torsion}
Regard $H_\Gamma$ as an $\R[X]$-module by $Xx=\EuD x$. Its torsion submodule is exactly $\FinS{\Gamma}$: % ed.: source 52's bare D, its Euler operator, renamed \EuD
\[
 \{x:(\exists p\in\R[X]\setminus\{0\})\ p(\EuD)x=0\}=\FinS{\Gamma}.
\]
Its nonzero eigenspaces are precisely $\R t^\gamma$, with eigenvalue $\gamma\in\Gamma$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The equation $p(\EuD)x=0$ is the coefficientwise condition $p(\gamma)x_\gamma=0$. A nonzero real polynomial has finitely many roots, so $x$ has finite support. Conversely, a vector with support $\{\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m\}$ is killed by $\prod_j(\EuD-\gamma_j)$. The same argument for $p(X)=X-\gamma$ gives the eigenspaces.
\end{proof}
Thus the direct sum of all eigenspaces is a proper subspace, even though $\EuD$ is diagonal in the Hahn representation. Infinite-coordinate diagonalization is not algebraic diagonalization by a Hamel eigenbasis.

\subsection{Exact polynomial solution operators}
For $p\in\R[X]\setminus\{0\}$ put
\[
 Z_p=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:p(\gamma)=0\},\qquad
 P_{Z_p}x=\sum_{\gamma\in Z_p}x_\gamma t^\gamma,
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:green}
 G_p(x)=\hSum_{\substack{\gamma\in\Gamma\\p(\gamma)\ne0}}
 \frac{x_\gamma}{p(\gamma)}t^\gamma.
\end{equation}
These maps preserve supports, so they are strong and preserve both $\FinS{\Gamma}$ and $\LfS{\Gamma}$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite resonance is the only obstruction]\label{rvs:eu:thm:resonance}
For every nonzero $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:greenidentities}
 p(\EuD)G_p=G_pp(\EuD)=I-P_{Z_p}.
\end{equation}
Furthermore,
\[
 \ker p(\EuD)=H[Z_p],\qquad
 \im p(\EuD)=\{y:y_\gamma=0\text{ for all }\gamma\in Z_p\}.
\]
Consequently $p(\EuD)$ has finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel of the same dimension $|Z_p|$; in the algebraic Fredholm sense its index is zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a nonresonant exponent, multiplication by $p(\gamma)$ and division by it cancel. At a resonant exponent both compositions vanish. This proves \eqref{rvs:eu:eq:greenidentities}. The kernel and image assertions follow from the same coefficient equations; the cokernel is represented by the missing finitely many coordinate values.
\end{proof}
Repeated roots of $p$ contribute only one real coordinate each. Jordan chains do not arise: $\EuD$ is already coefficientwise scalar on each monomial line.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical rational-function vector space]\label{rvs:eu:thm:rational}
Let $Q_\Gamma=H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma}$. There is a unique $\R(X)$-vector-space structure on $Q_\Gamma$ extending its real scalar structure and making $X$ act by the induced Euler operator. For $p,q\in\R[X]$, $q\ne0$, it is given by
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:rationalaction}
 \frac{p(X)}{q(X)}\cdot[x]=[p(\EuD)G_q(x)].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $p(\EuD)$ preserves $\FinS{\Gamma}$ and descends to the quotient. By Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:resonance}, the induced maps of $p(\EuD)$ and $G_p$ are mutually inverse because $P_{Z_p}$ has image in $\FinS{\Gamma}$. Hence every nonzero polynomial acts invertibly. The commuting $\R[X]$-action therefore extends uniquely to its field of fractions, with the displayed formula.

Directly, different representatives of a rational function give identical coefficient multipliers outside the finite set of their poles, so their difference on any vector has finite support. Likewise, changing $x$ by an element of $\FinS{\Gamma}$ changes the result by an element of $\FinS{\Gamma}$. Thus neither fraction representatives nor vector representatives introduce ambiguity.
\end{proof}
The quotient is not a quotient \emph{field}: $\FinS{\Gamma}$ contains $1$ and is not an ideal of $H_\Gamma$. The new scalars are \emph{operators}, not multiplication by surreal representatives of rational functions. This distinction prevents a common but serious misreading of the construction.

\subsection{Exact dimension over the larger scalar field}
\begin{lemma}[Exponential-polynomial independence]\label[lemma]{rvs:eu:lem:expopoly}
Let $0<r_1<\cdots<r_m$ be distinct real numbers and let $p_1,\ldots,p_m\in\R[X]$. If $\delta>0$ and
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m p_j(n\delta)r_j^n=0
\]
for all sufficiently large integers $n$, then all the polynomials are zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose the largest $r_j$ whose polynomial is nonzero, and let that polynomial have degree $d$ and leading coefficient $a\ne0$. Divide the identity by $r_j^n n^d$. Its own contribution tends to $a\delta^d\ne0$. Every smaller-base contribution tends to zero, because an exponential decay dominates any fixed power of $n$. This is a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Continuum many independent rational-calculus directions]\label{rvs:eu:thm:rationaldim}
For $0\ne\Gamma\subseteq\R$,
\[
 \dim_{\R(X)}Q_\Gamma=\cont.
\]
For any fixed $\delta>0$ in $\Gamma$, the explicit family
\[
 \left\{\left[\frac1{1-r t^\delta}\right]:r>0\right\}
\]
is $\R(X)$-linearly independent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A finite rational-function relation may be multiplied by a common nonzero polynomial denominator, which is invertible on $Q_\Gamma$. We obtain
\[
 \sum_j p_j(\EuD)q_{r_j}\in \FinS{\Gamma}.
\]
At the exponents $n\delta$, this says $\sum_jp_j(n\delta)r_j^n=0$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Lemma~\ref{rvs:eu:lem:expopoly} forces all $p_j=0$, and hence all original rational coefficients vanish. This gives the lower bound $\cont$. The cardinality bound $|H_\Gamma|=\cont$ from Corollary~\ref{rvs:eu:cor:continuum} gives the upper bound.
\end{proof}
These witnesses are simple rational expressions in $t^\delta$. Their independence is linear over a field of \emph{Euler operators}; it is not algebraic independence over the ambient Hahn field. Continuum cardinality does not imply that this displayed family is a basis, as the following explicit counterexample shows.

\begin{proposition}[An explicit direction missed by all geometric classes]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:factorialwitness}
Fix $\delta>0$ in $\Gamma$. The vector
\[
 f=\hSum_{n\ge0}n!\,t^{n\delta}
\]
has a class in $Q_\Gamma$ outside the $\R(X)$-span of all $[q_r]$, $r>0$. More generally, the enlarged family
\[
 \left\{\left[\hSum_{n\ge0}(n!)^\rho r^n t^{n\delta}\right]:
          \rho\ge0,\ r>0\right\}
\]
is $\R(X)$-linearly independent. Thus the previously displayed geometric family is definitely not a basis, despite already having the correct cardinality.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All displayed supports are $\{n\delta:n\ge0\}$, hence are Hahn-admissible. The positive real powers $(n!)^\rho$ are ordinary real numbers. After clearing rational-function denominators, a finite relation modulo $\FinS{\Gamma}$ gives an eventual identity
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m p_j(n\delta)(n!)^{\rho_j}r_j^n=0,
\]
where the pairs $(\rho_j,r_j)$ are distinct. Suppose some polynomial is nonzero. Among the corresponding pairs choose the largest lexicographically, first in $\rho$ and then in $r$. Let $d$ be the degree of its polynomial. Divide by its $(n!)^\rho r^n n^d$. Its contribution tends to its nonzero leading coefficient times $\delta^d$. Terms with the same $\rho$ but smaller $r$ tend to zero exponentially. Terms with smaller $\rho$ tend to zero because, for every $\epsilon>0$, $A>0$, and fixed integer $k$,
\[
 \frac{n^k A^n}{(n!)^\epsilon}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For instance, $n!\ge(n/2)^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ makes the latter bound immediate. This contradicts the relation. Taking $(\rho,r)=(1,1)$ together with finitely many pairs $(0,r_j)$ proves the first assertion.
\end{proof}
This is a linear independence statement for operator scalars, not an asymptotic restriction on allowed Hahn coefficients. Factorial growth creates no difficulty for Hahn summability: only the support matters.


\section{Automatic strongness and nonsplitting}\label{rvs:eu:sec:commutant}

\subsection{The whole algebraic commutant is coordinatewise}
The next theorem begins with an arbitrary everywhere-defined real-linear map. Neither continuity nor compatibility with Hahn sums is assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Automatic strongness of the Euler commutant]\label{rvs:eu:thm:commutant}
For $0\ne\Gamma\subseteq\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:commutant}
 \{T\in\End_\R(H_\Gamma):T\EuD=\EuD T\}\simeq\R^\Gamma % ed.: source 52's bare D, its Euler operator, renamed \EuD
\end{equation}
as real algebras. The isomorphism sends $(a_\gamma)$ to the coefficient multiplier
\[
 M_a(x)=\hSum_\gamma a_\gamma x_\gamma t^\gamma.
\]
Every such endomorphism is strong and satisfies $v(M_ax)\ge v(x)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\gamma\in\Gamma$. Because $T$ commutes with $\EuD$, it sends the one-dimensional eigenspace $\R t^\gamma$ to itself; write $T(t^\gamma)=a_\gamma t^\gamma$.

For arbitrary $x$, define
\[
 z=\hSum_{\eta\ne\gamma}\frac{x_\eta}{\eta-\gamma}t^\eta.
\]
This is a Hahn series because its support is contained in $\supp_t x$. It gives the exact decomposition
\[
 x=x_\gamma t^\gamma+(\EuD-\gamma)z.
\]
Applying $T$ and taking the coefficient at $\gamma$ yields
\[
 (Tx)_\gamma=a_\gamma x_\gamma,
\]
because every vector in the image of $\EuD-\gamma$ has zero $\gamma$-coefficient. This works at every exponent and proves $T=M_a$.

Conversely, every coefficient multiplier commutes with $\EuD$. Supports cannot increase, so it preserves summability, commutes with all allowed sums, and cannot decrease valuation. Composition corresponds to pointwise multiplication of the weights.
\end{proof}
This explains a useful rigidity phenomenon: although arbitrary Hamel-linear maps on $H_\Gamma$ can be highly non-coordinatewise, a single natural operator $\EuD$ forces every commuting map back into Hahn coordinate geometry.

\begin{corollary}[Euler-compatible projections are exactly support projections]\label[corollary]{rvs:eu:cor:Eulerprojections}
A real-linear idempotent $P:H_\Gamma\to H_\Gamma$ commutes with $\EuD$ if and only if $P=P_A$ for a subset $A\subseteq\Gamma$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:commutant}, $P=M_a$. Idempotence means $a_\gamma^2=a_\gamma$ in $\R$, so each weight is $0$ or $1$.
\end{proof}
The spectral coordinate projections are therefore determined by the algebraic operator, even without a strongness hypothesis. What fails is an algebraic projection onto the \emph{sum of all} its monomial eigenspaces.

\subsection{No compatible Hamel complement}
\begin{theorem}[Nonsplitting of the finite-coordinate extension]\label{rvs:eu:thm:nonsplit}
The exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow \FinS{\Gamma}\longrightarrow H_\Gamma
 \longrightarrow Q_\Gamma\longrightarrow0
\]
splits as real vector spaces but not as $\R[X]$-modules with $X=\EuD$. Equivalently, there is no real-linear projection onto $\FinS{\Gamma}$ commuting with $\EuD$, and no $\EuD$-invariant algebraic complement of $\FinS{\Gamma}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A real-linear complement exists by extending a Hamel basis of $\FinS{\Gamma}$. Suppose a commuting projection onto $\FinS{\Gamma}$ existed. Since it fixes every monomial, its weights in Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:commutant} would all equal $1$. It would be the identity on all of $H_\Gamma$, whose image is not $\FinS{\Gamma}$.

If a $\EuD$-invariant complement existed, both it and $\FinS{\Gamma}$ would be invariant, making the associated projection commute with $\EuD$. Conversely, a commuting projection gives such a complement. This proves all formulations.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[No nonzero divisible submodule upstairs]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:nodivisible}
The $\R[X]$-module $H_\Gamma$ has no nonzero divisible submodule. Here divisible means that every nonzero polynomial acts surjectively on the submodule.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $W$ be divisible and $w\in W$. For each $\gamma\in\Gamma$, write $w=(\EuD-\gamma)z$ with $z\in W$. Then $w_\gamma=0$. All coordinates vanish, so $w=0$.
\end{proof}
The quotient $Q_\Gamma$ is divisible and torsion-free by Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:rational}, whereas no nonzero divisible submodule can represent it inside $H_\Gamma$. This is a second proof and a conceptual strengthening of the nonsplitting result.

\subsection{Many quotient operators cannot be lifted}
Define the ideal
\[
 J_\Gamma=\{a\in\R^\Gamma:\supp_t a\text{ is reverse well ordered}\}.
\]
It is an ideal for pointwise multiplication: subsets and finite unions of reverse-well-ordered sets are reverse well ordered.

\begin{proposition}[The exact algebra of Euler-compatible lifts]\label[proposition]{rvs:eu:prop:nonlift}
The coefficient multipliers induce a faithful real-algebra embedding
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:liftalgebra}
 \R^\Gamma/J_\Gamma
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\End_{\R(X)}(Q_\Gamma).
\end{equation}
This embedding is not surjective. Consequently some $\R(X)$-linear endomorphisms of $Q_\Gamma$ have no real-linear lift to $H_\Gamma$ commuting with $\EuD$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A coefficient multiplier preserves $\FinS{\Gamma}$ and commutes with rational Euler actions on the quotient. It induces zero exactly when it maps every Hahn series to a finite-support series. If its weight support is reverse well ordered, its intersection with each Hahn support is finite, proving this condition. If the weight support is not reverse well ordered, it contains an infinite increasing sequence $(\gamma_n)$. The series $\sum_n t^{\gamma_n}$ is a Hahn series whose image has infinite support. This proves that the kernel is exactly $J_\Gamma$.

The source of \eqref{rvs:eu:eq:liftalgebra} is commutative. The target is not: by Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:rationaldim}, $Q_\Gamma$ has at least two independent vectors over $\R(X)$; extend them to a basis and define two noncommuting elementary matrix endomorphisms. Thus the image cannot be the whole target. Every commuting lift is a coefficient multiplier by Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:commutant}, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}
\begin{warning}[The kernel is larger than finite-support weights]
For $\Gamma=\Z$, the indicator of the negative integers belongs to $J_\Gamma$ although it has infinite support. Every Laurent series has only finitely many negative terms, so the corresponding projection always lands in $\FinS{\Gamma}$. Replacing $J_\Gamma$ by the ideal of finitely supported weight functions would give a false lifting theorem.
\end{warning}
This ideal is the same support condition that appeared in the strong real dual, but here it controls which diagonal endomorphisms disappear after quotienting by all finite-monomial vectors.

\section{Two distinct infinite-coordinate layers}\label{rvs:eu:sec:layers}
Continue to assume $0\ne\Gamma\subseteq\R$. The completion $\LfS{\Gamma}$ is preserved by every coefficient multiplier, including $\EuD$ and all $G_p$.

\begin{theorem}[Rational scalar structure on both completion layers]\label{rvs:eu:thm:layerstructure}
There is a canonical exact sequence of $\R(X)$-vector spaces
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:layers}
 0\longrightarrow \LfS{\Gamma}/\FinS{\Gamma}
 \longrightarrow H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma}
 \longrightarrow H_\Gamma/\LfS{\Gamma}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The first term has dimension $\cont$ over $\R(X)$. The last term is zero for cyclic $\Gamma$ and has dimension $\cont$ for noncyclic $\Gamma$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The operators $G_p$ preserve $\LfS{\Gamma}$, and their errors in \eqref{rvs:eu:eq:greenidentities} have finite-support image. Hence all three quotients carry the rational scalar actions and all maps in \eqref{rvs:eu:eq:layers} respect them.

For any $\delta>0$ in $\Gamma$, the support $\{n\delta:n\ge0\}$ is cofinal in $\Gamma$ because $\Gamma\subseteq\R$. Thus every $q_r$ in Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:rationaldim} belongs to $\LfS{\Gamma}$. Their classes remain independent in $\LfS{\Gamma}/\FinS{\Gamma}$, giving dimension $\cont$.

If $\Gamma$ is cyclic, $\LfS{\Gamma}=H_\Gamma$. If it is noncyclic, it is dense in $\R$: absence of a least positive element gives positive group elements smaller than every prescribed real $\epsilon$, whose integer translates meet every real interval. For each $n\ge1$, choose
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eu:eq:slowexponents}
 \gamma_n\in\Gamma\cap\left(-\frac1n,-\frac1{n+1}\right).
\end{equation}
Then $\gamma_n$ is strictly increasing to zero and $|\gamma_n|$ is comparable to $1/n$. For $r>0$ let
\[
 y_r=\hSum_{n\ge1}r^n t^{\gamma_n}.
\]
These are valid Hahn series, all supported below zero.

Suppose a finite rational relation between their classes modulo $\LfS{\Gamma}$ holds. Clear denominators to obtain
\[
 z=\sum_jp_j(\EuD)y_{r_j}\in \LfS{\Gamma}.
\]
Since its support is bounded above by zero, left-finiteness forces $z$ to have finite support. Therefore
\[
 \sum_jp_j(\gamma_n)r_j^n=0
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$. Let $r_j$ be the largest base with $p_j\ne0$. Write $p_j(X)=X^{d_j}u_j(X)$ with $u_j(0)\ne0$. By \eqref{rvs:eu:eq:slowexponents}, $|p_j(\gamma_n)|$ is bounded above and below by positive constants times $n^{-d_j}$. For every smaller base, the ratio
\[
 \frac{p_i(\gamma_n)r_i^n}{p_j(\gamma_n)r_j^n}
\]
tends to zero, since it is bounded by a fixed power of $n$ times $(r_i/r_j)^n$. Dividing the relation by the largest-base term gives $1+o(1)=0$, a contradiction. Thus the classes $[y_r]$ are $\R(X)$-independent. The cardinality upper bound is again $\cont$.
\end{proof}

For noncyclic real exponent groups, the two layers have genuinely different support behavior. The first consists of infinite expansions whose finite subsums converge in the chosen valuation topology. The second records infinite supports accumulating below a fixed precision cutoff. Both survive as full continuum-dimensional spaces even after the new rational-function scalars are admitted.

\begin{corollary}[Splitting after quotient, nonsplitting before quotient]\label[corollary]{rvs:eu:cor:layersplits}
The exact sequence \eqref{rvs:eu:eq:layers} admits a noncanonical $\R(X)$-linear splitting. By contrast, if $\Gamma$ is noncyclic, the sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow \LfS{\Gamma}\longrightarrow H_\Gamma
 \longrightarrow H_\Gamma/\LfS{\Gamma}\longrightarrow0
\]
has no $\R[X]$-linear splitting.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every exact sequence of vector spaces over a field splits by choosing bases, giving the first assertion. The last quotient is nonzero and divisible over $\R[X]$. A module splitting would embed it as a nonzero divisible submodule of $H_\Gamma$, contradicting Proposition~\ref{rvs:eu:prop:nodivisible}.
\end{proof}
The contrast is instructive. A complement becomes available after the finite-coordinate torsion is removed, but not as a compatible subspace of the original Hahn space. The obstruction is structural, not merely a failure to find a clever basis.

\section{Concrete examples and computational consequences}\label{rvs:eu:sec:examples}

\subsection{A Laurent slice: infinite coordinates without a completion gap}
Take $\Gamma=\Z$ and $t=\omega^{-1}$. Then
\[
 H_\Z=\R((t)),\qquad \FinS{\Z}=\R[t,t^{-1}],\qquad \LfS{\Z}=H_\Z.
\]
Every Laurent series is the internal valuation limit of its finite truncations, yet
\[
 \dim_\R(H_\Z/\FinS{\Z})=\dim_{\R(X)}(H_\Z/\FinS{\Z})=\cont.
\]
Thus completion may add maximally many Hamel directions even when it supplies all admissible series. The Euler operator is $\EuD=t\,d/dt$, and the quotient has no real eigenvalues: $\EuD-\lambda$ is invertible there for every $\lambda\in\R$.

Consider $x=(1-2t)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}2^nt^n$. For $p(X)=X-2$, the equation $(\EuD-2)y=x$ has no solution in $H_\Z$, because the $t^2$ coefficient of the left side is always zero while that of $x$ equals $4$. The canonical nonresonant solution is
\[
 y=\hSum_{\substack{n\ge0\\n\ne2}}\frac{2^n}{n-2}t^n,
 \qquad (\EuD-2)y=x-4t^2.
\]
Modulo $\FinS{\Z}$, this is the unique solution class to $(\EuD-2)[y]=[x]$. Finite resonance, not an analytic convergence issue, is the entire obstruction.

\subsection{A rational-exponent slice: the completion gap is visible}
For $\Gamma=\Q$, let
\[
 u=\hSum_{n\ge1}t^{-1/n}.
\]
This is a valid Hahn series with increasing support bounded above by zero. It is not in $\LfS{\Q}$, because infinitely many support exponents lie below zero. Its finite subsums never approximate it beyond the cutoff zero, although their Hahn sum is perfectly well defined.

Since the exponent $-1/n$ is nonzero,
\[
 \EuD\left(-\hSum_{n\ge1} n\,t^{-1/n}\right)=u.
\]
The unbounded coefficient factor $n$ causes no support problem. This illustrates why support criteria, rather than ordinary coefficient magnitude estimates, govern the Euler calculus here.

\subsection{A finite-dimensional canonical basis with infinite tails}
Let
\[
 a=1+t+t^2+t^3+\cdots,\qquad b=1+2t+3t^2+4t^3+\cdots,
\]
and $V=\Span_\R\{a,b\}\subseteq\R((t))$. The pivot exponents are $0$ and $1$. The unique reduced basis from Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:echelon} is
\[
 b_0=2a-b=1-t^2-2t^3-3t^4-\cdots,
\]
\[
 b_1=b-a=t+2t^2+3t^3+\cdots.
\]
Their coefficients at exponents $0$ and $1$ are $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Every nonzero member of $V$ has leading exponent $0$ or $1$, despite the infinitely many later coordinates. Finite-dimensional leading-coordinate elimination is exact, not an approximation by dropping tails.

\subsection{Recommended symbolic representation}
For computation, the normal-form representation should be the primary interface. A series object needs an exponent domain, a support specification with an admissibility argument, and a coefficient rule. It should not merely be an unconstrained dictionary indexed by every surreal.

The natural primitive operations are finite-support arithmetic, coordinate extraction, support projection, certified Hahn summation, leading-term elimination, and diagonal coefficient operators. Polynomial Euler inversion additionally returns the finite resonance projection $P_{Z_p}x$ as an explicit obstruction certificate. This is more informative than asking a symbolic system for an unspecified Hamel basis.

For finitely many explicitly represented Hahn vectors, the row-echelon algorithm successively finds the first nonzero coefficient functional, normalizes a pivot, and eliminates it. In exact symbolic infinite-support input, termination of each search requires an effective leading-term or zero test. The abstract finite-dimensional theorem by itself does not provide such an algorithm for arbitrary computable coefficient streams.

\section{Monomial symmetries, derivations, and algebraic independence}
\label{rvs:sec:multiplicative}

The Hahn basis is not only linear.  Because its indices form the additive
surreal group, linear transformations of the monomials often extend to field
automorphisms and derivations.

\subsection{Monomial-preserving ordered-field automorphisms}

Let \(\phi:(\No,+,<)\to(\No,+,<)\) be an ordered additive class
automorphism, and let
\[
  \chi:(\No,+)\longrightarrow(\R_{>0},\cdot)
\]
be a positive real character.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:T-chiphi}
 T_{\chi,\phi}\!\left(
   \sH_{\gamma\in S}r_\gamma\omega^\gamma\right)
 =\sH_{\gamma\in S}
   r_\gamma\chi(\gamma)\omega^{\phi(\gamma)}.
\end{equation}


\noindent\wtag{} \emph{Repository results.}  The forward direction of the
next theorem is already in the collection.  For \(\chi=1\) the map
\(T_{1,\phi}\) is the canonical Hahn lift of the exponent-rigidity report
(\path{surreal/exponential-automorphism-rigidity}, Proposition 8.3,
``Canonical field lift''), formalized as
\path{Surreal.Foundations.SignSequence.exponentAutomorphism}
(\path{Surreal/Foundations/NormalFormExponentAutomorphisms.lean}).  With a
character, \(T_{\chi,\phi}\) is the monomial map \(M_{\chi,\tau}\) of the
omnific-preserving report (\path{surreal/omnific-preserving-automorphisms},
(5.1)), written there in \(t\)-exponents.  The converse is the case of that
report's Theorem 5.1 (strong valued factorization \(\sigma=M_{\chi,\tau}\circ u\),
after Kuhlmann--Serra) in which the factor \(u\) fixes every monomial and is
therefore the identity; there it is stated for set-sized Hahn fields.  Source
51 cites neither result (its repository table, \cref{rvs:sec:repo-relation},
names the exponent-rigidity report only as complementary).  Its proof is printed as a second route; only the
class-level formulation on all of \(\No\) is not already in the collection.

\begin{theorem}[Monomial automorphisms]\label{rvs:thm:monomial-aut}
The map \(T_{\chi,\phi}\) is a strong \(\R\)-linear ordered-field
automorphism of \(\No\).  Conversely, every strong \(\R\)-linear
ordered-field automorphism that permutes the monomial lines
\(\{\R\omega^\gamma:\gamma\in\No\}\) has this form for unique
\(\chi\) and \(\phi\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (source 51); a second route to the results cited above]
Because \(\phi\) is an order isomorphism, it sends reverse-well-ordered sets
to reverse-well-ordered sets.  The columns in \eqref{rvs:eq:T-chiphi} have
singleton supports with distinct output exponents, so
\cref{rvs:thm:strong-matrix} gives strong real linearity.  The inverse is obtained
from \(\phi^{-1}\) and the character
\(\gamma\mapsto\chi(\phi^{-1}\gamma)^{-1}\).

For monomials,
\begin{align*}
 T_{\chi,\phi}(\omega^\gamma\omega^\delta)
 &=\chi(\gamma+\delta)\omega^{\phi(\gamma+\delta)}\\
 &=\chi(\gamma)\chi(\delta)
   \omega^{\phi(\gamma)+\phi(\delta)}\\
 &=T_{\chi,\phi}(\omega^\gamma)
   T_{\chi,\phi}(\omega^\delta).
\end{align*}
Strong distributivity extends this identity to arbitrary normal forms.
Positive real coefficients and the increasing map \(\phi\) preserve the sign
of the leading term, hence the order.

Conversely, suppose \(T\) strongly permutes monomial lines.  Write uniquely
\[
  T(\omega^\gamma)=\chi(\gamma)\omega^{\phi(\gamma)},
  \qquad \chi(\gamma)>0.
\]
Bijectivity on lines makes \(\phi\) a bijection.  Multiplicativity of \(T\)
gives
\(\phi(\gamma+\delta)=\phi(\gamma)+\phi(\delta)\) and
\(\chi(\gamma+\delta)=\chi(\gamma)\chi(\delta)\).  Preservation of the order
of positive monomials makes \(\phi\) increasing.  Strong linearity then
forces \(T\) to act on every normal form by \eqref{rvs:eq:T-chiphi}.
\end{proof}

This elementary subgroup is one of the reasons the monomial basis is useful:
its symmetries are controlled by familiar group data.  The full automorphism
group is richer; Kaplan, Krapp, and Serra give a systematic Hahn-field
decomposition and distinguish strongly linear automorphisms from general
ones \cite{KKS}.

\begin{example}[Exponent dilations]\label[example]{rvs:ex:exponent-dilation}
For every positive surreal \(a\), multiplication
\[
  \phi_a(\gamma)=a\gamma
\]
is an ordered additive automorphism of \(\No\).  Taking \(\chi=1\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:sigma-a}
  \sigma_a\!\left(\sH_\gamma r_\gamma\omega^\gamma\right)
  =\sH_\gamma r_\gamma\omega^{a\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Then \(\sigma_a(\omega)=\omega^a\),
\(\sigma_a^{-1}=\sigma_{a^{-1}}\), and
\(\sigma_a\sigma_b=\sigma_{ab}\).
These are ordered-field automorphisms fixing \(\R\).  Preservation of the
surreal exponential or of the omega map as an additional named operation is
a separate and much more rigid requirement.
\end{example}

\noindent\wtag{} For a rational \(a=r\ne1\) the exponent-rigidity report
proves more (its Theorem 8.4, ``Rational dilations do not lift''): no
automorphism of \(\No\) commuting with the exponential induces the value
action \(\gamma\mapsto r\gamma\).  In particular \(\sigma_r\) does not commute
with \(\exp\).  This answers part of \cref{rvs:w:q:exprigidity}.


\begin{theorem}[Dilation-invariant strong functionals]
\label{rvs:thm:dilation-invariant-dual}
Let \(\sigma_a\) be the exponent-dilation automorphism
\eqref{rvs:eq:sigma-a}.  A strong real-linear functional
\(\Lambda:\No\to\R\) satisfies
\[
  \Lambda\circ\sigma_a=\Lambda
  \qquad\text{for every }a\in\No_{>0}
\]
if and only if \(\Lambda\) is a real multiple of constant-term extraction
\(\ct=[\omega^0]\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(w(\gamma)=\Lambda(\omega^\gamma)\).  Invariance gives
\(w(a\gamma)=w(\gamma)\) for all \(a>0\).  Any two positive exponents are
related by multiplication by a positive surreal, so \(w\) is constant on
\(\No_{>0}\); similarly it is constant on \(\No_{<0}\).  If the positive
constant were nonzero, the active set would contain the decreasing sequence
\(1>1/2>1/3>\cdots\), contradicting
\cref{rvs:prop:summation-finite-seq}.  If the negative constant were nonzero, it
would contain \(-1>-2>-3>\cdots\), again a contradiction.  Thus only the
weight at exponent \(0\) may survive, giving a multiple of \(\ct\).  The
converse is immediate because every \(\sigma_a\) fixes exponent \(0\) and its
coefficient.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Coefficient characters]
If \(d:(\No,+)\to(\R,+)\) is an additive class homomorphism, then
\(\chi_d(\gamma)=\exp(d(\gamma))\) is a positive character.  The resulting
automorphism fixes every monomial line and rescales it by a positive real.
A concrete choice is \(d=\ct\), constant-term extraction applied to the
\emph{exponent} \(\gamma\):
\[
  \omega^\gamma\longmapsto
  e^{\ct(\gamma)}\omega^\gamma.
\]
\end{example}

\subsection{Diagonal strong derivations}\label{rvs:subsec:derivations}

The same additive characters produce derivations rather than automorphisms.

\noindent\wtag{} The dilation-invariant dual (\cref{rvs:thm:dilation-invariant-dual})
is not in the collection.  The derivations of the next theorem are: for a
set-sized Hahn field they are the Euler derivations \(\partial_\lambda\) of the
omnific--Diophantine report (\path{surreal/omnific-diophantine-geometry},
(7.1) and Lemma 7.1 for divisible \(H\) and \(\Q\)-linear \(\lambda\); (16.1)
and Lemma 16.2 for any \(\Gamma\) and additive \(\lambda\)), with
\(\lambda=d\).  Source 51 proves the forward direction on the whole class and
adds the converse, which is new but short.


\begin{theorem}[Diagonal derivations]\label{rvs:thm:diagonal-derivation}
Let \(d:(\No,+)\to(\R,+)\) be additive.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:Dd}
 D_d\!\left(\sH_\gamma r_\gamma\omega^\gamma\right)
 =\sH_\gamma r_\gamma d(\gamma)\omega^\gamma
\end{equation}
is a strong \(\R\)-linear derivation of \(\No\):
\[
  D_d(xy)=xD_d(y)+yD_d(x).
\]
Conversely, every strong \(\R\)-linear derivation satisfying
\(D(\omega^\gamma)\in\R\omega^\gamma\) for every \(\gamma\) is uniquely of
this form.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (source 51); its forward direction is a second route]
The support of \(D_dx\) is contained in \(\supp(x)\), so the map is strong.
For monomials,
\begin{align*}
 D_d(\omega^{\gamma+\delta})
 &=d(\gamma+\delta)\omega^{\gamma+\delta}\\
 &=(d(\gamma)+d(\delta))\omega^{\gamma+\delta}\\
 &=\omega^\gamma D_d(\omega^\delta)
   +\omega^\delta D_d(\omega^\gamma).
\end{align*}
Hahn distributivity and local finiteness extend the Leibniz identity to all
normal forms.

Conversely, write
\(D(\omega^\gamma)=d(\gamma)\omega^\gamma\).  The Leibniz identity on a
product of two monomials forces
\(d(\gamma+\delta)=d(\gamma)+d(\delta)\).  Strong linearity determines \(D\)
on every normal form, giving \eqref{rvs:eq:Dd}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A surreal Euler-type derivation]
With \(d=\ct\),
\[
  D_{\ct}(\omega^r)=r\omega^r\qquad(r\in\R),
\]
so in particular \(D_{\ct}(\omega)=\omega\), while
\(D_{\ct}\) kills real constants.  It is diagonal in the Conway--Hahn basis.
It is not asserted here to commute with the surreal exponential; differential
compatibility is an additional structure studied by Berarducci--Mantova and
later work \cite{BerarducciMantova,ADH}.
\end{example}

\noindent\wtag{} Restricted to a real-exponent workspace,
\(D_{\ct}=-\EuD\), where \(\EuD\) is source 52's Euler operator of
\cref{rvs:w:part:euler}; the sign comes from \(t^\gamma=\omega^{-\gamma}\).


\subsection{Algebraic independence of monomials}

Linear coordinates also give a clean field-theoretic criterion.

\begin{theorem}[Rational exponents versus algebraic monomials]
\label{rvs:thm:monomial-alg-ind}
For \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n\in\No\), the monomials
\(\omega^{\gamma_1},\ldots,\omega^{\gamma_n}\) are algebraically independent
over \(\R\) if and only if the exponents
\(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n\) are linearly independent over \(\Q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that the exponents are \(\Q\)-dependent.  Clearing
denominators gives a nonzero integer relation
\(\sum_i m_i\gamma_i=0\).  Moving negative terms to the other side gives an
equality between two distinct monomials in the
\(\omega^{\gamma_i}\), hence a nonzero polynomial relation over \(\R\).

Conversely, assume the exponents are \(\Q\)-independent and let
\[
 P(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in F}
 c_{\mathbf{k}}X_1^{k_1}\cdots X_n^{k_n}
\]
be a nonzero polynomial with finite exponent set
\(F\subseteq\N^n\).  Distinct multiindices \(\mathbf{k}\ne\mathbf{l}\)
give distinct surreal exponents
\[
  \sum_i k_i\gamma_i\ne\sum_i l_i\gamma_i,
\]
by rational independence.  Therefore
\[
 P(\omega^{\gamma_1},\ldots,\omega^{\gamma_n})
 =\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in F}
 c_{\mathbf{k}}\omega^{\sum_i k_i\gamma_i}
\]
is a nonzero finite normal form.  No cancellation between distinct exponents
is possible.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Every nontrivial monomial \(\omega^\gamma\), with \(\gamma\ne0\), is
transcendental over \(\R\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[An explicit proper-class independent family]
\label[corollary]{rvs:cor:proper-class-alg-independent}
The family
\begin{equation}\label{rvs:eq:proper-class-AI}
  \{\omega^{\omega^\alpha}:\alpha\in\On\}
\end{equation}
is algebraically independent over \(\R\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The exponent family \(\{\omega^\alpha:\alpha\in\On\}\) is real-linearly,
hence rationally, independent: every finite relation would be a finite
normal form with distinct exponents \(\alpha\).  Apply
\cref{rvs:thm:monomial-alg-ind}.
\end{proof}

Thus the field extension \(\No/\R\) has proper-class transcendence complexity
in the evident class-theoretic sense.  An actual transcendence basis again
requires class choice and is not supplied canonically by normal form; the
explicit family \eqref{rvs:eq:proper-class-AI} gives a natural lower bound without
such a choice.

\noindent\wtag{} The independent-surreal-copies report
(\path{surreal/independent-surreal-copies}) proves algebraic independence of
other explicit families, including a class-level statement for support
subfields; it selects no proper-class Hamel basis.  Its results and
\cref{rvs:cor:proper-class-alg-independent} concern different families, and
neither is used for the other.

\section{What the natural basis should mean}\label{rvs:cv:sec:recommendation}

The preceding results support a precise hierarchy of answers.

\begin{resultbox}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\Alph*.},leftmargin=2.4em]
\item The \textbf{canonical basis-like object} is the monomial Hahn basis
$\{\omega^a:a\in\No\}$ with coefficient functionals $c_a$ and strong summation.

\item The \textbf{canonical ordinary basis} exists only after passing to the
associated graded algebra: the degree-$a$ line is generated by $[\omega^a]$.

\item A \textbf{global Hamel basis} is possible only under a stated class-choice
framework. It is noncanonical and necessarily contains vectors with supports of
unbounded set cardinality.

\item The monomials are \textbf{not a natural-valuation topological basis}; their
finite span is already closed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{resultbox}

This hierarchy reconciles two common but apparently conflicting statements:
``Conway normal form gives coordinates'' and ``the monomials do not form a vector
space basis.'' Both are correct once the permitted support and summation law are
specified.

\subsection{Why a Hamel basis is the wrong invariant for most surreal analysis}

A Hamel basis deliberately forgets order, valuation, support, multiplication,
and the simplicity hierarchy. In a finite-dimensional real vector space that loss
may be harmless because all norms and bases are equivalent up to manageable linear
transformations. In $\No$ it is catastrophic. A Hamel basis must compress a normal
form of arbitrarily large support into a finite list of basis vectors; by
\cref{rvs:cv:thm:support-complexity}, the complexity is merely relocated into the basis
vectors themselves.

By contrast, the monomial Hahn basis simultaneously exposes:
\begin{itemize}
\item the total order, through the greatest exponent with nonzero coefficient;
\item the valuation, through the leading exponent;
\item multiplication, through addition of exponents and convolution of coefficients;
\item convex subspaces, through downward-closed exponent cuts;
\item canonical projections and diagonal operators;
\item strong summability, through reverse-well-ordered supports and finite coefficient
fibres; and
\item exponent symmetries, through permutation of monomials.
\end{itemize}
No ordinary Hamel basis is known or expected to package all these features.

\subsection{What is genuinely specific to the vector-space question}

The literature generally invokes Conway normal form to study order, fields,
valuations, derivations, or automorphisms.  The present organization instead
starts from the linear question and follows its consequences:
\begin{enumerate}
\item it separates Hamel, Hahn, graded, and Schauder meanings of basis;
\item it proves the no-set-spanning theorem directly from support;
\item it quantifies the local Hamel-dimension explosion inside support
windows;
\item it exposes the quotient \(\No/\Fin\) by an explicit proper-class
independent family;
\item it derives an intrinsic strong real dual and contrasts it with the zero
order dual;
\item it explains why the full proper-class valuation topology cannot realize
normal forms as set-indexed limits.
\end{enumerate}
These are primarily structural deductions from classical coordinates.  Their
value is to prevent category errors when importing finite-dimensional or
Banach-space intuitions into surreal analysis.

\subsubsection{Proposed Lean implementation plan}

A practical formalization could proceed in the following order.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{L\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.8em]
\item \pathcode{Surreal/Linear/MonomialSpan.lean}: prove monomial independence;
define $\Fin$; identify it with the finite-support group algebra; prove the

\item \pathcode{Surreal/Linear/SupportProjection.lean}: define $P_S$ for set-sized

\section{Further research questions and proposed program}
\label{rvs:eu:sec:questions}\label{rvs:cv:sec:future}
\label{rvs:sec:research}

The vector-class viewpoint opens a research program that is distinct from,
but compatible with, the existing surreal analysis, automorphism, and
formalization programs.  The questions below are ordered roughly from
foundational to analytic and computational.

\subsection{Foundations and definability}

\begin{question}[Choice strength for class bases]
\label[question]{rvs:w:q:foundations}
What is the weakest conservative class theory over ZFC that proves every
class vector space whose carrier is bijective with \(\On\) has a Hamel class
basis?  In particular, how much of global choice is needed for
\cref{rvs:thm:class-basis}, and can the statement fail in natural models of NBG
without global choice?
\end{question}

The staged proof uses global choice to select basis extensions through a
proper class of stages.  A reverse-mathematical analysis would separate the
choice needed for this particular highly structured vector class from a
general class-basis principle.

\noindent\wtag{} Sources 53 and 52 ask the same question.  Source 53 asked
it for the hypothesis ``MK, or GBC with ETR'' of its staged construction; as
corrected after \cref{rvs:prop:basis-containing-monomials}, GBC suffices, and
what remains open is the question above: how much of global choice is
needed.

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Source 53 (``Weakest basis principle).}
What is the weakest natural class theory proving that every class vector space
presented as an ordinal-indexed union of set-sized subspaces has a class Hamel
basis? Determine the exact strength needed for \cref{rvs:cv:prop:class-basis-existence},
separating global choice, elementary transfinite recursion, and stronger class
choice schemes.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Source 52 (``Class foundations and definability of Hamel extensions).}
Determine the exact foundational strength needed for a class Hamel basis of $\No$ extending all monomials, and distinguish existence from definability in enriched surreal structures. The argument here assumes a set-like global well-order. Which weaker class-choice principles suffice? Conversely, which naturality conditions are incompatible with definable selection even when existence is assumed? The explicit symmetry obstruction is a starting point, not a complete definability theorem.
\end{quote}

\begin{question}[Definable Hamel bases]
Does \(\No\) admit a Hamel basis definable, without arbitrary parameters, in
natural class universes such as \((V,\in)\), \(L\), or HOD?  Can one prove
that no such basis is invariant under specified large subgroups of
\(\Aut(\No,+,\cdot,<)\)?
\end{question}

A useful intermediate target is a definable basis containing all monomials,
as in \cref{rvs:prop:basis-containing-monomials}, together with a definable rule
for selecting the necessary infinite-support supplement.  Symmetry may make
this impossible in the full universe even when a basis exists abstractly.
\begin{quote}\small
\wtag{} \emph{Source 53 asks the same (``Definable Hamel bases):}
Can one define a Hamel basis of a set-sized surreal universe from its canonical
well-order, and under what hypotheses can one define a global class Hamel basis?
Study ordinal-definability, parameter-definability, and the behavior in $V=L$,
HOD, or canonical inner models.
\end{quote}


\begin{question}[Class matroids]
Develop a class-matroid framework in which linear independence on \(\No\),
basis extension, closure, and rank are formulated without forcing all ranks
to be set cardinals.  Which ordinary matroid theorems survive for set-finitary
class matroids under global choice?
\end{question}

The linear matroid of \(\No\) is finitary---dependence is witnessed by a
finite subset---but its ground class and every basis are proper.  This makes
it a natural test case for class-sized combinatorial geometry.

\subsection{Support strata and quotient geometry}

\begin{question}[Structure of \(\No/\Fin\)]
Classify the real vector-class quotient \(\No/\Fin\) and its natural
filtrations by support order type, cofinality, and leading exponent.  Is there
a canonical hierarchy of subquotients whose set-sized pieces have computable
Hamel dimensions?
\end{question}

The block family in \cref{rvs:thm:quotient-independent} gives only a lower bound.
A finer analysis could distinguish countable support, bounded support order
type, and support contained in specified exponent intervals.

\noindent\wtag{} \Cref{rvs:eu:thm:classdefect} and
\cref{rvs:cv:thm:support-complexity} give further lower bounds; the
question is open.


\begin{question}[Support-rank dimension spectra]
For an infinite cardinal \(\kappa\), let \(\No^{(<\kappa)}\) consist of
surreals whose normal-form support has cardinality below \(\kappa\), or order
type below \(\kappa\).  Determine the Hamel dimensions of set-sized
intersections of these classes and the strictness of the resulting hierarchy.
\end{question}

The countable-window theorem suggests cardinal-exponent formulas, but order
type and ambient exponent geometry can interact when the allowed support
window itself is not reverse well ordered.

\begin{question}[Canonical complements]
Which naturally defined real subspaces of \(\No\) admit canonical strong
complements?  Support-defined subspaces do by \cref{rvs:thm:support-splitting};
what happens for birthday cutoffs, differential kernels, logarithmic strata,
or fields generated by selected monomials?
\end{question}

The contrast between support subspaces and birthday subspaces may reveal
which decompositions belong to the Hahn geometry and which depend on the
genetic construction.

\noindent\wtag{} For the Euler kernel and its finite-monomial part, source
52's \cref{rvs:eu:thm:nonsplit} shows that no \(\EuD\)-invariant complement
exists, although algebraic complements do.


\subsection{Strong operator algebras and duality}

\begin{question}[The algebra of Hahn-compatible matrices]
Give intrinsic necessary and sufficient conditions for composition,
invertibility, triangular factorization, and spectral decomposition of the
class matrices in \cref{rvs:thm:strong-matrix}.  Identify natural operator ideals:
finite-row, finite-column, support-contracting, valuation-increasing, and
nuclear analogues.
\end{question}

A promising route is to first solve the problem on every set-sized support
window and then formulate uniform class conditions.  The recent theory of
automorphisms and derivations on algebras with formal infinite sums should be
compared directly \cite{BagayokoEtAl}.

\noindent\wtag{} Source 53's question ``Strongly linear matrices'' asked for
the classification itself; it is answered by \cref{rvs:thm:strong-matrix}
(\cref{rvs:w:rem:answered-matrices}).  The question above, on the algebra of
these matrices, stays open.


\begin{question}[Invariant strong duals]
For important automorphism groups \(G\) of \(\No\), compute
\[
  (\No^{\vee}_{\mathrm{str}})^G.
\]
Theorem~\ref{rvs:thm:dilation-invariant-dual} shows that invariance under all
positive exponent dilations leaves exactly the constant-term line.  What are
the invariant strong functionals for valuation-preserving, omega-preserving,
or exponential automorphism groups?
\end{question}

This is a tractable bridge between the linear theory developed here and the
automorphism decomposition of Kaplan--Krapp--Serra.

\begin{question}[Duality beyond real scalars]
Classify strong, order-bounded, and topology-continuous maps from \(\No\) to
other codomains: a set-sized Hahn field, \(\No\) itself, the surcomplex field,
or quotient spaces such as \(\No/\Fin\).  Which codomains avoid the collapse
of \cref{rvs:thm:order-dual-zero}?
\end{question}

Real-valued positive states vanish because the codomain is Archimedean.
Surreal-valued positive maps may exist and could provide a better foundation
for surreal measure, probability, and convex analysis.

\subsection{Topology and generalized bases}

\begin{question}[Coarser topologies with genuine Schauder expansions]
Classify set-generated valuation or bornological structures on \(\No\) for
which selected classes of Conway normal forms are limits of their finite
truncations.  What is the largest natural subspace on which the monomials form
a Schauder basis for a given set-sized scale system?
\end{question}

Any answer must evade \cref{rvs:thm:eventually-constant} by replacing the full
proper-class neighborhood basis with a set-sized cofinal family, restricting
the domain, or allowing class-indexed convergence.
\begin{quote}\small
\wtag{} \emph{Source 53 asks the same (``Topologies making monomials a basis):}
Classify coarsenings of the natural valuation, coefficientwise topologies, and
bornologies on $\No$ for which finite monomial sums are dense and every normal
form is the limit of a canonical net of truncations. Determine when such a topology
is Hausdorff, complete, spherically complete, compatible with multiplication, or
compatible with surreal exponentiation.
\end{quote}


\begin{question}[Class-indexed convergence]
Can class-indexed nets or filters be developed safely enough to make every
Conway normal form a topological sum without collapsing separation axioms or
leaving conservative class theory?  How does such convergence compare with
strong Hahn summation?
\end{question}

Strong summation may already be the correct replacement.  A topological
reconstruction would be valuable only if it adds robust continuity,
completion, or functional-analytic theorems.

\begin{question}[Bornological and locally convex analogues]
Is there a useful class-bornology on \(\No\) whose bounded linear dual lies
strictly between the strong dual and the zero order-bounded real dual?  Can
one formulate barrelledness, nuclearity, or reflexivity using only set-sized
bounded pieces?
\end{question}

The support windows \(\Hahn(A)\) provide a canonical directed family of
set-sized test spaces.  An inductive-limit or bornological approach may be
more faithful than the full fine topology.

\subsection{Differential and exponential compatibility}

\begin{question}[Derivation-adapted bases]
How does the Berarducci--Mantova derivation act relative to the monomial Hahn
basis, and is there a more refined basis---perhaps using log-atomic numbers or
transseries trees---in which its matrix is triangular or locally finite?
\end{question}

The diagonal derivations of \cref{rvs:thm:diagonal-derivation} are completely
controlled by additive real characters, but the canonical surreal derivation
changes scales in a subtler way.  A basis adapted simultaneously to
multiplication, exponentiation, and derivation would be highly significant.
\begin{quote}\small
\wtag{} \emph{Source 53 asks the same (``Matrix of the surreal derivation):}
Express the Berarducci--Mantova derivation in monomial coordinates \cite{BerarducciMantova}. Determine its
support matrix, triangularity properties, and induced operator on the associated
graded algebra. Identify the principal symbol of differentiation at each asymptotic
scale.
\end{quote}


\begin{question}[Exponential rigidity of monomial automorphisms]
\label[question]{rvs:w:q:exprigidity}
Determine exactly which pairs \((\chi,\phi)\) in
\cref{rvs:thm:monomial-aut} commute with the surreal exponential or preserve the
omega map.  Existing rigidity results rule out broad subclasses; an explicit
intersection with the monomial automorphism group should be computable.
\end{question}

A direct coefficient-and-leading-scale proof could provide an independent
check on more general automorphism theorems.

\noindent\wtag{} \emph{Partly answered.}  The exponent-rigidity report's
Theorem 8.4 shows that the rational dilations \(\phi(\gamma)=r\gamma\),
\(r\in\Q_{>0}\setminus\{1\}\), have no exponential lift; so
\((1,\phi_r)\) does not commute with the exponential.  The question for
general \((\chi,\phi)\) stays open.


\begin{question}[Transcendence bases compatible with support]
Can one construct a class transcendence basis of \(\No/\R\) whose elements
have controlled normal-form support, and then describe the algebraic closure
of the generated field inside \(\No\)?  How far is the explicit family
\(\{\omega^{\omega^\alpha}\}\) from a transcendence basis?
\end{question}

The answer should distinguish finite field generation from Hahn closure;
normal-form completeness is far larger than rational-function generation.

\subsection{Tensor constructions and geometry}

\begin{question}[Completed tensor powers]
Define and compare the algebraic tensor powers of the class vector space
\(\No\) with support-completed Hahn tensor products.  Do the monomials
\(\omega^{\gamma_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega^{\gamma_n}\) form a natural
multi-Hahn basis, and which support conditions are forced by contraction and
symmetrization?
\end{question}

This would connect the present scalar decomposition with the repository's
finite-dimensional tensor-field program and with infinite-dimensional Hahn
Hilbert geometry.

\begin{question}[Exterior and symmetric class algebras]
Describe the exterior, symmetric, and Clifford algebras of \(\No\) over
\(\R\) under both Hamel and Hahn completions.  Which constructions retain a
canonical grading by surreal exponents?
\end{question}

The algebraic versions depend on a Hamel basis only through isomorphism, while
support-completed versions may preserve much more of the normal-form geometry.

\subsection{Questions of source 52}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52 lists its questions as subsections; they are
numbered here.  Its question on class foundations and definability is
printed with \cref{rvs:w:q:foundations}, and its formal proof library with
source 51's formalization question.

The following questions are proposed by this article. Unless explicitly stated, they are not being advertised as established open problems in the published literature.
\begin{question}[Priority and exact scope of the Euler package (source 52)]
Locate the quotient $\R((t))/\R[t,t^{-1}]$ and its generalized-series analogues in the literature on divisible modules, diagonal operator commutants, and formal differential equations. Is the combination of rational scalar structure, automatic strongness, and nonsplitting already known in this form? The first deliverable should be a citation-level comparison, not another novelty assertion. The proofs here remain useful regardless of priority.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[An explicit spanning family for the Euler quotient (source 52)]
The geometric classes $[(1-r t^\delta)^{-1}]$, $r>0$, are independent over $\R(X)$ and have the right cardinality, but Proposition~\ref{rvs:eu:prop:factorialwitness} proves that they do not span. In the Laurent case, their span consists of coefficient streams that, outside a finite set, are finite sums of rational functions of $n$ times positive real exponentials $r^n$. The enlarged factorial-exponential family is independent as well; characterize its span and construct explicit directions beyond it. Can a richer, naturally described family actually span the quotient? A ``basis'' claim must address spanning, not just cardinality.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[The extension class behind nonsplitting (source 52)]
Study the sequence $0\to \FinS{\Gamma}\to H_\Gamma\to Q_\Gamma\to0$ in
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\R[X]}(Q_\Gamma,\FinS{\Gamma}).
\]
Can one describe its extension class using the coordinate residues at the eigenvalues? Does the Hahn support restriction distinguish it from the unrestricted product module $\R^\Gamma$? The no-divisible-submodule theorem gives a concrete invariant that any classification must preserve.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Higher-rank Euler systems (source 52)]
A single injective real-valued exponent character is unavailable for non-Archimedean ordered groups. Replace $\EuD$ by a commuting family
\[
 \EuD_j(t^\gamma)=\ell_j(\gamma)t^\gamma
\]
of additive real-valued characters separating exponents, when such a family is chosen. Determine when their joint commutant is automatically strong, and identify the correct multi-variable localization quotient. Polynomial zero sets in several variables can meet the exponent image infinitely, so simply quotienting by finite support will no longer remove every resonance.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Finite fibers and matrix-valued coefficients (source 52)]
For a noninjective character $\ell:\Gamma\to\R$, eigenspaces are whole fiber-support spaces. Classify all commuting real-linear maps and identify conditions forcing strongness within each fiber. With finite-dimensional vector coefficients, scalar weights become matrices, suggesting a block version of Theorem~\ref{rvs:eu:thm:commutant}. Infinite-dimensional fibers are a genuine obstruction, not a cosmetic change of notation.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Strong-invariant subspaces versus coordinate subspaces (source 52)]
Proposition~\ref{rvs:eu:prop:coordinatesubspace} requires invariance under every coordinate projection. What follows from strong closure and invariance under $\EuD$ alone? More generally, which $\R[X]$-submodules of $H_\Gamma$ admit strong retractions, and which quotient subspaces lift to support-controlled subspaces upstairs? The ideal $J_\Gamma$ and the nonliftability theorem provide test cases.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Birthday-sensitive finite-dimensional elimination (source 52)]
Given a finite-dimensional surreal subspace generated by vectors of bounded birthdays, bound the birthdays of its canonical reduced basis and the complexity of its pivot exponents. Ordinary row reduction gives algebraic control but not sharp surreal simplicity bounds. A useful project would begin with Laurent and rational-exponent slices before attempting arbitrary normal forms.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Topologies that retain ordinary real scalar continuity (source 52)]
The valuation and full order topologies do not make the usual $\R$ act continuously. Investigate set-sized topologies that combine ordinary coefficient convergence with support control. State explicitly which Hahn sums remain continuous and whether the Euler commutant retains automatic continuity. A successful topology should not erase the difference between unbounded support and bounded accumulating support.
\end{question}

\subsubsection{Class foundations, choice, and definability}

\noindent\wtag{} ``Weakest basis principle'': see \cref{rvs:w:q:foundations}.

\begin{question}[Extending the monomials]
Over weak class theories, is ``the monomial class extends to a Hamel basis of
$\No$'' strictly stronger than ``$\No$ has some class Hamel basis''? Can the gap
be calibrated by standard subsystems of second-order set theory?
\end{question}

\noindent\wtag{} ``Definable Hamel bases'': printed with source 51's
question of the same name.

\begin{question}[Absoluteness]
How do class Hamel bases of $\No$ behave between transitive models with the same
ordinals? Which basis-existence, spanning, or definability statements are absolute,
and which can change after forcing that adds real coefficients or ordinal sequences?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[No-go theorems for natural Hamel bases]
Prove impossibility results for Hamel bases satisfying combinations of invariance
under monomial shifts, ordered additive exponent automorphisms, truncations,
conjugation in the surcomplexification, or simplicity-preserving maps. The
rigidity in \cref{rvs:cv:cor:projection-shift-rigidity} suggests that strong naturality
requirements may force a coordinate system to be the Hahn basis rather than a
Hamel basis.
\end{question}

\noindent\wtag{} Source 52's \cref{rvs:eu:thm:naturalobstruction} is one
such no-go theorem: no projective Hamel basis is invariant under the
coordinate reweightings.

\begin{question}[A category of Hahn-based vector spaces]
Axiomatize real vector spaces equipped with an ordered coordinate class, admissible
supports, and a strong summation operation. Determine the correct morphisms:
coefficientwise continuous maps, maps preserving all strong sums, or maps preserving
truncations. Establish analogues of bases, tensor products, exactness, completion,

\end{question}

For a chosen chain of convex subspaces or a quotient of the value group, compute

\section{Conclusions}\label{rvs:sec:conclusion}\label{rvs:cv:sec:conclusion}

\wtag{} Each source ends with its own conclusion; all three are printed.

\subsection{Source 51}

The surreal numbers are unquestionably a vector class over \(\R\), but the
ordinary phrase ``choose a basis'' suppresses the structure that makes them
surreal.  A Hamel basis sees only finite sums.  It exists under global choice,
may be chosen to contain every monomial, and is necessarily supplemented by a
proper class of infinite-support vectors.  Such a basis is useful for abstract
algebraic existence arguments but not for normal-form computation, order, or
asymptotics.

The natural answer is instead
\[
  \boxed{\M=\{\omega^\gamma:\gamma\in\No\}}.
\]
This class is canonical in three mutually reinforcing senses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item it is the Conway--Hahn basis giving every surreal a unique strongly
summable real expansion;
\item it is the basis of the one-dimensional dominance grades, with
\(\gr(\No)\cong\R[\No_{\mathrm{add}}]\);
\item it is normalized by simplicity, since \(\omega^\gamma\) is the
simplest positive representative of its Archimedean class.
\end{enumerate}
Its basis law is therefore not
\(\No=\bigoplus_\gamma\R\omega^\gamma\), but the sharper statement
\[
  \No=\classprod_{\gamma\in\No}\R\omega^\gamma,
\]
where the product sign denotes set-supported, reverse-well-ordered Hahn
coordinates.  (Equivalently, use \eqref{rvs:eq:direct-vs-hahn}; the displayed
formula is conceptual rather than new notation.)

Several consequences follow immediately once the correct synthesis rule is
fixed.  Support subsets give canonical projections.  Strong operators have
Hahn matrices.  Strong real functionals are precisely summation-finite
weighted coefficient sums, while order-bounded real functionals vanish.
Set-sized support windows reveal an enormous gap between Hahn and Hamel
dimensions.  The full fine topology is too refined for set-indexed partial
sums, so normal forms are formal strong sums rather than Schauder series.
Monomial symmetries and diagonal derivations reduce to group maps on the
exponents, and rational exponent independence becomes algebraic monomial
independence.

The resulting picture is analogous to several familiar distinctions, but is
not identical to any one of them: Fourier series versus finite trigonometric
polynomials, formal power series versus polynomials, completed graded objects
versus their direct sums, and topological bases versus algebraic bases.  The
surreal case adds two features absent from those set-sized analogies: the
exponent group is itself the proper class \(\No\), and every individual
series still has only set support.  That combination is exactly why the
Conway--Hahn basis is both global and rigorously manageable.

\subsection{Source 52}

Surreal numbers are naturally a real vector \emph{class with Hahn coordinates}, not a finite-coordinate enlargement of $\R$. Conway monomials are the right canonical coordinate objects, but not an algebraic basis. Their finite span omits a full-dimensional quotient in every nontrivial set-sized Hahn field, and no set of extra vectors repairs the full surreal class.

The positive finite-dimensional theorem and the negative global theorem fit together: every finite-dimensional subspace has a canonical reduced leading-coordinate basis, yet no valuation-independent Hamel basis exists for the whole Hahn space. Completion does not generally close the gap between finite spans and strong Hahn sums, and the full surreal topology is finer still.

The Euler operator gives the missing directions additional structure. On real exponent slices the quotient by finite monomial sums is canonically a continuum-dimensional vector space over $\R(X)$. All operators commuting with Euler are forced to be coefficientwise, but no Euler-invariant algebraic complement of the finite-monomial subspace exists. This combination supplies both a rigorous answer to the basis question and a concrete program for further research.

\subsection{Source 53}

The surreal numbers do form a vector space over $\R$, but ordinary basis language
is too coarse to capture their structure. The canonical coordinate directions are
the monomials $\omega^a$. They are linearly independent, their coefficient maps
separate points, and every surreal is uniquely assembled from them. Yet the assembly
operation is a set-supported Hahn sum, not a finite Hamel combination and not, in
the full natural valuation, a limit of finite combinations.

This distinction yields a coherent answer:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\{\omega^a\}$ is the natural Hahn basis of $\No$;
\item it is the natural homogeneous basis of $\gr\No$;
\item it is only a basis of $\Fin$ in the Hamel sense;
\item any Hamel basis of all $\No$ is a noncanonical proper class with vectors of
unbounded support complexity; and
\item the valuation, order, convex geometry, support projections, and natural
operator algebra are all organized directly by the monomial coordinates.
\end{itemize}

The resulting linear theory is not merely terminological. It gives exact dimensions
of support slices, explicit large independent families, a canonical projection and
diagonal calculus, a classification of convex subspaces and positive diagonal maps,
a coefficientwise reduction of finite linear systems, and a sharp separation
between algebraic span, topological closure, and strong-summation closure. These
results make the monomial Hahn basis the correct starting point for a systematic
``linear analysis of $\No$'' and provide a concrete formalization roadmap for the
\repo\ project.

\subsection{Source 52}

\subsubsection{Main dependency chain}
The chain for the principal operator result is short:
\[
 \text{support-preserving division}
 \Longrightarrow \text{finite resonance identity}
 \Longrightarrow \R(X)\text{-action on }Q_\Gamma;
\]
\[
 \im(\EuD-\gamma)=\ker(x\mapsto x_\gamma)
 \Longrightarrow \text{coefficientwise commutant}
 \Longrightarrow \text{nonsplitting}.
\]
The dimension lower bound uses eventual exponential-polynomial independence, while the upper bound uses countable supports in $\R$. The two-layer theorem adds bounded supports approaching zero at a controlled reciprocal rate. No unproved derivative formula at nonreal surreal exponents enters these arguments.

\subsubsection{Load-bearing hypotheses}
The group $\Gamma=\{0\}$ is excluded from the infinite-dimensional statements: then $H_\Gamma=\FinS{\Gamma}=\R$ and every quotient discussed there is zero. The real-exponent condition in the Euler sections is not optional. The polynomial zero set is finite because eigenvalues are distinct real exponents. Hahn sums are always set-indexed and have well-ordered support. Global choice is invoked only for the explicit class-basis discussion; ordinary set-sized choices suffice elsewhere.

The ideal controlling quotient lifts is $J_\Gamma$, not finitely supported weights. An infinite reverse-well-ordered weight support can have finite intersection with every individual Hahn support. This distinction was checked separately because it is easy to confuse ``finite output support for each input'' with ``finite global weight support.''

The finite-dimensional canonical basis theorem asserts exact leading-coordinate reduction in the entire subspace, not a numerical finite-prefix algorithm for arbitrary infinite inputs. The no-valuation-basis proof uses nested balls rather than an unjustified sum of basis vectors. The full surreal topology statements concern set-indexed nets; they say nothing automatic about proper-class-indexed notions of convergence.

\subsubsection{What has not been supplied}
There is no explicit Hamel basis spanning all of $H_\Gamma$ or $\No$, no classification of all invariant subspaces, no formal verification of the new theorems, and no claimed all-rank extension of the Euler quotient. The requested natural-basis question is answered by the Hahn/Hamel distinction; the stated theorems do not require any of these additional constructions.

\subsection{The sources' abstracts}

\paragraph{Source 51.}
\begin{quote}\small

The surreal numbers form a proper-class vector space over their canonical
copy of the real field.  The immediate question---what is its natural
basis?---has two different answers, depending on what ``basis'' means.
The canonical answer is the monomial class
\(
  \M=\{\omega^\gamma:\gamma\in\No\}.
\)
Every surreal has a unique Conway normal form, a set-supported,
reverse-well-ordered real linear combination of these monomials.  Thus
\(\M\) is a genuine \emph{Hahn basis}, a basis for strong transfinite
summation, and the canonical basis of the associated graded vector class.
It is not a Hamel basis, because Hamel combinations are finite and most
surreals have infinite normal-form support.

Working in NBG set theory with global choice, we prove that a Hamel class
basis does exist, but no set-sized family spans \(\No\), every such basis is
a proper class, and every Hamel basis contains a proper class of
infinite-support vectors.  The quotient by the finite-support skeleton
already contains an explicit proper-class linearly independent family.
On each infinite reverse-well-ordered support window \(A\), the set-sized
space of all series supported in \(A\) has Hamel dimension
\(\lvert\R\rvert^{\lvert A\rvert}\); in particular, a countable window has
continuum dimension, although its monomial Hahn basis is countable.

The normal form also yields a canonical Boolean algebra of support
projections and identifies the associated graded algebra with the real
group algebra of the additive surreal class.  We classify strongly real
linear maps by Hahn-compatible column families, and specialize this to an
exact classification of strongly summation-preserving real-valued
functionals.  Such a functional is a weighted coefficient sum whose active
exponents meet every reverse-well-ordered set only finitely; this permits
proper-class active sets, including the ordinal exponents.  In sharp
contrast, every order-bounded real linear functional is zero, hence there
is no nonzero positive real linear state and no nonzero order-compatible
real-valued seminorm on all of \(\No\).

Finally, the full natural valuation topology admits no non-eventually-
constant set-indexed convergent net, so Conway normal forms are strong
formal sums rather than Schauder expansions in that topology.  We describe
monomial-preserving ordered-field automorphisms, diagonal strong
derivations, and an explicit proper-class algebraically independent family.
A detailed research program closes the article.  Classical inputs are
Conway normal form, the omega map, Hahn--Neumann support arithmetic, and the
Erd\H{o}s--Kaplansky dimension theorem; the structural package assembled here
is offered as an unrefereed research draft, not as a claim of priority.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Source 52.}
\begin{quote}\small

The surreal numbers form a proper-class vector space over the reals. Their natural coordinates are Conway's monomials $\omega^a$, but these are a Hahn basis, not a Hamel basis: arbitrary vectors require set-sized, reverse-well-ordered supports. We make this distinction precise and develop its consequences.

For every nontrivial set-sized ordered abelian group $\Gamma$, the finite-monomial span $\FinS{\Gamma}$ has the largest possible algebraic defect in $H_\Gamma=\R((t^\Gamma))$: both $H_\Gamma$ and $H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma}$ have real dimension $|H_\Gamma|$. Finite-dimensional subspaces have canonical reduced valuation bases, whereas the full Hahn space has no valuation-independent Hamel basis. We prove an automorphism obstruction to a natural Hamel basis, classify the valuation completion of the monomial span, and separate strong from merely continuous real-valued coordinate functionals.

The main further development concerns nonzero additive subgroups $\Gamma\subseteq\R$. The Euler derivation $\EuD(t^\gamma)=\gamma t^\gamma$ makes $H_\Gamma/\FinS{\Gamma}$ canonically a vector space over $\R(X)$ of dimension $2^{\aleph_0}$. Every real-linear endomorphism of $H_\Gamma$ commuting with $\EuD$ is automatically a strong coefficient multiplier. Consequently $\FinS{\Gamma}$ has no $\EuD$-invariant complement, despite having algebraic complements. For dense $\Gamma$, the completion defect itself splits into two continuum-dimensional $\R(X)$-layers. Complete proofs, explicit witnesses, finite exact-arithmetic checks, a provenance audit, and further research questions are included.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Source 53.}
\begin{quote}\small

The surreal numbers form an ordered vector space over their canonical copy of
$\mathbb R$, but the usual phrase ``choose a basis'' conceals three separate
issues: $\No$ is a proper class; a Hamel basis permits only finite combinations;
and Conway normal forms are transfinite Hahn sums rather than ordinary convergent
series. This report separates those issues and gives a precise answer. The
canonical monomials $\omega^a$ form a Hahn or summation basis, not a Hamel basis.
The finite monomial span is the class group algebra $\mathbb R[\No]$, whereas
all of $\No$ is the corresponding proper-class Hahn field
$\mathbb R((\omega^{\No}))$.

From this viewpoint we prove a collection of linear-algebraic consequences.
No set can span $\No$; under an explicit class-choice hypothesis a class Hamel
basis exists and may be chosen to contain all monomials, but every such basis has
vectors of arbitrarily large support. For a set-sized reverse-well-ordered exponent
set $A$, the support slice $\Hahn(A)$ is canonically $\mathbb R^A$ and has Hamel
dimension $|\mathbb R|^{|A|}$ when $A$ is infinite. A concrete Vandermonde family
of continuum many independent geometric-series surreals already occurs in a
countable slice. The leading-exponent filtration has one-dimensional successive
layers and associated graded algebra $\mathbb R[\No]$. Support cuts give canonical
linear projections; their commutant consists exactly of coefficientwise diagonal
operators. We classify the positive diagonal operators and all convex real
subspaces. Finite real linear systems over $\No$ reduce coefficientwise to the
same systems over $\mathbb R$. Finally, we prove a topology/summation trichotomy:
the algebraic span and natural-valuation closure of the monomials are both the
finite-support subspace, while closure under admissible Hahn sums is all of $\No$.
The final sections map these results to existing Lean developments in \repo\ and
propose a program of further research and formalization.
\end{quote}
