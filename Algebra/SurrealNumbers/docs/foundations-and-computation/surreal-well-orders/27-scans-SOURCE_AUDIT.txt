2. SCOPE AND DOCUMENTATION FINDINGS

This was a targeted source and mathematical audit supporting the new article,
not a repository-wide correctness certification or a Lean build.

The downloaded merged surreal report contains Parts I-VIII. It refers to a
Part IX that is not present in that body. The accompanying README describes
an earlier four-source report and does not fully reflect the expanded body.
The older research-question index is dated 2 October, while later status
updates dated 3 October mark several of those questions as resolved.

The continuation therefore uses the current body and its later status
summaries. It does not treat a result as new merely because an advertised
Part IX proof is absent. Separately distributed source archives for the
missing installment were not audited, so no source-archive priority claim
is made.

3. EXISTING RESULTS CREDITED TO THE CORPUS

The article uses, with attribution, the general-alphabet enumeration spectrum,
set-length surreal coding and word isomorphisms, direct labelled comparison
of raw class well-orders, canonical first set-like blocks, bounded-support
cores, represented and exhaustive cut recognition, previously established
termination-convention analysis, centered increasing-word character results,
and the exact cardinal reserve needed for partial-permutation completion.

The already established relation-table equimorphism and endpoint results are
also distinguished from the scan-dependent topology proved in the new paper.
The source already identifies least ordinal support height, as distinct from
support cardinality, as a further problem.

4. CONTINUATIONS PROVED IN THE ARTICLE

The principal further results concern:

* Exact ordinal spectra for arbitrary set scans and the density/cellularity
  contrast between initial-cardinal scans and six delayed coordinates.
* Density of the same bounded-support core in raw relation-table comparison.
* Exact terminal-cut factorization for increasing words, its centered product
  form, additional zero-convention identities, and convex interval structure.
* Branch transitivity of the ordered injective prefix tree, failure of intrinsic
  exhaustiveness recognition, sufficient coverage bounds, and the effect of
  retaining cross-node label equality.
* Whole convex copies omitted by every uniform ordinary class-indexed family
  of raw global well-orders, plus the set analogue at birthday cutoffs.
* A necessary and sufficient ordinal support cutoff and its exact least value.
* Reconstruction of a model's admitted classes from embeddings fixing the
  displayed bounded-support core pointwise.
* An explicit full-rank model criterion and a detailed foundational ledger.

The article supplies proofs. These are extensions relative to the inspected
corpus, not claims of historical novelty. Some statements repackage familiar
foundational arguments to make the relevant size assumptions explicit.

5. FORMALIZATION STATUS

The inspected Lean modules provide the sign-sequence carrier, its numerical
order, ordinal embedding, small-family birthday bounds, bounded-birthday
smallness, small-cut interpolation, and obstructions to unrestricted cuts.
The exact declaration names are tabulated in Section 11.

Ordinal.{u} and SignSequence.{u} both live in Type (u+1). Small.{u} refers to
resizing into the lower universe u. Relations and predicate collections over
a fixed carrier do not automatically require a further universe at every step.

The new lexicographic theorems have not been machine-checked. The proposed
module order is a development plan, not a description of completed Lean code.

7. REMAINING QUESTIONS

The paper does not claim a complete classification of arbitrary relation scans,
all unfilled cuts at general increasing-word gap thresholds, or the full group
preserving exhaustive branches. The proposed isomorphism between the centered
product and the nonempty shorter-first word order remains a research question.
These limits are stated explicitly in the twelve-question research agenda.
