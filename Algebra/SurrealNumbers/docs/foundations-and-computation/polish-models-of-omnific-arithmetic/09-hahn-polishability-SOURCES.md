## Primary mathematical literature

1. Elliot Glazer, *A Topological Tennenbaum Theorem*, arXiv:2311.13699v1 (2023).
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13699
   PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.13699
   Read for the actual arithmetic-topology connection. Theorem 2 and
   Corollary 1 were checked on PDF page 3, including a rendered page.
   The new article does not claim a new answer to Glazer's printed questions.

2. Ruiyuan Chen, *On the Pettis–Johnstone theorem for localic groups*,
   arXiv:2109.12721v1 (2021).
   https://arxiv.org/html/2109.12721v1
   The introduction and discussion of consequences identify the classical
   Pettis, automatic-continuity, and open-mapping toolkit. The article supplies
   its own short classical proofs; it does not use the localic machinery.

3. Alexander S. Kechris, *Classical Descriptive Set Theory*, GTM 156,
   Springer (1995).
   https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4
   Publisher metadata checked. Cited as the standard reference for analytic
   sets and well-founded-tree completeness, not represented as a complete
   cover-to-cover review of this book in the session.

4. Bjorn Poonen, *Maximally complete fields*, L'Enseignement Mathématique
   39 (1993), 87–106.
   https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/amsval.pdf
   Author-hosted paper read for generalized series, Corollary 4 (algebraic
   closedness of the appropriate full Hahn fields), and Corollary 7
   (Newton–Puiseux in characteristic zero). Bibliographic first page was
   inspected in a rendered view. Classical real-closedness inputs are credited.

5. Khodr Shamseddine and Martin Berz, *Analysis on the Levi-Civita field,
   a brief overview*, Contemporary Mathematics 508 (2010), 215–237.
   https://www2.physics.umanitoba.ca/u/khodr/Publications/RS-Overview-offprints.pdf
   Background for the classical real-coefficient construction. The manuscript
   explicitly distinguishes that nonseparable field from its countable-
   coefficient Polish construction.
