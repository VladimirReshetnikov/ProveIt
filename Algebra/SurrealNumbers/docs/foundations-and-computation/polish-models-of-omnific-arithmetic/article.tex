\begin{abstract}
Elliot Glazer's \emph{A Topological Tennenbaum Theorem} shows that an
uncountable Polish model of $Q+I\mathrm{Open}$ cannot have continuous
addition and Borel multiplication, and asks (Question~2) whether some
uncountable Polish space supports a model of Presburger arithmetic with
continuous addition. Part~I answers that question, as printed, affirmatively.
The nonnegative cone
$\MR=(\R_{>0}\times\Z)\cup(\{0\}\times\N)$ of the lexicographic group
$\R\lex\Z$, with the usual topology on the real coordinate and the discrete
one on the integer coordinate, is a perfect, locally compact, uncountable
Polish model of the full theory $\Th(\N;0,1,+,<)$ whose addition is
continuous. Replacing $\R$ by the lexicographically ordered group $\Q^{\N}$
with discrete coordinates gives a model homeomorphic to Baire space, with
Type-2 computable addition and division by standard integers. Every
definable relation is $\Dtwo$, and this is optimal for the order; the
truncated subtraction and the predecessor are discontinuous exactly on
explicit loci; no unital semiring multiplication exists on either model; the
self-embeddings are classified (positive dilations in the real case,
row-finite matrices with increasing positive pivots in the Baire case), and
both models embed additively in Conway's omnific integers $\Oz$ as
$r\omega+n$ and $\sum_j a_j\omega^{1/(j+1)}+n$. Part~I also isolates the
coset-invariance hypothesis needed to carry the repository's integer Cooper
lemmas to nonstandard groups. Glazer himself speculated that the answer to
Question~2 would be negative; the affirmative answer is given for the
question as printed, and its priority has not been established.

Part~II is the other side of the same boundary. For a countable linear
order $L$, its well-ordered subsets form a Borel set exactly when $L$ is
scattered, and a $\boldsymbol\Pi^1_1$-complete set otherwise; the same
condition decides whether a full real Hahn field $\R((t^\Gamma))$ has a
standard Borel parametrization with Borel coefficient maps. The finite
Puiseux principal-part ring $\A$, of which $\MR$ is one stratum, satisfies
open induction and has a Polish presentation on which addition and
multiplication are continuous off explicit closed nowhere dense cancellation
loci; by Glazer's Corollary~1 no Borel-compatible Polish recoding makes its
addition continuous everywhere.

The report merges three independently written manuscripts; it states which
source proved each result, prints shared results once, keeps every source's
limitations, and cites the collection's earlier results instead of
reprinting them as new. It is an AI-assisted draft: it has not been
refereed, and nothing in it has been formalized.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, sources and conventions}\label{pma:sec:intro}

This report collects three manuscripts written on the same day, October 3,
2026, in answer to one research brief: where Elliot Glazer's topological
Tennenbaum programme meets the repository's omnific arithmetic. Their common
spine is the question which arithmetic operations of a Polish or standard
Borel presentation of a substructure of $\Oz$ can be continuous.
\Cref{pma:part:pr} (label prefix \texttt{pma:pr:}) constructs Polish models of
Presburger arithmetic with continuous addition and studies them; its base
text is manuscript~05, and the further results of manuscript~04 are printed
as marked sections. \Cref{pma:part:bor} (label prefix \texttt{pma:bor:}) is
manuscript~03: Borel presentations of Hahn fields, support barriers, and the
finite principal-part ring on which Glazer's theorem forbids continuous
addition. The two parts meet in \cref{pma:sec:boundary}.

\subsection{Glazer's paper and its two questions}\label{pma:sec:glazer}

Glazer's paper \cite{Glazer} is an eight-page preprint, dated November 20,
2023 on its first page and submitted to arXiv on November 22, 2023 as
arXiv:2311.13699. On October 3, 2026 the arXiv record listed one version (v1)
and no journal reference. It answers negatively a question of Enayat, Hamkins
and Wcis\l o \cite{EHW}: no model of Peano arithmetic on a perfect Polish
space has continuous addition and multiplication (its Theorem~1). Its
Theorem~2 says that if $(M,+,\cdot)\models Q+I\Delta_0+\mathrm{Exp}$ lives on an
uncountable Polish space with Borel addition and multiplication, then neither
operation is continuous. Glazer notes that the axiom $\mathrm{Exp}$ is used
only to obtain the function $x\mapsto2^x$, and only for the discontinuity of
multiplication; this gives his Corollary~1: \emph{there is no model of
$Q+I\mathrm{Open}$ on an uncountable Polish space in which addition is
continuous and multiplication is Borel}. The paper ends with two questions.
Question~1 asks whether Theorem~2 is provable in $\mathsf{ATR}_0$. Question~2
is quoted in \cref{pma:pr:sec:overlap}: does some uncountable Polish space
support a model of Presburger arithmetic with continuous addition?

\paragraph{Glazer's speculation.} Immediately after Question~2, Glazer
writes that he speculates that an affirmative answer to Question~1
``would lead to a negative answer to Question~2'' \cite[p.~8]{Glazer}. His
reason is an analogy: removing the exponential function from the proof that
multiplication is discontinuous would parallel removing multiplication from
a proof that addition is discontinuous. Neither source of \cref{pma:part:pr}
mentions this sentence; it is recorded here because \cref{pma:part:pr} goes
the other way.

\paragraph{How this report reads it \writetag.} \Cref{pma:part:pr} answers
Question~2 \emph{as printed} affirmatively (\cref{pma:pr:thm:main}). The
question asks only for an uncountable Polish space, a model of Presburger
arithmetic and continuous addition; it does not ask that the topology be the
order topology, and it mentions no multiplication. The speculation is a
heuristic about proofs, not a stated implication, and nothing here bears on
Question~1, which stays open; no conclusion about Question~1 is drawn from
the affirmative answer. Three facts locate the difference. First, the
affirmative models are not an artefact of a missing definability condition:
every definable relation in them is $\Dtwo$
(\cref{pma:pr:thm:definableborel}), so the order and every other definable
relation are Borel, as in the Borel models of Glazer's theorems. Second, what fails in them is multiplication: no
unital semiring multiplication exists on them at all
(\cref{pma:pr:thm:no_semiring}), so Corollary~1 does not apply. Third, on a
ring that does carry Borel multiplication and open induction, Corollary~1
does forbid continuous addition under every Borel-compatible Polish recoding
(\cref{pma:bor:thm:glazer}), and \cref{pma:sec:boundary} shows that the real
model of \cref{pma:part:pr} sits inside that ring. We do not know whether
Glazer intended Question~2 with a further hypothesis, for instance an
expansion to a model of a stronger theory, under which his speculation could
hold; that would be a different question.

\paragraph{Priority.} Priority of the affirmative answer is \textbf{not
established}. Source~05 searched for the exact question and combinations of
its terms and found no earlier answer, and says that this does not prove that
none exists; source~04 says that historical priority has not been established.
The intake re-checked only that the arXiv record has one version and no
journal reference (October 3, 2026). The answer is an elementary
construction; an earlier publication of it, or a published variant of the
question, may exist.

\subsection{Relation to the repository}\label{pma:sec:repo}

\paragraph{The formal project \fn{Logic/PresburgerArithmetic}.} Both sources of
\cref{pma:part:pr} inspected this project at their pins, 05 its Lean file
\fn{Lean/PresburgerArithmetic/Cooper.lean} and 04 its Rocq file
\fn{Coq/Cooper.v}. The project decides Presburger sentences over the ordinary
integers. Its periodicity lemmas assume a predicate on $\Z$ invariant under one
standard shift; \cref{pma:pr:ex:falseperiod,pma:pr:prop:periodicity} show that
this hypothesis cannot be carried verbatim to nonstandard $\Z$-groups, and
\cref{pma:pr:lem:finite,pma:pr:thm:boundary} give the model-uniform
replacement. This is a porting requirement, not a defect of the integer
theorems. \Cref{pma:pr:sec:formalrepo} names the declarations. \textbf{Nothing
in this report is formalized}, and its placement in the Surreal collection,
with a pointer from the formal project, confers no formal status.

\paragraph{The Surreal collection.} Results already in the collection are
cited, not reprinted as new:
\begin{itemize}
\item \replabel{exr:prop:division} and the paragraph after it in the
\fn{exponential-relations-over-omnific-integers} report: $\Oz$ is a
$\Z$-group, hence a model of Presburger arithmetic in its ordered-group
presentation. \Cref{pma:pr:prop:zgroup,pma:pr:thm:qe} prove the classical
facts again for the split groups $\GD$.
\item \replabel{isg:cf:main:presburger} in the
\fn{discrete-initial-subgroups-and-omnific-normalization} report: a set-sized
$\Z$-group has an initial realization in $\Oz$ if and only if it splits as
$G^{\mathrm{dv}}\lex\Z$ with $G^{\mathrm{dv}}=\bigcap_n nG$. The groups $\GD$
of \cref{pma:part:pr} are split by construction, with $\bigcap_m m\GD=D\times\{0\}$
(\cref{pma:pr:cor:congruences}), so that theorem applies to them \mergetag;
whether 05's maps $\iota_{\R}$, $\iota_{\mathrm B}$ are initial
realizations in its sense is not checked here.
\item \replabel{onot:neg:lem:kbtree}, \replabel{onot:neg:thm:pionesone} and
\replabel{onot:neg:rem:secondroute} in the \fn{omnific-notations} report: the

\section{The research overlap and the precise target}
\label{pma:pr:sec:overlap}

The relevant intersection is \emph{topological and effective model theory
of weak arithmetic}. Glazer's work supplies a sharply formulated question;
ProveIt supplies two concrete mathematical interfaces: an executable
Cooper quantifier-elimination development and a normal-form construction
of the omnific integers. This makes it possible to connect an external
Polish topology, internal induction, and exact surreal coordinates without
appealing to the repository's unreviewed research claims.

In the final section of \cite{Glazer}, Glazer asks:
\begin{quote}
Does some uncountable Polish space support a model of Presburger
arithmetic with continuous addition?
\end{quote}
The source is Question 2 on page 8 of arXiv:2311.13699v1. We use precisely
that formulation. The topology need not be the arithmetic order topology,
and no multiplication is required. These two distinctions are essential.

Glazer's obstruction for stronger arithmetic includes the impossibility
of an uncountable Polish model of $Q+I\mathrm{Open}$ with continuous
addition and Borel multiplication \cite[Corollary 1]{Glazer}. The earlier
study of Enayat, Hamkins, and Wcis\l o \cite{EHW} motivates the broader
problem of finding topological presentations of arithmetic. Our
construction does not contradict those results: it supports the additive
language, and its additive structure prevents a semiring expansion.

\section{The construction in one theorem}
\label{pma:pr:sec:main}

We include $0$ in $\N$. For an ordered abelian group $D$, let
\[
 \GD=D\lex\Z
\]
mean the direct-product additive group with the $D$ coordinate more
significant in the order. Thus
\begin{equation}
 (d,n)<(e,m)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 d<e\ \text{ or }\ (d=e\text{ and }n<m).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:lex}
\end{equation}
Write $\one=(0,1)$ and let
\begin{equation}
 \MD=(\GD)_{\geq0}
      =(D_{>0}\times\Z)\cup(\{0\}\times\N).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:cone}
\end{equation}
The ambient product topology, whenever specified, is separate from
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:cone}'s arithmetic order.

\begin{theorem}[Polish and effective Presburger models]
\label{pma:pr:thm:main}
The following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item With $D=\R$ in its usual topology, $\MR$ is an uncountable,
locally compact Polish space without isolated points. Coordinatewise
addition is continuous, and
\[
 (\MR;0,\one,+,<)\models\Th(\N;0,1,+,<).
\]
\item With $D=\Q^{\N}$, ordered by the first differing coordinate and
with each rational coordinate discrete, the space $\MB=\MD$ is
homeomorphic to $\Baire$. There is an explicit such homeomorphism under
which addition, successor, and division by each positive standard integer
are Type-2 computable.
\item In both models, every first-order definable relation, with
parameters, is $\Dtwo$. The order is neither open nor closed. In the Baire
presentation, each formula has a computable bound on the number of
changes in a convergent truth approximation, relative to its parameter
codes.
\item Both models embed as ordered additive submonoids of $\Oz$, fixing
$0$ and $1$. Neither admits any unital semiring multiplication with its
given addition, zero, and unit.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proofs occupy \cref{pma:pr:sec:algebra,pma:pr:sec:topology,pma:pr:sec:baire,pma:pr:sec:borel,pma:pr:sec:omnific,pma:pr:sec:multiplication}.
The first assertion alone answers the source question. The later assertions
explain how much regularity is possible and where it necessarily stops.

For orientation, an element $(r,-17)$ with $r>0$ is a \emph{positive}
element of $\MR$. Indeed it exceeds every standard numeral $(0,k)$.
Nevertheless,
\begin{equation}
 (1/j,0)\longrightarrow(0,0)
 \qquad(j\longrightarrow\infty).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:smallseq}
\end{equation}
in the chosen topology. Arithmetic size and topological proximity are
different notions. The construction would fail if one silently demanded
that this topology be the order topology.

\section{Full Presburger arithmetic, not merely a few axioms}
\label{pma:pr:sec:algebra}\label{pma:pr:s04:sec:algebra}

\subsection{Discretely ordered groups with standard residues}

\begin{definition}
A \emph{$\Z$-group} in this article is a linearly ordered abelian group
$G$ with a distinguished least positive element $\one$, such that for
every positive standard integer $m$ and every $x\in G$ there exist
$q\in G$ and $r\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 x=mq+r\one.
 \label{pma:pr:eq:division}
\end{equation}
Here $mq$ denotes repeated addition by a \emph{standard} integer, not a
new multiplication operation on $G$.
\end{definition}

Ordered abelian groups are torsion-free. The quotient and remainder in
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:division} are unique: if $m(q-q')=(r'-r)\one$ and $q>q'$, then
$q-q'\geq\one$, whereas $r'-r<m$; the other inequality is similar.
We suppress $\one$ in standard integer constants when no confusion is
possible.

\begin{proposition}[The split lexicographic group]
\label[proposition]{pma:pr:prop:zgroup}\label[proposition]{pma:pr:lem:division}
If $D$ is a divisible ordered abelian group, then $D\lex\Z$ is a
$\Z$-group. Its standard division algorithm is
\begin{equation}
 (d,n)=m\bigl(d/m,\lfloor n/m\rfloor\bigr)+(0,n\bmod m).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:explicitdivision}
\end{equation}
If $(d,n)\geq0$, the displayed quotient is nonnegative.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Coordinatewise addition preserves the lexicographic order. A positive
$(d,n)$ either has $d>0$, in which case it exceeds $(0,1)$, or has $d=0$
and $n\geq1$. Thus $(0,1)$ is the least positive element. Divisibility
and torsion-freeness of $D$ give the unique element $d/m$. Ordinary
Euclidean division of $n$ now proves \eqref{pma:pr:eq:explicitdivision}.
If $d>0$, then $d/m>0$, regardless of the integer quotient. If $d=0$
and $n\geq0$, the integer quotient is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

Source~04 proves the same statement (its Lemma~2.2) with the same explicit
quotient and the same nonnegativity clause; its uniqueness argument compares
coordinates instead of using least positivity.

\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 04)]
If $(d,z)>0$ and $d>0$, then $(d,z)>(0,1)$. If $d=0$, positivity says
$z\geq1$. This proves discreteness. Existence in
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:division} follows from divisibility of $D$ and ordinary integer
Euclidean division, including for negative $z$.

For uniqueness, equality of $mq+r\one$ and $mq'+r'\one$ first gives equality
of the $D$-coordinates of $q,q'$. Equality of the integer coordinates then
gives ordinary uniqueness of quotient and remainder. Finally, if $d>0$,
then $d/m>0$. If $d=0$ and $z\geq0$, then $\lfloor z/m\rfloor\geq0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\src{04}]\label{pma:pr:cor:congruences}
For $m\geq1$,
\[
 mG_D=D\times m\Z,
 \qquad
 (d,z)\equiv(e,w)\pmod{mG_D}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ z\equiv w\pmod m.
\]
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:divisiblepart}
 \bigcap_{m\geq1}mG_D=D\times\{0\}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every $D$-coordinate is an $m$-multiple. The only ordinary integer divisible
by every positive integer is zero.
\end{proof}

The standard subgroup $\Z\one$ is convex, and $G_D/\Z\one$ is the ordered
divisible group $D$. This gives the usual quick recognition of $G_D$ as a
Presburger group. For the precise model-theoretic conclusion, we next give
a direct elimination argument.

The implication from these axioms to \emph{full} Presburger arithmetic is
important enough to prove. We use the classical elimination architecture
of Presburger and Cooper; \cite{Haase} supplies historical and algorithmic
context. The proof below makes explicit which algebraic hypotheses are
needed in a nonstandard group.

\subsection{Coset invariance versus standard-shift periodicity}
\label{pma:pr:sec:periodicity}

For $m\geq1$, write $mG=\{mh:h\in G\}$. A predicate $P$ is
\emph{$mG$-invariant} if
\begin{equation}
 y-z\in mG\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(P(y)\leftrightarrow P(z)).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:cosetinv}
\end{equation}
This is stronger than invariance under the single standard shift $m\one$.

\begin{example}[A false generalization of an integer lemma]
\label[example]{pma:pr:ex:falseperiod}
In $G=\R\lex\Z$, let $P(d,n)$ mean $d>0$. For every positive standard
$m$,
\[
 P(x+m\one)\leftrightarrow P(x)
\]
holds. Yet $P$ has witnesses and is false at every standard residue
$0,1,\ldots,m-1$. Thus
\[
 (\exists x\ P(x))\ \not\Longleftrightarrow\
 \bigvee_{0\leq r<m}P(r\one).
\]
It also fails the naive finite-interval reduction with lower bound $0$
and upper bound $(1,0)$. No flaw in the repository's \texttt{Int} theorem
is involved: its domain is the ordinary integers, where every multiple
is obtained by a standard finite iteration.
\end{example}

\paragraph{The general counterexample \src{04}.} Source~04 phrases coset
invariance as a definition and gives the counterexample for every nonzero
divisible $D$, with the predicate $d\ne0$ in place of $d>0$. In its text $G$
is a linearly ordered abelian group with a least positive element $\one$ and
unique division by positive standard integers as in
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:division}, that is, a $\Z$-group.

\begin{definition}[\src{04}]\label{pma:pr:def:residueinvariance}
A predicate $P\subseteq G$ is \emph{$m$-residue invariant} if
\[
 u-v\in mG\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(P(u)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ P(v)).
\]
No definability hypothesis is imposed in this definition.
\end{definition}

The condition is stronger than invariance under the single translation
$x\mapsto x+m\one$. This distinction is invisible over the ordinary group
$\Z$, but is essential over nonstandard groups.

\begin{proposition}[One-step periodicity is insufficient \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:prop:periodicity}
If $D\ne0$, the external predicate
\[
 P(d,z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d\ne0
\]
on $G_D$ satisfies $P(x+m\one)\leftrightarrow P(x)$ for every standard
$m\geq1$. Nevertheless $P$ is nonempty and is false at every standard
residue $0,1,\ldots,m-1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A standard integer translation does not change the $D$-coordinate. A
nonzero $D$-coordinate exists, whereas every standard integer has
$D$-coordinate zero. In particular $P$ is not $m$-residue invariant:
$(d,0)-(0,0)\in mG_D$ for every $d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The coset-invariant finite search]
\label[lemma]{pma:pr:lem:finite}
Let $G$ be a $\Z$-group, $m\geq1$ standard, and $P$ an $mG$-invariant
predicate. For $L,U\in G$,
\begin{align}
 &\exists y\ (L\leq y\leq U\ \wedge\ P(y))
 \nonumber\\[-.2em]
 &\hspace{1.3cm}\Longleftrightarrow
 \bigvee_{0\leq r<m}\bigl(L+r\leq U\ \wedge\ P(L+r)\bigr).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:finitesearch}
\end{align}
With only an upper bound, candidates $U-r$ suffice; with only a lower
bound, candidates $L+r$ suffice. With neither bound, the candidates are
$0,\ldots,m-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given a witness $y\geq L$, write $y-L=mh+r$ with $0\leq r<m$.
One has $h\geq0$: otherwise $h\leq-1$, and then
$mh+r\leq-m+(m-1)=-1$, contradicting $y-L\geq0$.
Consequently
\[
 L\leq L+r\leq y\leq U.
\]
The difference $y-(L+r)=mh$ lies in $mG$, so $P(L+r)$ holds.
Conversely every displayed candidate is already a witness. The same
argument applied to $U-y$ proves the upper-bound version. Without bounds,
use the standard remainder of $y$ itself.
\end{proof}

Congruence predicates have exactly the stronger invariance
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:cosetinv}. If $M$ is a common multiple of finitely many standard
moduli, any Boolean combination of congruences with those moduli is
$MG$-invariant. This remains true with arbitrary nonstandard parameters:
adding $Mh$ to a variable changes an affine congruence term by an element
of each relevant modulus subgroup.

\subsection{Second route \src{04}: finite witnesses at a boundary}\label{pma:pr:sec:qe}

Source~04's form of \cref{pma:pr:lem:finite} works with strict bounds and an
explicit finite candidate set. It is the same theorem with a different
normalization of the bounds, and it is the step that 04 uses in its
elimination proof below. Here $G$ is a $\Z$-group, and a standard integer $r$
also denotes $r\one$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite boundary criterion \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:thm:boundary}
Let $A,B$ be finite lists of elements of $G$, let $m\geq1$, and let $P$ be
$m$-residue invariant. Put
\[
 \Phi(x):=P(x)\ \wedge\bigwedge_{a\in A}(a<x)
                    \ \wedge\bigwedge_{b\in B}(x<b).
\]
Define a finite candidate set $C$ by
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:candidates}
 C=
 \begin{cases}
  \{a_*+1,\ldots,a_*+m\},&A\ne\varnothing,\quad a_*=\max A,\\
  \{b_*-1,\ldots,b_*-m\},&A=\varnothing,\ B\ne\varnothing,\quad b_*=\min B,\\
  \{0,\ldots,m-1\},&A=B=\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:boundaryequivalence}
 (\exists x\in G)\,\Phi(x)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigvee_{c\in C}\Phi(c).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The reverse direction is immediate. Suppose first that $A\ne\varnothing$
and $x$ is a witness. Divide $x-a_*-1$ by $m$ to obtain
\[
 x-a_*=mq+s,\qquad 1\leq s\leq m.
\]
Here $q$ is an element of $G$, not necessarily a standard integer. If
$q<0$, discreteness gives $q\leq-1$, whence $mq+s\leq0$, contradicting
$x>a_*$. Thus $q\geq0$ and $c=a_*+s\leq x$. This $c$ is larger than every
lower bound and smaller than every upper bound because $c\leq x<b$.
Also $x-c=mq\in mG$, so $P(c)$ holds.

If $A$ is empty and $B$ is nonempty, divide $b_*-x-1$ instead. The result is
$b_*-x=mq+s$ with $q\geq0$ and $1\leq s\leq m$. The candidate
$c=b_*-s$ satisfies $x\leq c<b_*\leq b$ for every upper bound and is in
the same residue class as $x$. Finally, when both lists are empty, take the
unique standard representative of $x$ modulo $mG$.
\end{proof}

This proof uses no induction through a nonstandard interval. Its only
finite searches have standard lengths fixed by the formula being
eliminated. Weak bounds can be converted to strict ones using discreteness:
$a\leq x$ is $a-1<x$, and $x\leq b$ is $x<b+1$.

\subsection{A self-contained elimination argument}

Expand the ordered-group language by predicates
\[
 D_m(t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\in mG
 \qquad(m\geq2),
\]
and regard $D_1$ as always true. All of these are definable by existential
formulas in the group language.

\begin{theorem}[Classical Presburger elimination over $\Z$-groups]
\label{pma:pr:thm:qe}
In the expanded language
\[
 \{0,\one,+,-,\leq,(D_m)_{m\geq2}\},
\]
the axioms of $\Z$-groups eliminate quantifiers and form a complete theory.
In particular every $\Z$-group is elementarily equivalent to
$(\Z;0,1,+,-,\leq,(m\mid\cdot)_m)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to eliminate one existential quantifier from a quantifier-free
formula. Rewrite the formula in disjunctive normal form. Negated
inequalities are replaced using discreteness, for example
$\neg(s\leq t)\leftrightarrow t+1\leq s$. An equality is the conjunction
of two inequalities; a disequality is a disjunction of strict inequalities.
Finally, standard residue exhaustiveness gives
\[
 \neg D_m(t)\ \longleftrightarrow\
 \bigvee_{r=1}^{m-1} D_m(t-r).
\]
Thus it is enough to eliminate $\exists x$ from a conjunction of affine
inequalities and positive divisibility atoms, along with conjuncts not
involving $x$.

Let $A$ be a positive common multiple of every nonzero absolute coefficient
of $x$ occurring in that conjunction; take $A=1$ if there are none. Set
$y=Ax$ and add the condition $D_A(y)$. An atom
\[
 ax+t\leq0,\qquad a\neq0,
\]
can be multiplied by the positive standard integer $k=A/|a|$, giving
\[
 \operatorname{sgn}(a)y+kt\leq0.
\]
It is now an upper or lower bound on $y$. For a congruence, torsion-free
cancellation gives the equivalence
\begin{equation}
 D_m(ax+t)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D_{km}\bigl(\operatorname{sgn}(a)y+kt\bigr).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:congscale}
\end{equation}
Indeed $kz\in kmG$ if and only if $z\in mG$: one direction multiplies a
witness, and the other cancels $k$ from $kz=kmh$.
The additional atom $D_A(y)$ is exactly what ensures that every candidate
$y$ comes from a unique $x$.

After normalizing signs, we have finitely many lower bounds $L_i\leq y$,
finitely many upper bounds $y\leq U_j$, and a conjunction $P(y)$ of
congruence conditions. Let $M$ be a positive common multiple of all their
moduli, including $A$. Then $P$ is $MG$-invariant.

If lower bounds exist, choose their maximum $L$. By
\cref{pma:pr:lem:finite}, a witness exists exactly when one of
$L,L+1,\ldots,L+M-1$ satisfies all the normalized conjuncts. The maximum
need not be introduced as a new function symbol: take a finite disjunction
over the indices $i$, require $L_h\leq L_i$ for every $h$, and substitute
$L_i+r$ for $y$. This produces a quantifier-free formula in the parameters.
If there are upper bounds but no lower bounds, choose a minimal $U_j$
and substitute $U_j-r$. If no bounds exist, substitute the standard
residues $r$. Conjuncts independent of $x$ are retained throughout.

This eliminates one existential quantifier. Induction on formula
complexity, replacing universal quantification by negated existential
quantification, eliminates all quantifiers.

A quantifier-free sentence uses only standard integer multiples of
$\one$. Their order and their residues are the same in every $\Z$-group,
by least positivity and uniqueness of standard remainders. Therefore all
$\Z$-groups satisfy the same sentences. The ordinary integers are one
such group, proving completeness and the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Second route \src{04}: normalization and elimination}\label{pma:pr:sec:qe04}

Source~04 proves \cref{pma:pr:thm:qe} by a differently organized argument: it
keeps negated congruences instead of rewriting them through residues,
substitutes away equalities in the eliminated variable, and uses strict bounds
with \cref{pma:pr:thm:boundary}. Its statement is uniform over all groups $G$
of \cref{pma:pr:sec:qe}.

Use the group language
\[
 L_{\mathrm{Pr}}=\{0,1,+,-,<,(\equiv_m)_{m\geq2}\},
\]
where $u\equiv_m v$ means $u-v\in mG$. Affine terms have integer
coefficients. The congruence predicates are definable by existential
quantification in the underlying additive language.

\begin{theorem}[Quantifier elimination, with a uniform proof \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:s04:thm:qe}
Every $L_{\mathrm{Pr}}$ formula is equivalent, uniformly in all groups $G$
satisfying the hypotheses above, to a quantifier-free Boolean combination
of affine inequalities, affine equalities, and congruences.
Consequently every such $G$ is elementarily equivalent to
$(\Z;0,1,+,-,<)$ in the reduct language.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to eliminate one existential quantifier from a conjunction of
literals, after writing the quantifier-free part in disjunctive normal form.
Negated equality is split into two strict inequalities, and negated
inequalities are converted by discreteness. Negated congruences can be
kept as negated congruences.

Let $x$ be the variable to eliminate. If it occurs with no nonzero
coefficient, the quantifier is vacuous. Otherwise let $L$ be the least
common multiple of the positive absolute values of its nonzero
coefficients. Introduce $y=Lx$ and impose $y\in LG$. There is a unique
$x$ for every such $y$ because $G$ is torsion-free.

For an atom with term $ax+t$, $a\ne0$, put $B=L/|a|$. Multiplying an
inequality or equality by the positive standard integer $B$ replaces its
$x$-coefficient by $\operatorname{sgn}(a)$ on $y$. For divisibility use
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:scalingcongruence}
 ax+t\in nG
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{sgn}(a)y+Bt\in nBG.
\end{equation}
Indeed the forward implication multiplies a witness by $B$, and the
reverse implication cancels $B$ in the torsion-free group. Atoms with
zero $x$-coefficient remain independent conditions.

The resulting conjunction has only coefficient $1$ or $-1$ on $y$ in its
inequalities, equalities, and congruences. An equality involving $y$ permits
substitution. In the absence of such an equality, the inequalities form
finite strict lower and upper lists. The remaining congruences and
negated congruences form a predicate $P(y)$ invariant modulo $mG$, where
$m$ is the least common multiple of their moduli, including $L$. For example,
if $n\mid m$, replacing $y$ by $y+mg$ cannot change membership of
$\pm y+t$ in $nG$.

Apply \cref{pma:pr:thm:boundary}. The maximum or minimum appearing there is not an
extra function symbol: take a finite disjunction over choices of a largest
lower bound, or a smallest upper bound, and add the finitely many
inequalities that express that choice. Substitution of the finite
candidates leaves a quantifier-free formula in the remaining variables.
Negation handles universal quantifiers, so induction on the external
formula tree proves elimination.

Finally a closed quantifier-free formula has the same truth value in every
such $G$ as in $\Z$. Its terms are standard numerals. Their equality and
order are fixed, and their congruences have the usual residues by division.
Eliminating all quantifiers therefore proves elementary equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The entire induction scheme]
\label[corollary]{pma:pr:cor:fullpresburger}\label[corollary]{pma:pr:cor:model}
For every divisible ordered abelian group $D$,
\[
 (\MD;0,\one,+,<)\models\Th(\N;0,1,+,<).
\]
In particular it satisfies induction for every first-order formula of
this additive language, with arbitrary parameters from $\MD$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{pma:pr:prop:zgroup,pma:pr:thm:qe}, $\GD\equiv\Z$. The nonnegative cone is
uniformly definable by $x\geq0$. Relativizing every quantifier to that
cone translates any sentence about $\MD$ into a sentence about $\GD$.
The same translation in $\Z$ gives the ordinary nonnegative integers.
Each universally closed induction instance is therefore true in $\MD$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Definable least elements]
\label[proposition]{pma:pr:prop:least}
Every nonempty definable subset of $\MD$, allowing parameters, has a
least element.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Eliminate quantifiers and take a finite disjunction of conjunctions as
in \cref{pma:pr:thm:qe}. Include $x\geq0$ explicitly. For each conjunction,
the substitution $y=Ax$ introduces a lower bound $y\geq0$. Hence a
nonempty conjunction has a least solution among the finitely many valid
candidates at its maximum lower bound; the residue argument shows that
one of these candidates lies below every witness. Dividing by $A$ is
order-preserving. Finally take the minimum of the least solutions of the
nonempty disjuncts.
\end{proof}

This also gives a direct induction check after elimination: a nonempty
set of counterexamples to an induction instance would have a least
member. It cannot be $0$, and its predecessor would contradict the
induction step. The external standard part
$\{(0,n):n\in\N\}$ is a proper inductive subset when $D\neq0$, but is
not definable, even with parameters. Induction quantifies over definable
sets, not over every external subset.

Source~04 proves \cref{pma:pr:cor:fullpresburger} (its Corollary~3.5, for
$\mathrm{Pr}_{\N}$) by the same relativization, and adds \src{04}:

The same argument shows that every definable subset of $M_D^k$ is a
Boolean combination of restrictions of group affine inequalities,
equalities, and standard congruences. This observation will control its
Borel complexity without an induction over projections of Borel sets.

\section{Polishness and a locally compact example}
\label{pma:pr:sec:topology}

A Polish space is a separable space whose topology is induced by some
complete metric. A particular convenient metric may be incomplete
without the space ceasing to be Polish. This distinction matters for
open rays.

\subsection{A useful exact criterion}

We first record the standard subspace criterion with a proof, so that the
completeness issue is visible.

\begin{lemma}[Polish subspaces]
\label[lemma]{pma:pr:lem:gdelta}
A subspace $A$ of a Polish space $X$ is Polish if and only if $A$ is a
$G_\delta$ subset of $X$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compatible complete metric $d$ on $X$. If $A=\bigcap_j U_j$ with
$U_j$ open, consider on $A$ the continuous functions
$f_j(x)=1/d(x,X\setminus U_j)$; when $U_j=X$, set $f_j=0$.
The graph of $x\mapsto(f_j(x))_j$ is closed in $X\times\R^{\N}$.
Indeed a limit outside some $U_j$ would force $f_j$ to tend to infinity,
not to a real number. Projection identifies this closed graph
homeomorphically with $A$. Countable products and closed subspaces of
complete separable metric spaces are complete and separable.

Conversely, let $\delta$ be a complete metric inducing the subspace
topology on $A$. For each $n$, let $W_n$ be the union of all ambient open
sets $U$ that meet $A$ and satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_d(U)<2^{-n},\qquad
 \operatorname{diam}_{\delta}(U\cap A)<2^{-n}.
\]
These unions cover $A$. If $x\in\bigcap_n W_n$, choose $x\in U_n$ and
$a_n\in U_n\cap A$. Then $a_n\to x$ in $d$. For each fixed $n$, all
sufficiently late $a_k$ belong to $U_n$, so $(a_k)$ is $\delta$-Cauchy.
Its $\delta$-limit lies in $A$, and agreement of the two topologies
identifies that limit with $x$. Thus $A=\bigcap_n W_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The positive-cone criterion]
\label{pma:pr:thm:criterion}
Let $D$ be a divisible ordered abelian group equipped with a Polish
topological-group topology, not necessarily its order topology. Give
$\Z$ the discrete topology. Then the subspace $\MD\subseteq D\times\Z$
is Polish if and only if $D_{>0}$ is $G_\delta$ in $D$. When this holds,
$D_{>0}$ is in fact $\Dtwo$, and addition on $\MD$ is continuous.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $D_{>0}$ is $G_\delta$, so is its union with the closed singleton
$\{0\}$. On each clopen integer slice, the carrier \eqref{pma:pr:eq:cone} is
either $D_{>0}$ or $D_{\geq0}$. A countable topological sum of
$G_\delta$ subspaces is $G_\delta$ in the corresponding sum: its
complement is a countable union of $F_\sigma$ subsets of the clopen
slices. Apply \cref{pma:pr:lem:gdelta}.

Conversely, the slice with integer coordinate $-1$ is closed in $\MD$
and homeomorphic to $D_{>0}$. If $\MD$ is Polish, so is that slice, and
\cref{pma:pr:lem:gdelta} makes $D_{>0}$ a $G_\delta$ subset of $D$.
Negation is a homeomorphism on $D$, so $D_{<0}$ is $G_\delta$ as well.
The complement of $D_{>0}$ is $D_{<0}\cup\{0\}$, also $G_\delta$.
Thus $D_{>0}$ is both $F_\sigma$ and $G_\delta$.

Finally the cone is closed under coordinatewise addition. Restricting
continuous addition on $D\times\Z$ to a subspace with its image in that
subspace gives a continuous map $\MD^2\to\MD$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Admissible coefficient groups and continuous division \src{04}}\label{pma:pr:sec:transfer}

Source~04 proves a sufficient version of \cref{pma:pr:thm:criterion} under a
stronger hypothesis, and obtains in return the continuity of division by
standard integers for every such $D$; source~05 proves that continuity only
for its two explicit models (\cref{pma:pr:prop:selfsimilar}). The Polish
clause of \cref{pma:pr:thm:transfer} is the case of \cref{pma:pr:thm:criterion}
in which $D_{>0}$ is open.

\begin{definition}[\src{04}]
An \emph{admissible topological divisible group} is a divisible linearly
ordered abelian group $D$ with a Polish group topology such that $D_{>0}$ is
open and each map $d\mapsto d/m$, $m\geq1$, is continuous. The specified
topology is additional data; it is not silently identified with the order
topology of $G_D$ or $M_D$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:thm:transfer}
Let $D$ be an admissible topological divisible group, and give $G_D$ the
product topology with discrete $\Z$. Then $M_D$, with its subspace
topology, is Polish. Addition on $M_D$ is jointly continuous. The functions
\[
 Q_m(d,z)=\left(d/m,\lfloor z/m\rfloor\right),\qquad
 R_m(d,z)=(0,z\bmod m)
\]
are continuous maps from $M_D$ to itself for each fixed $m\geq1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ambient group is Polish. Its complement of $M_D$ is
\[
 (D_{<0}\times\Z)\cup(\{0\}\times\Z_{<0}).
\]
The first set is open and the second is closed. In a metrizable space every
open set is $F_\sigma$, so this complement is $F_\sigma$ and $M_D$ is
$G_\delta$. A $G_\delta$ subspace of a Polish space is Polish. Equivalently,
the integer-coordinate slices are a countable disjoint sum of copies of
the Polish spaces $D_{\geq0}$ and $D_{>0}$.

The set $M_D$ is closed under addition; restriction of the continuous
ambient addition is therefore continuous as a map into the subspace.
The displayed formulas for $Q_m,R_m$ are continuous in the ambient
coordinates: division on $D$ is continuous, and every function on the
discrete coordinate is continuous. Their images belong to $M_D$ by
\cref{pma:pr:lem:division}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The real-coordinate model, including a complete metric}

For $D=\R$, the positive cone is open. More explicitly, the slice
\[
 X_n=\{r:(r,n)\in\MR\}
\]
is $[0,\infty)$ when $n\geq0$ and $(0,\infty)$ when $n<0$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \MR\ \cong\
 \coprod_{n\geq0}[0,\infty)\ \sqcup\
 \coprod_{n<0}(0,\infty).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:rays}
\end{equation}
This proves separability, local compactness, and absence of isolated
points directly.

For a fully explicit compatible complete metric, define
\[
 d_n(r,s)=
 \begin{cases}
 \min\{1,|r-s|\},&n\geq0,\\
 \min\{1,|\log r-\log s|\},&n<0,
 \end{cases}
\]
and set
\begin{equation}
 d((r,n),(s,m))=
 \begin{cases}d_n(r,s),&n=m,\\2,&n\neq m.\end{cases}
 \label{pma:pr:eq:completemetric}
\end{equation}
Every Cauchy sequence is eventually contained in one slice. The closed
ray with the first metric is complete; the open ray with the logarithmic
metric is isometric, before truncation, to $\R$. Thus \eqref{pma:pr:eq:completemetric}
is complete. It induces exactly the topology in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:rays}, which
is the original product-subspace topology. Continuity of addition follows
from that topology, not from a claim of uniform continuity in this metric.

The map $r\mapsto(r,0)$ on $r>0$ establishes uncountability.
Together with \cref{pma:pr:cor:fullpresburger}, this completes the simplest proof
of the affirmative answer.

\begin{remark}[Why the arithmetic order topology is not used]
The least positive element is $\one$. In $\GD$, the order interval
$(x-\one,x+\one)$ is the singleton $\{x\}$. The positive cone has the
same discreteness at its minimum. Consequently its arithmetic order
topology is discrete, and an uncountable such topology cannot be Polish.
The topology of \eqref{pma:pr:eq:rays} is different, as \eqref{pma:pr:eq:completemetric}
and the convergence in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:smallseq} explicitly demonstrate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The real-coordinate model, source 04's account \src{04}}\label{pma:pr:sec:real}

Source~04 gives the same slice decomposition and the same complete metric as
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:completemetric}, except that distinct slices are at
distance~$1$; it adds two arithmetic illustrations and a remark.

Take $D=\R$ with the usual order and topology. It is admissible, so
\cref{pma:pr:cor:model,pma:pr:thm:transfer,pma:pr:thm:delta2} apply to \eqref{pma:pr:eq:realmodel}.

\paragraph{Topology, perfection, and a complete metric.}

For $z\geq0$, the slice of integer coordinate $z$ is a copy of
$[0,\infty)$; for $z<0$, it is a copy of $(0,\infty)$. Each slice is clopen
in $\MR$. Thus $\MR$ is uncountable, locally compact, and has no isolated
points. In particular every standard numeral $(0,n)$ is nonisolated.

It is important not to assert that the inherited Euclidean metric is
complete: $(0,\infty)$ with that metric is not. Here is a compatible
complete metric, with different integer slices distance $1$ apart:
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:realmetric}
 \rho((r,z),(s,w))=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&z\ne w,\\
 \min\{1,|r-s|\},&z=w\geq0,\\
 \min\{1,|\log r-\log s|\},&z=w<0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The last line only occurs for positive $r,s$. A Cauchy sequence is eventually
in one slice. On a nonnegative slice use completeness of $[0,\infty)$;
on a negative slice use the homeomorphism $r\mapsto\log r$ onto $\R$.
The truncated metrics are metrics, and the inter-slice distance preserves
the triangle inequality. Countable dense subsets in the slices give a
countable dense subset of the whole space.

\paragraph{Arithmetically huge elements converging to zero.}

Put $a_j=(1/j,0)$ for $j\geq1$. Then
\[
 a_j>n\one\quad\text{for every standard }n,
 \qquad a_j\longrightarrow0\quad\text{topologically}.
\]
There is no contradiction. The arithmetic order and the chosen topology
are distinct structures. The arithmetic order topology on $M_D$ would
be discrete, since every nonzero point has a predecessor and a successor,
and $0$ is isolated by its immediate successor. That discrete topology is
\emph{not} the topology used in the construction.

For a concrete division example, in $\MR$ one has
\[
 (2,-7)=3(2/3,-3)+(0,2).
\]
The quotient $(2/3,-3)$ is positive because its real coordinate is positive.
This illustrates why restricting the integer coordinate to nonnegative
values at all positive real coordinates would destroy the desired
arithmetic: the negative integer sheets are necessary.

\begin{remark}[Why a Cartesian product of two positive cones is not used \src{04}]
The universe is not $\R_{\geq0}\times\N$. The latter does not have the
required predecessor property at points $(r,0)$ with $r>0$. By contrast,
in $\MR$ every positive $(r,z)$ has predecessor $(r,z-1)$, except that the
ordinary zero remains the least element as required.
\end{remark}

\section{An explicit Baire-space realization}
\label{pma:pr:sec:baire}

Let
\[
 D_{\mathrm B}=\Q^{\N}
\]
with coordinatewise addition. Each factor $\Q$ is discrete, \emph{not}
given its Euclidean subspace topology. Two distinct sequences are compared
at the least index at which they differ. This lexicographic order makes
$D_{\mathrm B}$ a divisible ordered abelian group. Its product topology is
Polish and zero-dimensional; for example,
\[
 d_D(a,b)=2^{-\min\{j:a_j\neq b_j\}}
 \quad(a\neq b),\qquad d_D(a,a)=0
\]
is a compatible complete metric.

The sets
\[
 C_{k,q}=\{a:a_0=\cdots=a_{k-1}=0,\ a_k=q\},
 \qquad k\in\N,\ q\in\Q_{>0},
\]
are clopen cylinders, and
\begin{equation}
 (D_{\mathrm B})_{>0}=\coprod_{k,q>0}C_{k,q}.
 \label{pma:pr:eq:positivecylinders}
\end{equation}
No restriction is placed on later coordinates. Each cylinder is
homeomorphic to $\Q^{\N}$. The nonnegative cone
$(D_{\mathrm B})_{\geq0}$ is closed, since the negative cone is open.
\cref{pma:pr:thm:criterion} already proves that
\[
 \MB=\bigl(D_{\mathrm B}\lex\Z\bigr)_{\geq0}
\]
is Polish. We now identify its homeomorphism type without relying on a
space-classification theorem.

\subsection{The zero branch and its explicit tree code}

Fix computable bijections
\[
 e:\N\to\Q,\qquad e_+:\N\to\Q_{>0},\qquad
 e_{\geq0}:\N\to\Q_{\geq0},
\]
with computable inverses. A code $s\in\Baire$ determines a sequence
$a\in(D_{\mathrm B})_{\geq0}$ as follows. As long as all previously
chosen coefficients are zero, set $a_j=e_{\geq0}(s_j)$. Once the first
positive coefficient has appeared, use $a_j=e(s_j)$ at all later stages.
This is a bijection: the inverse uses the corresponding enumeration at
each step. A finite output prefix depends on exactly a finite input
prefix, in both directions. Thus it is a homeomorphism
\begin{equation}
 \Baire\ \cong\ (D_{\mathrm B})_{\geq0}.
 \label{pma:pr:eq:zerobranch}
\end{equation}
The all-zero rational sequence is not omitted; it is the branch that
continues choosing zero indefinitely.

\begin{theorem}[Baire-space carrier]
\label{pma:pr:thm:baire}
There is an explicit homeomorphism $h:\Baire\to\MB$.
In particular $\MB$ is uncountable, has no isolated points, and is
nowhere locally compact.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Partition $\MB$ into the following countably many clopen blocks. For each
$n\geq0$, take the entire slice
$(D_{\mathrm B})_{\geq0}\times\{n\}$. For $n<0$, partition that slice
further into $C_{k,q}\times\{n\}$ using
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:positivecylinders}. By \eqref{pma:pr:eq:zerobranch}, every nonnegative
slice is homeomorphic to Baire space; every negative-slice block is so
by coding its unrestricted rational tail. A countable disjoint sum of
copies of $\Baire$ is homeomorphic to $\Baire$: use the first natural
coordinate to choose a block and the remaining coordinates to choose a
point in it.

For completeness, no nonempty basic cylinder of $\Baire$ is relatively
compact. Its projection to the next unrestricted discrete coordinate
has infinite image, whereas the continuous image of a compact set in a
discrete space is finite. Every neighborhood contains such a cylinder.
The other two assertions follow from the elementary properties of the
product $\N^{\N}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A total code for every sequence of natural numbers}

Here is a fully specified block indexing, also used in the accompanying
program. Let $\langle a,b\rangle$ be Cantor's pairing function.
The first digit of a Baire code is a tag. An even tag $2n$ specifies the
nonnegative integer coordinate $n$ and the tree code
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:zerobranch}. An odd tag
\begin{equation}
 2\langle a,\langle k,b\rangle\rangle+1
 \label{pma:pr:eq:tag}
\end{equation}
specifies integer coordinate $-a-1$, first positive rational coordinate
$k$, and value $e_+(b)$ there. The remaining digits code the unrestricted
rational tail using $e$.

Every natural tag is of exactly one of these forms. Every infinite
natural sequence therefore codes a valid element; no hidden domain test
is required. Conversely, a point with negative integer coordinate has a
first positive rational coefficient, so its tag and tail are uniquely
recoverable. This describes $h$ and $h^{-1}$ explicitly.

\begin{theorem}[Computable continuous arithmetic]
\label{pma:pr:thm:effectiveops}
Under this presentation, the operations
\[
 x+y,\qquad x+\one,\qquad
 q_m(x)=\lfloor x/m\rfloor,\qquad r_m(x)=x\bmod m
\]
are Type-2 computable for every positive standard integer $m$. Here
$q_m(x)$ is the unique quotient in the model's Euclidean division by $m$,
not a real-valued floor operation. The remainders have their finite
discrete topology.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A Type-2 computation reads finitely many input digits to produce each
requested output digit. Decode the integer coordinates $n,z$ of the two
inputs. Each rational coefficient $a_j+b_j$ is computable from finite
prefixes. If $n+z\geq0$, output tag $2(n+z)$ and encode the coefficient
sequence by the nonnegative-cone tree. The tree decisions only inspect
finite prefixes.

If $n+z<0$, at least one input has a negative integer coordinate and hence
a strictly positive coefficient sequence. Its tag supplies an index $k$
of a positive leading coefficient. Since both coefficient sequences are
lex-nonnegative, their sum is strictly positive and its first nonzero
coordinate occurs at or before $k$. Search these finitely many
coordinates, output the negative tag \eqref{pma:pr:eq:tag}, and then encode the
tail. Thus the tag itself is computable; the algorithm does not attempt
to decide whether an arbitrary sequence is identically zero.

For division use
\[
 q_m(a,n)=(a/m,\lfloor n/m\rfloor),\qquad
 r_m(a,n)=n\bmod m.
\]
If the quotient's integer coordinate is negative, the input integer
coordinate was negative as well, and the same known leading index can
be used. Otherwise the nonnegative tree code applies. Constants and
successor are special cases of these finite-prefix procedures.
\end{proof}

The adjective ``computable'' here concerns operations on infinite
sequence codes. It does not assert that every element is computable,
or that equality and order of arbitrary streams can be decided in finite
time. The distinction will be quantified next.

\subsection{Second route \src{04}: explicit charts}\label{pma:pr:s04:sec:baire}

Source~04 builds the same space: its $M_\omega$ is $\MB$, with the same group
$\Q^{\N}$, the same discrete coordinates and the same lexicographic order. Its
charts differ from the tag coding of \cref{pma:pr:thm:baire}: they go from the
model to Baire space and code the nonnegative and the negative integer slices
separately. Source~04's homeomorphism $H$, its charts $H_{\ge}$, $H_{>}$ and
its integer coding $h$ are written $\Psi$, $\Psi_{\ge}$, $\Psi_{>}$ and
$\zeta$ here (\cref{pma:sec:notation}).

\subsubsection{The lexicographic rational product}

Let
\[
 D_{\mathrm B}=\Q^{\N}
\]
with coordinatewise addition, the product topology in which \emph{each copy
of $\Q$ is discrete}, and lexicographic order by the first unequal
coordinate. A nonzero sequence has a least nonzero index, so the order is
defined on all sequences, not merely on finite-support ones. It is a linear
order compatible with addition. Division by standard integers is
coordinatewise.

The metric
\[
 d(u,v)=
 \begin{cases}0,&u=v,\\2^{-k},&k=\min\{i:u_i\ne v_i\},\end{cases}
\]
is a complete compatible ultrametric. Finite-support rational sequences
form a countable dense set. The positive cone is
\[
 (D_{\mathrm B})_{>0}
 =\bigcup_{k\in\N}\ \bigcup_{q\in\Q_{>0}}
 \{d:d_0=\cdots=d_{k-1}=0,\ d_k=q\},
\]
an open union of clopen cylinders. Hence $D_{\mathrm B}$ is admissible.
Write
\[
 \MB=M_{D_{\mathrm B}}.
\]
It is already an uncountable Polish Presburger model with continuous
addition. We now identify its space explicitly with $\Baire$.

\subsubsection{Two elementary coding homeomorphisms}

Fix bijections
\[
 c:\Q\longrightarrow\N,\qquad
 b:\Q_{>0}\longrightarrow\N_{>0},\qquad
 a:\N\times\Q_{>0}\longrightarrow\N,
 \qquad \zeta:\Z\longrightarrow\N.
\]
Since their domains and codomains are discrete, these are homeomorphisms
between discrete spaces.

For $d\in(D_{\mathrm B})_{\geq0}$ define $\Psi_{\geq}(d)\in\Baire$ as follows.
Send the zero sequence to the zero sequence. If $k$ is the first nonzero
index of $d$, then $d_k>0$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:nonnegativecode}
 \Psi_{\geq}(d)_i=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&i<k,\\
 b(d_k),&i=k,\\
 c(d_i),&i>k.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Every nonzero element of $\Baire$ has a first positive entry, so this coding
has an evident inverse. Both it and its inverse preserve information in
finite initial segments: to determine the first $n$ coordinates, only the
first $n$ coordinates are needed. It is therefore a homeomorphism, including
at the zero sequence.

For $d\in(D_{\mathrm B})_{>0}$, let $k$ again be its first nonzero index and put
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:positivecode}
 \Psi_{>}(d)_0=a(k,d_k),\qquad
 \Psi_{>}(d)_{j+1}=c(d_{k+1+j})\quad(j\geq0).
\end{equation}
The pair $(k,d_k)$ identifies a clopen positive cylinder; within that
cylinder the tail is an arbitrary rational sequence. This is another
homeomorphism onto $\Baire$.

\begin{theorem}[A concrete Baire homeomorphism \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:s04:thm:baire}
The map
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:bairehomeo}
 \Psi:\MB\longrightarrow\Baire,\qquad
 \Psi(d,z)=
 \begin{cases}
 \zeta(z)\,{}^\frown \Psi_{\geq}(d),&z\geq0,\\
 \zeta(z)\,{}^\frown \Psi_{>}(d),&z<0,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
is a homeomorphism. Here $\zeta(z)\,{}^\frown u$ means prepend the one entry
$\zeta(z)$ to the sequence $u$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The integer slices of $\MB$ are clopen. Their images are exactly the clopen
cylinders of $\Baire$ specified by the first coordinate. On each slice the map
is one of the two homeomorphisms just proved. These slice maps assemble to
a bijection whose map and inverse are continuous.
\end{proof}

Transporting addition by $\Psi$ gives a binary operation on the \emph{literal}
set $\Baire$:
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:transportaddition}
 u\oplus v=\Psi\bigl(\Psi^{-1}(u)+\Psi^{-1}(v)\bigr).
\end{equation}
This proves the Baire-space assertion of \cref{pma:pr:thm:answer}; it does not
merely produce a Borel-isomorphic universe. The space is perfect and
nowhere locally compact. For the latter assertion, every nonempty basic
cylinder in $\Baire$ has infinitely many disjoint next-coordinate cylinders
covering it, with no finite subcover. If a compact neighborhood existed,
a closed basic cylinder inside it would be compact, a contradiction.

\subsubsection{Effective operations on infinite streams}
\label{pma:pr:subsec:effective}

A map on Baire space is \emph{Type-2 computable} here if an algorithm, given
oracle access to the input sequences, returns every requested output
coordinate after reading finitely many input coordinates. The number of
queries may depend on the input. This is a statement about computations
with infinite input oracles, not an enumeration of an uncountable model by
ordinary natural-number indices.

Choose the bijections above effectively. For example, index positive
rationals by the binary tree starting at $1/1$, with children
\[
 \frac pq\longmapsto\frac{p}{p+q},\quad\frac{p+q}{q}.
\]
Every positive rational in lowest terms has a unique finite path: repeatedly
subtract the smaller numerator or denominator from the larger. The sum
strictly decreases until $(1,1)$ is reached. Recording whether the larger
entry was the numerator or denominator and reversing the record recovers
the path. This proves both bijectivity and effective invertibility.
Enumerate $\Q$ by interleaving positive and negative rationals and adding
zero. Combine this enumeration with an effective pairing of two natural
numbers to obtain $a$ and with a signed-integer coding to obtain $\zeta$.

\begin{theorem}[Effective strengthening \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:thm:effective}
The homeomorphism $\Psi$ and its inverse can be chosen Type-2 computable.
With this choice, the addition $\oplus$ in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:transportaddition},
and quotient and remainder by each fixed positive integer, are Type-2
computable on $\Baire$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $\Psi_{\geq}$, a requested coordinate $i$ requires only the first $i+1$
coefficients: either all are zero, the first nonzero is at $i$, or it occurred
earlier. These are precisely the three cases in
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:nonnegativecode}. The same finite-prefix procedure computes its
inverse. No decision whether an entire infinite sequence is zero is made.

For $\Psi_{>}$, search for the first nonzero coefficient. The search terminates
because this chart has a positive, not merely nonnegative, domain. Once
found, all output coordinates are computable; its inverse reads the encoded
pair in the first coordinate and then the tail. Reading the integer
coordinate chooses the appropriate chart of $\Psi$ and $\Psi^{-1}$.

Coordinatewise rational addition and division are computable, as are
integer addition, quotient, and remainder. Compose these algorithms with
$\Psi$ and $\Psi^{-1}$. Whenever the output has negative integer coordinate its
$D_{\mathrm B}$-coordinate is strictly positive, so the positive-chart search
is guaranteed to terminate. For addition, for instance, a negative sum of
integer coordinates requires at least one negative input integer coordinate;
that input has strictly positive $D_{\mathrm B}$-coordinate, and the other has
nonnegative $D_{\mathrm B}$-coordinate. Their sum is strictly positive.
\end{proof}

The companion \code{verification.py} implements these maps as memoized
indexed streams. A caller supplies a function returning the $i$th natural
number of a sequence. The module exports \code{baire\_add},
\code{baire\_quotient}, and \code{baire\_remainder}. Its round-trip checks
include long initial zero blocks, which exercise the boundary chart at the
zero coefficient sequence.

\section{Definability, effective truth, and the optimal order rank}
\label{pma:pr:sec:borel}

A subset of a metrizable space is $\Dtwo$ when it is both $F_\sigma$ and
$G_\delta$. Finite Boolean combinations of such sets are again $\Dtwo$.
Closed sets, open sets, and clopen sets belong to this class.

\begin{theorem}[All definable relations have low Borel rank]
\label{pma:pr:thm:definableborel}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{pma:pr:thm:criterion}, every first-order definable
subset of $\MD^k$, with parameters, is $\Dtwo$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In $\GD=D\times\Z$, the strict positive cone is
\[
 (D_{>0}\times\Z)\cup(\{0\}\times\Z_{>0}).
\]
It is $\Dtwo$, as is its complement. Affine group terms are continuous
maps to $\GD$, so their inequalities have $\Dtwo$ truth sets. Equalities
have closed truth sets because the ambient group is Hausdorff.

Divisibility is especially simple. Since $D$ is divisible,
\begin{equation}
 m\GD=D\times m\Z.
 \label{pma:pr:eq:modslice}
\end{equation}
This is clopen. Thus congruence atoms have clopen truth sets, including
when their affine terms contain parameters. Translate a cone formula
into the group and apply \cref{pma:pr:thm:qe}. The result is a finite Boolean
combination of these atoms. Finally restrict to $\MD^k$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Second route \src{04}.} Source~04 proves \cref{pma:pr:thm:definableborel}
for its admissible groups (\cref{pma:pr:sec:transfer}), a special case of the
hypotheses of \cref{pma:pr:thm:criterion}, by an explicit description of the
order relation:

\begin{theorem}[Low complexity of all definable relations \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:thm:delta2}
For every admissible $D$, every first-order definable relation with
parameters on $M_D$, in every finite arity, is simultaneously $F_\sigma$
and $G_\delta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
It suffices by \cref{pma:pr:s04:thm:qe} to consider affine atoms. Affine group maps
are continuous, so an affine equality defines a closed set. A congruence
condition depends only on finitely many discrete integer coordinates and
is clopen, by \cref{pma:pr:cor:congruences}.

For the order, write two group elements as $(d,z),(e,w)$. The strict
comparison is
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:ordertopology}
 (d,z)<(e,w)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 e-d>0\quad\text{or}\quad (e=d\text{ and }z<w).
\end{equation}
The first condition is open; the second is closed because equality of
$D$-coordinates is closed and the integer comparison is clopen. Thus the
order relation is $F_\sigma$. Its complement has the identical form with
the opposite strict comparison and weak integer comparison, hence is
also $F_\sigma$. Pulling back by affine maps preserves both classes.
Finite Boolean combinations of $\boldsymbol\Delta^0_2$ sets remain
$\boldsymbol\Delta^0_2$, which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

This is stronger than merely obtaining a Borel presentation. The proof
also explains why quantification does not raise the complexity in this
particular presentation: its apparent projections are replaced by finite
residue searches and finite Boolean operations.

\subsection{A formula-by-formula bound on changes of mind}

\begin{theorem}[Effective finite-mind-change truth]
\label{pma:pr:thm:mindchange}
For each first-order Presburger formula $\varphi(\bar x;\bar p)$, one can
compute a natural number $K_{\varphi}$ and an algorithm producing
\[
 A_s(\bar x;\bar p)\in\{0,1\}\qquad(s\in\N)
\]
from finite prefixes of the Baire codes, such that
\[
 \lim_s A_s(\bar x;\bar p)
  =\mathbf 1_{\MB\models\varphi(\bar x;\bar p)}.
\]
For every input tuple the displayed sequence changes value at most
$K_{\varphi}$ times. Parameter codes are supplied as oracles; they are
not assumed computable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compute a quantifier-free group formula equivalent to the relativized
cone formula, by \cref{pma:pr:thm:qe}. An affine group term has the form
$(c,n)$, where $n$ is an exactly computable integer and each rational
coefficient $c_j$ is computable from finite prefixes of the inputs.

To approximate the sign of $(c,n)$, first use the sign of $n$. At stage
$s$, inspect $c_0,\ldots,c_{s-1}$. If a nonzero coefficient has been
found, use the sign of the first such coefficient instead. If $c=0$,
the initial answer was correct. Otherwise the answer becomes correct
when the first nonzero coefficient is reached and never changes again.
An equality atom can be handled in the same way: it starts with the
answer determined by $n$ and becomes permanently false if a nonzero
coefficient is found. A congruence atom is decided immediately from $n$
by \eqref{pma:pr:eq:modslice}.

Evaluate the fixed Boolean combination of these approximations at each
stage. Its value can change only when an equality or inequality atom
changes. The number of those atom occurrences is a computable bound
$K_{\varphi}$. Each stage reads only finitely many digits. The truth set
is effectively $F_\sigma$ because it is
\[
 \bigcup_N\bigcap_{s\geq N}\{\bar x:A_s(\bar x;\bar p)=1\},
\]
where the stage sets are effectively clopen relative to the parameter
codes. The same expression with $0$ proves effective $G_\delta$ status.
\end{proof}

No effective stopping time is asserted: a later rational coordinate may
be the first nonzero one. Deciding sentences and deciding arbitrary
parameter instances are different problems. The former is ordinary
Presburger decidability; the latter is represented here by a convergent,
uniformly bounded-change process.

\subsection{Why rank one is impossible for the order}

\begin{theorem}[Open or closed arithmetic order forces discreteness]
\label{pma:pr:thm:orderoptimal}
Let $X$ carry a model of Presburger arithmetic and a topology in which
successor $x\mapsto x+\one$ is continuous. If either of the relations
$<$ or $\leq$ is open or closed in $X^2$, then $X$ is discrete.
Consequently, in any uncountable Polish model with continuous addition,
the arithmetic order is neither open nor closed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\leq$ is open, then for fixed $a$ both sets
$\{x:x\leq a\}$ and $\{x:a\leq x\}$ are open. Their intersection is
$\{a\}$, so every point is isolated.

If $<$ is closed, taking the complement and reversing coordinates makes
$\leq$ open. If $<$ is open, the discrete-order equivalence
\[
 x\leq y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x<y+\one
\]
and continuity of successor make $\leq$ open. Finally, if $\leq$ is
closed, then
\[
 x<y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x+\one\leq y
\]
makes $<$ closed, reducing to the previous case.
An uncountable discrete space is not separable, hence not Polish.
\end{proof}

Together, \cref{pma:pr:thm:definableborel,pma:pr:thm:orderoptimal} make the $\Dtwo$
order bound optimal. This is a statement about the order relation, not
about every individual definable set: many congruence sets are clopen.
It also rules out a continuous Boolean-valued order test on the Baire
model, despite its computable addition.

\subsection{A general obstruction from closed order \src{04}}\label{pma:pr:sec:regularity}

In a Presburger model $M$, define truncated subtraction by
\[
 x\monus y=
 \begin{cases}z,&y+z=x\text{ for some }z\in M,\\0,&x<y.\end{cases}
\]
The witness in the first case is unique. Equivalently this is
$\max\{x-y,0\}$ in the ordered group completion.

\begin{theorem}[Closed order forces discreteness \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:thm:closedorder}
Let a model of $\mathrm{Pr}_{\N}$ carry a Hausdorff topology. If its weak
order relation is closed in $M^2$, then every point of $M$ is isolated.
If the topology is also separable, then $M$ is countable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By totality, the complement of the converse weak order is strict order.
Thus $<$ is open in $M^2$. Fixing either argument gives open order rays.
For $x>0$, let $x-1$ be its predecessor. Discreteness of the arithmetic
order gives
\[
 \{x\}=\{y:x-1<y<x+1\}.
\]
At zero one has $\{0\}=\{y:y<1\}$. Each singleton is open, so the topology
is discrete. A dense subset of a discrete space must contain every point;
separability therefore implies countability.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No continuous monus on an uncountable separable model \src{04}]
\label{pma:pr:cor:monusobstruction}
If truncated subtraction on a Hausdorff Presburger model is jointly
continuous, the topology is discrete. In particular no uncountable
separable Hausdorff Presburger model has continuous truncated subtraction.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The relation $x\leq y$ is exactly $x\monus y=0$. A singleton is closed
in a Hausdorff space, so continuity would make weak order closed. Apply
\cref{pma:pr:thm:closedorder}.
\end{proof}

No continuity hypothesis on addition was needed for this obstruction.
Consequently, extending the signature of our examples by their definable
monus operation destroys the assertion that all named operations are
continuous. Definitional equivalence of first-order theories does not by
itself preserve this topological property.

\begin{remark}[Two incomparable order obstructions \mergetag]\label{pma:pr:rem:twoorder}
\Cref{pma:pr:thm:orderoptimal} (source~05) and \cref{pma:pr:thm:closedorder}
(source~04) both show that the order of a nondiscrete model cannot be too
simple, under incomparable hypotheses. 05 assumes that successor is
continuous, assumes no separation axiom, and treats four cases ($<$ or $\le$,
open or closed). 04 assumes only that the weak order $\le$ is closed and the
topology Hausdorff, with no continuity at all. Neither implies the other: 04
covers a closed $\le$ in a topology where successor is discontinuous, 05
covers open orders and topologies that are not Hausdorff. Both are therefore
printed. For the metrizable models of this part, where successor is
continuous, both give the same conclusion, and so does
\cref{pma:pr:cor:monusobstruction} for truncated subtraction, whose exact
discontinuity locus is \cref{pma:pr:thm:truncated}.
\end{remark}

\section{Which definable operations are continuous?}
\label{pma:pr:sec:continuity}

The coordinate topology makes several arithmetic operations exceptionally
regular, but not every definable operation. This section identifies exact
boundaries in the two explicit models.

\begin{proposition}[Euclidean self-similarity]
\label[proposition]{pma:pr:prop:selfsimilar}
In $\MR$ and $\MB$, for each positive standard integer $m$ the map
\begin{equation}
 x\longmapsto\bigl(q_m(x),r_m(x)\bigr)
 \label{pma:pr:eq:selfsimilar}
\end{equation}
is a homeomorphism from the model onto
$M\times\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$, where the finite factor is discrete.
Its inverse is $(q,r)\mapsto mq+r\one$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Uniqueness and existence are \cref{pma:pr:prop:zgroup}. In both chosen
coefficient topologies, division of $d$ by $m$ is continuous. The integer
maps $n\mapsto\lfloor n/m\rfloor$ and $n\mapsto n\bmod m$ are
continuous on a discrete space. Hence \eqref{pma:pr:eq:selfsimilar} is
continuous into the product. Repeated addition and addition of a fixed
standard numeral make its inverse continuous.
\end{proof}

The same formula also explains why all standard congruences ignore the
entire coefficient sequence. Two elements have the same remainder modulo
every positive standard integer if and only if their integer coordinates
agree. The forward direction uses the elementary fact that an ordinary
integer divisible by every positive integer is zero. Their infinite
coordinates can still be arbitrary and different.

\begin{proposition}[The total predecessor has exactly one discontinuity]
\label[proposition]{pma:pr:prop:pred}\label[proposition]{pma:pr:prop:predecessor}
Define
\[
 p(0)=0,\qquad p(x)=x-\one\quad(x>0).
\]
In either explicit model, $p$ is continuous exactly at the points other
than $0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Away from $0$, this is a restriction of translation by $-\one$ in the
ambient topological group. Since $\{0\}$ is closed, the nonzero domain
is open, and translation has its image in the cone.

In the real model let $t_j=1/j$; in the Baire model let $t_j=e_j$, the
rational sequence with $1$ in coordinate $j$ and zeros elsewhere. In
either case $t_j>0$ and $t_j\to0$ in $D$. Then
\[
 (t_j,0)\to0,\qquad p(t_j,0)=(t_j,-1).
\]
The latter sequence does not converge to $p(0)=0$, because its integer
coordinate is always $-1$ and integer coordinates are discrete.
\end{proof}

Source~04 proves \cref{pma:pr:prop:pred} (its Proposition~7.4), treating the
numerals $(0,n)$, $n\ge1$, separately:

\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 04)]
At points with positive $D$-coordinate, $p(d,z)=(d,z-1)$ locally. At an
ordinary numeral $(0,n)$ with $n\geq1$, the same formula holds on its
integer-coordinate slice and maps into $M_D$, so it is continuous there.
At zero, the sequence $(s_j,0)\to0$ used above has
$p(s_j,0)=(s_j,-1)$, which cannot converge to zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact discontinuity locus of truncated subtraction]
\label{pma:pr:thm:truncated}\label{pma:pr:thm:monuslocus}
For $M=\MR$ or $\MB$, let
\[
 T(x,y)=\max\{x-y,0\}
\]
using the arithmetic order in the ambient group. Its discontinuity locus
is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \bigl\{((d,n),(d,z))\in M^2:n\neq z\bigr\}.
 \label{pma:pr:eq:disclocus}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both positive and negative cones in the coefficient group $D$ are open
for our two examples. If the coefficient difference $d-e$ is nonzero,
its sign is locally fixed. Thus $T$ locally agrees either with the
continuous ambient difference or with the constant zero map.

Suppose instead that $x=y$. Along inputs sufficiently near this pair,
the integer difference is zero. The output is either their ambient
difference or zero. For any neighborhood $V$ of zero in the ambient
group, both possible outputs lie in $V$ when the inputs are sufficiently
near $(x,x)$. This proves continuity there.

It remains to consider equal coefficient coordinates and $k=n-z\neq0$.
Use the positive null sequence $t_j$ from \cref{pma:pr:prop:pred}.
If $k>0$, replace $y$ by $y+(t_j,0)$. These inputs converge to $(x,y)$,
but their truncated differences are zero, whereas $T(x,y)=(0,k)$.
If $k<0$, replace $x$ by $x+(t_j,0)$. The outputs are $(t_j,k)$, which
cannot converge to the original value $0$ because their integer
coordinate is the nonzero integer $k$. All perturbed inputs remain in
the positive cone. This proves exactly \eqref{pma:pr:eq:disclocus}.
\end{proof}

Source~04 states \cref{pma:pr:thm:truncated} as its Theorem~7.3, for the
same two models and with the same locus, written
$\Disc(\monus)=\{((d,z),(e,w)):d=e\text{ and }z\ne w\}$, and adds \src{04}:
\emph{this locus is a closed nowhere dense subset of $M^2$, and truncated
subtraction is continuous on its dense open complement}. Its proof covers the
addition.

\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 04), with the nowhere-density clause]
Suppose first that $d\ne e$. The sign of $d-e$ is locally constant because
both strict cones of $D$ are open. On a neighborhood the monus operation
is therefore either the continuous ambient difference or the constant
zero map. Its values lie in the positive cone, so it is continuous as a
map to that subspace.

Next let $d=e$ and $z=w$. In a sufficiently small neighborhood the two
integer coordinates stay equal to these fixed integers. Monus has integer
coordinate zero and $D$-coordinate $\max\{u-v,0\}$. This positive-part
map is continuous at zero in both groups under consideration. Over $\R$
this follows from the usual absolute-value estimate. Over $D_{\mathrm B}$,
if $u-v$ has its first $n$ coordinates zero, so does its positive part:
it is either $u-v$ or zero. These finite-prefix neighborhoods prove
continuity at the point.

For the converse, choose $s_j>0$ in $D$ with $s_j\to0$: use $1/j$ in
$\R$ or the $j$th unit vector in $D_{\mathrm B}$. Let $x=(d,z)$, $y=(d,w)$
and $z\ne w$. If $z<w$, replace $x$ by
$x_j=(d+s_j,z)\in M_D$. Then $x_j\to x$ but
\[
 x_j\monus y=(s_j,z-w),\qquad x\monus y=0.
\]
The output integer coordinate is constantly negative and cannot converge
to the zero integer coordinate. If $z>w$, instead replace $y$ by
$y_j=(d+s_j,w)$. Then $x\monus y_j=0$ for every $j$, whereas
$x\monus y=(0,z-w)\ne0$. This proves discontinuity in both cases,
including at the boundary $d=0$.

The set in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:disclocus} is closed: equality of the $D$-coordinates
is closed and inequality of the discrete integer coordinates is clopen.
It has empty interior. At any of its points, perturbing the first
$D$-coordinate upward by a sufficiently small positive element stays in
$M_D^2$, lies in any prescribed neighborhood, and makes the two
$D$-coordinates unequal. Thus the closed set is nowhere dense.
\end{proof}

The standard numerals form a closed, relatively discrete subset of both
models, yet zero is not isolated in the whole space. This causes no
conflict with \cref{pma:pr:prop:pred}. Nor does continuity of standard division
imply continuity of every definable piecewise operation: the branch
boundary is decisive.

\noindent Source~04 continues \src{04}:

The failure is not an indiscriminate loss of regularity: the graph of
monus is still $\boldsymbol\Delta^0_2$ by \cref{pma:pr:thm:delta2}, because monus
is definable. Its ordinary predecessor specialization has a smaller
exceptional set.

\subsection{Polish completeness need not be translation-invariant \src{04}}\label{pma:pr:sec:invmetric}

\begin{proposition}[A complete-metric obstruction \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:prop:invariantmetric}
If zero is nonisolated in a metrizable Presburger model, there is no
compatible complete metric invariant under all additive translations.
More generally, for any compatible complete metric, the successor map
cannot be a uniform homeomorphism onto its image.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Successor is a bijection from the model onto $M\setminus\{0\}$.
If it were a uniform homeomorphism onto this subspace, every Cauchy sequence
in the image would pull back to a Cauchy sequence in the complete domain
and would therefore converge in the image. Thus the image would be a
complete subspace of a metric space, hence closed. Since zero is
nonisolated, $M\setminus\{0\}$ is not closed, a contradiction. A
translation-invariant metric would make successor an isometry, which is
a special case.
\end{proof}

This applies to both main examples. Their Polishness and continuous
addition therefore cannot be replaced by a claim of completeness in the
uniform structure of an invariant additive metric.

\section{Explicit set-sized fragments of the omnific integers}
\label{pma:pr:sec:omnific}

We use Conway normal-form notation
\[
 x=\sum_{\gamma\in S}r_\gamma\omega^\gamma,
\]
where $S$ is reverse well-ordered and all coefficients are real. In the
omnific ring the occurring exponents are nonnegative, and the coefficient
at exponent zero is an ordinary integer. This is the interface recorded
in the repository's surreal development \cite{RepoSurreal}. Addition is
coefficientwise, and a nonzero normal form has the sign of its leading
coefficient.

\begin{theorem}[One-scale and countable-scale realizations]
\label{pma:pr:thm:omnific}\label{pma:pr:prop:surreal}
There are injective ordered additive maps, fixing $0$ and $1$,
\begin{align}
 \iota_{\R}:\R\lex\Z&\longrightarrow\Oz,
 &\iota_{\R}(r,n)&=r\omega+n,
 \label{pma:pr:eq:realembedding}\\
 \iota_{\mathrm B}:\Q^{\N}\lex\Z&\longrightarrow\Oz,
 &\iota_{\mathrm B}(a,n)&=
       \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_j\omega^{1/(j+1)}+n.
 \label{pma:pr:eq:baireembedding}
\end{align}
Their positive images, equipped with the transported coefficient
topologies, are respectively the Polish models $\MR$ and $\MB$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first formula is a finite normal form. For the second, the fixed
exponents
\[
 1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\ldots
\]
strictly decrease and are positive. Every nonempty subset of these
exponents has a largest element, namely the one with least index.
Thus every possible support in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:baireembedding} is reverse
well-ordered, and adjoining exponent zero preserves that property.
The expression is a legitimate normal form, even when infinitely many
coefficients are nonzero. All positive-exponent coefficients are real,
and the constant term is the integer $n$, so the value is omnific.

Uniqueness of normal forms gives injectivity. Addition of two such
expressions is coordinatewise on a common fixed support, giving
additivity. The leading nonzero coefficient is the first nonzero $a_j$;
if all are zero, the sign is that of $n$. Therefore the map preserves
and reflects the lexicographic order. The same reasoning proves the
one-scale case. Transporting a topology through a bijection onto its
image preserves all the asserted topological properties.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What this realization does and does not preserve}

These are ordered additive embeddings, \emph{not} ring embeddings. Both
images contain $\omega$, but neither contains $\omega^2$. Their
multiplicative nonclosure is therefore immediate from the normal forms.
The stronger theorem in \cref{pma:pr:sec:multiplication} will show that replacing
surreal multiplication by some other multiplication cannot repair the
problem while retaining the same carrier, addition, and unit.

The topologies in \cref{pma:pr:thm:omnific} are precisely specified coefficient
topologies. The real coordinate is Euclidean in
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:realembedding}; the rational coordinates are discrete and
combined by a product topology in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:baireembedding}. Neither is
the discrete arithmetic order topology. No assertion that they are a
canonical topology on all omnific integers is intended.

The ordinary constant-term map becomes simply
\[
 \ct\bigl(\iota(d,n)\bigr)=n.
\]
It is continuous into the discrete integers for these models. Standard
congruence data see only this coordinate, matching
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:modslice}. The standard part itself remains externally
specified and nondefinable in the Presburger structure.

\subsection{Why no proper-class difficulty arises}

The source sets $\R\times\Z$ and $\Q^{\N}\times\Z$ are ordinary sets.
The normal-form constructions above are definable assignments on those
sets. Replacement therefore gives set-sized images. The set of birthdays
of their elements has an ordinal supremum; choosing a larger ordinal
places both images inside a set-sized birthday cutoff of $\No$.
No explicit optimal cutoff is needed for any theorem here.

Accordingly, one can conduct the entire argument in the product-group
models and use \cref{pma:pr:thm:omnific} only as a final realization theorem.
This is also the most economical formalization strategy. It avoids
making a class-sized object the carrier of a Polish space or a
first-order structure in ordinary set-based semantics.

\paragraph{Source 04's account \src{04}.} Source~04 proves
\cref{pma:pr:thm:omnific} as its Proposition~10.1 with the same proof; its
second map is written $\iota_\omega$ there and $\iota_{\mathrm B}$ here. It
adds the following remarks on the normal-form rule and on the transported
topologies.

The construction is purely set-sized, but it has a natural expression in
the surreal normal-form language. We use only the classical normal-form
rule: a reverse-well-ordered set of surreal exponents with real
coefficients defines a surreal sum; addition is coefficientwise, and order
is decided by the greatest exponent with nonzero coefficient. Omnific
integers have positive growth exponents and an ordinary integer constant
term. This is the normal-form description relevant to the ordered-group
study in \cite{EhrlichKaplan} and to the repository's omnific development
\cite{proveit-surreal}.

We transfer the topologies from the coefficient models to these images.
This is not a claim that the whole proper class $\Oz$ has a Polish topology,
nor that a canonical global surreal topology is being restricted. For
example, $\omega/j$ converges to zero in the transported real-coefficient
topology on the first image, despite each term being arithmetically
infinite. Likewise $\omega^{1/(k+1)}$ converges to zero in the second
coefficient topology.

These are additive subgroups, not subrings. Squaring $\omega$ yields
$\omega^2$, which belongs to neither displayed image. The absence of
inherited multiplicative closure is consistent with the stronger
nonexistence result \cref{pma:pr:cor:nosemiring} for \emph{any} compatible
semiring multiplication on these positive cones.

For foundations, the domains $\R\times\Z$ and $\Q^{\N}\times\Z$ are sets.
One may work entirely with their normal-form codes in ZFC and interpret
the notation inside a sufficiently large set-sized portion of the
surreal construction. Alternatively one may use a class theory for the
ambient surreal field while retaining these set-sized domains. None of
the proofs quantifies over a set of all surreals, a set of all class maps,
or a Polish space whose points form a proper class.

\section{An absolute obstruction to multiplication}
\label{pma:pr:sec:multiplication}\label{pma:pr:s04:sec:multiplication}

A Borel obstruction would not rule out a wildly discontinuous
multiplication. For the models constructed here, the obstruction is
stronger and purely algebraic.

\begin{definition}
In a Presburger model $M$, a positive element $u$ is a
\emph{cofinal infinite additive unit} if
\[
 u>n\one\quad\text{for every standard }n,
\]
and for every $x\in M$ there is a positive standard integer $N$ with
$x\leq Nu$.
The word ``unit'' here refers only to cofinality under repeated addition;
it is not a multiplicative unit.
\end{definition}

Source~04 splits the hypothesis of \cref{pma:pr:thm:no_semiring} into two
notions and proves a positivity lemma first:

\begin{definition}[\src{04}]
An element $h$ in an ordered additive monoid is an \emph{order unit} if for
every $x$ there is a positive standard integer $N$ with $x\leq Nh$. It is
\emph{arithmetically infinite} if $h>n\one$ for every standard $n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Positivity is automatic in a semiring expansion \src{04}]
\label{pma:pr:lem:positiveproduct}
Suppose a Presburger monoid is expanded to a unital semiring with the same
addition, zero, and one. Then the product of two positive elements is
positive, and multiplication by a positive element is strictly increasing
on either side.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $x,y>0$, write $x=1+a$, $y=1+b$ with $a,b\geq0$. Distributivity and
the unit laws give $xy=1+a+b+ab\geq1$. If $x>y$, write $x=y+c$ with
$c>0$. For $z>0$, one has $xz=yz+cz>yz$ and similarly on the other side.
Here the order is the additive natural order of the Presburger model, so
no separate order axiom for the multiplication has been assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cofinal-infinite-element obstruction]
\label{pma:pr:thm:no_semiring}\label{pma:pr:thm:orderunit}
A Presburger model with a cofinal infinite additive unit cannot be
expanded to a unital semiring with the same addition, zero, and
multiplicative unit $\one$. No continuity, measurability, commutativity
of multiplication, or induction involving multiplication is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose such a multiplication exists. Cofinality applied to $u^2$ gives
a positive standard $N$ such that $u^2\leq Nu$. Since $u$ exceeds every
standard numeral, it has a decomposition
\[
 u=(N+1)\one+v\qquad(v\in M).
\]
Distributivity and the unit law give
\[
 u^2=(N+1)u+vu\geq(N+1)u>Nu,
\]
a contradiction. The first inequality uses only that $vu$ belongs to the
nonnegative carrier. The last one follows from $u>0$ and additive
cancellation. In any semiring expansion of this additive monoid, its
natural order $x\leq y\leftrightarrow\exists z\ (x+z=y)$ is already the
Presburger order, so no additional order-compatibility axiom was hidden
in the argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label[corollary]{pma:pr:cor:no_semiring_models}\label[corollary]{pma:pr:cor:nosemiring}
Neither $\MR$ nor $\MB$ admits a unital semiring multiplication with the
displayed addition and unit.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In $\MR$ take $u=(1,0)$. It exceeds every standard numeral, and an
integer $N>r$ gives $(r,n)<Nu$. In $\MB$ take $u=(e_0,0)$, where $e_0$
has first coefficient $1$ and all later coefficients zero. For any
$(a,n)$ choose an integer $N>a_0$; comparison at coordinate zero gives
$(a,n)<Nu$. Apply \cref{pma:pr:thm:no_semiring}.
\end{proof}

In their omnific realizations, this cofinal element is $\omega$.
The missing larger scale $\omega^2$ is not merely a defect of one chosen
multiplication: every possible semiring multiplication would have to
produce an element growing faster than every standard multiple of
$\omega$, which the additive carrier cannot contain.

For a different uncountable Polish Presburger model that does allow a
semiring expansion algebraically, Glazer's cited Borel obstruction for
$Q+I\mathrm{Open}$ would still matter. Our argument is not a replacement
for that general theorem; it identifies a stronger obstruction in these
particular additive structures.

An arithmetically infinite order unit in 04's sense is exactly a cofinal
infinite additive unit in 05's sense, so \cref{pma:pr:thm:no_semiring} is
04's Theorem~9.3 and \cref{pma:pr:cor:no_semiring_models} its Corollary~9.4.
Source~04's proof routes the strict inequality through
\cref{pma:pr:lem:positiveproduct}:

\begin{proof}[Second proof of \cref{pma:pr:thm:no_semiring} (source 04)]
Suppose such a multiplication exists and let $h$ be the order unit.
Choose a standard $N\geq1$ with $h^2\leq Nh$. Since $h>N\one$,
\cref{pma:pr:lem:positiveproduct} gives $h^2>Nh$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

This algebraic argument is specific to the supplied models and does not
replace the general topological theorem about stronger arithmetic.
Presburger models need not be additive reducts of models of Peano
arithmetic.

\subsection{A variation with no largest growth class \src{04}}\label{pma:pr:sec:laurent}

Let $D_{\mathrm L}=\Q((t))$ be the additive group of formal Laurent series
\[
 f=\sum_{k\geq k_0}a_kt^k,\qquad k_0\in\Z,\quad a_k\in\Q,
\]
ordered by the sign of the coefficient at the least nonzero exponent.
Use the $t$-adic topology, with rational coefficients discrete. Its basic
neighborhoods at zero are
\[
 F_n=\{f:v(f)\geq n\},\qquad n\in\N,
\]
where $v(0)=+\infty$. A bounded compatible metric is
$\min\{1,2^{-v(f-g)}\}$ for $f\ne g$.

\begin{proposition}[A Laurent-series Polish construction \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:prop:laurent}
The group $D_{\mathrm L}$ is admissible, and
$\ML=M_{D_{\mathrm L}}$ is a Polish Presburger model homeomorphic to Baire
space, with continuous addition and fixed quotient and remainder. Unlike
the two main models, $\ML$ has no arithmetically infinite order unit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A Cauchy sequence of Laurent series eventually has the same entire negative
part, because its pairwise differences eventually lie in $F_0$. That common
negative part is finite. Every subsequent coefficient stabilizes in turn,
giving a Laurent-series limit. Thus the metric is complete. Laurent
polynomials with rational coefficients form a countable dense set.
The leading sign is determined on a basic neighborhood, so the strict
positive cone is open. Addition and division by fixed positive integers
are coefficientwise continuous.

For the asserted homeomorphism, decompose $D_{\mathrm L}$ into the clopen
cosets $p+\Q[[t]]$, where $p$ is a finite negative-power rational
polynomial. There are countably many such cosets, and each is a rational
product. If $p\ne0$, the sign on its coset is constant, determined by its
leading coefficient. The coset $p=0$ contributes respectively
$(D_{\mathrm B})_{>0}$ or $(D_{\mathrm B})_{\geq0}$ to the positive or nonnegative
cone. Thus both Laurent cones are countable disjoint sums of spaces
explicitly homeomorphic to $\Baire$, by the codings in \cref{pma:pr:s04:sec:baire}.
Prepending an index codes such a sum by $\Baire$. The integer slices of
$\ML$ are another such sum, proving the claim.

Finally, for $f>0$ with least exponent $k$, the element $(t^{k-1},0)$ is
larger than every standard multiple of $(f,z)$: its leading exponent is
strictly earlier. Standard finite elements also fail to be order units.
\end{proof}

The absence of an order unit does not establish the existence of a
semiring expansion. In this example another simple obstruction decides
that issue negatively.

\subsubsection{A growth-order obstruction}

For positive arithmetically infinite elements, define
\[
 x\asymp y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (\exists m,n\in\N_{>0})(x\leq my\ \wedge\ y\leq nx).
\]
Let $\mathcal A(M)$ be the set of these Archimedean growth classes, ordered
so that $[x]<[y]$ means $nx<y$ for every positive standard $n$. These are
external set-sized classes of elements of our set-sized model; no
first-order definability is being asserted.

\begin{proposition}[Growth classes must embed into their strict final segments \src{04}]
\label{pma:pr:prop:growthobstruction}
If a Presburger monoid $M$ has a unital semiring expansion, then for every
arithmetically infinite $h$, the map
\[
 [x]\longmapsto[hx]
\]
is an order embedding of $\mathcal A(M)$ into its strict final segment above
$[h]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Multiplication by $h>0$ preserves and reflects every comparison
$x\leq ny$. Preservation follows from distributivity and positivity.
For reflection, if $x>ny$ then strict monotonicity gives $hx>n(hy)$.
It follows that multiplication induces an order embedding on growth
classes. Because $x$ is infinite, $x>n\one$ for every standard $n$,
so $hx>nh$ for every $n$. Thus its class lies strictly above $[h]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\src{04}]\label{pma:pr:cor:laurentnoring}
The Laurent-series model $\ML$ also has no unital semiring expansion.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Its infinite growth classes are determined exactly by the least Laurent
exponent, with increasing growth corresponding to decreasing exponent.
Hence $\mathcal A(\ML)$ has order type $\Z$. A strict final segment of
$\Z$ is well-ordered, while $\Z$ contains an infinite strictly decreasing
sequence. There is no order embedding of $\Z$ into such a final segment.
Apply \cref{pma:pr:prop:growthobstruction}.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is not a statement that every split Presburger group
forbids multiplication. For example, take $D=X\Q[X]$ ordered by its
highest-degree nonzero coefficient, and let the constant term be an
ordinary integer. The ordered ring
\[
 \Z+X\Q[X]
\]
has this split additive group, least positive element $1$, and a positive
cone closed under its polynomial multiplication. Its growth classes have
order type $\N_{>0}$, which does embed in each of its strict final
segments. This countable comparison makes the scope of the obstruction
explicit.

\section{Endomorphisms, elementary submodels, and a contrast}
\label{pma:pr:sec:embeddings}\label{pma:pr:s04:sec:embeddings}

The two models have markedly different self-embedding behavior. All
homomorphisms in the next theorem preserve addition, zero, and $\one$;
continuity and injectivity are \emph{not} assumed.

\subsection{The real model: endomorphisms and elementary submodels}\label{pma:pr:sec:realendos}

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity of the real-coordinate model]
\label{pma:pr:thm:realendos}\label{pma:pr:thm:realembeddings}
Every unital additive endomorphism $f:\MR\to\MR$ has the form
\begin{equation}
 f(r,n)=(cr,n)\qquad(c\in\R_{>0}).
 \label{pma:pr:eq:endo}
\end{equation}
Conversely each such formula is an endomorphism. All of them are
homeomorphisms and Presburger automorphisms. In particular $\MR$ has no
proper elementary self-embedding.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The group completion of the cancellative monoid $\MR$ is
$G=\R\lex\Z$. Thus $f$ extends uniquely to an additive group map
$F:G\to G$ fixing $\one$. Inside $G$,
\[
 \bigcap_{m\geq1}mG=\R\times\{0\}.
\]
One inclusion follows from real divisibility; the other follows because
the integer coordinate of an element of the intersection is divisible
by every positive integer. Every additive group map preserves this
intersection. Hence
\[
 F(r,0)=(h(r),0),\qquad F(0,n)=(0,n)
\]
for some additive map $h:\R\to\R$.

Since $f$ maps the positive cone into itself, $h(r)\geq0$ whenever
$r\geq0$. Thus $h$ is monotone. If $c=h(1)$, rational comparisons and
additivity give $h(r)=cr$ for every real $r$: sandwich $r$ between
rational numbers and use the corresponding inequalities for $h$.
Here $c\geq0$. But $c=0$ would send the positive element $(1,-1)$ to
$(0,-1)$, outside the cone. Thus $c>0$.

Formula \eqref{pma:pr:eq:endo} and its inverse with scalar $1/c$ preserve the
cone, addition, order, and unit, and are continuous in the product
coordinates. This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All elementary submodels of the real model]
\label{pma:pr:thm:submodels}
The elementary submodels of $\MR$ are exactly
\begin{equation}
 M_E=(E\lex\Z)_{\geq0},
 \qquad E\text{ a }\Q\text{-linear subspace of }\R.
 \label{pma:pr:eq:submodels}
\end{equation}
Among them, the only ones Polish in the inherited topology are the
standard copy $\N$ and the whole model $\MR$. Every nonzero proper
choice of $E$ gives a dense, non-Polish elementary submodel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $E$ is a $\Q$-linear subspace, then $E\lex\Z$ is a $\Z$-group.
Its inclusion in $\R\lex\Z$ preserves and reflects the expanded
quantifier-free language, including all congruences, since a standard
modulus depends only on the integer coordinate. Quantifier elimination
makes this inclusion elementary. Relativizing to positive cones proves
$M_E\preccurlyeq\MR$.

Conversely, let $S\preccurlyeq\MR$, and let $H=S-S$ be its group
completion inside $G$. If $a,b\in S$ and $a-b\geq0$, the unique solution
of $b+x=a$ lies in $S$ by elementarity. Therefore
$S=H\cap\MR$. Since $H$ contains every standard integer, subtracting
the integer coordinate from any $(r,n)\in H$ shows that
$H=E\times\Z$ for an additive subgroup $E\leq\R$.
For $r\in E_{\geq0}$, the model's unique division of $(r,0)$ by a
positive standard $m$ has remainder zero and quotient $(r/m,0)$.
Elementarity puts that quotient in $S$. Applying the same observation
to negatives in $H$ shows $E$ is divisible, hence $\Q$-linear.

If $E\neq0$, it contains all rational multiples of a nonzero real and
is dense in $\R$. Thus $M_E$ is dense in every slice of $\MR$.
Suppose $M_E$ is also Polish in the inherited topology. Its closed slice
of integer coordinate zero is homeomorphic to $E\cap[0,\infty)$.
By \cref{pma:pr:lem:gdelta}, this is $G_\delta$ in $\R$. Its negative is also
$G_\delta$, so their union $E$ is $G_\delta$ in $\R$.
For any $r\in\R$, the dense $G_\delta$ sets $E$ and $r+E$ intersect
by the Baire category theorem. Hence $r\in E-E=E$, giving $E=\R$.
Finally $E=0$ gives the relatively discrete standard copy $\N$, which
is Polish, and $E=\R$ gives the whole model.
\end{proof}

For example, $E=\Q$ gives a countable dense elementary submodel. Its
inherited topology is not Polish; there is no inconsistency between its
density and the complete metric on the ambient model.

\subsection{Reduction to the divisible part \src{04}}\label{pma:pr:sec:reduction}

This section considers embeddings in the arithmetic language containing
$0,1,+$. Naming $1$ is explicit. Order is already definable, so its
preservation is not an extra independent assumption.

\begin{proposition}[All unital additive maps \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:prop:embeddingreduction}
Let $D,E$ be divisible linearly ordered abelian groups. Every map
$f:M_D\to M_E$ preserving $0,1,+$ is injective and has the unique form
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:embeddingform}
 f(d,z)=(T(d),z),
\end{equation}
where $T:D\to E$ is a $\Q$-linear order embedding. Conversely every such
$T$ defines a map of this kind. Every one of these maps is an elementary
embedding of Presburger models.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $x>y$, then $x=y+1+u$ for some $u\in M_D$. Thus
$f(x)=f(y)+1+f(u)>f(y)$, so $f$ strictly preserves order and is injective.
Every group element is a difference of nonnegative elements. The rule
\[
 \bar f(x-y)=f(x)-f(y)
\]
therefore extends $f$ uniquely to an additive order embedding of group
completions. It is well defined because $x-y=x'-y'$ implies
$x+y'=x'+y$, and $f$ preserves that equality.

For each $d\in D$ and every standard $m\geq1$, the element $(d,0)$ is an
$m$-multiple. Its image is consequently in every $mG_E$, whose intersection
is $E\times\{0\}$ by \eqref{pma:pr:eq:divisiblepart}. Hence
$\bar f(d,0)=(T(d),0)$. Since $f(1)=1$, the map fixes every standard
integer, giving \eqref{pma:pr:eq:embeddingform}. Divisibility and torsion-freeness
make $T$ $\Q$-linear; order preservation and injectivity are inherited.
The converse follows directly from the formula.

Such a group map preserves and reflects order, equality, and all
congruences, the last because integer coordinates are unchanged.
Quantifier elimination in \cref{pma:pr:s04:thm:qe} makes it elementary on the groups.
Relativizing to the nonnegative cones makes $f$ elementary as well.
\end{proof}

Source~04 proves \cref{pma:pr:thm:realendos} (its Theorem~8.2) from
\cref{pma:pr:prop:embeddingreduction}:

\begin{proof}[Second proof (source 04)]
By \cref{pma:pr:prop:embeddingreduction}, it remains to classify increasing
additive maps $T:\R\to\R$. Put $c=T(1)>0$. For rational $q$ one has
$T(q)=cq$. For a real $r$, compare $r$ with rational numbers from below
and above and use monotonicity. The resulting inequalities force
$T(r)=cr$. The inverse dilation is $f_{1/c}$, and both dilations are
continuous in the specified product topology.
\end{proof}

Here $1$ in $T(1)$ denotes the real vector $1\in D$, not the arithmetic
unit $(0,1)$. The distinction prevents a mistaken conclusion that $c$
must equal one. The model is co-Hopfian for unital additive maps but is
not rigid.

\subsection{The Baire model: all self-embeddings \src{04} and clopen self-copies}\label{pma:pr:sec:bairemaps}

Source~04 classifies every unital additive self-map of $\MB$; source~05's
shift theorem follows.

For $d\in D_{\mathrm B}\setminus\{0\}$ define
\[
 v(d)=\min\{k:d_k\ne0\}.
\]
Let $e_k$ be the rational sequence with a single $1$ in coordinate $k$,
and put
\[
 F_k=\{d:d_0=\cdots=d_{k-1}=0\}.
\]
Thus $F_0=D_{\mathrm B}$; the subgroups $F_k$ form a neighborhood basis at zero.

\begin{theorem}[Complete matrix classification \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:thm:matrices}
Every $\Q$-linear order embedding $T:D_{\mathrm B}\to D_{\mathrm B}$, without any
assumed continuity, has the form
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:matrix}
 (Td)_i=\sum_{k:p(k)\leq i}a_{ik}d_k,
\end{equation}
where $p:\N\to\N$ is strictly increasing and
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:pivotconditions}
 a_{ik}=0\ (i<p(k)),\qquad a_{p(k),k}>0.
\end{equation}
The coefficients below each pivot, $a_{ik}$ with $i>p(k)$, are arbitrary
rationals. Conversely every such matrix defines an order embedding.
All the sums in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:matrix} are finite. Every such map is continuous
and has closed image. It is surjective exactly when $p(k)=k$ for all $k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $T$ first be an arbitrary $\Q$-linear order embedding. Since $Te_k>0$,
its first nonzero coefficient is positive. Define $p(k)=v(Te_k)$. The
inequalities
\[
 ne_{k+1}<e_k\qquad(n=1,2,\ldots)
\]
remain true after applying $T$. If $p(k+1)<p(k)$, the image on the left
already dominates the right for $n=1$. If the pivots were equal, a large
enough standard $n$ would make the positive leading rational coefficient
on the left larger than the one on the right. Both are impossible.
Thus $p(k+1)>p(k)$, and in particular $p(k)\geq k$.

Every $d\in F_k$ satisfies $|d|<Ne_k$ for some standard integer $N>0$:
choose $N>|d_k|$, with a further unit of margin. Order preservation implies
$|Td|<NTe_k$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:filtration}
 T(F_k)\subseteq F_{p(k)}\subseteq F_k.
\end{equation}
This already proves continuity at zero, and therefore everywhere by
additivity. It also excludes noncontinuous linear maps arising from a
choice of a vector-space basis.

Let $a_{ik}=(Te_k)_i$. To compute coordinate $i$ of $Td$, subtract the
finite initial sum $\sum_{k=0}^{i}d_ke_k$. The remainder belongs to
$F_{i+1}$, whose image has zero coordinates through $i$ by
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:filtration}. Thus
$(Td)_i=\sum_{k=0}^{i}a_{ik}d_k$. Terms with $p(k)>i$ vanish, yielding
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:matrix} and \eqref{pma:pr:eq:pivotconditions}.

Conversely, a strictly increasing $p$ satisfies $p(k)\geq k$, so each row
in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:matrix} has finite support. The formula defines a linear
continuous map on all rational sequences. If $d\ne0$ has first nonzero
index $k$, then all rows before $p(k)$ vanish and
\[
 (Td)_{p(k)}=a_{p(k),k}d_k.
\]
Its sign is the sign of $d_k$. This proves order preservation and
injectivity, including comparison of arbitrary pairs by their difference.

To describe the range, for any proposed target sequence $y$ recursively
recover
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:pivotrecovery}
 d_k=\frac{y_{p(k)}-\sum_{j<k}a_{p(k),j}d_j}{a_{p(k),k}}.
\end{equation}
Each recovered coordinate is a continuous function of finitely many
coordinates of $y$. The target belongs to the range exactly when, for
every nonpivot row $i$,
\begin{equation}\label{pma:pr:eq:nonpivotconstraints}
 y_i=\sum_{k:p(k)<i}a_{ik}d_k.
\end{equation}
These are closed, indeed clopen, finite-coordinate conditions. Their
intersection is closed. If every row is a pivot, the recovery solves the
system for every target. This happens exactly when $p(k)=k$. If a row
$j$ is omitted, no image has its first nonzero coordinate equal to $j$,
so surjectivity fails.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Automatic continuity and the topology of self-images \src{04}]
\label{pma:pr:cor:imagetopology}
Every unital additive self-map of $\MB$ is elementary, continuous, and a
homeomorphism onto a closed image. Its image is clopen when the complement
of $p(\N)$ is finite, and closed nowhere dense when that complement is
infinite. The automorphisms are exactly the infinite lower triangular
rational matrices with strictly positive diagonal, acting on the divisible
coordinate and fixing the integer coordinate.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine \cref{pma:pr:prop:embeddingreduction,pma:pr:thm:matrices}. The inverse on the
image is continuous by the coordinate-recovery formula. The group image
is $T(D_{\mathrm B})\times\Z$, and its intersection with $\MB$ is exactly the
monoid image, because $T$ reflects order.

When finitely many rows are not pivots, \eqref{pma:pr:eq:nonpivotconstraints}
gives a finite intersection of clopen conditions, so the group and monoid
images are clopen. When infinitely many rows are not pivots, they occur
arbitrarily far out. At an image point whose $D_{\mathrm B}$-coordinate is
positive, keep an initial segment containing its leading coordinate and
change a later nonpivot coordinate so that its constraint fails. The
result remains positive and lies in any sufficiently specified cylinder.
At a point $(0,z)$ in the image, necessarily $z\geq0$. In any given
zero-prefix cylinder first insert a positive pivot far beyond that prefix,
using a small-in-topology image vector $Te_k$. Then violate a still later
nonpivot constraint. Positivity is unchanged and the resulting point is
outside the image. Thus the image has empty interior even at the boundary.
It is closed, hence nowhere dense.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Two contrasting proper elementary copies \src{04}]\label{pma:pr:ex:shifts}
The shift
\[
 S(d)_0=0,\qquad S(d)_{k+1}=d_k
\]
has pivots $p(k)=k+1$ and produces a proper clopen elementary self-copy of
$\MB$. The insertion map
\[
 E(d)_{2k}=d_k,\qquad E(d)_{2k+1}=0
\]
has pivots $p(k)=2k$ and produces a closed nowhere dense elementary
self-copy. Both are homeomorphisms onto their images. Their order-theoretic
and model-theoretic behavior is identical at the level of elementary
embedding, while their image interiors are different.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Clopen elementary self-copies of the Baire model]
\label{pma:pr:thm:shift}
Let
\[
 \sigma((a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots),n)
   =((0,a_0,a_1,a_2,\ldots),n).
\]
Then $\sigma:\MB\to\MB$ is a proper elementary embedding and a
homeomorphism onto a clopen subspace. Its iterates satisfy
\[
 \bigcap_{k\geq0}\sigma^k(\MB)=\{(0,n):n\in\N\}.
\]
Thus a descending sequence of proper clopen elementary self-copies has
intersection exactly the standard elementary submodel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inserting an initial zero preserves and reflects the lexicographic
order, addition, and every standard congruence. It maps the divisible
coefficient group into the subgroup with first coordinate zero.
Quantifier elimination gives elementarity, first for the groups and
then for their cones. The image is exactly the subset with $a_0=0$,
which is clopen because the first rational coordinate is discrete.
Deleting that zero is a continuous inverse on the image. It is proper,
since elements with $a_0>0$ are omitted.
The $k$-th image has its first $k$ rational coordinates zero. Membership
in all images therefore means that every rational coordinate is zero;
positivity then forces $n\geq0$.
\end{proof}

These clopen submodels are external subsets, not Presburger-definable
subsets, even with parameters: a proper definable subset containing zero
and closed under successor would violate induction. Topological
simplicity and internal definability are different properties.

\noindent\mergetag{} The map $\sigma$ of \cref{pma:pr:thm:shift} (source~05)
is the shift $S$ of \cref{pma:pr:ex:shifts} (source~04), with pivots
$p(k)=k+1$. Source~05 adds the intersection of the iterated images; source~04
adds the insertion map $E$, whose image is closed and nowhere dense.

\section{A compactness obstruction independent of multiplication}
\label{pma:pr:sec:compact}

The positive answer on Baire space does not extend to every familiar
uncountable Polish space. In particular compact carriers are impossible.
The underlying fact is the standard compact-cancellative-monoid argument,
which we include in its commutative form.

\begin{lemma}[Compact cancellation produces inverses]
\label[lemma]{pma:pr:lem:compactmonoid}
Every compact Hausdorff commutative cancellative topological monoid is
a group algebraically.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the identity as $0$ and fix $a$. Let $S$ be the closure of
$\{na:n\geq1\}$. Continuity of addition makes $S$ a nonempty compact
subsemigroup. By the compact intersection property and Zorn's lemma,
$S$ has a minimal nonempty compact subsemigroup $K$.

For $x\in K$, the set $x+K$ is a nonempty compact subset of $K$ and is a
subsemigroup: for $u,v\in K$,
\[
 (x+u)+(x+v)=x+(x+u+v)\in x+K.
\]
Minimality gives $x+K=K$. Since $x\in K$, there is $y\in K$ with
$x+y=x=x+0$, and cancellation gives $y=0$. Thus $0\in K\subseteq S$.
But $a+X$ is compact and hence closed, and contains every positive
multiple of $a$. Therefore it contains $S$ and in particular $0$.
So $a+b=0$ for some $b\in X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No compact positive-cone model]
\label[corollary]{pma:pr:cor:compact}
No model of Presburger arithmetic has a compact Hausdorff topology with
continuous addition. In particular Cantor space cannot be a carrier,
although Baire space can.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Its addition is a commutative cancellative monoid. The lemma would give
an additive inverse of $\one$, but no nonnegative element can sum with
$\one$ to zero.
\end{proof}

This obstruction uses neither Borel definability nor multiplication. It
is separate from \cref{pma:pr:thm:no_semiring}: the real model is locally
compact but not compact, and the Baire model is nowhere locally compact.

\subsection{The nonstandard Cooper boundary must be explicit}

The inspected repository theorem \texttt{periodic\_interval} has an
ordinary-integer predicate and a one-step periodicity hypothesis
\cite{RepoCooper}. Its proof can iterate by any ordinary integer quotient.
Over a general $\Z$-group, the quotient in $x-L=mq+r$ is an element of
that group, not necessarily a standard numeral. A proof by ordinary
natural-number induction cannot supply invariance under that shift for
an arbitrary external predicate.

The correct interface is therefore a predicate on the quotient $G/mG$,
or equivalently the hypothesis \eqref{pma:pr:eq:cosetinv}. Affine congruence
atoms satisfy it by algebra, and Boolean combinations preserve it. This
makes the generalized arithmetic lemma reusable and prevents an
incorrect formal transfer from being hidden by notation. The original
integer theorem should be retained as an instance; it does not need to
be weakened or retracted.

The existing decision procedure's semantics are over integers. Reusing
its syntax and transformations is plausible, but proving soundness over
all $\Z$-groups is an additional obligation. The repository's existence
of an executable integer eliminator, by itself, does not certify
\cref{pma:pr:cor:fullpresburger} for a new carrier.

property directly, so \cref{pma:pr:thm:boundary} supplies the needed uniform lemma.

\subsubsection{A concrete implementation sequence}

A formalization can be divided into independently reviewable stages.
The following are proposed new components, not assertions about existing
repository declarations.

\paragraph{Abstract arithmetic semantics.}
Define a class of linearly ordered abelian groups with a named least
positive unit and a standard finite quotient--remainder principle.
Interpret the existing affine syntax in an arbitrary group of this class.
Keep the syntactic integers as \emph{coefficients}; they must not be
confused with the semantic carrier.

\paragraph{Finite boundary theorem.}
Formalize $m$-residue invariance as invariance under differences in $mG$.
Prove \cref{pma:pr:thm:boundary}, separating existence of a finite candidate from
any decidability hypothesis on its predicate. Then specialize to a
Boolean evaluator when the residue conditions are decidable. This keeps
the mathematical statement valid even when the carrier has undecidable
equality or order in a computational representation.

\paragraph{Model-uniform elimination.}
Reuse the finite syntax transformations, with a fresh semantic correctness
proof for coefficient normalization, cancellation in
\eqref{pma:pr:eq:scalingcongruence}, bound selection, and finite substitution.
The correctness endpoint should quantify over all groups satisfying the
abstract assumptions. Specialization to $\Z$ must recover the current
integer semantics rather than replace it.

\paragraph{Split models and topological structure.}
Construct $G_D$ and its nonnegative cone, prove explicit division, and
obtain the complete Presburger theory by relativization. Next formalize
the $G_\delta$ argument, continuity of restricted addition, and the
coordinate formulas for fixed quotient and remainder. For $D_{\mathrm B}$,
construct the two coding homeomorphisms rather than invoking a black-box
classification of zero-dimensional Polish spaces.

\paragraph{Self-embeddings.}
First prove extension from the positive cone to the group completion and
preservation of the divisible subgroup. For $D_{\mathrm B}$, prove the
filtration lemma \eqref{pma:pr:eq:filtration} before introducing matrices.
This exposes the reason for automatic continuity and avoids an unproved
assumption that an arbitrary linear map is determined by its values on
the coordinate vectors. Those vectors are \emph{not} a vector-space basis
of the full product; the finite-row conclusion requires the order argument.

\subsection{Questions asked by both sources}\label{pma:pr:sec:qshared}

\begin{question}[A formal certificate for the affirmative answer \src{05, 04}]\label{pma:pr:q:formal}
\emph{Source 05}: Can the one-scale model, the full $\Z$-group elimination
theorem, and its Polish topology be formalized with a compact audit boundary in
ProveIt? The key initial milestone is the generic coset-invariant Cooper lemma,
rather than merely a finite test of arithmetic identities.
\emph{Source 04}: Can the complete proof of \cref{pma:pr:thm:answer}, including
the Baire homeomorphism and the full induction scheme via model-uniform
elimination, be added to \repo\ with no new axioms? The first milestone should
be the finite boundary lemma with residue invariance, followed by the semantic
specialization checks described in \cref{pma:pr:s04:sec:formalization}.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Weak metatheory \src{05, 04}]\label{pma:pr:q:metatheory}
\emph{Source 05}: What weak foundational theory suffices to certify the
affirmative construction, including the chosen representation of Polishness?
The real model has an explicit complete metric, and the Baire model has an
explicit sequence code, so a general model-existence theorem is unnecessary.
This is distinct from Glazer's Question~1 concerning the strength needed for
his stronger-arithmetic obstruction.
\emph{Source 04}: What is the weakest useful subsystem of second-order
arithmetic in which the Baire-space construction, its stream algorithms, and
its formula-by-formula model correctness can be coded and proved? Distinguish
coding all points of Baire space from asserting a set of all such points. This
question concerns the positive construction, not a resolution of the separate
reverse-mathematical problem posed in Glazer's Question~1 \cite{Glazer}.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Connected carriers \src{05, 04}]\label{pma:pr:q:connected}
\emph{Source 05}: What additional obstruction prevents or permits a
continuous-addition model on a connected Polish space? Our locally compact
model has countably many ray components, and the arithmetic order cannot be
open or closed. A proof for a prescribed connected carrier must use more than
the order-topology observation alone.
\emph{Source 04}: Can a connected uncountable Polish space support a
Presburger model with continuous addition? Our examples do not decide this:
their integer coordinate is a continuous map onto a countable discrete space.
Requiring all fixed remainders to be continuous would already force
nontrivial clopen partitions, so the connected problem must separate
continuity of addition from continuity of these definable functions.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Multiplication and growth orders \src{05, 04}]\label{pma:pr:q:multiplication}
\emph{Source 05}: Can one give an equally explicit Polish continuous-addition
Presburger model whose underlying additive structure admits a unital semiring
expansion? Any candidate must avoid a cofinal infinite additive unit. If one
also requires $Q+I\mathrm{Open}$, the multiplication cannot be Borel by the
cited obstruction. Separating algebraic expandability from Borel
expandability is the central issue.
\emph{Source 04}: Find admissible uncountable topological groups $D$ whose
infinite growth order embeds into each of its strict final segments, thereby
passing \cref{pma:pr:prop:growthobstruction}. For their positive cones,
determine whether any semiring multiplication exists, and, separately, which
descriptive regularity it can have. Glazer's obstruction to Borel
multiplication under stronger arithmetic remains relevant, but it does not by
itself classify bare semiring expansions.
\end{question}

\subsection{Further questions of source 05}\label{pma:pr:sec:q05}

\begin{question}[Prescribed zero-dimensional locally compact carriers]
Which uncountable zero-dimensional locally compact Polish spaces support
continuous-addition Presburger models? Compact spaces are excluded and
Baire space is realized, but Baire space is nowhere locally compact.
This leaves a meaningful topological region not settled by our examples.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Classifying admissible coefficient groups]
Which divisible ordered Polish abelian groups have $G_\delta$ positive
cone? \Cref{pma:pr:thm:criterion} turns this into an exact criterion for the
split construction. Can their possible underlying topologies, order
ranks, and continuous order-preserving endomorphisms be classified under
useful additional hypotheses?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Continuity of a richer definable language]
How much of Presburger definable choice can be made continuous in one
uncountable Polish presentation? Standard quotients and remainders are
continuous here, but total predecessor and truncated subtraction are
not everywhere continuous. Is there a natural maximal collection of
definable operations admitting simultaneous continuity?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Optimal finite-mind-change complexity]
For a given Presburger formula, what is the smallest possible uniform
change bound in the Baire presentation? The proof gives the number of
order/equality atom occurrences after elimination. That bound need not
be optimal and may be much larger than necessary. Relating the minimal
bound to the finite difference hierarchy or to formula structure would
refine the $\Dtwo$ result.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Polish elementary-submodel lattices]
Which lattices of closed or clopen elementary submodels can occur in a
Polish continuous-addition Presburger model? The real model has only two
Polish elementary submodels with inherited topology, whereas the Baire
model has infinitely many proper clopen elementary self-copies with
standard intersection. What intermediate behaviors can be realized?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Canonical surreal cutoffs and topologies]
Find useful explicit birthday bounds for the two fixed-support omnific
images and formalize the coefficient-topology transport inside those
cutoffs. More generally, which support sets admit Polish coefficient
topologies with continuous addition and full Presburger induction on
the resulting positive cone? Such a classification should keep ring
closure separate from additive closure.
\end{question}

\subsection{Further questions of source 04}\label{pma:pr:sec:q04}

The last question below and the last question of \cref{pma:pr:sec:q05}

\begin{question}[Beyond split topological models]
Which Polish presentations of Presburger groups admit a continuous

\end{question}

The Baire presentation has computable addition and fixed division, but not

\end{question}

What useful uniform continuity properties of addition can be retained by

\end{question}

\section{Conclusions of Part I}\label{pma:pr:sec:conclusion}

The affirmative construction can be summarized by one change of viewpoint:
use the lexicographic order to interpret arithmetic, and a coefficient
topology to interpret continuity. Divisibility of the leading-coordinate
group supplies all standard residues; quantifier elimination then
supplies full Presburger induction. Neither countability of the standard
model nor discreteness of the arithmetic order forces the external
presentation to be countable or discrete.

The real model gives a locally compact answer, while the rational-sequence
model gives a genuinely effective answer on Baire space. Their order
relations have the smallest possible nontrivial Borel rank, yet they
exhibit different elementary self-embedding behavior. Their realization
inside $\Oz$ connects these results directly to the repository's
normal-form arithmetic. Finally, the cofinal-infinite-element argument
shows exactly why these additive examples cannot be mistaken for
topological models of full arithmetic.

\paragraph{Source 04's conclusion \src{04}.}

The separation needed for the construction is simple: place a divisible
ordered group above a discrete integer coordinate algebraically, while
retaining the ordinary or product topology on that divisible group.
The arithmetic becomes discretely ordered without forcing the topology
to be discrete. Unique standard residues give the whole Presburger theory,
not just a fragment.

The real-coordinate construction supplies a perfect locally compact
example; the rational product supplies literal Baire space and effective
stream operations. Their regularity is sharply delimited: every definable
relation has Borel rank at most two, whereas monus is discontinuous
exactly on an explicitly described geometric boundary. Their
self-embeddings range from unavoidable surjective dilations to closed
nowhere dense elementary copies, but all are automatically continuous.

For \repo, the most immediate next step is precise and finite: generalize
the Cooper residue lemma with the correct subgroup-invariance hypothesis,

\section{The intersection and the questions answered}
\label{pma:bor:sec:intro}

Glazer's paper \emph{A Topological Tennenbaum Theorem} studies the interaction
between arithmetic, Borel definability, and complete separable topologies.
The part used here is particularly well matched to omnific arithmetic:
an uncountable Polish model of Robinson arithmetic plus open induction cannot
have continuous addition and Borel multiplication
\cite[Corollary 1]{Glazer}. This is weaker than the hypothesis needed in his
separate multiplication obstruction; we preserve that distinction throughout.

The relevant ProveIt material is the surreal-number project, especially the
reports on omnific notations and computer algebra, and its construction of the
omnific ring and floor. The inspected repository guides explicitly distinguish
research drafts, finite verification programs, and statements mapped to Lean
proofs \cite{RepoMain,RepoNotations}. That distinction is retained here. The
repository already reports a $\Pi^1_1$ classification of unrestricted support
validity, using Kleene--Brouwer coding. Thus merely repeating a
well-founded-tree reduction would not constitute a new advance on this material.

Instead, we ask for an exact boundary and a positive arithmetic counterpart.
For which countable exponent orders is support validity Borel? Does the answer
also determine whether the whole Hahn field can be given a standard Borel
presentation with readable coefficients? Can a concrete uncountable integer
part have arithmetic that is continuous almost everywhere in the category
sense, even though Glazer rules out global continuity? Precisely where does
continuity fail?

\subsection{Main theorem map}

The report proves the following connected package.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Support and field-presentation dichotomy.}
For countable $L$, $\WO(L)$ is Borel if and only if $L$ contains no copy of
$\Q$. Otherwise it is coanalytic-complete
(Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:dichotomy}). For countable ordered abelian $\Gamma$, the same
condition characterizes coefficient-observable standard Borel presentations
of $\R((t^\Gamma))$ (Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:field}).
\item \textbf{Exact benchmark complexities and a root-closure obstruction.}
The support predicates for $\Z$ and $\Z^d_{\mathrm{lex}}$, $d\geq2$, are
respectively $\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_2$-complete and
$\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$-complete. A nontrivial countable exponent group in a
square-root-closed full real Hahn field cannot lie on the Borel side
(Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:finitelex}, Corollary~\ref{pma:bor:cor:rootjump}).
\item \textbf{Uniform bounds and localization.}
Every Borel coefficient-observable family of valid series has its support
order types bounded below one countable ordinal. Arbitrarily large countable
support types, and coanalytic-complete validity, already occur in a fixed
$0$--$1$ coefficient slice inside $[X,2X)$
(Theorems~\ref{pma:bor:thm:bounded} and~\ref{pma:bor:thm:local}).
\item \textbf{A positive model with exact negative boundaries.}
The finite principal-part model $\PP$ satisfies open induction and has a Polish
presentation with explicit closed nowhere dense discontinuity sets for both
operations. Yet no Polish topology with the same Borel sets makes addition
continuous everywhere (Theorems~\ref{pma:bor:thm:arithmetic},
\ref{pma:bor:thm:cancellation}, and~\ref{pma:bor:thm:glazer}).
\end{enumerate}

The order-theoretic dichotomy is deduced from classical Hausdorff theory and
well-founded-tree completeness. The uniform bound is an application of
classical analytic boundedness, for which we include a proof. The integer-part
model is of Shepherdson type, not a newly discovered model of arithmetic.
The report's claims of contribution concern the explicit combined
classification, the observable-field criterion, the benchmark calculations,
and the exact topological analysis. These are proved here; bibliographic
priority is not asserted.

\section{Conventions and the meaning of a presentation}
\label{pma:bor:sec:conventions}

\subsection{Two opposite exponent conventions}

For an ordered abelian group $\Gamma$, a Hahn series is written
\[
 f=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma} a_\gamma t^\gamma,
 \qquad a_\gamma\in\R,
\]
with $\supp(f)=\{\gamma:a_\gamma\ne0\}$ well-ordered in the increasing order
of $\Gamma$. Its sign is the sign of the coefficient at the least exponent.
Thus $t$ is infinitesimal when $\Gamma=\Q$ and $t=t^1$.

For the omnific applications we instead write $X=t^{-1}$, so $X$ is positive
infinite. In
\[
 f=\sum_{q\in\Q} b_q X^q,
\]
the support must be \emph{reverse} well-ordered: every nonempty subset has a
greatest exponent. The change $q\mapsto-q$ converts one convention into the
other. All descriptive-set-theoretic statements in Sections~\ref{pma:bor:sec:order}
and~\ref{pma:bor:sec:lex} use the increasing convention. Section~\ref{pma:bor:sec:local}
explicitly reverses it.

The ring
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:H}
 \HQ=\left\{n+\sum_{q\in S} b_qX^q:
 n\in\Z,\ S\subseteq\Q_{>0}\text{ reverse well-ordered}\right\}
\end{equation}
is a set-sized rational-exponent omnific ring. Replacing $X^q$ by the Conway
monomial $\omega^q$ identifies it with the corresponding normal-form subring
of $\Oz$. Hahn addition and multiplication agree with those normal-form
operations. The normal-form viewpoint and its ordered-algebraic context are
standard; see \cite{Neumann,EhrlichKaplan}.

\subsection{Borel spaces, completeness, and observability}

A Polish space is a separable topological space admitting a compatible
complete metric. A standard Borel space is a measurable space isomorphic to a
Borel subset of a Polish space. We use the usual boldface pointclasses:
$\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_2$ consists of countable unions of closed sets,
$\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$ of countable intersections of
$\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_2$ sets, and $\boldsymbol\Pi^1_1$ denotes coanalytic sets.
An analytic set is a projection of a Borel set in a product of Polish spaces.
Completeness is with respect to continuous reductions from zero-dimensional
Polish spaces. Standard references are \cite{Kechris,Tserunyan}.

\begin{definition}
\label{pma:bor:def:observable}
Let $K$ be a set of series over a countable exponent alphabet. A
\emph{coefficient-observable standard Borel parametrization} of $K$ consists
of a standard Borel space $Y$ and a surjection $\theta:Y\to K$ such that, for
every exponent $\gamma$, the map
\[
 y\longmapsto [t^\gamma]\theta(y)
\]
is Borel as a map to $\R$. Repetitions of names are allowed.
\end{definition}

No Borel structure on $K$ is presupposed in this definition. Since the alphabet
is countable, coefficient observability says exactly that the composite code
map $Y\to\R^\Gamma$ is Borel. This is stronger than the mere existence of a
set-theoretic bijection with the reals, and different from an abstract Borel
presentation that is allowed to make every canonical coefficient unobservable.
Our negative results explicitly retain the observability hypothesis.

\begin{definition}
For a countable linear order $L$, put
\[
 \WO(L)=\{S\in2^L:(S,<_{L})\text{ is well-ordered}\}.
\]
The empty support is allowed. The order $L$ is \emph{scattered} if it contains
no order-isomorphic copy of $\Q$.
\end{definition}

A subset of $2^L$ is a set of codes, not a new collection of proper-class
objects. In particular, asking whether $\WO(L)$ is Borel is an ordinary
set-sized descriptive-set-theoretic question.

\section{The exact support dichotomy}
\label{pma:bor:sec:order}

\subsection{A complete obstruction on a dense countable alphabet}

\begin{lemma}
\label{pma:bor:lem:coanalytic}
For every countable linear order $L$, $\WO(L)$ is coanalytic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A support $S$ fails to be well-ordered exactly when there exists
$(x_n)_{n\in\N}\in L^\N$ with $x_n\in S$ and $x_{n+1}<_{L}x_n$ for every
$n$. The relation on $(S,(x_n))$ specified by these countably many coordinate
conditions is closed, with $L$ discrete. Its projection is analytic. Its
complement is $\WO(L)$.
\end{proof}

Let $\Tr$ be the closed space of trees $T\subseteq\N^{<\N}$, where trees
are closed under initial segments, and let $\WF\subseteq\Tr$ be the
well-founded trees. Recall briefly why $\WF$ is coanalytic-complete. If
$A$ is coanalytic in Baire space, its complement is the projection of the body
of a tree $R$ on pairs of finite sequences. For $x\in\N^\N$, let
\[
 T_x=\{s:(x\upharpoonright |s|,s)\in R\}.
\]
Membership of a fixed $s$ depends on finitely many coordinates of $x$, so
$x\mapsto T_x$ is continuous, and $x\in A$ exactly when $T_x$ has no branch.
This is the standard universal-tree proof \cite[Section 16.B]{Tserunyan}.

\begin{lemma}[Kleene--Brouwer coding]
\label{pma:bor:lem:KB}
There is a fixed injection $\muKB:\N^{<\N}\to\Q\cap(0,1)$ such that
\[
 T\in\WF\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \muKB[T]\in\WO(\Q).
\]
Consequently $\WO(\Q)$ is $\boldsymbol\Pi^1_1$-complete.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order the finite sequences by the Kleene--Brouwer order: a proper extension
precedes its prefix, and otherwise the first differing entry determines the
order, with the ordinary order on $\N$. This is a countable linear order.
Embed it in $\Q\cap(0,1)$ as follows. Enumerate its elements. At each finite
stage, put the next element at a rational strictly between its nearest
already assigned lower and upper neighbors, using $0$ and $1$ as external
bounds when needed. A midpoint works. The resulting $\muKB$ is one fixed order
embedding of the entire tree alphabet, not an embedding selected separately
for each $T$.

If $T$ has a branch, its successively longer prefixes form a strictly descending
Kleene--Brouwer sequence. If $T$ is well-founded, well-founded induction over
its nodes shows that the Kleene--Brouwer order on each descendant subtree is a
well-order: it is the increasing $\N$-indexed sum of the child-subtree orders,
followed by the root. Therefore $\KB(T)$ is a well-order exactly when $T$ is
well-founded.

The map $T\mapsto \muKB[T]$ is continuous. At an exponent in the range of $\muKB$,
its characteristic function reads exactly one tree-membership bit; outside
the range it is constantly zero. Thus it reduces $\WF$ to $\WO(\Q)$.
Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:coanalytic} supplies membership in the pointclass.
\end{proof}

\noindent\writetag{} \Cref{pma:bor:lem:KB} and part~(ii) of
\cref{pma:bor:thm:dichotomy} below are boldface counterparts of results
already in the collection's \fn{omnific-notations} report:
\replabel{onot:neg:lem:kbtree} (a tree is well-founded exactly when its
Kleene--Brouwer order is a well-order), \replabel{onot:neg:thm:pionesone}
($\Pi^1_1$-completeness, for recursive trees, of the validity of decidable
dyadic supports in $[1,2)$ with coefficient one) and
\replabel{onot:neg:rem:secondroute} (the midpoint codings of that report's
sources 08 and 09, which are the construction of $\muKB$ up to the target
interval). Source~03 reproves the reduction to make the continuous
(boldface) form explicit, as its contribution ledger says
(\cref{pma:bor:app:audit}). What is new in \cref{pma:bor:sec:order} is the
dichotomy between scattered and nonscattered orders.

\subsection{Why scattered alphabets are different}

We use the classical countable Hausdorff characterization: starting with
finite linear orders, closing under $\Z$-indexed sums and finite sums through
countable ordinal stages produces precisely the countable scattered orders.
A precise formulation is recalled in \cite[Definition 1]{AlvirRossegger}.
Only this characterization, not its more advanced rank consequences, is
needed below.

\begin{lemma}
\label{pma:bor:lem:Zsum}
Suppose $L=\sum_{i\in\Z} L_i$ is a lexicographic sum and each
$\WO(L_i)$ is Borel. Then $\WO(L)$ is Borel.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $S\subseteq L$, let $S_i=S\cap L_i$ and
$I(S)=\{i:S_i\ne\varnothing\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:Zcriterion}
 S\in\WO(L)
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \bigl(\forall i\in\Z\;S_i\in\WO(L_i)\bigr)
 \ \wedge\
 \bigl(\exists N\in\Z\;\forall i<N\;S_i=\varnothing\bigr).
\end{equation}
Necessity of the second condition follows because unboundedly negative occupied
indices yield an infinite descending sequence, choosing one element from each
of a decreasing sequence of such indices. Conversely, when the condition
holds, $S$ is a well-ordered sum of well-ordered fibers over a subset of an
integer tail.

Each restriction map $S\mapsto S_i$ is continuous. The fiber-empty conditions
are closed. The right-hand side of \eqref{pma:bor:eq:Zcriterion} is therefore Borel.
For a finite sum the analogous criterion has only finitely many fiber
conditions and no additional obstruction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Support dichotomy]
\label{pma:bor:thm:dichotomy}
For every countable linear order $L$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item if $L$ is scattered, $\WO(L)$ is Borel;
\item if $L$ is not scattered, $\WO(L)$ is
$\boldsymbol\Pi^1_1$-complete.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, Borelness of $\WO(L)$ is equivalent to scatteredness of $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a finite order the support predicate is the whole finite code space.
Induction through the Hausdorff construction, using
Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:Zsum} and the finite-sum observation, proves (i).

For (ii), fix an order embedding $j:\Q\to L$. Extending a support by zero
outside $j[\Q]$ gives a continuous map $S\mapsto j[S]$ from $2^\Q$ to
$2^L$, preserving and reflecting well-orderedness. Compose this map with
Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:KB}, and use Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:coanalytic}.
A coanalytic-complete set is not Borel, completing the equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The quantification here is over \emph{all subsets of one fixed countable
order}. It is not a statement about the Borel complexity of the class of all
scattered orders, nor about finding a Hausdorff decomposition uniformly from
an arbitrary code for $L$. The fixed decomposition used in the positive proof
need not be computable.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact benchmarks: integers, finite lexicographic powers, and rationals}
\label{pma:bor:sec:lex}

The dichotomy gives a qualitative boundary. On useful discrete alphabets one
can calculate the precise Borel level.

\begin{theorem}
\label{pma:bor:thm:finitelex}
With the product topology on the corresponding binary support spaces:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\WO(\Z)$ is $\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_2$-complete;
\item for every integer $d\geq2$, $\WO(\Z^d_{\mathrm{lex}})$ is
$\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$-complete;
\item every well-ordered subset of $\Z^d_{\mathrm{lex}}$ has order type at
most $\omega^d$, and this bound is attained.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A subset of $\Z$ is well-ordered if and only if it is bounded below. Hence
\[
 \WO(\Z)=\bigcup_{N\in\Z}
 \{S:\forall n<N\ (n\notin S)\},
\]
a countable union of closed sets. The continuous map
$A\subseteq\N\mapsto\{-n-1:n\in A\}$ reduces the familiar
$\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_2$-complete set $\Fin$ of finite subsets of $\N$ to
$\WO(\Z)$. This proves (i).

For completeness of the benchmark being used, $\Fin$ is countable and dense
in $2^\N$. Were it $G_\delta$, it would be comeager, while it is also meager;
the Baire theorem rules this out. The usual completeness criterion for Borel
sets in zero-dimensional Polish spaces then gives its
$\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_2$-completeness \cite[Section 16.A]{Tserunyan}.

For $d=2$, view the order as $\sum_{i\in\Z}\Z$. Formula
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:Zcriterion} is the intersection of a
$\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$ condition (every fiber is bounded below) and a
$\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_2$ condition (the occupied outer indices are bounded
below). The result is $\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$.
For $d>2$, the same formula and induction work: countable intersections of
$\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$ sets remain $\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$.

For hardness, use
\[
 \Fin^\N=
 \{a\in2^{\N\times\N}:\forall m\;\{n:a(m,n)=1\}\text{ is finite}\}.
\]
This set is $\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$-complete. Indeed, any
$\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$ subset of a zero-dimensional Polish space is a countable
intersection of $\boldsymbol\Sigma^0_2$ sets; reduce each of these sets to
$\Fin$ and take the product of the reductions.
Map $a$ to the support
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:rowreduction}
 S_a=\{(m,-n-1):a(m,n)=1\}\subseteq\Z^2_{\mathrm{lex}}.
\end{equation}
The outer indices are automatically bounded below by zero. A fiber is
well-ordered exactly when its row is finite. Thus \eqref{pma:bor:eq:rowreduction} is
a continuous reduction. Embedding $\Z^2_{\mathrm{lex}}$ into
$\Z^d_{\mathrm{lex}}$ gives hardness for all $d\geq2$.

Finally, a well-ordered subset of $\Z$ has type at most $\omega$. Inductively,
a well-ordered support in $\Z^d$ is a sum of at most $\omega$ fibers of type
at most $\omega^{d-1}$, and so has type at most
$\omega^{d-1}\cdot\omega=\omega^d$. The subset $\N^d$, with the same
lexicographic convention (first coordinate most significant), has type exactly
$\omega^d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{tabular}{@{}P{.36\textwidth}P{.27\textwidth}P{.25\textwidth}@{}}

\section{An observable-presentation dichotomy for full Hahn fields}
\label{pma:bor:sec:field}

Fix a countable ordered abelian group $\Gamma$. Let
\[
 V_\Gamma=\{a\in\R^\Gamma:\supp(a)\in\WO(\Gamma)\}.
\]
As a set, $V_\Gamma$ is the canonical coefficient presentation of the full
Hahn field $K_\Gamma=\R((t^\Gamma))$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{pma:bor:lem:coefficientdomain}
If $\Gamma$ is scattered, $V_\Gamma$ is Borel. If $\Gamma$ is nonscattered,
$V_\Gamma$ is $\boldsymbol\Pi^1_1$-complete.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The support map
\[
 \sigma:\R^\Gamma\to2^\Gamma,\qquad
 \sigma(a)(\gamma)=1\Longleftrightarrow a_\gamma\ne0
\]
is Borel. Thus the scattered case follows from
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:dichotomy}. In all cases, failure of validity is witnessed
by an infinite descending sequence of nonzero coordinates, an analytic
condition, so $V_\Gamma$ is coanalytic. The binary-coefficient inclusion
$2^\Gamma\hookrightarrow\R^\Gamma$ is continuous and reduces
$\WO(\Gamma)$ to $V_\Gamma$, proving completeness in the remaining case.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Observable Hahn-field criterion]
\label{pma:bor:thm:field}
For countable ordered abelian $\Gamma$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\Gamma$ is scattered as a linear order;
\item $V_\Gamma$ is a Borel subset of $\R^\Gamma$;
\item $K_\Gamma$ admits a coefficient-observable standard Borel
parametrization, allowing repeated names;
\item $K_\Gamma$ admits a standard Borel presentation with Borel coefficient
maps, Borel addition and multiplication, Borel inversion on the nonzero
elements, and Borel order.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:coefficientdomain} gives (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii).
Assume (ii), and use $V_\Gamma$ itself as the standard Borel domain.
Addition and negation are coordinatewise and therefore Borel.
For $\Gamma=\{0\}$ all assertions reduce to those for $\R$. Otherwise,
for multiplication enumerate $\Gamma=\{\delta_0,\delta_1,\ldots\}$ without
repetition. At a
fixed exponent $\gamma$, the product coefficient is
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:conv}
 (ab)_\gamma=\lim_{N\to\infty}
 \sum_{j<N}a_{\delta_j}b_{\gamma-\delta_j}.
\end{equation}
For valid Hahn supports, only finitely many terms are nonzero. To see the
finiteness, infinitely many contributing $\delta$ would contain a strictly
increasing sequence in the well-ordered support of $a$; the corresponding
$\gamma-\delta$ would be a strictly decreasing sequence in the support of
$b$. This is impossible. Thus the sums in \eqref{pma:bor:eq:conv} eventually stabilize.
They are continuous on the ambient product, and their pointwise limit on the
Borel domain is Borel.

The standard Hahn-field theorem supplies closure under multiplication and the
existence of multiplicative inverses; its well-ordered-support lemma is the
usual Neumann lemma \cite{Neumann}. The graph
\[
 \{(a,b)\in(V_\Gamma\setminus\{0\})\times V_\Gamma:ab=1\}
\]
is Borel and has exactly one point in each vertical section. The
Lusin--Novikov theorem gives Borel inversion. Equivalently, for an open set
$U$ the inverse image under inversion is the projection of this graph
intersected with $V_\Gamma\times U$, whose sections have at most one point.

Positivity is Borel because it is expressed by
\[
 \exists\gamma\in\Gamma\;
 \bigl(a_\gamma>0\ \wedge\ \forall\delta<\gamma\;a_\delta=0\bigr).
\]
There are only countably many coordinates. Order follows by subtraction.
This proves (iv), which immediately implies (iii).

Assume (iii). The coefficient code map from its standard Borel parameter
space to $\R^\Gamma$ is Borel, and its image is exactly $V_\Gamma$.
Consequently $V_\Gamma$ is analytic. For nonscattered $\Gamma$,
Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:coefficientdomain} makes it coanalytic-complete and hence
nonanalytic. Thus $\Gamma$ must be scattered, proving (i).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the negative direction does and does not exclude]
The theorem excludes even a surjective system of Borel-observable names, not
just unique or canonical names. It does not exclude abstract Borel models in
which the coefficient functions fail to be Borel. Nor does it exclude useful
Borel subfields of $K_\Gamma$, including real closed subfields. The word
\emph{full} is essential.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Root closure forces the non-Borel side for full fields}

\begin{corollary}
\label{pma:bor:cor:rootjump}
Suppose $\Gamma\ne\{0\}$ is countable and every positive element of the
full Hahn field $\R((t^\Gamma))$ has a square root in that field. Then
$\Gamma$ is nonscattered, and the field admits no coefficient-observable
standard Borel parametrization. In particular, this holds whenever the full
Hahn field is real closed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For every $\gamma\in\Gamma$, the positive monomial $t^\gamma$ has a root
$f$ with $f^2=t^\gamma$. Taking least exponents gives
$2\min\supp(f)=\gamma$. Thus $\Gamma$ is $2$-divisible. For $a<b$ in
$\Gamma$, the element $a+(b-a)/2$ lies strictly between them. A nontrivial
ordered group has no endpoints, so $\Gamma$ is a countable dense linear order
without endpoints and is order-isomorphic to $\Q$. Apply
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:field}.
\end{proof}

This is a necessary-condition argument; it does not use a classification of
which Hahn fields are real closed. It also explains the source of the jump:
arbitrary well-ordered supports over a densely divisible scale are the
problem, not the mere availability of algebraic roots. The Puiseux field in
Section~\ref{pma:bor:sec:Puiseux} supplies the contrasting real closed Borel subfield.

\section{Every observable family has a countable support bound}
\label{pma:bor:sec:rank}

We now strengthen the nonexistence statement into a restriction on every
individual Borel system of names. The essential input is the classical
boundedness theorem for analytic families of well-founded trees
\cite{Kechris}. The following proof makes the particular form we need explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Analytic boundedness, with proof]
\label{pma:bor:lem:boundedtrees}
If $\mathcal T\subseteq\WF$ is analytic, there is a countable ordinal
$\beta$ bounding the root ranks of all trees in $\mathcal T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The empty tree is assigned rank zero and can be discarded. If no trees remain,
take $\beta=1$. Otherwise write the remaining analytic family $\mathcal T$ as the projection
of a closed set $C\subseteq\N^\N\times\Tr$. Fix a binary coding of trees
using an enumeration of $\N^{<\N}$.
Construct a countable tree $U$ whose level-$k$ nodes are triples
$(s,u,v)$ with $s,u\in\N^k$, $v\in2^k$, for which some $(y,T)\in C$
satisfies
\[
 u=y\upharpoonright k,\qquad
 v=\operatorname{code}(T)\upharpoonright k,\qquad s\in T.
\]
Taking initial segments preserves these conditions, so $U$ is a tree.

If $U$ had an infinite branch, its three coordinates would give
$b,y\in\N^\N$ and a binary code $z$. For each $k$ choose a witness
$(y_k,T_k)\in C$ for the level-$k$ node. The witnesses converge to $(y,T)$,
where $T$ has code $z$: for every fixed number of coordinates the witnesses
agree with $(y,z)$ once $k$ is large. Since $C$ and $\Tr$ are closed,
$(y,T)\in C$. For every $j$, the node $b\upharpoonright j$ belongs to $T_k$
for all $k\geq j$, and hence belongs to $T$. Thus $T\in\mathcal T$ has a
branch, a contradiction. Therefore $U$ is well-founded.

For each $(y,T)\in C$, the assignment
\[
 s\longmapsto
 (s,y\upharpoonright |s|,\operatorname{code}(T)\upharpoonright |s|)
\]
embeds the extension tree $T$ into $U$, taking children to children. Induction
on node ranks gives $\rk(T)\leq\rk(U)$. A well-founded countable tree has
countable rank. Taking $\beta=\rk(U)+1$ gives a strict bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform support bound]
\label{pma:bor:thm:bounded}
Let $L$ be countable, $Y$ standard Borel, and
$c:Y\to\R^L$ Borel. Suppose every $\supp(c(y))$ is well-ordered in $L$.
Then there is $\alpha<\omega_1$ such that
\[
 \otp(\supp(c(y)))<\alpha\qquad\text{for every }y\in Y.
\]
The same conclusion holds for reverse well-ordered supports, with the reverse
support order used to compute the type.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $S\subseteq L$, form the descending-sequence tree $D_S$. Its root is the
empty sequence, and its other nodes are finite strictly decreasing sequences
of members of $S$. The map $S\mapsto D_S$ is continuous on binary support
codes; the composite $y\mapsto D_{\supp(c(y))}$ is Borel, since each nonzero
coefficient test is Borel. Its image is an analytic family of well-founded
trees. Apply Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:boundedtrees}.

For a well-ordered $S$ of type $\xi$, the root rank of $D_S$ is exactly $\xi$.
Indeed, the rank of a node ending in $s\in S$ is the order type of the
initial segment of $S$ below $s$; induction verifies the recursion. The root
rank is the supremum of these ranks plus one, namely $\xi$.
Reversing $L$ proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{pma:bor:cor:rankversion}
For a countable linear order $L$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $L$ is scattered;
\item the order types of all well-ordered subsets of $L$ have a common
countable upper bound;
\item $\WO(L)$ is analytic;
\item $\WO(L)$ is Borel.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:dichotomy} gives (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(iv) and excludes
(iii) in the nonscattered case. If (iv) holds, apply
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:bounded} to the identity binary-coefficient coding of
$\WO(L)$ to obtain (ii). If $L$ is nonscattered, it contains $\Q$, and every
countable ordinal embeds in $\Q$ by the finite-stage rational insertion
construction used in Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:KB}. Its well-ordered suborders therefore
have unbounded countable types, contradicting (ii).
\end{proof}

The bound is uniform over the entire parameter space, even if it is
uncountable. It is not asserted to be computable from an arbitrary code for
the Borel map. In particular, this is a boldface descriptive theorem, not an
unqualified statement that every effective system has a computable ordinal
bound.

\section{The obstruction already lies in one omnific interval}
\label{pma:bor:sec:local}

Return to the $X$-convention. Let $I=\Q\cap(1/3,2/3)$ and consider the compact
space of candidate coefficient arrays
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:localcodes}
 \mathcal C=\left\{X+\sum_{q\in I}\varepsilon_qX^q:
                   \varepsilon_q\in\{0,1\}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here the displayed sums are only \emph{candidate codes}. A member of
$\mathcal C$ denotes a Hahn or surreal normal form only when its nonzero
support is reverse well-ordered.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-interval localization]
\label{pma:bor:thm:local}
The valid-code subset $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{valid}}$ of
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:localcodes} is $\boldsymbol\Pi^1_1$-complete. Every valid value lies
in the omnific interval $[X,2X)$. The reverse support order types in this slice
are unbounded in $\omega_1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take the fixed increasing Kleene--Brouwer embedding $\muKB$ from
Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:KB} and put
\[
 e(s)=\frac23-\frac{\muKB(s)}3\in I.
\]
This reverses the Kleene--Brouwer order. Define the candidate code
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:zT}
 z_T=X+\sum_{s\in T}X^{e(s)}.
\end{equation}
The map $T\mapsto z_T$ is continuous in the coefficient product topology.
The support below the leading exponent $1$ is reverse well-ordered exactly
when $\KB(T)$ is well-ordered, exactly when $T\in\WF$. Appending the
single larger exponent $1$ changes none of these equivalences.
Coanalytic membership follows from the descending-sequence characterization
applied to the reverse exponent order.

For every valid code, the lower part is nonnegative because all its nonzero
coefficients are positive, while $2X-z_T$ has greatest exponent $1$ and
coefficient $1$. Hence $X\leq z_T<2X$; the lower equality occurs when the
lower support is empty.

Every countable ordinal $\xi$ embeds into $I$ in its reverse order, by the
same rational-insertion principle. Taking that binary support gives total
reverse support type $1+\xi$. These types are unbounded below $\omega_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{pma:bor:cor:localobservable}
There is no coefficient-observable standard Borel parametrization onto
$\mathcal C_{\mathrm{valid}}$, even with repeated names. More generally,
no such parametrization can cover a family whose support types are
unbounded in $\omega_1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The general statement is the contrapositive of
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:bounded}. Apply it to Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:local}.
\end{proof}

This localization removes several possible explanations for the obstruction.
It is not due to large coefficients, an unbounded range of leading degrees,
or a need to represent the proper class of all surreals. The leading monomial
is fixed, the other coefficients are bits, the remaining exponents lie in one
fixed rational band, and all actual values lie in one bounded omnific interval.

\begin{remark}[Relation to ProveIt's existing lower bound]
The use of well-founded trees, and the fact that general support validation
is coanalytic-hard, are already recorded in the omnific-notations report
\cite{RepoNotations}. The present localization is included as an explicit
corollary with a fixed coefficient cylinder. The more substantial extensions
are the scattered/nonscattered classification and its observable-field and
uniform-family formulations. None of these descriptions establishes global
priority for a result outside the inspected material.
\end{remark}

\noindent\writetag{} \Cref{pma:bor:thm:local} and the collection's
\replabel{onot:neg:thm:pionesone} are the same phenomenon in two slices, as
the remark above says: there, lightface completeness for recursive trees,
with decidable dyadic supports in $[1,2)$ and coefficient one; here, boldface
completeness with the fixed leading monomial $X$, bits on the band
$(1/3,2/3)$ and values in $[X,2X)$. The unboundedness of the support types and
\cref{pma:bor:cor:localobservable} are not in the omnific-notations report.

\section{A real closed Borel subfield and its finite omnific integer part}
\label{pma:bor:sec:Puiseux}

The preceding obstructions do not make exact non-Archimedean algebra
impossible. A particularly useful positive model separates finite support,
bounded-denominator infinite support, and arbitrary Hahn support.

Let
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:Puiseux}
 \Pui=\bigcup_{n\geq1}\R((t^{1/n})),\qquad X=t^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The union is a field: any two denominators have a common multiple.
Newton--Puiseux says that the corresponding complex field
$\bigcup_n\mathbb C((t^{1/n}))$ is algebraically closed
\cite{Puiseux,ParanVo}. It is exactly $\Pui(i)$, and $\Pui$ is ordered by the
leading coefficient. Hence $\Pui$ is real closed.

Define
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:A}
 \A=\left\{n+\sum_{q\in S}a_qX^q:
 n\in\Z,\ S\subseteq\Q_{>0}\text{ finite},\ a_q\in\R\right\}.
\end{equation}
Zero coefficients may be removed to obtain a unique finite normal form.
The notation $\Z\oplus\bigoplus_{q>0}\R X^q$ describes an additive direct
sum. It does not mean that $\A$ is an $\R$-algebra: its constant subring is
$\Z$, not $\R$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact principal-part floor]
\label{pma:bor:lem:floor}
The ring $\A$ is an integer part of $\Pui$. More explicitly, write uniquely
\[
 f=p+r+\varepsilon,
\]
where $p$ contains the positive powers of $X$, $r\in\R$ is the constant
term, and $\varepsilon$ has only negative powers of $X$. Then $p$ is finite,
$\varepsilon$ is infinitesimal, and the unique $a\in\A$ with
$a\leq f<a+1$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:floor}
 a=p+\begin{cases}
 \lfloor r\rfloor-1,&r\in\Z\text{ and }\varepsilon<0,\\
 \lfloor r\rfloor,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $f\in\R((t^{1/n}))$, its exponents are bounded below in the lattice
$\frac1n\Z$. Only finitely many are negative in the $t$-convention, so the
positive-$X$ principal part $p$ is finite. Every remaining nonconstant term
has negative $X$-exponent; if the tail is nonzero, its greatest exponent is
negative, so it is smaller in absolute value than every positive real.

When $r\notin\Z$, the fractional part $r-\lfloor r\rfloor$ lies strictly
between $0$ and $1$, and adding an infinitesimal preserves both inequalities.
When $r\in\Z$ and $\varepsilon\geq0$, the remainder is in $[0,1)$.
When $r\in\Z$ and $\varepsilon<0$, subtracting one from the proposed floor
makes the remainder $1+\varepsilon$, which also lies in $[0,1)$.
This proves the inequalities.

The ring is discretely ordered. A positive element with nonempty positive
support exceeds every real, in particular $1$; a positive constant is an
ordinary positive integer. Thus $1$ is its least positive element, and two
distinct elements of $\A$ cannot both satisfy the floor inequalities.
\end{proof}

The boundary correction in \eqref{pma:bor:eq:floor} is essential. For example,
$\lfloor-X^{-1}\rfloor_{\A}=-1$, not $0$. This correction is also explicitly
recorded in ProveIt's current omnific-floor description \cite{RepoMain}; it is
not a new correction discovered by this report.

\noindent\writetag{} \Cref{pma:bor:lem:floor} is the collection's
\replabel{odg:thm:floor} (\fn{omnific-diophantine-geometry} report) read in
the Puiseux field. Under 03's identification of $X$ with $\omega$
(\cref{pma:bor:sec:conventions}), $\Pui$ is a subfield of $\No$; $p$, $r$ and
$\varepsilon$ are the positive-growth part, the real constant and the
infinitesimal tail of \replabel{odg:eq:floor}; and $\Oz\cap\Pui=\A$, because
the positive-growth part of a Puiseux series is finite. Formula
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:floor} is therefore the same formula, and its
negative-infinitesimal correction is the case $r\in\Z$, $\varepsilon<0$ there.
\replabel{odg:thm:floor} is proved in Lean for every surreal
(\fn{Algebra/SurrealNumbers/Surreal/Foundations/OmnificFloor.lean}:
\texttt{omnificFloor\_spec}, \texttt{existsUnique\_omnific\_integerPart},
\texttt{omnificFloor\_of\_integer\_negative\_tail}; row ``Full
\texttt{odg:thm:floor}'' of \fn{docs/FORMALIZATION.md}). The Lean development
does not mention $\Pui$ or $\A$, so the lemma as stated here, with the
finiteness of $p$ and the integer-part property of $\A$ in $\Pui$, is not
formalized. Source~03 credits the Surreal README for the correction.

\begin{theorem}
\label{pma:bor:thm:arithmetic}
The nonnegative cone $\PP$ is an uncountable model of Robinson arithmetic
plus open induction in the ordered semiring language.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A discretely ordered ring gives the elementary semiring and predecessor
axioms on its nonnegative cone. In particular, if $x>0$ then $x-1\geq0$.
Uncountability follows already from the distinct elements $aX$, $a>0$ real.

We prove open induction directly, using the familiar integer-part direction
of Shepherdson's theorem \cite{Shepherdson,Glivicky}. Let $W\subseteq\PP$ be
a nonempty set defined by a quantifier-free formula with parameters from
$\A$. Interpret the same formula in the real closed field $\Pui$. Its truth
set is a finite union of intervals and points: the finitely many roots of the
polynomials appearing in the formula partition the line into regions of
constant sign.

Intersect these finitely many cells with $[0,\infty)$ and choose the first
one meeting $\A$. A point cell contributes its own element. For an interval
cell with lower endpoint $b\in\Pui$, the integer-part property gives the least
integer-part element at or above $b$, or the least one strictly above $b$
when the endpoint is excluded. This element is nonnegative; if it were above
the interval, that interval would contain no element of $\A$ at all.
For a cell beginning at $0$, the same argument starts with $0$ or $1$.
Thus $W$ has a least element.

If a quantifier-free induction instance failed while its base and successor
steps held, the nonempty quantifier-free set of counterexamples would have a
least member $m$. It would satisfy $m>0$, so $m-1\in\PP$ would satisfy the
formula. The successor step would then force the formula at $m$, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

\noindent\writetag{} The proof of \cref{pma:bor:thm:arithmetic} is the
Shepherdson route of the collection's \replabel{dsn:cor:openinduction}
(\fn{definable-surreals-and-omnific-integers} report: open induction for the
integer part $I_A$ of a real closed field $K_A$) and of the paragraph citing
Shepherdson's theorem in the subsection ``Sums of squares and induction'' of
the \fn{omnific-diophantine-geometry} report (open induction for the
omnific integers of a divisible Hahn workspace). Here it is applied to $\A$
inside the real closed field $\Pui$; uncountability and the semiring axioms
are 03's. \replabel{dsn:cor:openinduction} is pending in
\fn{docs/FORMALIZATION.md}. As 03 says, $\PP$ is a Shepherdson-type model, not
a new model of arithmetic.

\subsection{Why the Puiseux field itself has Borel codes}

In the ambient coefficient space $\R^\Q$, the field \eqref{pma:bor:eq:Puiseux} is
exactly the union, over $n\geq1$ and $k\in\Z$, of the closed sets
\[
 \{a:a_q=0\text{ whenever }q\notin\tfrac1n\Z
                  \text{ or }q<k/n\}.
\]
It is therefore $F_\sigma$ and standard Borel. The convolution and unique
graph arguments of Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:field} make its field operations Borel.
Every such support has increasing order type at most $\omega$.

This gives a useful three-way contrast. Finite principal parts have finite
support; the real closed Puiseux field has a uniform support bound $\omega$;
the full rational-exponent Hahn field has supports of unbounded countable
type. Real closedness alone does not prevent a Borel coefficient presentation.
Full arbitrary-support closure is what makes
Corollary~\ref{pma:bor:cor:rootjump} apply.

\section{A Polish finite-normal-form presentation}
\label{pma:bor:sec:topology}

\subsection{The topology and its Borel structure}

Write an element of $\PP$ uniquely as $(S,n,(a_q)_{q\in S})$, where all
$a_q\ne0$. If $S\ne\varnothing$, positivity means
$a_{\max S}>0$ and the integer $n$ is unrestricted. If $S=\varnothing$,
we require $n\geq0$.

For finite nonempty $S\subseteq\Q_{>0}$ and $n\in\Z$, let
\[
 U_{S,n}=\{a\in(\R\setminus\{0\})^S:a_{\max S}>0\}.
\]
For $S=\varnothing$, let $U_{\varnothing,n}$ be a singleton when $n\geq0$.
Put on $\PP$ the topological disjoint union
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:tau}
 (\PP,\tau_{\FS})=\coprod_{S,n}U_{S,n}.
\end{equation}
Support and integer constant are locally constant in this topology.

\begin{proposition}
\label{pma:bor:prop:Polish}
The topology $\tau_{\FS}$ is Polish. Its Borel sets are exactly the Borel
sets obtained from the canonical finite-support coefficient coding.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each nonsingleton component is open in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space,
and hence Polish. There are countably many components. A countable topological
disjoint union of Polish spaces is Polish: choose bounded complete metrics
on the components and put distance $2$ between distinct components. Taking the
union of countable dense sets gives separability.

Every coefficient map is continuous for \eqref{pma:bor:eq:tau}. Conversely, an exact
support condition consists of nonzero tests at finitely many coordinates and
zero tests at countably many others, so is Borel in the coefficient product.
On each such Borel piece the coefficient coordinates give exactly the usual
finite-dimensional Borel structure. Since there are countably many pieces,
the two Borel structures agree.
\end{proof}

This is not the order topology. The order topology on a discretely ordered
uncountable arithmetic domain is discrete and therefore not separable. Nor is
$\tau_{\FS}$ the ambient coefficient subspace topology, where convergence
need not make the exact support eventually constant.
The distinction is responsible for the cancellation discontinuities below.

\subsection{A general support-normalization lemma}

\begin{lemma}
\label{pma:bor:lem:polynormalize}
Let $U\subseteq\R^d$ be nonempty and open, and let
$(p_j)_{j\in J}$ be a finite family of real polynomials. Define a map whose
output is the finite list of nonzero coordinates $p_j(u)$, with the target
topologized as a disjoint union by exact nonzero support. Let
$E=\{j:p_j\not\equiv0\}$. Its discontinuity set is exactly
\[
 \mathcal Z=\bigcup_{j\in E}\{u\in U:p_j(u)=0\}.
\]
This set is closed and nowhere dense in $U$. The map is continuous on
countably many closed subsets covering $U$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Outside $\mathcal Z$, all nonidentically-zero coordinates stay nonzero on a sufficiently
small neighborhood, while the others are always zero. The output support is
therefore constant and the coordinate formulas are continuous.

At $u\in \mathcal Z$, choose $j\in E$ with $p_j(u)=0$. A nonzero real polynomial
cannot vanish on a nonempty open set. Thus arbitrarily near $u$ there are
points where this coordinate is nonzero. Their output support differs from
the support at $u$, so their images lie outside the clopen target component
containing the image of $u$. Continuity fails. This also proves that the
finite union of zero sets is nowhere dense; closedness is immediate.

For $Z\subseteq E$ and integer $k\geq1$, use the closed set
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:closedpieces}
 F_{Z,k}=\{u\in U:p_j(u)=0\ (j\in Z),\quad
                        |p_j(u)|\geq1/k\ (j\in E\setminus Z)\}.
\end{equation}
These sets cover $U$: at any point choose the actual zero set and then a
large enough $k$ for the finitely many remaining coordinates. On each piece,
the output support is constant and the map is continuous.
For a zero-dimensional component consisting of one point, the same conclusion
holds with empty discontinuity set.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact arithmetic cancellation loci}

Fix an input component $U_{S,n}\times U_{T,m}$. Extend the variable coefficient
families by $a_q=0$ for $q\notin S$ and $b_q=0$ for $q\notin T$.
For addition, the output coefficient at $r>0$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:pluscoeff}
 A_r=a_r+b_r,
\end{equation}
and its constant is $n+m$. For multiplication, the output constant is $nm$
and the coefficient at $r>0$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{pma:bor:eq:timescoeff}
 M_r=n b_r+m a_r+\sum_{p+q=r}a_p b_q.
\end{equation}
Only finitely many exponents need to be considered:
$S\cup T$ for addition and $S\cup T\cup(S+T)$ for multiplication.

For multiplication define
\[
 E_{S,T,n,m}=\{r\in S\cup T\cup(S+T):M_r\not\equiv0\},
\]
where identity means polynomial identity in the independent input coefficient
variables after fixing the integers $n,m$. This removes absent terms and
zero-factor components correctly.

\begin{theorem}[Exact cancellation theorem]
\label{pma:bor:thm:cancellation}
On the Polish presentation \eqref{pma:bor:eq:tau}, addition and multiplication are
Borel. Their discontinuity sets are given componentwise by
\begin{align}
 D_+\cap(U_{S,n}\times U_{T,m})
   &=\bigcup_{r\in S\cap T}\{a_r+b_r=0\},\label{pma:bor:eq:Dplus}\\
 D_\times\cap(U_{S,n}\times U_{T,m})
   &=\bigcup_{r\in E_{S,T,n,m}}\{M_r=0\}.
   \label{pma:bor:eq:Dtimes}
\end{align}
Globally, both sets are closed and nowhere dense. Both operations are
continuous on countably many closed pieces. In particular, both are
continuous at every point of the common dense open set
$\PP^2\setminus(D_+\cup D_\times)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On an input component, the actual normal form is obtained from
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:pluscoeff} or \eqref{pma:bor:eq:timescoeff} by deleting the zero
coefficients. The integer output constant is fixed. Apply
Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:polynormalize}. For addition, a coefficient occurring in only
one input support is constrained to be nonzero, so the possible zero sets
are precisely those in \eqref{pma:bor:eq:Dplus}. For multiplication the expected
polynomial support is exactly $E_{S,T,n,m}$.

Every input component is clopen, and these components partition the domain.
A set whose intersection with each component is closed is globally closed;
a set whose intersection with each nonempty open component is nowhere dense
has empty interior and, when closed, is nowhere dense globally. This gives the
assertions about $D_+$ and $D_\times$.

The pieces \eqref{pma:bor:eq:closedpieces} are closed relative to a clopen component,
hence closed in the full domain. Taking them over countably many components,
zero patterns, and integers $k$ gives countable closed covers for both
operations. To see Borelness directly, the inverse image of an open set is a
countable union of sets open relative to these closed pieces. Such sets are
Borel (indeed $F_\sigma$ in a metric space).
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Addition: cancellation below the leading term]
For real $\delta$ sufficiently near $0$, set
\[
 x=X^2+X,\qquad y_\delta=X^2+(-1+\delta)X.
\]
All these elements are positive and remain in fixed input components. But
\[
 x+y_\delta=2X^2+\delta X.
\]
At $\delta=0$ the output support loses exponent $1$, so the outputs do not
converge to $2X^2$ in $\tau_{\FS}$. Leading coefficients of positive inputs
cannot cancel each other; the lower coefficient is what fails here.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Multiplication: a vanished middle coefficient]
Let $x=X+1$ and $y_\delta=(1+\delta)X-1$. Near $\delta=0$ both inputs are
positive, their supports and integer constants are fixed, and
\[
 xy_\delta=(1+\delta)X^2+\delta X-1.
\]
The middle term disappears at $\delta=0$. Thus $(X+1,X-1)\in D_\times$.
Multiplication by $0$, on the other hand, is constant and continuous; this
explains the exclusion of identically zero coefficient polynomials.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[A measurability qualification]
Countable closed-piece continuity is the statement proved here. We do not
identify it without further hypotheses with the Baire-one class of maps into
this disconnected target. Nor does the dense-open conclusion say that an
algebraic cancellation can be ignored by exact computation: deleting a single
zero coefficient changes the canonical component.
\end{remark}

\section{Glazer's obstruction survives every compatible Polish recoding}
\label{pma:bor:sec:glazer}

We use precisely the following published result.
\begin{theorem}[Glazer, Corollary 1 of \cite{Glazer}]
\label{pma:bor:thm:Glazerinput}
There is no model of Robinson arithmetic plus $\IOpen$ on an uncountable
Polish space in which addition is continuous and multiplication is Borel.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[No removal of the addition obstruction]
\label{pma:bor:thm:glazer}
No Polish topology on the set $\PP$ with the same Borel sets as
$\tau_{\FS}$ makes addition continuous. More generally, there is no Polish
presentation Borel-isomorphic to the canonical one in which transported
addition is continuous.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:arithmetic}, the algebraic structure is an uncountable
model of the required arithmetic. By Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:cancellation},
multiplication is Borel in the canonical Borel structure. Changing the Polish
topology without changing its Borel sets leaves multiplication Borel on the
product; finite products of standard Borel spaces have the same product
Borel structure. Continuous addition would contradict
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:Glazerinput}. Transporting the operations through a Borel
isomorphism changes none of these hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
The distinction between global continuity and continuity off a closed nowhere
dense set is genuine for this explicit model: the latter is attained for both
operations, while the former is impossible for addition under any
Borel-compatible Polish presentation.
\end{corollary}

This is a sharp distinction between an empty discontinuity set and an explicit
closed nowhere dense one. It is not a proof that our particular zero loci have
minimal cardinality, minimal dimension, or a minimal descriptive rank among
all recodings.

Glazer's Theorem 2 also excludes continuous multiplication for uncountable
Borel models of the stronger theory
$\mathsf Q+I\Delta_0+\mathsf{Exp}$ \cite{Glazer}. We have proved only open
induction for $\PP$. The multiplication obstruction from that stronger
result is therefore \emph{not} transferred to every compatible topology on
$\PP$. Multiplication fails to be continuous in our displayed topology by
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:cancellation}; whether a different compatible Polish topology
can make multiplication continuous remains a separate question here.

\section{Where the two parts meet \mergetag}\label{pma:sec:boundary}

The real model of \cref{pma:part:pr} is one stratum of the cone $\PP$ of
\cref{pma:bor:sec:topology}. Read $\omega$ as $X$ and let
\[
 \iota:\MR\longrightarrow\A,\qquad \iota(r,n)=rX+n,
\]
which is 05's map $\iota_{\R}$ of \cref{pma:pr:thm:omnific} in 03's notation.
Put
\[
 C_{\R}=\{a\in\PP:\supp a\subseteq\{1\}\}
 =\bigcup_{n\in\Z}U_{\{1\},n}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{n\ge0}U_{\varnothing,n}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[The real model as a stratum \mergetag]\label{pma:prop:stratum}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\iota$ is an injective ordered additive map onto $C_{\R}$, fixing $0$
and $1$. The set $C_{\R}$ is clopen in $(\PP,\tau_{\FS})$ and closed under
addition, but not under multiplication.
\item On $\{(r,n)\in\MR:r>0\}$, $\iota$ is a homeomorphism onto
$\bigcup_nU_{\{1\},n}$, for the topology of $\MR$.
\item At the standard numerals the two topologies differ: every ordinary
integer $n\ge0$ is isolated in $\tau_{\FS}$, but $(0,n)$ is not isolated in
$\MR$. Thus $\tau_{\FS}$ induces on $C_{\R}$ a topology strictly finer than
the one transported from $\MR$.
\item Addition is continuous on $(C_{\R},\tau_{\FS})$. Consequently
$(C_{\R},\tau_{\FS})$ is another uncountable, locally compact Polish model of
$\Th(\N;0,1,+,<)$ with continuous addition; it is not perfect, its isolated
points being exactly the standard numerals.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) An element of $\A$ with support in $\{1\}$ is $n+aX$; by
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:tau} it lies in $\PP$ when $a>0$, or $a=0$ and $n\ge0$. These
are exactly the values of $\iota$. Addition is coordinatewise and the order is
decided by the leading coefficient, as in \eqref{pma:pr:eq:lex}. The set
$C_{\R}$ is a union of components of the topological sum
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:tau}, hence open, and its complement is the union of the
other components, hence also open. Finally $X\cdot X=X^2\notin C_{\R}$.

(ii) Both $U_{\{1\},n}$ and the part $r>0$ of the slice of integer coordinate
$n$ in $\MR$ are the open ray $(0,\infty)$ with its Euclidean topology, and
$\iota$ is the identity on the coefficient.

(iii) $U_{\varnothing,n}$ is a singleton component of \eqref{pma:bor:eq:tau}.
In $\MR$, $(1/j,n)\to(0,n)$ by \eqref{pma:pr:eq:rays}.

(iv) On a component $U_{S,n}\times U_{T,m}$ with $S,T\subseteq\{1\}$,
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:Dplus} is a union over $r\in S\cap T\subseteq\{1\}$ of the
sets $\{a_1+b_1=0\}$, which are empty because both leading coefficients are
positive. So $C_{\R}^2$ does not meet $D_+$, and addition maps $C_{\R}^2$ into
$C_{\R}$. A clopen subspace of a Polish space is Polish, the components are
points and open rays, and the algebraic structure is that of $\MR$, so
\cref{pma:pr:cor:fullpresburger} applies.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The boundary.} Glazer's Corollary~1 concerns a model of
$Q+I\mathrm{Open}$ with Borel multiplication. The ring cone $\PP$ is such a
model (\cref{pma:bor:thm:arithmetic,pma:bor:thm:cancellation}), so no
Borel-compatible Polish topology on $\PP$ makes its addition continuous
(\cref{pma:bor:thm:glazer}). The stratum $C_{\R}$ is not such a model: as an
additive monoid it is $\MR$, which carries no unital semiring multiplication at
all (\cref{pma:pr:cor:no_semiring_models}), and the multiplication of $\PP$
leaves it. On $C_{\R}$ addition is continuous for $\tau_{\FS}$ itself; the
discontinuities of addition on $\PP$ lie on the cancellation loci
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:Dplus}, which need two inputs whose supports share an
exponent below their leading ones, and so lie outside $C_{\R}^2$. Thus
\cref{pma:part:pr} and \cref{pma:bor:thm:glazer} delimit, inside one ring,
where Corollary~1 applies: the additive stratum admits continuous addition
for both topologies, while the ring, with its Borel multiplication and open
induction, admits it for none.

\paragraph{The Baire model.} The image $\iota_{\mathrm B}(\MB)$ of
\cref{pma:pr:thm:omnific} lies in 03's rational Hahn ring $\HQ$ of
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:H}: its supports are contained in
$\{1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\dots\}\cup\{0\}$ and have reverse order type at most
$\omega+1$. Its elements with infinitely many nonzero coefficients lie neither
in $\A$ nor in the Puiseux field $\Pui$, whose denominators are bounded. Each
coefficient of $\iota_{\mathrm B}(a,n)$ is $a_j$, $n$ or $0$, a continuous
function on $\MB$, so the composite of 05's homeomorphism
$h:\Baire\to\MB$ (\cref{pma:pr:thm:baire}) with $\iota_{\mathrm B}$ is a
coefficient-observable standard Borel parametrization of
$\iota_{\mathrm B}(\MB)$ in the sense of \cref{pma:bor:def:observable}. This is
consistent with \cref{pma:bor:thm:bounded}, whose bound can be taken to be
$\omega+2$ here, while the full field $\R((t^{\Q}))$ lies on the non-Borel
side of \cref{pma:bor:thm:field}. The Baire model is one of the restricted
support families that \cref{pma:bor:sec:consequences} calls for.

\section{Consequences for exact notation systems and formalization}
\label{pma:bor:sec:consequences}

\subsection{A practical separation of three design goals}

The results separate three tasks that can otherwise be conflated.
\emph{Arithmetic} asks whether operations can be performed on valid inputs.
\emph{Validation} asks whether an arbitrary code is a valid well-based normal
form. \emph{Topological presentation} asks whether the operations respect a
complete separable topology on the names or values. A positive answer to one
is not a positive answer to the others.

In the finite-support model, every code carries an explicit finite list, and
arithmetic is polynomial coefficient arithmetic followed by exact zero tests.
The canonical domain is Polish, the operations are Borel, and their failure
of continuity is exactly the support-changing cancellation calculated above.
No general theorem of computability over arbitrary real coefficients is
claimed: a real coefficient supplied by an unrestricted approximation oracle
need not have decidable equality with zero.

For full Hahn fields on scattered exponent groups, support validation is a
Borel predicate. This is not the same as decidability by a finite algorithm.
The $\boldsymbol\Pi^0_3$ examples already involve quantification over
infinitely many rows. For dense rational exponents the obstruction is stronger:
no standard Borel system of fully observable names covers every valid series.

A useful exact system must therefore limit its admissible support families,
relax coefficient observability, or accept a nonstandard measurable domain.
The Puiseux field demonstrates that a severe-looking support restriction may
still permit real closedness. Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:bounded} says every observable
Borel system necessarily has some uniform countable support bound, even when
that bound is not built into the notation explicitly.

\subsection{No paradoxes are needed}

All main results are in ZFC and concern sets. For countable $\Gamma$, the
ambient coefficient space $\R^\Gamma$ is a set of cardinality continuum.
Its valid-code subset is a set whether or not it is Borel. The rational Hahn
ring $\HQ$ and its finite subring $\A$ are also sets. The construction never
forms a set of all surreal numbers or quantifies over all class maps as a set.

The optional identification with Conway normal forms can be read as a
set-indexed definable embedding into the proper class $\No$, or carried out
inside a sufficiently large set-sized normal-form universe. Standard
Replacement handles the image of a fixed set of codes. No global choice,
large cardinal, or class-sized Polish topology is used.

\subsection{A formalization plan with an explicit dependency boundary}

A Lean development can be organized into independent interfaces rather than
starting from the entire proper-class surreal hierarchy.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Layer \arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Finite algebra and normalization.}
Use finitely supported rational-exponent coefficient maps with an integer
constant. Establish convolution, leading-exponent sign, discreteness, and the
coefficient identities \eqref{pma:bor:eq:pluscoeff}--\eqref{pma:bor:eq:timescoeff}. The exact
cancellation examples are small executable tests at this layer.
\item \textbf{Series and arithmetic.}
Use a set-sized Hahn or Puiseux field, prove the principal-part floor with its
negative-infinitesimal case, and derive quantifier-free induction via the
one-variable sign-cell argument. Newton--Puiseux is a named external dependency
until its real-closedness interface is formally supplied.
\item \textbf{Topological support components.}
Formalize the countable disjoint union, the polynomial normalization lemma,
and the closed-piece covers. None of this requires descriptive ranks or
proper-class surreals.
\item \textbf{Descriptive-set-theoretic structure.}
Formalize the fixed Kleene--Brouwer embedding, Hausdorff-sum support criterion,
analytic boundedness, and observable-field equivalence. The classical
Hausdorff characterization and Glazer theorem must be proved or explicitly
listed as hypotheses; importing them informally is not a checked proof.
\end{enumerate}

This is a proposed architecture, not a claim that these layers have been
implemented or that all required library APIs currently exist. The provided
Python artifact checks finite rational instances, not Lean terms or
infinitary assertions.

\section{Further research questions and concrete next targets}
\label{pma:bor:sec:questions}

The following questions are proposed here. Unless explicitly attributed,
``open'' means unresolved in this report, not certified as absent from the
entire literature.

\begin{question}[Continuous multiplication on the finite model]
\label{pma:bor:q:multiplication}
Does $\PP$ admit a Polish topology with the canonical Borel sets in which
multiplication is continuous?
\end{question}
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:glazer} settles the analogous addition question negatively.
Glazer's stronger multiplication theorem cannot be invoked without establishing
its extra arithmetic hypotheses. A positive solution would isolate a concrete
asymmetry between the two operations; a negative solution should identify an
obstruction valid already for this particular open-induction model.

\begin{question}[How small can the discontinuity set be?]
Among all Borel-compatible Polish presentations of $\PP$, what restrictions
hold on the discontinuity set of addition? Can it be countable? Can it be
contained in a prescribed small-dimensional or otherwise canonical set?
\end{question}
The present model gives a nonempty closed nowhere dense set. Glazer excludes
only its complete disappearance, not every possible reduction in complexity.
This is a natural refinement of the topological theorem that can first be
studied on explicit finite-support components.

\begin{question}[Exact complexity beyond finite lexicographic rank]
For a fixed countable scattered order $L$, compute the least Borel level of
$\WO(L)$ directly from an economical Hausdorff decomposition. Which Wadge
degrees occur among these support predicates?
\end{question}
The qualitative dichotomy is settled by Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:dichotomy}, while
Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:finitelex} shows that simply increasing finite order rank need
not increase Borel level. A satisfactory answer should distinguish ordinal
support capacity from descriptive complexity rather than treating them as
interchangeable ranks.

\begin{question}[Borel, algebraically rich subfields of the rational Hahn field]
Classify Borel subfields $F\subseteq\R((t^\Q))$ containing all rational
monomials and stable under specified truncations or algebraic-root operations.
Which countable support bounds can such fields attain?
\end{question}
The Puiseux field is one real closed example with supports of type at most
$\omega$. The full Hahn field is excluded. An interesting target is a
well-described hierarchy of truncation-closed real closed Borel subfields
between them, together with provably sharp support bounds at each stage.
No claim is made that every ordinal cutoff is preserved by inversion.

\begin{question}[How much coefficient observability is necessary?]
If only a selected family of coefficient combinations or truncations is
required to be Borel, which versions of Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:field} survive?
\end{question}
The current proof uses all countably many coefficient maps to obtain one
Borel map into the full product. This pinpoints a possible escape route and
suggests investigating minimal observation families that still recover the
binary support cylinder in Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:local}.

\begin{question}[Certified support languages]
Construct useful exact languages whose valid supports carry explicit
well-foundedness certificates, and determine their closure under arithmetic,
root extraction, and named truncations.
\end{question}
The repository already contains effective restricted omnific calculi
\cite{RepoNotations}. The present results suggest comparing them by their
Borel parameter spaces and support bounds as well as by decidable equality.
A certificate verifier for a fixed language is not a total validator for all
raw rational-exponent supports; Theorem~\ref{pma:bor:thm:local} forbids conflating
those objectives.

\begin{question}[A minimal arithmetic hypothesis for the presentation barrier]
Which concrete consequences of open induction suffice to rule out continuous
addition in the finite principal-part model or related integer parts?
\end{question}
A proof specialized to finite Puiseux principal parts might replace the full
open-induction interface with explicitly stated root-approximation or
division properties. This would clarify the boundary between algebraic
normal-form obstructions and arithmetic induction.

\begin{question}[Formalized transfer from support theory to arithmetic]
Can the observable Hahn-field criterion and the finite-support cancellation
theorem be formalized as reusable results, with the classical Hausdorff and
Glazer inputs exposed as exact dependency interfaces?
\end{question}
The suggested first milestone is the support-sum criterion
\eqref{pma:bor:eq:Zcriterion} together with the normalization
Lemma~\ref{pma:bor:lem:polynormalize}. Both are modular and elementary enough to be
reviewed independently before the larger descriptive-set-theoretic machinery
is introduced.

\noindent\mergetag{} \Cref{pma:bor:q:multiplication} is the multiplicative
counterpart, on $\PP$, of \cref{pma:pr:q:multiplication}. The question on the
size of the discontinuity set of addition concerns all of $\PP$; on the clopen
stratum $C_{\R}$ of \cref{pma:prop:stratum} that set is empty.

\section{Conclusions of Part II}\label{pma:bor:sec:conclusion}

The intersection with Glazer's research produces a precise hierarchy rather
than a blanket impossibility theorem. Full Hahn support is Borel exactly on
scattered countable exponent orders. Dense alphabets carry a coanalytic-complete
validation problem, and no standard Borel family with observable coefficients
can exhaust supports of unbounded countable type. This obstruction persists
inside one fixed interval of omnific integers.

At the same time, a real closed Puiseux subfield and its finite principal-part
integer part have explicit Borel presentations. The latter even admits a
Polish topology where both arithmetic operations are continuous off closed
nowhere dense sets, with exact polynomial equations describing every failure.
Glazer's theorem makes the remaining addition discontinuity unavoidable under
all compatible Polish recodings. The positive construction and the negative
result constrain one another: Borel arithmetic is attainable, globally
continuous addition is not, and arbitrary well-based support introduces a
separate obstruction before topological continuity can even be asked in the
canonical standard-Borel setting.

\subsection{A negative constant term is compatible with positivity}

In $\MR$, take
\[
 x=(\tfrac32,-17),\qquad y=(\tfrac14,6).
\]
Both are positive, and
\[
 x+y=(\tfrac74,-11).
\]
Division of $x$ by $5$ gives
\[
 q_5(x)=(\tfrac3{10},-4),\qquad r_5(x)=3,
\]
because $-17=5(-4)+3$. The quotient is positive because its real
coordinate is positive. A definition that incorrectly demanded a
nonnegative integer coordinate for every element would break the
predecessor and division axioms.

\subsection{A nonstandard finite-candidate calculation}

Let $L=(\tfrac12,-5)$, $U=(\tfrac23,0)$, and require
\[
 y\equiv(\tfrac7{13},2)\pmod{3G}.
\]
The real coefficient of a congruence parameter is irrelevant because
$\R$ is divisible. Among $L,L+1,L+2$, the candidate $L+1$ has integer
coordinate $-4\equiv2\pmod3$, so it is a witness. Every candidate lies
below $U$, since its leading coefficient is $1/2<2/3$.
No iteration a nonstandard number of times is used: the finite search is
justified by full invariance on $3G$-cosets.

\subsection{A Baire convergence that order cannot detect continuously}

Let $e_j$ be the rational unit vector at coordinate $j$. Then
\[
 (e_j,0)\to(0,0),\qquad (e_j,0)>n\one
 \quad\text{for every fixed standard }n.
\]
The initial coefficients eventually agree with zero to every prescribed
finite length. In omnific notation this sequence is
\[
 \omega,\quad\omega^{1/2},\quad\omega^{1/3},\quad\ldots,
\]
which converges to zero in the \emph{specified coefficient topology},
not in the arithmetic order topology. This is a concrete illustration,
not a claim about an unspecified topology on surreal numbers.

\section{Limitations and non-claims of the sources}\label{pma:app:nonclaims}

Every limitation stated by a source is kept. They are grouped by source; the
items written for the merge come last.

\paragraph{Source 05.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=05.\arabic*]
\item The construction answers Glazer's Question~2 \emph{as written};
independent bibliographic priority has not been established. The literature
search verified the question in the source PDF and checked the arXiv record;
it did not establish the absence of an earlier solution.
\item Classical Presburger elimination, the $\Z$-group facts and elementary
topological-group facts are not claimed as new; the lexicographic algebra is
classical.
\item The manuscript is AI-assisted, not independently refereed and not
Lean-verified; no new Lean or Rocq formalization is included; the proposed
Lean modules are a plan, not existing files.
\item The executable tests are finite instances and stream prefixes; they do
not quantify over arbitrary elements of $\Q^{\N}$, and finite prefixes do not
decide equality of infinite streams.
\item No answer to Glazer's Question~1 is claimed; no uncountable Polish model
of Peano arithmetic; no continuous arithmetic order on these nondiscrete
spaces; no terminating decision of equality of arbitrary infinite sequences
(comparison is approximated with at most one change).
\item The omnific maps are additive and order-preserving, not ring
embeddings; the topologies are specified coefficient topologies, not a
canonical topology on $\Oz$; no topology on a proper class is used; all
constructions are set-sized in ZFC.
\item Neither model admits any unital semiring multiplication with the given
addition and unit; nothing contradicts Glazer's obstruction for stronger
arithmetic, and the no-semiring argument does not replace it.
\item The repository's integer Cooper lemmas are correct on \texttt{Int}; the
existence of the integer eliminator does not certify the theorem for a new
carrier; the original integer theorem need not be weakened or retracted.
\item The questions are proposed directions, not certified as open in the
literature; the user's repository was not modified.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Source 04.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=04.\arabic*]
\item The construction answers Question~2 as printed; historical priority has
not been established; the literature check did not establish that the
construction has never appeared elsewhere.
\item The manuscript is unrefereed; no new theorem is represented as
machine-checked; Lean/Rocq certification is not supplied.
\item The classical $\Z$-group characterization and quantifier elimination
are not claimed as new.
\item The difference between one-step periodicity and residue invariance is a
porting requirement, not a reported bug in the integer proof.
\item Baire coordinates encode the arithmetic structure and are not base
digits; the stream API cannot decide totality of an arbitrary oracle, and
partial, negative or noninteger entries are not supported.
\item The source audit was limited to the named files at its pin; no
repository files were modified and no repository-wide build was performed;
the formalization stages are proposals, not assertions about existing
declarations.
\item The tests are finite, exact and partial; the model-theoretic,
infinite-matrix and topological statements rest on the proofs.
\item No third-party font files or paper copies are included.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Source 03.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=03.\arabic*]
\item A research draft, not refereed and not a Lean proof; global priority
and independent correctness review are not established.
\item The repository's coanalytic support obstruction and the
negative-infinitesimal floor correction are credited, not claimed as new; the
integer-part model is of Shepherdson type, not new; analytic boundedness and
the Hausdorff characterization are classical.
\item The full-Hahn nonpresentation results need observable coefficients and
coverage of the whole field; they do not exclude unobservable Borel
presentations or Borel real closed subfields.
\item Only addition is shown impossible to make continuous; Glazer's
multiplication obstruction (his Theorem~2) is not transferred, since only open
induction is proved for $\PP$; continuous multiplication under another
compatible topology is open.
\item The zero loci are not claimed minimal in cardinality, dimension or
descriptive rank; countable closed-piece continuity is not identified with the
Baire-one class.
\item The dichotomy is per fixed $L$, not about the class of scattered orders,
nor uniform in a code of $L$; the Hausdorff decomposition used need not be
computable; the uniform support bound need not be computable from a code.
\item No general computability over arbitrary real coefficients is claimed.
\item All results are set-sized in ZFC; no class-sized Polish topology, global
choice or large cardinal is used.
\item The Python checks are finite (they do not test well-foundedness of
infinite trees, completeness, or the external theorems); the formalization
plan is proposed only; no repository files were changed and the whole
repository was not audited.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The merge.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=M.\arabic*]
\item The affirmative answer concerns Question~2 as printed. Glazer
speculated the opposite; this report draws no conclusion about his
Question~1 or about any variant of Question~2 with further hypotheses.
\item \Cref{pma:prop:stratum} and the observations of \cref{pma:sec:boundary}
are elementary consequences of the sources' theorems; they are not presented
as new results about Glazer's problem.
\item Citing a collection label does not certify that label: of those cited,
only \replabel{odg:thm:floor} is formalized.
\item Nothing in this report is formalized. The pointer from the formal
project \fn{Logic/PresburgerArithmetic} and the placement in the Surreal
collection confer no formal status.
\end{enumerate}
