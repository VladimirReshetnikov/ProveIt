PROOF AND SCOPE AUDIT
Polish Arithmetic: Multiplication Obstructions and Local Compactness
4 October 2026 UTC

This audit records the claims established in the manuscript and the reasoning
checked in separate AI-assisted mathematical review passes. It is not an
external referee report or a proof-assistant certificate.

A. EXISTING BACKGROUND

The real and Baire coefficient constructions, the basic affirmative answer to
Glazer's Question 2, and the earlier special multiplication obstructions were
already part of the project material. They are not presented as new results.
Presburger quantifier elimination, automatic continuity, the LCA principal
structure theorem, measurable-order obstructions to compact subgroups, and
finite-support ordered-group techniques are classical dependencies.

B. BOREL MULTIPLIER OBSTRUCTION (THEOREM 3.2)

Checked points:
- Positive absolute values of a nonzero null sequence still tend to zero;
  continuity of the arithmetic order is never assumed.
- Borel additive maps become continuous by the full category argument.
- The complete metric is not assumed translation invariant. Each term is
  chosen small at every one of finitely many existing partial sums.
- Binary subseries converge uniformly and define a continuous Cantor map.
- Finite changes yield an exact signed finite-sum identity after limits.
- A finite-block counting argument gives a bound of 2^(-k) for small values.
- Complementation yields probability one half for a Borel event.
- Invariance under finite changes is pointwise, making the event a tail event.
- The zero-one contradiction uses no arithmetic induction in the modeled
  structure. It does use ordinary classical mathematical reasoning.

The probabilistic mechanism is explicitly attributed to Glazer. The article's
packaging in terms of positive endomorphisms is an extension of that mechanism,
not a claim to have independently invented it.

C. ALGEBRAIC CARDINALITY OBSTRUCTION (THEOREM 3.6)

For an uncountable Archimedean subgroup H of a discretely ordered ring, every
positive element of H exceeds every standard integer. Otherwise Archimedean
cofinality would force H into the countable standard integer subgroup.
For fixed h > 0 in H, multiplication gives the additive injection

  a -> a h + H : H -> (R,+)/H.

The proof checks that a h dominates all of H whenever a > 0 belongs to H.
Negation handles a < 0. Convexity of H is not assumed.

D. LOCAL COMPACTNESS CLASSIFICATION (THEOREMS 4.5 AND 5.1)

Checked points:
- A compact cone-neighborhood K reflects to the ambient compact neighborhood
  K union (-K). Thus local compactness is deduced, not initially assumed.
- Haar measure contradicts any nontrivial compact ordered subgroup.
- The classical principal structure theorem supplies an open R^n component.
- The rational-hull splitting works even when the whole group is not divisible.
- Countably many open cosets give the countable discrete complementary group.
- Local compactness of the cone forces a closed total cone in R^n, and the
  connected-sphere argument forces n <= 1.
- Borel orders on the real component are usual or reversed; each coset section
  is a line, an open ray, a closed ray, or empty.
- A least positive element supplies infinitely many distinct closed-ray and
  open-ray components through its standard integer translates.

The open real component need not be convex in the arithmetic order.
Local compactness of a coefficient group alone is insufficient: Euclidean R^2
with lexicographic order has a non-locally-compact cone at zero.
The theorem covers inherited group topologies, not all Polish monoid topologies.

E. COMPACT-OPEN ZERO OBSTRUCTION (THEOREM 5.4)

A finite-intersection argument shows that a nonempty compact subsemigroup of
a Hausdorff cancellative commutative positive monoid must be {0}. Uniformity
over a compact open neighborhood then forces zero to be isolated. The conclusion
does not rule out every zero-dimensional locally compact Presburger model.

F. ELEMENTARY ARITHMETIC BOUNDARY (THEOREMS 6.1 AND 6.2)

Let H be the open real identity component and let C consist of elements smaller
in absolute value than every positive element of H. Checked points:
- C is a convex subgroup; addition uses comparison with h/2.
- C intersects H trivially, so it injects into the countable quotient G/H.
- Its discrete topology follows from openness of H.
- Closed-ray endpoints are exactly the nonnegative elements of C.
- Division with standard remainder stays in C by convexity.
- Purity preserves congruence predicates; Presburger quantifier elimination
  makes C's positive cone an elementary initial submodel.
- Conversely, R lex (N-N), with the second factor discrete, realizes every
  countable Presburger model N as the entire manifold boundary.

The manifold boundary is distinct from the boundary inside an ambient group.

G. EXACT CONTINUUM FAMILY (THEOREM 7.1)

The alternating finite-condensation classes of the countable order L_S recover
every bit of S. Finite-support rational groups recover L_S through their ordered
Archimedean classes. The appended real and integer classes are intrinsically
the final two classes, so there is no absorption ambiguity. Monoid isomorphisms
extend uniquely to ordered difference-group isomorphisms.

The upper bound counts continuous binary operations through their restriction
to a countable dense subset. The displayed continuum family has standard N as
its boundary in every case.

H. ABSOLUTE MULTIPLICATION BARRIER (THEOREM 8.1)

The open real subgroup has countable index and is Archimedean. Any semiring
multiplication extends algebraically to the difference group. Its positive
products are strictly positive because a,b >= 1 implies a b >= 1.
The cardinality theorem gives the contradiction. No measurability or
commutativity of the multiplication is used.

I. OMNIFIC EMBEDDING (THEOREM 8.3)

An order-reversing embedding q:L -> Q intersect (1,2) yields finite normal forms

  sum_l v(l) omega^(q(l)) + r omega + k.

Largest-exponent comparison agrees with the ordered-group sign. Normal-form
uniqueness gives injectivity. The construction is set-sized and additive.
The coefficient topology is transported explicitly; it is not the native
discrete order topology. The usual product omega squared leaves the image,
and the absolute obstruction excludes every alternative semiring product.

J. SHARPNESS AND LIMITS

The degree-stratum topology on the positive cone of Z + t R[t] is locally
compact and Polish, with continuous addition and multiplication and full
Presburger additive theory. It fails open induction with multiplication via
the formula x^2 < t. Its topology cannot extend to a Polish additive-group
topology. This construction shows why the inherited-group hypothesis cannot
be removed from the stated multiplication and carrier classifications.

The usual integers with the 2-adic topology show why complete metrizability
matters. The ordinary real field shows why a least positive element matters.
