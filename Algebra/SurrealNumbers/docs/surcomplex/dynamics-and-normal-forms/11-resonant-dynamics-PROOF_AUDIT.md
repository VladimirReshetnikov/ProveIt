# Proof audit and verification boundary

This file is an internal proof-obligation record, not an independent referee
report. No machine-checked proof of the new theorems is included.

## Imported inputs

1. Neumann's positive-support lemma, including finite word representations at
   each exponent (Marker, Lemma 2.36).
2. Algebraic closedness of C((t^Gamma)) for divisible ordered Gamma
   (Poonen, Corollary 4).
3. Standard Conway normal forms for the class-level interpretation.
4. Elementary field theory: unique extension of a derivation to finite
   characteristic-zero algebraic extensions and the norm product formula.

## Sensitive proof steps

### Support control (Section 3)

The coefficients are not merely bounded below in valuation. They lie in one
ring A_S whose supports belong to the finite-sum monoid generated by a fixed
positive well-ordered set. Neumann's finite-word statement bounds the number
of powers of an infinitesimal that can contribute at one Hahn exponent.
Formal composition is rearranged only after this finiteness is established.
Example 3.6 gives an actual infinite-collision counterexample to the weaker
“integral coefficients suffice” assertion.

### Resonant cancellation (Theorem 4.2)

Equivariance removes all degrees except 1 modulo m. At each remaining degree,
the numerator of the formal recursion has an actual factor d. The denominator
is d times a polynomial in d with nonzero constant coefficient -km. Division
therefore takes place by a unit within the same A_S after cancelling d.
The same support ring also contains the formal inverse coefficients.

### All-period exclusion (Sections 5 and 8)

The polynomial T_n is constructed by a divided-difference recursion, avoiding
any division by a nonunit leading coefficient. When psi reduces to the
identity, T_n reduces to n as a polynomial. In the less restrictive integral
case, only its constant term is known to reduce to n, which is enough at an
infinitesimal argument but not at an arbitrary finite unit. The two domains
are kept separate.

### Radius-shell count (Theorem 5.4)

Factoring a finite polynomial over an algebraically closed Hahn field and
normalizing each factor by its root valuation gives the count from the
residue degree without assuming a rank-one Newton polygon. Constant term 1
excludes positive-valuation roots. Repeated roots are counted with algebraic
multiplicity; unique lifting is claimed only for simple residue roots.
A periodic point obstructs any injective conjugacy on a larger centered ball,
not necessarily pointwise summability of the original series at every shell
point.

### Boundary vector field (Theorem 6.1)

Taylor expansion is a formal coefficientwise identity with finitely many
contributions in each ordinary variable degree. It is divided by d before
coefficientwise reduction. Integral normalized coefficients are already
proved. The resulting ordinary formal equation is not an evaluation on the
radius shell.

### Transcendence (Theorem 6.4)

The norm of an algebraic logarithmic-derivative solution has rational
logarithmic derivative, giving simple poles and rational residues. The
converse for h_0 follows from an explicit finite product of rational powers.
For the implication upstairs, a finite polynomial relation is rescaled to
have integral coefficients and a nonzero reduction. This prevents the false
argument of reducing a relation that becomes identically zero.
No converse from algebraic h_0 to algebraic H is claimed.

### Shell realization and omnific presentation (Sections 9 and 10)

The all-infinitesimal-period theorem, not just substitution at selected roots,
excludes extra infinitesimal cycles. A finite-product coefficient bound gives
one radius inequality; the known outermost-valued periodic root gives the
other, so cancellation among coefficients causes no gap.
The reciprocal construction excludes the projective fixed point infinity
from its list of finite points. A periodic orbit cannot visit that fixed
point and return to another point. Clearing all coefficient exponents by
L = 1 + m sum(rho_l) gives positive growth support in every coefficient.
This is a rational-map presentation, not a claim of global ring preservation.

### Surcomplex class transfer

Every individual argument takes place in a set-sized divisible Hahn workspace.
New evaluation points require enlargement, which is explicitly permitted.
Formal-recursion uniqueness and coefficientwise strong summation guarantee
compatibility. No proper-class sum or fixed workspace containing all
surcomplex points is used.

## Mechanical evidence

The recorded finite run passed 1,863 exact assertions in six groups:

- 173 formal conjugacy and inverse assertions;
- 107 parameter-reduction and small-divisor assertions;
- 325 boundary recurrence and logarithmic-derivative assertions;
- 38 iterate-quotient assertions;
- 24 shell and reciprocal-map assertions;
- 1,196 lexicographic scale and finite support-collision assertions.

These are finite symbolic regression checks. They do not independently verify
any infinitary theorem, all-period exclusion for arbitrary inputs, class
foundations, or novelty. No numerical floating-point evidence is used as a
substitute for proof.
