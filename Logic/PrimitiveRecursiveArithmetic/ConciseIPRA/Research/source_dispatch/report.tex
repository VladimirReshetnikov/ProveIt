\begin{abstract}
The current native scalar presentation of iPRA uses equality transport and
conditional-universal elimination as two overlapping possibilities in its
proof checker. We prove that primitive equality transport need never start
at the literal term $0$. The missing case is recovered internally by
equation induction, a fixed scalar singleton test, and induction on formula
syntax. This permits the source term to select a unique elimination major.
The resulting calculus has exactly the same native consequence relation,
including arbitrary retained assumptions, and therefore inherits the
whole-domain bi-interpretation with full first-order intuitionistic PRA.

Three complete Python checkers improve the current attained minima from
$261$ to $259$ significant tokens, from $289$ to $283$ AST nodes, and from
$529$ to $526$ normalized bytes. These are separate profiles, not the size
coordinates of one program. The input and verdict contracts are unchanged.
The archive includes the full mathematical derivation, the inherited
arithmetic bridge, an executable proof transformation, concrete certificates,
comparison sources, and reproducible measurements. No absolute minimality or
proof-assistant verification of the new theorem is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and comparison boundary}
\label{sec:result}

The baseline is the public \file{ConciseIPRA} directory at the following
pinned snapshot~\cite{snapshot}:
\begin{quote}\small
Commit: \texttt{b17dd5487f34adea473a0204c5320fb3eeb8910e}\\
Research tree: \texttt{1770db22fe06b1e5c3089b557d9165625a133574}
\end{quote}
It contains twenty-two research packages and a Lean development of the native
calculus and its interpretation into iPRA. The mathematical language and
arithmetic basis used here are inherited from that development.

\paragraph{The new theorem.}
The primitive rule
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
      {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}
\]
may be restricted to $s\synneq0$, where this is a \emph{syntactic} test.
For example, $I(2,1;u.0)$ is an allowed source although it denotes zero.
The restriction does not ask the checker to decide an arithmetical
inequality. Every omitted rule instance is derivable.

\paragraph{The implementation consequence.}
For canonical certificates the source is falsy exactly when it is the
literal numerical zero. The equality and conditional-universal branches can
therefore use the single comparison
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
m == ([s, u] if s else [t] * 3)
\end{lstlisting}
instead of comparing against both possible major patterns. In particular,
the correctness of this change does not rely on recoding zero as a truthy
scalar or on assuming a missing zero-source transport rule.

\input{evidence/metrics_table.tex}

The frozen comparison set contains 56 primary checker modules from the
current research packages. Its attained coordinate minima agree with the
repository's documented frontier. An attained minimum in this finite set is
not a lower bound on all possible proof checkers. All counts include the
complete parser/driver, substitution machinery, arithmetic instructions,
inference checking, and \file{ACCEPT} output. The Python interpreter and its
standard JSON library are shared runtime dependencies, as in the baseline.
No proof database, translator, or producer is imported by a selected checker.

Each selected program also improves its matched predecessor without making
any of the three main coordinates worse. The byte and AST profiles compose
the new matching rule with a small ordering/fusion change. The byte profile
also reuses the checker function as an invalid substitution sentinel. These
two implementation changes are distinguished from the admissibility theorem
in Section~\ref{sec:implementation}.

\paragraph{What is preserved.}
The numerical sort, actual equality, scalar iterator, native binders,
induction restriction, and all old theorems are preserved. Both interpretation
domains remain the whole numerical domain, and both numerical composites have
the internally provable identity graph $x=y$. The contribution is a smaller
primitive elimination interface, rather than a replacement of the scalar
arithmetic foundation.

\section{The complete proposed calculus}
\label{sec:inventory}

\subsection{Terms, formulas, binding, and falsity}

There is one numerical sort. Its terms are
\[
 t::=x\mid0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]
Only $u$ is bound in the update $h$; the counter $n$ and initializer $a$
are outside that binder. All other variables in $h$ are fixed numerical
parameters. The iterator has no access to a varying counter in its update.
Every term is finite syntax. In an ordinary first-order realization, each
fixed earlier-stage update description gives a function symbol with its
displayed numerical parameters; there is no universal evaluator.

Native formulas are
\[
 A::=(s=t)\mid Q_y A\mid D_{x,y}A.
\]
The second constructor binds $y$, and the third binds distinct variables
$x,y$. Substitution is capture-free, after renaming bound variables.
Contexts are finite lists of native formulas, with reusable assumptions.
The designated contradiction is $\bot_*:=(0=S0)$. There is no separate
primitive falsity-elimination rule. It is derived after full transport is
recovered, by the inherited reconstruction of intuitionistic logic.

The intended ordinary meanings are
\[
 Q_y B(y):=B(0)\to\forall y\,B(y),\qquad
 D_{x,y}B:=\exists x\bigl(B(x,0)\to\forall y\,B(x,y)\bigr).
\]
These meanings specify the interpretation, rather than adding ordinary
logical rules to the native checker.

\subsection{All primitive axioms and rules}

In every retained context the two computation schemas are
\begin{align*}
 I(0,a;u.h)&=a,\\
 I(Sn,a;u.h)&=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u].
\end{align*}
There are no additional primitive successor, equality, or arithmetic axioms.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.}
If $A\in\Gamma$, infer $\Gamma\vdash A$.

\paragraph{Elimination with a source.}
For a native template $F(v)$ define the required major by finite syntax:
\[
 M(s,t;F):=
 \begin{cases}
  Q_vF(v),&s\equiv0,\\
  s=t,&s\synneq0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The single elimination pattern is
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash M(s,t;F)\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
      {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}.                 \tag{SE}
\]
Thus SE is exactly QE when $s$ is literal zero, and restricted transport
otherwise. In the proof below it is convenient to refer to these two
specializations as QE and $\mathsf{Tr}^{-}$.

\paragraph{Paired elimination.}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
       \Gamma,Q_yB(b,y)\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash A}.                   \tag{DE}
\]
Here $b$ is fresh for $\Gamma,A$ and the fixed parameters of $B$.

\paragraph{Paired introduction by cases.}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)}.        \tag{DI-case}
\]
The fresh parameter $z$ is absent from $\Gamma,t$ and the fixed parameters
of $B$. The displayed base assumption is discharged.

\paragraph{Paired introduction by equation progress.}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,E(t,z)\vdash E(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}E(x,y)},
       \qquad E\text{ a literal equation}.\tag{DI-progress}
\]
The same freshness conditions hold, and the displayed equation assumption
is discharged. An equation under a binder is not a literal equation.

This is the complete inventory. Exchange, weakening, contraction, cut,
renaming, reflexivity, ordinary logical connectives, numerical cases,
equation induction, and eventually unrestricted numerical substitution
are derived. In the initial proof of the reduction, arbitrary numerical
substitution is deliberately not assumed: it could turn a permitted variable
source into the forbidden literal zero.

\section{The mechanism of the reduction}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The difficulty is not the syntactic test. From $0=t$, a direct attempt to
reverse equality normally transports the reflexivity of zero, which is
exactly the removed case. A general existential naming argument is also
unsafe as a starting point: the standard formula-selector proofs already use
full equality transport. The following arithmetic construction avoids that
dependency.

Use $1=S0$, $2=S1$, and write
\[
 C(n,a,b)=I(n,a;u.b),\quad Z(n)=C(n,1,0),
\]
\[
 N(n)=I(n,0;u.Z(u)),\quad
 R(n)=I(n,0;u.C(u,1,2)),\quad L(n)=N(R(n)).
\]
These are fixed scalar terms. Before any predecessor construction, the
restricted calculus proves
\[
 L(1)=1,\qquad L(SSn)=0,\qquad L(Sn)=Z(n),\qquad
 n=C(Z(n),n,0).
\]
The case where two nonempty expressions both compute to zero needs a
separate joining lemma. It is proved using the common nonempty iterator
$I(0,0;u.0)$; it does not silently use transitivity through zero.

For a fixed $t\synneq0$, retain $0=t$ and perform equation induction on
\[
                     E(n):\quad n=I(n,t;u.Su).
\]
Its base uses the allowed source $t$, and its step uses a fresh variable as
source. At $n=1$ this yields $1=St$. Applying the singleton test and then the
unconditional filter identity gives the proof chain
\[
 \boxed{\quad 0=t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad1=St
     \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z(t)=1
     \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t=0.\quad}
\]
Every displayed intermediate use of transport has a variable, successor,
or iterator as source. The assumption $0=t$ stays fixed during induction.
The numerical case proofs are unconditional, so no retained assumption is
silently changed during a case split.

This establishes unrestricted symmetry. Numerical congruence and equality
transitivity follow, with the zero-middle case handled by the joining lemma.
Transport for an equation template then follows from that equality algebra.
External induction on the formula template recovers transport through
$Q$ and $D$, using only case introduction and the corresponding eliminations.
The next section supplies the full derivation and its computation-certificate
compatibility proof.

\section{Whole-domain bi-interpretability with iPRA}
\label{sec:bridge-overview}

The admissibility theorem preserves every native sequent. Thus it preserves
the established interpretation bridge exactly, rather than merely preserving
the truth of numerical equations in the standard model. We spell out that
bridge and reproduce its mathematical ingredients in the appendices.

\subsection{The ordinary scalar theory}

Let $T_{\rm eq}$ be intuitionistic first-order logic with actual equality over
the scalar signature, with both computation schemas, coverage
\[
 \forall n\,(n=0\lor\exists m\,n=Sm),
\]
separation $(0=S0)\to\bot$, and contextual induction for literal equations.
The separation clause is essential: positive equations alone admit a
singleton structure. It is explicit in the ordinary realization, while
native falsity is the equation $0=S0$ itself.

The translations of $Q,D$ are those in Section~\ref{sec:inventory}.
Restricted transport is sound in ordinary equality logic. DI-case is sound
by coverage, and DI-progress by equation induction under its conditional
base. Conversely the new theorem recovers the original full transport rule;
the native logical macros then derive all rules and axioms of $T_{\rm eq}$.
The ordinary and native translations have provable formula round trips,
including formulas nested under either binder.

\subsection{From scalar arithmetic to all primitive recursive descriptions}

The inherited proof proceeds in a necessary order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Construct scalar predecessor $P$ and prove $P0=0$, $P(Sn)=n$ by
literal-equation induction and numerical cases. No pairing or equality
detector is presupposed.
\item Construct truncated subtraction and a Boolean characteristic of actual
equality on the whole domain. Compile each fixed quantifier-free formula
into a characteristic equation, recovering quantifier-free induction.
\item Define pairing and projections using bounded square-root iteration,
then compile every fixed primitive-recursive description into a scalar term.
\end{enumerate}
Write $\pi_0,\pi_1$ for the two derived pairing projections, distinct from
the singleton-test notation above. For a target recursion with base $g$
and update $h$, the last step uses
\[
 V(n,\vec p)=I\bigl(n,\pi(0,g^*(\vec p));
 u.\pi(S\pi_0(u),h^*(\pi_0(u),\pi_1(u),\vec p))\bigr),
 \qquad f^*(n,\vec p)=\pi_1(V(n,\vec p)).
\]
All passive parameters stay fixed, and $\pi_0(V(n,\vec p))=n$ is an internal
equation-induction theorem. This is compilation of finite descriptions, not
postulation of a universal evaluator.

In the other direction, iPRA interprets every scalar iterator by ordinary
primitive recursion with a counter-independent update. Internal uniqueness
of the recursively defined operations proves the term round trips. Both
domain interpretations are $x=x$, equality stays actual equality, and the
composite isomorphisms are the graphs $x=y$. Formula round trips follow by
structural induction. Hence full first-order intuitionistic PRA and the
proposed calculus are bi-interpretable and equiconsistent.

The appendices reproduce the finite-state predecessor bootstrap, the
equality-reflection proof, the primitive-recursion compiler, and the logical
macros with attribution~\cite{basis,bridge,erasure,forward,progress}.
The pinned Lean files \file{NativeComposite.lean} and
\file{BiInterpretation.lean} also state the existing native/$T_{\rm eq}$ and
$T_{\rm eq}$/iPRA equivalences~\cite{lean}. The new admissibility theorem has
not been formalized in Lean or Rocq, and the repository's Lean build was not
rerun for this report.

\section{Complete checkers and their trust boundary}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{Public contract and canonical expressions}

Each selected checker is a standalone Python module. It reads a JSON array
from standard input. The final element is a closed goal, and preceding
elements are instruction packets consumed from right to left. An unused
prefix is inert. Acceptance means normal exit with exactly
\file{ACCEPT} followed by a newline; an exception or nonzero process status
is rejection. The checks do not depend on Python assertions, so the same
contract holds under \file{python3 -O}. Resource exhaustion is rejection;
as with the baseline, the mathematical completeness statement abstracts
from a fixed machine's finite time, memory, and recursion limits.

The canonical old zero encoding is retained: scalar JSON \file{0} denotes
numerical zero, \file{[0]} denotes $S0$, unary formulas encode $D$, binary
formulas encode equality, and a canonical $Q$ resource consists of three
copies of its body. All variables are numerical. Bound-coordinate slots have
width two; numerical root depth is three. The common opening traversal adds
$5-\operatorname{arity}$ under an array. Successor and the iterator's
non-update children carry the inherited dummy zero slots. Admitted negative
integers are rigid fresh parameters. This detailed codec is inherited; its
full sorted interpretation and proof are supplied in
\file{reference/bridge/inherited\_arity\_checker.tex}~\cite{generated}.

For the main and byte profiles, an instruction is $[m,s,u,t]$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.25\linewidth}p{0.65\linewidth}}
\toprule
Role & Fields and effect\\\midrule
Equality elimination & $s$ truthy; require $m=[s,u]$ and the target
instance of template $t$; check $m$ and continue from the source instance.\\
Conditional elimination & $s$ falsy; require $m=[t,t,t]$; check $m$ and
continue from the zero instance of template $t$.\\
Paired elimination & A proved unary major exports its three-copy $Q$
resource at a fresh numerical parameter.\\
Computation & $m=[]$, $u$ truthy; $s$ supplies zero or a successor
predecessor, $u$ is the initializer, and $t$ is the update.\\
Case introduction & $m=[]$, $u=[]$; $t$ is the existential witness.\\
Equation progress & $m=[]$, $u=0$; $t$ is the witness, and only a literal
equation receives a genuine induction assumption.\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The token profile inherits a global fresh-name supply and puts the
case/progress marker in $m$, as its matched predecessor does. Its producer
therefore uses the corresponding global protocol. This difference is
documented in \file{code/FORMAT.txt}; the three programs are not claimed to
accept identical raw byte streams.

\subsection{Why source dispatch is complete}

On canonical numerical syntax, a nonzero source has a truthy representation:
variables are negative rigid coordinates or positive protected coordinates,
and successor/iterator terms are nonempty arrays. Thus the conditional
major comparison implements exactly SE. Theorem~\ref{zs:main} restores every
omitted source-zero instance.

Computation packets exclude a literal-zero initializer and a literal-zero
successor predecessor. They are recovered with the inherited nonempty zero
$c_0=I(2,1;u.0)$, using only transport at source $c_0$.
Section~\ref{zs:certificate-computation} verifies this compatibility before
appealing to the new admissibility theorem. A proof transformation first
eliminates forbidden transport sources, then expands computation leaves,
then emits scoped packets. None of these larger untrusted producers is
loaded by the checker.

\subsection{Why accepted raw aliases remain sound}

The soundness argument is semantic, rather than a claim that every raw tree
is canonical or that opening is idempotent. At numerical positions, arrays of arity zero, two, or at least four
are zero annotations; at formula positions, arity zero and arities
at least four denote the fixed truth $0=0$. Successfully admitted scalar
formula padding also denotes truth. A ternary formula reads its last child
as a $Q$ matrix. The frozen codec proof gives capture-free semantic opening
at every meaningful sorted position.

If the new major comparison succeeds with a truthy source, the proved
major is an actual equality, and the replacement is ordinary sound
transport. If the source is falsy, the proved major is the specified $Q$
resource and the replacement is its zero-instance elimination. The target
match must also succeed. If it does not, no goal conversion occurs.
Tripling a proved unary major gives its $Q$ resource; tripling any other
nonempty array yields a truth annotation. No failed major proof is accepted
as an instruction marker. A raw truthy alias may denote arithmetic zero;
any resulting equality transport is sound in the full native calculus,
which Theorem~\ref{zs:main} identifies with the reduced one.

Fresh names and contexts retain the baseline discipline. The main profile
extends tuples and the byte profile constructs new lists; neither mutates
a caller's assumption context. Witnesses are opened before allocating the
next fresh parameter. For generated computation hypotheses, simultaneous
zero specialization of fresh names, rather than a false assertion of
uniform truth in those names, justifies the continuation. Fractional fresh
aliases, when a raw successful input admits them, receive the same
simultaneous-specialization treatment as in the frozen codec audit.

\subsection{Two small implementation compositions}

In the byte and AST profiles, the pure calculation of a possible replacement
goal precedes the recursive major proof. The context and lexical scope
floor do not change during that calculation. A successful major check
returns \file{None}; consequently it can be the first operand of the
already existing \file{or} expression for the exported hypothesis. Failure
still rejects the whole certificate. This removes a separate expression
statement without omitting a proof obligation.

The byte profile uses the existing function object \file{c} as the default
invalid numerical substitution image. JSON cannot supply that callable,
and attempting to use it as a numerical image raises before it can become
an accepted numerical image, just as the previous complex sentinel rejected. This is a
one-character source saving with no new trusted helper.

\section{Executable evidence and reproducibility}
\label{sec:validation}

The central regression is a previously accepted contextual-induction proof
whose old certificate uses a literal-zero-source transport. Directly changing
the matcher correctly rejects that old instruction. The new proof
transformation produces an accepted certificate of the same conclusion.
This distinguishes preservation of the theorem relation from preservation
of old serialized proofs. Further certificates exercise the recovered
transport under both $Q$ and $D$.

The new certificate generator audits its expanded native proof trees and
reports zero primitive transport nodes with a literal-zero source. It uses
the retained source $c_0$ for zero-computation repair. The normalizer is
untrusted: the standalone checker verifies every emitted certificate.

equivalence and bi-interpretability statements rest on the displayed

\section{Scope, provenance, and remaining limits}

This is a strict but small improvement under the repository's checker-size
metric. Longer untrusted derivations replace primitive rule cases. No
optimality of certificate length or checking time is claimed, and different
programs attain the three new minima.

Scalar arithmetic, the $Q/D$ macros, the arity codec, certified-zero repair,
and the iPRA bridge are inherited. The repository's truthy-zero encoding and
constructed-major protocol~\cite{uniformzero} are distinct earlier techniques;
the new theorem works with the old canonical zero encoding.

The repository-local comparison covered the package inventory and 107 main
or theory LaTeX modules. The earlier zero-source-only obstruction concerns
the opposite restriction~\cite{opposite}. No literature-wide priority,
absolute simplest theory, or universal source-size lower bound is claimed.

The new theorem and codec composition have written proofs, separate
assistant reviews, and finite executable evidence. Independent peer review
and proof-assistant formalization remain outstanding. The cited upstream
Lean bridge was not rebuilt, and this report supplies no internal
consistency proof for iPRA.
