\begin{abstract}
The current ConciseIPRA arithmetic and logical basis is retained. The new
result is a smaller complete implementation of its proof presentation,
measured with the repository's complete-checker convention. The certificate
type invariant and the arity table for formulas and terms are shared.
For the full equality/$Q$/$D$ language this reduces a matched, fully
validated checker from \FullOldBytes\ to \FullNewBytes\ normalized Python
bytes. An equality/$D$ public version reduces its matched control from
\EQDoldBytes\ to \EQDnewBytes\ bytes. These comparisons include input
validation and the complete public interface.

Separately, immutable tuple scopes permit a tail-loop conversion.
Composing the previously delivered scalar neutral resource with
exact-root introduction gives the compact coordinate envelope
\CompactNewTokens/\CompactNewAST/\CompactNewBytes\
(tokens/AST nodes/bytes), improving the incoming 257/279/522 envelope.
Different programs attain these coordinates.

The axioms, rules, interpretations, and both formula round trips are stated
explicitly. The implementation transformations are proved correct; the
nontrivial arithmetic bridge is attributed to the pinned repository and
reproduced in appendices. No new shorter arithmetic axiom basis, new
proof-assistant verification of Python, or absolute minimum is claimed.
This is a modest reproducible reduction under proof-checker size.
\end{abstract}

\section{Complete mathematical presentation}

\subsection{Syntax and ordinary reading}

There is one numerical sort:
\[
 t ::= x\mid0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]
Only $u$ is bound in $h$; the counter and initializer do not bind it.
All other update variables are fixed parameters. Substitution is
capture-avoiding. Full native formulas are
\[
 A ::= (s=t)\mid Q_yA\mid D_{x,y}A.
\]
The variables bound by a displayed $D$ are distinct. Their ordinary
readings are
\[
(s=t)^\circ=(s=t),\qquad
(Q_yB)^\circ=B^\circ(0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(y),
\]
\[
(D_{x,y}B)^\circ=
 \exists x\bigl(B^\circ(x,0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(x,y)\bigr).
\tag{Reading}\label{main:reading}
\]
The designated contradiction is $0=S0$; contexts are finite reusable
lists. The equality/$D$ public version omits $Q$ from the public grammar
but permits it as an intermediate resource.

\subsection{Every axiom and rule}

The two nonlogical computation schemas, available in every context, are
\[
\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash I(0,a;u.h)=a},\qquad
\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash I(Sn,a;u.h)=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u]}.
\tag{$I_0,I_S$}
\]
The remaining rules are
\[
\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash A}\quad(A\in\Gamma),
\tag{Hyp}
\]
\[
\frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
     {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}\quad(s\not\equiv0),
\tag{Tr$^{-}$}\label{main:tr}
\]
\[
\frac{\Gamma\vdash Q_yB(y)\qquad\Gamma\vdash B(0)}
     {\Gamma\vdash B(t)},
\tag{QE}
\]
\[
\frac{\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
      \Gamma,Q_yB(b,y)\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash A},
\tag{DE}
\]
\[
\frac{\Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
     {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)},
\tag{DI-case}
\]
\[
\frac{\Gamma,E(t,z)\vdash E(t,Sz)}
     {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}E(x,y)}
\quad(E\text{ a literal equation}).
\tag{DI-progress}
\]
The template $F$ may be any native formula, including a constant
template. The condition $s\not\equiv0$ is syntactic: an iterator denoting
zero is allowed. In DE, $b$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, the conclusion, and
fixed matrix parameters. In the introductions, $z$ is fresh for
$\Gamma$, the witness, and fixed parameters. Local assumptions are
discharged.

This is the entire primitive inventory. Ordinary logical connectives,
reflexivity, symmetry, successor injectivity, coverage, and induction
are derived. Weakening, contraction, exchange, renaming, and assumption
cut are admissible. Arbitrary numerical substitution into a restricted
derivation is not presumed during the bootstrap: it can turn a
permitted source into literal zero.

Tr$^{-}$ and QE have a shared packet dispatch: a nonzero source selects
the equality major; literal zero selects the $Q$ major. This is a
decision on finite syntax, not an excluded-middle principle in the
object theory.

\subsection{Serialization restrictions on computations}

The compact packet protocol uses a truthy initializer to distinguish
computation from introduction; a zero predecessor can also collide
with a marker. These restrictions do not remove computation schemas.

Put $c_0=I(2,1;u.0)$. The equation $c_0=0$ is a directly allowed
successor computation with nonzero predecessor and initializer.
For a computation instance with an offending zero initializer or
predecessor, abstract only those occurrences as $v$, giving $J(v)$.
The instance $J(c_0)$ is directly allowed. Transport along $c_0=0$
yields $J(0)$. The source is an iterator, hence permitted.
This inherited three-inference expansion is reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{zs:section}; it avoids a circular appeal to a
zero-source transport.

\section{Both interpretations with iPRA}\label{main:interpretations}

\subsection{Derived equality, induction, and logic}

The rules immediately derive numerical cases and contextual equation
induction:
\[
\frac{\Gamma\vdash A(0)\quad\Gamma\vdash A(Sz)}
     {\Gamma\vdash A(t)},\qquad
\frac{\Gamma\vdash E(0)\quad\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
     {\Gamma\vdash E(t)}.
\tag{Cases and EI}
\]
Introduce $D_{u,y}A(y)$ or $D_{u,y}E(y)$ with unused $u$, using the
appropriate introduction, then apply DE and QE with the base proof.
No transport is needed.

Reflexivity follows from computation. For $r=I(0,t;u.0)$, use $r=t$
as both transport premises with template $v=t$. The source $r$ is
syntactically nonzero. Recovering omitted zero-source transports is
less immediate. The full proof in Appendix~\ref{zs:section} establishes
safe equality operations, a singleton-one test, a zero filter, symmetry
under $0=t$, and finally full transport by induction on formula syntax.
It uses equation induction with fixed passive parameters, and does not
assume unrestricted substitution during the restricted-source bootstrap.

Thus the source-restricted and unrestricted native calculi have
identical consequence relations. The pinned Lean kernel comparison
\texttt{zprf\_iff\_nprf} supplies its formal counterpart; the inherited
comparison \texttt{iprf\_iff\_nprf} accounts for witnessed progress
and full-template transport.

For explicit logical macros define
\[
 C(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b),\quad
 \delta(v)=C(v,1,0),\quad p(v)=I(v,0;u.\delta(u)).
\]
On equality/$D$ formulas, set
\[
G_v(s=t)=(C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t)),\qquad
G_v(D_{x,y}B)=D_{x,y}G_vB,
\]
with fresh renaming. Then $G_0A$ is a theorem and
$G_{Sz}A\dashv\vdash A$.
The selector between arbitrary formulas is the finite recursion
\[
 M_v(s=t,r=q)=(C(v,s,r)=C(v,t,q)),
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_v(D_{x,y}A,D_{x,y}B)&=D_{x,y}M_v(A,B),\\
M_v(E,D_{x,y}B)&=D_{x,y}M_v(G_yE,B),\\
M_v(D_{x,y}A,E)&=D_{x,y}M_v(A,G_yE),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $E$ is an equation and binders are aligned freshly.
Each call decreases maximum binder depth. Padding and binder
congruence prove the two selector endpoint equivalences.

The full intuitionistic logical interface is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bot_*&=(0=1),\\
A\to_*B&=D_{u,z}M_z(A,B),\\
A\wedge_*B&=D_{u,z}G_zM_{p(z)}(A,B),\\
\exists_*x\,A(x)&=D_{x,y}G_yA(x),\\
A\vee_*B&=\exists_*x\,M_x(A,B),\\
\forall_*x\,A(x)&=A(0)\wedge_*D_{u,z}G_zA(z),
\end{aligned}
\tag{Macros}
\]
with fresh unused $u$. The natural deduction rules for these macros,
including elimination under retained assumptions, are proved in
Appendix~\ref{sec:choice-introduction}. They give full first-order
intuitionistic logic, without quantified excluded middle.

\begin{lemma}[The public $Q$ constructor is definable]
For fresh unused $u$,
\[
                        Q_yB(y)\dashv\vdash D_{u,y}B(y).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From $Q_yB$, introduce the right side by DI-case: its local base and
QE supply the successor premise. Conversely, eliminate the right
side and return the supplied closure assumption. The unused witness
does not occur in the conclusion. Structural induction and binder
congruence extend the equivalence to nested occurrences.
\end{proof}
Consequently the equality/$D$ public grammar is a definitional
presentation, not a loss of quantified expressive power.

\subsection{Whole-domain arithmetic and formula composites}

Let $T_I$ be ordinary intuitionistic first-order equality logic over
the scalar terms, with the two computation schemas, contextual
literal-equation induction, coverage, and separation:
\[
 \forall x(x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy),\qquad (0=S0)\to\bot.
\]
Separation is explicit; positive equations alone would admit a
one-element structure. Each fixed scalar update can be elaborated as
a fixed first-order function symbol with its two equations. This
staged signature has no universal evaluator.

Here iPRA means full intuitionistic first-order arithmetic with
symbols for all fixed primitive-recursive definitions, their
defining equations, successor separation and injectivity, and
induction for all quantifier-free formulas.

\begin{theorem}[Inherited whole-domain bridge]
$T_I$ is bi-interpretable with iPRA. Both domains are whole, equality
is actual equality, and $x=y$ is the internally proved isomorphism
from either numerical domain to its double interpretation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A scalar iterator is primitive recursion that ignores its counter
argument in the update. In the reverse direction, the internal
construction order is predecessor; monus and equality reflection;
quantifier-free induction; pairing with projections; compilation of
every fixed primitive recursion. The complete constructions are
reproduced in Appendices~\ref{sec:hybrid-bootstrap},
\ref{sec:eic-completion}, and \ref{sec:iterator-coding}.

For a recursion
\[
f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p),\quad
f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p),
\]
let $\pi,L,H$ be proved pairing and projections. Set
\[
\begin{split}
V(n,\vec p)&=I\bigl(n,\pi(0,g^*(\vec p));\\
 &\hspace{23mm}u.\pi(SL(u),h^*(L(u),H(u),\vec p))\bigr),\\
f^*(n,\vec p)&=H(V(n,\vec p)).
\end{split}
\]
Equation induction proves $L(V(n,\vec p))=n$; the projection
identities give both defining equations. Pairing stores a finite
recursion state and does not replace the numerical domain.

Internal equation induction also proves uniqueness of a fixed
iterator from its seed and update. The analogous fact holds for
ordinary primitive recursion. External induction on each finite
description identifies its double translation with the original
operation at every internal argument. Thus the composite assertion
is internal, not merely standard-model correctness.
\end{proof}

The native reading is sound in $T_I$: DI-case uses coverage;
DI-progress uses equation induction under its conditional base.
The macros and derived arithmetic rules give the reverse
interpretation. The crucial formula composite is
\[
 D_{x,y}B\dashv\vdash
 \exists_*x\bigl(B(x,0)\to_*\forall_*y\,B(x,y)\bigr).
\]
DE proves the forward direction; existential elimination and
DI-case prove the reverse. Gate and selector endpoints prove the
ordinary logical composites. Structural induction on formulas
gives both full round trips, uniformly in retained assumptions.

\begin{corollary}
The full native presentation and its equality/$D$ public version
are bi-interpretable and equiconsistent with iPRA. A native proof
of $0=S0$ translates to an iPRA proof of $\bot$, and conversely.
\end{corollary}
These claims do not assert that either theory proves its own
consistency. The new validated programs implement the same
consequence relations, so the interpretations transfer unchanged.

\section{Shared validation: the new reduction}\label{main:validation}

\subsection{Input contract and raw syntax}

The JSON certificate contains four-field packets $[m,s,u,t]$
followed by its goal. Packets are consumed right to left. The fields
serve as a major formula, a source or predecessor, an initializer
or mode, and a template or witness according to the checked branch.
Unused prefix packets are logically inert; the typed programs
nevertheless validate their integer/list type.

Success means normal exit with output exactly \texttt{ACCEPT}.
An exception rejects. The code uses no Python assertions, so
\texttt{-O} does not remove checks. Public goals are closed; free
parameters can be handled by universal closure. Internally the
fresh-parameter discipline is unchanged.

The inherited arity encoding represents an equation by a pair,
$D$ by a singleton, and $Q$ by a triple of identical matrices.
At numerical sort, successor is a singleton and the iterator a
triple. A traversal increments its depth by $5-\operatorname{len}(a)$.
Depth parity distinguishes formula and numerical positions.
Odd numerical codes and reserved padding slots implement binding.
The full decoder argument is included as
\path{reference/inherited_arity_checker.tex}; the user-facing
certificate specification gives the canonical constructors.

The numerical floor $k$ distinguishes available negative
eigenparameters from future ones. This check remains necessary
even for closed public goals, since intermediate packet data is
untrusted. The opening operation's parity factor likewise remains
a genuine sort barrier.

\subsection{Type invariant}

A certificate satisfies the exact recursive predicate
\[
 J(a)\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \operatorname{type}(a)=\texttt{int}\quad\vee\quad
 \bigl(\operatorname{type}(a)=\texttt{list}
            \ \wedge\ \forall b\in a\,J(b)\bigr).
\]
The exact integer test excludes Python booleans.

\begin{lemma}[Persistent invariant]\label{main:types}
After the entire certificate satisfies $J$, each raw subtree is
an integer or a list. Every syntax object subsequently constructed
by the checker has the same property, except for the deliberate
invalid-substitution sentinel.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The input claim is structural induction. The kernel constructs
syntax with list formation, integer floor decrements, repetition,
and recursive opening. Induction on a successful execution preserves
the invariant. The function sentinel is used only to make an
invalid numerical opening fail.
\end{proof}

On this domain, the dispatch tests $a*0=[]$ and $a*0\ne0$ agree:
both recognize lists and reject integers. A used function sentinel
raises in both versions. The shorter spelling is justified only
after the type invariant is established.

After the goal validator's integer case fails, a repeated list-type
check is redundant. This removes duplicate validation without
relaxing the public grammar.

\subsection{Shared grammar table}

At formula depth the equality/$D$ grammar has arities 1 and 2;
at numerical depth the term grammar has arities 1 and 3.
With even formula depth and odd numerical depth the single table is
\[
                    \operatorname{len}(a)
                    \in(1,\,2+(d\bmod2)).
\tag{Arity table}
\]
The full language adds just a diagonal triple at formula depth.
At numerical depth every triple is already allowed. Thus the
alternative $a=[a_0,a_0,a_0]$ adds precisely the required $Q$ case.

\begin{lemma}[Validator equivalence]\label{main:validator}
The shared validator accepts exactly the same canonical closed
formulas as its matched control, for each public grammar.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the finite tree. The integer cases have identical
zero, odd-variable, scope, and sort conditions. At numerical lists,
the shared table permits exactly successor and iterator; the
diagonal alternative adds no arity. At formula lists, it permits
exactly equality and $D$, plus exactly the canonical $Q$ triples
when the full interface is selected. Children are checked at the
same depth and satisfy the induction hypothesis. Invalid empty
lists can fail by an indexing exception instead of false; either
rejects under the acceptance contract.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete checker preservation]
For every finite JSON input the new validated native checker and
its matched control have the same acceptance behavior, apart from
ordinary finite resource exhaustion. The corresponding equality/$D$
statement also holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The type gates are equivalent. Conditional on passing, the goal
validators agree by Lemma~\ref{main:validator}, and the kernel
dispatches agree by Lemma~\ref{main:types}. Every successful
instruction, substitution, major proof, retained hypothesis,
scope boundary, and final goal is the same. An error remains
rejection, and the success output is identical. This is a
transformation proof; regression tests provide additional evidence.
\end{proof}

\section{Smaller guarded profiles}

The separate compact comparison uses the incoming guarded public
grammar. Terms are unchanged. A public formula is an equation, or
$D_{x,y}B$ where $B$ is compound or has the zero-initialized shape
\[
                     I(y,0;u.h)=I(y,0;u.k).
\tag{Guard}
\]
Intermediate formulas are unrestricted. Keep computation, Hyp,
Tr$^{-}$, QE, and DE, and use one root-directed introduction:
\[
\frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta_B(z))\vdash B(t,Sz)}
     {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B},\qquad
\eta_B(z)=
\begin{cases}z,&B\text{ an equation},\\0,&B\text{ compound}.\end{cases}
\tag{Root DI}
\]
The auxiliary equality bootstrap matters. Restricting every
intermediate formula to the public guard grammar while deleting
zero-source transport is not justified.

For soundness use the anchored ordinary reading
\eqref{main:reading} uniformly on all auxiliary formulas.
Atomic progress is sound by equation induction under its base;
compound case introduction is sound by coverage. This simpler
reading does not assert completeness on all auxiliary formulas.

The inherited guarded translation uses
\[
 L(E)=D_{u,v}G_vE,\qquad
 P(A)=\begin{cases}L(A),&A\text{ an equation},\\A,&A\text{ compound},\end{cases}
\]
\[
                    T(E)=E,\qquad
                    T(D_{x,y}B)=D_{x,y}P(TB).
\]
Padding, recovered equation induction, and both formula composites
are reproduced in Appendix~\ref{sec:guarded-core}. With the
source-restricted auxiliary bootstrap, the canonical guarded
consequence relation is the same as in the incoming guarded-source
package. Its whole-domain iPRA interpretations are preserved.

\subsection{Scalar administrative truth}

Let $c=I(0,0;u.0)$. The computation $c=0$, used twice in Tr$^{-}$
with $F(v)=(v=0)$, proves $0=0$.

\begin{lemma}[Neutral resource]
If $T$ is a fixed closed theorem, then
$\Gamma,T\vdash A$ iff $\Gamma\vdash A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Weakening gives one direction; cut a proof of $T$ into every use
of the extra assumption for the other. Fresh renaming suffices;
arbitrary numerical substitution into restricted proofs is not used.
\end{proof}

The local guarded profiles replace an empty-list spelling of this
neutral theorem by a scalar zero spelling. The canonical compiler
changes only the neutral major field, not arbitrary empty arrays.

This is not equality of raw accepted languages. For new successful
scalar-major cases, the list-valued equality/$Q$ matcher cannot
match; its exported scalar is still administrative truth.
Canonical guarded consequences are unchanged. The strict guarded
profile separately validates the public grammar.

\subsection{Immutable scopes permit the loop}

In the global-freshness token profile, replace the tail recursion
by a loop with tuple extension $g\gets g+(H,)$. An inner major
proof receives the old tuple and cannot mutate its caller's
context.

\begin{proposition}
The tuple loop and recursive control accept the same
global-freshness certificates under the same public-goal contract,
apart from ordinary resource exhaustion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Unroll a finite successful loop. An iteration has the same packet
consumption, recursive major verification, floor decrement,
hypothesis opening, and next-goal opening as one tail frame.
The shared floor changes in the same order. Tuple extension is
persistent; it produces the same context contents as recursive
list concatenation. Both openings are pure at the fixed new floor,
so their successful evaluation order is immaterial. The argument
reverses to fold a recursive trace into the loop.
\end{proof}
In-place list extension would invalidate the proof by leaking
local assumptions. The delivered profile uses immutable tuples.

\section{Verification and limitations}

Run \texttt{python3 reproduce.py} from the package root.
The script uses only the Python standard library, measures whole
modules, regenerates normalized gzip-9 artifacts, replays positive
and rejection certificates, and writes machine-readable receipts.
The trusted checker imports no producer, theorem database, or
reproduction helper.

The recorded validation contains \ValidationExecutions\ process
executions and \ValidationFailures\ unexpected outcomes.
Selected programs are tested normally and with \texttt{-O}.
Controls cover both computations, equality transport, nested
binders, used induction hypotheses, wrong endpoints, future
eigenparameters, and context leakage. Typed profiles also reject
noninteger/list data, including in otherwise unused prefixes.
These are regression controls, not a replacement for the universal
mathematical arguments.

The new checker equivalence proofs are written arguments. The
pinned repository provides Lean formalizations of the inherited
native/ordinary/iPRA bridge and round trips. Lean was not built
in this session; no new Lean or Rocq verification of Python is
claimed. The guarded auxiliary comparison retains its inherited
written proof boundary.

\paragraph{An unsound apparent saving.}
Removing the opening function's parity barrier produces a shorter
program but accepts the canonical guarded false goal
\[
            D_{x,y}(I(y,0;u.0)=I(y,0;u.1)).
\]
The zero instance is true, whereas the instance $y=1$ would require
$0=1$. The supplied \path{tests/parity_guard_original.json} exploits a
scalar template to confuse numerical aliases and formulas.
The delivered checkers keep the barrier and reject this attack.

\paragraph{What has been achieved.}
There is a strict complete-checker reduction with the same
validated native input contract and consequence relation.
The mathematical rule basis has not been shortened. No claim
compares a goal-only validator directly against a full-certificate
type validator, or treats gzip bytes as the optimization target.
The observed compact envelope is a separate comparison.

\paragraph{Deliverables.}
\path{README.txt} gives reproduction commands;
\path{CLAIMS.json} states the limits;
\path{evidence/metrics.json} and the validation receipt supply
exact measurements. Inherited proofs and exact matched controls
are attributed under \path{reference/}. The following appendices
