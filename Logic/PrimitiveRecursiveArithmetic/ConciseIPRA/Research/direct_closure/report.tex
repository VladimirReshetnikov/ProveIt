\begin{abstract}
The current ConciseIPRA presentation packages literal-equation induction
inside an existential introduction rule. A simpler primitive interface
introduces the conditional universal closure directly:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}{\Gamma\vdash\Q_yE(y)},
 \qquad \Q_yE(y):=E(0)\to\forall y\,E(y).
\]
The existential binder retains only its case-introduction rule. The
replacement removes one numerical witness and one bound coordinate from
the induction schema. It changes neither terms, formulas, contexts, nor
the consequence relation. Effective translations in both directions
preserve the final sequent and have linear inference-node bounds. The
whole-domain bi-interpretation with full first-order intuitionistic PRA
therefore transfers unchanged.

The direct closure rule itself is already a derived theorem in the
repository. The contribution here is its selection as primitive, explicit
bidirectional proof compilers, and a controlled implementation comparison.
Two independently executable strict Python checkers, differing only in
the progression branch, measure 5,199 versus 5,116 normalized bytes,
1,187 versus 1,169 significant tokens, and 1,358 versus 1,337 AST nodes.
The smaller source has a larger gzip representation. These checkers do
not improve the repository's 529-byte compact frontier. No globally
simplest axiomatization or new arithmetic foundation is claimed.
\end{abstract}
\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{ink}{pale}{\begin{minipage}{.92\linewidth}
\textbf{Result and limitation.} There is an exact simplification of the
primitive induction interface and a measurable reduction in matched,
explicit proof-checker implementations. A presentation below the current
best compact-checker frontier has \emph{not} been established. This is a
modest next iteration, not the requested substantial size breakthrough.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The task is about full first-order \ipra, not merely its equational

equiconsistency requirement by itself.

\section{The complete proposed axiomatization}
\label{sec:new-calculus}

Call the presentation $\CQ$. Its entire primitive inventory follows.

\subsection{Terms and formulas}

There is one numerical sort. Terms are finite expressions
\[
 t::=x\mid0\mid St\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]
Only $u$ in the update body $h$ is bound; it does not bind occurrences
in the counter $n$ or initializer $a$. Every update is fixed finite
syntax with passive numerical parameters. No object-language universal
evaluator, quantification over function codes, or higher-order domain is
postulated.

Native formulas are
\[
 A::=(s=t)\mid\Q_y A\mid\D_{x,y}A.
\]
The variables $x,y$ in $\D$ are distinct. The designated contradiction is
$\bot_*:=(0=S0)$. Contexts $\Gamma$ are finite reusable lists of formulas.
All substitutions below avoid capture; bound and eigenvariables may
first be renamed.

The intended ordinary readings are
\[
 (s=t)^\circ=(s=t),\qquad
 (\Q_yB)^\circ=B^\circ(0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(y),
\]
\[
 (\D_{x,y}B)^\circ=
 \exists x\bigl(B^\circ(x,0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(x,y)\bigr).
\]
These readings explain the symbols; they are not additional native
axiom schemas.

\subsection{The two computation schemas}

In every retained context:
\[
 \frac{}{\Gamma\vdash I(0,a;u.h)=a}\quad\mathrm{I0},
 \qquad
 \frac{}{\Gamma\vdash I(Sn,a;u.h)=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u]}\quad\mathrm{IS}.
\]

\subsection{All six other primitive inference schemas}

\begin{align*}
 &\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash A}\quad\mathrm{Hyp}\quad(A\in\Gamma),\\[5pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
        {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}\quad\mathrm{Tr},\\[5pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash\Q_yB(y)\qquad\Gamma\vdash B(0)}
        {\Gamma\vdash B(t)}\quad\mathrm{QE},\\[5pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
         \Gamma,\Q_yB(b,y)\vdash A}
        {\Gamma\vdash A}\quad\mathrm{DE},\\[5pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
        {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)}\quad\mathrm{DCase},\\[5pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
        {\Gamma\vdash\Q_yE(y)}\quad\mathrm{QInd}
        \quad(E\text{ a literal equation}).
\end{align*}

In Tr, $F(v)$ is any native formula template, including templates with
occurrences under binders. In DE, $b$ is fresh for the retained context,
the conclusion, and all fixed parameters of the matrix. In DCase, $z$ is
fresh for the retained context, the witness $t$, and every fixed matrix
parameter. In QInd, $z$ is fresh for the retained context and every fixed
parameter of $E$. Displayed local assumptions are discharged.

The phrase \emph{literal equation} means an equation at the matrix root.
An equation beneath a $\Q$ or $\D$ binder is not a literal equation.
That restriction is part of the theory and part of the executable checkers.

This is the whole primitive presentation: eight schemas when computation
leaves and Hyp are counted. There is no primitive reflexivity,
successor injectivity, predecessor, pairing, equality test, numerical
coverage, ordinary connective rule, or additional induction schema.
Weakening, exchange, contraction, renaming, numerical substitution and
cut are admissible properties, not unlisted certificate instructions.

\subsection{What has been removed}

The old calculus $\CD$ has the same first seven schemas but replaces
QInd by
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,E(t,z)\vdash E(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}E(x,y)}\quad\mathrm{DProg}.
\]
It has two variables in its matrix and an explicit existential witness
$t$. QInd has one matrix variable and no witness. DCase now gives the
only primitive introduction of $\D$; QInd gives the primitive introduction
of $\Q$. The number of schemas is unchanged, but induction no longer
performs existential bookkeeping.

\section{Structural properties and exact equivalence}

\begin{lemma}[Structural admissibility]
In $\CQ$, weakening, exchange, contraction, capture-free renaming,
numerical substitution, and ordinary-assumption cut are admissible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on derivations. For weakening and numerical substitution, rename
local eigenparameters away from the finitely many retained expressions
being inserted. Every computation schema is stable under substitution.
The major and minor formulas in Tr and QE substitute coherently. DE and
DCase retain their respective fresh variables after renaming.
For QInd, substitution preserves the equation at the matrix root and
acts only on its passive parameters; its distinguished fresh variable
continues to be fresh. Thus the same QInd rule applies to the substituted
premise. Exchange and contraction change only reusable context membership.

For cut, replace each use of the discharged assumption by its given proof,
weakened into the local context, again renaming eigenparameters first.
This proof substitution preserves every rule side condition. In
particular, no test on the literal spelling of a numerical witness changes
under substitution: the new induction rule has no such witness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Identity-language equivalence]
\label{thm:equivalence}
For every finite native context $\Gamma$ and native formula $A$,
\[
                 \Gamma\vdash_{\CQ}A
                 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                 \Gamma\vdash_{\CD}A.
\]
The transformations are effective on finite derivations and leave the
final sequent literally unchanged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All common rules translate recursively without modification. There is
exactly one changed case in each direction.

\emph{From $\CQ$ to $\CD$.}
Suppose the translated premise of QInd is
$\pi:\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)$. Choose a fresh variable $u$ absent
from $E$ and the retained context. Old DProg, with witness zero and
unused bound coordinate $u$, gives
\[
                   \Gamma\vdash_{\CD}\D_{u,y}E(y).
\]
Eliminate this formula by DE. Its local hypothesis is precisely
$\Q_yE(y)$, since the existential coordinate does not occur. Return
that hypothesis by Hyp and close DE. This proves the required closure
under exactly the original retained context. This is the construction
already formalized as \file{NPrf.qIProg}.

\emph{From $\CD$ to $\CQ$.}
Suppose an old DProg has matrix $E(x,y)$, witness $t$, and translated
premise $\pi:\Gamma,E(t,z)\vdash E(t,Sz)$. Set $F(y):=E(t,y)$.
The witness $t$ is now a passive parameter of this fixed literal-equation
template. QInd gives
\[
                      q:\Gamma\vdash_{\CQ}\Q_yF(y).
\]
Use DCase for the original matrix $E$ and the original witness $t$.
Its local assumption is $F(0)$. Weaken $q$ into that context and use
QE at $Sz$ to derive $F(Sz)$. DCase concludes the original
$\D_{x,y}E(x,y)$. The old freshness condition on $t$ is exactly the
one needed to make it a passive parameter in QInd. The original premise
$\pi$ is used once. Structural induction on the original proof now gives
both translations.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact inference-node bounds]
If a $\CQ$ derivation has $N$ inference nodes and $r$ QInd nodes,
the displayed translation has $N+2r\leq3N$ old-calculus nodes.
If a $\CD$ derivation has $N$ nodes and $r$ DProg nodes, its
translation has $N+3r\leq4N$ new-calculus nodes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
QInd is replaced by DProg, DE, and Hyp, adding two nodes. DProg is
replaced by QInd, DCase, QE, and Hyp, adding three. In both replacements
the transformed premise occurs once. All other nodes are copied once.
\end{proof}

The bounds concern inference trees. They are not bounds on formula
length, serialized JSON bytes, runtime, or normalized checker source.
The executable translators record these exact equalities on their
regression examples.

\section{Induction is now an immediate composition}

\begin{corollary}[Contextual equation induction]
For every literal equation $E$,
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash E(0)\qquad\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash E(t)}
\]
is admissible, with the usual freshness condition.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply QInd to the step premise and QE to the resulting closure and
the given base proof.
\end{proof}

For a concrete example, let $J(n,a)=I(n,a;u.u)$. We have
$J(0,a)=a$ by I0 and $J(Sz,a)=J(z,a)$ by IS. Under the induction
assumption $J(z,a)=a$, transport yields $J(Sz,a)=a$. Thus QInd proves
\[
                 \Q_y\bigl(J(y,a)=a\bigr),
\]
and QE with the base computation gives $J(t,a)=a$ for any $t$.
The package contains closed and parameterized certificates for this
example and verifies their translations both ways.

Allowing QInd at arbitrary formulas would be an actual strengthening:
the same two-rule composition would supply induction for arbitrary
quantified formulas. The literal-equation guard cannot be omitted merely
because it makes a checker shorter. Conversely, forcing progress for
all atomic $\D$ introductions would lose the atomic case rule; it is
not the present construction. Here that case rule is retained unchanged.

\section{Recovering full intuitionistic logic}

Theorem~\ref{thm:equivalence} transfers the entire existing logical
reconstruction, not just its closed theorems. Some details are given
here to identify precisely what is inherited; the complete written
construction is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{sec:choice-introduction}.

Equality reflexivity follows from computation and transport. Put
$r=I(0,t;u.0)$ and use $r=t$ twice in Tr with motive $v=t$; the
conclusion is $t=t$. Symmetry, transitivity and congruence follow by the
usual explicit equality motives.

Numerical cases for every native formula follow without induction:
from $\Gamma\vdash F(0)$ and $\Gamma\vdash F(Sz)$, DCase with an
unused existential coordinate produces $\D_{u,y}F(y)$. DE supplies
$\Q_yF(y)$ and QE gives $F(t)$. This is a zero/successor case rule,
not an induction rule for arbitrary $F$.

Let $C(v,a,b):=I(v,a;u.b)$. Its equations distinguish zero from a
successor. Recursive gates on fixed formula syntax give $G_vA$ with
\[
                  \vdash G_0A,\qquad G_{Sv}A\dashv\vdash A.
\]
A second recursive construction gives a selector $M_v(A,B)$ with
$M_0(A,B)\dashv\vdash A$ and $M_{Sv}(A,B)\dashv\vdash B$.
Unequal formula depths are aligned by the derivable padding
$A\dashv\vdash\D_{u,y}G_yA$. These are finite syntactic macros,
not quantification over formulas in the object theory.

With $p(z)=I(z,0;u.C(u,1,0))$, the full logical macro inventory is
\begin{align*}
 A\Rightarrow_* B&:=\D_{u,z}M_z(A,B),\\
 A\wedge_* B&:=\D_{u,z}G_zM_{p(z)}(A,B),\\
 \exists_*x\,A(x)&:=\D_{x,y}G_yA(x),\\
 A\vee_* B&:=\exists_*x\,M_x(A,B),\\
 \forall_*x\,A(x)&:=A(0)\wedge_*\D_{u,z}G_zA(z),\\
 \bot_*&:=(0=S0).
\end{align*}
All displayed unused variables are chosen fresh. The gate and selector
endpoints, cases and DE/QE establish the ordinary intuitionistic
introduction and elimination rules. In particular, the two fixed
instances $p(1)=1$ and $p(2)=0$ give both conjunction projections.
Disjunction elimination uses numerical cases on its existential selector.

Under $0=S0$, computation and transport equate arbitrary numerical
terms using $C(0,s,t)=s$ and $C(1,s,t)=t$. External induction on
finite formula syntax then derives every native formula. Thus the
designated contradiction has the required explosion rule without
postulating it separately.

The auxiliary $\Q_yB$ is interderivable with $\D_{u,y}B$ for unused
$u$. One direction uses DE and Hyp. The other uses DCase and QE under
the local base assumption. This works for arbitrary matrices; it does
not license unrestricted QInd.

\section{Bi-interpretability and equiconsistency}

\subsection{The ordinary realization}

Let $T_{\rm eq}$ be ordinary intuitionistic first-order equality logic
over the same staged iterator terms, with I0 and IS, contextual induction
for literal equations, numerical coverage
\[
                \forall x\,(x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy),
\]
and the explicit falsity bridge $(0=S0)\to\bot$.
Staging means that each fixed update description is introduced only after
its component descriptions; no numerical input is interpreted as an
arbitrary program to evaluate.

Every $\CQ$ rule is sound under the displayed ordinary readings.
For QInd, temporarily assume $E(0)$, apply contextual equation induction
to the supplied step, introduce the universal quantifier, and discharge
the base. DCase uses numerical coverage under the retained base assumption.
All eliminations and transport are ordinary logical inferences.

Conversely, the recovered logical operations give ordinary equality
logic; the new QInd/QE composition gives equation induction; the
derived cases give coverage; and native explosion supplies the falsity
bridge under the interpretation. The bridge must remain explicit in
the ordinary theory: positive equations alone admit a singleton model.

\subsection{The numerical compiler and its order of dependencies}

The inherited arithmetic proof proceeds in an order that avoids assuming
the very functions being constructed:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Scalar iteration, equation induction and cases yield a fixed term
      $P$ with $P0=0$ and $P(Sn)=n$.
\item Truncated subtraction and a numerical characteristic of actual
      equality are constructed and proved correct on the entire domain.
\item Each fixed quantifier-free formula is converted to a characteristic
      equation. Equation induction therefore gives its contextual
      quantifier-free induction instance.
\item Pairing and projections are then constructed, and every fixed
      general primitive-recursive description is compiled into a scalar
      iterator term.
\end{enumerate}
The complete predecessor, reflection and primitive-recursion arguments
are supplied in the attributed appendices.

For example, with pairing $\pi$ and projections $L,H$, a recursion
$f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p)$ and
$f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p)$ is implemented by
\[
 V(n,\vec p)=I\!\left(n,\pi(0,g^*(\vec p));
 u.\pi(SL(u),h^*(L(u),H(u),\vec p))\right),
 \qquad f^*(n,\vec p)=H(V(n,\vec p)).
\]
Equation induction proves $L(V(n,\vec p))=n$, and the projection
identities prove the required defining equations. This construction is
external recursion on a finite function description.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-domain bi-interpretation]
The ordinary realization of $\CQ$ is bi-interpretable with full
first-order \ipra. Both numerical composites are internally isomorphic
to the identity by the graph $x=y$. All formula composites are provably
equivalent, and $\CQ$ and \ipra\ are equiconsistent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The pinned development establishes the native $\CD$/$T_{\rm eq}$
formula round trip and the $T_{\rm eq}$/\ipra\ numerical and formula
round trips. Theorem~\ref{thm:equivalence} preserves every native
sequent on the same syntax, so these results already transfer.

More explicitly, \ipra\ interprets a fixed initialized iterator by
primitive recursion whose update ignores the recursion counter. It proves
the iterator equations, coverage, separation and equation induction.
The reverse interpretation uses the scalar compiler and the preceding
predecessor and equality-reflection results. Internal equation induction
proves uniqueness of each fixed iterator and each fixed primitive
recursion with its given defining equations. External induction on
descriptions therefore proves that double translation is internally
equal to the original function at every argument, including nonstandard
arguments in models.

The numerical domain and equality do not change. For logical syntax,
the recovered macros have their stated ordinary meanings, and in the
native calculus
\[
 \D_{x,y}B\dashv\vdash
 \exists_*x\bigl(B(x,0)\Rightarrow_*\forall_*y\,B(x,y)\bigr).
\]
Forward, DE opens its witness; under the base the local closure supplies
every instance, so introduce universal, implication and existential
macros. Backward, eliminate the existential and use DCase: the retained
base, implication and universal give the successor premise. The analogous
$\Q$ identity follows by the unused-witness conversion. External induction
on formulas proves both formula composites. The explicit falsity bridge
preserves contradiction. Effective proof translations in both directions
give equiconsistency; there is no claim of a theory proving its own
consistency.
\end{proof}

The baseline files \file{BiInterpretation.lean} and
\file{NativeComposite.lean} contain the formal statements
\file{biInterp}, \file{teq_consistent_iff},
\file{NEquiv.ofFm_toFm}, and \file{NPrf.iff_teq}.
This report inspected their source. It did not rebuild the repository's
Lean project, and it does not equate the Python regression evidence with
proof-assistant verification.

\file{ast.unparse} followed by one final newline. Significant tokens

\section{Executable proof translations and validation}

\subsection{Strict certificate syntax}

Expressions are tagged JSON arrays. Variables are \file{["v",i]},
zero is \file{["z"]}, successor is \file{["s",t]}, and iteration is
\file{["i",n,a,h]}. The update binds variable zero. Equations,
closures and paired formulas use tags \file{"e"}, \file{"q"} and
\file{"d"}; their field counts and binding arities are validated.
The $\D$ matrix has $y$ at index zero and $x$ at index one.

A certificate states its number of free numerical parameters, retained
context, goal, and proof tree. Every declared input formula is checked
before derivation. Each rule validates its local templates; the derived
formula is then compared with the declared goal. Integers used as
indices must be actual nonnegative Python integers, so JSON booleans do
not silently serve as variable or hypothesis indices. There is no
evaluation of certificate text as Python.

\begin{theorem}[Strict checker correctness]
Under adequate resources, the direct-$\Q$ checker accepts a certificate
exactly when its stated well-scoped proof tree is a $\CQ$ derivation of
its stated sequent. Every finite $\CQ$ derivation has such a certificate.
The analogous statements hold for the matched old checker and $\CD$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The signature validator is a structural recursion on finite expressions.
Its variable case checks the current numerical scope; every other case
checks the exact tag, sort and number of children, increasing scope only
in the prescribed binding fields. Simultaneous substitution extends an
image map under $b$ binders by fixing the first $b$ indices and shifting
each substituted outer term by $b$. Structural induction gives the usual
capture-free substitution and weakening lemmas.

Now induct on a successful derivation call. Hyp returns a valid context
entry. The computation cases construct precisely I0 and IS. Tr checks
its equality major and the source instance before returning the target
instance. QE checks its closure and base. DE checks its fresh-witness
branch against the shifted conclusion. DCase checks its witness-instantiated
matrix under the shifted zero instance and at the successor of the new
variable. QInd checks a literal equation and exactly its progression
premise. In each case the returned formula is the displayed rule's
conclusion under its stated side conditions. Unrecognized rules fail.
Initial well-scoping and these local checks establish soundness.

Conversely, encode a finite derivation using de Bruijn indices, renaming
eigenvariables first. Every valid rule application satisfies the corresponding
branch tests. Induction on the derivation proves acceptance. No
normalization of arbitrary raw aliases is required in this strict interface.
\end{proof}

The compilers in \file{code/translate.py} are untrusted producers. Their
outputs are accepted only after the independent destination checker checks
them. The compiler implements numerical substitution and context
embedding for proof trees, including local binders and retained-hypothesis
indices. These operations realize the two constructions in
Theorem~\ref{thm:equivalence}.

The principal suite tests computation, reflexivity, literal equation
induction, DCase, actual use of the fresh closure in DE, transport under
binders, free parameters, both proof translations and their round trips.
It also rejects wrong conclusions, unscoped terms, Boolean indices,
negative hypothesis indices, escaped eigenvariables and compound
progression. Tests run the public command-line checkers in ordinary
Python and with \file{-O}; the checks do not depend on assertions.
Additional targeted review tests exercise actual retained-hypothesis
reindexing and nonvacuous parameterized witnesses. Exact counts and
review notes are distributed in \file{evidence/}.

Finite tests are implementation evidence. The universal equivalence and
interpretation claims are the written mathematical theorems above and
their attributed inherited dependencies.

\section{Lean migration and its present verification boundary}

The proposed migration replaces the native constructor
\file{dIProg} by \file{qIInd}. The matrix terms move from
\file{Tm (n + 2)} to \file{Tm (n + 1)}, and the witness argument
\file{t : Tm n} disappears. The local constructor shrinks from 270
to 214 source bytes excluding its documentation; its explicit term
inputs fall from three to two, and two witness-instantiation expressions
disappear. These are local measurements, not a total Lean-project size
comparison.

The included patch also updates the corresponding induction cases in
weakening, substitution, cut and native soundness. It restores
\file{NPrf.dIProg} as a derived compatibility theorem, while
\file{NPrf.qIProg} becomes a one-line alias for the new constructor.
Keeping the old derived interface should reduce disruption for downstream
proofs.

\textbf{Verification status:} the patch passed \file{git apply --check}
against the pinned source copies. It has not been compiled or checked by
Lean in this environment. Patch applicability is not elaboration or kernel
verification. The patch is a reviewable migration proposal, not a claim
that the formal development has already been updated successfully.

\section{A small certificate tradeoff and a rejected false improvement}

The ancillary \file{experiments/counter_vectors/} directory explores an
independent change to the inherited compact checker. It supplies the
computation counter as an empty or nonempty source vector. The empty
vector selects zero computation; a vector beginning with $n$ selects
successor computation, even when $n$ is the literal zero term.
Consequently $I(S0,a;u.h)$ can be certified directly, without first
replacing its predecessor by a syntactically nonzero representative of zero.

The sound canonical branch uses \file{s[:1] or 0}. The vector's head is
used in the recursive call; additional tail annotations do not change
the computed predecessor. The whole checker has 533 normalized bytes,
271 tokens, 314 AST nodes, and a 336-byte gzip representation. It costs
four bytes more than the 529-byte baseline. Its 300 process checks have
zero mismatches. It is a certificate simplification with a size cost,
not the selected answer to the size-minimization problem.

A tempting 528-byte candidate omits \file{or 0}. It then writes the
empty array in base counters where the established logical and substitution
protocol uses scalar zero. Both expressions have the same intended
numerical decoding, but the checker compares raw syntax. A canonical
base certificate fails, and no universal alias-conversion proof was
established. Therefore the 528-byte source is included only as a rejected
experiment and is not counted as a complete proof checker. This is a
completeness gap, not a demonstrated proof of a false theorem.

\section{Conclusion and next concrete step}

Choosing direct closure induction removes an unnecessary existential
witness from the primitive induction interface. The presentation is
fully specified, preserves every native sequent, and retains the
whole-domain bi-interpretation and equiconsistency with \ipra. The
change makes the corresponding explicit checker branch shorter, and
the executable proof compilers make the equivalence reviewable at the
certificate level.

The outstanding target is a genuinely smaller complete implementation
below the current compact frontier. This investigation has not reached
it. The useful next formal step is to elaborate the supplied Lean
migration against the pinned project and preserve the old derived
\file{dIProg} interface. A further size claim should wait for a complete
checker whose soundness, canonical completeness and entire charged source
have all been established.
