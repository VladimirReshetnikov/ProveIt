\begin{abstract}
The current native calculus transports equality through a pair of numerical
term templates. We replace that rule by the single-context crossed rule
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash h(s)=u}
      {\Gamma\vdash u=h(t)}.
\]
Two applications recover the original two-template transport exactly.
Conversely, the original rule followed by derived symmetry proves the new
rule. All terms, formulas, scopes, contexts, and seven remaining primitive
constructors are unchanged. Consequently the two calculi derive exactly the
same sequents, and the existing full bi-interpretation with intuitionistic
primitive recursive arithmetic transfers with both numerical composites
still the identity graph.

This is a small structural simplification, not a new arithmetic basis or a
claim to an absolute minimum. Matched, explicitly typed standalone checkers
measure the effect of the narrower primitive rule. A separate implementation
result factors a strict public-goal validator into shape checking and the
scope checking already performed by the unchanged kernel. Its acceptance
language is proved identical to the corresponding existing strict checker.
The compact incoming envelope, 257 tokens / 279 AST nodes / 522 normalized
bytes, remains a different contract and is not claimed to be beaten.

The package includes the complete presentation, both proof translations,
standalone checkers, executable certificate tests, reproducible whole-module
measurements including gzip-9, and three Lean extension files. The new universal
metatheorems are written proofs. The Lean extensions were checked against the
pinned source signatures but could not be compiled in this environment.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is simpler, and what is not}
\label{sec:claims}
The requested comparison is with
\file{Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA}. Its current Lean
kernel, \code{NPrf}, has eight constructors. Its primitive equality rule is
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad
       \Gamma\vdash a(s)=b(s)}
      {\Gamma\vdash a(t)=b(t)}\quad(\Tr).
 \tag{1}\label{eq:old}
\]
The new calculus \(\Ocal\) replaces this by
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash h(s)=u}
      {\Gamma\vdash u=h(t)}\quad(\One).
 \quad}\tag{2}\label{eq:new}
\]
Only one term template is supplied and instantiated. The other endpoint
\(u\) is already present in the minor premise. The exchange of endpoints
makes two successive one-context replacements reproduce an arbitrary
simultaneous replacement in both endpoints.

The following distinctions matter when assessing the result.
\begin{itemize}
\item The primitive equality interface becomes smaller: two moving numerical
  templates become one. The rule has the same two premises.
\item The total number of primitive constructors remains eight. The term
  grammar, formula grammar, arithmetic equations, and induction restriction
  are unchanged.
\item The full native language remains available. There is no restriction to
  guarded public formulas and no new mixed interpretation of atomic binders.
\item The direct comparison is with the existing native kernel, not a claim
  that the idea of one-sided equational replacement is historically new.
  Related crossed transport is already in the incoming reversal package.
\item A smaller matched implementation does not imply a new minimum among
  all implementations. The shortest current arity-coded programs share
  machinery between logical elimination and arbitrary-template transport;
  that sharing is a separate consideration.
\end{itemize}

The repository README reports older compact minima, while six newer ZIP
submissions are present in its incoming directory. We authenticated all six
against their Git blob hashes and inspected their principal claims and
checkers. The strongest incoming coordinate envelope is already
\(257/279/522\), achieved by three different programs. These are observed
upper bounds on an unknown optimum, not a proved lower bound.
Section~\ref{sec:measure} gives comparisons under explicit input contracts.
\cite{readme,incoming,guardedsource,reversal}

\section{The complete native presentation}
\label{sec:presentation}
No ordinary logical calculus is silently included in this section. All
primitive syntax, axioms, inference rules, and freshness conditions are
listed here.

\subsection{Terms, formulas, and contexts}
There is one numerical sort. Terms are finite expressions
\[
 t ::= x\mid 0\mid S(t)\mid \I(n,a;v.h).
 \tag{3}
\]
In the iterator only \(v\) is bound in \(h\); it is not bound in \(n\) or
\(a\). Passive numerical parameters may occur in every term. Substitution
is capture-avoiding, including inside iterator bodies. Put \(1:=S0\).

Formulas are finite expressions
\[
 A ::= (s=t)\mid \Q_y B\mid \D_{x,y}B.
 \tag{4}
\]
The first binder binds \(y\). The second binds \(x\) and \(y\), with
\(y\) the innermost variable in the supplied de Bruijn implementations.
Contexts \(\Gamma\) are finite reusable lists of well-scoped formulas.
A derivation is finite. The designated contradiction is \(0=1\).

The notation \(h(s)\) in rule~\eqref{eq:new} means substitution for a
single \emph{designated variable}, not a restriction to one occurrence.
That variable may occur repeatedly or not at all in \(h\). In particular,
constant templates are legal. The terms \(s,t,u\) and the passive
parameters of \(h\) lie in the ambient scope. The designated placeholder
is not a new free variable in \(u\).

\subsection{All primitive axioms and rules}
The two computation axioms, available in every context, are
\begin{align}
 \Gamma&\vdash\I(0,a;v.h)=a, &&(\mathrm I_0)\tag{5}\label{eq:i0}\\
 \Gamma&\vdash\I(Sn,a;v.h)=h[\I(n,a;v.h)/v].
 &&(\mathrm I_S)\tag{6}\label{eq:is}
\end{align}
The other six constructors are
\[
 \frac{}{\Gamma\vdash A}\ (A\in\Gamma)\quad(\mathrm{Hyp}),
 \qquad
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash h(s)=u}
      {\Gamma\vdash u=h(t)}\quad(\One),
 \tag{7}
\]
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash\Q_yB(y)\qquad\Gamma\vdash B(0)}
      {\Gamma\vdash B(t)}\quad(\mathrm{QE}),
 \tag{8}
\]
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
       \Gamma,\Q_yB(b,y)\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash A}\quad(\mathrm{DE}),
 \tag{9}
\]
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)}\quad(\mathrm{DI}_{\rm case}),
 \tag{10}
\]
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash\Q_yE(y)}\quad(\mathrm{QI}_{\rm eq}),
 \qquad E\text{ a literal equation}.
 \tag{11}
\]
In DE, \(b\) is fresh for \(\Gamma,A\), and every passive parameter of
\(B\). In DI, \(z\) is fresh for \(\Gamma,t\), and the passive parameters
of \(B\). In QI, \(z\) is fresh for \(\Gamma\) and the passive parameters
of \(E\). Displayed temporary assumptions are discharged. Rules with
local assumptions are read after the usual capture-avoiding weakening
into the larger numerical scope.

There are no other primitive axioms or rules. Reflexivity, symmetry,
transitivity, congruence, transport inside arbitrary formulas, ordinary
logical connectives, numerical cases, and quantifier-free induction are
derived. In particular, unrestricted induction on arbitrary formulas is
\emph{not} licensed by (11).

\subsection{Ordinary interpretation of the binders}
The intended first-order readings are
\begin{align*}
 (\Q_y B)^\circ&:=B^\circ(0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(y),\\
 (\D_{x,y}B)^\circ&:=\exists x\bigl(B^\circ(x,0)\to
                            \forall y\,B^\circ(x,y)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Equality is actual numerical equality. These are definitions of a
translation, not extra primitive introduction or elimination rules.
They apply to every native formula in (4), including unguarded equations
under either binder.

\section{The exact two-step reduction}
\label{sec:equivalence}
Let \(\N\) be the current native system with rule~\eqref{eq:old} and the
same seven other constructors. Its existing development derives symmetry
from computation and transport. We first give direct equality facts in
\(\Ocal\), so the role of the crossed orientation is explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Elementary equality in the new calculus]
Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and numerical congruence are derivable
in \(\Ocal\), using only computation and \(\One\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For reflexivity at a term \(a\), put \(r:=\I(0,a;v.0)\).
The computation equation \(r=a\), used for both premises of \(\One\)
with the identity template \(h(w)=w\), yields \(a=a\).

For symmetry of \(s=t\), use this same proof in both premises and choose
the constant template \(h(w)=s\). The minor premise is \(s=t\), and the
conclusion is \(t=s\). This is why a constant template must be allowed.

For transitivity, let \(p:s=t\) and \(q:t=u\). Symmetry first gives
\(p':t=s\). Apply \(\One\) with major \(q\), identity template, and
minor \(p'\); the result is \(s=u\).

For congruence of a numerical template \(h\), derive
\(h(s)=h(s)\) by reflexivity. With major \(s=t\), \(\One\) gives
\(h(s)=h(t)\). No induction or numerical case distinction was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Two contexts from one]
\label{lem:two}
Rule~\eqref{eq:old} is admissible in \(\Ocal\). Each occurrence is
simulated by two occurrences of \(\One\), with the same major premise.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(p:\Gamma\vdash s=t\) and
\(q:\Gamma\vdash a(s)=b(s)\). The first application, with template
\(a\), gives
\[
 \frac{p:s=t\qquad q:a(s)=b(s)}{b(s)=a(t)}\ (\One).
\]
The second, with template \(b\), gives
\[
 \frac{p:s=t\qquad b(s)=a(t)}{a(t)=b(t)}\ (\One).
\]
The second fixed endpoint is the ambient term \(a(t)\). The two steps
introduce no variables or assumptions. Reusing \(p\) is legitimate in
the reusable native calculus.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The new rule in the old calculus]
\label{lem:one}
Rule~\eqref{eq:new} is admissible in \(\N\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \(\Tr\) with left template \(h\) and right template the constant
\(u\). From \(s=t\) and \(h(s)=u\) it gives \(h(t)=u\).
Derived symmetry in the existing native calculus gives \(u=h(t)\).
The existing symmetry proof does not use \(\One\), so this simulation
is not circular. In de Bruijn syntax the constant template is the
weakening of \(u\), and its instantiation at either endpoint is \(u\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact identity-language consequence equivalence]
\label{thm:exact}
For every numerical scope, every finite context \(\Gamma\), and every
well-scoped native formula \(A\),
\[
                  \Gamma\vdash_{\Ocal} A
             \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                  \Gamma\vdash_{\N} A.
 \tag{12}
\]
Both proof transformations are effective on finite derivations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the supplied derivation. For \(\Ocal\to\N\), use
Lemma~\ref{lem:one} in the only changed constructor. For
\(\N\to\Ocal\), use Lemma~\ref{lem:two}. In each of the seven other
cases, apply the identical constructor to the transformed premises.
All local scopes, hypotheses, eigenvariable conditions, and literal-equation
restrictions are unchanged. Thus the induction applies to open sequents
and to premises under both binders, not merely to closed theorems.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Structural rules]
Weakening, renaming, capture-avoiding numerical substitution, and cut are
admissible in \(\Ocal\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Translate to \(\N\), apply the corresponding established admissible
rule, and translate back. Alternatively substitution is directly stable:
substitute into \(h\) under its one designated placeholder and into
\(s,t,u\) in the ambient scope. Unlike a literal-source dispatch
restriction, \(\One\) has no syntactic nonzero side condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Proof size]
The local simulation uses two inference applications. A proof DAG can
share the major proof. A literal tree or nested JSON representation may
copy that subtree twice. Repeated translations can therefore enlarge
serialized proof trees substantially. No linear tree-size bound or
identity of proof objects is claimed. The interpretation statement is
about formulas and derivability.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Preserving the existing seven-rule presentation}

The same reduction applies to the repository's seven-rule calculus
$\mathcal W$, formalized as \texttt{WPrf} in
\texttt{NativeOneIntro.lean}. It need not increase the primitive rule
count. Define $\mathcal O_7$ by retaining its terms, formulas, ordinary
readings, hypothesis rule, two computation equations, QE and DE, and
replacing its two-context equality rule by the one-context crossed rule
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash h(s)=u}
      {\Gamma\vdash u=h(t)}.
\]
Its remaining primitive rule is the inherited single introduction
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta_{B,t}(z))\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)},
 \qquad
 \eta_{B,t}(z)=
 \begin{cases}
   z,&\text{if $B$ is a literal equation and $t$ is literally $0$,}\\
   0,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
Here $z$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, $t$, and every fixed parameter of $B$;
the displayed assumption is discharged. The condition concerns finite
syntax, not provable numerical equality. DE retains its usual fresh
witness condition. Thus the complete primitive inventory is Hyp,
$I_0$, $I_S$, one-context transport, QE, DE, and this introduction:
seven schemas in total.

The two-step transport derivation already given proves
$\mathcal W\to\mathcal O_7$ by induction on derivations, leaving every
other rule unchanged. In the converse direction, ordinary transport
with the constant right-hand term $u$, followed by the existing derived
symmetry rule, simulates one-context transport. Hence
\[
 \Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal O_7}A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal W}A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal N}A.
\]
The second equivalence is the repository's
\texttt{wprf\_iff\_nprf}; the new direct translations are specified as
\texttt{O7Prf.toWPrf} and \texttt{O7Prf.ofWPrf} in
\texttt{OneContextOneIntro.lean}. They yield
\texttt{o7prf\_iff\_wprf} and \texttt{o7prf\_iff\_nprf}.
The unchanged native interpretations therefore give the same
bi-interpretation and equiconsistency with iPRA, including both formula
round trips and identity numerical composites.

This composition retains the existing single-introduction construction;
the new simplification concerns its equality rule. Its literal-witness
dispatch also retains the existing limitation that substitution is not
proved by direct induction on constructors: a substitution can turn a
nonzero-looking witness into the literal term $0$. The equivalence
argument above assumes no such structural induction. The accompanying
Lean module is explicitly uncompiled. No standalone-checker measurements
are claimed for this seven-rule composition; the reported matched
checker comparison concerns $\mathcal N$ and the eight-rule
$\mathcal O$.

\section{Logical completeness is retained}
\label{sec:logic}
Equation~(12) immediately transfers all native derived logical rules.
For completeness of the presentation, this section spells out the
macros rather than leaving the words ``intuitionistic logic'' as an
additional trusted primitive. The constructions are the existing native
ones, now admissible in \(\Ocal\).\cite{gates,logic}

\subsection{Selectors on terms and formulas}
Define the term selector
\[
 C(v,a,b):=\I(v,a;u.b),
\]
where \(u\) is unused. Computation proves
\(C(0,a,b)=a\) and \(C(Sn,a,b)=b\).

The following mutual definitions are finite recursion on formula syntax.
Every newly displayed bound variable is renamed fresh for the fixed
arguments before the operation. First define a formula selecting between
an equation and a formula:
\begin{align*}
 K(v;s,t;r=w)&:=(C(v,s,r)=C(v,t,w)),\\
 K(v;s,t;\Q_yB)&:=\Q_y K(v;C(y,0,s),C(y,0,t);B),\\
 K(v;s,t;\D_{x,y}B)&:=\D_{x,y}
                       K(v;C(y,0,s),C(y,0,t);B).
\end{align*}
Put \(\Gate(v,A):=K(v;0,0;A)\). Then define
\begin{align*}
 \Sel(v,s=t,B)&:=K(v;s,t;B),\\
 \Sel(v,\Q_y A,B)&:=\Q_y\Sel(v,A,\Gate(y,B)),\\
 \Sel(v,\D_{x,y} A,B)&:=\D_{x,y}\Sel(v,A,\Gate(y,B)).
\end{align*}
These are macro definitions, not new constructors. The inherited proofs,
transferred through Theorem~\ref{thm:exact}, give
\[
 \vdash\Gate(0,A),\qquad
 \Gate(Sn,A)\dashv\vdash A,
\]
\[
 \Sel(0,A,B)\dashv\vdash A,\qquad
 \Sel(Sn,A,B)\dashv\vdash B.
\]
The gate clauses in the recursive definitions are needed under binders;
naively placing term selectors at matching atomic leaves would not
handle formulas of different shapes.

\subsection{All ordinary connectives}
Define
\[
 \parity(y):=\I(y,0;u.C(u,1,0)).
\]
With fresh auxiliary variables where appropriate, use the following macros.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Ordinary notation & Native formula\\
\midrule
\(\bot\) & \(0=1\)\\
\(A\to B\) & \(\Q_y\Sel(y,A,B)\)\\
\(A\wedge B\) & \(\Q_y\Gate(y,\Sel(\parity(y),A,B))\)\\
\(\exists x\,A(x)\) & \(\D_{x,y}\Gate(y,A(x))\)\\
\(A\vee B\) & \(\exists v\,\Sel(v,A,B)\), using the preceding macro\\
\(\forall x\,A(x)\) & \(A(0)\wedge\Q_xA(x)\)\\
\(\neg A\) & \(A\to\bot\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All introduction and elimination rules of intuitionistic natural deduction
are derivable for these exact formulas. No classical excluded-middle
schema is added.

For example, Q-introduction by cases is derived by applying DI with an
unused witness coordinate and then DE. Thus a derivation of \(B(Sz)\)
from \(B(0)\) gives \(\Q_yB\). This supplies implication introduction
through the selector. QE at \(1\) gives implication elimination. For
existential elimination, DE opens a numerical witness; its gated Q-clause
has a provable zero instance and QE at \(1\) gives the existential matrix.

Explosion is also derived. From \(0=1\), congruence of
\(C(-,s,t)\) and its two computation equations give every equation
\(s=t\). Formula induction using Q-introduction and DI then gives every
compound formula. This is a proof-theoretic fact of the native system;
positive numerical equations alone, without the designated contradiction
and logical interpretation, would admit a singleton algebra.

\section{Bi-interpretation with full first-order iPRA}
\label{sec:bi}
Here \(\ipra\) means intuitionistic first-order arithmetic with equality,
a symbol for every fixed finite primitive-recursive definition, the defining
equations, successor separation and injectivity, and quantifier-free
induction with arbitrary retained side assumptions. This is the theory
\code{PRAAx} used by the pinned repository. The description of a function
is external finite syntax; no uniform total evaluator for all descriptions
is inserted into the object language.\cite{nativebi,ordinarybi}

\subsection{The existing arithmetic bridge}
The intermediate ordinary theory \(T_{\rm eq}\) uses the scalar iterator
terms in intuitionistic equality logic. It has computation equations
\eqref{eq:i0}--\eqref{eq:is}, coverage
\[
 \forall n\,(n=0\vee\exists m\,n=Sm),
\]
separation \((0=S0)\to\bot\), and induction for literal equations with
side assumptions. The pinned development establishes the following in
order: scalar predecessor; reflection of actual equality by a Boolean
term; quantifier-free induction from equation induction; pairing and its
projections; and compilation of arbitrary primitive recursion.

These are substantial inherited theorems. The one-context argument does
not re-prove them and does not remove their hypotheses. In particular,
we do not cancel successor in a weaker self-seeded arithmetic before
injectivity has been established. The current Lean theorems already supply
the bridge, with formula round trips and whole-domain numerical identity.
The attributed written foundations are included as reference source files
in the archive.\cite{foundations,ordinarybi}

\subsection{The forward interpretation}
An iterator with a fixed body \(h\) and passive parameters is primitive
recursive: iterate the primitive-recursive function denoted by \(h\),
starting at the denotation of \(a\), for the number denoted by \(n\).
Translate terms recursively in this way. Translate equality as equality,
and translate Q and D by their ordinary readings in
Section~\ref{sec:presentation}.

For the new equality rule, substitution of equals gives
\(h(t)=u\); symmetry gives \(u=h(t)\). All other rule soundness is
inherited. Thus this translation sends every \(\Ocal\)-derivation to an
\(\ipra\)-derivation.

\subsection{The reverse interpretation}
Use the existing scalar compilation of each fixed primitive-recursive
function. Zero, successor, projections, and composition translate
directly. To see the shape of the only substantial clause, let
\(f(0,\vec a)=g(\vec a)\) and
\(f(Sn,\vec a)=h(n,f(n,\vec a),\vec a)\). Once scalar pairing
\(\langle-,-\rangle\) and projections \(L,R\) are established, the
compiled term is
\[
 R\!\left(\I\left(n,\langle0,g(\vec a)\rangle;
   u.\left\langle S(Lu),h(Lu,Ru,\vec a)\right\rangle\right)\right).
 \tag{13}
\]
Its state carries both the current counter and the recursive value.
Equation induction proves its defining equations and the needed
uniqueness facts. Ordinary logical operations translate to the macros in
Section~\ref{sec:logic}. Translate the resulting native proof from
\(\N\) to \(\Ocal\) by replacing each transport with the two-step
construction.

This is an algorithm on each finite function description and proof. It
does not assert that there is a single primitive-recursive evaluator for
arbitrarily complex primitive-recursive descriptions.

\begin{theorem}[Full bi-interpretation and equiconsistency]
The calculus \(\Ocal\), with its ordinary realization, is
bi-interpretable with full first-order \(\ipra\). Both numerical domains
are the entire original domain, interpreted equality is actual equality,
and both numerical composites are internally the identity graph \(x=y\).
Both formula composites are provably equivalent to the original formula.
Moreover,
\[
 \Ocal\vdash 0=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\ipra\vdash\bot.
 \tag{14}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(J:\N\to\ipra\) and \(K:\ipra\to\N\) be the pinned native
interpretations, including their established formula and numerical
round-trip theorems. The maps between \(\Ocal\) and \(\N\) in
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact} are literally the identity on every term and
formula; only derivations change. Compose those identity maps with
\(J\) and \(K\).

For a native formula \(A\), the existing native proofs of
\(A\dashv\vdash KJ(A)\) become \(\Ocal\)-proofs by exact sequent
equivalence. For an iPRA formula \(\varphi\), the existing iPRA proof of
\(\varphi\leftrightarrow JK(\varphi)\) is unchanged. The numerical
identity graphs are unchanged as well. This verifies both composites,
not merely mutual interpretation or agreement in the standard model.
The designated contradictions are preserved by these interpretations,
which gives (14).
\end{proof}

The theorem is relative only to the explicitly identified inherited
bridge, whose source is supplied and cited. It is not relative to an
unproved new arithmetic conjecture. It also does not assert either
theory's internal proof of its own consistency.

\section{A smaller strict checker without changing its kernel}
\label{sec:scope}
The second contribution concerns the existing arity-coded certificate
protocol. It is independent of the one-context theorem. Because of exact
sequent equivalence, a complete checker using the old derived transport
is also a complete theorem checker for \(\Ocal\). It need not use
\(\One\) as its serialized primitive instruction.

A strict public-goal checker validates two properties before accepting a
certificate: the sorted tree shape of the claimed formula, and the scope
of every variable in that formula. The existing proof kernel already
performs scope checking in its first no-image traversal of the goal,
\emph{before it consumes a certificate instruction}. We remove only the
duplicate scope work from the separate goal validator. The kernel and its
raw administrative proof language remain byte for byte unchanged.

\subsection{Relevant encoding invariant}
In the inherited codec, a formula starts at numerical depth \(d=0\).
Entering a list of arity \(r\) increments depth by \(5-r\). Thus formula
positions have even depth and term positions odd depth. In canonical
closed syntax, a numerical leaf is either zero or an odd bound-variable
slot \(j\geq3\) with \(j<d\). Formula trees use arities one for D,
two for equality, and, in the full profile, three equal children for Q.
Term trees use arity one for successor and three for the iterator.

The old validator checks all these conditions, including \(j<d\).
The kernel's opener also checks a leaf against its current scope
\(k\leq j<d\). On the initial call \(k=0\), no instantiation image
has been supplied. An out-of-scope canonical-shaped numerical leaf
therefore tries to open the non-JSON default sentinel and raises an
exception. It cannot reach the acceptance print statement. This is
fail-closed scope validation, not an assumption about the certificate.

\subsection{Factorization theorem}
Let \(V\) be the old strict goal validator. Let \(W\) retain its exact
integer/list type checks, sort checks, legal arities, canonical variable
parity and lower-bound checks, and Q repetition check, but omit the
upper-bound check \(j<d\). Its recursive state needs only the Boolean
sort rather than the numerical depth. Let \(U\) be successful initial
no-image opening by the unchanged kernel.

\begin{lemma}[Scope is already checked]
For every finite JSON tree admitted by the shape predicate \(W\),
\[
                      V(A)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W(A)\land U(A).
 \tag{15}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Under \(W\), every integer leaf is at a term position and is either
zero or an odd slot at least three. In particular it is nonnegative.
The old upper-bound condition is exactly the remaining initial opener
condition \(0\leq j<d\). If every leaf satisfies it, the opener preserves
all leaves and succeeds. If a leaf violates it, the depth is odd, so
the opener evaluates its missing instantiation image and fails. Structural
induction on the finite tree gives the result. All remaining old checks
are retained in \(W\); shape alone is not claimed to establish scope.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Identical accepted certificates]
Replace the old public-goal validation by \(W\), preserving the complete
kernel, driver, and any whole-certificate typing pass. For every finite
JSON certificate, the old checker accepts if and only if the new checker
accepts, under the normal-exit/\code{ACCEPT} convention, abstracting from finite machine resource exhaustion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the old validator accepts the goal, (15) shows that the new validator
accepts and the kernel's initial opening succeeds; both checkers then
execute the identical kernel on identical inputs. If the old validator
rejects and the new one accepts, (15) shows that the initial opening
fails before any proof instruction is consumed. Neither checker accepts.
Inputs rejected by \(W\), by JSON parsing, or by a preserved global type
check likewise cannot reach acceptance. No assertion statement is used
as an acceptance gate, so Python's optimized mode does not remove a
logical condition.
\end{proof}

For the Eq/D profile, the new validator's state is just
\(d=\mathrm{False}\) at a formula and \(d=\mathrm{True}\) at a term.
Its legal arities are \((1,2+d)\), and the child sort is
\(d\ \texttt{or}\ \mathrm{len}(A)=2\). This is a simplification of a
verified invariant, rather than a new interpretation of formerly unsafe
variables. The exact source and a separately written proof of the
factorization are included in the evidence directory.

\section{Implementations, measurements, and verification}
\label{sec:measure}
\subsection{Different contracts are kept separate}
The archive supplies two implementation families.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The direct tagged checkers implement \(\N\) and \(\Ocal\) with
  explicit syntax and scope validation, all eight constructors, and a
  common JSON harness. They are matched implementations: only the
  transport branch changes. The one-context checker is a complete
  standalone module and imports no proof producer or theorem database.
\item The arity-coded profiles retain the inherited smaller certificate
  kernel and apply Section~\ref{sec:scope}. Their public-goal validators
  are strict, while the rest of the certificate retains the specified
  inherited administrative language. A separate full-typing profile
  checks that every certificate node is an exact integer or list.
\end{enumerate}
The latter's soundness as an arithmetic proof checker relies on the
inherited kernel correctness theorem. The new factorization theorem is
stronger than a regression claim: it preserves its entire accepted
certificate language. The direct tagged checker provides an independent,
more explicit implementation of the new rule interface.

\begin{proposition}[Direct tagged checker correctness]
For either direct module, a valid finite proof tree is accepted for
\(\Gamma\vdash A\) exactly when it encodes a derivation of that sequent
in the corresponding eight-constructor calculus. Consequently every
derivable sequent has an accepted finite certificate, abstracting from
finite execution resources.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The term and formula validators implement (3)--(4) with de Bruijn scope
bounds. A structural induction on a well-formed tree shows that
\code{shift} is capture-avoiding weakening and \code{sub} is instantiation;
its zero-drop form implements replacement while retaining the ambient
scope. The iterator body adds one to the traversal depth, Q adds one,
and D adds two.

Now induct on the proof tree. Each \code{infer} branch checks the
corresponding constructor's parameters and recursively derives its
premises. DE shifts every retained assumption and the required conclusion
while adding a fresh witness; DI and QI construct exactly their shifted
local assumptions and successor targets. QI checks that its matrix is a
literal equation. In the new transport branch the template is validated
at one extra numerical variable, both recursively obtained premises must
be equations, and the minor's left endpoint must equal its source
instantiation. The returned equation is exactly the crossed conclusion.
The old branch similarly checks the two endpoint instantiations.
Finally the harness compares the inferred conclusion with the validated
public goal. This proves soundness of every successful inference.
Encoding each formal constructor by its displayed JSON alternative gives
the converse induction and completeness. Invalid tags, parameters, or
premises cannot supply an unchecked returned theorem.
\end{proof}

input parsing, substitutions, checks, and the acceptance driver. Only

convention. The source is normalized by stripping docstrings and applying
\file{ast.unparse}, with a final newline. Significant tokens exclude
layout, comments, and the end marker. AST nodes are counted by

\subsection{Tests and their meaning}
The direct receipt records 32 theorem fixtures, 12 negative fixtures,
240 command-line checks in ordinary and optimized Python, and zero
mismatches. It also records 300 substitution checks against an independent
standard-natural-number evaluator, with zero mismatches and zero budget

unique validator definition.

The direct suite contains proofs that use their induction hypothesis,
nested iterator bodies, opening of Q and D, numerical substitution,
and open equality contexts. The proof translations are executed in
both directions and checked by the target standalone module. Negative
controls include out-of-scope variables, wrong conclusions, compound
induction matrices, and malformed proof fields.

The strict-validator suite compares old and new outcomes on inherited
positive certificates and on invalid goals and raw protocol controls.
It also exercises the factorization invariant directly on finite syntax
samples. These are useful executable checks of the implementations and
proof translations. They do not constitute proof-assistant verification
of every Python execution or of the universal mathematical statements.

\section{Comparison with existing proposals}
The source inspection found several close antecedents that must not be
repackaged as new results.
\begin{itemize}
\item Equational-only transport, direct Q progress, the single
  witness-selected introduction, and the literal-zero source restriction
  are already present or formally compared in the current development.
\item Guarded atomic D matrices, deterministic introduction, and the
  source-dispatch combination already occur in the main research packages
  and the newer incoming submissions.
\item Protected iterator heads, mirror guards, and administrative orbit
  induction already repair specific source-restriction bootstrap problems.
\item Crossed transport of arbitrary formula templates, with reversal of
  an atomic conclusion, is already in \file{reversal_core}. Its
  induction-free retraction argument is not claimed here.
\end{itemize}
The local contribution in (2) is the restriction to a single numerical
term template together with the exact two-step theorem, the direct matched
checkers and translations, and the resulting transfer with unchanged
full formula syntax. The validator factorization is a separate local
implementation contribution. Neither is described as a major new
arithmetic foundation or a literature-wide priority result.

An absolute ``simplest axiomatization'' cannot be established by presenting
one shorter program: it requires a fixed admissible encoding and cost
model and a lower-bound argument. This report gives explicit finite
constructions and measured comparisons. It leaves that global optimization
question open.

\section{Further research questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can one-context transport be integrated with closure elimination in
  a compact protocol without reintroducing a second term template or
  losing the existing shared substitution code? A successful answer should
  beat the current compact envelope under the same public-goal contract.
\item Can the three Lean extension modules be checked in the pinned parent
  workspace and reduced to a smaller reusable equality-kernel interface?
  The proof translations are local enough to make this a focused next step.
\item Can a proof DAG certificate preserve sharing during both
  translations while keeping the complete checker smaller? Tree-size
  overhead should be measured separately from checker size.
\item Can a one-occurrence context restriction be proved adequate under
  the iterator binder? The present theorem allows repeated or absent
  occurrences and does not settle that stronger restriction.
\item Is there a genuinely smaller arithmetic basis that also reduces
  the complete checker after all compilation and interpretation machinery
  is accounted for? Removing a primitive equality test from the
  self-seeded proposals remains a substantive missing theorem, not a
  consequence of protected-orbit coverage.
\item Which strict input contract should be fixed for subsequent searches:
  Eq/D public formulas, full Eq/Q/D public formulas, or exact typing of
  the entire raw certificate? A stable contract would make future size
  comparisons easier to assess.
\end{enumerate}
