Independent audit: one-context crossed equality rule
====================================================

Reviewed against Native.lean from ConciseIPRA at the pinned snapshot.
This is a written mathematical audit, not a proof-assistant verification.

Candidate calculus X keeps all seven non-transport constructors of NPrf.
It replaces trEq by

        Gamma |- s=t       Gamma |- h(s)=u
        ---------------------------------
                   Gamma |- u=h(t).

Here h is ONE numerical term with one designated free placeholder. The
placeholder is syntactic and need not occur exactly once, or at all. The
terms s,t,u and the other free parameters of h are scoped in Gamma's
ambient numerical context. Substitution avoids capture beneath iterators.
No formula-level context and no extra induction schema is admitted.

1. Simulation of the old two-sided transport, exactly two X applications.

Given p:s=t and q:a(s)=b(s), first use h=a and u=b(s):
        r : b(s)=a(t).
Reuse p, use h=b and u=a(t), and r as the minor premise:
        a(t)=b(t).
Both u choices are ambient terms; neither binds the placeholder. This is
legal even when s,t,a,b share ambient free parameters. Reuse of p is
allowed because the native context is reusable, not linear.

2. Simulation of X in NPrf.

Apply old trEq with its left template h and its right template the
constant u, obtaining h(t)=u. Apply NPrf's already derived symmetry to
obtain u=h(t). The old symmetry theorem is derived independently from
NPrf's computation and old trEq rules, so there is no circularity.

3. Elementary equality facts inside X (useful for an independent proof).

Let r=I(0,v;z.0). The computation theorem e:r=v, used for both premises
of X with h(x)=x and u=v, yields v=v. Thus reflexivity is derived.

For p:s=t, use p for both premises, the constant context h(x)=s, and
u=t. This yields t=s. Thus symmetry needs one X application and no
extra reflexivity assumption.

Given p:s=t and q:t=v, the symmetric p is t=s. Applying X with major
t=s, h(x)=x, and minor q gives v=s; symmetry gives s=v. General
congruence also follows from p and h(s)=h(s), yielding h(s)=h(t).

4. Exact metatheorem.

For every ambient numerical scope, finite native context Gamma, and
native formula A, XPrf Gamma A iff NPrf Gamma A. Prove the two directions
by induction on derivations using items 1 and 2 in the transport cases
and the corresponding unchanged constructor in every other case.
All fresh-variable side conditions remain identical. The simulations
introduce no eigenvariables and no new assumptions.

Consequently every structural admissibility theorem, including weakening,
cut and capture-avoiding substitution, transfers from NPrf to XPrf.
One can also prove substitution directly: h is substituted under one
additional binder, while s,t,u are substituted at the ambient scope.
There is no literal-zero or root-shape side condition to invalidate
substitution.

5. Bi-interpretability and consistency.

The X-to-N and N-to-X maps are literally the identity on terms and
formulas and translate only proof steps. Compose them with the pinned
native/iPRA interpretations. The numerical domains, actual equality,
logical macro translations and both existing interpretation composites
are unchanged. Formula round trips and equiconsistency therefore follow
from the exact sequent equivalence, not from a standard-model argument.

6. Accurate complexity claims.

This is a strict simplification of the SHAPE of the primitive equality
rule: one moving numerical context replaces two moving contexts. It
does not reduce the total number of constructors (still eight), the
number of premises, or the arithmetic/formula grammar. It is not a
semantically weaker theory, since all derivable sequents coincide.

Do not claim a smaller complete Python checker without matched actual
measurements. The old checker fuses arbitrary-template equality
transport with Q-elimination; narrowing the primitive rule can destroy
that implementation sharing. A checker retaining old trEq as an
admissible macro remains correct for X but does not evidence a size
reduction. This is not an absolute-minimality theorem or, by itself,
a result below the current 257-token/279-node/522-byte incoming envelope.

7. Boundaries and traps.

* 'One context' means one term context, not one occurrence of a variable.
  Restricting the hole to a single occurrence would require an additional
  proof, especially for iterator bodies and repeated parameters.
* u must be an ambient term. The crossed conclusion does not authorize
  a new free placeholder occurring in u.
* Both contexts a and b in the two-step simulation are the original
  templates. Do not substitute t into b before the second step.
* Do not identify proof objects or assert inverse proof translations.
  The interpretations are inverse on formulas up to the inherited
  provable equivalences; the derivation translations are not inverses.
* No weakening of literal-equation induction is established here.
* No deletion of either computation schema follows from this argument.

Conclusion: mathematically sound and proof-friendly, with an elementary
two-step exact comparison theorem. Its practical simplicity advantage
depends on the user's criterion; a strict whole-checker improvement
remains a separate implementation obligation.
