% New result: a standard-model obstruction for a tied-seed binary recursor.
% The result concerns numerical term representation, not all interpretations.
\section{A bounded-coordinate obstruction for a tied-seed recursor}
\label{sec:binary-recursor-obstruction}

One tempting way to remove a numerical constructor field is to make the
initializer a special evaluation of the update itself.  The following
obstruction shows that this particular reduction loses an essential
term-forming ability.  It is an obstruction to a proposed reduction, not
an impossibility theorem for every smaller presentation of iPRA.

\subsection{The candidate and its standard interpretation}

Consider finite numerical terms
\[
 t::=x\mid 0\mid B(n;u,v.h),
\]
where only $h$ binds the distinct old-counter variable $u$ and previous-state
variable $v$.  Every update is fixed finite earlier syntax; its other
variables are passive numerical parameters.  Define
\[
 Sa:=B(0;u,v.a)
\]
when $u,v$ are fresh for $a$, and propose the two computation schemas
\[
 B(0;u,v.h)=S(h[0/u,0/v]),\qquad
 B(Sn;u,v.h)=h[n/u,B(n;u,v.h)/v].
 \tag{B}\label{eq:tied-seed-B}
\]
In the standard natural-number interpretation these mean, for each fixed
previously interpreted function $h$,
\[
 B_h(0,\vec p)=1+h(0,0,\vec p),\qquad
 B_h(m+1,\vec p)=h(m,B_h(m,\vec p),\vec p).
 \tag{B$_{\mathbb N}$}\label{eq:tied-seed-standard}
\]
Thus the displayed abbreviation $S$ has exactly its ordinary successor
meaning.  Structural recursion on descriptions followed by primitive
recursion on $m$ supplies the standard interpretation of every term.
There is no object-language quantification over functions.

Several useful operations survive.  Put
\[
 D(n,a):=B(n;u,v.a),\qquad W(n):=B(n;u,v.u),\qquad
 P(n):=W(D(n,n)).
\]
The computation schemas give, without induction,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 D(0,a)=Sa,\quad D(Sn,a)=a,\qquad
 W(0)=1,\quad W(Sn)=n,\\
 P(0)=0,\qquad P(Sn)=n.
\end{gathered}
\]
In particular, loss of an immediate predecessor is not the difficulty
with this proposal.

\subsection{A property of every numerical term}

For a function $f:\mathbb N^r\to\mathbb N$, say that coordinate $i$ is
a \emph{bounding coordinate} if there is a nondecreasing function
$F:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ such that
\[
             f(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\le F(x_i)
             \quad\text{for every }\vec x.
 \tag{BC}\label{eq:bounding-coordinate}
\]
Equivalently, for every fixed bound $b$, its image on $x_i\le b$ is
bounded independently of all the other coordinates.  A globally bounded
function is allowed as a separate case, including when $r=0$.
The bound need not be a term of the candidate language.  In the proof
below it can always be chosen primitive recursive.

\paragraph{Bounded-coordinate theorem.}
Every term in the $0,B$ language has a standard interpretation which is
either globally bounded or has a bounding coordinate among its free
numerical variables.  A coordinate and a nondecreasing primitive-recursive
bound can be obtained compositionally from its finite syntax.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Use structural induction, treating the bound variables of an update as
additional independent input coordinates when processing its body.
A variable $x_i$ has bound $F(z)=z$ in coordinate $i$, and $0$ is
globally bounded by $0$.

For the inductive step write $t=B(n;u,v.h)$ and let $H$ be the bound
already obtained for $h$, whose free variables are among
$u,v,\vec p$.  Bound names are first made fresh for $n$ and the passive
parameters.  There are four cases.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If $h$ is globally bounded by a constant $K$, its initial value
is at most $K+1$ and each subsequent value is at most $K$.
Consequently $B_h(m,\vec p)\le K+1$ for every $m,\vec p$.

\item Suppose a passive parameter $p_j$ is the chosen bounding coordinate
of $h$.  Both $h(0,0,\vec p)$ and every
$h(m,B_h(m,\vec p),\vec p)$ are at most $H(p_j)$, uniformly in the
counter and previous state.  Therefore
\[
                 B_h(m,\vec p)\le H(p_j)+1.
\]
This remains true when the counter term $n$ also depends on $p_j$ or
on any of the other passive parameters: the estimate is independent
of its value.

\item Suppose $u$ is the chosen bounding coordinate of $h$.  Then
$h(u,v,\vec p)\le H(u)$ uniformly in $v,\vec p$.  Define the
nondecreasing primitive-recursive function
\[
                 K(m):=1+\max_{0\le q\le m}H(q).
\]
The base value is at most $H(0)+1\le K(0)$; at every positive
counter $m+1$ the value is at most $H(m)\le K(m+1)$.
Hence $B_h(m,\vec p)\le K(m)$ for all arguments.

\item Suppose $v$ is the chosen bounding coordinate of $h$.  Then
$h(u,v,\vec p)\le H(v)$ uniformly in $u,\vec p$.  Define
\[
 K(0):=H(0)+1,\qquad
 K(m+1):=\max\{K(m),H(K(m))\}.
\]
The initial value of $B_h$ is at most $H(0)+1$ because its
previous-state argument in $h(0,0,\vec p)$ is exactly zero.
If $B_h(m,\vec p)\le K(m)$, monotonicity of $H$ gives
\[
 B_h(m+1,\vec p)
 \le H(B_h(m,\vec p))
 \le H(K(m))\le K(m+1).
\]
Ordinary metatheoretic induction on the standard natural number $m$
therefore gives $B_h(m,\vec p)\le K(m)$.  The sequence $K$ is
nondecreasing and primitive recursive.
\end{enumerate}

In the last two cases the term is bounded by $K(n(\vec p))$.
Apply the structural induction hypothesis to the counter term $n$.
If $n$ is globally bounded by $N$, then $K(N)$ is a constant bound
for the whole term.  Otherwise $n(\vec p)\le N(p_i)$ for a chosen
coordinate $p_i$ and a nondecreasing primitive-recursive function $N$;
then $K(N(p_i))$ is the required bound.  This exhausts the possible
bounding coordinates of $h$ and completes the proof.
\hfill$\square$

This induction processes nested occurrences of $B$ and arbitrary repeated
passive variables.  Capture-free substitution does not escape the result:
if an already bounded expression is composed with finitely many terms,
apply the bound to its chosen argument term and then use that term's
chosen bound.  Unused arguments cannot create a second unbounded input.
Finite macro expansion, including all occurrences of $S,D,W,P$, therefore
preserves the theorem.  The argument is about standard values and does
not assume that this metatheoretic bound is itself provable in a weak
candidate theory.

\subsection{What the obstruction rules out}

\paragraph{No ordinary two-branch selector.}
There is no $0,B$ term with standard values
\[
 C(0,a,b)=a,\qquad C(n+1,a,b)=b.
\]
Such a function is not globally bounded.  The control coordinate $n$
cannot bound it, since $n=0$ leaves $a$ arbitrary.  The coordinate $a$
cannot bound it, since $a=0,n=1$ leaves $b$ arbitrary.  The coordinate
$b$ cannot bound it, since $b=0,n=0$ leaves $a$ arbitrary.  These are
all three input coordinates, contradicting the theorem.

\paragraph{No ordinary addition or a numerically injective pairing term.}
Addition $a+b$ has no bounding coordinate: fixing either argument at zero
leaves the other unbounded.  More generally, no binary term can be an
injection $\mathbb N^2\to\mathbb N$.  If one coordinate bounds such a
term, fixing it at zero confines infinitely many distinct pairs to a
finite set of output values; a globally bounded term fails injectivity
for the same reason.

\paragraph{A one-sided selector does not repair the problem.}
If literal-equation induction is available, the candidate does define
the one-sided mask
\[
 M(n,a):=P\bigl(P(B(a;u,v.D(n,v)))\bigr),
\qquad
 M(0,a)=a,\quad M(Sn,a)=0.
\]
For $n=0$ its update is successor and its initial value is $2$;
equation induction gives $B(a;u,v.D(0,v))=S^2a$.
For $n=Sx$ its update is the identity and its initial value is $1$;
equation induction gives $B(a;u,v.D(Sx,v))=1$.
The two predecessor equations then establish the mask endpoints.
This causes no contradiction: $a$ is a bounding coordinate of $M$.
Combining two such masks into an unrestricted selector is precisely an
operation that the theorem excludes in this term language.

\subsection{Exact logical scope}

The obstruction applies to standard-model \emph{term representation}.
Adding sound logical rules or sound induction principles cannot make a
term compute addition, selection, or an injective numerical pairing in
$\mathbb N$: the semantic obstruction is independent of such proofs.
In particular this proposal cannot justify a direct compiler from the
old scalar-iterator language, because that language already has the
ordinary selector $I(n,a;u.b)$.

It does not prove that a theory using this numerical syntax cannot be
equiconsistent or bi-interpretable with iPRA.  The user explicitly permits
other domains and languages, and an interpretation may use definable
relations, quotients, several coordinates, or functions defined by
unique-existence formulas rather than numerical terms.  No exclusion of
all such interpretations is claimed.  Nor does the result address the
different successor-seeded recursor with an \emph{independent initializer}
$R(n,a;u,v.h)$: that initializer invalidates the bounded-coordinate
induction and is exactly the field whose removal was being considered.

The positive methodological conclusion is concrete: eliminating an
initializer by setting it to $1+h(0,0)$ is not a sound route to a
term-equivalent arithmetic basis, even though it retains successor,
ordinary predecessor, and a useful one-sided mask.
