\begin{abstract}
This investigation produces a substantive negative result and a complete
implementation checkpoint. The requested significant improvement of the
positive axiomatization remains unresolved.

The main new result is that ordinary tied-seed arithmetic $T_B$, with
its computation equations, coverage, separation, and literal-equation
induction, has \emph{no finite-dimensional bi-interpretation with
\ipra}. Arbitrary formula-defined domains, operation graphs, and quotient
equality are allowed. The proof combines a finite-profile invariant for
terms, finite witness extraction from existential-free axioms, and the
source-side isomorphism required by bi-interpretability. It applies more
generally to existential-free extensions in the same numerical language
whose axioms hold in the standard model. It does not rule out equiconsistency alone or
arbitrary one-way interpretations.

The finite-profile invariant also excludes numerical equality testing,
minimum, and truncated subtraction as tied-seed terms. Separately, a
weaker one-coordinate bound rules out a term-defined two-sided unit on
an infinite quotient of any finite-dimensional domain, even after
adjoining bounded-valued numerical operations. A zero-start counter
recursor is available; adjoining an unbounded zero selector restores
initialized primitive recursion by an explicit numerical compiler.

The delivered standalone checker retains the independent initializer
and the inherited whole-domain bi-interpretation with \ipra. Local
eigenparameter scopes replace its mutable global allocator. The whole
program has \textbf{668 normalized bytes, 284 tokens, and 323 AST nodes},
with a \textbf{391-byte gzip-9} encoding. The inspected corpus's separate
minima are \textbf{672 bytes, 277 tokens, and 311 AST nodes}. Only the
byte coordinate improves, by four bytes. This is an implementation
transformation, not a new arithmetic or logical basis.

The package includes full primitive rules, attributed inherited bridge
proofs, executable certificates, negative controls, source hashes, and
reproducible measurements. The arguments are written mathematical proofs,
not Lean or Rocq formalizations.
\end{abstract}

\section{The complete axiomatization retained by the checker}
\label{sec:calculus}

The target is full first-order intuitionistic primitive recursive
arithmetic: ordinary intuitionistic first-order logic with actual
equality and falsity, finite primitive-recursive descriptions and their
defining equations, successor boundary laws, and induction restricted
to quantifier-free formulas. We do not replace this by the merely
equational theorem set or by classical PRA. The distinction between
classical and constructive presentations is substantive; see, for
example, Avigad~\cite{avigad}.

The following native presentation is inherited from the generated-assumption
and Choice-DI developments~\cite{generated,constructed}. It is stated
explicitly because the small checker must account for logic as well as
arithmetic. Its reuse is not claimed as a new axiomatization.

\subsection{Numerical syntax and the two computation schemas}

There is one numerical sort, with terms
\[
             t::=x\mid0\mid St\mid I(n,a;v.h).
\]
Only the update $h$ binds $v$; other variables in it are fixed passive
parameters. The only numerical computation schemas are
\begin{align}
 I(0,a;v.h)&=a,\tag{I0}\label{eq:main-I0}\\
 I(Sn,a;v.h)&=h[I(n,a;v.h)/v].\tag{IS}\label{eq:main-IS}
\end{align}
Every description is fixed finite syntax. One can equivalently elaborate
the binder into a staged first-order signature, introducing $i_h$ only
after its body has been described. There is no quantified program variable
and no uniformly total evaluator for all primitive-recursive descriptions.

\subsection{Formula syntax and every primitive logical rule}

For the checker it is convenient to allow the auxiliary closure in formulas:
\[
             A::=(s=t)\mid \Q_y A\mid \D_{x,y}A.
\]
The paired variables are distinct. The ordinary readings are
\[
 \Q_y B\;\mapsto\;B(0)\to\forall y B(y),\qquad
 \D_{x,y}B\;\mapsto\;\exists x\bigl(B(x,0)\to\forall y B(x,y)\bigr).
\]
Contexts $\Gamma$ are finite reusable lists. The full primitive rule
inventory is the following:
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash A}\quad(A\in\Gamma)
 &&\mathsf{Hyp},\\[4pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
        {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}
 &&\mathsf{Tr},\\[4pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash\Q_y B(y)\qquad\Gamma\vdash B(0)}
        {\Gamma\vdash B(t)}
 &&\mathsf{QE},\\[4pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
         \Gamma,\Q_yB(a,y)\vdash F}
        {\Gamma\vdash F}
 &&\mathsf{DE},\\[4pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
        {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)}
 &&\mathsf{DI}_{\rm case},\\[4pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma,E(t,z)\vdash E(t,Sz)}
        {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}E(x,y)}
 &&\mathsf{DI}_{\rm progress}.
\end{align*}
Here $F$ is any fixed capture-free native formula template. In
$\mathsf{DI}_{\rm progress}$ the matrix $E$ must be a literal
equation. A $\Q$ or $\D$ formula with an equation below its binder
does not qualify. In DE, $a$ is fresh for the retained context, the
conclusion, and the fixed matrix parameters. In both introductions,
$z$ is fresh for the retained context, the witness, and the fixed
matrix parameters. Local assumptions are discharged. All substitutions
avoid capture, with bound variables renamed when necessary.

Computation is supplied by the rule
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,H\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash A}\quad\mathsf{Comp},
 \qquad H\text{ an instance of I0 or IS}.
\]
The designated contradiction is the native equation $0=S0$.
There are no additional logical or numerical axioms, no primitive
reflexivity or explosion rule, no ordinary coverage axiom, no general
induction schema, and no ambient first-order proof checker. Structural
weakening, exchange, contraction, capture-free substitution, and cut
are admissible by external induction on derivations, with freshening.
Comp is equivalent to taking I0 and IS as theorem leaves: one direction
uses cut, and the other uses Comp followed by Hyp.

An auxiliary $\Q_y B$ is interderivable with
$\D_{v,y}B$ for unused $v$. Case introduction gives one direction;
paired elimination followed by the supplied closure assumption gives
the other. Uniform matrix congruence then removes nested occurrences.
Thus the exported Eq/$\D$ language and this convenient auxiliary
language have the same interpreted logical force.

\section{Why the retained theory is bi-interpretable with iPRA}
\label{sec:bridge}

The full inherited proofs are reproduced, unchanged and attributed, in
Appendices~\ref{sec:choice-introduction}--\ref{sec:ordinary-bridge}.
The following account identifies the constructions and both composites;
equiconsistency alone would not meet the requested equivalence.

\subsection{Logic is recovered before the full arithmetic compiler}

Progress introduction with an unused existential coordinate, followed
by DE and QE, derives contextual induction for a literal equation.
Case introduction similarly derives numerical zero/successor cases for
every fixed formula template. Reflexivity follows from a computation
equation and transport. In the scalar numerical basis define
\[
 C(n,a,b)=I(n,a;v.b),\qquad Z(n)=C(n,S0,0).
\]
Then $C(0,a,b)=a$ and $C(Sn,a,b)=b$ by computation.
These terms make formula gates and two-way selectors possible.

For example, a gate on an equation is
\[
 G_z(s=t)\;:=\;(C(z,0,s)=C(z,0,t)),
\]
extended recursively below binders. Its zero instance is provable and
its successor instance is interderivable with the original formula.
By aligning and padding binders, the inherited construction gives a
formula selector $M_z(A,B)$ with zero instance $A$ and successor
instance $B$, up to proved interderivability. Consequently one can
define implication by $\D_{u,z}M_z(A,B)$ and existential
quantification by $\D_{x,y}G_yA(x)$. The same construction gives
conjunction, disjunction, and universal quantification, with all their
ordinary intuitionistic introduction and elimination rules. It does
not appeal to excluded middle for quantified formulas.

The native contradiction also yields explosion: under $0=S0$,
transport through $C$ gives every numerical equation, and structural
recursion on fixed formula syntax gives every native formula. The full
details, including universal elimination in a context containing the
specialized parameter, are in Appendix~\ref{sec:choice-introduction}.

\subsection{The arithmetic dependency order matters}

The scalar iterator update receives only its previous state, not its
recursion counter. Its equivalence with the general PR language is
therefore a theorem, not a notational convention. The inherited proof
proceeds as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Construct a scalar predecessor term using finite cycles and a
factor map; prove $P0=0$ and $P(Sn)=n$ by literal-equation induction
and numerical cases. Pairing and arbitrary quantifier-free induction
are not inputs to this construction.
\item Construct truncated subtraction and a Boolean characteristic
of actual numerical equality. The proof uses a residual equation,
not an assumed equality decision procedure.
\item Translate each fixed quantifier-free formula into its Boolean
characteristic. Equation induction on that characteristic yields the
corresponding quantifier-free induction instance.
\item Only then construct pairing and projections, and compile each
fixed general primitive-recursive description into scalar iteration.
\end{enumerate}
These identities hold internally at arbitrary elements, including in
nonstandard models. Numerical experiments would not establish this
fact. Each induction keeps passive parameters fixed; later diagonal
substitutions are made only into previously proved identities.

\subsection{The two interpretations and their composites}

Let $T_I$ be ordinary intuitionistic first-order equality logic in the
staged iterator signature, with I0, IS, literal-equation induction,
coverage $\forall x(x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy)$, and the explicit
separation sentence $(0=S0)\to\bot$. Its ordinary logic has the usual
hypothesis and equality rules, the introduction/elimination rules for
$\to,\wedge,\vee,\forall,\exists$, and explosion, with their standard
eigenvariable restrictions. These describe the comparison theory; they
are not extra uncounted primitives of the native checker.

Interpret native equality by actual equality, $\Q$ and $\D$ by
the formulas above, and the domain by $x=x$. Every native rule is
sound in $T_I$: case introduction uses coverage, and progress
introduction uses equation induction. Conversely, the reconstructed
logical interface, coverage, equation induction, and explosion recover
$T_I$ in the native theory. The falsity bridge must be explicit in
$T_I$: its positive numerical equations alone admit a singleton model.

Interpret each scalar iterator in iPRA by its fixed PR definition.
In the other direction use the scalar compiler for each fixed PR
description. Equation induction proves uniqueness of a prescribed
recurrence with a fixed initializer. That uniqueness supplies coherence
under substitution and proves that each interpreted-back function
agrees with its original on the whole domain. Both domain composites
are therefore witnessed by the identity graph $x=y$. The gate and
selector proofs give the formula composites by structural induction.
This is a whole-domain bi-interpretation with actual equality, and hence
an equiconsistency result. The new scope mechanism leaves these
interpretations and their composites unchanged.

\section{Local eigenparameter scope instead of a global allocator}
\label{sec:local}

The inherited program decreases one global integer whenever any
subproof allocates a fresh numerical name. Proving a major premise
therefore permanently advances the allocator used by the subsequent
continuation, even though the major's local hypotheses and eigenparameters
have been discharged. That global history is unnecessary.

The new program passes the current floor $k$ to each checking call.
The substitution routine is a local closure over that call's floor.
A recursive major-premise proof receives the caller's $k$ by value;
its internal allocations cannot change the caller's binding. When it
returns, the continuation may reuse the next local name.
The input cursor still consumes a global certificate tape. This is a
change to eigenparameter scope, not a claim that the whole program is
pure or mutation-free.

\begin{theorem}[Local scope preserves the represented calculus]
\label{thm:local}
With the same sorted tree decoding and numerical/logical rule
correspondence as the inherited generated-assumption kernel, the local
scope kernel is sound for that calculus. Every finite canonical
derivation of the calculus has a local-scope certificate. In particular,
the represented closed native theorem set and the bi-interpretations
in Section~\ref{sec:bridge} are unchanged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At entry to a call $c(a,g,k)$, the scope contains the rigid integer
labels $k,k+1,\ldots,-1$. The initial call has $k=0$ and empty
context. Each continuation appends exactly one context entry and
decreases its floor by one. Hence, for canonical traversals,
$k=-|g|$ at every call entry. Administrative truth assumptions are
included in this count.

Induct on a finite successful checking trace. The entry traversal
checks the goal at its current scope before allocating a new name.
The major-premise call, when present, checks its input formula at the
same scope. Its conclusion therefore has no free occurrence of a
name first allocated inside that subproof. Context extension uses a
new list, so none of its local assumptions escape either. By the
induction hypothesis it supplies precisely the available major needed
by the caller. The caller may now allocate its own fresh $k-1$;
the discharged side proof places no freshness requirement on this
independent binding event.

All local openings use the inherited traversal and depth increments.
After the local decrement, the closure sees the extended scope, so
opening the successor goal and the new hypothesis has the same rule
meaning as before. Equality transport, closure use, paired rules, and
the generated arithmetic assumption are consequently justified by
the same local rule-correspondence argument. The hypothesis branch
has its usual meaning. This proves soundness by induction on the trace.

For completeness, start with a finite named derivation and freshen
its binders. At a branch with $d$ context entries, label active
eigenparameters by the appropriate members of $-1,\ldots,-d$.
Serialize a major proof with this same environment and discard its
private extensions on return. Serialize the continuation using
$-(d+1)$ for its new name. The inherited encoding of each rule and
computation instance is otherwise unchanged. Induction on the named
derivation constructs a local certificate. This also gives an
effective alpha-translation to a globally fresh derivation: assign a
distinct global label to each binding event, retain ancestor mappings,
and discard a major subproof's private map on return.
\end{proof}

Generated arithmetic assumptions need one inherited qualification.
The common continuation can open a computation packet as $H(w)$ at
its newly allocated parameter. The required property is a provable
zero instance $H(0)$, rather than $\forall wH(w)$. If $w$ is fresh
for $\Gamma,A$, the rule
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash H(0)\qquad\Gamma,H(w)\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash A}
\]
follows by substituting zero in the second premise and cutting. The
eager recursive-image validation and structural zero-completion
argument are inherited from the generated-assumption source. The
local scope proof changes no part of that argument. When renaming
such a packet, its generated hypothesis is interpreted at the
post-allocation extended scope.

\subsection{What is and is not preserved at the byte level}

Two sibling subproofs can reuse the same negative raw label in the
new representation while denoting distinct eigenparameters. They
cannot do that under the old monotonically global allocation schedule.
Conversely, old packets can mention a numerically allocated name from
a finished side proof as a later passive parameter. The new format
has only its active lexical scope. Such a parameter can be eliminated
by substitution and the proof reserialized; it does not add a theorem.

Consequently the claim is preservation of the calculus under
scope-respecting alpha-renaming, not equality of the two programs'
accepted byte strings. The delivered producer starts with named proof
trees and emits the new convention. It is not a claimed universal
raw-certificate converter.

\subsection{The fresh-witness check is essential}

Consider
\[
                 \D_{x,y}\bigl(I(y,0;v.Sx)=y\bigr).
\]
Its zero instance is true. Its ordinary interpretation would require
all positive numbers to equal one fixed $Sx$, so the formula is false
in the standard model. If an introduction were allowed to choose
$x$ as the not-yet-fresh step parameter $z$, the purported step
\[
                    I(Sz,0;v.Sz)=Sz
\]
would be a genuine computation. This gives an explicit unsoundness
regression for deleting the lower scope check. The supplied forged
packet is rejected by both implementations and accepted by a mutant
with that guard removed, in ordinary and optimized Python.

\section{A small countermodel to an attractive logic merger}
\label{sec:congruence-obstruction}

Another investigated reduction tries to replace equality transport
and closure use by one first-order clause eliminator. Encoding an
equation $s=t$ as a clause that sends $r=s$ to $r=t$ gives root
replacement. It does not recover numerical congruence for free.

\begin{proposition}[Root replacement does not replace congruence]
Numerical computation equations, ordinary zero/successor cases,
induction, reflexivity, and root replacement are insufficient to
derive successor congruence for a primitive equation predicate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the standard domain $\mathbb N$ and standard numerical functions.
Interpret the primitive equation predicate by the equivalence relation
$\approx$ whose only nonsingleton class is $\{2,3\}$. Every
computation equation holds because both sides have the same ordinary
value. Reflexivity and root replacement are sound because
$\approx$ is an equivalence relation. Cases and induction are sound
on the standard natural numbers for every interpreted formula.
Nevertheless $2\approx3$ holds while $S2\not\approx S3$, since
$3\not\approx4$. Hence successor congruence is not derivable in
the weakened presentation.
\end{proof}

This is a countermodel to a proposed rule set; it is not the equality
interpretation used for the complete theory above. The complete
theory uses actual equality. A restricted Leibniz clause
$P(s)\to P(t)$, schematic in a formula context $P$, does merge
the two eliminations, but it moves the work into context application,
sorted formation, and higher-order substitution. No smaller complete
checker for that route is established here.

\section{Validation and reproducibility}
\label{sec:validation}

The delivered producer regenerates thirty named certificate examples,
fourteen positive and sixteen negative. Running the compact and
readable complete programs in ordinary and optimized Python, including
fourteen malformed-input cases, gives \textbf{176 successful checks
of the expected verdict}. Nine targeted controls exercise mandatory
major proofs, eager predecessor validation, the literal-equation
progress restriction, witness freshness, and propagation of the
current scope into a major proof. All nine produce the intended
distinction. These are recorded executions, not a proof of universal
soundness and not a Lean/Rocq verification.

The positives include both computation equations, state-using and
constant updates, reflexivity by transport, atomic progress, arbitrary
term specialization, compound case introduction, nonvacuous witnesses,
paired elimination, transport below a closure, and contextual induction.
The negatives include a false equation, failed majors, forged witnesses,
unallocated parameters, forbidden compound progress, malformed counters,
unsupported used data, and incorrect public input shapes. The new
fresh-witness regression checks a false standard-model statement rather
than merely causing an arbitrary parser error.

\section{Further research questions}
\label{sec:further}

\paragraph{Which additional primitive escapes the complete obstruction?}
Theorem~\ref{rel:no-biinterpretation} closes the arbitrary relational
bi-interpretation route for the specified ordinary tied-seed theory.
A successful repair must change at least one of its hypotheses, for
example by adding numerical operations that violate the finite-profile
invariant or by supplying positive existence axioms that cannot be
eliminated in the old language. The cost of those additions must be
charged to a complete checker.

Section~\ref{sec:selector-repair} supplies a direct numerical repair
with an unbounded zero selector. A formula definition of that selector
alone does not justify closing the old recursion and induction schemes
under the new symbol. Bounded numerical extensions preserve the weaker
term-interpretation obstruction, but need not preserve the finite-profile
invariant used in the full bi-interpretation theorem. These are distinct
remaining research questions. One-way relational interpretations on
complicated domains are not excluded by the return-map argument.

\paragraph{A focused calculus with fewer proved-major cases.}
Simply deleting recursive verification of elimination majors is
incomplete: equation induction currently introduces a progress closure
and then eliminates it. A sound focused redesign would need a proved
normalization theorem or an explicit local induction-clause generator,
with the replacement machinery included in its checker size.

\paragraph{Faithfulness of the countdown action.}
Section~\ref{sec:countdown-action} proves equality of all countdown
observations for a successor clock. Can fixed countdown uniqueness
alone force those observations to distinguish counters? The supplied
completion uses a level-two observation axiom. Removing it remains a
specific open proof obligation, not an available simplification.

\paragraph{Proof-assistant verification of the scalar bridge.}
The largest logical risk is the long internal predecessor and equality
reflection construction, not the four-byte change in scope storage.
A Lean or Rocq verification should state its domain, actual equality,
staged descriptions, passive-parameter conventions, and both
interpretation composites explicitly. It should avoid reintroducing
general quantifier-free induction before the equality characteristic
has been proved correct.

\paragraph{An improvement under more than one source metric.}
The next complete program should improve the relevant normalized
coordinates under the same public contract, while keeping a readable
implementation alongside it. Maximum gzip size should continue to be
reported as descriptive metadata. Removing the rejection interface
only from a new program, or hiding a theorem table outside its counted
source, would not establish the requested advance.
