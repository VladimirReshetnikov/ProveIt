\begin{abstract}
The current ConciseIPRA collection separately contains introduction at the
newest numerical parameter and discharge with a replacement inferred from
two matrix rows. We show that these restrictions can coexist. The missing
argument is a conditional-copy theorem: for every native formula $H(x)$,
the formula $\DD_{x,y}\Sel(y,H(t),H(x))$ is uniformly provable without
equality transport. Its proof uses a predecessor that computes on
successors, followed by one clause specialization. This transfers an
arbitrary introduction witness into the newest parameter position without
assuming parameter weakening of the restricted calculus.

The resulting presentation has one numerical recursor, equality and one
paired formula binder, a fixed reflexivity clause, and three inference
families: Use, Close, and Export. Close has no supplied witness. Export has
no supplied replacement term, equality proof, or formula template. The
ordinary realization is bi-interpretable with full first-order
intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic; both numerical domains are
whole and both composite isomorphisms are the identity. The contribution
is a proved compatibility theorem and a smaller interface. A second
presentation uses supplied-proof Name at discharge: its complete checker
has 1907 normalized bytes, 619 significant tokens, and 686 AST nodes,
versus 1925, 630, and 701 for the current parameter-introduction checker.
The fully automatic Export presentation has 1844 bytes but more tokens
and AST nodes. No absolute minimum is claimed. Executable evidence
accompanies the written proof; the new metatheorems are not
proof-assistant verified.
\end{abstract}
\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{ink}{pale}{\begin{minipage}{0.91\linewidth}
\textbf{The new mathematical step.} An arbitrary term can be moved into a
fresh witness position through
\[
 \boxed{\quad\Delta_t(H)=\DD_{x,y}\Sel(y,H(t),H(x)).\quad}
\]
This packet is a theorem even when $H(t)$ is not. Once $H(t)$ is supplied,
the opened packet supplies $H(b)$ at a fresh $b$. This is conditional
copying of a formula, not an assertion that $b=t$.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\file{Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA} in ProveIt, pinned to

\begin{theorem}[Main result]\label{thm:main}
The calculus $\K$ specified below, and its supplied-proof Name variant
$\KN$ in Section~\ref{sec:name}, are complete presentations of an ordinary
first-order theory $T_R$ that is bi-interpretable with $\ipra$. Both
interpretation domains are $x=x$, equality is actual equality, and both
numerical composites are internally isomorphic to the identity by the
graph $x=y$. Every formula has an internal round-trip equivalence.
Consequently the theories are equiconsistent.
\end{theorem}

Here ``full first-order'' concerns the logical language. Induction in
$\ipra$ is quantifier-free induction, not induction for arbitrary formulas.
The theory below initially needs induction only for literal equations.
The claims about simplicity concern the displayed rule interface and
explicit measured comparisons. No lower bound proving this presentation
globally simplest is known.

For a direct size improvement over \file{parameter_intro}, use $\KN$:
it removes the separately supplied source of the discharge equality,
fixing that source to the opened witness. Its backward checker is
smaller in all three reported coordinates. For fewer proof inputs, use
$\K$: both the introduction witness and the discharge replacement are
automatic. These are two interfaces to the same theory, not different
strength claims.

\section{Complete syntax and arithmetic convention}\label{sec:syntax}
There is one numerical sort. Raw terms are finite expressions
\[
 t::=x\mid 0\mid R(n,a;i,u.h),
 \qquad S(a):=R(0,a;i,u.0),\qquad \one:=S0.
\]
Only $i,u$ are bound in $h$. They are not bound in $n$ or $a$.
The computation equations are
\begin{align}
 R(0,a;i,u.h)&=S(a),\label{eq:r0}\\
 R(Sn,a;i,u.h)&=h[n/i,R(n,a;i,u.h)/u].\label{eq:rs}
\end{align}
All substitutions are capture avoiding. The substitution on the right of
\eqref{eq:rs} is simultaneous.

Public terms are full computation normal forms. To define this without a
circular rewrite $R(0,a;0)\to R(0,a;0)$, temporarily use distinct raw
constructors $0,S,R$ and orient \eqref{eq:r0}--\eqref{eq:rs}. Print the
normal constructor $Sa$ as $R(0,a;i,u.0)$. Every rule substitution is
followed by normalization $\NF$. Thus computation is part of syntax,
and the normalizer is part of the trusted implementation and its size.

This binding notation is a staged first-order description language. For
each fixed finite body $h(i,u,\vec p)$ built from earlier descriptions,
introduce the fixed first-order function described by $R(n,a;i,u.h)$.
Variables range over numbers, never over functions or codes. There is no
object-language universal evaluator.

\begin{lemma}[Normalization]\label{lem:nf}
Every finite well-sorted term has a unique normal form. Normalization
preserves scope and satisfies
\[
 \NF(t[\sigma])=\NF(\NF(t)[\NF\circ\sigma]).
\]
The same statement holds componentwise for formulas.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Embed the raw syntax into the numerical fragment of the natural-number
recursor with initial value $Sa$ and step $(i,u)\mapsto h(i,u)$.
The usual reducibility induction is particularly simple here: there is
no application of a variable as a function. Computable numerical
arguments and a computable finite update give a computable recursor.
At zero it reduces to the initial value; at a successor use the
predecessor recursion and then the computable update; at a neutral
counter use computability of its reducts and proper subterms.
Structural induction, allowing computable substitutions for the free
variables, gives strong normalization, also under update binders.
The two root rules are disjoint and left linear. Parallel reduction
commutes with capture-avoiding substitution, so nested reductions and
duplicated reducts join. This proves confluence. Uniqueness follows.
Substituting into a computation conversion gives a conversion, which
proves the displayed coherence law after normalization. No right-hand
side introduces a new free variable.
\end{proof}

The operational normalizer evaluates normal children and recursively
unfolds a successor counter. Totality is an external statement about
finite terms. It does not provide a uniform primitive-recursive bound on
normalization of all descriptions. A fuel-bounded checker is included
for a primitive-recursive certificate relation.

\subsection{Formulas and clauses}
Native formulas and two distinguished equations are
\[
 A::=(s=t)\mid\DD_{x,y}B(x,y),\qquad
 \ttop:=(0=0),\quad\bbot:=(0=\one).
\]
The ordinary reading is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d-reading}
 \DD_{x,y}B:=\exists x\bigl(B(x,0)\to\forall y\,B(x,y)\bigr).
\end{equation}
A clause is $[A;y.B(y)]$, read $A\to\forall yB(y)$, with $y$ absent
from $A$. It may have no antecedent: $[\ ;y.B(y)]$ means $\forall yB(y)$.
An ordinary assumption $F$ is the constant clause $[\ ;v.F]$.
Contexts are finite reusable lists of clauses. Every context contains
the one fixed clause
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refl}
                  \mathcal R=[\ ;y.y=y].
\end{equation}
An ordered numerical environment records the available free parameters.
Write $\ell$ for its newest parameter, or $0$ if that environment is empty.
Fresh variables introduced below are absent from the outer environment
and context. Formula binders and recursor binders never identify their
bound variables with an outer parameter.

\section{The three inference families}\label{sec:rules}
These rules, the fixed clause \eqref{eq:refl}, and the syntax convention
above are the entire presentation. All displayed formulas and instances
are normalized.

\paragraph{Use.}
For a retained clause $[A;y.B(y)]$ and an outer-scope term $t$,
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash\NF(B(t))}.
 \tag{Use}
\]
There is no premise when the antecedent is empty. In particular,
$\mathcal R$ proves every $t=t$.

\paragraph{Close.}
To conclude $\DD_{x,y}B$, the witness is automatically $\ell$.
For a fresh $z$ set
\[
 \eta=\begin{cases}
 z,&\NF(B(\ell,0))\text{ is literally }\ttop,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{rule:close}
 \frac{\Gamma,[\ ;v.\NF(B(\ell,\eta))]\vdash
                     \NF(B(\ell,Sz))}
      {\Gamma\vdash\DD_{x,y}B}.
 \tag{Close}
\end{equation}
The selected witness is the newest \emph{outer} parameter, chosen before
$z$ is adjoined. The new $z$ is available only in the premise.
The instruction supplies neither a witness nor a mode. The exact test
$\ttop$ is narrower than ``any reflexive equation'' and suffices for
completeness.

\paragraph{Export.}
Prove a major $\DD_{x,y}B$ and open it at fresh $b$. Compute
\[
 B_0=\NF(B(b,0)),\qquad B_1=\NF(B(b,\one)),\qquad
 r_B=\begin{cases}
 t,&B_0\equiv\ttop\text{ and }B_1\equiv(b=t),\\
 b,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here $\equiv$ means literal identity of normal syntax, including the
orientation of the equation in the first case. Add the clause
$\mathcal C_B(b)=[B_0;y.\NF(B(b,y))]$ and infer
\begin{equation}\label{rule:export}
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash\DD_{x,y}B\qquad
       \Gamma,\mathcal C_B(b)\vdash F}
      {\Gamma\vdash\NF(F[r_B/b])},
 \qquad b\notin\FV(\NF(F[r_B/b])).
 \tag{Export}
\end{equation}
The branch formula $F$ may mention $b$. The inferred term $r_B$ may also
mention $b$; substitution is one simultaneous pass and does not recurse
into a newly inserted copy. The normalized output must lie in the outer
scope. No equality proof, replacement term, or separate formula template
is supplied to this rule.

\begin{lemma}[Soundness]\label{lem:sound}
Every inference of $\K$ is valid in intuitionistic equality logic with
the recursor equations, numerical coverage, separation of $0$ and $1$,
and contextual induction for literal equations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use is implication and universal elimination. In Close's case mode,
numerical coverage combines the assumed zero row and the supplied
successor branch to give the required conditional universal. If the
zero row is $\ttop$, the matrix must be a literal equation, because
normalization does not erase formula binders. Its zero row is provable
and equation induction applies to the given progressive premise.
Thus no quantified induction is used. The automatically chosen $\ell$
is a legal existential witness in both cases.

For Export, open the existential in \eqref{eq:d-reading}. In the
selected case reflexivity proves $B_0$, and specialization at $1$
gives $b=r_B$. Equality substitution changes $F$ into $F[r_B/b]$.
In the default case this is simply substitution by $b$ itself.
Existential elimination is then legal because the normalized result
does not contain $b$. Normalization is internally justified by the
computation equations and fixed-update extensionality
(Section~\ref{sec:ordinary}).
\end{proof}

\section{Equality-free preparations}\label{sec:preparations}
The following terms have useful literal normal-form equations:
\begin{align*}
 P(n)&=R(n,0;i,u.i),& P(0)&\equiv1,&P(Sn)&\equiv n,\\
 C(n,a,b)&=P(R(n,a;i,u.Sb)),& C(0,a,b)&\equiv a,&C(Sn,a,b)&\equiv b,\\
 J(n,a;i,u.h)&=P(R(n,a;j,v.S(h[j/i,P(v)/u]))).
\end{align*}
The last macro, with simultaneous substitution and fresh $j,v$, satisfies
\[
 J(0,a;h)\equiv a,\qquad
 J(Sn,a;h)\equiv h[n/i,J(n,a;h)/u].
\]
Notice $P(0)=1$, not $0$. A conventional predecessor is
$p(n)=C(n,0,P(n))$. The scalar iterator is $I(n,a;u.h)=J(n,a;i,u.h)$
with unused counter coordinate $i$.

\paragraph{Ordinary elimination.}
If $F$ is independent of an opened witness $b$, Export's substitution
does nothing to it. Thus ordinary $D$ elimination is available, without
knowing which row pattern Export selects. Write
\[
                    \qq_y A(y):=\DD_{d,y}A(y)
\]
with unused $d$. Opening it and using the resulting clause gives
$A(t)$ from $\qq_yA$ and $A(0)$. The witness selected by Close is
irrelevant for this unused-witness binder.

\paragraph{Numerical cases.}
Given persistent constructions of $A(0)$ and $A(Sz)$ in their respective
scopes, Close introduces $\qq_y A(y)$, then Use gives $A(t)$.
If its zero row is $\ttop$, supply reflexivity in place of the old
zero-row assumption and ignore the progressive assumption. Otherwise
the rule is already in case mode. The same observation permits
case introduction of $\qq$ from a branch that assumes $A(0)$.

\paragraph{Gates and truth towers.}
Every native formula is a finite chain of $D$ binders ending in one
equation. Gate a formula $A$ by replacing each side $s$ of its final
equation by $C(v,0,s)$, lifting $v$ under intervening binders. Call the
result $\GG_v A$. If $A$ has $d$ binders, then
\[
 \GG_0 A\equiv T_d,\qquad \GG_{Sz}A\equiv A,\qquad
 T_0=\ttop,\quad T_{d+1}=\DD_{x,y}T_d.
\]
Every $T_d$ has a uniform proof in any environment: use reflexivity at
depth zero and Close above it. None of its witnesses occurs in its body.

\paragraph{Selectors.}
For formulas $A,B$ with the same number of $D$ binders, freshen and align
those binders and replace corresponding equation sides $s,t$ by
$C(v,s,t)$. This defines $\Sel(v,A,B)$ with literal endpoints
\[
              \Sel(0,A,B)\equiv A,\qquad
              \Sel(Sz,A,B)\equiv B.
\]
Substitution in numerical terms does not change the number of formula
binders. Consequently $H(s)$ and $H(x)$ always have the same depth;
the construction at the heart of this paper needs no padding.
For general logical encodings, padding is available:
$L(A)=\qq_v\GG_v A$ is interderivable with $A$ using its truth zero
row and specialization at $1$. Iterating $L$ equalizes depths.

All constructions in this section are explicit builders in their actual
contexts. They do not assume that an arbitrary completed $\K$ proof can
be weakened by appending a newer numerical parameter.

\section{The conditional-copy theorem}\label{sec:copy}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional copy]\label{thm:copy}
For every native one-place formula $H(x)$ and every well-scoped term $t$,
the formula
\[
             \Delta_t(H):=\DD_{x,y}\Sel(y,H(t),H(x))
\]
has a uniform $\K$ proof in any ambient environment and context. The
construction uses no equality transport and no nontrivial equality
premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Construct the unused-witness closure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:copy-closure}
                    \qq_v\GG_v\Delta_{P(v)}(H).
\end{equation}
Its zero instance is a truth tower. In its successor branch, $v=Sz$
with fresh $z$, normalization makes the required conclusion exactly
$\Delta_z(H)$. The parameter $z$ is now newest. Close of that inner
formula therefore selects $x=z$. Its zero row and successor row are
both literally $H(z)$:
\[
 \Sel(0,H(z),H(z))\equiv H(z),\qquad
 \Sel(Sw,H(z),H(z))\equiv H(z).
\]
If $H(z)\not\equiv\ttop$, the inner rule is in case mode and its
local assumption proves its conclusion by Use. If $H(z)\equiv\ttop$,
the rule selects progress and reflexivity proves its successor row.
The outer rule is in case mode, since its zero row still has at least
one $D$ binder. Its local base assumption is unused.

Open \eqref{eq:copy-closure} by ordinary elimination, supply the truth
tower zero row, and use its clause at $S(t)$. The gate disappears and
$P(S(t))\equiv t$, so the result is exactly $\Delta_t(H)$. It is
independent of the closure's unused fresh witness, which is discharged.
Every step can be regenerated in any ambient scope.
\end{proof}

This theorem asserts $\exists x(H(t)\to H(x))$ in a deliberately useful
native form. It does not prove $H(t)$ and does not identify $x$ with $t$.
Opening the theorem produces a fresh parameter at which the available
instance of $H$ can be copied. That is precisely what the newest-parameter
restriction needs.

\section{Removing arbitrary introduction witnesses}\label{sec:restriction}
Let $\Liberal$ have exactly the syntax, Use, and Export of $\K$, but let
Close supply an arbitrary outer term $t$ in place of $\ell$. Its mode
test is the same: progress exactly when $\NF(B(t,0))\equiv\ttop$.

\begin{theorem}[Witness removal]\label{thm:restriction}
For every numerical environment, clause context $\Gamma$, and native
formula $A$, the calculi $\K$ and $\Liberal$ derive the same sequent
$\Gamma\vdash A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $\K$ Close is an instance of liberal Close, so one direction is
immediate. For the other direction, translate a liberal derivation by
induction on its finite proof tree. The stronger induction statement
allows an injective renaming $\theta$ of source numerical parameters into
an arbitrary target environment and a map of source clause positions to
target clause positions. The clause at every mapped target position must
be literally the normalized $\theta$-renaming of its source clause.
Additional target parameters and clauses are allowed. This is a
membership invariant, not an assumption of arbitrary weakening of
already completed target derivations.

Use follows the clause-position map and translates its antecedent proof.
Export translates its major, maps the old fresh parameter to the actual
new target parameter, and extends the clause-position map by the opened
clause. Its two row tests are preserved and reflected by injective
renaming and coherent normalization. The only substantive case is a source
Close at $t$, with matrix $B(x,y)$ and branch
\[
           \Gamma,B(t,\eta)\vdash B(t,Sz).
\]
Put $H(x)=\qq_y B(x,y)$. The translated source branch proves $H(t)$:
the new $q$ witness is unused, and its zero-row test is exactly the
source test at $t$. In particular its assumption and successor goal
agree with the original branch after renaming.

Now prove $\Delta_t(H)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:copy} and open it at fresh
$b$. Its clause is
\[
            [H(t);v.\Sel(v,H(t),H(b))].
\]
Specialization at $1$ will give $H(b)$ whenever $H(t)$ is supplied.
Immediately introduce the desired $\DD_{x,y}B$. The newest outer parameter
is $b$, so the restricted Close chooses exactly $b$.

In its successor branch a further fresh $z$ is present. Reconstruct
$H(t)$ there by the induction hypothesis on the original source branch,
then obtain $H(b)$ from the retained packet clause. Open $H(b)$;
its clause is $[B(b,0);y.B(b,y)]$. If the enclosing Close selected cases,
its local assumption supplies $B(b,0)$. If it selected progress, that
zero row is literally $\ttop$ and reflexivity supplies it. Use at $Sz$
therefore gives $B(b,Sz)$. Discharge the unused witness of $H(b)$.
Close now gives $\DD_{x,y}B$, independent of $b$, and ordinary
elimination discharges the packet witness.

Every recursive translation call just described is on the original
source Close's strictly smaller branch. The extra packet and truth
proofs are explicit finite constructions. Thus the induction is
well founded, even though a smaller source proof may be regenerated
several times. At each recursive call the source branch's fresh parameter
is mapped to the parameter just introduced by the reifying $q$-Close,
and its local assumption is literally that $q$-Close's local clause.
The mode tests agree there because they use the same instantiated zero
row. This verifies the clause-membership invariant and completes the
well-founded translation. The delivered executable translator implements
these numerical and clause-position maps explicitly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the mode mismatch is harmless]
The test at the original term $t$ need not agree with the test at $b$.
The proof transfers the whole conditional closure $H$, not a progressive
premise. At $b$ a progressive mode has a reflexive zero row, so this
closure still yields the required successor conclusion. No equality
reasoning repairs a failed mode test.
\end{remark}

The argument also applies to supplied-proof Name in place of Export.
Its stronger consequence here is that introduction and discharge can
both be automatic. A naive composition had appeared circular: obtaining
$h(b)=u$ from $h(t)=u$ under $b=t$ would already need equality transport.
Conditional copy avoids that task altogether.

\section{Completeness of the liberal comparison calculus}\label{sec:liberal}
This section makes explicit the inherited comparison and the small
adaptations needed for $\Liberal$. The scalar native kernel, its
full-normal presentation, its deterministic Export rule, and the
ordinary arithmetic bridge are proved in the pinned repository
\cite{repo,lean,export}. Counter-aware $R$ syntax and the exact-zero
introduction selector have written treatments in the research packages
\cite{uniform,parameter,rootfree}. The following derivations explain
why these ingredients apply together, rather than treating a combination
of existing checker programs as a proof.

\subsection{Structural facts in the liberal calculus}
Clause weakening and injective parameter renaming are direct inductions
on liberal derivations. The supplied witness remains available after
renaming. Injectivity preserves and reflects both literal row tests,
and normalization commutes with renaming. Thus adding fresh parameters
is admissible here. Cutting an ordinary assumption $[\ ;v.A]$ is
then another direct induction: replace a Use of that clause by its
supplied proof and weaken that proof into local scopes using the
already established structural facts. These proofs use no equality
transport and no arbitrary numerical substitution into proof trees.
They apply to $\Liberal$ at this stage, not automatically to $\K$.

\subsection{Equality from an inferred discharge}
Given equations $s=t$ and $h(s)=u$, use the matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality-packet}
 E(x,y):\quad
 C(y,0,C(P(y),x,h(x)))=
 C(y,0,C(P(y),t,u)).
\end{equation}
Its normal rows are
\[
 E(x,0)\equiv\ttop,\qquad E(x,1)\equiv x=t,\qquad
 E(x,S(Sv))\equiv h(x)=u.
\]
In $\Liberal$, introduce $\DD_{x,y}E$ at the supplied witness $s$.
The mode is progress. Its successor goal is proved by numerical cases
on the predecessor counter, using the two supplied equations; the local
progress assumption is unused. Open the packet at $b$. Its first two
rows cause Export to select $t$. Use at $2$, with reflexive zero-row
premise, yields $h(b)=u$. Export concludes $h(t)=u$.

This is ordinary one-term transport. Reflexivity and this rule yield
symmetry, transitivity, and congruence. Equation-template transport is
two such replacements. Full formula transport follows by induction on
formula depth: for a $D$ formula open its old witness, introduce at that
witness, transport the new zero-row assumption backwards through the
smaller matrix, use the old closure at the successor, and transport the
result forwards. If the new zero row is $\ttop$, supply reflexivity
instead. The final formula is independent of the opened witness, so
ordinary elimination discharges it.

The liberal structural facts justify moving supplied premises into the
local scopes of this construction. When the result is later translated
to $\K$, Theorem~\ref{thm:restriction} regenerates its branches in the
actual target scopes. Liberal parameter weakening is not being silently
assigned to the restricted calculus.

\subsection{Cases, logic, and equation induction}
Liberal Close simulates arbitrary case introduction: if a case
introduction happens to select progress because its base is $\ttop$,
replace the old base assumption by reflexivity and ignore the new
progress assumption. Ordinary $D$ elimination and closure Use were
already derived in Section~\ref{sec:preparations}.

For equation induction on $E(y):s(y)=t(y)$, retain its given base
$E(0)$ and put
\[
 K(y):\quad C(y,0,s(y))=C(y,0,t(y)).
\]
Its zero row is literally $\ttop$. The following decoder equations are
proved by numerical cases, without induction:
\begin{align*}
 C(y,s(0),C(y,0,s(y)))&=s(y),\\
 C(y,s(0),C(y,0,t(y)))&=t(y).
\end{align*}
The first has reflexive zero and successor branches. The second uses
$E(0)$ in the zero branch and reflexivity at successors. From $K(z)$,
congruence with $v\mapsto C(z,s(0),v)$ and the two decoders give $E(z)$.
Cut this into the supplied step proof $E(z)\vdash E(Sz)$; the conclusion
is literally $K(Sz)$. Primitive progress therefore introduces $\qq_yK(y)$.
Its reflexive zero row lets Use give $K(a)$ at any target $a$, and the
decoders recover $E(a)$. This proves contextual literal-equation induction
without substituting a compound term into an arbitrary Export derivation.
If a conditional closure $\qq_yE(y)$ is wanted, introduce it by cases:
retain $E(0)$ as its local base and use the just-derived induction rule
for the successor goal.

For completeness, the ordinary logical vocabulary has explicit native
macros. Equalize binder depths by $L$ before each binary selector.
The selector's literal endpoints are these padded formulas; their
interderivability with the original operands supplies the connective
interfaces stated below:
\begin{align*}
 (A\to B)^*&=\qq_y\Sel(y,A^*,B^*),\\
 (A\land B)^*&=\qq_y\GG_y\Sel(P(y),A^*,B^*),\\
 (\exists x A)^*&=\DD_{x,y}\GG_y A^*,\\
 (A\lor B)^*&=(\exists v\,\Sel(v,A^*,B^*))^*,\\
 (\forall x A)^*&=\qq_y\GG_y A^*(P(y)),\qquad
 \bot^*=\bbot.
\end{align*}
In the disjunction line the star denotes expansion of the already
defined existential macro, not a second translation of $A^*,B^*$.
Conjunction's rows are truth, $A^*$, and then $B^*$, at $0,1$, and
all $S(Sv)$ respectively. Two numerical case splits introduce it;
Use at $1,2$ eliminates it. Implication's zero row is its antecedent
and its positive rows its consequent. The existential gate has a truth
base. Numerical coverage gives the disjunction elimination rule.
Transport along $0=1$ changes the provable gate $\GG_0F$ into
$\GG_1F\equiv F$, proving explosion for every native $F$.
These give the standard intuitionistic introduction and elimination
rules under arbitrary retained assumptions. There is no excluded-middle
rule for quantified formulas.

The ordinary reading of each macro is equivalent to the intended
connective: split numerical coverage for its selector, specialize at
the indicated small numerals for the reverse implications, and use
truth of the gate's zero row. The universal macro has a truth zero row
and successor row $A^*(z)$ because $P(Sz)\equiv z$; use it at $S(t)$
to obtain $A^*(t)$. Its introduction needs only a fresh renaming of a
proof of $A^*(z)$, not substitution into an arbitrary proof tree.
Induction on formula construction proves
these equivalences in both directions. Expanding the ordinary reading
of $D$ back through the macros gives the original $D$ by its derived
introduction and elimination rules.

\section{Whole-domain bi-interpretation with iPRA}\label{sec:ordinary}
\subsection{Compilers and internal computation}
Interpret each fixed $R$ description in $\ipra$ by primitive recursion
with initial value $Sa$ and update $h(i,u)$, after compiling its proper
subdescriptions. Conversely, compile initial functions and composition
directly and a fixed primitive-recursive definition by
\[
 \begin{split}
 f(0,\vec p)&=g(\vec p),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p),\\[-2pt]
 f^*(n,\vec p)&=J(n,g^*(\vec p);i,u.h^*(i,u,\vec p)).
 \end{split}
\]
No pairing is necessary because the update sees the old counter.
The equations for $p$ and $C$ are immediate. Applying $P$ proves
successor injectivity. If $Sx=0$, applying $C(-,0,1)$ gives $1=0$,
and separation yields falsity.

Reductions inside an update body must also be internally valid. If
$h(i,u)=k(i,u)$ holds uniformly, equation induction on
$R(n,a;h)=R(n,a;k)$ proves update extensionality: their bases are
both $Sa$; their successor values agree by the induction equation and
the pointwise identity. Each finite normalization conversion therefore
has an internal equality proof, including conversions under binders.
This justifies treating normal forms as canonical syntax without adding
a uniform evaluation axiom.

\subsection{Recovering quantifier-free induction}\label{sec:reflection}
Here are the arithmetic ingredients of the inherited equality-reflection
argument, included to make the strength claim checkable. Define addition
and monus by $J$:
\[
 x+0=x,\quad x+Sy=S(x+y),\qquad
 d(x,0)=x,\quad d(x,Sy)=p(d(x,y)).
\]
Put $\operatorname{sg}(u)=C(u,0,1)$ and $K(u)=C(u,1,p(u))$.
Coverage and constructor separation give
\[
 \operatorname{sg}(u)\in\{0,1\},\quad
 \operatorname{sg}(u)=0\leftrightarrow u=0,\quad
 K(u)=0\leftrightarrow u=1.
\]
Here the finite-set notation abbreviates an ordinary disjunction.
Four literal-equation inductions establish
\[
 0+y=y,\quad Sx+y=S(x+y),\quad
 d(Sx,Sy)=d(x,y),\quad d(Sa,a)=1.
\]
For example, applying $p$ to the induction hypothesis proves the monus
cancellation step; the diagonal identity then follows by cancellation.

The useful residual invariant is the single equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 C(\operatorname{sg}(d(x,y)),x,y+d(x,y))=x.
\end{equation}
Induct on $y$, holding $x$ fixed. At zero, $0+x=x$ makes both
selector alternatives equal. For the step set $r=d(x,y)$ and
$r'=p(r)=d(x,Sy)$. If $r'=0$, the target selector returns $x$.
Otherwise coverage gives $r=Sr'$ (the zero alternative contradicts
$r'\ne0$). The induction equation gives $y+r=x$, and
$Sy+r'=y+Sr'=x$ proves the successor invariant. All case splits are
coverage; the induction formula remains \eqref{eq:residual}.

It follows that $d(Sa,b)=1\leftrightarrow a=b$: the forward direction
uses \eqref{eq:residual} to get $b+1=Sa$, then injectivity; the reverse
direction is the diagonal identity. Therefore
\[
 e(a,b)=\operatorname{sg}(K(d(Sa,b)))
\]
has values $0,1$ and satisfies $e(a,b)=0\leftrightarrow a=b$
throughout the entire numerical domain. This is actual equality
reflection, not agreement only at standard numerals.

For a quantifier-free formula $A$, define its Boolean characteristic
externally by finite recursion on its syntax:
\begin{align*}
 b_{s=t}&=e(s,t),&b_\bot&=1,\\
 b_{A\land B}&=C(b_A,b_B,1),&
 b_{A\lor B}&=C(b_A,0,b_B),&
 b_{A\to B}&=C(b_A,b_B,0).
\end{align*}
Induction on this finite syntax proves internally that $b_A$ is Boolean
and $A\leftrightarrow b_A=0$. For implication, split the already proved
Boolean range of $b_A$: if it is zero, $A$ holds and the implication
is equivalent to $B$; if it is one, $\neg A$ holds and the implication
is true. This uses no excluded middle for arbitrary formulas.
Literal-equation induction on $b_A(n)=0$ now gives every contextual
quantifier-free induction instance. Contexts may contain quantified
formulas.

\subsection{Both round trips, internally}
For two fixed recursive descriptions with equal initial values and
provably identical updates, equation induction on equality of their
values proves uniqueness at every internal counter. Apply this lemma
while inducting externally on a finite description: each double
translation has the same base and step as its original. This proves
both numerical round-trip identities in the respective theories, with
all passive parameters arbitrary.

Both domain formulas are $x=x$ and both interpretations preserve actual
equality. Hence the composite isomorphism in either direction is the
identity graph $x=y$. The connective equivalences in
Section~\ref{sec:liberal}, followed by induction on each full formula,
give both formula round trips. This proves bi-interpretability, not
merely mutual conservation of closed equations or agreement in the
standard model. Falsity translates to $0=1$, whose ordinary realization
implies $\bot$ by the explicit separation axiom. Finite contradiction
proofs translate in both directions, establishing equiconsistency and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{A smaller supplied-proof Name presentation}\label{sec:name}
Let $\KN$ keep precisely the syntax, fixed reflexivity clause, Use rule,
and newest-only Close rule of $\K$. Replace Export by
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash\DD_{x,y}B\qquad
       \Gamma,\mathcal C_B(b)\vdash b=t\qquad
       \Gamma,\mathcal C_B(b)\vdash F}
      {\Gamma\vdash\NF(F[t/b])},
 \quad b\notin\FV(\NF(F[t/b])).
 \tag{Name}
\]
The replacement $t$ is any well-scoped local numerical term; it may
contain $b$. Substitution inserts it once. The source of the equality
is fixed to $b$, whereas general substitutive discharge supplies both
sides of an equality and a formula template.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:name}
The open consequences of $\KN$, $\K$, and $T_R$ coincide under their
displayed readings. In particular, $\KN$ is bi-interpretable with
$\ipra$ with the same whole domains and identity graphs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Name is sound in $T_R$: equality gives the substituted branch formula,
and the outer-scope condition permits existential elimination. Every
Export instance has a Name replacement in the same scope. In the default
case use reflexivity $b=b$. In the selected case the opened clause has
base $0=0$ and first row $b=t$; reflexivity and Use at $1$ give the
required equality. This translation adds no numerical scope, so it does
not change any Close witness or mode. Induction on the proof gives
$\K\subseteq\KN$. Completeness of $\K$ and soundness of $\KN$ give
the reverse consequence inclusion and the stated interpretation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why the compact backward certificate is exact}
The delivered backward checker supplies a template $T(v)$ and term $t$,
checks the two branch conclusions $b=t$ and $F=\NF(T[b/v])$, and
requires the outer goal to be $A=\NF(T[t/v])$. It does not separately
forbid explicit occurrences of $b$ in $T$. The following lemma explains
why that omission preserves exactly Name, even when $t$ contains $b$.

\begin{lemma}[Template representation]\label{lem:name-template}
For a well-scoped template $T(v)$ and local term $t$, put
$F=\NF(T[b/v])$ and $A=\NF(T[t/v])$. If $A$ is $b$-free, then
\[
                         \NF(F[t/b])=A
\]
as literal normal syntax.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An arbitrary capture-free renaming of numerical parameters preserves a
full normal form. Injectivity is unnecessary: recursor reduction tests
only zero and successor constructors, and identification of parameters
creates neither. Equality formulas have no reduction turning equal
sides into $0=0$.

Choose a fresh $c$ absent from $T,t$ and set
$U=\NF(T[v:=t,b:=c])$, with simultaneous substitution. Identifying
$c$ with $b$ therefore gives the already normal formula
\[
 U[c\mapsto b]=\NF(U[c\mapsto b])=\NF(T[t/v])=A.
\]
Since $A$ is $b$-free, $U$ contains neither $b$ nor $c$; hence $U=A$.
Substitute $t$ for $c$ and use normalization coherence to get
$\NF(T[v:=t,b:=t])=A$. Finally
\[
 \NF(F[t/b])
 =\NF((T[b/v])[t/b])
 =\NF(T[v:=t,b:=t])=A.
\]
No substitution is performed inside a newly inserted copy of $t$.
\end{proof}

The checker validates templates through their normalized endpoint
formulas, as in the existing \file{parameter_intro} protocol
\cite{parameter}. Here is the needed completion argument. The traversal
checks every proper child at its required sort before an erasing
reduction. A successful formula endpoint also forces the original root
to be an equation or $D$: a numerical root stays numerical, and a list
of any other length cannot reduce to a formula. Thus only excess
numerical parameters and reducible numerical terms can remain in a raw
accepted template.

Interpret excess negative levels and positive indices above the
placeholder's binding depth as extra free parameters. Replace all of
them by $0$, fixing the placeholder and permitted local parameters.
The checked arguments $b,t$ and both checked endpoint formulas contain
none of these extras. Normalization coherence therefore preserves both
endpoints literally. Normalizing the completed template gives a
well-scoped template to which Lemma~\ref{lem:name-template} applies.
The equality premise validates $t$, and each endpoint is independently
validated in its proper scope; these checks are essential. Conversely, abstracting
all free occurrences of $b$ in $F$ into $v$ represents every Name
instance. Thus the backward representation is exact, not merely sound
on the delivered examples.

This profile retains the replacement, equality proof, and template at
discharge. Its advantage is a smaller complete implementation relative
to the current backward parameter-introduction checker. Conditional
copy is what permits the restricted introduction and restricted equality
source to coexist without losing arithmetic or first-order logic.

\section{Further research questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Formalize conditional copy independently of arithmetic.}
The proof only needs an exact successor predecessor, numerical gates,
clause specialization, newest-parameter Close, and ordinary elimination.
A small generic Lean interface for these operations could make the
witness-removal theorem reusable across several native presentations.
\item \textbf{Reduce the remaining specialization interface.}
Use still supplies an arbitrary term. The present proof specifically
specializes its outer closure at $S(t)$. Replacing that operation by a
fixed specialization requires a new way to deliver $t$; deleting it is
not justified by the theorem proved here.
\item \textbf{Quantify proof expansion.}
The translation regenerates a smaller branch inside extra scopes and
can duplicate it. The finite certificates demonstrate feasibility,
but no polynomial bound on general certificate size is asserted.
Sharing source proof builders through a checked DAG may improve size
without enlarging the trusted mathematics.
\item \textbf{Normalize less.}
The literal equations $P(St)\equiv t$ and the selector endpoints do the
work. A canonical syntax that guarantees these particular reductions
while avoiding full normalization could reduce the trusted checker.
It would need a new coherence proof and a fresh size comparison.
\item \textbf{Separate interface minima from program minima.}
Three rule families with fewer supplied fields do not imply a
coordinatewise minimum in bytes, tokens, or AST nodes. A useful next
benchmark would fix the syntax-validation contract and measure several
straightforward implementations of all competing calculi.
\end{enumerate}

        raise ValueError('invalid certificate')

    if type(a) is int:
        need(not form)
