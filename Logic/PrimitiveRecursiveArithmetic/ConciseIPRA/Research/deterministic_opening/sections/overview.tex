\section{Results and the meaning of simpler}
\label{sec:results}

This report gives a further mathematical restriction of the inference
interface for full first-order intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic.
The principal rule is \emph{deterministic witness opening}.  An opened
formula supplies, when it has the prescribed form, its own equality for
specializing the witness.  A proof certificate no longer supplies a replacement
term, a proof of the witness equality, or an intermediate branch formula.
Only the major proof and a proof of the unchanged outer goal are supplied.

The construction has three inference families, Use, Introduce, and Open,
and one explicit universal-reflexivity axiom clause.  Its terms are full
computation normal forms.  Both normalization and the deterministic witness
calculation belong to the trusted specification and to the measured checker.
The theory proves exactly the same canonical clause sequents as the preceding
presentation.  Its ordinary realization is therefore bi-interpretable and
equiconsistent with full first-order iPRA, with whole numerical domains,
actual equality, and numerical composite maps having graph $x=y$.

\paragraph{Two distinct improvements.}
The mathematical rule restriction and the smallest-program result are
different results.  The former has a complete, readable checker of
\textbf{2,253 normalized bytes}, compared with 2,273 bytes for its immediate
predecessor.  The latter refactors the existing compact calculus and reduces
its complete checker from \textbf{516 to 508 normalized bytes}.  The 508-byte
program implements the inherited compact calculus; it is not a checker for
the new three-rule presentation.

The first pair isolates the mathematical interface change in the supplied
goal-directed implementation.  It saves 20 bytes (about $0.88\%$), two significant tokens, and four
AST nodes. The mathematical comparison therefore improves all three
coordinates in this matched implementation.  The compact byte
profile saves 8 bytes (about $1.55\%$), while using two more tokens.
The observed corpus minimum of 256 significant tokens is not improved.
No single program dominates every old coordinate.  These are attained
upper bounds, not mathematical lower bounds on all possible presentations.
An absolute simplest axiomatization is not established here.

\subsection{The inspected comparison}

The comparison is pinned to ProveIt commit
\begin{center}
\texttt{17898ea7e6a1300dfdcad71040d5daeed67916d4}.
\end{center}
The inspected project is
\path{Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA}.
Its README lists 67 filed research packages and a Lean development.\cite{baseline}
The comparison also includes all six ZIP archives in its
\path{docs/incoming} directory at that commit:
\texttt{clause\_contexts}, \texttt{clause\_guards},
\texttt{mirror\_core}, \texttt{seed\_evaluation},
\texttt{uniform\_binding}, and \texttt{witness\_discharge}.
The last is the immediate mathematical predecessor used here.\cite{wd} Source and
archive identities are recorded in the accompanying provenance file.

Several ingredients are inherited and are not claimed as new:
the scalar iterator, the paired binder, the clause representation, the
fixed reflexivity clause, full normalization, the two-row equality package,
and the whole-domain arithmetic interpretation.  The new restriction is to
determine the witness from a fixed probe and change the opened clause
accordingly.  The proof that this restricted rule still suffices needs a
substitution theorem; simply reusing a branch under a different clause
would be invalid.

\subsection{Verification status}

The mathematical equivalence in this report is a written proof.  The archive
also contains standalone checkers, executable proof examples, negative
controls, source measurements, and independent audit notes.  These tests
check the implementation; they do not establish the universal mathematical
theorems by finite sampling.  The new rule and Python programs have not been
verified in Lean or Rocq.  The inherited Lean bridge is identified by its
actual theorem names and pinned source, rather than being presented as a
newly rebuilt formalization.
