Independent review of the predecessor-state evaluation calculus
==============================================================

Review scope: the proposed raw six-rule calculus and code/checker.py.
This is an independent assistant review, not external peer review or a
proof-assistant verification.

Conclusion
----------
The evaluation rule is sound for the ordinary scalar arithmetic reading.
Reflexivity and this rule recover both raw iterator equations and all
numerical-template transport. Replacing I0, IS, Tr by Refl, Eval therefore
preserves every native sequent. Numerical terms, formulas, the four logical
rules, and the numerical interpretation are unchanged. The new equality
subsystem is stable under numerical substitution without normalization or
a numeral-zero shortcut.

This establishes a six-rule presentation on the old raw syntax. It does not
establish absolute minimality or a global compact-checker record.

Rule audited
------------
Given s=t and b=r, conclude r=rho(s,t,b), where:
  b=s                              : rho=t;
  b=I(0,a;u.h), s=a                 : rho=t;
  b=I(Sn,a;u.h), s=I(n,a;u.h)       : rho=h[t/u].
All other shapes are rejected. The source s is unrestricted. The body h
binds its state variable u; substitution is capture avoiding. The rule has
two proof arguments and no separately supplied term context.

Soundness
---------
The first case is common-left equality cancellation. In the second case,
I0 proves b=a and the major premise says a=t. In the third case, IS proves
b=h(I(n,a;h)); the major premise identifies the predecessor state with t,
so ordinary congruence gives b=h(t). In every case b=r turns b=rho into
the crossed conclusion r=rho. These arguments use only rules already
derivable in the old native calculus.

Recovery of the old equality subsystem
--------------------------------------
No logical rule and no induction occurs in this derivation.

1. Eval with b=s gives common-left cancellation: from s=t and s=r infer r=t.
2. Symmetry: use major s=s and minor s=t to obtain t=s.
3. Transitivity: from s=t obtain t=s; use major t=u and minor t=s to
   obtain s=u.
4. I0: use major a=a and minor I(0,a;h)=I(0,a;h).
5. IS: use major I(n,a;h)=I(n,a;h) and minor
   I(Sn,a;h)=I(Sn,a;h). This gives the exact raw IS equation for every n,
   including literal zero. Each computation needs two Refl leaves and
   one Eval node.
6. Arbitrary congruence: given s=t and a fixed numerical context f(u), put
       c=I(0,s;u.f(u)),    b=I(1,s;u.f(u)).
   I0 gives c=s. Eval of c=s against b=b gives b=f(s). The zero Eval
   case applied directly to s=t and c=c gives c=t. Eval of c=t against
   b=f(s) gives f(s)=f(t).
7. Crossed transport: given additionally f(s)=r, common-left cancellation
   of f(s)=f(t) against f(s)=r gives r=f(t).

Thus all three old arithmetic/equality rules I0, IS, Tr are admissible.
Conversely, Refl and each Eval case are admissible in the old system.
The four logical rules are copied. Structural induction on finite proof
trees proves identity-language equivalence in both directions, for every
well-scoped context and conclusion. The existing full iPRA bi-interpretation
therefore transfers with the same numerical domains, equality, and both
identity numerical composite graphs.

In proof-tree nodes, the displayed congruence adds exactly eight nodes
to the supplied proof of s=t. Crossed transport adds one further Eval
and includes its supplied minor proof once. Thus each old Tr node is
replaced by nine nodes and each computation node by three. The complete
old-to-new translation has at most 9N nodes for an N-node old proof.
This is a node count, not a serialized-byte, formula-size, or runtime bound.

Substitution
------------
The evaluation cases use raw term constructors. Substitution preserves
both I(0,a;h) and I(Sn,a;h), and sends their source terms to a' and
I(n',a';h'), respectively. The usual substitution-composition law sends
h[t/u] to h'[t'/u]. The source cannot collapse syntactically to the whole
iterator: in the zero case it is a proper subterm; in the successor case
the two counters are n' and S(n'), which cannot be the same finite term.
Therefore an Eval instance translates to an Eval instance in the same
branch, even when substitution turns a neutral n into literal zero.

This claim is specifically about Refl and Eval. The inherited witness-
sensitive DI still needs the already known admissibility argument: a
substitution can turn its witness into literal zero. Full substitution
admissibility follows from identity-language equivalence with the old
native calculus; no stronger rule-by-rule claim about DI is made.

Implementation invariant
------------------------
For validated JSON input, infer(p,g,n), when it returns, always returns a
well-scoped native formula with n ambient variables.

Hyp and Refl preserve this immediately. QE instantiates a scoped Q matrix
and checks its zero instance. DE validates its result before opening the
fresh witness and requires the local result to equal its one-variable
shift. Its opened Q matrix has n+1 ambient variables because the source D
matrix originally has n+2 variables. DI validates its two-variable matrix
and its witness, instantiates x underneath the y binder, and compares its
branch with the successor instance that preserves the fresh y slot.

In Eval, both premises are equations by the invariant. The common-left
case returns an already scoped term. The zero case checks the initializer.
The successor case checks the exact predecessor iterator and substitutes
a scoped term into an already scoped iterator body. Computing that
substitution before checking the predecessor equality does not permit a
bad certificate: the explicit require still rejects it, and the operation
has no side effects on retained syntax.

The code deliberately omits redundant equation and iterator tag tests.
Exactly an equation formula has length three; Q and D have length two.
Consequently successful triple destructuring of a proved formula can only
be an equation. Exactly an iterator term has length four; z has length
one, while v and S have length two. Successful four-way destructuring of
a scoped term can only be an iterator. This relies on validated JSON
syntax and the invariant, not on arbitrary unvalidated Python containers.

The validator explicitly rejects Boolean values where integer variable
counts, variable indices, or hypothesis indices are required. Every gate
uses require and explicit exceptions; rejection does not depend on Python
assert statements and is not disabled by the -O interpreter option.

Independent regression suite
----------------------------
code/adversarial.py exposes a list named cases and a run() function. Its
51 JSON-round-tripped cases all passed against code/checker.py during
this audit. The suite covers:
  * each evaluation case, including exact raw IS at zero;
  * retained predecessor states at a variable counter;
  * rejection of a neutral counter, wrong predecessor counter or body,
    incorrect initializer, and the superseded initializer-only shortcut;
  * nested iterator scopes and capture avoidance;
  * QE antecedent matching;
  * valid DE freshness and rejection of witness escape;
  * DI progress, cases, and eigenvariable/witness checks;
  * malformed formula and proof tags and arities;
  * Boolean/int confusion, out-of-scope variables, malformed envelopes,
    and missing or extra certificate fields.

These are implementation regressions. Finite successful tests do not
replace the universal mathematical proof or certify the Python interpreter.
