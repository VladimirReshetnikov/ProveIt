\section{The result, the comparison, and the unmet part}
\label{sec:result}

The requested target was a simpler axiomatization than the current
\texttt{ConciseIPRA} proposal. The investigation produced a smaller
\emph{implementation of existing complete presentations}, but no new
axiomatically simpler arithmetic basis. This distinction is material:
the large reduction below is in public-goal validation, while the only
new compact byte record is a one-byte Boolean-expression change.
It would be misleading to describe either as a mathematical breakthrough.

The comparison is the supplied repository at the commit on the title
page~\cite{snapshot}. The current incoming packages already improve the
figures summarized for the older filed packages. All three incoming ZIP
files were inspected and checked against their Git blob hashes; their
provenance is included in the archive. In particular, the current compact
coordinate envelope is
\[
 (\text{tokens},\text{AST nodes},\text{normalized bytes})=(256,278,521),
\]
attained by \emph{three different programs}, not one program of that size.
The incoming \texttt{one\_context} package already contains crossed
one-context equality transport, and \texttt{kernel\_refinement} already
contains the left-occurrence refinement~\cite{incoming}.
Neither is proposed again here.

\section{The complete inherited native axiomatization}
\label{sec:native}

This section states the mathematics being preserved, rather than counting
an unchanged arithmetic theory as a new construction. It is convenient
to use the inherited Choice-DI presentation~\cite{choice}, which has
the same sequents as the Lean native kernel described in the repository.

\subsection{Numerical terms and formulas}

There is one numerical sort. Terms are finite expressions
\[
 t::=x\mid0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]
Only the state variable $u$ is bound in $h$. The counter $n$ and seed $a$
are outside its scope. The update has no recursion-counter argument;
other variables in it are fixed parameters. There is no numerical
quantification over function descriptions and no uniform evaluator.

Formulas and the designated contradiction are
\[
 A::=(s=t)\mid\Q_y A\mid\D_{x,y}A,
 \qquad \bot_*:=(0=S0).
\]
Bound variables are renamed before substitution. The ordinary readings
are
\begin{align*}
 (\Q_y B)^\circ&=B^\circ(0)\longrightarrow\forall y\,B^\circ(y),\\
 (\D_{x,y}B)^\circ&=\exists x\bigl(B^\circ(x,0)
             \longrightarrow\forall y\,B^\circ(x,y)\bigr).
\end{align*}
The $\Q$ constructor can be omitted from public syntax: an unused
existential coordinate under $\D$ represents it. This omission is
already part of the inherited construction, not a new reduction.

\subsection{Every axiom and inference rule}

The two nonlogical axiom schemas, available in every retained context,
are
\begin{align}
 I(0,a;u.h)&=a,\tag{I0}\label{main:I0}\\
 I(Sn,a;u.h)&=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u].\tag{IS}\label{main:IS}
\end{align}
Contexts are finite reusable lists of formulas. The remaining primitive
patterns of the Choice-DI presentation are the following:
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{A\in\Gamma}{\Gamma\vdash A}\quad\mathrm{Hyp},\qquad
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\quad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
      {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}\quad\mathrm{Tr},\\[1ex]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash\Q_yB(y)\quad\Gamma\vdash B(0)}
      {\Gamma\vdash B(t)}\quad\mathrm{QE},\\[1ex]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B\quad
       \Gamma,\Q_yB(b,y)\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash A}\quad\mathrm{DE},\\[1ex]
 &\frac{\Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)}\quad\mathrm{DI}_{\rm case},\\[1ex]
 &\frac{\Gamma,E(t,z)\vdash E(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}E(x,y)}\quad\mathrm{DI}_{\rm prog}.
\end{align*}
Here $F$ is any fixed native formula template, and $E$ in the last rule
must be a \emph{literal equation}. In DE, $b$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, the
conclusion and the fixed parameters of the matrix. In either DI rule,
$z$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, the witness $t$, and the fixed matrix
parameters. Displayed local assumptions are discharged.

These are eight patterns when Hyp and the two computation schemas are
counted separately. Equality reflexivity, successor injectivity,
ordinary logical rules, and quantifier-free induction are not further
primitive axioms. Weakening, renaming, contraction, and cut are
admissible. In the Lean native kernel, Tr may instead be restricted to
equation templates, and progressive introduction directly yields
$\Q_yE(y)$; the repository proves equivalence of those presentations.

The measured native checker uses the inherited source restriction
$s\not\equiv0$ in Tr and the two introduction choices. Recovery of
omitted transport instances is established in the included
\file{reference/foundations/zero_source_admissibility.tex} and the
repository's \texttt{NativeZeroSource} formalization. The source
restriction is syntactic, not the assertion $s\ne0$ inside arithmetic.

\subsection{Derived logic and induction}

Put $1=S0$ and
\[
 C(v,a,b):=I(v,a;u.b),\qquad
 C(0,a,b)=a,\quad C(Sv,a,b)=b,
\]
with unused $u$. Reflexivity follows from the computation
$r=t$, where $r=I(0,t;u.0)$, by transporting $r=t$ along itself with
template $v=t$. Symmetry and transitivity then follow by the usual
equation templates.

Case DI with an unused witness, followed by DE and QE, gives numerical
cases for every formula:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash A(0)\quad\Gamma\vdash A(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash A(t)}.
\]
Progress DI with an unused witness similarly gives contextual equation
induction:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash E(0)\quad\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash E(t)}.
\]
The distinction between these two derived rules is essential.

There is a formula gate $G_vA$ that is true at $v=0$ and equivalent to
$A$ at a successor. On equations it is
\[
 G_v(s=t):=C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t),
\]
and it commutes with the logical binders. A selector $M_v(A,B)$ is
equivalent to $A$ at zero and to $B$ at a successor. It is built by
aligning binder depths using the equivalence
$A\dashv\vdash\D_{r,z}G_zA$, with unused $r$.

For $p(z)=I(z,0;u.C(u,1,0))$, one may use the macros
\begin{align*}
 A\Rightarrow_*B&:=\D_{r,z}M_z(A,B),\\
 A\wedge_*B&:=\D_{r,z}G_zM_{p(z)}(A,B),\\
 \exists_*x\,A(x)&:=\D_{x,y}G_yA(x),\\
 A\vee_*B&:=\exists_*x\,M_x(A,B),\\
 \forall_*x\,A(x)&:=A(0)\wedge_*\D_{r,z}G_zA(z).
\end{align*}
The newly bound coordinates are fresh. The first two fixed parity
computations $p(1)=1$, $p(2)=0$ give conjunction projections. Cases and
the selector give disjunction elimination and numerical coverage.
The inherited appendix gives all introduction and elimination arguments,
including both formula composites. Under $0=1$, selector computation and
transport yield every equation, and case DI extends explosion to every
native formula. No excluded middle for arbitrary quantified formulas is
introduced.

\section{Why the theory is bi-interpretable with full iPRA}
\label{sec:bridge-main}

\subsection{An ordinary first-order realization}

Elaborate each fixed iterator description into a function symbol. Given
an earlier-stage update $h(u,\vec p)$, introduce $i_h(n,a,\vec p)$ with
the equations \eqref{main:I0}--\eqref{main:IS}. Let $T_I$ be ordinary
intuitionistic first-order equality logic over these symbols with those
equations, contextual induction for literal equations, and
\[
 \forall x\,(x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy),\qquad
 (0=S0)\longrightarrow\bot.
\]
The last axiom is an explicit falsity bridge. A singleton structure would
otherwise satisfy positive computation equations, so it must not silently
be omitted from the ordinary realization.

The readings of $\Q$ and $\D$ in Section~\ref{sec:native} give native
soundness in $T_I$. Conversely, the derived macros recover its logical
rules, coverage, equation induction, and falsity. The paired-formula
composite is the internal equivalence
\[
 \D_{x,y}B\dashv\vdash
 \exists_*x\bigl(B(x,0)\Rightarrow_*\forall_*yB(x,y)\bigr).
\]
This is more than agreement on standard numerical examples.

\subsection{The arithmetic interpretations}

In one direction, interpret each fixed scalar iterator in iPRA by
ordinary primitive recursion that ignores the recursion counter in its
update. All the axioms of $T_I$ follow.

For the reverse direction, the inherited internal argument has the
following order, which must be respected to avoid a circular proof:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Construct a scalar predecessor $P$ using equation induction and
numerical cases, and prove $P0=0$, $P(Sn)=n$ throughout the original
domain. No pairing or faithful equality test is assumed here.
\item Construct truncated subtraction and an equality characteristic;
prove reflection of actual equality, not only correctness on standard
numerals.
\item Translate every fixed quantifier-free formula into a characteristic
equation. Obtain quantifier-free induction, including contextual
instances.
\item With this induction available, construct pairing $\pi$ and
projections $L,H$, then compile every primitive-recursive description.
\end{enumerate}

For example, for the primitive recursion
\[
 f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p),
\]
the scalar compiler uses
\begin{align*}
 V(n,\vec p)&=I\bigl(n,\pi(0,g^*(\vec p));\,
 u.\pi(SL(u),h^*(L(u),H(u),\vec p))\bigr),\\
 f^*(n,\vec p)&=H(V(n,\vec p)).
\end{align*}
Equation induction proves $L(V(n,\vec p))=n$; the projection identities
then prove both defining equations of $f$. This construction is external
recursion on finite descriptions, not an internal evaluator of arbitrary
program codes.

\subsection{Both composites and consistency}

An iterator is uniquely determined, internally, by its seed and update:
equation induction on the equality of two candidate iterates proves this
with all passive parameters fixed. The analogous argument holds for
ordinary primitive recursion. External induction on finite descriptions
therefore proves that every operation equals its double translation.

Both domains are the whole numerical domain, equality is actual equality,
and the numerical isomorphism in either composite is the identity graph
$x=y$. Structural induction on formulas, using the paired composite and
the logical macros, proves both formula round trips. Contradiction is
preserved by the explicit falsity bridge. Effective proof translations
give equiconsistency. Neither theory is claimed to prove its own
consistency.

These are inherited results, with their full written arithmetic
development reproduced in Appendices~\ref{sec:choice-introduction}--\ref{sec:ordinary-bridge}.
The repository also provides the Lean statements
\texttt{biInterp}, \texttt{native\_roundTrip},
\texttt{pra\_native\_roundTrip}, and
\texttt{native\_inconsistent\_iff}~\cite{snapshot}.
No claim is made that the Python programs delivered here were verified
by Lean, or that the upstream Lean project was rebuilt in this run.

\section{The guarded public language of the smallest checker}
\label{sec:guarded-main}

The 520-byte and 921-byte programs have the same different interface:
their public formulas are guarded Eq/D formulas, while auxiliary
formulas used during a proof may be more general. This section gives
the exact distinction needed for their bi-interpretation claims.

\subsection{Public and auxiliary syntax}

An equation is public. A paired formula $\D_{x,y}B$ is public if $B$ is
a compound public formula, or if its atomic matrix has the literal form
\begin{equation}
 B(x,y)\equiv I(y,0;u.h)=I(y,0;u.k).
 \label{main:guard}
\end{equation}
The update terms may contain all the indicated variables and fixed
parameters. Let this public language be $\G$.

Auxiliary proof formulas have the unrestricted syntax
$(s=t)$, $\Q_yA$, and $\D_{x,y}A$. The primitive rules are Hyp, I0,
IS, QE, DE, source-restricted Tr in auxiliary templates, and the single
introduction
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta_B(z))\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B},\qquad
 \eta_B(z)=\begin{cases}
 z,&B\text{ is a literal equation},\\
 0,&B\text{ is compound}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Freshness and discharge are as in Section~\ref{sec:native}.
There is no atomic case choice. Every atomic public matrix has a
provable zero instance by \eqref{main:guard}.

\subsection{Sound interpretation of auxiliary formulas}

Equality and $\Q$ retain their ordinary readings. For soundness of the
entire auxiliary calculus use
\[
 (\D_{x,y}B)^\dagger=
 \begin{cases}
 \exists x\,\forall y\,(B(x,y)\to B(x,Sy)),&B\text{ is an equation},\\
 \exists x\,(B^\dagger(x,0)\to\forall yB^\dagger(x,y)),&B\text{ is compound}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Atomic introduction is sound immediately. Its elimination is sound
because equation induction converts progress into the exported
conditional closure. Compound introduction uses coverage, as before.
All other rules have their ordinary sound interpretations.

On a guarded atomic matrix the zero instance is true. Consequently its
progressive and conditional readings are equivalent: progress plus the
true base gives all instances, and all instances imply progress.
Structural induction gives agreement of the two readings throughout
$\G$.

The qualification cannot be removed. For a matrix independent of $x$,
take $E(y)\equiv(y=1)$. In iPRA,
\[
 E(0)\to\forall yE(y)
\]
is true because $0\ne1$, whereas
\[
 \forall y(E(y)\to E(Sy))
\]
is false: the instance $y=1$ would give $2=1$. Thus the two meanings
already disagree on this very small unguarded matrix. Soundness of
auxiliary calculations does not supply a formula round trip for every
auxiliary expression.

\subsection{Public completeness and the composites}

For an equation $E$ define
\[
 L(E):=\D_{r,z}G_zE,
\]
where $r$ is unused. This is public and interderivable with $E$:
forward introduction ignores the progress assumption; backward
elimination uses the true zero gate and its instance at $1$.
Let $P(A)=L(A)$ for an equation, and $P(A)=A$ for a compound.
Then the translation from the old Eq/D language is
\[
 T(s=t)=(s=t),\qquad
 T(\D_{x,y}B)=\D_{x,y}P(TB).
\]
An old $\Q$ is represented by an unused existential coordinate.

The target matrix of every translated old case introduction is compound,
so the deterministic case rule suffices. Old atomic progress transfers
using auxiliary equation induction under the padded base, followed by
compound introduction. Elimination pads and unpads its instances.
Equality is recovered using the inherited zero-source admissibility
proof; public-template transport follows by induction on public syntax.
At guarded atomic binders the zero base is a theorem, which is precisely
what is needed in that induction.

Writing $J$ for literal inclusion of public formulas into the old native
language, the padding equivalence and binder congruence give
\[
 A\dashv\vdash JT(A),\qquad
 B\dashv\vdash TJ(B)\quad(B\in\G).
\]
Together with auxiliary soundness and agreement on public formulas,
these imply equality of the public consequence relations. Composing
with Section~\ref{sec:bridge-main} gives the claimed whole-domain
bi-interpretability and equiconsistency of the public theory.
This argument is inherited from the deterministic-auxiliary
construction~\cite{auxiliary}; its complete source is included among
the references. It is unchanged by the implementations below.

\section{Removing duplicate validation in the guarded checker}
\label{sec:factorization}

This is the main new implementation result. All kernel instructions,
context operations, scope updates, and input/output behavior are kept.
Only the public-goal validator is simplified.

\subsection{The representation and the original predicate}

The public codec uses arrays of arity one for D, two for equality,
one for numerical successor, and three for iteration. Protected
numerical slots are numbered with canonical representatives
$3,5,7,\ldots$; zero is the integer $0$. The extra slots under successor
are dummy zero slots in the inherited codec. They are not additional
object-language binders. Raw negative labels are allocated rigid
parameters inside proofs, not canonical free names in a closed public
goal.

If $p$ is the number of protected slots, the original term validator
accepts an exact Python integer precisely when
\[
 a=0\quad\text{or}\quad 3\le a<3+2p\text{ and }a\text{ is odd}.
\]
It accepts exact lists of arities one and three, recursing with $p+2$
and $p+1$, respectively. The formula validator accepts Eq and D;
Eq passes the same $p$ to its terms and D passes $p+2$ to its matrix.
If that matrix is atomic, each side must be a ternary iterator with
literal first two entries $[5,0]$. This is exactly
\eqref{main:guard} in the protected-slot codec.

\subsection{The smaller shape predicate}

The new predicate retains only a Boolean sort $d$: false for formula,
true for term. It checks exact types and legal arities, and checks that
each numerical integer is either zero or odd and at least three.
After recursive shape validation, an atomic matrix under D is checked
by the two-entry prefix test alone:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
all(b[:2] == [5, 0] for b in a[0])
\end{lstlisting}
No separate iterator-type or arity test is necessary at this point.

\begin{lemma}[Guard prefix after term validation]
On already shape-validated matrix sides, the new prefix check has
exactly the success cases of the old explicit guard check, with
exceptions counted as rejection.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A side is either an exact integer or an exact list with arity one or
three. Slicing an integer raises and rejects. A unary list has a prefix
of length one and cannot equal $[5,0]$. For a ternary list, equality of
the prefix states exactly the old two coordinate tests. Its update
has already been recursively validated. These exhaust the possibilities.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The existing opener supplies every omitted bound}

The kernel already begins each call with a no-image opening of the goal.
For an array of arity $r$, its traversal changes numerical depth from
$e$ to $e+5-r$. A formula starts at $e=0$. Equality adds three;
D and successor add four; iteration adds two. Thus term positions
have odd depth and formula positions have even depth.

At initial name floor $k=0$, a scalar is left unchanged exactly when
$0\le a<e$. An out-of-range term leaf tries to insert the absent image.
In the byte-profile implementation that default image is the function
object \texttt{c}; the scalar arithmetic operation on it raises. This
failure happens before a certificate instruction is read.

\begin{lemma}[Shape and initial opening]
Let $V$ be the old guarded public-goal validator, $W$ the new shape
predicate, and $O$ successful initial no-image opening. For every
finite JSON goal, abstracting from implementation resource limits,
\[
 V(A)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W(A)\land O(A).
\]
Whenever either side holds, the opener leaves $A$ literally unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a numerical position with $p$ protected slots, the traversal depth
is $3+2p$. The new shape check supplies exact integer type,
nonnegativity and the canonical parity/lower-bound conditions. Successful
opening supplies exactly the remaining upper bound $a<3+2p$.
All admitted scalar leaves are numerical, so their depths are odd;
an invalid leaf cannot be discarded as a true formula annotation.
Every public constructor is recursively visited. In-range leaves and
array structure are unchanged. The preceding lemma supplies exactly
the old atomic guard. Induction on finite input shape proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Preservation of the guarded certificate relation]
The 921-byte guarded module and its 1,201-byte comparator define the
same accepted mathematical certificate relation, abstracting from
implementation resource limits. In particular they accept exactly the
same proofs of every canonical guarded public formula.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The parser, goal extraction, kernel and final success marker are
identical. If the old validator accepts, the new one accepts and the
initial opener sees the same goal and succeeds. All subsequent
instructions are identical. If the new predicate rejects, the old one
cannot accept. If the new shape predicate succeeds but the old predicate
fails, the initial opener fails before any instruction is consumed.
Thus no malformed proof suffix or inert prefix can make that case
accept. This proves both directions.
\end{proof}

Runtime memory and recursion limits are excluded from this mathematical
identity. A validator refactoring can change the stack depth at which
an extremely nested input raises \texttt{RecursionError}. The finite
process comparisons record actual behavior within the tested bounds.
The theorem does not assert identical resource exhaustion thresholds.

\section{The two smaller local changes}
\label{sec:local}

\subsection{Full native public formulas: 803 to 788 bytes}

The native Eq/D public interface does not impose the guard
\eqref{main:guard}. Its kernel retains the inherited introduction-mode
choice. Its validator already factors scope checking as described in the
incoming \texttt{one\_context} package. The present change merely replaces
its early integer return and remaining list return by one conditional
expression:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
return integer_result if type(a) is int else list_result
\end{lstlisting}
Both branch expressions, their short-circuit order, and recursive calls
are unchanged. On an exact integer both versions evaluate only the
integer expression; otherwise both evaluate only the same list
expression. Structural induction gives the same predicate on finite
inputs. The rest of the module has identical AST. Consequently the
accepted certificate relation and inherited full-native metatheory are
unchanged. This saves 15 normalized bytes, one token and one AST node.
It is a code-structure improvement, not an axiom deletion.

\subsection{Compact guarded profile: 521 to 520 bytes}

The entire change is
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
([s, u] if s else [t] * 3)
\end{lstlisting}
replaced by
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
(s and [s, u] or [t] * 3)
\end{lstlisting}
If $s$ is false, both choose the triplicated template. If $s$ is true,
the list $[s,u]$ is nonempty and therefore true, so both choose it.
The branch values and relevant evaluations agree for all parsed JSON
values, not merely canonical mathematical inputs. Nothing else changes.

This reduction worsens AST size from 300 to 303 and gzip size from 327
to 329. It is reported because it gives a strictly smaller normalized
byte coordinate under the repository metric, but its mathematical
significance should not be exaggerated.

\section{Rejected shortcuts and remaining mathematical work}
\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Why context length did not remove freshness cost}

For the local-scope checker, the exact entry invariant is
\[
 k=1-|g|.
\]
It holds initially, major-premise recursion preserves the same context,
and each continuation appends one resource while decrementing $k$.
Thus a two-argument implementation can reconstruct $k$ at entry.
The correct version, however, measures 529 normalized bytes.

An apparently 514-byte version reconstructed a fixed threshold from
context length and removed the decrement before final opening. This is
invalid: the opener closes over $k$, and the original program updates
that bound \emph{before} validating the inserted fresh name. The altered
version rejects valid proofs. It is not delivered as a smaller kernel.
The correct 529-byte variant and analysis are retained only as a research
note, not a new best profile.

\subsection{Other non-results}

Deleting public $\Q$ syntax would repeat inherited clause syntax.
One-sided crossed transport would repeat the current incoming proposal.
Removing image revalidation during substitution was not justified:
its role includes rejecting out-of-scope witness terms before a fresh
eigenparameter becomes available. Introducing an arbitrary quantified
matrix into the progress rule would strengthen the theory toward full
formula induction and is not an allowed simplification of iPRA.

No smaller initialized-iteration basis was established. In particular,
introducing a function by a total, unique quantifier-free graph does not
by itself show that an equation-induction or quantifier-free-induction
schema is preserved in the expanded language. Eliminating the new
function can introduce existential quantifiers. A proposed zero-start
iterator bootstrap must discharge this obligation, rather than borrowing
the existing scalar bridge unchanged.

\subsection{Further research questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Can the case/progress interface be simplified on the full native
language without changing the meaning of unguarded atomic D formulas?
A solution should explain both formula composites, including the
$E(y)\equiv y=1$ test in Section~\ref{sec:guarded-main}.
\item Can administrative Q resources be made unforgeable by the
certificate format, so that one simpler elimination or introduction
dispatcher suffices? The format decoder and its scope checks must be
included in the measured trusted module.
\item Can a genuinely new scalar predecessor construction avoid one
term constructor or one computation schema? Any proposal must work in
arbitrary models, preserve restricted induction, and establish the
internal uniqueness needed for the two interpretation composites.
\item Can a finite syntax-directed validator be mechanically shown to
refine the existing Lean native kernel? That would close the remaining
gap between the formalized mathematics and the Python certificate codec.
\item Can one program improve all three coordinates of the current
compact envelope? The 520-byte profile does not do this, and gzip size
should continue to be reported separately rather than optimized.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Artifacts, reproduction, and assurance}
\label{sec:reproduction}

The archive contains the three complete standalone programs, exact
comparators, source provenance, representative proof and rejection
certificates, machine-readable claims, measurements, validation receipts,
this LaTeX source and its compiled PDF. No checker imports a producer,
measurement module, or test helper.

Run the portable reproduction with Python 3.12:
\begin{lstlisting}
python3 reproduce.py
\end{lstlisting}
The normalization convention is
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
ast.unparse(ast.parse(source)) + '\n'
\end{lstlisting}
The complete normalized UTF-8 source is measured. AST size counts every
node from \texttt{ast.walk}. Significant tokens exclude encoding,
comments, whitespace/indentation, newline markers and end markers.
Gzip uses compression level 9 and timestamp zero. The run used CPython
3.12.14. Exact environment details and source hashes are in the
measurement receipt.

During development the guarded refinement passed 30,255
validator-factorization comparisons and 284 full process runs on 71
fixtures, across normal and optimized Python. The native refinement
passed 10,255 validator comparisons and 168 full process runs on 42
fixtures. There were no mismatches or timeouts. Some inherited inputs
are expected rejections because they belong to another public interface;
the receipts record actual outcomes rather than treating every supplied
file as a positive theorem. The portable reproduction is a separate
selected suite whose exact count is recorded in its own receipt.

These finite checks support the implementations. They do not replace the
universal preservation proofs in Sections~\ref{sec:factorization} and

The new metatheorems were also independently reviewed within this run.

verification. The archive makes no claim of minimality or of a new
