Independent review: positive_iteration_obstruction.tex
====================================================

Verdict
-------
The standard-model zero-profile theorem and its stated term-definability
consequences are correct for the exact staged language {variables, 0, S,
R+}, where R+(n,a;v.h) performs n+1 applications of the fixed update.
I found one correction needed in the later graph-definition observation:
add successor separation 0 != S(m) to coverage and injectivity before
claiming its graph is total and unique.

Structural induction and binding
--------------------------------
Take all bound update variables fresh for the counter, seed, and passive
parameters (or track free-variable identities under ordinary alpha
renaming). The induction hypothesis for the fixed body h treats its state
variable v and every passive parameter as free arguments. Its zero set is
either empty or the simultaneous vanishing of a fixed subset H of those
arguments. A nested iterator within h causes no gap: h is a structurally
smaller fixed term, so its complete denotation, including the nested
iteration, is covered by the induction hypothesis.

At a fixed valuation of the passive parameters, let k be the conjunction
of their required zero conditions. If v is absent from H, one update sets
the zero-status bit to the constant k. If v is in H, it sends the previous
zero-status bit b to b AND k. Both maps are idempotent after one application.
If h has empty zero set, the updated state is always nonzero. Thus an
arbitrary positive number of iterations has exactly the profile claimed
in the text. If v is present, a never-zero seed remains never zero; this
justifies the absorbing-bottom convention. If v is absent, the seed's
profile truly drops out.

The outer counter n may share free variables with the seed and update.
This is harmless: first fix the entire valuation. It fixes the update map,
the seed, and a nonnegative counter value, so there is at least one update.
Any variable appearing separately in h or a is still present in H or the
seed profile when required. Only the numerical value of the outer counter
as an iteration count drops out. Suggested clarification: "The counter's
value contributes only the guarantee of at least one update; variables
that also occur in the seed or update retain their ordinary profiles."

Successor has no zeros; zero has all inputs as zeros; a variable vanishes
exactly at its own zero coordinate. These base cases and the update
argument close structural induction for every fixed term. No internal
induction or standard-domain assumption has been smuggled into a claimed
arbitrary-model theorem: the result is explicitly a standard-N theorem.

Consequences
------------
For one free input, the only possible zero sets are empty, {0}, and N.
Predecessor has zero set {0,1}; the 1-at-zero, 0-at-positive zero-test has
zero set N minus {0}. Neither is term-definable in this language. A term
compiler for general initialized iteration would yield that zero-test
from constant update zero and seed one, hence is impossible. The claim is
properly restricted to terms, not relational definitions or changed domains.

Graph-definition correction
---------------------------
For each fixed old-language update h, the graph
  (n=0 AND z=a) OR exists m (n=S(m) AND z=R+(m,a;h))
is total by coverage. It is unique using successor injectivity within the
second branch and successor separation to exclude overlap of the two
branches. Coverage and injectivity alone do not exclude that overlap.

There is an explicit countermodel showing the missing separation matters.
Take domain {0,1}, interpret zero by 0, and let successor exchange 0 and 1.
Interpret every staged positive iterator as
  R+(0,a;h) = h(a),
  R+(1,a;h) = h(h(a)).
Every unary function h on a two-element set satisfies h^3=h (the four maps
are identity, exchange, constant zero, and constant one). Therefore both
R+ equations hold at both counter values, for each parameter valuation,
and the staged definition works for every fixed nested description.
Successor is injective and coverage holds, but zero is a successor.
For n=0, a=0, and h constant one, the proposed graph holds for z=0 by its
first branch and z=1 by its second branch with m=1. It is not unique.

After adding separation explicitly, the graph observation is correct.
The report appropriately does not infer full bi-interpretability from it:
induction after graph expansion and closure under updates using the new
functions remain separate obligations. The logical bootstrap's dependence
on a selector is likewise correctly identified as a possible circularity.

Scope discipline
----------------
The theorem ceases to apply after adjoining a zero-test primitive or any
other operation whose standard zero set violates the profile invariant.
No claim about such extensions, arbitrary relational interpretations,
finite-dimensional quotients, or changed numerical domains follows from
this proof alone. The existing exact-scope paragraph states that correctly.
