\begin{abstract}
We give a smaller complete presentation of full first-order intuitionistic
primitive recursive arithmetic. Numerical syntax has variables, zero, and
one ternary recursor; successor is a special instance. The recursor and the
logical binder each bind two numerical variables in their final child.
There is one fixed reflexivity clause and three inference families:
clause use, introduction, and substitution at witness discharge.
Computation is part of canonical syntax and is included in the checker.
We prove the recovery of equality, full intuitionistic logic, literal-equation
and quantifier-free induction, and both interpretation round trips with iPRA.
A direct compiler for primitive recursion avoids pairing.

The construction combines existing ideas in the inspected repository;
its contribution is the resulting smaller presentation and its checked
implementation, not priority for its individual ingredients. A separate
refinement of the existing native checker removes a duplicate grammar
traversal. All size comparisons include complete standalone programs.
No absolute minimality theorem or new Lean/Rocq verification is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result, comparison, and precise scope}\label{sec:result}

The reference is \path{VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt}, directory
\path{Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA}, at the commit
printed on the title page. We inspected its current overview, the relevant
filed checkers and proofs, and all six incoming archives in that snapshot.
In particular, the comparison includes \texttt{inferred\_witness},
\texttt{deterministic\_opening}, \texttt{clause\_discharge},
\texttt{first\_instance}, \texttt{prefix\_clauses}, and
\texttt{substitutive\_discharge}. These are part of the baseline, even
where the overview has not yet incorporated their results.

\paragraph{The mathematical simplification.}
The existing three-family scalar discharge presentation has distinct
successor and iterator constructors. The presentation $\RN$ uses a
counter-aware recursor $R$ whose zero case returns the successor of its
initializer. It removes the separate successor constructor, and makes
the binding convention of numerical recursion identical to that of the
logical paired binder. The rule count stays at three. Full normalization
is an explicit part of the specification and of the measured code.

\paragraph{The separate implementation simplification.}
The current native public-language checker performs one traversal to
check grammar and another to check scope. Its existing depth counter
already records the syntactic sort by parity. Fusing the checks removes
the independent validator while preserving the entire certificate
acceptance relation. This refinement implements the existing native
calculus; it is not an alternative count for the new $\RN$ checker.

\begin{theorem}[Main result]\label{thm:main}
The calculus $\RN$ specified below is a faithful proof presentation of an
ordinary intuitionistic theory bi-interpretable with first-order iPRA.
Both interpretation domains are the whole numerical domain; equality
is actual equality. In each direction the numerical composite is
internally isomorphic to the identity by the graph $x=y$, and every
formula is provably equivalent to its double translation. The systems
are equiconsistent.
\end{theorem}

\section{The entire primitive presentation}\label{sec:calculus}

\subsection{Numerical syntax and canonical computation}
There is one numerical sort. Raw terms are
\[
 t ::= x\mid 0\mid R(n,a;i,u.h).
\]
Only $i,u$ are bound in $h$; they are not bound in $n$ or $a$. All
parameters of $h$ other than $i,u$ remain fixed during recursion.
Define the notation
\[
 S(a):=R(0,a;i,u.0),\qquad \one:=S0.
\]
Thus $S$ is not an additional term constructor. The computation equations
are
\begin{align}
 R(0,a;i,u.h)&=S(a),\label{eq:Rzero}\\
 R(Sn,a;i,u.h)&=h[n/i,R(n,a;i,u.h)/u].\label{eq:Rsucc}
\end{align}
Substitution on the right is simultaneous and capture avoiding.

Public terms are the full computation normal forms for these equations,
written $\NF(t)$. At a zero counter the body is represented canonically
by $0$, so $R(0,a;i,u.0)$ is the canonical successor node. A canonical
nonzero recursion node has a counter that is neither zero nor
successor headed. All its children, including its body, are canonical.
All rule substitutions below include normalization. No formula binder
is erased by normalization.

More explicitly, normalize the children $n,a,h$. If $n=0$, return
$R(0,a;i,u.0)$. If $n=R(0,m;i,u.0)$, normalize the predecessor
recursion and then the simultaneous substitution in
\eqref{eq:Rsucc}. Otherwise retain the recursion node. The zero-body
erasure is performed after the supplied children have been checked;
it is not a license to hide malformed syntax in an erased child.

\subsection{Formulas, clauses, and falsity}
Native formulas are
\[
 A ::= s=t\mid \DD_{x,y}B(x,y).
\]
The paired binder binds $x,y$ throughout $B$. Its ordinary reading is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dreading}
 \DD_{x,y}B(x,y)\;:=\;
 \exists x\bigl(B(x,0)\to\forall y\,B(x,y)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The designated contradiction is $\bot_*:=(0=\one)$.

A context is a finite reusable list of clauses $[A;y.B(y)]$, read as
$A\to\forall yB(y)$, where $y$ is absent from $A$. An empty antecedent
is allowed and is written $[\ ;y.B(y)]$, meaning $\forall yB(y)$.
An ordinary assumption $F$ is the constant clause $[\ ;v.F]$ for unused
$v$. Every context contains the single fixed axiom clause
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflexivity-clause}
                    [\ ;y.y=y].
\end{equation}
The initial closed-proof context consists of this clause alone.

\subsection{Exactly three inference families}

\paragraph{Use.}
For a retained clause $[A;y.B(y)]$ and any well-scoped term $t$,
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash\NF(B(t))}.
\]
If the antecedent is empty, the rule has no premise. In particular,
\eqref{eq:reflexivity-clause} derives every $t=t$.

\paragraph{Close.}
Let $t$ be a term in the outer context and $z$ fresh for that context,
the conclusion, and $t$. Set
\[
 \eta=\begin{cases}
 z,&\text{if $B$ is a literal equation and $t$ is literally $0$},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:close}
 \frac{\Gamma,\NF(B(t,\eta))\vdash\NF(B(t,Sz))}
      {\Gamma\vdash\DD_{x,y}B(x,y)}.
\end{equation}
The added assumption is an unconditional constant clause and is
discharged. This is a single syntactically determined rule. It does
not allow induction on arbitrary quantified formulas.

\paragraph{Name.}
From a proof of $\DD_{x,y}B$, introduce a fresh numerical parameter $b$
and the clause
\[
 \mathcal B_b=[\NF(B(b,0));y.\NF(B(b,y))].
\]
The rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:name}
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash\DD_{x,y}B\qquad
       \Gamma,\mathcal B_b\vdash b=t\qquad
       \Gamma,\mathcal B_b\vdash F}
      {\Gamma\vdash\NF(F[t/b])},
 \qquad b\notin\FV(\Gamma,\NF(F[t/b])).
\end{equation}
The replacement term $t$ and intermediate formula $F$ may mention $b$.
Substitution replaces its free occurrences once and does not revisit
an inserted copy of $t$. The exported normalized formula must be in
the outer scope. The special case $t=b$ is permitted.

\paragraph{No other primitives.}
The syntax conventions, fixed clause, and these three families are the
whole calculus. There is no primitive equality-transport rule, hypothesis
rule separate from Use, computation inference, ordinary logical axiom,
successor axiom, or separate induction rule. Computation and the
normalization algorithm nevertheless count as trusted specification.

\section{Canonical syntax and implementation invariants}\label{sec:norm}

\subsection{Why normalization is legitimate}

\begin{lemma}[Normalization and substitution]\label{lem:norm}
Every well-scoped raw numerical term has a unique computation normal
form. Normalization preserves scope and is compatible with renaming
and capture-avoiding substitution:
\[
 \NF\bigl(\NF(t)[\NF(\sigma)]\bigr)=\NF(t[\sigma]).
\]
The same statements hold componentwise for formulas and clauses.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the termination argument, temporarily use an auxiliary language with
an independent successor and the ordinary base-type primitive recursor.
Read $R(n,a;i,u.h)$ as recursion with initial value $S(a)$ and update
$h(i,u)$. Its zero and successor reductions are the base-type fragment
of the reductions of G\"odel's system $T$, whose strong normalization
is established by the usual reducibility argument; a primary account
of the surrounding typed normalization framework is cited in
\cite{urciuoli}. The ordinary recursive syntax here has only numerical
variables and numerical results: higher-type recursion is not being
added to the object theory.

One can see the relevant fragment of the argument directly. Declare
a numerical expression computable when it is strongly normalizing;
declare a fixed update computable when substituting computable
numerical arguments gives a computable expression. Induction on term
formation gives closure under the zero and successor constructors.
For a recursor, use the reduction heights of its arguments and a
subsidiary induction on the successor spine of a normal counter.
At a neutral counter there is no head reduction. At a successor
counter the recursive call has the shorter spine and the computable
update preserves computability. The same argument under computable
substitutions treats open bodies and their reductions. This is the
standard base-type recursor case of the reducibility proof.

In the auxiliary translation, treat the designated canonical node
$R(0,a;i,u.0)$ directly as $S(a)$; translate every other $R$ node as
the ordinary successor-seeded recursor. With this convention an erasure
of another zero-body node is a zero reduction in the auxiliary syntax.
If a reduction inside a body creates the canonical successor shape,
follow its translated reduction by the corresponding zero reduction.
Every genuine successor contraction
is an ordinary recursor reduction. Changes inside a body later erased
are already covered by strong normalization of that body. Thus the
canonical notation does not introduce an infinite reduction.

The ordinary zero and successor rules have disjoint head patterns;
reductions inside their arguments commute with the head reduction,
using substitution in the successor case. Erasure of an unused body
also commutes with reductions inside that body. These observations
give local confluence. Termination and local confluence give unique
normal forms. Reductions are stable under capture-avoiding
substitution, so the two displayed terms are convertible and have the
same unique normal form. Binder structure at formula nodes is fixed,
so the formula and clause statements follow.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{No universal primitive-recursive normalizer is assumed.}
Each fixed term denotes a primitive recursive function. It does not
follow that evaluation over arbitrary term descriptions is primitive
recursive uniformly. Such a universal evaluator would yield the usual
diagonal contradiction. The unbounded normalizer is an external total
recursive procedure. The archive also supplies an explicitly fuel-bounded
checker. Bounded simulation gives a primitive-recursive certificate
predicate with the same set of provable formulas, since every finite
successful run has some sufficient budget. No internal assertion that
all description codes normalize is an axiom of $\RN$ or iPRA.

\subsection{A binding detail that matters}
The concrete representation uses $0$ for zero, negative integers for
allocated free parameters, and positive one-based de Bruijn indices
for bound variables. Terms are ternary arrays $[n,a,h]$, equations
are binary arrays $[s,t]$, and $\DD$ formulas are unary arrays $[B]$.
For both the recursor body and the paired formula body, index $1$ is
the inner variable ($u$ or $y$), and index $2$ is the outer variable
($i$ or $x$).

In a successor contraction, remove the counter binder first, under the
retained state binder, and remove the state binder second:
\[
 \operatorname{sub}\bigl(
   \operatorname{sub}(h,n,\mathit{depth}=1),
   \NF(R(n,a;i,u.h))\bigr).
\]
The depth-one insertion lifts the counter term correctly. Substituting
the previous state first without lifting it would capture an ambient
positive index under the still-present counter binder. Nested-binder
regressions in the executable suite specifically check this case.

The checker synthesizes a formula at each proof node, then validates
its canonical form and numerical scope. Name obtains its matrix from
the major proof and its target from the equality proof; neither is
trusted merely because it appears in the certificate. An exported
formula is checked in the outer scope, so witness escape is rejected.
The public result is compared with the fully validated claimed goal.

\section{Arithmetic definitions without a separate successor}\label{sec:arithmetic-defs}

All displayed definitions denote fixed finite terms; there is no
quantification over function codes or an internal universal evaluator.
Write equality of canonical syntax as $\equiv$.

\begin{definition}[Predecessor on successors and selection]
Define
\begin{align}
 W(n)&:=\NF(R(n,0;i,u.i)),\label{eq:W}\\
 C(n,a,b)&:=\NF\bigl(W(R(n,a;i,u.Sb))\bigr),\label{eq:C}\\
 P(n)&:=C(n,0,W(n)),\label{eq:P}\\
 K(y)&:=C(W(y),\one,0).\label{eq:K}
\end{align}
The bound variables $i,u$ in the selector are unused in its update.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint computations]\label{lem:endpoints}
The following are literal normal-form identities:
\begin{align*}
 W(0)&\equiv\one,&W(Sn)&\equiv n,\\
 C(0,a,b)&\equiv a,&C(Sn,a,b)&\equiv b,\\
 P(0)&\equiv0,&P(Sn)&\equiv n,\\
 K(\one)&\equiv\one,&K(S\one)&\equiv0.
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The successor equation for $W$ returns its counter argument and ignores
its state. The zero equation returns $\one$. In $C(0,a,b)$ the inner
recursor returns $Sa$, to which $W$ applies as a left inverse. In
$C(Sn,a,b)$ the inner recursor returns $Sb$. The statements about $P$
and $K$ are then immediate substitutions. No induction, equality
transport, or range theorem for $K$ is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Ordinary initialized recursion]\label{def:J}
For a fixed update $h(i,u)$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
 J(n,a;i,u.h):=
 \NF\left(W\left(R(n,a;i,v.S\bigl(h(i,W(v))\bigr))\right)\right).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[A direct primitive-recursion interface]\label{lem:J}
For arbitrary terms and fixed passive parameters,
\[
 J(0,a;i,u.h)\equiv a,\qquad
 J(Sn,a;i,u.h)\equiv
 \NF\bigl(h(n,J(n,a;i,u.h))\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At zero the outer $W$ cancels the successor of the initializer. At a
successor the update returns the successor of
$h(n,W(R(n,a;\ldots)))$; the outer $W$ cancels that successor.
Lemma~\ref{lem:norm} identifies the nested normalizations.
\end{proof}

Initial functions and composition are now immediate, and a primitive
recursion $f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p)$,
$f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p)$ compiles as
\[
 f^*(n,\vec p)=J(n,g^*(\vec p);i,u.h^*(i,u,\vec p)).
\]
This is external recursion on a finite PR description. Unlike the scalar
iterator compiler, it requires no pairing, projection construction, or
encoded clock. Each defining equation follows by literal normalization
and reflexivity. The scalar iterator remains available as
$I(n,a;u.h):=J(n,a;i,u.h)$ with $i$ unused.

\section{Recovering equality and induction}\label{sec:equality}

We give the proof because simply moving equality into witness discharge
would otherwise leave an apparent circularity. The construction adapts
the two-row packet of the existing \texttt{witness\_discharge} proof
\cite{witness}; its only numerical requirements are the endpoint identities
of Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoints}.

\subsection{Structural rules, ordinary elimination, and cases}
Weakening, exchange, contraction, injective renaming of free parameters,
and cut of an ordinary assumption are admissible. Prove these by induction
on finite derivations, freshening local parameters when needed. An ordinary
assumption is used through its constant unconditional clause; replace
each such use by the supplied derivation. No arbitrary numerical
substitution theorem is needed for these structural facts.

\begin{lemma}[Ordinary paired elimination]\label{lem:elim}
From $\Gamma\vdash\DD_{x,y}B$ and
$\Gamma,\mathcal B_b\vdash A$, with $b\notin\FV(\Gamma,A)$,
one may infer $\Gamma\vdash A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In Name take $t=b$. Its equality premise is reflexivity $b=b$,
and its formula premise is $A$. The export is literally $A$.
\end{proof}

For an unused witness coordinate abbreviate
$\QQ_y A(y):=\DD_{x,y}A(y)$.
\begin{lemma}[Closure and numerical cases]\label{lem:cases}
The following rules are derived:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,A(0)\vdash A(Sz)}{\Gamma\vdash\QQ_y A(y)},
 \qquad
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash\QQ_y A(y)\quad\Gamma\vdash A(0)}
      {\Gamma\vdash A(t)}.
\]
Consequently $\Gamma\vdash A(0)$ and
$\Gamma\vdash A(Sz)$, with $z$ fresh, imply $\Gamma\vdash A(t)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Introduce the unused witness at $\one$, which is not literal zero, so
Close selects cases. For elimination, open the paired formula using
Lemma~\ref{lem:elim}; its clause is $[A(0);y.A(y)]$. Use the supplied
base proof and target $t$. For numerical cases weaken the successor
proof by the unused base assumption and compose these two steps.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transport from one equality-bearing discharge}
\begin{lemma}[Term transport]\label{lem:transport}
For every fixed term template $h(w)$,
\[
 \Gamma\vdash s=t,\quad\Gamma\vdash\NF(h(s))=r
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma\vdash\NF(h(t))=r.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $x,y$ fresh and set
\[
 B(x,y):=
 \bigl(C(y,0,C(K(y),x,\NF(h(x))))
       =C(y,0,C(K(y),t,r))\bigr).
\]
To introduce $\DD_{x,y}B$ at witness $s$, its successor row is
$C(K(Sz),s,\NF(h(s)))=C(K(Sz),t,r)$.
Apply numerical cases to
$C(v,s,\NF(h(s)))=C(v,t,r)$ at $v=K(Sz)$.
The zero case is $s=t$, and every successor case is $\NF(h(s))=r$.
This ignores the temporary assumption of Close and therefore works
in either selected mode, including $s=0$.

Open the package at fresh $b$. Its zero row is $0=0$.
Use its clause at $\one$ and $S\one$ to obtain respectively
$\NF(h(b))=r$ and $b=t$. In Name use these as the formula and
equality premises. The export is $\NF(h(t))=r$ by normalization--
substitution coherence. None of the passive data contains $b$.
\end{proof}

Symmetry follows by taking $h(w)=w$ and $r=s$. Congruence follows
using a reflexive minor premise; transitivity follows by joining
at the root. Repeating transport gives replacement on both sides
of an equation.

\begin{lemma}[Formula transport]\label{lem:formula-transport}
From $s=t$ and $F(s)$ one may derive $F(t)$ for every native formula
template $F$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct externally on the number of paired binders. The atomic case
is two applications of term transport. For
$F(v)=\DD_{x,y}B(v,x,y)$, eliminate $F(s)$ at a fresh $c$.
Introduce $F(t)$ at witness $c$, which is not literal zero and so
uses cases. Under $B(t,c,0)$, the induction hypothesis and symmetry
give $B(s,c,0)$. Use the old clause at $Sz$, and transport its result
to $B(t,c,Sz)$ by the induction hypothesis. Close and eliminate.
Every recursive transport has a strictly smaller formula template.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The missing zero-witness case is derived}
Let $H=(0=0)$ and, for an equation $E=(s=t)$, put
\[
 G_vE:=(C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t)),\qquad L(E):=\QQ_vG_vE.
\]
The endpoints are $G_0E\equiv H$ and $G_{Sw}E\equiv E$.
Closure and Use show $E\dashv\vdash L(E)$ in every retained context.

Suppose $\Gamma,E(0,0)\vdash E(0,Sz)$ is a case premise for an
atomic matrix at witness zero. Apply the same premise, through the
uniform equivalence just proved, to the compound matrix
$A(x,y)=L(E(x,y))$. Close at witness zero now selects cases because
$A$ is compound. Open $\DD A$ at fresh $c$; introduce $\DD E$ at
witness $c$, wrapping its base, using the opened clause, and unwrapping
its successor row. This recovers the omitted atomic case introduction.
It does not rely on a closed nonliteral term that normalizes to zero.

\begin{corollary}[Unrestricted cases and equation induction]\label{cor:induction}
Case introduction is derived for every matrix and witness. In addition,
for each literal equation $E(n)$ and arbitrary retained context,
\[
 \Gamma\vdash E(0),\qquad\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Gamma\vdash E(t).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For induction use Close in progress mode at witness zero with a
matrix independent of its witness coordinate. This produces
$\QQ_yE(y)$, which Use specializes from the given base.
\end{proof}

The derived liberal calculus with ordinary elimination, equality
transport, unrestricted case introduction, and equational progress
is useful for translations: it avoids assuming that the literal-zero
mode test is preserved by substitution. In particular, it handles
translations whose normalized witness becomes zero.

\section{Full intuitionistic logic is present}\label{sec:logic}

For completeness we specify the logical macros and their proof
interfaces. These are abbreviations constructed from Eq and $\DD$;
they are not additional primitive connectives or rules.

\subsection{Gates, padding, and formula selection}
First observe that paired binders preserve pointwise interderivability
of their matrices in arbitrary retained contexts. To convert
$\DD B$ into $\DD C$, open the former at a fresh $b$ and introduce
the latter at witness $b$, hence in case mode. Convert $C(b,0)$
back to $B(b,0)$, use the opened clause at $Sz$, and convert
$B(b,Sz)$ forward to $C(b,Sz)$. The reverse direction is symmetric.
This binder-congruence principle is a derived finite proof transformation.

Extend $G_v$ recursively through paired binders, keeping $v$ fresh for
their bound variables. Then $G_0A$ is derivable and
$G_{Sw}A\dashv\vdash A$. At an equation these facts are endpoint
computation and reflexivity. At a binder, use unrestricted case
introduction and ordinary elimination with the smaller-matrix facts.
If the argument already depends on the control, read these statements
pointwise: $G_0A(0)$ is derivable, and
$G_{Sw}A(Sw)\dashv\vdash A(Sw)$. This convention is used in
the conjunction and universal-quantifier macros below.

The padding operation $L(A)=\QQ_vG_vA$ is equivalent to $A$ and
increases its number of $\DD$ binders by one. Every native formula
is a finite chain of paired binders ending in one equation. Pad two
formulas to the same depth. Define $\operatorname{Sel}_v(A,B)$ by
retaining their common binder prefix and using $C(v,-,-)$ on their
two corresponding equation endpoints. Bound variables are renamed
and shifted consistently before alignment. Computation and binder
congruence give
\[
 \operatorname{Sel}_0(A,B)\dashv\vdash A,\qquad
 \operatorname{Sel}_{Sw}(A,B)\dashv\vdash B.
\]
Numerical cases consequently give selection from two proofs and case
elimination of a selected formula. This alignment construction also
handles formulas of different initial depths; it does not assume
that an unused constant paired binder preserves a formula.

\subsection{The macros and all ordinary rule interfaces}
Write $\exists^*_xA:=\DD_{x,y}G_yA(x)$. Then use the following
abbreviations, with all displayed control variables fresh:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
Ordinary operation & Native abbreviation\\\midrule
$\bot$ & $0=\one$\\
$A\to B$ & $\QQ_y\operatorname{Sel}_y(A,B)$\\
$A\land B$ & $\QQ_yG_y\operatorname{Sel}_{K(y)}(A,B)$\\
$A\lor B$ & $\exists^*_v\operatorname{Sel}_v(A,B)$\\
$\exists xA(x)$ & $\exists^*_xA(x)$\\
$\forall xA(x)$ & $\QQ_yG_yA(W(y))$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\begin{lemma}[Natural deduction and formula meanings]\label{lem:logic}
These abbreviations admit every introduction and elimination rule of
intuitionistic first-order natural deduction with equality, including
the usual eigenvariable conditions and explosion. Under
\eqref{eq:Dreading}, each abbreviation is equivalent to its ordinary
logical meaning.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For implication, closure introduction is implication introduction:
the zero row is $A$, and the successor row is $B$. Use at $\one$
is modus ponens. For conjunction, the gate makes its zero row
derivable. Its first two positive rows are $B$ and $A$ respectively
because $K(\one)=\one$ and $K(S\one)=0$. Conversely proofs of
both conjuncts give the selector at every index by numerical cases,
and then closure introduction gives the conjunction.

For $\exists^*$, a proof of $A(t)$ supplies every positive row of
its gate, so case introduction gives the existential introduction
rule. On elimination, the opened clause has a derivable zero row
and yields $A(b)$ at $\one$; the fresh witness can then be
discharged. Selection and this existential give both disjunction
introductions and disjunction elimination.

For universal quantification the zero gate row is derivable and
$W(Sz)=z$, so its positive row is $A(z)$. Generalization at a
fresh $z$ supplies all positive rows; specialization at $St$
returns $A(t)$. The ordinary reading says exactly that every
$A(t)$ holds, by these same endpoint identities and coverage.

From $0=\one$, transport the control of $C(-,s,t)$ to obtain
$s=t$ for arbitrary terms. Induction on formula depth with case
introduction derives every native formula, giving explosion.
Equality rules were derived in Section~\ref{sec:equality}.
All context-management steps use the structural admissibility
proved there. Each displayed argument is intuitionistic and also
proves the asserted equivalence under the ordinary reading.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical coverage.}
The derived numerical-case rule proves
$t=0\lor\exists z\,t=Sz$: its zero case uses the left disjunct;
its successor case uses the exhibited predecessor. Because
$P(Sz)=z$, it also proves the stronger equation-based coverage
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coverage}
                  t=0\lor t=S(Pt).
\end{equation}
This is a theorem on the whole numerical domain, not an appeal
to the intended standard model.

\section{From equation induction to iPRA}\label{sec:qfi}

This section supplies the arithmetic step that cannot be replaced by
claiming that primitive recursive functions have been represented.
The reflection construction is adapted from the existing
\texttt{equality\_reflection.tex} proof \cite{reflection}; its
prerequisites are now available directly from $R$.

Work temporarily in the ordinary realization: intuitionistic
first-order logic with actual equality, the $R$ computation equations,
coverage \eqref{eq:coverage}, separation $0=\one\to\bot$, and
contextual induction for literal equations. Successor is injective,
since applying $P$ to $Sx=Sy$ gives $x=y$. Put
\[
 \operatorname{sg}(x)=C(x,0,\one),\qquad
 T(x)=C(x,\one,Px).
\]
Coverage gives
$\operatorname{sg}(x)=0\leftrightarrow x=0$ and
$\operatorname{sg}(x)=0\lor\operatorname{sg}(x)=\one$.
It also gives $T(x)=0\leftrightarrow x=\one$.
Applying sign to $Sx=0$ yields $\one=0$, so every successor is
separated from zero. These arguments require no induction.

Using $J$, define addition and truncated subtraction by
\[
 x+0=x,\quad x+Sy=S(x+y),\qquad
 d(x,0)=x,\quad d(x,Sy)=P(d(x,y)).
\]
Literal-equation induction proves
\begin{gather*}
 0+y=y,\qquad (Sx)+y=S(x+y),\\
 d(Sx,Sy)=d(x,y),\qquad d(Sa,a)=\one.
\end{gather*}
For the cancellation identity induct on $y$ with $x$ fixed: its
base is $P(Sx)=x$ and its step applies $P$ to the previous
identity. The diagonal identity then follows by induction on $a$.

\begin{lemma}[Residual reflection]\label{lem:reflection}
The ordinary realization proves
\[
 d(Sa,b)=\one\ \leftrightarrow\ a=b.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First prove by equation induction on $y$, with $x$ fixed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 C(\operatorname{sg}(d(x,y)),x,y+d(x,y))=x.
\end{equation}
At zero both selected branches are $x$. For the step put
$r=d(x,y)$ and $r'=Pr=d(x,Sy)$. If $r'=0$, the new selector
immediately returns $x$. Otherwise coverage says $r'$ is a
successor. Coverage for $r$ and $P0=0$ then imply $r=Sr'$;
the induction equation gives $y+r=x$. The elementary addition
identity $(Sy)+r'=y+Sr'=y+r$ gives the required selected
branch. Every induction formula used here is a single equation;
the case splits use only coverage.

Substitute $x=Sa$, $y=b$ in \eqref{eq:residual}. If the
residual is $\one$, then $b+\one=Sa$, hence $Sb=Sa$ and
$b=a$ by predecessor. The converse follows from
$d(Sa,a)=\one$ and equality replacement.
\end{proof}

Define
\[
 e(a,b):=\operatorname{sg}(T(d(Sa,b))).
\]
The preceding results yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eqtest}
 e(a,b)=0\leftrightarrow a=b,
 \qquad e(a,b)=0\lor e(a,b)=\one.
\end{equation}
These are internal statements about actual equality for all
domain elements. In particular, equality is decidable; this
has been derived without assuming excluded middle for arbitrary
formulas.

For a fixed quantifier-free formula $A$, define its zero-true
characteristic by external recursion:
\begin{align*}
 b_{s=t}&=e(s,t),&b_\bot&=\one,\\
 b_{A\land B}&=C(b_A,b_B,\one),&
 b_{A\lor B}&=C(b_A,0,b_B),\\
 b_{A\to B}&=C(b_A,b_B,0).
\end{align*}
Using the two-valued ranges and a case split on $b_A$ proves
\[
             b_A=0\leftrightarrow A,
             \qquad b_A=0\lor b_A=\one.
\]
For implication, the zero branch of $b_A$ gives $A$ and hence
reduces $A\to B$ to $B$; the one branch gives $\neg A$ and
therefore the implication by explosion. This uses only the
proved decidability of the finite characteristics.

Now translate the base and step of any quantifier-free induction
instance to the equation $b_A(n)=0$, apply equation induction,
and translate back. The retained context may contain arbitrary
quantified formulas. Thus the presentation recovers precisely
the contextual quantifier-free induction of first-order iPRA.

\section{Both interpretations and their composites}\label{sec:biinterp}

\subsection{Ordinary realization and its exact boundary}
Let $T_R$ be ordinary intuitionistic first-order logic with actual
equality and genuine falsity, the staged $R$ descriptions and
equations \eqref{eq:Rzero}--\eqref{eq:Rsucc}, coverage,
separation $0=\one\to\bot$, and contextual equation induction.
Staging means that each recursion description has a fixed
earlier-stage numerical update, with explicit finite parameters.
There is no additional sort of functions.

The separation sentence must be included: the positive Eq/$\DD$
formulas read in isolation admit singleton structures. The
designated contradiction and full ordinary realization are
part of the claim, so that this singleton issue is not silently
discarded.

\begin{lemma}[Native/ordinary adequacy]\label{lem:adequacy}
The native calculus $\RN$ and $T_R$ interpret one another
faithfully under \eqref{eq:Dreading} and the logical macros,
for arbitrary finite contexts. Their formula composites are
interderivable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Read a native equation as equality and a native clause as its
conditional universal meaning. Use is ordinary implication and
universal elimination. In case-mode Close, retain its zero row
and use coverage to obtain every row; then introduce the chosen
witness. In progress-mode Close, use equation induction under
the zero row. Name uses ordinary existential elimination and
equality replacement in its branch; its exported result is
fresh-witness free. Normalization replaces terms by provably
equal terms: each finite reduction uses a computation equation;
replacement inside a recursive body is justified by equation
induction comparing the two recursive values.

Conversely, the logical rules are Lemma~\ref{lem:logic},
coverage is \eqref{eq:coverage}, and equation induction is
Corollary~\ref{cor:induction}. Computation equations normalize
to reflexive equations, and separation is the native explosion
principle. Each logical abbreviation has its claimed meaning.
For a native paired binder, the expansion of
$\exists x(B(x,0)\to\forall yB(x,y))$ implies $\DD B$ by
case introduction using the expanded witness and closure;
the reverse implication opens $\DD B$ and reconstructs that
existential. Induction on the matrix gives the native formula
round trip; induction on ordinary formula syntax gives the
ordinary one. The same argument applies to clauses.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The arithmetic interpretations}
Interpret $R(n,a;i,u.h)$ in iPRA by the ordinary primitive
recursion whose base is $Sa$ and whose step is $h(i,u)$.
The domain formula is $x=x$ and equality is unchanged.
Its computation equations, coverage, separation and equation
induction are iPRA theorems.

In the reverse direction, translate each finite PR description
using the direct compiler in Lemma~\ref{lem:J}. The PR defining
equations hold by computation. Section~\ref{sec:qfi} supplies
the successor axioms and all quantifier-free induction instances;
the logical part is unchanged in the ordinary realization.

\begin{lemma}[Internal uniqueness]\label{lem:uniqueness}
Two fixed recursions with provably equal bases and pointwise
equal updates are internally equal at every counter.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use literal-equation induction on the equality of their values,
with all passive parameters fixed. The base uses the given
base equality. At a successor, the induction equality and
congruence identify the state arguments, and the assumed
update equality identifies the new values. The same proof
applies to $J$, $R$, and ordinary primitive recursion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The two translations just specified are interpretations.
Induct externally on a fixed finite numerical description.
Its double translation satisfies its original defining
equations, using the external induction hypothesis for its
earlier descriptions. Lemma~\ref{lem:uniqueness} proves that
it equals the original operation at every internal argument.
This is not merely agreement on standard numerals.

Both domains are the whole numerical domain, so $x=y$ is an
internally total bijection preserving each interpreted function
and actual equality. Formula composites follow from numerical
uniqueness, Lemma~\ref{lem:adequacy}, and external induction on
formula syntax. Ordinary falsity maps to $0=\one$, which the
ordinary theory proves equivalent to falsity by separation
and explosion. Consequently a finite contradiction proof in
either presentation translates into one in the other. This
gives equiconsistency, with no assertion that either theory
proves its own consistency.
\end{proof}

\section{The standalone certificate checker}\label{sec:checker}

The public input is a JSON object with exactly the two keys
\texttt{goal} and \texttt{proof}. The goal is a closed canonical
Eq/$\DD$ formula. Acceptance means exit status zero and exactly
\texttt{ACCEPT} on standard output. Exceptions reject, and no
trust decision uses Python \texttt{assert}.

At context length $n$, the available free parameters are
$-1,\ldots,-(n-1)$ and the next fresh parameter is $-n$.
The initial context length is one because of the reflexivity
clause. A proof packet is one of:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{['u',j,t,q]}: use clause $j$ at $t$; $q$ proves
its antecedent if it has one. An unconditional clause has no
premise, and its unused payload is conventionally \texttt{null}.
\item \texttt{['i',B,t,r]}: introduce $\DD B$ at witness $t$;
$r$ is the branch proof under the automatically selected assumption.
\item \texttt{['e',q,e,r]}: $q$ proves the paired major,
$e$ proves $b=t$ in its opened context, and $r$ proves $F$ there.
The rule synthesizes the export $\NF(F[t/b])$.
\end{itemize}

The sole unconditional axiom is encoded by the initial clause
\texttt{([], [1,1])}. Since equations and recursion terms have
different arities, one recursive walker can validate and rebuild
both, adding two binder slots only in the final child of a unary
formula node or a ternary numerical node. This shared binding
rule is the specific syntax simplification.

\begin{theorem}[Checker correspondence]\label{thm:checker}
For a closed canonical goal $A$, the standalone $\RN$ checker
accepts a finite certificate exactly when that certificate
describes a derivation of $A$ using the three specified families.
Every finite $\RN$ derivation has an accepted certificate under
the usual unbounded finite-storage idealization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on active proof packets. The synthesized formula of each
recursive call has been checked for grammar, canonicality, and
scope. In Use, its term is checked before instantiation and any
antecedent proof must synthesize exactly the retained antecedent.
In Close, the supplied matrix and its witness are checked,
the generated local assumption is exactly the rule's selected
row, and the branch result must equal the generated successor
row. In Name the major must be unary, the equality must be
binary with left endpoint exactly the freshly allocated
parameter, and the final scope check excludes that parameter
from the normalized export. Lemma~\ref{lem:norm} and the
counter-first binding convention identify the computations with
the stipulated substitutions.

Conversely encode a finite derivation recursively. Use its rule
data and induction certificates for its premises. The canonical
and freshness side conditions make every check pass. There is
no trusted proof producer, theorem table, or omitted validator.
The same construction with a budget exceeding the finite run
cost gives an accepted fuel-bounded certificate.
\end{proof}

The counted source and a commented explanation are included in the
archive. The minimal proof is
\begin{lstlisting}
{"goal":[0,0],"proof":["u",0,0,null]}
\end{lstlisting}
which derives $0=0$ from the fixed clause.

\section{A separate smaller strict native checker}\label{sec:fusion}

This section concerns the current native scalar codec, not $\RN$.
Its baseline is the incoming \texttt{deterministic\_opening}
native public-language checker. The following argument proves
an exact implementation refinement without changing any axiom,
rule, numerical interpretation, or certificate.

The existing arity walker increases its depth by
$5-\operatorname{arity}(a)$ at every list node. If a formula
position has $p$ protected numerical slots, its depth is $2p$;
a term position has depth $3+2p$. Thus even depth means formula
and odd depth means term. Public list arities are exactly
\[
                   1\quad\hbox{or}\quad 2+(d\bmod2).
\]
At a numerical leaf the canonical public integer is either zero
or an odd integer at least three. The existing interval test
checks its scope. The list cases respectively mean paired
binder/equation or successor/iterator, with the codec's existing
protected-slot conventions unchanged.

Enable this grammar predicate only for the initial no-image
scope traversal, then disable it before reading any proof
instruction. Structural induction on the public tree proves
that the fused traversal succeeds exactly when the old grammar
test and old scope traversal both succeed, and returns the
same tree. Once the flag is off, the old walker and every
subsequent kernel transition are unchanged. Therefore the
entire certificate translation in both directions is the
identity, including administrative payload aliases and unused
prefixes. Resource-exhaustion thresholds and failure diagnostics
are not equated.

The selected version states its added predicate explicitly.
The smaller optional variant integrates it into the existing
cases and uses immediate failure of the absent-image operation
for malformed values. Its rejection does not rely on exhausting
the Python recursion limit. Both are supplied and audited;
the clearer version is the recommended strict-native implementation.

Traversal fusion already occurs conceptually in earlier
\texttt{scope\_kernel}, \texttt{validated\_core}, and related
packages. The new claim is the specific measured refinement
of the latest native checker, not priority for the idea or a
new mathematical axiomatization.

\section{Executed evidence, reproduction, and limits}\label{sec:validation}

\input{evidence/validation_summary.tex}

The main suite checks actual theorem certificates, malformed and
unsound-input attacks, binding coherence, independent arithmetic
semantics, and the bounded checker. The syntax audit for the
separate native refinement uses an independently written decoder
whose state is the number of protected slots, not the kernel's
affine depth expression. Finite testing supports the implementations;
the preceding proofs supply the universal claims.

Run \texttt{python3 reproduce.py} from the extracted directory to
regenerate the validation and whole-program measurements. Build
the paper with \texttt{python3 build\_pdf.py}; a normal TeX Live
installation is required only for the PDF. The acceptance modules
use only Python's standard library and make no network calls.

\paragraph{What is new.}
The numerical grammar and the three-family discharge presentation
are composed with one common two-variable binding convention,
and the recursor-specific selector and direct PR compiler prove
that the composition still has the required strength. The full
normalizer, its capture-avoiding implementation, and the matched
size comparison are part of that result. The separate native
validator refinement establishes a larger source-size saving
for an unchanged theory.

\paragraph{What is inherited.}
The paired-binder reading, witness-selected introduction,
three-family Name interface, two-row transport construction,
compound-envelope repair, logical gate constructions, and the
equation-reflection argument come from the inspected research
series. Counter-aware successor-seeded recursion and uniform
binding also occur separately in that series. They are credited
where used. This work claims a reduction by composition and
implementation, not independent discovery of those ingredients.

\paragraph{What remains unproved.}
There is no global lower bound for a complete iPRA checker, no
absolute simplest-axiomatization theorem, and no new Lean or
Rocq verification of the delivered programs. The transparent
arithmetic construction does not beat the compact codec in
bytes. Normalization is fully counted, but concrete Python
resource limits can still reject a valid very large proof.
These limitations do not change the stated whole-domain
bi-interpretation theorem.
