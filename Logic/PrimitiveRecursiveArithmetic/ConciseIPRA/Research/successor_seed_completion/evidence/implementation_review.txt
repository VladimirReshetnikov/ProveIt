INDEPENDENT ASSISTANT IMPLEMENTATION REVIEW
=========================================

Scope
-----
Reviewed theory/implementation.tex against the complete byte checker and
AST checker present in this package. This is a mathematical/source audit,
not formal verification or external peer review. The audit covers finite
successful executions and the canonical completeness construction.

Result
------
No mathematical source-correspondence error was found. The changed J0
base, its zero-instance supply argument, the truthy-zero witness change,
and the AST profile's local iterable aliases are mutually consistent.

1. Successor-seeded base generation
----------------------------------
The actual generator is [source,[initializer]], not [source,initializer].
Its left initializer is decoded under the J node's two dummy zero slots.
The right initializer is decoded under the successor node's one dummy
zero slot. Internal binder slots are preserved; old passive parameters
are rigid negative names. Coordinates exposed by the one-slot difference
are replaced by the newly allocated w. After substituting w=0 they agree
with the dummy zeros on the left. Thus the equation's zero instance is
J0: J(0,a;h)=S(a).

A diagnostic example is source [0,3,0] at initial floor zero. The generated
opened equation is [[0,3,0],[-1]]. Its ordinary meaning is 1=S(w), because
the source's coordinate 3 is a dummy zero while the right coordinate is
exposed to w. This equation need not hold for every w. At w=0 it is 1=1,
exactly the required known-instance supply. No universal assertion of the
fresh equation is used. The original goal and retained context exclude w;
substitution followed by cut discharges the generated assumption.

The zero-extension claim is applied to these actual codec positions: all
passive parameters outside a term's internal binders are old rigid names.
It should not be read as permission to shift arbitrary meaningful positive
outer coordinates without renaming them.

2. Successor computation and the eager inner check
-------------------------------------------------
A successful true-counter computation has a unary counter [p], or the AST
profile's documented unary alias. The eager numerical opening of
[*counter,initializer,update] validates the predecessor J term before it
is substituted into the update. It checks arity, scope and update
annotations even if the update ignores its state.

This check also prevents coordinates exposed in the update from being
mistaken for state aliases. The source successor has one additional dummy
zero slot compared with the checked predecessor. Every surviving meaningful
coordinate is therefore aligned correctly. The resulting computation is
JS, or its zero-instance form; no predecessor operation is presumed by
the checker.

3. Logical dispatch and fresh parameters
----------------------------------------
The truthy-major branch always checks its major first. A matched endpoint
request gives transport or Q elimination; a mismatch supplies truth and
keeps the goal. The ternary major's slice gives the opened Q clause of D
elimination. Scalars cannot be finite successful major goals in the strict
profile; malformed major arities reject.

DI is selected only after the current goal has been opened/validated.
The witness image is checked at numerical root when used. The shared
fresh successor and the matrix-arity/marker test give exactly case DI or
literal-equation progress DI. Zero-arity truth matrices represent the
literal equation 0=0; compound Q/D matrices cannot receive progress.

The initial goal has no meaningful future numerical parameter, and all
stored assumptions have already been opened. A major proof can allocate
additional names, but its checked conclusion cannot export one of its
later eigenparameters. The next shared continuation allocates a genuinely
fresh name below that enlarged floor.

4. The changed zero witness
--------------------------
[0,0,0] now denotes J(0,0;0)=1. It must not be used as an essential
zero-valued DI witness. The replacement J(1,0;0) denotes zero by JS and
has a truthy array representation. For completeness, an original zero
witness is replaced by this term and its equality to zero is transported
through the premise. This computation proof needs no DI. The package
includes both a nonvacuous successful use of the new witness and rejection
of the old replacement.

5. Additional AST aliases
-------------------------
For a successful used opening, the additional iterable normalizations are
  empty string -> [],
  empty dictionary -> [],
  dictionary with its sole key equal to the empty string -> [[]].
The last dictionary's value is ignored. Nonempty strings recur on a
nonempty character and cannot complete a finite successful opening;
nonempty dictionary keys therefore cannot survive a used opening either.

For the accepted aliases, scope opening commutes with normalization,
lengths and truthiness agree, and any positive raw equality match implies
a corresponding normalized equality. A dictionary major cannot finish
m[2:]. A sole-empty-key dictionary used as a successor counter expands to
the empty-string predecessor, which normalizes to zero before the eager
predecessor check. This is the intended J(1,a;h) computation.

The claim is local to inspected syntax and successful openings. Raw
instruction packets can contain ignored fields, and unread prefixes are
inert; no wholesale normalization of all such data is required.

The finite-success correspondence statement is correct. With an ideal
unbounded call stack, nonempty strings need not terminate in the direct
iterable opener. Actual Python recursion limits cause rejection, and
canonical arrays/integers have structural termination. The report does
not claim an ideal total decider for every malformed JSON value.

6. Completeness boundary
------------------------
Every mathematical native formula can be represented canonically with
arrays and integers. Completeness concerns existence of a canonical
certificate, not acceptance of every raw encoding of an equivalent
formula. Computation leaves become constant supply instances followed
by Hyp. Eigenparameter allocation includes arithmetic and neutral
continuations. The example producer is correctly described as partial
validation infrastructure rather than a complete verified compiler.
