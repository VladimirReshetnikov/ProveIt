\begin{abstract}
The current ConciseIPRA directory contains a highly compressed, formally
studied presentation of full first-order intuitionistic primitive recursive
arithmetic. Its six incoming research archives must also be included in the
comparison: they already attain 257 significant tokens, 279 AST nodes, and
522 normalized Python bytes, in different complete programs.

This investigation obtains a smaller checked presentation by composing two
existing ideas: the latest guarded-source checker and the scalar neutral
resource established in the reversal package. The resulting byte profile
has 521 normalized bytes, 263 significant tokens, 300 AST nodes, and 327
gzip-9 bytes. A separate profile has 278 AST nodes. The 257-token frontier
is unchanged. The mathematical public theory and its bi-interpretation
with iPRA are inherited without alteration.

\textbf{The improvement is implementation-level. A new, simpler
mathematical axiomatization was not obtained.} No absolute minimum or
significant arithmetic breakthrough is claimed. The report specifies the
theory, the inherited equivalence boundary, the exact code change, and 258
successful process expectations. It also proves a sharp criterion for when
conditional and progressive equation closures coincide, explaining the
limitation of several tempting rule deletions. The archive includes the
full inherited mathematical report, exact comparison sources, executable
checkers, certificates, measurements, and reproduction commands.
\end{abstract}

\section{The target theory and the inherited presentation}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Meaning of iPRA}

The target is first-order intuitionistic logic with equality, one numerical
sort, zero, successor, and one function symbol for each fixed primitive
recursive definition. Such definitions are generated from zero, successor,
projections, composition, and primitive recursion. The defining equations
are included for each definition. For example, a primitive recursion
description introduces a symbol $f$ with
\[
 f(0,\vec a)=g(\vec a),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec a)=h(n,f(n,\vec a),\vec a).
\]
The nonlogical axioms also include successor injectivity,
$Sx\ne0$, and induction for quantifier-free formulas, with numerical
parameters. In particular the induction schema is
\[
 A(0,\vec a)\wedge
 \forall n\bigl(A(n,\vec a)\to A(Sn,\vec a)\bigr)
 \ \to\ \forall n\,A(n,\vec a),
\]
where $A$ is quantifier-free. There is no induction over arbitrary
first-order formulas. This is the convention used by the repository's
deeply embedded \file{PR} theory and its equivalence theorems.

\subsection{Numerical terms}

The presented calculus uses the scalar numerical syntax
\[
 t::=x\mid0\mid St\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]
Only $u$ is bound in $h$. The counter $n$ and initializer $a$ are outside
that binder; $h$ can contain fixed parameters. Bound variables are
renamed to avoid capture. Set $1=S0$.

There are exactly two computation schemas, in every retained context:
\begin{align}
 I(0,a;u.h)&=a,\tag{I0}\label{eq:i0}\\
 I(Sn,a;u.h)&=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u].\tag{IS}\label{eq:is}
\end{align}
Each finite iterator description denotes a primitive recursive function.
No numerical variable ranges over descriptions and no uniform evaluator
for all primitive recursive functions is assumed.

\subsection{Public formulas and auxiliary proof formulas}

Public formulas are equations and paired formulas:
\[
 A::=(s=t)\mid\D_{x,y}B.
\]
At an atomic matrix beneath $\D$, the canonical public grammar requires
\begin{equation}
 B(x,y)\equiv I(y,0;u.h)=I(y,0;u.k).
 \tag{Guard}\label{eq:guard}
\end{equation}
The update terms may have parameters, including $x,y$, as well as their
state variable. Compound matrices satisfy the same grammar recursively.
An equation at the top level is unrestricted.

Proofs may additionally use auxiliary formulas
\[
 F::=(s=t)\mid\Q_yF\mid\D_{x,y}F
\]
with unrestricted atomic matrices. Thus the public formula language is
smaller than the auxiliary proof language. This boundary is part of the
presentation. The full auxiliary language is not asserted to have the
same formula round trips as the public theory.

The ordinary public interpretation keeps numerical equality and uses
\begin{equation}
 (\D_{x,y}B)^\circ
   :=\exists x\bigl(B^\circ(x,0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(x,y)\bigr).
 \tag{D}\label{eq:publicD}
\end{equation}
The designated contradiction is $0=1$. An administrative scalar zero
used by the checker is \emph{not} this contradiction: the contradiction
is a binary equation node.

\subsection{Every primitive inference pattern}

Contexts are finite reusable lists of auxiliary formulas. Besides
\eqref{eq:i0} and \eqref{eq:is}, the calculus has the following four
patterns. They are inherited from guarded-source \cite{guardedsource}.

\paragraph{Hypothesis.}
\[
 \frac{}{\Gamma\vdash F}\quad(F\in\Gamma).
 \tag{Hyp}
\]

\paragraph{Elimination selected by a numerical source.}
For a fixed auxiliary template $F(v)$ set
\[
 M(s,t;F)=
 \begin{cases}
   \Q_vF(v),&s\text{ is literally }0,\\
   s=t,&s\text{ is not literally }0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash M(s,t;F)\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
      {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}.
 \tag{SE}\label{eq:se}
\]
The source restriction is syntactic. A variable or an iterator that
denotes zero still belongs to the second branch.

\paragraph{Paired elimination.}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
       \Gamma,\Q_yB(b,y)\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash A}.
 \tag{DE}\label{eq:de}
\]
Here $b$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, $A$, and the passive parameters of $B$.
The displayed local assumption is discharged.

\paragraph{Deterministic paired introduction.}
Put $\eta_B(z)=z$ for a literal equation $B$, and $\eta_B(z)=0$
for a compound $B$. Then
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta_B(z))\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)}.
 \tag{DI}\label{eq:di}
\]
The parameter $z$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, $t$, and the passive parameters
of $B$. The local assumption is discharged. The rule has no independent
case/progress certificate flag: the matrix root fixes the choice.

There are no further primitive logical, equality, successor, coverage,
or induction axioms. Weakening, assumption cut, and fresh renaming are
admissible. The presentation is the consequence relation of this auxiliary
calculus on public contexts and conclusions. The neutral-resource code
change adds no mathematical rule to this inventory.

\section{Why the inherited theory has the required strength}
\label{sec:equivalence}

The complete inherited proof is supplied as both
\file{reference/guarded_source_report.pdf} and its source. It includes the
earlier scalar arithmetic, logical, and coding arguments with attribution.
This section identifies the dependency precisely and gives the short
parts relevant to the present change. Reusing an established equivalence
is different from claiming to have newly formalized it.

\subsection{Soundness, including auxiliary induction}

Use the following auxiliary interpretation into iPRA:
\begin{align*}
 (s=t)^*&=(s=t),\\
 (\Q_yB)^*&=B^*(0)\to\forall y\,B^*(y),\\
 (\D_{x,y}B)^*&=
 \begin{cases}
 \exists x\,\forall y\,(B(x,y)\to B(x,Sy)),&B\text{ an equation},\\
 \exists x\,(B^*(x,0)\to\forall y\,B^*(x,y)),&B\text{ compound}.
 \end{cases}
\end{align*}
Numerical substitution preserves a matrix's root constructor. Therefore
the interpretation commutes with numerical substitution.

The computation schemas, hypothesis, and both branches of SE are sound.
Atomic DI proves the indicated progressive statement directly. Compound
DI is sound by zero-or-successor coverage. In atomic DE, equation induction
turns progress into the conditional closure exported by $\Q$. Compound
DE is ordinary existential elimination. These observations give soundness
by induction on the finite derivation.

For a guarded atomic $E(x,y)$, computation proves $E(x,0)$. Consequently
the progressive and conditional readings agree, by equation induction in
one direction and the true zero instance in the other. Structural
induction on public formulas proves
\[
 \ipra\vdash A^*\leftrightarrow A^\circ.
\]
This agreement is needed only for the public context and conclusion.

\subsection{The completeness bootstrap}

Auxiliary atomic DI, DE, and the zero branch of SE already derive
contextual equation induction:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash E(0)\qquad\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash E(r)}.
\]
To see this, introduce $\D_{x,y}E(y)$ using an unused witness coordinate
$x$, eliminate it to obtain $\Q_yE(y)$, and apply the base $E(0)$ at $r$.
No equality repair or ordinary logical macro is needed for this step.

The inherited equational dependency theorem then restores transport
whose source is literal zero. Its proof uses equation induction,
computation, and transport with permitted sources. In particular it does
not presuppose unrestricted substitution into a restricted derivation.
After numerical equality is restored, an external induction on public
templates restores transport through public paired formulas.

The guarded padding construction consequently recovers the previously
established native scalar calculus on public formulas. That calculus
reconstructs all intuitionistic first-order connectives, numerical
coverage, separation, and quantifier-free induction. The scalar bridge
constructs a predecessor, reflection of equality into terms, pairing, and
a compiler for each primitive recursive definition. These inherited
steps yield the reverse interpretation of ordinary iPRA.

\begin{theorem}[Inherited equivalence, transferred unchanged]
The public calculus of Section~\ref{sec:theory} is bi-interpretable and
equiconsistent with full first-order iPRA. Both numerical domains are
whole, numerical equality is actual equality, and both numerical
composites are identified with the identity by the graph $x=y$.
Changing the administrative neutral encoding from an empty list to scalar
zero preserves this public theory and all these conclusions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the first assertion, use the public consequence and formula-composite
theorems of the inherited guarded-source report, composed with the native
scalar/iPRA bridge. In the scalar-to-iPRA direction, translate each fixed
iterator by its primitive recursive definition. In the reverse direction,
compile primitive recursive definitions into scalar terms using the
inherited pairing and recursion construction, and translate the ordinary
logical connectives by the native and guarded macros.

The term round trips are proved from computation and equation induction;
the formula round trips are the stated inherited logical composites.
Neither direction restricts the numerical domain or replaces equality by
a quotient relation. The contradiction is $0=1$ in the scalar presentation
and maps to the usual arithmetic contradiction. Hence the interpretations
also give equiconsistency.

The final assertion is proved by the neutral-resource invariant in
Section~\ref{sec:neutral}. Public terms, formulas, and inference patterns
are unchanged. Thus the interpretation maps and their composite proofs
are unchanged as well.
\end{proof}

The existing Lean development proves the native scalar/iPRA bridge.
The incoming guarded-source extension is a written mathematical proof;
this work has not supplied a new Lean or Rocq verification of it. The
selected Lean files in the archive are references, not a complete build
environment. The existing Lean project was not rebuilt during this work.

\section{The neutral-resource reduction}
\label{sec:neutral}

\subsection{Certificate interface}

Each compact checker reads a finite JSON array. Its final item is the
goal; preceding items are instruction packets consumed from right to
left. A used packet has four fields
\[
                [m,s,u,t].
\]
The major premise $m$ is recursively checked before it can supply an
assumption. An empty major in the old local convention is a neutral
truth resource used by computation and introduction packets. The new
convention spells that resource as the scalar integer zero.

Formula arities 1, 2, and 3 encode $\D$, equality, and a $\Q$ resource.
A canonical $\Q$ resource is a triple of identical matrix encodings.
Canonical numerical zero is also scalar zero, but occurs in the numerical
sort, not at formula depth. The arity-aware traversal distinguishes these
roles. A unary numerical array encodes successor, and a ternary numerical
array encodes the iterator. Protected binding slots and local eigenparameter
floors implement capture-avoiding opening and freshness.

The compact contract takes a canonical public statement as its proposed
conclusion. The checkers can accept additional auxiliary goals. The strict
profile explicitly checks the public goal grammar and its scope. It does
not additionally impose exact integer/list typing on every unused value
in the whole input. That stronger interface is a distinct contract.

\begin{proposition}[Neutral resource replacement]
The scalar-neutral variants are sound for the same public theory and
complete under the same canonical-public-statement convention as their
empty-list-neutral predecessors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At formula depth, administrative scalar zero denotes truth. Replacing
the initially retained administrative truth by this spelling preserves
the successful-trace soundness invariant: each retained item is a decoded
assumption, a computation theorem, or administrative truth. The arithmetic
supplies, matching of elimination majors, local floors, and deterministic
introduction calculation are otherwise identical.

For completeness, use the inherited well-sorted producer and emit each
neutral major as zero. It closes against the new initial truth. Both
zero and the old empty list are falsy, so the ensuing computation or
introduction branch is unchanged. The non-neutral majors, public formulas,
numerical terms, and witness/template fields are unchanged. Thus every
public theorem still has a certificate.

This is the neutral-resource argument already established in the reversal
package, applied to the guarded-source kernel. It does not identify the
sets of all accepted raw JSON strings. In particular, stale certificates
with the old neutral major may be rejected.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The actual conversion is deliberately narrow}

The supplied untrusted converter changes only a four-field command's
major-premise field when that field is exactly \file{[]}:
\begin{lstlisting}
if isinstance(command, list) and len(command) == 4 and command[0] == []:
    command[0] = 0
\end{lstlisting}
It does not replace every empty list recursively. In particular a falsy
introduction-mode field is preserved, as are all numerical terms and
public formulas. The audit checks every positive frozen producer packet
for unexpected empty-list positions. The conversion claim concerns this
well-sorted certificate interface, not arbitrary malformed input.

The following is the entire trusted program, including input and verdict.
It imports only the standard JSON library. The normalized module has 521
UTF-8 bytes; presentation line wrapping below does not change that file.

\section{An exact criterion for conditional and progressive closure}
\label{sec:criterion}

This section isolates a mathematical fact useful for further reductions.
It is proved here; no literature-wide priority claim is made. Its role is
to identify what a proposed replacement of case introduction by progress
must actually preserve.

Let $E(y,\vec a)$ be a literal equation, with the parameters $\vec a$
fixed. Write
\[
 B:=E(0,\vec a),\qquad
 C(E):=B\to\forall y\,E(y,\vec a),\qquad
 P(E):=\forall y\bigl(E(y,\vec a)\to E(Sy,\vec a)\bigr).
\]
All following proofs are intuitionistic. Only equation induction is used.

\begin{lemma}[Anchored progress]
In iPRA,
\[
 P(E)\to C(E),\qquad
 C(E)\leftrightarrow\bigl(B\to P(E)\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume $P(E)$ and $B$. Equation induction on $E$ gives all its instances,
proving the first implication. For the second assertion, $C(E)$ together
with $B$ yields $\forall y\,E(y)$, which immediately gives $P(E)$.
Conversely, assume $B\to P(E)$ and then $B$. Obtain $P(E)$ and apply the
first implication with the retained $B$. Discharge $B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp agreement criterion]
Suppose the base $B=E(0,\vec a)$ is decidable. Then
\begin{equation}
 \bigl(C(E)\leftrightarrow P(E)\bigr)
 \quad\leftrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(\neg B\to P(E)\bigr).
 \label{eq:criterion}
\end{equation}
The hypothesis holds for equations in iPRA. More generally the result
holds for any predicate with decidable base for which the indicated
induction instance is available.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First suppose $C(E)\leftrightarrow P(E)$ and $\neg B$. From a hypothetical
$B$ one obtains contradiction, and hence every $E(y)$ by explosion.
Thus $C(E)$ holds; the assumed equivalence gives $P(E)$.

Conversely suppose $\neg B\to P(E)$. The implication $P(E)\to C(E)$
has already been proved. To prove $C(E)\to P(E)$, assume $C(E)$ and
split by the decidability $B\vee\neg B$. In the $B$ branch, $C(E)$ gives
all instances of $E$ and hence $P(E)$. In the $\neg B$ branch use the
assumed implication. Discharge $C(E)$.

The only induction used is the literal-equation instance in the previous
lemma. Decidability is used at one explicit split, not as unrestricted
excluded middle.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Consequences and examples}

The syntactic guard \eqref{eq:guard} makes $B$ a theorem, so it satisfies
\eqref{eq:criterion}. A true zero instance is sufficient, but not
necessary: a predicate already closed under successor also satisfies it.
For example $E(y)\equiv(0=1)$ is false independently of $y$. Both $C(E)$
and $P(E)$ are provable intuitionistically. The criterion therefore
describes a strictly broader semantic family than true-base guards.

The tempting unrestricted replacement fails at
\[
                         E(y)\equiv(y=1).
\]
Its base is false, so iPRA proves $C(E)$. Its progressive statement would
take $E(1)$ to $E(2)$, contradicting $2\ne1$. Thus iPRA proves
$\neg P(E)$. This is a separation inside arithmetic, not merely a
finite-model suggestion. The same obstruction is already recognized in
the inherited guarded work; the contribution here is the exact criterion.

\subsection{Existential witnesses require a separate qualification}

For $E(x,y)$ define $C_x$ and $P_x$ with $x$ fixed. Pointwise agreement
$\forall x(C_x\leftrightarrow P_x)$ implies
\[
                    \exists x\,C_x\leftrightarrow\exists x\,P_x.
\]
The converse need not hold. Different existential witnesses can conceal
a pointwise failure.

For a concrete arithmetical example, let
$C_{\mathrm{sel}}(x,a,b):=I(x,a;u.b)$, with $u$ unused. It is $a$ at
$x=0$ and $b$ at successors. Set
\[
              E(x,y)\equiv C_{\mathrm{sel}}(x,1,y)=1.
\]
At $x=0$ this equation is always true, so both existential closures are
provable with witness zero. At $x=1$ it reduces to $y=1$, where the
pointwise readings differ. Consequently the pointwise criterion must not
be advertised as a necessary condition for agreement after existential
quantification. The correct existential analogue permits changing the
witness:

\begin{theorem}[Exact criterion after existential binding]
Let $D_C:=\exists x\,C_x$, $D_P:=\exists x\,P_x$, and
$F:=\exists x\,\neg E(x,0)$. Then iPRA proves
\[
              (D_C\leftrightarrow D_P)
              \quad\leftrightarrow\quad(F\to D_P).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation induction gives $D_P\to D_C$. If $D_C\to D_P$ and $F$
hold, open a witness to the false base. Its conditional closure follows
by explosion, so $D_C$ holds and hence $D_P$.

Conversely assume $F\to D_P$, and open a witness $a$ to $D_C$.
Decide the equation $E(a,0)$. If it holds, its conditional closure gives
every $E(a,y)$, and hence $P_a$ and $D_P$. If it fails, $a$ witnesses
$F$ and the assumed implication gives $D_P$, possibly with a different
witness. Discharge the opened witness. No quantified formula is subjected
to excluded middle.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A base-retaining normalization}

There is a useful exact normalization which also explains why a semantic
improvement need not shorten a checker. With $B=E(0)$, put
\[
                         A(y):=B\wedge E(y).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
             C(A)\ \leftrightarrow\ P(A)\ \leftrightarrow\ C(E).
             \label{eq:base-normalization}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $A(0)\leftrightarrow B$, and its conditional closure is
equivalent to $B\to\forall y\,E(y)$. If $C(E)$ and $A(y)$ hold,
the retained $B$ gives $E(Sy)$, hence $A(Sy)$; thus $P(A)$ follows.
Conversely, under $B$, $P(A)$ gives the progress step for $E$; equation
induction yields every $E(y)$. This proves \eqref{eq:base-normalization}
without needing a decision of $B$ in this particular argument.

The normalized predicate can again be made a literal equation. If
$E(y)$ is $s(y)=t(y)$ and $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is a proved
injective primitive recursive pairing function, use
\[
 \widehat E(y):=
 \langle s(0),s(y)\rangle=\langle t(0),t(y)\rangle.
\]
Injectivity makes this equivalent to $A(y)$. Thus the normalization is
available within iPRA and, after its arithmetic bridge, within the scalar
theory. Its zero instance need not be true. If it is false, every instance
is false, which makes progress vacuous.

This is a proved normal form, not a new measured kernel. Using it in a
primitive presentation would require deriving pairing and its injectivity
without a circular appeal to the sought completeness theorem, and charging
the term expansion or the new constructor and its axioms. Also, the direct
rule retaining both $E(0)$ and $E(z)$ is already the older anchored
introduction from which the repository's Choice-DI system was derived.
That rule is not a new result of this investigation.

\subsection{Why the criterion does not itself produce a smaller checker}

Checking the semantic condition $\neg E(0)\to P(E)$ would require a proof
or an adequately specified syntactic class. It cannot be silently
treated as a constant-cost parser test. The existing true-base guards
give a simple checkable sufficient condition. Replacing them by the full
semantic criterion without counting proof certificates and their
verification would hide complexity outside the trusted program.

For supplementary verification, \file{closure_criterion.py} checks all
8190 Boolean sequences of lengths 1 through 12, treating progress as
closure along each finite sequence. The code checks $P\to C$,
$C\leftrightarrow(B\to P)$, and
$(C\leftrightarrow P)\leftrightarrow(\neg B\to P)$, and records the
false--true--false counterexample. It does not test the existential theorem
or the pairing normalization. These finite checks do not replace the
intuitionistic proofs above.

\section{Further research questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A genuine arithmetic reduction.}
Can a numerical constructor or binding operation be removed while the
entire equivalence proof and complete checker both become simpler?
The existing successor-seeded, self-seeded, and register presentations
should be comparison points. An initialized iterator compiler cannot be
assumed before the necessary predecessor and selector have been derived.

\item \textbf{A better checkable class of matrices.}
Can the condition in \eqref{eq:criterion} be realized by a syntax simpler
to validate than the current guarded grammar? A candidate must include
both the proof of full public expressiveness and the cost of checking its
grammar. Semantic agreement alone is not a small axiom recognizer.

\item \textbf{Unrestricted ordinary formula readings.}
Can deterministic introduction coexist with the full original Eq/$Q$/$D$
language and its ordinary readings, with fewer complete-checker resources
than the existing witness-selected rule? The counterexample $y=1$ rules
out the direct progress-only replacement, not every possible redesign.

\item \textbf{Scope represented by proof binders.}
A tree certificate could allocate opaque eigenparameters when entering
a binder, preventing raw input from naming them. This may simplify
freshness proofs. The additional tree opening and opaque-atom handling
must be counted; no size improvement for this architecture was verified
in this investigation.

\item \textbf{Mechanize the current best public/auxiliary boundary.}
The native bridge is already formalized. Formalizing the guarded-source
extension and its neutral-resource decoding would close the most relevant
verification gap without re-proving the entire arithmetic development.

\end{enumerate}
