\section{Complete executable checkers and measured scope}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{Canonical statements and administrative proof data}

The public mathematical language is precisely the canonical language
of Section~\ref{mir:section}.  The small programs use the repository's
arity encoding of that language.  The same raw arrays also provide
administrative resources for proof construction, including closure
clauses and harmless zero or truth aliases.  This is a specified decoding
contract; the compact profiles do not reject every noncanonical
end statement.  The separate \file{code/strict_checker.py} does validate
the public goal before checking its proof, and its complete cost is
reported separately.

Input is one JSON array.  The last item is the goal; preceding items
are instruction packets, consumed from right to left.  The sole
successful outcome is normal termination with exactly
\texttt{ACCEPT} and a newline.  An exception, a nonzero exit, a timeout,
or resource exhaustion is not acceptance.  Unused prefix data is
inert, as in the pinned protocol.  There is no imported producer,
normalizer, axiom database, or metatheory package in a checker.

The raw numerical and formula constructors are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cll}
\toprule
Array length & Numerical position & Formula position\\
\midrule
0 & zero & a fixed true equation\\
1 & successor & paired binder $D$\\
2 & zero annotation & equality\\
3 & iterator & administrative closure $Q$\\
$\ge4$ & zero annotation & truth annotation\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A canonical closure resource has three copies of its matrix.  A unary
major $[B]$ exports $[B,B,B]$ when its witness is opened; tripling any
other nonempty formula arity creates only a truth annotation.  In the
canonical numerical syntax, literal $0$ is the integer zero and
$S0$ is \texttt{[0]}.

Protected numerical slots have width two.  Numerical root depth is
3, formula root depth is 0, and traversal increases depth by
$5-\text{array length}$.  The coordinates $0,1,2$ denote zero;
the odd coordinate $3+2j$ canonically names protected slot $j$.
Negative coordinates name rigid parameters allowed by the current
freshness floor.  Successors introduce two dummy slots; an iterator
introduces one slot, dummy in its counter and initializer and genuine
state in its update.  The inherited sorted-opening and freshness
argument is retained in \file{reference/inherited_arity_checker.tex}.

The strict validator checks the mirrored initializer after raising
the left-hand numerical term across the iterator's additional slot.
Comparing the two raw initializer arrays directly would incorrectly
reject genuine bound-variable occurrences, including nonvacuous
witnesses.  Its goal traversal and raising routine are included in
the measured module.

\subsection{The two dispatch simplifications}

In a lexical-scope packet \texttt{[m,s,u,t]}, the first field is a
major premise or the neutral truth marker.  Source selection is
\begin{lstlisting}
m == ([s, u] if s else [t] * 3)
\end{lstlisting}
For a canonical source other than literal zero, the major must be the
equation $s=u$.  At a literal-zero source it must be the closure
resource with body $t$.  The theorem restoring zero-source transport
justifies this loss of a primitive equation-major alternative.

The retained local assumption for an introduction uses
\begin{lstlisting}
(m or not len(a) % 2) and k
\end{lstlisting}
instead of the preceding protocol's comparison with a certificate
mode field.  A meaningful even formula root is an equation and
receives the fresh progress parameter.  A meaningful odd root is
compound and receives zero.  The mirrored grammar and its
bi-interpretation justify deleting this independent choice.

Computation packets construct a zero or successor instance of the
iterator equations.  The packet format requires a nonempty
initializer and, for a successor computation, a nonempty predecessor.
These restrictions are discharged by the inherited certified zero
\[
                       c_0=I(2,1;u.0),\qquad c_0=0.
\]
Its equation is directly admitted.  Replace the offending literal-zero
positions of a desired computation by $c_0$, produce the admitted
equation, then transport using $c_0=0$.  This uses an iterator-headed
source and so remains available before zero-source restoration.
It changes the proof, not the literal zero or initializer occurring
in a public mirror matrix.

\subsection{Soundness, completeness, and the trusted boundary}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical checker theorem]
For each closed canonical $\Mcal$ formula $A$, there exists an
accepting finite certificate with encoded goal $A$ exactly when
$\Mcal\vdash A$, in the ideal unbounded execution model.
The strict checker additionally rejects noncanonical public goals.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For soundness, interpret the administrative formulas by the ordinary
anchored reading of the inherited native calculus: $Q_yB$ means
$B(0)\to\forall yB(y)$, and $D_{x,y}B$ means its existential closure.
An atomic progress introduction is sound for this reading by
literal-equation induction; a compound case introduction is sound
by numerical coverage.  Thus no progressive interpretation of a raw
unguarded binder, and no formula-composite claim for arbitrary raw
arrays, is needed.

Induct on a finite successful call tree.  The inherited opening
lemma verifies numerical substitution, dummy slots, and fresh
parameters.  The major is checked before its result contributes
any proof resource.  Source-selected transport and closure use are
instances of the inherited rules.  A unary major supplies its
correct clause; other exported arities are truth.  Constructed
computations are discharged by the inherited computation-supply
argument.  The root test admits progressive hypotheses only at
literal equations or truth annotations; all compound matrices receive
their zero instance.  These are sound inherited proof steps.  Hence
every successful canonical end statement has a proof of its ordinary
reading in iPRA.  The established faithfulness and round trips
translate that proof back to $\Mcal$.

For completeness, encode a finite $\Mcal$ derivation with fresh
integer parameters.  Use the inherited packets for clause use,
transport, and paired elimination.  Every permitted source is
nonempty in canonical encoding, so its equation major is selected.
Every atomic DI is progressive and every compound DI is by cases,
exactly as the root test requires.  Certified-zero expansion supplies
all computation instances.  This produces a finite accepting
certificate.  The strict validator accepts the stipulated goal
encoding, so it has the same canonical completeness.
\end{proof}

The program may accept an unguarded administrative goal whose ordinary
reading is provable.  It is not asserted to be complete for all such
goals, nor are they new object-language formulas of $\Mcal$.  The
strict profile makes this public boundary executable.  A comparison
requiring rejection of every out-of-language public input must use
the strict rows, not the compact rows alone.

The AST profile uses lexical scopes and an explicit loop; the byte
profile uses a recursive continuation with the same lexical scopes.
The token profile uses the inherited global supply of fresh parameter
names.  Certificates for the global profile are emitted separately.
All three are complete standalone programs rather than partial
fragments.  A readable independent expansion is also included.

\subsection{Executable evidence}

\input{evidence/validation_summary}

The central certificate tests the part most at risk of circularity:
recovering a zero-source equality consequence after the two primitive
restrictions have been imposed.  The named proof is audited for every
atomic introduction and every transport source before serialization. A separate audit also traverses every formula goal
and transport motive, including nested binders, rather than inspecting
only introduction nodes.
The accepted expansion uses only mirrored atomic matrices and no
primitive zero-source transport.  The direct forbidden transport
certificate is separately rejected.

Additional cases exercise both computation equations, padding,
shared-left implication, nonvacuous witnesses, compound case
introduction, induction with a used hypothesis, and public grammar
validation.  Negative controls cover context and eigenparameter
escape, malformed packets, false goals, and forbidden compound
progress.  These checks support the implementation claims; they
are not substitutes for the universal proofs in the preceding sections.
