\section{Assessment and limits}
\label{sec:assessment}

The improvement has three separate parts.  The atomic public guard
uses one iterator with its initializer repeated on the left.  The
introduction rule is determined by the matrix root.  Source-selected
elimination retains the previous source restriction.  The compatibility
proof, especially shared-left implication and the use of
$J(y)\to E(y)$ rather than $J(y)\to E(Sy)$ during the induction
bootstrap, is what permits the last two reductions to coexist in a
canonical theory with the required interpretations.

The earlier two-iterator guarded proposal already eliminated the
introduction choice.  The earlier source-dispatch proposal already
eliminated literal-zero transport sources.  Neither result alone
supplies the new compatibility theorem; the inert-hypothesis
obstruction explains the missing step.  The single-sided orientation,
its safe expansion argument, the substitution-free induction recovery,
and the compiled canonical bootstrap are the contributions of this
package.  The scalar arithmetic compiler, logical macros, arity codec,
certified-zero computation repair, and most of the zero-symmetry
construction are inherited and credited.

The measured compact frontier is strictly improved, but by a small
amount.  This is not a claim of an absolute simplest axiomatization:
without fixing a representation language, allowed encodings, and
trusted runtime, a global minimum is not even a stable comparison.
Nor does a smaller compact kernel show that a strict input validator
or the shortest explanatory proof is smaller.  The public grammar
restriction and the separate strict profile make those costs visible.

The numerical domain was deliberately retained.  A change to tuples,
functions, or another coding domain is not needed for this reduction;
keeping actual equality makes the two identity composites explicit.
No extra induction strength, reflection principle, or universal
evaluator was introduced.  The next substantive verification step
would be the Lean extension described in Section~\ref{sec:new-bridge},
whose missing status is stated rather than implied by the executable
Python tests.
