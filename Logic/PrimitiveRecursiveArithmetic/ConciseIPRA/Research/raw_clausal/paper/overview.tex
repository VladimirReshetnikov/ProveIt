\section{Result, scope, and the exact comparison}
\label{sec:overview}

The construction is a five-pattern calculus $\CE$ with the following
primitive inventory:
\[
\boxed{\text{Reflexivity, Eval, clause use, paired elimination, paired introduction.}}
\]
Its terms are the ordinary finite expressions
\[
 t ::= x\mid 0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,a;u.h),
\]
where the iterator binds $u$ only in $h$. Its formulas are
\[
 A ::= (s=t)\mid D_{x,y}A.
\]
All substitutions are ordinary capture-avoiding substitutions on raw
syntax. Computation is a derivable equation; it is not performed by the
term constructor or substitution operation.

The ordinary interpretation of the only logical constructor is
\[
 D_{x,y}B(x,y)\ :=\
 \exists x\bigl(B(x,0)\to\forall y\,B(x,y)\bigr).
\]
This is a presentation of full intuitionistic first-order logic coupled
to the intended arithmetic. The logical macros include implication,
conjunction, disjunction, both quantifiers, and falsity; these are given
explicitly in the inherited reference development. There is no use of
excluded middle for quantified formulas.

\subsection{Which current proposal is being improved?}

The repository index at the pinned snapshot lists fifty filed research
packages~\cite{baseline}. Its two incoming archives must also be considered.
In particular, the incoming clause presentation already has five
rules~\cite{clausal}. It uses a successor-seeded recursor whose zero case
is built into canonical syntax, together with one supplied equality
template. The present construction keeps the five-rule count while
returning to raw initialized iterators and removing that template.

The resulting tradeoff is explicit:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{.31\linewidth}p{.28\linewidth}p{.29\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Feature & Canonical clause basis & Present raw clause basis\\
\midrule
Primitive inference patterns & Five & Five\\
Formula constructors & Equality and $D$ & Equality and $D$\\
Arithmetic term constructors & $0$ and $R$ & $0$, $S$, and $I$\\
Canonicalization during substitution & Required & None\\
Equality template in a certificate & One term template & None\\
Primitive equality interface & Computation and transport & Reflexivity and Eval\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Variables are understood in both term inventories. Thus the simplification
does not reduce every syntactic coordinate: the raw language has an
independent successor constructor. Its benefit is a smaller complete
matched checker with a more direct substitution specification.

\subsection{Measured size, including all validation}

The comparison programs use the same clause representation, binder-opening
convention, closed-goal input contract, validation policy, error handling,
and normalization protocol. Every program is a standalone module.
Neither a proof compiler nor any imported project code is needed to run
one of these checkers.

The raw six-pattern control has the two computation equations and
one-term crossed transport in place of Reflexivity and Eval. The canonical
five-pattern control implements the current successor-seeded clause
basis using the same variable encoding and clause interface as the new
checker. These are the controlled comparisons. The unchanged upstream
canonical implementation is reported separately for orientation.

Metrics are taken after removing Python docstrings and applying
\code{ast.unparse}, followed by one line feed. Significant-token counts
exclude formatting, comments, and the end marker. AST nodes are counted
by \code{ast.walk}. Gzip uses compression level 9 and a zero timestamp;
it is supplementary information, not the optimization objective.
The reproduction script records the exact Python version.
For completeness, the readable new source is 3,456 bytes and its
gzip-9 file is 1,191 bytes; those figures include its explanatory
docstring and original formatting. The normalized measurements above
are the controlled source-complexity comparison.

The current compact lower envelope includes a 516-byte checker with a
different proof interface and highly compressed implementation
\cite{canonical}. This report does not beat that envelope. It establishes
a measured improvement in the transparent fully validated family, together
with the mathematical equivalence of the resulting presentation. It does
not prove that any particular source string, number of rules, or axiom
system is globally minimal.
For another reference point, the existing tagged native six-rule
computational-equality checker is 3,039 normalized bytes with an
open-sequent public interface. Its different representation and contract
are not the matched comparison used above; no claim to improve every
existing checker is intended.

\subsection{Attribution and the novelty boundary}

The two component ideas are inherited. Refl--Eval is the repository's
computational-equality interface~\cite{computational}; clause contexts and
the removal of first-class $Q$ are from the incoming clause
presentation~\cite{clausal}. The present contribution is their raw
five-pattern combination, the complete matched implementations, and their
explicit equivalence and verification package.

The novelty screen checked the current index, the claim summaries of all
fifty filed packages (using their root README when no claim summary was
present), both incoming reports, and the relevant full specifications.
This exact raw clause/Refl--Eval combination was not found there. That is
a scoped repository comparison, not a priority claim about the wider
literature or a file-by-file review of every archived artifact.

The separate successor-rigidity result in
Section~\ref{sec:successor-rigidity} is a further mathematical observation.
It is deliberately kept separate from the completed presentation: its
remaining equality-reflection bootstrap is not assumed anywhere in the
proof that $\CE$ is bi-interpretable with $\ipra$.
