\begin{abstract}
We give a further reduction of the current \emph{raw clausal} proposal
in ProveIt's ConciseIPRA collection. Terms remain raw scalar iterator
expressions; formulas are equations and one binary binding constructor
$\D$. Contexts contain reusable clauses. There are five inference
patterns: reflexivity, computational equality, clause use, paired
elimination, and paired introduction. Computational equality has two
cases, instead of the three in the existing clausal proposal.
The removed case is simulated by two zero-iteration steps through a
freshly constructed frame, with no induction or normalization.
Consequently every sequent is preserved, and a proof with $N$ inference
nodes and $k$ removed-case nodes translates to one with exactly $N+2k$
nodes. We spell out the theory, the reconstruction of equality and
logic, and both interpretation composites with full first-order iPRA.
The archive contains complete Python checkers, a proof translator,
reproducible measurements, and adversarial regression checks.
This is a verified composition of two reductions already present
separately in the repository, not a claim of an absolute minimum or a
new arithmetic-strength theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and comparison point}

The reference is the ConciseIPRA directory at commit
\begin{center}\small\texttt{5d85023b0688e03b75f0ec3a04bb43ef0bb51705}.\end{center}
It was inspected through GitHub on November 11, 2026, Pacific time~\cite{overview}.
Its adopted six-pattern calculus already has two-case computational
equality. Its \texttt{raw\_clausal} package removes the separate
$\Q$ constructor and hypothesis pattern, but retains a redundant third
case of evaluation. The proposal here combines these two reductions~\cite{clausal,frame}.
The comparison is to that exact snapshot, not to an earlier eight-rule
kernel. The relevant source paths are listed in Section~\ref{sec:sources}.

Let $\Cthree$ denote the existing raw clausal calculus and $\Ctwo$
the calculus below. The principal statement is
\[
       \Gamma\vdash_{\Cthree}F
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \Gamma\vdash_{\Ctwo}F
\]
for every finite retained clause context $\Gamma$ and every native
formula $F$. Terms, formulas, contexts, witnesses, and ordinary
capture-avoiding substitution are unchanged. One family of primitive
evaluation instances is deleted. In particular, this reduction does
not transfer computation into a normalizer or into an external
proof search procedure.

The checker comparison has two layers. A literal deletion in the
upstream implementation gives a tightly controlled measure of the
mathematical change. A second, readable implementation shares its
syntax traversal and infers the conclusion of a paired elimination;
it has its own matched three-case control. These implementation changes
are identified separately. The collection's historical compact
encodings are smaller than these readable modules, so we do not claim
a repository-wide byte record. There is also no language-independent
theorem that five displayed patterns is the fewest possible.

\section{Complete specification of the theory}\label{sec:spec}

\subsection{Numerical terms, formulas, and clauses}

There is one numerical sort. Terms are finite raw expressions
\[
       t ::= x\mid 0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]
Only $u$ in $h$ is bound. The counter $n$ and initializer $a$ lie
outside that binder. Other numerical variables in $h$ are passive
parameters. An iterator is a term constructor with a bound state
variable, not quantification over functions. There are no additional
function symbols or universal program evaluator.

Formulas are
\[
                  F ::= (s=t)\mid \D_{x,y}F.
\]
Both displayed coordinates of $\D$ are bound. We work modulo
renaming bound variables and use capture-avoiding substitution.
All other syntactic equality tests below are literal tests on these
raw expressions. In particular, $I(0,a;u.h)$ is not identified
definitionally with $a$.

A context is a finite list of reusable clause occurrences
\[
                [A;\,y.B(y)],\qquad y\notin\FV(A).
\]
The clause binds $y$ only in its consequent. Clauses are context
objects, not a third formula constructor. Put $H=(0=0)$ and write
\[
        \ord(F):=[H;\,v.F],\qquad v\notin\FV(F).
\]
Thus $\Gamma,F$ in a rule abbreviates
$\Gamma,\ord(F)$. Occurrences are retained separately even if their
clause values are identical. The designated contradiction is
\[
                           \bot_* := (0=S0).
\]

\subsection{Two-case computational equality}

For terms $s,t,b$, the partial operation $\Phi_2(s,t,b)$ is defined
by exactly the following table:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Shape of $b$ & Required source $s$ & Result $\Phi_2(s,t,b)$\\
\midrule
$I(0,a;u.h)$ & $a$ & $t$\\
$I(Sn,a;u.h)$ & $I(n,a;u.h)$ & $h[t/u]$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Every required equality in this table is a literal syntax comparison.
The operation is undefined in every other case. In particular,
$b\equiv s$ is no longer an evaluation case.

\subsection{Every primitive axiom and inference rule}

The entire theory consists of the following five patterns:
\begin{align}
 &\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash s=s}
       &&\text{Refl},\label{rule:refl}\\[3pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash b=r}
        {\Gamma\vdash r=\Phi_2(s,t,b)}
       &&\text{Eval, if defined},\label{rule:eval}\\[3pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash B(t)}
       &&\text{Use, if }[A;y.B]\text{ occurs in }\Gamma,\label{rule:use}\\[3pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
         \Gamma,[B(b,0);y.B(b,y)]\vdash F}
        {\Gamma\vdash F}
       &&\text{Elim},\label{rule:elim}\\[3pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
        {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y)}
       &&\text{Intro},\label{rule:intro}
\end{align}
where
\[
 \eta=\begin{cases}
 z,&\text{$B$ is literally an equation and $t$ is literally $0$},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
In Elim, $b$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, $F$, and all passive parameters
of the matrix. In Intro, $z$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, $t$, and all
passive matrix parameters. Supplied syntax is well scoped. Witnesses
and specialization targets may be arbitrary numerical terms.

Refl is the only axiom schema. There are no further primitive logical,
equality, computation, successor, or induction axioms. The two rows of
Eval are part of the specification, as is the mode test in Intro.
Counting them explicitly avoids disguising a case distinction as a
single unexplained rule. The calculus has five displayed patterns,
with two evaluation cases and two introduction modes.

\subsection{Structural operations}

Ordinary hypothesis use is derived: prove $H$ by Refl at $0$ and
apply Use to $[H;v.F]$. Weakening and injective fresh renaming follow
by induction on a derivation. Ordinary-assumption cut is also
admissible. For a distinguished occurrence $[H;v.F]$, replace each
Use selecting that occurrence by the supplied proof of $F$, weakened
to the current context. Freshen eigenvariables before doing so.
All other nodes are transformed recursively.

These elementary transformations do not change numerical witnesses.
We do not assume that arbitrary substitution into a derivation
preserves every rule tag: a nonzero raw witness can become literally
$0$, changing Intro's mode. Full substitution admissibility for
ordinary formula sequents will follow from the equivalence proof.

\section{The deleted equality case}\label{sec:deletion}

The extra case in $\Cthree$ is
\[
            \frac{s=t\qquad s=r}{r=t}.
            \tag{Root}
\]
This is ordinary equality composition with a shared left side.
The following identity-frame argument is the same reduction already
proved for the six-pattern calculus in
\texttt{equality\_from\_iteration} and
\texttt{NativeEval.lean}; here it is applied inside the clausal
calculus~\cite{frame,lean}.

\begin{lemma}[Root evaluation is derived]\label{lem:root}
Given $p:\Gamma\vdash s=t$ and $q:\Gamma\vdash s=r$, two zero
evaluations and one reflexivity leaf derive $\Gamma\vdash r=t$.
Neither premise is duplicated.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $J_s=I(0,s;u.0)$. Refl gives $J_s=J_s$. Zero evaluation with
the major premise $q:s=r$ gives
\[
                  J_s=r.
\]
Zero evaluation of this result with major premise $p:s=t$ gives
$r=t$. In proof notation,
\[
 \Root(p,q):=\ev_0\bigl(p,\ev_0(q,\refl(J_s))\bigr).
\]
The initializer of $J_s$ is literally $s$ in both steps, so both
source checks succeed. The update body is unused and may equally
well be the bound identity variable. No numerical evaluation of
$s$ is performed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact equivalence with the current clausal proposal]
For every retained clause context $\Gamma$ and formula $F$,
\[
       \Gamma\vdash_{\Cthree}F
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \Gamma\vdash_{\Ctwo}F.
\]
The translations preserve every term, formula, and retained assumption
of the end sequent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every primitive $\Ctwo$ inference is already a $\Cthree$ inference.
In the other direction, recurse over a finite $\Cthree$ proof and
replace each Root occurrence by Lemma~\ref{lem:root}. All other
inferences, including their freshness conditions and Intro modes,
are unchanged. The construction works under arbitrary clause
contexts, including those opened inside Elim and Intro.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact inference-node cost]
If the input proof has $N$ nodes and $k$ Root nodes, the translation
has exactly $N+2k$ nodes, hence at most $3N$ nodes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each removed node becomes two Eval nodes and one Refl node. Its two
original premise trees occur once each. Every other node is copied
once. Sum these local changes over the input tree.
\end{proof}
This statement counts inference nodes. It is not a bound on serialized
JSON bytes or running time: term labels are copied into the new frame.

\section{Equality and arithmetic computation are recovered}

Write $R_s$ for Refl at $s$. Lemma~\ref{lem:root} immediately gives
\[
 \operatorname{sym}(p:s=t)=\Root(R_s,p):t=s,
\]
and, for $q:t=v$,
\[
 \operatorname{trans}(p,q)=\Root(q,\operatorname{sym}(p)):s=v.
\]
Both iterator equations are derived, not additional axioms:
\begin{align*}
 \ev_0(R_a,R_{I(0,a;u.h)})
      &:I(0,a;u.h)=a,\\
 \ev_S(R_{I(n,a;u.h)},R_{I(Sn,a;u.h)})
      &:I(Sn,a;u.h)=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u].
\end{align*}

\begin{lemma}[Congruence without a primitive template rule]
For every fixed numerical term template $h(u)$,
\[
                   s=t\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(s)=h(t).
\]
This includes templates with several occurrences of $u$ and
occurrences inside nested iterator bodies.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $A_s=I(0,s;u.h)$ and $B_s=I(S0,s;u.h)$. For $p:s=t$ form
\begin{align*}
 z_s&=\ev_0(R_s,R_{A_s}) &&:A_s=s,\\
 c_s&=\ev_S(z_s,R_{B_s}) &&:B_s=h(s),\\
 z_{s,t}&=\ev_0(p,R_{A_s}) &&:A_s=t,\\
 \ev_S(z_{s,t},c_s)&&&:h(s)=h(t).
\end{align*}
The successor source is literally $A_s$ in each use. Capture-avoiding
body substitution supplies the appropriate free occurrences. The
input equality is used once.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Crossed transport and equation transport]
The following rules are admissible:
\[
 s=t,\ h(s)=r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=h(t),
\]
\[
 s=t,\ a(s)=b(s)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a(t)=b(t).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Root to $h(s)=h(t)$ and $h(s)=r$. For the second claim,
apply the first rule with templates $a$ and $b$ successively:
$b(s)=a(t)$, then $a(t)=b(t)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Case introduction for every witness]\label{lem:cases}
For every matrix $B$, every witness $t$, and fresh $z$,
\[
 \Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B(x,y).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Only an equation matrix at literal witness $0$ is missing from the
primitive case mode. Put $c=I(0,0;u.0)$. This term is not literally
$0$, and derived zero computation proves $c=0$.
Under $B(c,0)$, equation transport gives $B(0,0)$. Cut in the
supplied branch to obtain $B(0,Sz)$ and transport back to
$B(c,Sz)$. Primitive Intro at witness $c$ now uses its case mode
and concludes the original paired formula. Only equation transport
was used; transport through a compound formula is not assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Transport in every native formula]\label{lem:fulltr}
For any Eq/$\D$ template $F(v)$,
$s=t,F(s)\vdash F(t)$ is admissible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use external structural induction on $F$. The equation case was
proved above. For $F(v)=\D_{x,y}B(v,x,y)$, eliminate $F(s)$ at
fresh $b$, obtaining $[B(s,b,0);y.B(s,b,y)]$. Introduce $F(t)$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:cases}, with witness $b$. Under its local
assumption $B(t,b,0)$, use the induction hypothesis backwards to
obtain $B(s,b,0)$, Use at $Sz$ to obtain $B(s,b,Sz)$, then the
induction hypothesis forwards to obtain $B(t,b,Sz)$. Close the
introduction and elimination. Recursive transports concern strictly
smaller formulas.
\end{proof}

\section{Conditional closure and ordinary intuitionistic logic}

\subsection{Closure as a macro}

Define, with $w$ unused,
\[
                          q_yB(y):=\D_{w,y}B(y).
\]
Case introduction proves $q_yB$ from $B(0)\vdash B(Sz)$.
Elim on $q_yB$ opens exactly $[B(0);y.B(y)]$, independently
of the new witness; Use then gives
\[
                    q_yB,\ B(0)\vdash B(t).
\]
Conversely a retained clause $[B(0);y.B(y)]$ proves $q_yB$:
assume its base, specialize it at $Sz$, and use case introduction.

For a literal equation $E(y)$, Intro at unused witness $0$ gives
$q_yE$ from $E(z)\vdash E(Sz)$. Hence
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash E(0)\qquad\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash E(t)}
 \tag{Equation induction}
\]
is derived. For arbitrary $F$, case introduction and specialization
similarly give the numerical case rule
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash F(0)\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash F(t)},
 \tag{Numerical cases}
\]
where $z$ is fresh for retained parameters. The distinction matters:
unrestricted progress induction for compound formulas is not a primitive rule.

\subsection{Explicit logical macros}

We record the entire interface so that no ordinary logical rules are
silently added to the kernel. Write $1=S0$, and define
\[
 C(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b),\quad
 \delta(v)=C(v,1,0),\quad p(v)=I(v,0;u.\delta(u)).
\]
The update variable in $C$ is unused. Computation gives
$C(0,a,b)=a$ and $C(Sv,a,b)=b$.

The gate of a formula is defined recursively by
\[
 G_v(s=t)=(C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t)),\qquad
 G_v(\D_{x,y}B)=\D_{x,y}G_vB.
\]
Bound variables are freshened away from $v$.
It satisfies $\vdash G_0A$ and $G_{Sz}A\iffder A$.
An equation can be padded by
$E\iffder\D_{x,y}G_yE$ for fresh unused $x,y$.

A selector between arbitrary formulas is defined recursively by
\begin{align*}
 M_v(s=t,a=b)&=(C(v,s,a)=C(v,t,b)),\\
 M_v(\D_{x,y}A,\D_{x,y}B)&=\D_{x,y}M_v(A,B),\\
 M_v(E,\D_{x,y}B)&=\D_{x,y}M_v(G_yE,B),\\
 M_v(\D_{x,y}A,E)&=\D_{x,y}M_v(A,G_yE),
\end{align*}
where $E$ is an equation and the binding coordinates are aligned
and fresh. Each recursive call decreases the maximum binder depth.
Gate padding and binder congruence give
$M_0(A,B)\iffder A$ and $M_{Sz}(A,B)\iffder B$.

The ordinary connectives are the following macros, with all new
coordinates fresh and $w$ unused:
\begin{align*}
 A\mathbin{\to_*}B&:=q_zM_z(A,B),\\
 A\mathbin{\wedge_*}B&:=q_zG_zM_{p(z)}(A,B),\\
 \exists_*x\,A(x)&:=\D_{x,y}G_yA(x),\\
 A\mathbin{\vee_*}B&:=\exists_*v\,M_v(A,B),\\
 \forall_*y\,A(y)&:=A(0)\mathbin{\wedge_*}q_yA(y),\\
 \bot_*&:=(0=S0).
\end{align*}

Their introduction and elimination rules are derived as follows.
Implication uses the selector's zero and successor endpoints.
Existential introduction uses the given numerical witness and the
successor gate; existential elimination opens a fresh witness,
proves the zero gate, and specializes at $1$. Disjunction uses
witnesses $0$ and $1$; its elimination uses numerical cases on the
selector control after existential elimination. Conjunction uses a
true zero gate, while the specializations $z=1,2$ recover its two
operands, since $p(1)=1$ and $p(2)=0$. For its introduction,
from $A$ and $B$ use numerical cases at an arbitrary control $v$
to derive $M_v(A,B)$; instantiate $v=p(Sz)$, apply the successor
gate, and introduce the closure. Universal introduction
packages a zero instance and a closure; elimination uses the closure
and its base.

From $0=1$, equation transport and selector computation imply
$s=C(0,s,t)=C(1,s,t)=t$ for arbitrary terms. External induction on
formula syntax, using case introduction at the paired step, then
derives every formula. This proves falsity elimination. These
constructions recover intuitionistic logic; excluded middle for
arbitrary formulas is not postulated.

\section{Exact link to the formalized native calculus}
\label{sec:native}

The repository's $\E$ (Lean name \texttt{EPrf}) uses the same raw
numerical terms and Eq/$\Q$/$\D$ formulas. Its six patterns are
Hyp, Refl, two-case Eval, QE, DE, and the same witness-directed
introduction. Its equivalence to the native kernel is the theorem
\texttt{eprf\_iff\_nprf} in \texttt{NativeEval.lean}~\cite{lean}.
We use this established bridge explicitly; the new checker has not
been verified by Lean.

Define $\tau$ from Eq/$\Q$/$\D$ formulas to Eq/$\D$ formulas by
\[
 \tau(s=t)=(s=t),\qquad
 \tau(\D_{x,y}B)=\D_{x,y}\tau(B),\qquad
 \tau(\Q_yB)=\D_{w,y}\tau(B),
\]
where $w$ is fresh and unused. Let $j$ be inclusion of the Eq/$\D$
language. Numerical terms are unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Faithfulness in both directions]\label{thm:faithful}
For every native context $\Gamma$ and formula $A$,
\[
 \Gamma\vdash_{\E}A
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \ord(\tau\Gamma)\vdash_{\Ctwo}\tau A.
\]
For every Eq/$\D$ context $\Delta$ of ordinary formula assumptions,
\[
 \ord(\Delta)\vdash_{\Ctwo}F
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 j\Delta\vdash_{\E}jF.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the forward direction, translate a proof by induction. Hyp is
ordinary-assumption use, Refl and Eval are unchanged, and QE is the
derived closure specialization. Introduction is copied: $\tau$
preserves equation versus compound roots and leaves witnesses alone,
so it preserves the selected mode. At DE, the new Elim opens a clause
$[\tau B(b,0);y.\tau B(b,y)]$. That clause derives
$q_y\tau B(b,y)$ by the closure interface. Cut this proof into the
translated minor branch, whose old assumption was
$\tau(\Q_yB(b,y))$, and close Elim.

For the reverse direction, retain the provenance of each clause
occurrence. An initial ordinary clause and a local Intro clause
$[H;v.G]$ are represented by the native assumption $jG$.
A clause introduced by Elim is represented by $\Q_yjB(b,y)$.
At Use, an ordinary clause translates to Hyp and an Elim clause
translates to QE, using its translated antecedent proof. Refl and
Eval are unchanged. Elim becomes DE, and Intro has the same matrix,
witness, and local assumption. Provenance belongs to occurrences,
so equal-looking clauses with different origins cause no ambiguity.
This proves the reverse implication. The forward implication on
the common language follows because $\tau j$ is the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Both formula composites]
For every Eq/$\D$ formula $F$, $\tau jF=F$ up to bound renaming.
For every native formula $A$,
\[
                          A\iffder_{\E}j\tau A.
\]
Both numerical translations are identities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The essential equivalence is
$\Q_yB\iffder\D_{w,y}B$ with $w$ unused. Forward, assume
$B(0)$, specialize the closure at $Sz$, and introduce the paired
formula by cases. Backward, DE opens precisely $\Q_yB$ and Hyp
closes the branch. Uniform pointwise interderivability lifts through
$\D$: eliminate the source, introduce the target by cases at the
opened witness, convert its base backwards, specialize the source
clause, and convert the result forwards. The same argument works
for $\Q$. External induction on $A$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

The argument proves more than agreement on closed equations or on
standard natural numbers. It preserves every ordinary retained-context
sequent and supplies both formula round trips. Arbitrary external
clauses can also be represented through the derived formula
$A\to_*\forall_*yB$, using the logical interface above.

\section{Bi-interpretability with full first-order iPRA}
\label{sec:biinterp}

\subsection{The precise target and ordinary realization}

The target iPRA is intuitionistic first-order logic with equality,
one function symbol for each fixed primitive-recursive definition,
its defining equations, successor injectivity, $Sx=0\to\bot$,
and contextual induction for quantifier-free formulas, with numerical
parameters. This is the repository's \texttt{PRAAx} theory.

For an ordinary first-order reading, elaborate each fixed iterator
body into a function symbol with the two iterator equations. Let
$\Teq$ use ordinary intuitionistic equality logic, these equations,
contextual literal-equation induction, and
\[
 \forall x\,(x=0\lor\exists y\,x=Sy),\qquad
                         0=S0\to\bot.
\]
The native reading is
\[
 (s=t)^\circ=(s=t),\qquad
 (\D_{x,y}B)^\circ=
    \exists x\bigl(B^\circ(x,0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(x,y)\bigr),
\]
and a clause reads as $A^\circ\to\forall yB^\circ$.
The explicit separation sentence must not be omitted from this
ordinary realization. Positive equations alone admit the singleton
structure; defining native falsity as $0=1$ is not a reason to
drop the ordinary falsity bridge.

\subsection{Arithmetic translation, in the required dependency order}

The repository has a substantial internal proof of
$\Teq\equiv\ipra$, not merely a standard-model interpretation.
The five supporting mathematical source files are included in
\texttt{reference/foundations/}~\cite{bridge}. They establish, in order:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item A scalar predecessor $P$ with $P0=0$ and $P(Sn)=n$,
       using equation induction and numerical cases, before pairing
       or quantifier-free induction is available.
 \item Truncated subtraction and a term reflecting actual numerical
       equality throughout the whole domain.
 \item A characteristic term for each fixed quantifier-free formula,
       giving every quantifier-free induction instance from equation
       induction.
 \item Pairing with projections and a compiler for every fixed
       primitive-recursive definition.
\end{enumerate}
This dependency order prevents a circular appeal to predecessor,
pairing, or a Boolean equality detector during their own construction.
The inherited details are not claimed as new results of this note.

For clarity, the final recursion compiler is explicit. If
\[
 f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p),
\]
and $\pi,L,R$ are proved pairing and projection terms, set
\begin{align*}
 V(n,\vec p)&=
 I\bigl(n,\pi(0,g^*(\vec p));
 u.\pi(SL(u),h^*(L(u),R(u),\vec p))\bigr),\\
 f^*(n,\vec p)&=R(V(n,\vec p)).
\end{align*}
Equation induction proves $L(V(n,\vec p))=n$; projection equations
then establish both defining equations of $f^*$. The construction
recurses externally over each fixed finite function description.
No variable ranges over arbitrary descriptions and no uniform total
evaluator is asserted inside iPRA.

In the other direction, every fixed iterator is primitive recursive:
its update ignores the recursion-counter argument. Thus each scalar
term has a direct iPRA interpretation.

\subsection{Why mutual interpretability is not the whole claim}

\begin{theorem}[Whole-domain bi-interpretation and equiconsistency]
The ordinary realization presented by $\Ctwo$ is bi-interpretable
with full first-order iPRA. Both interpretations use the whole
numerical domain and actual equality. The graph of both numerical
composite isomorphisms is $x=y$. The theories are equiconsistent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:faithful} and the formula-composite theorem give
inverse interpretations between $\Ctwo$ and the formalized raw
native calculus, with the identity on numerical terms. The repository
proves the native/$\Teq$ equivalence and the $\Teq$/iPRA
bi-interpretation. We compose these established interpretations.

The internal reason for the numerical composites is uniqueness.
Two fixed iterators with the same initializer and provably identical
updates are equal at all counters by equation induction on their
value equality. The same argument holds for each ordinary primitive
recursion. Induction on a finite function description shows that its
double translation satisfies its original defining equations and is
therefore provably equal to the original operation. These are
identities at arbitrary internal arguments, not statements just about
standard numerals. Domain restriction or quotient equality is never
introduced, so $x=y$ is the definable composite isomorphism.

The logical macros have their ordinary meanings. In particular,
\[
 \D_{x,y}B\iffder
 \exists_*x\bigl(B(x,0)\to_*\forall_*yB(x,y)\bigr).
\]
Forward, Elim opens a clause; under its base it supplies all instances,
allowing universal, implication, and existential introduction.
Backward, eliminate the existential witness and introduce the paired
formula by cases, using implication and universal elimination in the
successor branch. Structural induction supplies all formula composites.

Finally, $0=S0$ corresponds to ordinary falsity in both directions.
Finite proof translations therefore take a contradiction in either
theory to a contradiction in the other, proving equiconsistency.
No claim that either theory proves its own consistency is made.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete checker and trusted boundary}\label{sec:checker}

The delivered \texttt{code/checker.py} is a standalone module. It
reads one JSON value, verifies the declared closed goal, and prints
\texttt{ACCEPT} on success. Exceptions produce \texttt{REJECT} and
a nonzero exit code. It imports only Python's \texttt{json} and
\texttt{sys} modules. No theorem library, normalizer, solver, or
external proof checker is called.

\subsection{Input and syntax}

The top-level object has exactly the fields
\begin{lstlisting}
{"goal": F, "proof": P}
\end{lstlisting}
The public interface accepts closed theorems. Open hypotheses and
clauses appear only in locally checked branches. A sequent with
ordinary assumptions can also be internalized using the derived
implication and universal macros.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
JSON expression & Meaning\\
\midrule
\texttt{['z']} & $0$\\
\texttt{['v',i]} & numerical variable\\
\texttt{['s',t]} & $S(t)$\\
\texttt{['i',n,a,h]} & $I(n,a;u.h)$, one binder in $h$\\
\texttt{['=',s,t]} & equation\\
\texttt{['d',B]} & paired formula, two binders in $B$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The table uses Python quoting for readability; certificates use JSON
double quotes. Positive variable indices are locally bound indices,
starting at $1$. Negative integers name fresh free levels opened by
the checker. At scope $k$ and local binder depth $d$, exactly
\[
                    -k\le i\le d,\qquad i\ne0
\]
is allowed. Booleans and floating-point values are not integer indices.
The root scope is zero. In a paired matrix, index $1$ is $y$ and
index $2$ is $x$. A fresh branch parameter is the new negative level
$-k-1$.

One recursive traversal implements constructor typing, binder depth,
and bound-variable opening. In an opening of a binder beyond depth
$d$, index $d+1$ is replaced, larger indices are decreased by one,
and all others are unchanged. Opened values have no ambient positive
indices: they are well-scoped terms at depth zero. Consequently they
can be inserted beneath local binders without capture; their negative
free levels remain distinct from all bound indices.

\subsection{Proof packets}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Packet & Rule\\
\midrule
\texttt{['r',t]} & Refl at $t$\\
\texttt{['v',p,q]} & Eval on two derived equations\\
\texttt{['u',j,t,p]} & Use clause occurrence $j$ at target $t$\\
\texttt{['e',p,q]} & Elim; paired major $p$, local branch $q$\\
\texttt{['i',B,t,p]} & Intro of $\D B$, witness $t$, branch $p$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The elimination packet does not repeat its intended conclusion.
Instead, the checker infers the branch conclusion and validates it
again in the parent scope. This excludes an escaping eigenparameter.
It is an implementation simplification, not a new logical rule.

\subsection{Acceptance invariant}

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of the checker algorithm]
If the checker returns a formula at scope $k$ under a well-scoped
retained clause context, that formula is well scoped at $k$ and has
a derivation in $\Ctwo$ from that context. Conversely, every finite
closed $\Ctwo$ derivation has a certificate in this format.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induct on a successful proof-tree computation. Refl validates its
term. Use checks a nonnegative in-range integer occurrence index,
validates its target term, and compares the derived premise to the
retained antecedent. Opening the consequent is then the formal Use
instance. At Eval, successful derivations yield formulas of exactly
the declared grammar. Destructuring three fields therefore forces
an equation; destructuring four fields for its left term forces an
iterator. The counter check and source equality select precisely
one of the two permitted evaluation rows. The returned equation is
its formal conclusion.

Elim checks that the major is paired, opens its outer witness at
the new negative level, and adds the corresponding clause. The
recursive branch is well scoped at $k+1$. Validating its result at
$k$ supplies the eigenvariable condition, so Elim is sound.
Intro validates its matrix and witness before opening them, constructs
the prescribed local assumption using exactly the stated mode test,
and compares the recursively derived branch with the required
successor instance. Its witness cannot mention the new level.

For completeness, encode a finite derivation after fresh renaming.
Use the corresponding packet at each node; choose retained occurrence
indices and encode each eigenparameter by the next negative level.
The same induction verifies each packet. This is an idealized
unbounded finite-tree algorithm statement. An actual Python process
can reject an otherwise valid extremely deep certificate through its
finite recursion or memory limits; it does not accept an invalid
certificate as a result.
\end{proof}

The shared walker preserves local binder depths for iterators and
paired matrices separately. It neither evaluates terms nor compares
their numerical denotations. In particular, equality source matching
is syntactic, and $0=1$ is never accepted because two Python values
happen to compare equal after an unintended coercion.

\section{Limits and next deletions}

\subsection{Why reflexivity has not simply been removed}

The current raw-clausal report gives a useful obstruction~\cite{obstruction}. Fix any
finite collection of true closed equation seeds and let $A$ be their
finite set of ordinary numerical values. Interpret an atomic equation
$s=t$ as
\[
             \llbracket s\rrbracket=\llbracket t\rrbracket\in A,
\]
and interpret the paired formulas and clauses by their ordinary
conditional-universal readings on standard natural numbers.
The remaining rules preserve this interpretation: an evaluation's
minor equation forces its output's left value into $A$, and ordinary
computation equates the output values; the logical rules use ordinary
cases and induction on standard numbers. Thus these finitely many
seeds cannot yield $n=n$ for a numeral outside $A$.

Consequently replacing Refl by $0=0$, or by any finite collection
of true closed seeds, fails with the other rules fixed. Adding the
permanent clause $[0=0;y.y=y]$ restores reflexivity, but that merely
relocates its universal content into initialization.

\subsection{Neither raw evaluation row can be dropped unchanged}

If the zero row is deleted, interpret
$I(n,a;u.h)=h^n(Sa)$ on standard natural numbers, with actual equality.
Successor evaluation, Refl, and all the logical rules remain sound,
but $I(0,0;u.0)=0$ is false. If the successor row is deleted,
interpret $I(n,a;u.h)=a$ for every counter. Zero evaluation and the
other rules are sound, but successor computation fails. These are
relative irredundancy results for this fixed language and rule set;
they do not rule out a different arithmetic encoding.

\subsection{What has and has not been achieved}

The result is a strict deletion of primitive evaluation instances
from the current five-pattern raw clausal theory, with unchanged
consequences and a smaller complete checker in controlled comparisons.
It also combines the repository's two-case equality interface with
its smaller Eq/$\D$ formula grammar. It is an incremental composition
of existing ideas, supported by a full equivalence argument and a
checked proof translation. It does not establish absolute minimality,
improve the historical compact byte record, or replace the repository's
formalized arithmetic bridge with a new proof.
