Independent mathematical and scope review of checker_work/ce5_two.py

Theorem-level result. The two-case calculus is exactly equivalent, on the
same raw Eq/D formulas and clause contexts, to raw_clausal's three-case CE5.
The only deleted case is the root equality-composition case.

Root-case derivation. Given p:s=t and q:s=r, put Z=I(0,s;u.u).
Eval(q, Refl(Z)) gives Z=r using zero evaluation. Applying Eval to p and
that result gives r=t using zero evaluation again. This inserts exactly one
new reflexivity leaf and two Eval nodes, with p and q each appearing once.
No logical rule, numerical induction, normalization, or derived equality
principle is used. The derivation works in every retained clause context.

Thus translation from three-case CE5 to the two-case system proceeds by
structural recursion on the proof, replacing root Eval nodes by this frame.
The reverse map is inclusion. Both term and formula translations are literal
identities. Composing with raw_clausal's Eq/D <-> native Eq/Q/D equivalence
and the inherited scalar <-> iPRA bridge gives the required bi-interpretation.
The component root-deletion lemma already exists in equality_from_iteration;
the present contribution is its composition with the clause formulation.

Checker invariant. At derive(P,C,k), every clause antecedent in C is a
well-scoped formula at k; every clause body is a well-scoped formula with
one extra bound index. Every successful return is a well-scoped Eq/D formula
at k. Initially C is empty. The invariant is proved by recursion on P:
- r validates its term, so its returned equality is well scoped.
- u checks the index and target, recursively checks the antecedent proof,
  and opens the one-bound-variable body with an ambient term.
- v recursively obtains equations: the first return must have length 3,
  the second must have length 3 and an iterator-headed left term of length 4.
  Formula/term typing from the invariant makes these arities unambiguous.
  The zero row checks equality of the iterator seed with the major source.
  The successor row checks the actual 's' tag and the whole preceding
  iterator, preventing the false-iterator-counter attack.
- e opens a D matrix at fresh free level -k-1, retains its conditional
  universal clause, and checks the inferred result at the old scope k.
  This final syntax check is exactly the no-eigenvariable-escape condition.
- i validates its D matrix and witness at k, opens the witness, and adds
  a constant clause for the prescribed local equation/formula assumption.
  Its recursive result must equal the exact successor instance. The fresh
  level -k-1 cannot have been in the validated outer data.

The opener is capture avoiding because ambient free variables use negative
levels and bound indices are positive. Supplied values have no free positive
indices; they need no depth shift when inserted under surviving binders.
Internal bound indices in the inserted term still refer to its own binders.
Opening the outer D coordinate starts at depth 1 and leaves its inner
coordinate intact. Walk uses one additional binder for an iterator body and
two for a D matrix. Ordinary assumption bodies have no free positive index,
so their subsequent clause-use opening is a no-op as required.

No scope/type soundness bug found in this review. This is a written review,
not a machine-checked proof of the Python implementation. Public JSON parsing
and syntax checks reject Python booleans as variable indices and reject
unrecognized/malformed constructors; resource exhaustion is a rejection.

Research boundaries. Full-normalization plus clauses is a combination of
already existing reductions and did not give a measured source-size gain.
Replacing general reflexivity with finitely many true closed equation seeds
is blocked by raw_clausal's finite-value counterinterpretation. A permanent
universal reflexivity clause only relocates that axiom. The SR0-to-full-iPRA
bootstrap remains unfinished: zero-equation induction does not by itself
justify the unbounded addition/rotation identities used to construct the
scalar predecessor and faithful equality test. No claim of its solution
should appear in the delivered theory.
