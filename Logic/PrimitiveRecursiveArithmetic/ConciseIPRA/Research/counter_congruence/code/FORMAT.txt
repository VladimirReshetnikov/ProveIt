ROOT REPLACEMENT AND COUNTER CONGRUENCE: COMPLETE CHECKER CONTRACT
================================================================

Status
------
checker.py is a complete readable implementation of the newly proposed
restricted equality calculus. It is not a claim to beat the supplied compact
checker frontier. All logical, numerical, binding, parsing, rejection, and
public-verdict code is included in the measured module. It imports only
Python's standard json module. No certificate string is executed as code.

Mathematical syntax
-------------------
Numerical terms are variables, 0, S(a), and R(n,a;u,v.h). Only h binds u,v;
u is the predecessor counter and v is the recursive value. The update's
other numerical parameters are retained. S is a convenient displayed
abbreviation: the R0 instance with update 0 identifies it with R(0,a;u,v.0).
The checker stores this abbreviation as an S node. It does not automatically
convert all expanded and contracted forms; an R0 equality and the derived
equality rules may be needed when a certificate changes their representation.
The numerical axioms are exactly:

  R(0,a;u,v.h)  = S(a)                                      R0
  R(Sn,a;u,v.h) = h[n/u, R(n,a;u,v.h)/v]                    RS

Object formulas are equations and D_xy B(x,y). The reusable internal clause
Q_y B(y) is allowed in contexts and as an available QE major. It is not an
exported goal or a constructor inside an object formula. Its ordinary meaning
is B(0) -> forall y B(y). The ordinary meaning of D is
exists x (B(x,0) -> forall y B(x,y)).

Contexts are finite reusable ordered lists; the ordering affects only the
certificate's zero-based hypothesis indices. Weakening, exchange, contraction,
renaming, and cut are mathematical admissibility properties, not extra
certificate opcodes. Object assumptions are directly usable as hypotheses.
An internal Q clause is available only by context membership; no other rule
may prove a Q goal. Object Eq/D majors may be supplied by arbitrary derivations.

Every primitive rule
--------------------
Hyp: conclude an object assumption already in the context. At a QE-major
edge, the same checked lookup makes a retained Q clause available.

RL (root-left replacement): from s=t and s=r, conclude t=r. Both premises
are literal equations. There is no formula template or term context in this
primitive rule.

CC (counter congruence): from s=t, conclude
R(s,a;u,v.h)=R(t,a;u,v.h). The seed and update must be identical up to bound
variable renaming. The checker does not allow primitive successor, seed, or
update congruence.

QE: from available Q_y B(y) and B(0), conclude B(t).

DE: from D_xy B(x,y) and a derivation of F under the additional clause
Q_y B(a,y), conclude F. The numerical eigenparameter a is fresh for the old
context, old numerical environment, major, and conclusion. The clause is
discharged when the body ends.

DI-case: from a derivation of B(t,Sz) under the additional object assumption
B(t,0), conclude D_xy B(x,y). The eigenparameter z is fresh for the old context,
old numerical environment, witness t, and fixed matrix parameters.

DI-progress: the same construction with hypothesis E(t,z) instead of E(t,0),
but only when the opened matrix E is a literal equation. A Q or D matrix is
rejected before its body is checked.

R0, RS, Hyp, RL, CC, QE, DE, and the two DI modes are the entire inventory.
In particular, general equality transport and arbitrary term congruence are
not primitive. There is no reflexivity opcode and no arbitrary-formula
induction. The delivered producer derives reflexivity from two RS instances
and RL; its other equality proofs expand into the listed primitives.

Input and output
----------------
The standard input is one JSON object:

  {"parameters": ["p", ...], "goal": FORMULA, "proof": PROOF}

parameters may be omitted. A successful parameterized packet denotes a
derivation with those arbitrary free numerical parameters; its universal
closure is the corresponding sentence. The list contains no repetitions.
The checker never assumes a value for a free parameter.

Success prints exactly ACCEPT and exits zero. Any failed check, malformed
input, or unavailable resource causes a nonzero exit; ordinary rejected
packets print REJECT and a short diagnostic. Resource limits can prevent
acceptance of a very large valid proof. Mathematical completeness concerns
finite proof trees with unbounded physical resources.

Tagged numerical arrays:
  ["0"]
  ["v", "name"]
  ["S", TERM]
  ["R", COUNTER, SEED, "counter_name", "state_name", UPDATE]

Tagged object arrays:
  ["=", LEFT_TERM, RIGHT_TERM]
  ["D", "witness_name", "index_name", OBJECT_MATRIX]

Tagged internal clause:
  ["Q", "index_name", OBJECT_MATRIX]

Named binders may shadow older binders. Their names must be strings; the two
binders of one R or D must be distinct. Variables are resolved lexically when
parsed. All comparisons use internal de Bruijn indices, so alpha-equivalent
bound expressions compare equally. Free names remain tagged free parameters,
including when they share a spelling with a binder elsewhere in the proof.

Proof node fields
-----------------
  {"rule":"hyp", "index": INTEGER}
  {"rule":"R0"}
  {"rule":"RS"}
  {"rule":"RL", "equality": EQUATION, "major": PROOF, "minor": PROOF}
  {"rule":"CC", "major": PROOF}
  {"rule":"QE", "closure": Q_CLAUSE, "target": TERM,
   "major": HYP_PROOF, "base": PROOF}
  {"rule":"DE", "paired": D_FORMULA, "major": PROOF,
   "eigen": "fresh_name", "body": PROOF}
  {"rule":"DI", "mode":"case" OR "progress", "witness": TERM,
   "eigen": "fresh_name", "body": PROOF}

Extra unused JSON fields are inert. All fields actually used by a rule are
checked. A hypothesis index must be an integer rather than a Boolean, and
must be nonnegative and in range. Every numerical term and formula supplied
by a certificate is parsed in the current numerical environment before use.

Scoping and substitution
------------------------
The internal representations distinguish bound indices from free names.
R adds two binders only in its update; D adds two binders in its matrix; Q
adds one. Within R, state has index 0 and counter index 1. Within D, the
universal/index coordinate has index 0 and witness coordinate index 1.
Opening removes the specified binder, shifts inserted bound coordinates
under nested binders, and decreases the indices of remaining outer binders.

RS first substitutes the predecessor for counter index 1, then the recursive
value for state index 0. DI/DE first substitute the witness for D index 1.
DI then opens index 0 at zero/current/successor as prescribed by its mode;
DE packages the surviving index as an internal Q clause. The old conclusion
is parsed before a DE eigenparameter is allocated, and a DI witness before
its eigenparameter is allocated, so they cannot mention a future eigenname.

Untrusted producer and evidence
-------------------------------
producer.py contains no additional trusted rule. It constructs ordinary JSON
proof trees. Its fixed internal binder names are chosen fresh for the delivered
templates. Its replace() helper is not a general capture-avoiding compiler for
arbitrary user templates; this is a targeted certificate generator. The
trusted checker independently uses capture-avoiding de Bruijn substitution.
The producer's equality reifier is

  W(n)   = R(n,0;u,v.u),
  H_F(n) = R(n,F(0);u,v.S(F(Su))),
  J_F(n) = W(H_F(n)).

It proves J_F(0)=F(0) and J_F(Sn)=F(Sn) with R0/RS, RL, and CC. Numerical
cases, expanded into DI-case/DE/QE, give J_F(t)=F(t) at arbitrary symbolic
t. Two CC applications lift s=t through H_F and W; root equation algebra
then gives F(s)=F(t). This proves the delivered instances of successor,
seed, and update congruence with arbitrary retained parameters. No compound
transport expansion is implemented in this producer; its general mathematical
admissibility proof belongs to the report.

verify.py regenerates these certificates, runs every positive and negative
case through the standalone checker under ordinary and optimized Python,
and records whole-module normalized source metrics. Finite certificates and
tests are supporting evidence, not a formal proof of the implementation or
of the universal bi-interpretability theorem.
