\begin{abstract}
We inspect the current \texttt{ConciseIPRA} proposals at commit
\texttt{62396b06020f} (full hash recorded below).
The published coordinatewise envelope of complete compact checkers is
256 significant Python tokens, 278 AST nodes, and 516 normalized bytes.
Three delivered programs lower the corresponding coordinates to
\textbf{253 tokens, 275 AST nodes, and 515 bytes}.  The first two infer
an elimination's major premise when its target instance matches the goal;
the third uses the divisor pattern of the three meaningful formula arities.
We give successful-trace and canonical-completeness arguments, exact
controls, a readable strict checker, and reproducible executable evidence.

These are three different programs implementing the inherited arithmetic
presentation.  No single delivered program is below all three old minima,
no mathematical axiom deletion is claimed, and the figures do not prove
absolute minimality.  The compact input contract remains explicit.
Separately, we prove a mathematical obstruction: if existential witnesses
are restricted to parameters and zero, specialization by any fixed finite
menu of primitive recursive operations, even under arbitrary composition,
does not recover the native consequences.  A proper cut in a nonstandard
model supplies a closed, guarded counterexample.  This explains why two
separately complete restrictions in the current corpus cannot simply be
combined.
\end{abstract}

\section{The arithmetic and the complete logical interface}
\label{sec:calculus}

\subsection{The target theory}

The target is full first-order \emph{intuitionistic} primitive recursive
arithmetic: intuitionistic predicate logic with equality, symbols and
defining equations for each finite primitive recursive description,
successor injectivity and separation from zero, and induction for
quantifier-free formulas, with passive parameters.  It is not merely
equational PRA, its negative translation, or the set of its true numerical
equations.

We retain the scalar language already justified in the repository:
\[
 t ::= x\mid 0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,a;u.h),
 \qquad
 A ::= (s=t)\mid \Q_y A\mid\D_{x,y}A.
\]
Only $u$ is bound in $h$ in an iterator.  The bound variables of $\D$
are distinct.  All substitutions are capture avoiding.  Write $1=S0$ and
\[
 \Sel(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b)
\]
with unused $u$.  The ordinary meanings of the two binders are
\begin{align*}
 (\Q_y B)^\circ&=B^\circ(0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(y),\\
 (\D_{x,y}B)^\circ&=\exists x\bigl(B^\circ(x,0)
                     \to\forall y\,B^\circ(x,y)\bigr).
\end{align*}
The designated native contradiction is $0=1$.

\subsection{A complete native reference basis}

For clarity, the following is the native reference calculus.  No ambient
classical logical rules are tacitly available.  Contexts retain formulas;
a displayed local assumption is discharged.

\begin{tabular}{p{0.14\linewidth}p{0.79\linewidth}}
\toprule

Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, congruence, explosion, numerical cases,
quantifier-free induction, and the usual intuitionistic connectives are
derived.  For example, if $r=I(0,t;u.0)$, transporting the computation
$r=t$ along itself with template $v=t$ gives $t=t$.  The fact that a
designated equation serves as contradiction is justified by derived
explosion and the falsity bridge in Section~\ref{sec:bridge}; it is not a
license to omit ordinary falsity from the target theory.

\subsection{The smaller guarded presentation actually encoded}

The compact checkers use an auxiliary calculus on the same terms and
Eq/$\Q$/$\D$ formulas.  Its public formulas use Eq and $\D$ only, and every
immediate equation matrix of a $\D_{x,y}$ must have the literal form
\[
 I(y,0;u.h)=I(y,0;u.k).
 \tag{G}\label{eq:guard}
\]
The bodies may depend on passive parameters and on the displayed variables.
Auxiliary formulas inside proofs need not all satisfy the public guard.
This public/auxiliary distinction is inherited, and is essential.

Replace the last two reference introduction schemes by the one
root-determined scheme
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta_B(z))\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash\D_{x,y}B},
 \qquad
 \eta_B(z)=\begin{cases}
 z,&B\text{ is a literal equation},\\
 0,&B\text{ is compound}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{Intro}
\]
All eigenvariable conditions remain.  Restrict the source of transport to
terms other than the literal $0$.  Combine transport and closure use into
the following single interface.  Given a template $F$, a source $s$ and a
target $t$, the required major is
\[
 M(s,t,F)=\begin{cases}s=t,&s\ne 0\text{ syntactically},\\
                         \Q_vF(v),&s=0\text{ syntactically}.
          \end{cases}
\]
The inference is
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash M(s,t,F)\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
      {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}.\tag{Use/transport}
\]
Together with hypothesis, the two computation schemas, paired opening,
and Intro, this specifies the six inference families being encoded.
There is no unlisted induction principle for compound formulas.

At an atomic public matrix, the zero guard supplies its base, so progress
suffices.  Equations elsewhere can be padded by
\[
 L(s=t)=\D_{r,v}\bigl(\Sel(v,0,s)=\Sel(v,0,t)\bigr),
 \qquad r\text{ unused}.
\]
One proves $L(E)\dashv\vdash E$ by introducing its successor row from $E$,
and conversely opening it and using the closure at $1$.  Padding permits
numerical cases on equations through compound case introduction, and then
recovers equation induction.  The source-zero restriction has an inherited
admissibility proof, reproduced among the attributed foundation files.
In particular, this restriction is not justified merely by saying that
zero is ``another variable.''

For a completely guarded translation, put
\[
 p(s=t)=L(s=t),\quad
 p(\Q_yB)=\D_{r,y}p(B),\quad
 p(\D_{x,y}B)=\D_{x,y}p(B),
\]
with fresh unused $r$.  The inherited guarded presentation theorem gives
the same translated native consequences, with both formula round trips.
The compact codecs use administrative zero-instance supply for computation;
they are certificate implementations of this consequence relation, not
additional arithmetic axiom schemas.  The full decoder and its checking
proof are included as attributed reference material~\cite{arity}.

\section{Why the interpretation is a bi-interpretation}
\label{sec:bridge}

This section states the inherited bridge precisely enough to expose what
the new checker changes depend on.  The full arithmetic constructions are
included under \texttt{reference/foundations/}; the new work does not claim
to shorten those constructions or to reverify them in Lean.

Elaborate each fixed iterator body $h(u,\vec p)$ into a staged first-order
function symbol $i_h(n,a,\vec p)$, with its two defining equations.
There is no internal universal evaluator for arbitrary function codes.
Let $T_I$ use ordinary intuitionistic first-order logic with actual equality
and genuine falsity, these equations, contextual induction for equations,
and
\[
 \forall x\,(x=0\vee\exists y\,x=Sy),
 \qquad (0=S0)\to\bot.
\]
The last sentence is explicit: a theory of positive equations alone would
admit a singleton structure, so it would not provide the claimed ordinary
interpretation.

The arithmetic dependency order is as follows.  From scalar iteration,
equation induction, and numerical cases one constructs a scalar predecessor
$P$ with $P0=0$ and $P(Sn)=n$.  Next come truncated subtraction and a
characteristic of actual equality on the whole domain.  These compile fixed
quantifier-free formulas into characteristic equations and recover all
quantifier-free induction instances.  Pairing and projections can then be
constructed and used to compile general primitive recursion.  Pairing is
not assumed in the predecessor proof.

For example, with a proved pairing $\pi$ and projections $L,H$, the
primitive recursion
\[
 f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p)
\]
is compiled using
\begin{align*}
 V(n,\vec p)&=I\bigl(n,\pi(0,g^*(\vec p));
     u.\pi(SL(u),h^*(L(u),H(u),\vec p))\bigr),\\
 f^*(n,\vec p)&=H(V(n,\vec p)).
\end{align*}
Equation induction proves $L(V(n,\vec p))=n$ and the required defining
equations.  The other interpretation sends each fixed scalar iterator to
ordinary primitive recursion ignoring the recursion-counter argument.

For the composites, internal equation induction proves uniqueness of two
iterations with the same seed and extensionally equal updates.  External
induction on each finite description therefore proves that its double
translation agrees with the original operation.  Both numerical domains
are the whole domain, defined by $x=x$, equality remains actual equality,
and the comparison isomorphism in each theory has graph $x=y$.  The
ordinary meanings of $\D,\Q$ and the derived logical macros supply the
formula composites.  Thus the bridge is a bi-interpretation, not an appeal
to standard-model agreement or to equal proof-theoretic strength alone.

\begin{proposition}[Inherited bridge preserved by the new programs]
On the declared canonical guarded public language, each new checker
accepts exactly the same theorem set as its matched control.  Consequently
its ordinary realization is bi-interpretable and equiconsistent with full
first-order $\ipra$, with the same whole-domain identity composites.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Sections~\ref{sec:inferred} and~\ref{sec:remainder} establish soundness and
canonical completeness relative to the matched controls.  Compose these
proof translations with the inherited checking, guarded presentation,
and arithmetic bridge theorems.  No arithmetic interpretation is changed.
\end{proof}

\section{The compact representation and its limits}

Certificates are JSON arrays consumed from right to left.  The last element
is the goal; each remaining consumed instruction has four fields
$[m,s,u,t]$.  The fields are not a new numerical domain.  They encode a
finite proof and its term/formula templates.  The meaning depends on the
position, numerical or formula, in which an array is used.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cll}
\toprule
Arity & Numerical position & Formula position\\
\midrule
0 & $0$ & $\top$\\
1 & successor & $\D$\\
2 & ignored annotation, meaning $0$ & equality\\
3 & scalar iterator & $\Q$ (third child is the body)\\
$\ge4$ & ignored annotation, meaning $0$ & $\top$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Canonical closures are $[B,B,B]$; tripling a unary paired major produces
its closure, whereas tripling another meaningful nonempty arity produces
an administrative truth.  Here $\top$ is the fixed equation $0=0$.
Scalar formula padding also denotes $\top$.  These are fixed semantic
aliases, not arbitrary additional connectives.

The opener $i(a,t,d)$ adds $5-\operatorname{len}(a)$ to its depth at every
array.  Meaningful increments are therefore $4,3,2$ for arities $1,2,3$.
Numerical root depth is $3$, formula root depth is $0$, and protected
numerical slots have width two.  Under $p$ protected slots, coordinates
$3+2j$ and $4+2j$ name slot $j$ for $0\le j<p$; the coordinates $0,1,2$
denote zero.  Canonical terms choose the odd representative of each slot.
Negative integers admitted by the floor $k$ denote rigid free parameters.
A numerical hole is filled only after its image is checked at numerical
root depth $3$.  Missing images at used holes reject.

The scope floor decreases immediately before the continuation's goal and
new hypothesis are opened.  This ordering is retained.  Although in a
local profile $k=1-|\Gamma|$ on entry, it is false during that intermediate
phase with the old context length.  Replacing the state by context length
without repairing the phase would reject valid certificates and can spoil
freshness reasoning.  We do not make that change.

The compact acceptance protocol is successful process exit with exactly
\texttt{ACCEPT} and one line feed.  Exceptions reject, including under
\texttt{python -O}; no trusted check is an \texttt{assert}.  Unused
certificate prefixes are inert.  Public theorem claims are for the
canonical guarded input contract.  Neither the old nor the new compact
profile is presented as an exact theorem recognizer for arbitrary strings,
floats, Boolean leaves, or arbitrary public formula shapes.

\section{Inferring the elimination major}
\label{sec:inferred}

The original packet supplied $m$ even when the source, target, and template
already determined it.  Its relevant code was
\begin{lstlisting}
if m == ([s, u] if s else [t] * 3) and i(t, u) == a:
    a = i(t, s)
\end{lstlisting}
The new token and AST profiles use
\begin{lstlisting}
if i(t, u) == a:
    m = [s, u] if s else [t] * 3
    a = i(t, s)
\end{lstlisting}
The rest of the module still recursively checks the actual major $m$.
Only its redundant supplied spelling is ignored on the matching branch.
This is target-directed premise inference, not omission of a premise.

\begin{theorem}[Successful-trace repair]
For every successful new execution on the inherited compact interface,
there is a repaired packet stream accepted by the old profile.  Its goal
is unchanged.  Repair each consumed packet by replacing its first field
with the value of $m$ actually used after the new test.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On a triggered instruction, the repaired first field is exactly
$[s,u]$ when $s$ is true and $[t,t,t]$ otherwise.  The old major comparison
and its target comparison therefore succeed, and both programs replace
$a$ by $i(t,s)$.  On a non-triggered instruction the first field is left
unchanged; the old conjunction cannot select an elimination because its
target comparison is false whenever evaluated.

Both programs next check the same major, consume the same repaired
substream for that proof, build the same retained hypothesis, and open
the same continuation at the same fresh floor.  Induct on the finite
successful execution tree.  The token profile's global floor is also
identical: constructing the inferred major is pure and occurs before
the recursive major proof.  The local profile preserves the same tuple
contexts.  The repairs are determined by the successful new trace, not
by a guess at which instruction was intended.
\end{proof}

Eager evaluation of $i(t,u)$ may newly reject malformed packets which the
old conjunction short-circuited.  This theorem does not assert equality of
all raw acceptance languages.  It does show that an accepted new proof
cannot acquire an unproved arithmetic major.

\begin{lemma}[No accidental elimination in canonical nonelimination packets]
A canonical numerical tree has no binary array node, whereas every
canonical Eq/$\Q$/$\D$ formula contains a binary equality node.
For numerical $t,u$, a successful formula-root opening $i(t,u)$ has no
binary node.  It therefore cannot equal a canonical formula goal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Canonical numerical arrays have arity one or three.  Opening preserves
those constructors and substitutes only numerical trees at numerical
holes; scalar padding introduces no binary node.  Structural induction
gives the claim.  Every finite canonical formula ends in equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical completeness of inferred majors]
Every canonical derivation supported by the control has a certificate
accepted by the new profile.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compile intended transport and closure-use instructions normally; their
target tests hold and their inferred majors are the required premises.
Computation and introduction packets have numerical $t,u$, so the lemma
prevents unintended selection.  At ordinary paired opening, set the
inactive field $t$ to $0$; its target opening is scalar $0$, which cannot
equal a canonical formula.  Hypothesis leaves need no instruction.
The canonical compiler already establishes the required scope conditions.
Induction on the derivation now completes the construction.
\end{proof}

Constructed elimination majors occur earlier in \texttt{uniform\_zero},
and prevalidation occurs in \texttt{prevalidated\_elimination}~\cite{repo}.
The measured change here is the target-match trigger and its placement
in the two current smallest profiles.  It is not a claim to have invented
premise inference or removed an arithmetic axiom.

\section{The one-byte arity refinement}
\label{sec:remainder}

The 516-byte control selects a fresh progress parameter using
\texttt{\string~len(a) \% 2}.  Replace only that expression by
\texttt{3 \% len(a)}.  The three meaningful formula arities have the table
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
n&1&2&3\\\hline
\mathord{\sim}n\bmod2&0&1&0\\
3\bmod n&0&1&0
\end{array}
\]
so the actual equality/case choice is unchanged.  The new expression
has one fewer significant token, two fewer AST nodes, and one fewer
normalized byte.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness and canonical completeness of the remainder selector]
The 515-byte profile has the same canonical guarded public theorems as
its 516-byte control, under the inherited decoder and input contract.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On arities $1,2,3$ the decision is exactly the displayed table.
At arity zero the new modulo operation raises an exception; this can
only remove traces involving an empty truth alias.  At arities at least
four the formula is the fixed truth alias.  Changing its zero instance
to a fresh-parameter instance cannot create a new meaningful formula:
opening preserves the outer arity and all children are ignored by the
formula decoder.  Introducing a paired truth or retaining a truth
hypothesis is sound in either mode.  For a computation-supply step the
inherited proof uses its known zero instance and the freshness of the
new parameters; it does not require the displayed goal's arity to confer
a new induction rule.  This same supply argument remains valid.

Thus the inherited successful-call soundness induction still applies.
Canonical meaningful goals and introduction matrices have arity $1,2,3$,
so their compiled proofs keep the old mode.  Administrative truth exports
can be retained without using an empty or surplus truth as a nontrivial
pending goal.  The canonical completeness compiler is unchanged.
\end{proof}

There is deliberately no claim of raw trace equality on empty and surplus
aliases.  In particular, applying a progress rule to a meaningful ternary
closure would be wrong.  Both selectors give zero at arity three.

\section{Executable evidence and reproducibility}

The archive contains the three complete compact profiles, exact pinned
controls, a readable strict companion, actual positive and negative
certificates, source provenance, measurement code, and validation code.
Run
\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
python3 reproduce.py
python3 build.py
\end{lstlisting}
from the archive's top directory.  The first command uses only Python's
standard library.  The second requires pdfLaTeX and the packages named
in this source.  CPython 3.12.14 reproduces the exact source measurements;
other Python versions can have different \texttt{ast.unparse} output.

The inference validation compares the local and global scope profiles
against their own controls.  It exercises inherited valid certificates,
future-witness capture and context-leak attacks, and deliberately erased
major fields.  Repaired control certificates are actual files, not just
descriptions of a proposed repair.  Normal and optimized Python runs are
both checked.  The byte validation covers the meaningful arities and
the intentional truth-alias differences.  Exact counts, outputs, and
hashes are in the generated evidence files.

The final reproduction ran 631 separate checker processes without an
unexpected outcome: 423 for inferred-major validation, 152 for the byte
selector, and 56 for the strict companion.  The inference suite includes
31 generated certificates with 7,607 redundant major fields erased and
31 actual repaired control certificates.  The readable strict companion
measures 580 tokens, 666 AST nodes, and 2,473 normalized bytes; it is an
audit interface, not a new strict-checker size record.

These tests are finite executable evidence.  They do not prove universal
soundness, the nonstandard-cut theorem, or bi-interpretability.  Those
claims rest on the written arguments and the explicitly inherited bridge.
No new Lean or Rocq verification is claimed.  The repository's Lean
development concerns the mathematical calculus and its translations; it
does not automatically verify the delivered Python implementations.
Python recursion and memory limits can reject very large valid certificates;
the theoretical checking algorithm is understood with unbounded finite
resources, as are the matched controls.

\section{Further research questions}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Can one complete program, with the same input and output contract,
beat all three old minima simultaneously?  The three separate new
profiles do not answer this question.
\item Can a mathematical reduction lower the best complete strict checker,
including public-language validation, instead of refining the compact
codec?  Any comparison should count normalization, typing, and decoding
that move into the trusted front end.
\item Can a structurally generated infinite family of specialization
targets be simpler than arbitrary terms while defeating the finite-menu
obstruction?  The theorem does not cover an iterator constructor whose
body is supplied freely.
\item Does a useful quotient or different numerical domain evade the
counterreading while retaining both interpretation composites?  Failure
of the identity/native consequence translation alone does not settle
that question.
\item Can the semantic decoder and successful-call soundness theorem for
the compact checker be formalized once, with verified small refinements
as corollaries?  This would give a stronger assurance boundary than testing
each tiny source independently.
\item Can the arithmetic bridge itself be shortened, particularly the
scalar predecessor and formula selector constructions, while retaining
the full intuitionistic formula round trips?  Reducing those mathematical
dependencies would be more substantial than another byte reduction.
\end{enumerate}
