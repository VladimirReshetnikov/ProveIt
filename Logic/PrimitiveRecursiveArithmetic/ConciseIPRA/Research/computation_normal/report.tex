\begin{abstract}
We investigate a simplification of the current ConciseIPRA proposal at a
fixed repository snapshot. There are two separate results.
First, both iterator computation axioms are replaced by computation-normal
syntax and reflexivity. We prove that a six-rule calculus results and give
the round trips required for bi-interpretability with intuitionistic
primitive recursive arithmetic. The main new completeness argument is a
fresh-zero lemma: a locally introduced variable provably equal to zero
replaces the closed zero alias that full normalization destroys.

Second, two existing validation ideas are composed without changing the
arithmetic calculus. The complete checker has 860 normalized source bytes,
377 significant tokens and 433 AST nodes, versus 870, 383 and 442 for its
matched public Eq/Q/D baseline. Maximum-level gzip is 508 bytes, versus
507. The acceptance contract is preserved.

These results do not establish an overall simplest axiomatization.
The repository already contains a six-rule zero-normal presentation and a
516-byte checker with a different arity-envelope interface. Fair comparisons
show that full normalization does not robustly reduce complete checker
size; it also introduces a total-recursive, non-primitive-recursive uniform
normalizer. We recommend the small validation reduction as an
implementation improvement and retain the new calculus as a proved
mathematical alternative, with its costs explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and comparison contract}
\label{sec:baseline}

The requested objective is a simpler theory that is both equiconsistent
and bi-interpretable with full first-order \ipra. Preserving only its
equational fragment would not suffice. We use the size of a complete
proof-checker implementation as the operative complexity criterion, and
record gzip at compression level 9 as an informational measurement.

The baseline is repository commit \repo, which introduces
\code{NativeZeroNormal.lean}. Its UTC commit time is 12 November 2026,
03:01:27, or 11 November in Pacific time. Comparisons are pinned to that
snapshot rather than to a moving branch \cite{repository}.

The snapshot distinguishes the eight-rule native kernel, the seven-rule
single-introduction variant, the adopted seven-rule zero-normal kernel,
and a delivered six-rule zero-normal variant. It also contains much
smaller arity-coded Python checkers. Their validation contracts differ.
The 516-byte compact source is an implementation refinement separate
from its package's alternative recursor theory \cite{canonical}.

\paragraph{Established results.}
The calculus \Six\ below has six rule kinds, with reflexivity in place
of computation equations. Its normalized sequents are exactly those of
the native calculus, and both interpretation composites are provably
the identity. The fresh-zero lemma makes its single introduction
complete despite the loss of closed nonliteral zero aliases.
Independently, deleting a duplicated public-syntax scope check produces
a smaller complete checker for the unchanged existing theory.

\paragraph{Limits of the claim.}
Neither construction is a proof of global minimality. The 860-byte
program does not beat the different 516-byte interface. Full
normalization simplifies the explicit axiom table but does not win
robustly in matched complete-checker measurements. No new Lean or Rocq
formalization is claimed: the extension consists of written proofs,
executable checkers, concrete certificates, and independent review.
The native/\ipra\ bridge is inherited from the pinned repository.

\section{The inherited arithmetic and logic}
\label{sec:inherited}

\subsection{The reference \ipra}

We use the repository's first-order intuitionistic presentation. There
is one numerical sort, actual equality, zero, successor, and one
function symbol for each primitive-recursive definition built from
zero, successor, projections, composition and primitive recursion.
The nonlogical axioms are the defining equations, successor
injectivity, $Sx=0\to\bot$, and induction for quantifier-free formulas,
with parameters and side assumptions. The ambient logic is
intuitionistic first-order natural deduction with equality. The
primitive-recursive function family is an effective schema, not a
hidden finite table.

\subsection{The raw native calculus}

Raw terms are
\[
 t\bnf x\mid0\mid S(t)\mid\I{c}{a}{u.h}.
\]
Only $u$ is bound in $h$, not in the counter $c$ or seed $a$. The
computation equations are
\begin{align}
 \I{0}{a}{u.h}&=a,\label{eq:zero}\\
 \I{Sc}{a}{u.h}&=h[\I{c}{a}{u.h}/u].\label{eq:succ}
\end{align}
Formulas have the grammar
\[
 A\bnf(s=t)\mid Q_yB\mid D_{x,y}B.
\]
The designated contradiction is $0=S0$. Their ordinary intuitionistic
readings are
\begin{align}
 (Q_yB)^\circ&:=B^\circ(0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(y),\label{eq:q}\\
 (D_{x,y}B)^\circ&:=\exists x\,
  \bigl(B^\circ(x,0)\to\forall y\,B^\circ(x,y)\bigr).\label{eq:d}
\end{align}
No classical principle is implicit in these readings.

The raw reference calculus \Raw\ is \code{NPrf}: hypothesis, the two
computation equations, crossed one-term transport, $Q$ elimination,
$D$ elimination, $D$ introduction by cases and $Q$ introduction by
equational progress. Its two introductions are
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)}
 \qquad
 \frac{\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash Q_yE(y)},
\]
where $z$ is fresh and $E$ is a literal equation.

\subsection{The inherited bi-interpretation}

The intermediate ordinary theory $T_{\rm eq}$ uses intuitionistic logic
with equality, the iterator equations, coverage
$\forall x(x=0\lor\exists y\,x=Sy)$, separation $0=S0\to\bot$,
and equation induction with side assumptions. The pinned development
proves that \Raw, $T_{\rm eq}$ and \ipra\ are bi-interpretable, with
whole numerical domains and the identity graph $x=y$ on the composites.

The final results are named as follows:
\begin{quote}\small
Equivalence: \code{native_iff_pra}, \code{pra_iff_native}.\\
Composites: \code{native_roundTrip}, \code{pra_native_roundTrip}.\\
Consistency: \code{native_inconsistent_iff}.
\end{quote}
Their definitions are in \code{NativeBiInterpretation.lean}.
The archive includes that source
for precise attribution; the dependency chain remains in the pinned
repository. We do not replace it with a new unproved representation
assertion.

The bridge derives predecessor, reflection of equality into a term,
quantifier-free induction, pairing, and a compiler for general
primitive recursion. The logical layer defines
$\bot,\to,\land,\lor,\exists,\forall$ by selector and gate formulas and
proves all their intuitionistic natural-deduction rules.
Consequently a faithful new presentation of \Raw\ retains full
first-order intuitionistic arithmetic.

\section{Computation-normal syntax}
\label{sec:normal}

Orient \eqref{eq:zero} and \eqref{eq:succ} from left to right, allowing
reduction everywhere, including under iterator binders.

\begin{definition}
The normal syntax, with scopes understood, is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 n&\bnf x\mid\I{n}{a}{u.h},\\
 a,h&\bnf n\mid0\mid S(a).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first line describes neutral terms. Every iterator counter has a
neutral root, and all subterms are normal. Formula constructors are
unchanged; their numerical terms must be normal.
\end{definition}

For example, $\I{x}{0}{u.0}$ is normal, although its intended value is
always zero. In contrast, $\I{Sx}{0}{u.0}$ normalizes to $0$.
Computation normalization does not decide arithmetic equality.

Write $\nf{t}$ for the normal form. Substitution in the new calculus is
always \emph{normal substitution}:
\[
 t\langle s/x\rangle:=\nf{t[s/x]}.
\]
We use ordinary substitution notation in rules with this convention in
force. Formula normalization changes terms only and preserves the
equation/$Q$/$D$ tree.

\begin{lemma}[External normalization]
Every finite well-scoped raw term has a unique normal form.
Normalization is idempotent, preserves scope, and satisfies
\[
 \nf{t\sigma}=\nf{\nf{t}\,(\nf{\sigma})}.
 \tag{N}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For termination, embed the syntax in G\"odel's System T. All numerical
variables have base type $\mathbb N$, and the iterator maps to
\[
 \operatorname{Rec}_{\mathbb N}
 (a,\lambda k^{\mathbb N}.\lambda u^{\mathbb N}.h,c),
\]
with $k$ fresh and unused. A source zero contraction becomes a
recursor contraction; a successor contraction becomes a recursor
contraction followed by two beta contractions. Translation respects
contexts and capture-avoiding substitution. Thus an infinite source
reduction would give an infinite System-T reduction, contrary to
strong normalization. A primary reference with a mechanized proof is
Urciuoli \cite{urciuoli}. System T is used in the metatheory, not added
to the arithmetic being presented.

For confluence, use parallel reduction. The two left-hand sides have
different constructor patterns and are left-linear. Reductions in
counter, seed and body commute with root contraction after taking
parallel reducts. The duplicated terms in a successor contraction are
reduced in parallel. Induction proves the substitution property
$h\Rightarrow h'$, $r\Rightarrow r'$
implies $h[r/u]\Rightarrow h'[r'/u]$, and then the diamond property
for parallel developments. Termination and confluence yield unique
normal forms. Stability of reduction under substitution proves (N);
idempotence and scope preservation follow.
\end{proof}

This is an external termination proof. It asserts no \ipra-internal
uniform evaluator theorem. Injective variable renaming preserves
neutral roots and normal syntax. In particular, eigenvariable shifts
commute with normalization. Equation (N) supplies the identity and
associativity laws of normal substitution.

\begin{lemma}[Finite conversion soundness in \Raw]
For every individual raw term $t$, \Raw\ proves $t=\nf{t}$.
Every native formula is interderivable with its normalization, over
all its free parameters.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Root reductions are computation axioms; reductions in counter and seed
use ordinary congruence. Reduction in a bound update body needs more
care: the iterator is not an ordinary ternary function of three numbers.

If $h(u)=h'(u)$ is provable with $u$ fresh, raw equation induction on
$c$ proves
$\I{c}{a}{u.h}=\I{c}{a}{u.h'}$.
At zero both sides are $a$. At a successor, unfold, specialize the
pointwise body equality at the recursive value, and use the induction
hypothesis inside the other body. This is the required body-congruence
lemma. Lift and compose the finitely many reduction steps.
The established raw $Q$ and $D$ congruences then lift term equalities
to formula interderivability.
\end{proof}

The body-congruence argument uses induction in the already established
raw calculus. It does not assume the new calculus's target theorem.

\section{The complete six-rule axiomatization}
\label{sec:rules}

Contexts are finite lists of normal formulas, and hypotheses may be
reused. Every substitution below is normal substitution. In
\rulename{DE}, $b$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, $A$, and $B$; in
\rulename{DI}, $z$ is fresh for $\Gamma$, $B$, and the witness $t$. These
six kinds are the entire primitive calculus \Six.

\paragraph{Hypothesis and reflexivity.}
\[
 \frac{A\in\Gamma}{\Gamma\vdash A}\;(\rulename{Hyp}),
 \qquad
 \frac{}{\Gamma\vdash t=t}\;(\rulename{Refl}).
\]
The reflexivity term is any well-scoped normal term.

\paragraph{Crossed transport in one term.}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash h(s)=r}
      {\Gamma\vdash r=h(t)}\;(\rulename{Tr}).
\]
The normal template $h$ has one displayed argument in addition to
ambient parameters; it may use the argument repeatedly or not at all.

\paragraph{Closure elimination.}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash Q_yB(y)\qquad\Gamma\vdash B(0)}
      {\Gamma\vdash B(t)}\;(\rulename{QE}).
\]

\paragraph{Witness elimination.}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)
       \qquad\Gamma,Q_yB(b,y)\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash A}\;(\rulename{DE}),\qquad b\text{ fresh}.
\]

\paragraph{Single witness introduction.}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)}\;(\rulename{DI}),\qquad z\text{ fresh},
\]
where
\[
 \eta=
 \begin{cases}
 z,& B\text{ is literally an equation and }t\text{ is literally }0,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The guard is syntactic, not a test whether $t$ is provably zero.

There are no other primitive logical axioms, equality laws, successor
axioms, induction schemas, structural rules, or computation equations.
The computation clauses belong to the \emph{specified substitution
algorithm}; its implementation is fully counted. Arithmetic computation
has not disappeared from the trusted machinery.

\subsection{Ordinary soundness of introduction}

In case mode, the premise gives $B(t,0)\to B(t,Sz)$ for arbitrary $z$.
Coverage, using $B(t,0)$ in the zero case, yields
$B(t,0)\to\forall yB(t,y)$; existential introduction gives the reading
of $D$. In progress mode the matrix is a literal equation of
primitive-recursive terms, so quantifier-free induction gives exactly
the needed closure. There is no induction on arbitrary compound
$Q/D$ formulas.

\section{The fresh-zero completeness lemma}
\label{sec:freshzero}

\begin{lemma}
Weakening, injective renaming and cut are admissible for \Six,
without first assuming arbitrary numerical substitution.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use induction on derivations. Injective renaming preserves normality,
literal zero, the matrix root, and freshness. For cut, under each
eigenvariable rule first rename and weaken the inserted proof.
No guard depends on membership or multiplicity of context formulas,
so the ordinary substitution of derivations for hypotheses applies.
\end{proof}

Arbitrary numerical substitution can turn a syntactically nonzero
witness into literal zero, changing the selected premise. A direct
induction claiming substitution admissibility for the single
introduction would therefore be invalid. We derive it later through
a stable equivalent basis.

\subsection{Atomic equality reasoning}

Symmetry of $s=t$ is one \rulename{Tr} step with major $s=s$, identity
template and second premise $s=t$. Congruence uses major $s=t$ and
second premise $h(s)=h(s)$. For transitivity, reverse $s=t$ to $t=s$
and transport it along $t=u$, obtaining $s=u$.

Two applications give two-sided atomic transport:
from $s=t$ and $a(s)=b(s)$ derive $b(s)=a(t)$ and then
$a(t)=b(t)$. Equation (N) ensures the normalized template instances
match exactly.

\subsection{The lost zero alias}

The old zero-normal six-rule argument can use
$\I{S0}{0}{u.0}$ as a closed, nonliteral term provably zero.
Full normalization erases that distinction. Indeed every closed
full-normal term is a numeral: following the counter chain of a
neutral term would otherwise eventually require a free variable.

\begin{lemma}[A fresh name for zero]
\label{lem:namedzero}
Put $Z(x,y)\equiv\I{y}{0}{u.x}=0$. Then
$\Six\vdash D_{x,y}Z(x,y)$. In its \rulename{DE} scope,
the fresh witness $b$ satisfies $b=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Introduce $D Z$ at witness $0$. The matrix is an equation, so the rule
selects progress mode. Its successor target normalizes to $0=0$,
proved by reflexivity without using the progress hypothesis.

In the elimination scope assume $Q_yZ(b,y)$. The base $Z(b,0)$
normalizes to $0=0$. Use reflexivity for that base and \rulename{QE}
at $S0$. The result $Z(b,S0)$ normalizes to $b=0$.
\end{proof}

The local witness is a variable, despite being provably zero. It is
discharged before the final conclusion; no permanent constant or
additional domain element has been introduced.

\subsection{Recover the omitted atomic case}

Suppose $E(x,y)$ is an equation and a proof is given of
\[
 \Gamma,E(0,0)\vdash E(0,Sz),\qquad z\text{ fresh}.
 \tag{C0}
\]
The desired $D_{x,y}E(x,y)$ is not a primitive case introduction at
witness zero, because the guard would demand progress.

Open Lemma~\ref{lem:namedzero}, obtaining fresh $b$ and $b=0$.
Under $E(b,0)$, atomic transport gives $E(0,0)$.
Cut into (C0), then transport the resulting $E(0,Sz)$ back along
$0=b$. This gives $E(b,Sz)$.
Apply primitive \rulename{DI} at witness $b$, where case mode is
selected. Its conclusion $D E$ and the context do not mention $b$,
so \rulename{DE} closes the fresh-zero scope.

Only atomic transport, weakening, injective renaming and cut have
been used. Full formula transport and arbitrary term substitution
were not premises of this recovery.

\section{Equivalence and both interpretation composites}
\label{sec:equivalence}

Let \Seven\ have the same normal syntax and five shared rules, but
replace the single \rulename{DI} by unrestricted $D$ introduction by
cases and $Q$ introduction by equational progress:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,0)\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)}
 \qquad
 \frac{\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash Q_yE(y)}.
\]
The equation restriction and freshness conditions remain in force.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:sixseven}
\Six\ and \Seven\ derive the same normal sequents.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All case instances except the equation/witness-zero case are already
primitive in \Six; Section~\ref{sec:freshzero} derives that last case.
For progress, add a dummy witness $x$ to $E(y)$ and apply
\rulename{DI} at zero. Eliminate $D_{x,y}E(y)$; the local hypothesis
$Q_yE(y)$ gives the conclusion.

Conversely, case-mode \rulename{DI} is the case rule of \Seven.
For progress-mode \rulename{DI}, use equational progress to obtain
$Q_yE(0,y)$. Under $E(0,0)$, \rulename{QE} gives $E(0,Sz)$.
The case rule at witness zero gives $D_{x,y}E(x,y)$.
\end{proof}

Every \Seven\ rule is stable under arbitrary normal substitution.
Its substitution induction is valid and transfers the admissibility
result to \Six\ through this equivalence.

\begin{theorem}[Faithfulness to the raw kernel]
\label{thm:faith}
For every raw native context $\Gamma$ and formula $A$,
\[
 \Gamma\vdash_{\Raw}A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \nf{\Gamma}\vdash_{\Six}\nf{A}.
 \tag{F}
\]
The translations preserve free variables.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the forward direction normalize every formula in a raw derivation,
translating into \Seven. Both computation equations become reflexivity.
Transport, closure elimination and the two introductions commute with
normal substitution by (N). Witness elimination commutes with fresh
renaming. Induction on the raw proof gives the result in \Seven,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:sixseven} gives \Six.

For the converse first translate \Six\ into \Seven. Include each
normal term as a raw expression. Reflexivity is a raw theorem.
Each normalized substitution instance is raw-provably equal or
interderivable with the corresponding raw instance, by finite conversion
soundness. The raw calculus's already established equality and formula
transport then realize \rulename{Tr}, \rulename{QE} and the case rule.
Raw equational induction realizes progress; witness elimination is
unchanged up to renaming. The resulting raw proof of $\nf{A}$ from
$\nf{\Gamma}$ converts back to the original sequent using the formula
normalization equivalences.
\end{proof}

The reverse translation uses properties of the \emph{raw} calculus that
precede the new result. This is not an appeal to the target theorem
inside its own proof.

\subsection{The round trips contain more than theorem preservation}

Let $N$ normalize raw syntax and let $J$ include normal syntax into raw
syntax. For a normal formula $A$, $N(J(A))=A$ literally.
For each raw term $t$, the raw theory proves $J(N(t))=t$; for each
raw formula it proves the corresponding interderivability over all
free parameters.

Compose $J$ with the existing native-to-\ipra\ interpretation, and
compose the existing \ipra-to-native interpretation with $N$.
The inherited round trips and the normalization round trips give:

\begin{corollary}
The ordinary realization of \Six\ is bi-interpretable with \ipra.
Both numerical domains are whole, equality is actual equality, and
each composite domain isomorphism is defined by $x=y$.
\end{corollary}

The normal language is a computational presentation: terms are
canonical representatives and substitution computes representatives.
Its ordinary realization reads them as raw iterator terms. This does
not change the domain into codes for standard numbers or discard
nonstandard model elements.

\begin{corollary}[Equiconsistency]
\[
 \Six\vdash0=S0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\ipra\vdash\bot.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The contradiction is already normal. Apply (F), then use the inherited
consistency theorem to identify raw contradiction with
\ipra's contradiction.
\end{proof}

These are ordinary external mathematical statements. They do not
silently assert a primitive-recursive uniform proof translator or an
\ipra-internal normalization theorem.

\section{The cost of full normalization}
\label{sec:cost}

\begin{proposition}
A uniform full normalizer over codes for all iterator terms cannot
be primitive recursive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose it were. Enumerate finite well-formed unary iterator terms,
replacing malformed descriptions by the constant-zero program.
Syntax checking, forming a closed instance at a numeral, and reading
the final numeral are primitive-recursive operations.
The inherited representation theorem says these terms represent
every primitive-recursive unary function.
The assumed normalizer would therefore give a primitive-recursive
universal evaluator $U(e,n)$ for those functions.

Then $d(n)=U(n,n)+1$ would itself have a program code $e_d$.
At $n=e_d$ this gives
$U(e_d,e_d)=U(e_d,e_d)+1$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Each fixed term still defines a primitive-recursive function. The
obstruction is to a single uniform evaluator over all descriptions.
This is why eliminating a computation rule does not eliminate its
cost from the trusted implementation.

The mathematical unbounded checker is total recursive on well-formed
syntax. Like the baseline recursive Python programs, physical execution
is subject to memory and recursion limits. Source-size measurements do
not imply efficient checking. Every closed full-normal term is an
expanded numeral, so short closed expressions can have enormous
normal forms.

\subsection{A fuelled certificate interface}

The archive includes a bounded version. A certificate supplies a
nonnegative integer fuel, and each successor contraction spends one
unit. Exhaustion rejects. Zero contractions and the intervening walks
are structural.

The corresponding abstract verification relation is primitive
recursive: a bound on nonstructural contractions yields
primitive-recursive bounds on intermediate syntax growth and structural
work. Every successful unbounded proof has a finite execution and
therefore some sufficient fuel. Existentially quantifying supplied
fuel preserves the external theorem set. The extra checking code is
included in its measured size.

\section{The smaller checker for the existing theory}
\label{sec:validator}

The directly recommended implementation change is independent of
\Six. It preserves the existing arithmetic and logical calculus,
proof packets, and kernel function \code{c}.
The matched file is
\begin{center}\small
\code{validated_presentation/code/native_typed_full.py}.
\end{center}
Only its public syntax validator \code{v} changes.

\subsection{Exact input and acceptance contract}

The input is a JSON array. The final element is the closed public goal;
earlier elements are proof packets consumed from the end. An unused
prefix is allowed. Acceptance means exit status zero and exactly
\code{ACCEPT} followed by a newline.

A lexical gate checks the \emph{entire} certificate for integer/list
data. It rejects booleans, floats, strings, nulls and mappings, even in
an unused prefix. The public validator checks canonical Eq/Q/D goal
syntax. Intermediate resources use the inherited arity-envelope
interpretation. Thus this contract is not the tagged typing of every
packet field used by the readable checker in the earlier sections.

Before reading any proof packet, the kernel performs an exact scope
traversal. The old public validator separately checks an upper bound
on each variable slot. That second copy of the upper-bound test is
redundant.

\subsection{The invariant justifying the deletion}

The old validator carries integer depth $d$. In formula mode, arity
two changes to term mode, while arities one and three preserve formula
mode. In term mode, valid arities one and three preserve term mode.
The new validator needs only the Boolean $\delta=d\bmod2$.
Its update is \code{d or len(a) == 2}.

The old scalar check allows zero or an odd slot satisfying $2<a<d$.
The new check allows zero or an odd slot satisfying $a>2$.
Parity and the lower bound remain. The upper bound is delegated to the
kernel's unavoidable initial scope opener.

\begin{theorem}[Same successful traces]
The 860-byte checker and its 870-byte baseline accept exactly the
same certificates, under their common mathematical execution semantics.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On integer/list data, induction on shape shows that Boolean mode
equals the old depth parity wherever shape is valid. All old checks
remain except the scalar upper bound. Hence every old-valid goal
is new-valid.

Suppose a newly admitted goal has an out-of-scope slot. Before any
packet is consumed, the unchanged kernel calls its opener \code{i(a)}.
At that slot the condition \code{k <= a < d} fails, and the opener
attempts to use its absent substitution argument. In the initial call
that argument is the function \code{c}, not an integer/list term;
the attempted term operation raises an exception. No proof packet
can prevent or repair this failure.

Therefore every newly admitted goal violating the old bound is still
rejected before proof processing. All other inputs execute the same
kernel with the same packets. The lexical gate, arity and parity
checks, context rules and acceptance output are unchanged.
\end{proof}

This composes ideas already present in \code{scope_kernel} and
\code{validated_presentation}. It is an implementation improvement
for an unchanged axiomatization, not a new arithmetic axiom deletion.

\section{Measurements and executable verification}
\label{sec:metrics}

\subsection{Whole-source measurement}

Gzip is \code{gzip.compress(source, compresslevel=9, mtime=0)},

\subsection{The initially apparent full-normal saving does not survive}

The original readable zero-normal checker is 3,095 normalized bytes.
A full-normal version with a term-shaped reflexivity certificate is
3,048, an apparent 47-byte gain. But the old computation certificate
can equally be represented by its iterator term. That change reduces
the old checker to 3,029 bytes without changing its calculus.
The matched full-normal checker with a neutral-counter test is
3,044 bytes: fifteen bytes and one token larger, although three AST
nodes and seven gzip bytes smaller.

A second comparison uses goal-directed programs. Both share an empty
leaf certificate and inspect the supplied validated goal.
The zero-normal program is 2,982 bytes, full-normal 3,063, and
fuelled full-normal 3,189. Thus the simpler explicit axiom inventory
does not by itself give a smaller complete checker.

The negative comparison is part of the result: certificate ergonomics
must not be advertised as a mathematical breakthrough.

\subsection{Verification performed}

The recommended validator change was checked on 68 fixtures through
both old and new programs, under ordinary and optimized Python:
272 complete process runs, all matching the expected outcomes.
A separate 9,000-case comparison covers canonical, malformed and
out-of-scope preflights, with no difference.

For full normalization the suite checks a closed fresh-zero certificate
and a recovered atomic case-at-zero proof that uses its hypothesis.
The latter has context $a=b$ and matrix
$\I{y}{a}{u.b}=\I{y}{x}{u.0}$.
It also performs 1,000 small normalization, semantic, scope,
idempotence and substitution checks with fixed random seed, plus
rejections of a false equation, nonnormal goal, Boolean hypothesis
index and escaped variable. Goal-directed translations and both
sufficient-fuel acceptance and zero-fuel rejection are checked.

These tests exercise substantive failure boundaries; they are not
proof-assistant verification. The general mathematical arguments and
their finite executable evidence are reported separately.

\section{Recommendation}

Retain the current arithmetic presentation for immediate repository
use, and adopt the 860-byte validator composition if its canonical
public Eq/Q/D contract is desired. Its benefit is modest and measured,
and no new arithmetic soundness theorem is needed.

The full-normal calculus is a useful research alternative.
Its explicit equational axiom is simple, and the fresh-zero lemma
solves a real completeness problem. Its costs are also clear:
matched total checker size does not uniformly improve, normalization
can be enormous, and the uniform evaluator is not primitive
recursive. It should not replace the structural zero-normal kernel
solely because its arithmetic rule table looks shorter.

A valuable next formal step is to define full conversion with finite
witnesses, prove normalized substitution laws, formalize the fresh-zero
and omitted-case derivations, and connect them to
\code{native_iff_pra}. This report does not claim that this Lean
development has already been completed.

\section{Tagged certificate format}

The readable candidate uses the following JSON terms and formulas:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Object & Encoding\\
\midrule
Variable & \code{["v", i]}\\
Zero & \code{["z"]}\\
Successor & \code{["s", t]}\\
Iterator & \code{["i", c, a, h]}\\
Equation & \code{["=", s, t]}\\
Closure & \code{["q", B]}\\
Paired binder & \code{["d", B]}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In an iterator body, de Bruijn index zero is the accumulator.
In a $Q$ body it is the bound variable.
In a $D_{x,y}$ body, index zero is $y$ and index one is $x$.

A certificate has exactly \code{variables}, \code{context},
\code{goal} and \code{proof}; the bounded variant also has \code{fuel}.
The first field is the ambient scope size.
A normal term itself is its reflexivity certificate.
Other nodes are
\code{["hyp", i]}, \code{["tr", h, p, q]},
\code{["qe", t, p, q]}, \code{["de", A, p, q]}, and
\code{["di", B, t, p]}.
The context, goal, templates, witnesses and used embedded
term/formula fields are explicitly checked.
