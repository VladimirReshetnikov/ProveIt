\begin{abstract}
Both equations of scalar iteration can be made definitional by using full
computation normal forms and hereditary substitution. The resulting presentation
of full first-order intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic has six
inference patterns: hypothesis, reflexivity, crossed one-term equality transport,
closure elimination, witnessed-closure elimination, and one introduction rule.
There is no separate computation-equation axiom. We prove strong normalization,
confluence, coherent substitution, exact consequence equivalence with the
existing native calculus, and both interpretation composites.

The main new completeness argument replaces a numerical zero alias with a
compound formula envelope. It recovers the atomic case-introduction instance
that would otherwise be lost when normalization changes a witness to literal
zero. This proof does not require a nonliteral term equal to zero.

A complete tagged checker uses 2,529 normalized Python bytes, against 2,532
for a matched zero-only-normalizing control and 2,569 for a matched raw control.
The 3-byte improvement over the closest control is narrow: token and AST counts
increase, and the repository's separate compact arity codec remains much smaller.
This is a local presentation and source-byte reduction, not a proof of an
absolutely simplest theory or a simultaneous improvement of every complexity
coordinate. Uniform normalization is total recursive but not primitive
recursive; its code is included in the measured kernel. The new universal
results are written mathematical proofs, supported by executable translations
and finite tests, rather than a new Lean or Rocq formalization.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Attribution and the inherited arithmetic foundations}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Attribution and the inherited arithmetic foundations}
The five sections that follow reproduce the mathematical source files in
\file{Research/certified_zero/reference/} of the pinned repository snapshot.
They are included so that the scalar arithmetic and its full first-order
interpretation bridge can be checked without fetching external material.
Their wording, including phrases such as \emph{new result} or \emph{this paper},
belongs to those earlier reports, not to the novelty claim of the present
construction. The corresponding local files are byte-preserved, and
\file{PROVENANCE.json} records their hashes and exact source URLs.
The source repository's MIT No Attribution license is included.

The present contribution is full normalization of \emph{both} computations,
the compound-envelope repair, the resulting equivalence theorem, and the
complete matched implementations. The predecessor construction, reflection
of equality, primitive-recursion compiler, logical macros, and original
native/iPRA bridge are inherited and explicitly credited~\cite{basis,bridge,erasure,forward,progress}.
