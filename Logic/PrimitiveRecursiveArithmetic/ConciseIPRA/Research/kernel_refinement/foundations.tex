\begin{center}
{\LARGE\bfseries Inherited Mathematical Foundations}\\[8pt]
{\large Companion to \emph{A Smaller Checked Presentation of iPRA}}\\[9pt]
Attributed source compilation, November 11, 2026
\end{center}
\noindent This supplement compiles the mathematical chain inherited by the
smaller checkers. The first part is reproduced from the mathematical
sections of the incoming guarded-source report. The appendices reproduce
the earlier guarded and scalar foundations supplied with it. These are
\emph{not new mathematical results of the present package}. Historical
phrases such as ``new'', ``current'', or ``proposed'' inside the reproduced
sections refer to their original reports.

The source snapshot is ProveIt commit
\texttt{a4e5e96c456303cf231eab2aeeb3291d8889833f}, directory
\file{Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA}; the immediate source
is the archive \file{ipra_guarded_source_2026-11-11.zip} in
\file{docs/incoming/}. Its full original report source is preserved as
\file{reference/inherited_report.tex}. Every reproduced foundation module
is preserved in \file{reference/foundations/}. The repository's MIT No
Attribution license is included. Citation aliases in this compilation
identify the inherited reports and do not assert an independent literature
priority for their contents.

The new checker changes and their finite tests are in the main report.
This supplement is written mathematics. It is not a fresh build of the
upstream Lean development or a formal verification of the new Python
modules.
The arity appendix describes its original predecessor protocol, including
the old introduction-choice convention. Its theorem is used together with
the guarded composition and the main report's preservation arguments;
it is not presented as a literal description of every new checker line.
In particular, the main report explains why computation-supply
specialization is performed after translation into ordinary iPRA.
