\large Removing the introduction witness without a naming exception}

\begin{abstract}
The native scalar calculus in the current ConciseIPRA proposal introduces
$D_{x,y}B$ by supplying an arbitrary numerical term as witness. That choice
can be removed. Always use the newest free numerical parameter, and use
$0$ only when the numerical scope is empty. Keep the separate progress
rule for literal equations. The resulting raw calculus proves exactly the
same open sequents as the existing kernel, with the identity translation
on terms and formulas. Consequently its ordinary realization remains
equiconsistent and bi-interpretable with full first-order iPRA, with both
numerical domains whole and both comparison graphs $x=y$.

The key construction obtains a fresh name for any term through numerical
cases and successor injectivity. No special naming-pattern test and no
normalizer is needed. A careful proof uses procedures that rebuild proofs
under additional numerical parameters; it does not assume the structural
closure it is trying to establish. The same reduction applies to the
guarded clause calculus. Its matched complete strict checker has
1,070 significant Python tokens, 1,243 AST nodes, and 3,425 normalized
bytes, versus 1,078/1,252/3,442 for the unrestricted control and
1,159/1,339/3,688 for the previous deterministic proposal.

This is a proved local simplification, not a theorem of absolute minimality.
The new equivalence is a written proof. The archive includes executable
checkers, checked symbolic proof packets, arithmetic checks, and the
reproducible comparison; it does not claim a new Lean verification.
\end{abstract}

\section{The baseline and the precise improvement}

The comparison is pinned to the ConciseIPRA directory at commit
\texttt{8fa5ade009}, retrieved on
12 November 2026~\cite{repository}. Its README lists eighty-four research
packages. The native eight-rule calculus is defined by \texttt{NPrf};
the existing Lean development proves its equivalence and formula round
trips with iPRA~\cite{native,biinterp}. The README's Further question 2
asks whether case-introduction witnesses restricted to $0$ and variables
are sufficient. The present theorem answers that question affirmatively
and eliminates even the choice between available variables.

The improvement is an actual restriction of a rule on unchanged raw
syntax. The terms supplied as introduction witnesses previously ranged
over all finite scalar terms. In the proposed rule there is no supplied
witness, no witness-shape check, and no naming-pattern exception. The
scope alone determines the instance. The proof checker still checks
capture avoidance, scopes, formula grammar, every premise, and the final
conclusion. No computation or proof search has been hidden in a trusted
helper.

The distinction between mathematical simplicity and source-code
compression is essential. Some existing compact codecs are much shorter
than the strict implementations compared here. We do not claim to beat
their unrelated global byte records, or to prove that a simpler theory
cannot exist. The measured reduction concerns the same strict grammar,
certificate format, error handling, and arithmetic basis. The mathematical
contribution is the elimination of a previously required witness choice.

\section{A complete raw axiomatization}
\label{sec:axioms}

\subsection{Terms, formulas, and numerical scopes}

There is one numerical sort. Terms are the finite expressions
\[
 t ::= x\mid 0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]
Only $u$ is bound in $h$; the counter $n$ and initializer $a$ are outside
its scope. Other variables of $h$ are passive numerical parameters. An
iterator is a finite term description, not a code passed to an
object-language universal evaluator. Terms are raw syntax: no equation
is built into syntactic equality and there is no normalization convention.

Formulas are
\[
 A ::= (s=t)\mid Q_yA\mid D_{x,y}A.
\]
The two variables bound by $D$ are distinct. Their ordinary readings are
\begin{align*}
 Q_yB(y)&\;:=\;B(0)\to\forall y\,B(y),\\
 D_{x,y}B(x,y)&\;:=\;\exists x\bigl(B(x,0)\to\forall y\,B(x,y)\bigr).
\end{align*}
These readings explain the interpretation; ordinary connectives and
quantifiers are not additional primitives of the native proof language.
The designated native contradiction is $0=S0$.

A judgment has an ordered numerical parameter environment
$\Pi=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$, a finite list $\Gamma$ of formulas in that
environment, and a conclusion $A$. Write $\Pi;\Gamma\vdash A$.
The order is part of the proof state; unused parameters are permitted.
Define
\[
 \ell(\Pi)=\begin{cases}a_n,&n>0,\\0,&n=0.\end{cases}
\]
A newly introduced eigenparameter is appended to $\Pi$. All substitution
is capture avoiding. Bound variables are freshened when needed. Below,
$\Pi$ is suppressed except where it determines a witness.

\subsection{Exactly eight primitive rule families}

The following is the entire primitive inventory. Computation equations
are axiom schemas and may be used in any context.

\paragraph{1. Hypothesis.}
If $A$ occurs in $\Gamma$, then $\Gamma\vdash A$.

\paragraph{2--3. Iterator computation.}
For every well-scoped $n,a,h$,
\[
 \Gamma\vdash I(0,a;u.h)=a,
 \qquad
 \Gamma\vdash I(Sn,a;u.h)=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u].
 \tag{I0, IS}
\]

\paragraph{4. Crossed transport in one term.}
For any numerical term template $h(v)$,
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash h(s)=r}
      {\Gamma\vdash r=h(t)}.
 \tag{Tr1}
\]

\paragraph{5. Closure elimination.}
For any term $t$,
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash Q_yB(y)\qquad\Gamma\vdash B(0)}
      {\Gamma\vdash B(t)}.
 \tag{QE}
\]

\paragraph{6. Witness elimination.}
\[
 \frac{\Pi;\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
       \Pi,b;\Gamma,Q_yB(b,y)\vdash A}
      {\Pi;\Gamma\vdash A}.
 \tag{DE}
\]
Here $b$ is fresh for $\Pi,\Gamma,B,A$. It may occur only in the
displayed local branch and is absent from the exported conclusion.

\paragraph{7. Determined case introduction.}
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\Pi,z;\Gamma,B(\ell(\Pi),0)\vdash B(\ell(\Pi),Sz)}
      {\Pi;\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)}.}
 \tag{DI*}\label{eq:di-star}
\]
Here $z$ is fresh for the outer environment, context, and all fixed
parameters of $B$. The witness is selected \emph{before} $z$ is added.
There is no term argument in the inference certificate.

\paragraph{8. Equation progress.}
For a literal equation $E(y)$ only,
\[
 \frac{\Pi,z;\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Pi;\Gamma\vdash Q_yE(y)}.
 \tag{QI}
\]
The parameter $z$ is fresh for the outer context and all passive
parameters of $E$. A formula under a $Q$ or $D$ binder is not a literal
equation and cannot be used as an induction invariant here.

There are no additional primitive logical axioms, equality axioms,
successor axioms, structural rules, or induction schemas. For example,
reflexivity follows from I0 and Tr1: transport the equation
$I(0,t;u.u)=t$ along itself with the identity template.
Equation induction is QI followed by QE with a base proof. Full formula
transport, ordinary logical rules, numerical substitution, and cut are
derived later. This explicit boundary is necessary because DI* is not
preserved by merely appending an unused numerical parameter to a proof
tree.

\section{The witness-shape question and its exact limit}

Let $\mathcal W$ be the old native calculus with the witness of case
introduction restricted to the literal $0$ or a free numerical variable.
Let $\mathcal N$ and $\mathcal K$ denote the old and new calculi.
Every DI* witness is allowed in $\mathcal W$, and every witness allowed
in $\mathcal W$ is allowed in $\mathcal N$. Thus
\[
 \mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal W\subseteq\mathcal N.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:newest-equivalence} gives the opposite inclusion on
derivable sequents. Hence all three consequence relations agree. This is
the positive statement left open by \texttt{NativeWitnessShape.lean}
at the pinned commit~\cite{witnessshape}.

Both shapes are necessary if one restricts the witnesses solely by
choosing a subset of $\{0,\text{variables}\}$. With $0$ alone, interpret
$D_{x,y}B$ as its closure at $x=0$. The other rules remain sound in
the natural numbers, but
\[
 D_{x,y}\bigl(C(y,1,x)=1\bigr)
\]
is false under that reading, whereas the unrestricted rule proves it
at witness $1$. With variables alone, a closed empty-context derivation
cannot produce a first $D$ formula. More formally, when the numerical
scope is empty and the context contains only equations and closures of
equations, every derivable formula has one of those two forms; induction
on the rules proves this invariant. Yet the native kernel proves
$D_{x,y}(0=0)$. The repository already formalizes both negative
statements~\cite{witnessshape}.

This is a small optimality result about the two witness shapes. It is
not an optimality result about all proof calculi. It explains why DI*
uses $0$ at the closed root and why the presence of fresh numerical
parameters matters.

\section{Why this is full iPRA, including both interpretations}
\label{sec:ipra-bridge}

Exact native consequence equivalence is stronger than agreement on
standard numerical values or on closed equations. The existing
interpretations between the native kernel and iPRA can be reused without
changing a single term or formula. This section states those
interpretations explicitly enough to identify the transferred theorem.

\subsection{The ordinary arithmetic realization}

Elaborate each fixed iterator description into a function symbol. For a
previously defined term $h(u,\vec p)$, its symbol $i_h$ obeys
\[
 i_h(0,a,\vec p)=a,\qquad
 i_h(Sn,a,\vec p)=h(i_h(n,a,\vec p),\vec p).
\]
This is a staged first-order signature of finite descriptions. Let
$T_I$ have intuitionistic first-order equality logic over this signature,
the displayed computation equations, contextual induction for literal
equations, and
\[
 \forall x\,(x=0\lor\exists y\,x=Sy),\qquad
 (0=S0)\to\bot.
\]
The last implication is an explicit bridge to ordinary falsity. Merely
listing positive arithmetic equations would allow a singleton structure;
we do not identify an equation with ordinary falsity without this bridge.

The interpretation of native $Q,D$ is their displayed ordinary reading.
Its domain formula is $x=x$, and interpreted equality is actual
equality. DI* is sound because it is an instance of the old case rule;
coverage justifies that old rule. QI is sound by equation induction.

\subsection{Ordinary logical connectives as finite native macros}

For a convenient description use the equivalent $D$-only notation,
replacing $Q_yB$ by $D_{u,y}B$ with unused $u$. Set
$C(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b)$. Gate every bottom equation by
\[
 G_v(s=t)=(C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t)),\qquad
 G_v(D_{x,y}B)=D_{x,y}G_vB.
\]
All bound variables are fresh for the control $v$. Then $G_0A$ is
provable, $G_{Sw}A$ is equivalent to $A$, and
$D_{u,y}G_yA$ is a padding of $A$ by one unused existential coordinate.

A selector $M_v(A,B)$ between arbitrary formulas is defined externally
by finite structural recursion. At equations it is
\[
 M_v(s=t,r=q)=(C(v,s,r)=C(v,t,q)).
\]
For two $D$ formulas, align their bound variables and apply $M$ to their
matrices under one $D$. If only one side has a $D$ root, pad the
equational side with $G_y$ before descending. Each call decreases the
maximum binder depth. The selector has endpoint equivalences
$M_0(A,B)\dashv\vdash A$ and $M_{Sw}(A,B)\dashv\vdash B$.

Put $\delta(v)=C(v,1,0)$ and $p(z)=I(z,0;u.\delta(u))$. The following
finite macros are the inherited ordinary logical interface~\cite{choice}:
\begin{align*}
 A\Rightarrow_*B&=D_{u,z}M_z(A,B),\\
 A\wedge_*B&=D_{u,z}G_zM_{p(z)}(A,B),\\
 \exists_*x\,A(x)&=D_{x,y}G_yA(x),\\
 A\vee_*B&=\exists_*x\,M_x(A,B),\\
 \forall_*x\,A(x)&=A(0)\wedge_*D_{u,z}G_zA(z),\\
 \bot_*&=(0=S0).
\end{align*}
The coordinate $u$ is unused. The conjunction extracts its two operands
at $z=1,2$, using $p(1)=1$ and $p(2)=0$. The universal macro combines
the zero instance with all successor instances. Implication and
existential introduction use the selector and gate endpoints. These
macros have the ordinary introduction and elimination rules and both
formula round trips in the existing kernel; exact consequence
equivalence transfers their derivations to $\mathcal K$.

In particular, the theorem is not a reduction to classical arithmetic
or to the quantifier-free fragment. Quantified intuitionistic formulas
and their proof rules are recovered through these finite syntactic
translations. Progress remains restricted to equations.

\subsection{Arithmetic translations and internal round trips}

Interpret every fixed scalar iterator in iPRA by primitive recursion
whose update ignores the counter argument. Conversely, the existing
scalar construction first establishes predecessor, then an equality
test reflecting actual equality, then quantifier-free induction, and
then pairing with projections. This dependency order avoids assuming
pairing or equality reflection in the proof of predecessor.

Write the internally proved pairing and projections as $\pi,L,H$.
For a fixed primitive recursion
\[
 f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p),
\]
its scalar compilation is
\begin{align*}
 V(n,\vec p)&=I\bigl(n,\pi(0,g^*(\vec p));
   u.\pi(SL(u),h^*(L(u),H(u),\vec p))\bigr),\\
 f^*(n,\vec p)&=H(V(n,\vec p)).
\end{align*}
Equation induction proves $L(V(n,\vec p))=n$; the projection identities
then verify the defining equations. Compilation is external recursion
on each fixed finite description. There is no universal PR evaluator
in the object theory.

Both composites are justified internally. Equality induction proves
uniqueness of an iterator, and similarly uniqueness of each fixed
primitive recursion, from its initial equation and update. An external
induction on the finite function description shows that its double
translation has the same equations and hence the same values in the
theory. This proves preservation on all elements of every model, rather
than merely on standard numerals. The whole numerical domain and actual
equality are unchanged, so the comparison isomorphism in each direction
has graph $x=y$. Structural induction on formulas, using the logical
macros and the $Q,D$ readings, gives both formula composites.

The pinned \texttt{NativeBiInterpretation.lean} establishes
the following statements~\cite{biinterp}:
\begin{itemize}
\item Faithfulness: \texttt{native\_iff\_pra} and \texttt{pra\_iff\_native}.
\item Formula composites: \texttt{native\_roundTrip} and
      \texttt{pra\_native\_roundTrip}.
\item Equiconsistency: \texttt{native\_inconsistent\_iff}.
\end{itemize}
The new part is the
identity-language equivalence of Section~\ref{sec:logical-proof}; composing
it with those established translations proves the required
bi-interpretability and equiconsistency. There is no claim that a theory
proves its own consistency.

\section{Executable evidence and reproducibility}

The archive contains two standalone checkers. The native checker is a
readable implementation of the exact eight rules in
Section~\ref{sec:axioms}. Its JSON input may specify an open numerical
scope and assumptions. The guarded checker is the measured six-family
implementation. Each checker reads a certificate, performs all checks
locally, and returns \texttt{ACCEPT} or \texttt{REJECT}; neither imports
a theorem library or calls an external prover.

The native suite constructs actual raw proof trees for symbolic numerical
coverage and for the opened successor-alias branch. In particular, it
checks $X(St)$ with $t$ a free parameter; the proof is not a numerical
sample. It also checks the construction in scopes with zero, one, two,
and five ambient parameters. The scope-sensitive negative example
deliberately reuses a closed proof after adding a parameter; the kernel
rejects it, as the mathematical proof predicts. Other negative cases
cover eigenparameter escape, invalid bound indices, a supplied witness
field, a missing closure base, and compound progress.

The arithmetic suite separately runs 412 checks using the literal scalar
definitions, including $P(0),\ldots,P(7)$. It also checks seven symbolic
equation-case lemmas through fourteen branches and 127 individually
replayed rewrite steps, and rejects a damaged certificate. These checks
test the delicate algebra in the predecessor dependency audit. They
are not a complete formal proof of the predecessor theorem.

Run \texttt{python3 reproduce.py} from any directory. It rebuilds the
native certificates, runs the guarded and arithmetic suites, recomputes
the matched whole-module metrics, and checks the native checker through
its command-line interface. The evidence directory records the exact
results. Run \texttt{python3 build\_pdf.py} to compile this document in three passes
with pdfLaTeX. Only the document build requires a TeX installation;
the proof checkers and verification scripts use the Python standard
library.

\paragraph{Verification boundary.}
The proof of exact equivalence is a mathematical metaproof in this
report. It is not a new theorem already checked by Lean or Rocq. The
repository's existing Lean bridge is reused as an established result;
it was inspected at the pinned source revision, not rebuilt in this
environment. The finite checkers validate their supplied certificates,
and the arithmetic tests validate the stated finite and symbolic
instances. The report does not promote those tests to a machine-checked
universal completeness theorem.

\paragraph{What remains open.}
The result removes the introduction witness on raw syntax and resolves
the stated witness-shape question. It does not remove equation progress,
reduce the raw native rule count, prove an absolute source-size lower
bound, or settle the independence of progress. Further compression of
the measured checker would require a new matched comparison.
