\section{A measured six-family companion on guarded formulas}
\label{sec:guarded-companion}

The witness elimination also applies to the repository's strict guarded
clause presentation~\cite{deterministic}. This companion provides the matched executable size
comparison.  It uses raw terms and ordinary capture-avoiding substitution:
there is no computation normalizer in its trusted syntax.

\subsection{Exact language and inference rules}

Terms are $0$, numerical variables, $S(t)$, and $I(n,a;u.h)$, with $u$
bound only in $h$.  Write
\[
 C(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b),\qquad
 H=(I(0,0;u.0)=0).
\]
The state variable of $C$ is unused.  A formula is an equation or
$\mathsf D_{x,y}B$.  Every immediate equation matrix under a
$\mathsf D$ must have the literal guarded shape
\[
       I(y,0;u.h)=I(y,0;u.k).
       \tag{G}\label{eq:guarded-matrix}
\]
The bodies $h,k$ may contain $x,y$ and passive numerical parameters.
An equation at the outermost formula level need not be guarded.  A
compound matrix under $\mathsf D$ is permitted when its own nested
paired formulas satisfy the same grammar.  In particular, this is a
restriction of the public formula language, not a claim of completeness
for arbitrary unguarded paired formulas.

A context retains clauses $[A;v.B(v)]$, read as
$A\to\forall v B(v)$, with $v$ absent from $A$.  An ordinary
assumption $A$ means the constant clause $[H;v.A]$.  The environment is
an ordered finite list of numerical parameters.  Let $\ell$ be its
newest member, or $0$ when that list is empty.  The six inference
families are the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The zero computation $I(0,a;u.h)=a$.
\item The successor computation
      $I(Sn,a;u.h)=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u]$.
\item \emph{Use}: from $A$, and a retained clause $[A;v.B(v)]$,
      infer $B(t)$ at any well-scoped numerical term $t$.
\item \emph{Transport}: from $s=t$ and $F(s)$ infer $F(t)$,
      for any legal one-place formula template $F$.
\item \emph{Open}: from $\mathsf D_{x,y}B$ and a derivation of $A$
      under the additional clause $[B(b,0);y.B(b,y)]$, infer $A$.
      Here $b$ is fresh and absent from the exported conclusion.
\item \emph{Introduce}: infer $\mathsf D_{x,y}B$ from
\[
       \Gamma,B(\ell,\eta)\ \vdash\ B(\ell,Sz),
       \qquad
       \eta=\begin{cases}
             z,&B\text{ is a literal equation},\\
             0,&B\text{ is compound},
             \end{cases}
       \tag{GI}\label{eq:guarded-newest-intro}
\]
      where $z$ is fresh.  The choice of $\ell$ is made in the
      outer environment, before $z$ is adjoined.
\end{enumerate}
There is no supplied introduction witness and no naming-pattern exception.
The rule's equation/case selector and the guard grammar are unchanged
from the matched source calculus.  The designated contradiction is
$0=S0$.

The source calculus $\mathcal G$ has these same rules, except that
Introduce may choose an arbitrary supplied term $t$ instead of $\ell$.
Call the new calculus $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{new}}$.  Their ordinary
formula reading is identical:
\[
 \mathsf D_{x,y}B
   \quad\mapsto\quad
 \exists x\bigl(B(x,0)\to\forall y B(x,y)\bigr).
\]

\subsection{A fragment insensitive to the selected witness}

Call an introduction \emph{dummy} if its existential coordinate $x$
is absent from $B(x,y)$.  Its premise is then independent of the
selected witness.  Derivations using dummy introductions only can be
rebuilt under arbitrary substitutions of numerical terms, injective
renamings, and extensions of the numerical parameter environment.
Indeed, the deterministic witness may change but its matrix instance
does not; the other rules commute with the corresponding substitutions
after their local eigenparameters are freshened.  This observation
applies to this fragment only.  It is not an assumption of arbitrary
parameter weakening for $\mathcal G_{\mathrm{new}}$.

For an equation $A=(s=t)$ define
\[
 \operatorname{Pad}(A)=
 \mathsf D_{r,v}\bigl(C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t)\bigr),
 \qquad r\text{ unused}.
\]
This is a legal guarded formula.  From $A$ one derives
$\operatorname{Pad}(A)$ by a dummy progressive introduction: at a
successor the required equation reduces to $A$, and the progress
assumption is unused.  Conversely, Open and Use at $1=S0$ recover
$A$ from its padding; the required zero row follows from the zero
computation.  Thus padding and unpadding are uniform proof builders
using dummy introductions only.

If $A=(s=t)$ and $E=(p=q)$ are equations, put
\[
 \operatorname{Imp}(A,E)=
 \mathsf D_{r,y}\operatorname{Pad}
   \bigl(C(y,s,p)=C(y,t,q)\bigr),
 \qquad r\text{ unused}.
\]
Its immediate matrix is compound.  The zero row is provably
$\operatorname{Pad}(A)$ and each successor row is provably
$\operatorname{Pad}(E)$.  Consequently the ordinary introduction
and elimination rules for this encoded implication follow by dummy
case introduction, Open, Use, computation, and padding.  No arbitrary
existential witness is used in those constructions.

For a compound formula $F(y)$, the unused-witness formula
$\mathsf D_{r,y}F(y)$ directly gives numerical cases.  For an equation
$E(y)$, apply these cases to $\operatorname{Pad}(E(y))$ and then
unpad.  In particular, the following hypothetical split is available
for every equational conclusion:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,t=0\vdash E\qquad
       \Gamma,t=Sz\vdash E}
      {\Gamma\vdash E},
 \qquad z\text{ fresh}.
 \tag{HC}\label{eq:guarded-hyp-cases}
\]
To see this without assuming numerical disjunction, set
$F(y)=\operatorname{Imp}(t=y,E)$.  Implication introduction gives
$F(0)$ and $F(Sz)$.  Compound numerical cases at $t$ give $F(t)$;
reflexivity $t=t$ and implication elimination then give $E$.

\subsection{Equation induction and predecessor without active witnesses}

Let $E(y)$ be the equation $s(y)=t(y)$, and set
\[
 G(y)=\bigl(C(y,0,s(y))=C(y,0,t(y))\bigr).
\]
Suppose a base proof $E(0)$ and a step construction from $E(z)$ to
$E(Sz)$ are supplied uniformly in the ambient context.  Under $G(z)$,
the hypothetical split~\eqref{eq:guarded-hyp-cases} yields $E(z)$:
at $z=0$ use the base proof, and at $z=Sw$ compute the two guards.
The step gives $E(Sz)$ and hence $G(Sz)$.  Dummy progressive
introduction therefore proves $\mathsf D_{r,y}G(y)$.  Open and Use
at the desired term $a$ give $G(a)$.  A further hypothetical split,
using $E(0)$ in its zero branch, gives $E(a)$.

This recovers contextual induction on literal equations using dummy
introductions only.  The arithmetic constructions in the scalar
predecessor bootstrap therefore take place inside this stable fragment.
Its statements that the zero test or parity has values $0$ and $1$
can be used as elimination schemas for equational conclusions: split
the argument of the zero test, or split a parity counter and then
the argument of the zero test in its successor computation.  The
bootstrap does not require a disjunction theorem or existential
introduction to perform those splits.

It follows that the fixed scalar predecessor term $P$ satisfies
\[
                  P(0)=0,\qquad P(Sa)=a
\]
in the stable fragment.  Substitution into these helper derivations
is legitimate there.  Successor injectivity is obtained afterward by
applying $P$ to an equality $Sa=Sb$; it is not assumed in the
predecessor construction.

\subsection{Names are available without a special naming case}

Define the following legal guarded formula, with $y$ free:
\[
 A(y)=\mathsf D_{x,u}
 \bigl(C(u,0,C(y,0,Sx))=C(u,0,y)\bigr).
 \tag{N}\label{eq:guarded-naming-family}
\]
In any parameter environment, $A(0)$ can be introduced at its
automatically selected witness: the successor row reduces to $0=0$
whatever that witness is.  In the numerical successor branch
$A(Sz)$, the parameter $z$ is newest.  Rule~\eqref{eq:guarded-newest-intro}
therefore selects exactly $z$, and its successor row reduces to
$Sz=Sz$.  Both constructions ignore the local progress assumption.

Compound numerical cases now prove $A(Sa)$ for every term $a$.
This is Use at the term $Sa$, not numerical substitution into a
finished proof containing an active variable witness.  Open $A(Sa)$
at fresh $b$ and Use its clause at $1$.  Computation gives
$Sb=Sa$, and the predecessor equations give $b=a$.

This construction supplies a name for every term in every actual
ambient environment.  It uses no unrestricted introduction.  The
literal naming-pattern dispatch of the earlier deterministic checker
is consequently unnecessary.

\subsection{The translation respects every local parameter environment}

For precision, a \emph{persistent proof builder} recreates its indicated
proof after adjoining arbitrary further parameters and retained
resources, while fixing the old parameters and resources.  A source
clause realization is similarly an operation which, in every such
extension, turns a proof of its antecedent into a proof of its body at
any requested well-scoped term.  Retained target clauses have these
realizations directly by Use.  The stable arithmetic constructions and
the naming construction just established are persistent builders.

Induct on a source $\mathcal G$ derivation, universally quantified
over its numerical substitution into an arbitrary target environment
and over persistent realizations of its source clauses.  Computation
and transport are copied.  A Use invokes the corresponding clause
realization.  At Open, first adjoin the actual fresh target parameter
and its retained clause; then invoke the induction hypothesis on the
strictly smaller source branch in that environment.

Consider the remaining source introduction, with arbitrary supplied
witness $a$.  Build~\eqref{eq:guarded-naming-family} at $Sa$ and
open it at fresh $b$.  Extract the equality $b=a$.  The parameter $b$
is now newest, so the target introduction selects it.  Transport its
local assumption $B(b,\eta)$ to $B(a,\eta)$, invoke the induction
hypothesis on the strictly smaller source branch with this transported
assumption as a persistent builder, and transport the result
$B(a,Sz)$ back to $B(b,Sz)$.  The mode agrees in source and target:
substitution for an outer parameter does not change whether the
matrix is an equation or is compound.  The resulting
$\mathsf D_{x,y}B$ contains no $b$, so the naming Open closes.

Whenever a helper opens another parameter, the source induction
hypothesis is invoked in that actual further extension.  A completed
target proof is never silently weakened to a new numerical scope.
Every recursive source call is on a strict subderivation.  This
establishes the required persistent-builder induction and avoids using
target cut or arbitrary parameter weakening before they have been
proved admissible.

\begin{theorem}[Guarded consequence equivalence]
For every ordered numerical parameter environment, every legal clause
context $\Gamma$, and every legal guarded formula $F$,
\[
       \Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal G}F
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal G_{\mathrm{new}}}F.
\]
The interpretation is the identity on terms, formulas, and clauses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The persistent translation proves the forward direction.  Every new
introduction is a permitted instance of unrestricted introduction,
which proves the reverse direction by inclusion.  The other rules
are identical.  The argument applies to arbitrary legal contexts,
not only closed theorems.
\end{proof}

Combining this theorem with the inherited guarded presentation theorem
gives equiconsistency and bi-interpretability with iPRA.  The numerical
domains remain whole, equality remains actual equality, and both
numerical comparison isomorphisms have graph $x=y$.  The existing
formula round trips are unchanged.  Arbitrary target parameter
weakening, numerical substitution, and cut are now consequences of
the equivalence theorem; they were not premises of its proof.

\subsection{Matched complete-checker comparison}

The delivered strict checker is obtained from the existing
deterministic guarded checker by deleting its entire \texttt{witness}
function and instantiating the matrix directly at \texttt{-scope}.
At scope zero that integer is numerical zero; otherwise it is the
newest negative parameter level.  All other rules, the strict public
grammar validator, capture-avoiding substitution, eigenparameter
checks, JSON handling, and final-conclusion check are unchanged.

The measurement removes actual docstrings, applies
\texttt{ast.unparse}, and appends one line feed.  Tokens are significant
Python tokens; AST nodes count the complete parsed normalized module.
The same measurement reproduces the published baseline figures.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\hline
Complete strict guarded checker & Tokens & AST nodes & Bytes\\
\hline
Unrestricted witness control & 1078 & 1252 & 3442\\
Earlier deterministic naming dispatch & 1159 & 1339 & 3688\\
Newest parameter, no dispatch & 1070 & 1243 & 3425\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The reduction against the unrestricted control is eight tokens, nine
AST nodes, and seventeen normalized bytes.  Against the previous
deterministic proposal it is eighty-nine tokens, ninety-six AST nodes,
and 263 normalized bytes.  These are matched measurements of complete
strict checkers.  They establish a concrete reduction and do not
establish an absolute minimum among unrelated compact codecs or among
all possible axiomatizations.
