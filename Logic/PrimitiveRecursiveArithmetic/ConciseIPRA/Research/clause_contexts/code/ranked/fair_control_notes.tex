\section{A stronger matched implementation control}

The apparent advantage of ranked equations must be separated from the
advantage of arity-coded numerical terms. A straightforward recursive
formula encoding stores an equation as $[s,t]$ and a paired binder as
$[\texttt{d},B]$. Terms still use singleton successor and triple iterator.
The string tag distinguishes the paired binder from every numerical term,
so one opening traversal handles both formula constructors and numerical
terms. This avoids the rank-specific instantiation helper.

After the same code-only normalization, the resulting four-pattern
checker has 2,677 bytes, 751 significant tokens, 823 AST nodes, and
947 gzip bytes. It is smaller than the ranked version at 2,692 bytes,
781 tokens, 853 AST nodes, and 964 gzip bytes.
Therefore ranked equations are an optional lossless presentation of
formulas, not the best measured implementation among these controls.

The Step rule matches its major as a two-element list. If that list is
a paired formula, its first field is the string \texttt{d}; the required
source comparison with a numerical iterator term then fails.
The minor's iterator-shaped left endpoint itself rules out a paired
formula. Thus no additional equation-tag check is needed, provided the
derived-formula well-formedness invariant is maintained.
Malformed and compound-major/minor tests exercise this point explicitly.

For the fairest comparison, the inherited five-pattern uniform-Step
calculus was also implemented with exactly this recursive arity codec
and scope derived from context length. Its context starts empty, so
its scope is $\lvert\Gamma\rvert$, whereas the Horn version begins
with its fixed reflexivity clause and has scope
$\lvert\Gamma\rvert-1$.

Accordingly, the Horn factorization itself saves 53 normalized bytes,
two significant tokens, and five AST nodes in this matched implementation.
Its gzip size increases by eight bytes. Most of the larger difference
from the original fully tagged checker is a representation and scope
change. These figures support a modest simplification of the primitive
interface, not a large reduction in the trusted implementation.

The recursive four-pattern codec passes 86 positive certificates, 35
malformed-certificate cases, and 2,000 opening-coherence instances.
Its 242 standalone process checks include ordinary Python and
\texttt{-O}. The five-pattern control additionally passes all 86
backward-translated pinned fixtures under both modes (172 process checks).
