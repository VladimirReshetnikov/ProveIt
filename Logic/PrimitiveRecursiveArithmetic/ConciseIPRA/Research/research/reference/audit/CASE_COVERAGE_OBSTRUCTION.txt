Atomic induction without coverage: a finite-parameter obstruction
================================================================

Theory and scope
----------------
Let T_eq be ordinary intuitionistic first-order arithmetic with actual
equality, a symbol for each fixed finite primitive-recursive description,
its defining equations, and every induction schema whose induction formula
is one literal equation. Include 0 != 1, successor injectivity, and
Sx != 0 if desired. Do not include constructor coverage, namely
forall x (x=0 or exists y x=Sy), or general quantifier-free induction.

Theorem. T_eq is not bi-interpretable with iPRA by ordinary definable
first-order interpretations, even if the interpretation and its definable
composite isomorphism may use finitely many parameters. More generally the
same obstruction excludes bi-interpretation with any theory all of whose
models have a definable linear order preserved by every automorphism fixing
the interpretation parameters.

The elementary two-coordinate example
--------------------------------------
The classical structure N x N, with all fixed PR functions evaluated
coordinatewise, satisfies the stated computation and constructor equations.
For a fixed literal equation E(x,p), fixing p gives two subsets U,V of N
such that E(a,b) is equivalent to a in U and b in V. If E(0,0) holds, then
0 belongs to both sets. If E(x) implies E(Sx) for every pair x, hold the
second coordinate at zero to show successor closure of U and hold the
first at zero to show successor closure of V. External ordinary induction
gives U=V=N. Therefore every literal-equation induction schema holds.

Coverage fails at (0,1): this element is neither (0,0) nor a coordinatewise
successor. The coordinate swap is a nonidentity automorphism of order two.
This already obstructs parameter-free bi-interpretability with a definably
linearly ordered structure. Naming (0,1) destroys this particular symmetry,
which is why the following stronger model is needed for finite parameters.

A countable model with unavoidable symmetries
---------------------------------------------
Let M be the set of eventually constant sequences a:N->N. Equivalently,
an element is a constant sequence changed at finitely many coordinates.
Each element therefore has a finite description, and M is countable.
Interpret 0 and 1 as constant sequences and every fixed PR function pointwise.
The set M is closed under these operations: outside the union of finitely
many exceptional sets, all the arguments are constant, so the output is
constant. Every fixed finite PR defining equation holds coordinatewise.
The constructor laws and 0 != 1 hold as well.

For a literal equation E(x,p), fix the parameter sequences p. At coordinate i,
let U_i be the set of natural numbers n satisfying the component equation
E(n,p(i)) in ordinary N. Suppose M satisfies E(0,p) and
forall x (E(x,p) -> E(Sx,p)). The base says 0 is in every U_i.
Fix i and n in U_i. Take the sequence e_i(n) whose value is n at coordinate i
and zero elsewhere. It belongs to M. Its equation E(e_i(n),p) holds: the
chosen-coordinate equation holds by n in U_i, and all other coordinates
satisfy the base equation. The assumed successor step gives E(S(e_i(n)),p),
and its i-th coordinate says n+1 is in U_i. Thus U_i is successor-closed.
External induction in standard N yields U_i=N for every i. Consequently
E(a,p) holds for every a in M. This verifies the full parameter-uniform
literal-equation induction schema, not merely the induction rule on closed
theorems. Retained hypotheses pose no problem because the schema holds in
the structure under every parameter assignment.

Now fix any finite tuple of parameters from M. Beyond a common finite set
of exceptional coordinates, each parameter is constant. Choose distinct
indices i,j outside that finite set. Transposing coordinates i,j is an
automorphism alpha of M: it commutes with every pointwise PR operation and
preserves actual equality. It fixes the chosen parameters. It is nontrivial,
because it moves the spike e_i(1) to e_j(1), and alpha squared is the identity.
Thus EVERY finite-parameter expansion of M has a nontrivial involution.

Why interpretations cannot hide this involution
-----------------------------------------------
Suppose a structure B is interpreted in M, and the return interpretation
in B is definably isomorphic to M. Collect the finitely many source
parameters used by the interpretation and the isomorphism. If necessary,
include finite tuples representing any parameters from interpreted quotient
domains. Choose the involution alpha fixing all these source parameters.

Acting coordinatewise on the tuples representing interpreted elements,
alpha induces an automorphism beta of B. Because alpha squared is identity,
beta squared is identity. If B has a definable linear order preserved by
beta, then beta must be identity: if b<beta(b), applying beta gives
beta(b)<b, a contradiction; the reverse inequality is equally impossible.

Since beta is identity, the automorphism it induces on the return
interpretation J(B) is also identity. Let eta:M->J(B) be the definable
isomorphism. Its graph is invariant under alpha, because alpha fixes all its
parameters. Hence eta(alpha(a))=eta(a) for every a. Injectivity of eta gives
alpha(a)=a for every a, contradicting the nontrivial coordinate swap.
Only the definable return composite is needed for this argument; it does not
rely on a quoted automorphism-group theorem.

Application to iPRA
------------------
Every iPRA model has the usual definable strict linear order (represented by
a primitive-recursive comparison predicate). Its linear-order laws are
provable by quantifier-free induction. Hence an automorphism of an iPRA model
cannot be a nontrivial involution. The preceding faithful-action argument
therefore excludes any finite-parameter bi-interpretation of M with an iPRA
model, and excludes the corresponding theory bi-interpretability claim.

General universal-Horn obstruction
----------------------------------
The argument is not specific to PR operations. Let N* be any first-order
expansion of standard N by finitary functions, relations, and constants,
with the ordinary 0 and successor. Form the same eventually-constant power:
functions act pointwise, constants are constant sequences, and an atomic
relation R(a_1,...,a_k) holds exactly when
  for every coordinate i, N* satisfies R(a_1(i),...,a_k(i)).
Actual equality also has exactly this coordinatewise meaning.

Every universal Horn sentence true in N* remains true in this model.
Indeed, if its finite conjunction of atomic premises holds globally, it
holds at each coordinate; the coordinate Horn implication gives its atomic
conclusion at every coordinate. A false conclusion cannot arise because
the index set is nonempty. This directly covers Horn clauses with falsity
as consequent; no preservation theorem needs to be cited.

The spike proof above verifies induction for any atomic formula, not only
equations, because such a formula has the same coordinatewise form. It also
verifies induction for any finite conjunction of atoms. No computability
condition on the added standard relations or functions is needed; finitary
arity ensures that eventually constant sequences are closed under them.

Coordinate swaps preserve every operation and relation and fix any prescribed
finite parameter tuple after choosing two sufficiently far coordinates.
Consequently any theory axiomatized by universal Horn sentences true in a
standard-N expansion, together with atomic (or finite-conjunction-of-atoms)
induction, has a model which cannot be finitely-parametrically bi-interpreted
with any definably linearly ordered structure. In particular it cannot be
ordinarily bi-interpretable with full first-order iPRA.

This rules out a broad signature-changing strategy, rather than merely one
failed recursor. A genuinely additional case/discreteness principle is needed
to escape this obstruction. It does not deny familiar equational presentations
of quantifier-free arithmetic: agreement on equational consequences is weaker
than full first-order bi-interpretability, and other presentations may include
non-Horn case principles in their logic or their inference conventions.

Precise failure of stronger induction and Boolean claims
---------------------------------------------------------
Let sg(0)=0 and sg(Sn)=1. In either product model, the quantifier-free formula
  A(x) := (sg(x)=0) or (sg(x)=1)
holds at zero and at every successor, so its base and induction-step
antecedents are true. It fails at a nonconstant zero-one sequence. Therefore
general quantifier-free induction is false in M, even though every literal-
equation induction schema is true.

Do NOT claim this model refutes e(a,b)=0 iff a=b for the usual pointwise
equality characteristic: that equivalence is true. What fails is the global
two-value range e(a,b)=0 or e(a,b)=1. Likewise a classical one-node model
cannot refute the logical decidability sentence a=b or not(a=b); excluded
middle is automatically true in its classical semantics. The obstruction
concerns the arithmetical two-value characteristic and full QF induction.

Application to the progressive-binder alternative
-------------------------------------------------
Read H_xy B as exists x forall y (B(x,y)->B(x,Sy)), and internal P_y B as
forall y (B(y)->B(Sy)). Without the explicit constructor Cases rule, the
progressive-binder presentation's rules are sound in M: HI, HE, and Step
are ordinary logical rules, Tr is actual equality transport, computations
hold pointwise, and Iter uses the literal-equation induction just verified.
Thus, under the conventional first-order definable interpretation notion,
deleting Cases cannot be repaired merely by choosing another numerical
domain or interpretation. This does not assert an obstruction to arbitrary
syntactic proof translations that do not induce definable model interpretations;
those are a different notion from ordinary first-order bi-interpretability.

Novelty scope
-------------
No automorphism, coordinate-swap, torsion, or product-model discussion was found
by text search in the current extracted top-level draft TeX/Markdown sources.
This is a result established here relative to that inspected corpus, not a claim
of priority in the mathematical literature.
