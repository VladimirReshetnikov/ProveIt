\section{A finite-section obstruction for every fixed numeral seed}

The current report \texttt{ipra\_fixed\_seed\_obstruction\_2026-11-09}
leaves mixed seeds open. Its zero-seed support invariant and its
positive-seed relation invariant both fail when zero and positive seeds
are available together. The following different invariant covers any
collection of fixed numeral seeds and tolerates every unary primitive
recursive function as a primitive operation.

Fix \(C\subseteq\mathbb N\). Terms are formed from variables, fixed
numerals, unary primitive recursive functions, and
\[
 J_c(n;u.h),\qquad c\in C,
\]
where \(u\) is bound only in \(h\); all other variables are passive
parameters. Its standard meaning is
\[
 J_c(0;u.h)=c,\qquad J_c(Sn;u.h)=h[J_c(n;u.h)/u].
\]
There is no counter variable bound in \(h\). Nesting and substitution
are unrestricted. Each seed is a fixed numeral, not a free-input term.

\subsection{One active coordinate}

\begin{definition}
A function \(f:\mathbb N^r\to\mathbb N\) has a \emph{finite unary section
normal form} if there are an active coordinate \(j\), finite-valued unary
primitive recursive maps \(\chi_i:\mathbb N\to A_i\) for \(i\ne j\), and
unary primitive recursive functions \(g_\alpha\), indexed by
\(\alpha\in\prod_{i\ne j}A_i\), such that
\[
 f(\vec x)=g_{(\chi_i(x_i))_{i\ne j}}(x_j).                 \tag{FS}
\]
The sets \(A_i\) are fixed finite subsets of \(\mathbb N\), not
unbounded hidden inputs; any finite palette can be coded this way.
Empty color cells are harmless. Nullary functions are constants.
\end{definition}

Equivalently, one can use finite Cartesian partitions on every input
and a unary output function of the same fixed coordinate on each cell.
Any partition on that active coordinate is absorbed into its unary
output function. In particular, varying all inactive coordinates
produces only finitely many unary sections.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform reduction of a finite orbit]
Suppose the orbit of \(c\) under \(F:X\to X\) has at most \(b\ge1\)
elements. Put \(L_b=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,b)\) and
\[
 r_b(n)=
 \begin{cases}
 n&n<b,\\
 b+((n-b)\bmod L_b)&n\ge b.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then \(F^n(c)=F^{r_b(n)}(c)\) for all \(n\), and \(r_b\) has finite range.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The orbit has transient length at most \(b-1\) and eventual period
between \(1\) and \(b\). For \(n\ge b\), both indices are past the
transient and differ by a multiple of the actual period. Smaller
indices are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-seed finite-section theorem]
Every term of the displayed grammar has a finite unary section normal
form. The result includes mixed zero and positive seeds, arbitrarily
many available fixed numeral seeds, and arbitrary unary primitive
recursive helper operations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use structural induction. Constants and variables have the form.
Applying a unary primitive recursive function composes it with the
finitely many output pieces. Consider \(J_c(n;u.h)\), with parameter
variables \(\vec x=(x_1,\ldots,x_r)\). The nullary case is immediate;
otherwise add dummy variables to normal forms as necessary.

If \(u\) is the active coordinate of \(h\), write
\[
 h(u,\vec x)=H_{\sigma(\vec x)}(u),\qquad
 n(\vec x)=g_{\tau(\vec x_{\ne k})}(x_k),
\]
where each component of \(\sigma\) or \(\tau\) is a finite unary color.
Refine the colors of all coordinates except \(k\) by the colors
occurring in both expressions. After these outside colors are fixed,
the iterator is a unary function of \(x_k\):
\[
 x\longmapsto H_{\sigma(x)}^{\,g_\tau(x)}(c).
\]
Here the component of \(\sigma\) belonging to \(x_k\) is computed inside
this unary function. It is primitive recursive. There are finitely
many outside-color choices, so (FS) holds with active coordinate \(k\).

Suppose instead that a passive parameter \(x_j\) is active in \(h\).
Write its normal form as
\[
 h(u,\vec x)=H_{\eta(u),\sigma(\vec x_{\ne j})}(x_j),
 \qquad \eta:\mathbb N\to B,\quad |B|=q.
\]
For fixed parameters, all update values lie in the set
\[
 \{H_{\beta,\sigma}(x_j):\beta\in B\}.
\]
Including the seed, the entire orbit has at most \(b=q+1\) elements,
uniformly over the parameter tuple. Hence
\[
 J_c(n;u.h)=J_c(r_b(n);u.h).                             \tag{BR}
\]
Again write \(n=g_{\tau(\vec x_{\ne k})}(x_k)\).
If \(k=j\), fixing the outside colors already leaves a unary function
of \(x_j\). If \(k\ne j\), refine the color of \(x_k\) by the finite
vector
\[
 \bigl(r_b(g_\gamma(x_k))\bigr)_\gamma,
\]
where \(\gamma\) ranges over all finitely many counter-piece indices.
Refine the other inactive coordinates by the old update and counter
colors. After fixing these outside colors, the counter-piece index
\(\tau\) can still depend on the finite color of \(x_j\), but this
dependence is computed inside a unary function of \(x_j\). The stored
vector supplies the resulting bounded iteration count. Formula (BR)
therefore gives a unary primitive recursive function of \(x_j\) on
each outside-color cell. This is (FS) with active coordinate \(j\).

All refinements are finite and primitive recursive. The two cases
exhaust the active coordinates of \(h\), and the argument never
restricts the fixed seed \(c\).
\end{proof}

This is an external normal-form construction. Its unary programs need
not be expressible in the smaller fixed-seed syntax. There is no
uniform total primitive recursive evaluator for arbitrary primitive
recursive programs hidden in the argument: each construction is on one
fixed finite term.

\begin{corollary}
No term of the grammar defines addition, multiplication, minimum,
maximum, an injective binary pairing, or the equality characteristic
function \(\epsilon(x,y)\) on the whole standard domain. No term
defines \(C(b,a,d)\), equal to \(a\) for \(b=0\) and to \(d\) for \(b>0\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each named binary function has infinitely many distinct unary sections
in either coordinate, contrary to (FS) for whichever coordinate is
active. For multiplication use positive fixed arguments. For equality
the sections are the distinct singleton indicators. Injective pairing
has distinct sections by injectivity.

For the selector, activity in \(b\) fails by setting \(b=0\) and varying
\(a\) inside an infinite color cell. Activity in \(a\) fails by setting
\(b=1\) and varying \(d\) inside such a cell. Activity in \(d\) fails
by setting \(b=0\) and varying \(a\) inside such a cell.
\end{proof}

The invariant does \emph{not} exclude predecessor: it is vacuous for
unary functions, and the generous grammar explicitly permits
predecessor as a helper. The obstruction concerns independent inputs.

\subsection{Ordinary bi-interpretability via the prior amalgam}

The earlier report
\texttt{ipra\_input\_amalgamation\_2026-11-08}, section
\texttt{03\_amalgam.tex}, constructs a countermodel for a generous
unary theory \(T_1\). Its language contains every unary primitive
recursive function. Its logic is ordinary intuitionistic first-order
logic with actual equality and genuine falsity. Its nonlogical
families are all classically true universal quantifier-free sentences,
all true one-input sentences
\(\forall x\exists\vec y\,B(x,\vec y)\) with quantifier-free \(B\), and
full quantifier-free induction with arbitrary finite parameter tuples.
These all-true families are an obstruction device, not an effectively
enumerable small proof checker.

Let \(T_C\) be the analogous generous theory in the expanded language
containing each fixed-seed term operation as a function symbol with
its free numerical variables as arguments. Thus \(T_C\) includes all
computation equations, coverage, separation, decidable equality, and
even full parameterized quantifier-free induction.

\begin{theorem}[Mixed fixed seeds do not repair ordinary bi-interpretability]
For every seed set \(C\subseteq\mathbb N\), \(T_C\) is not ordinarily
first-order bi-interpretable with iPRA. This permits definable subsets
of finite Cartesian powers, definable equality quotients, and a fixed
finite tuple of interpretation parameters. Every subtheory of \(T_C\)
in this language has the same obstruction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each added operation has a quantifier-free unary-language graph
\[
 \bigvee_\alpha\left(
   \bigwedge_{i\ne j}\chi_i(x_i)=\bar\alpha_i
   \ \wedge\ y=g_\alpha(x_j)\right).                    \tag{G}
\]
The cases form a finite partition. Finite case expansion eliminates
nested expanded terms from any quantifier-free formula without
introducing quantifiers. It preserves the standard truth of that
formula and uses its actual logical connectives.

The prior model is worth recalling. Choose
\(M\succ\mathbb N_1\) proper elementary in the unary primitive recursive
language, and an infinite label set \(I\). Its domain is
\[
 D=\mathbb N\ \sqcup\bigl((M\setminus\mathbb N)\times I\bigr).
\]
Every unary operation acts in its input's copy of \(M\), retaining its
label for nonstandard outputs and returning the shared standard value
otherwise. The earlier report proves \(D\models T_1\). Finite atomic
diagrams can be copied into \(\mathbb N\), giving all true universal
sentences. One-input witnesses lie in a single copy. A quantifier-free
induction matrix with parameters restricts to a quantifier-free
matrix in each copy: an equation between a unary term of the induction
variable and a parameter value outside that copy becomes false,
and fixed parameter-only equations become their fixed truth values.
Induction in each elementary copy then supplies full contextual QFI.

Expand \(D\) using (G). Every true expanded universal sentence reduces
by finite case expansion to a true unary universal sentence. Expanded
one-input existence laws likewise reduce to the old family. Expanded
QFI matrices remain quantifier-free unary matrices with the same
parameters. The expansion is consequently a classical model of
\(T_C\), hence also a one-world intuitionistic Kripke model.

Permuting copy labels is an automorphism of \(D\) and its definable
expansion. Given finitely many parameters, swap two unused labels:
this is a nontrivial parameter-fixing involution. Every classical
iPRA model, however, has its definable linear order. An
order-preserving involution of a linear order is the identity.

Under ordinary bi-interpretations the source involution induces an
involution of the interpreted iPRA structure, hence the identity.
It therefore induces the identity on the double interpretation. The
definable isomorphism from the source to its double interpretation is
equivariant and injective, forcing the original involution to be the
identity, a contradiction. Finite tuple domains and equality quotients
do not change the argument; finite representative tuples for quotient
parameters can be included among the fixed source parameters.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope and attribution.}
The new step is the fixed-seed finite-section theorem and its transfer
to mixed fixed seeds. The amalgam and rigidity argument are inherited.
This does not exclude arbitrary additional axioms: two-input pairing
totality, for example, fails in the amalgam and evades the theorem.
It does not cover nonordinary translations changing the meanings of
logical connectives, and it does not obstruct equiconsistency alone.
It gives no absolute proof-checker size lower bound and no
below-frontier positive axiomatization.

The two normal-form cases identify the role of arbitrary
initialization. With a state-active update and fixed seed, the counter
is the only independent unbounded input. With a parameter-active
update, the orbit is uniformly finite, so the counter supplies only
a finite choice. In contrast,
\(I(n,a;u.Su)=a+n\) uses two independent unbounded inputs and escapes
both alternatives.
