\begin{abstract}
We give a numerical language with zero, one binary operation $B$, and
zero-seeded scalar iteration $J$. Successor is the abbreviation
$Sx=B(0,x)$. The entire numerical computation inventory is
\[
 B(Sa,b)=a,\qquad J(0;u.h)=0,\qquad
 J(Sn;u.h)=h[J(n;u.h)/u].
\]
One direct selector construction supplies predecessor, and one equation
invariant recovers an arbitrary initialized iterator. The construction
needs no imported parity, halving, doubling, equality test, pairing, or
universal evaluator. With the explicitly stated logical rules, it is
equiconsistent and whole-domain bi-interpretable with full first-order
$\ipra$. Both numerical isomorphisms are the identity.

Relative to the inspected four-schema finite-initializer proposal, this
removes one computation schema and substantially shortens the arithmetic
bootstrap. It does \emph{not} establish the requested global minimum in
complete proof-checker size. The current archives already contain smaller
implementation controls than several headline tables acknowledge. We
separate the new mathematical result, inherited logical machinery,
symbolic certificate evidence, and the unresolved global minimization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result, comparison target, and limits}
\label{sec:status}

The supplied repository was inspected at commit
\begin{center}
\texttt{6c902762efcb1e78a5dd25db745310bab74f47a5}.
\end{center}
At that snapshot, \file{docs/drafts} contains fourteen top-level ZIP
archives. Their downloaded byte lengths and Git blob hashes were checked;
the complete inventory and additional SHA-256 identities are supplied in
\file{evidence/source_snapshot.json}. There are 622 direct Python-member
occurrences and 351 distinct Python byte strings in those extracted
archives. Repository access was read-only.

The closest numerical predecessor is the finite initializer construction
in \cite{kernel}. It uses $0,G,J$, with $Sx=G(0,x)$, a doubling term
$D(a)$ and $E(a)=S^2D(a)$, and four computation schemas:
\[
 J0=0,\quad J(Sn)=h(Jn),\quad
 G(SD(a),b)=b,\quad G(E(a),b)=a.
\]
Here and below schematic $J$ equations abbreviate the correctly bound
forms. Its proof constructs several auxiliary numerical encodings before
recovering initialization. Our operation has the much simpler sole
equation $B(Sa,b)=a$. The zero branch is already successor by definition.
This produces the following precise improvement.

\begin{theorem}[New finite numerical basis]
\label{thm:main}
The theory $\TB$ specified in Section~\ref{sec:ordinary} is
whole-domain bi-interpretable and equiconsistent with $\ipra$.
Its numerical language has only $0,B,J$ and the three computation schemas
in the abstract. The native presentation $\CB$ in
Section~\ref{sec:native} has the same strength and derives its ordinary
logical interface, coverage, and the required falsity behavior.
\end{theorem}

The proof is constructive and concerns arbitrary elements of the source
domain. Agreement on standard numerals is not used as a substitute for
either interpretation composite. A $J$ update is a fixed finite term:
there is no internal variable over programs and no uniformly total
primitive-recursive interpreter.

\textbf{What is not established.} The new numerical presentation has not
been implemented as a complete proof checker smaller than the best
programs in the supplied corpus. Three computation schemas are not the
entire axiomatization. The surrounding logic, induction, binding,
substitution, and the public certificate interface also have a cost.
No universal minimum, literature-wide priority, or significant global
proof-checker breakthrough is claimed. The strict global minimization
requested in the question therefore remains unresolved.

The logical framework and the arithmetic completion strategy are inherited
from the supplied Choice-DI reports \cite{choice,closure,review}. The new
part is the binary operation, its three-schema presentation, the short
selector/predecessor construction, its initializer compiler, and the
resulting explicitly checked numerical identities. The report also gives
two simple boundary models clarifying what the reduction does not remove.

\section{The complete ordinary theory}
\label{sec:ordinary}

\subsection{Numerical syntax and the three computation schemas}

Numerical terms are generated by
\[
 t ::= x\mid 0\mid B(t,t)\mid J(t;u.t).
\]
Only the update body of $J(n;u.h)$ binds $u$; the counter $n$ does not.
All other variables in $h$ are fixed passive numerical parameters.
Bound variables are renamed before capture-free substitution. Define
\[
 Sx:=B(0,x),\qquad 1:=S0,\qquad W(x):=B(x,0).
\]
These are abbreviations, not additional primitive symbols or equations.
The numerical axioms, in every retained context, are exactly
\begin{align}
 B(B(0,a),b)&=a,\tag{B}\label{ax:B}\\
 J(0;u.h)&=0,\tag{J0}\label{ax:J0}\\
 J(B(0,n);u.h)&=h[J(n;u.h)/u].\tag{JS}\label{ax:JS}
\end{align}
All displayed numerical arguments may be arbitrary well-formed terms.
In particular the first equation says $B(Sa,b)=a$.

For a strictly first-order signature, elaborate the binder externally:
for each fixed earlier-stage body $h(u,\vec p)$ introduce a symbol
$j_h(n,\vec p)$ with the two $J$ equations. The symbol $B$ is present
at the initial stage. Each declaration and each proof uses only finitely
many descriptions. Actual arguments may coincide even though formal
parameters in a declaration are distinct. This is a finite-pattern
schematic presentation, not a claim of a finite set of ordinary
first-order sentences in a fixed finite signature.

There is a staging distinction worth making explicit. Once the template
$h(u,\vec p)$ is declared, substitution for a passive parameter changes
the arguments of the fixed symbol $j_h$. It does not identify $j_h$
with a separately declared symbol for the substituted body by fiat.
Such alternative elaborations are related by a derived coherence lemma:
if $\Gamma$ proves $h(v,\vec p)=k(v,\vec q)$ uniformly in a fresh
$v$, then it proves $j_h(n,\vec p)=j_k(n,\vec q)$ for every $n$.
Equation induction proves this last equality. Both zero values are zero;
the two successor equations, the uniform update equality and the induction
hypothesis give its step. External induction on finite description stages
handles nested iterators. Thus congruence in binder notation is an
admissible macro in strict staged first-order syntax. No additional
computation or function-extensionality axiom is introduced.

\subsection{Every ordinary logical rule}

Formulas use actual numerical equality, $\bot,\land,\lor,\to,\forall$
and $\exists$. Contexts are finite reusable collections of formulas.
The logical rules are ordinary intuitionistic natural deduction:
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{}{\Gamma,A\seq A},
 &&\frac{\Gamma\seq A\quad\Gamma\seq C}{\Gamma\seq A\land C},
 &&\frac{\Gamma\seq A\land C}{\Gamma\seq A},\quad
   \frac{\Gamma\seq A\land C}{\Gamma\seq C},\\[4pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\seq A}{\Gamma\seq A\lor C},
 &&\frac{\Gamma\seq C}{\Gamma\seq A\lor C},
 &&\frac{\Gamma\seq A\lor C\quad\Gamma,A\seq F\quad\Gamma,C\seq F}
          {\Gamma\seq F},\\[4pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma,A\seq C}{\Gamma\seq A\to C},
 &&\frac{\Gamma\seq A\to C\quad\Gamma\seq A}{\Gamma\seq C},
 &&\frac{\Gamma\seq\bot}{\Gamma\seq F},\\[4pt]
 &\frac{\Gamma\seq F(z)}{\Gamma\seq\forall xF(x)},
 &&\frac{\Gamma\seq\forall xF(x)}{\Gamma\seq F(t)},
 &&\frac{\Gamma\seq F(t)}{\Gamma\seq\exists xF(x)},\\[4pt]
 &&&\frac{\Gamma\seq\exists xF(x)\quad\Gamma,F(z)\seq C}{\Gamma\seq C}.
 \end{align*}
Universal introduction requires $z\notin\FV(\Gamma)$ and no other
unshown dependence on $z$; existential elimination requires
$z\notin\FV(\Gamma,C)$ and freshness for the fixed matrix parameters.
Witness substitution is capture-free. Equality has reflexivity and
substitution into every formula:
\[
 \frac{}{\Gamma\seq t=t},\qquad
 \frac{\Gamma\seq s=t\quad\Gamma\seq F(s)}{\Gamma\seq F(t)}.
\]
Weakening, exchange and contraction are allowed; cut is admissible by
substitution of derivations, after freshening local eigenvariables.
There is no classical excluded-middle rule.

\subsection{The remaining nonlogical requirements}

In addition to (B), (J0), (JS), the ordinary presentation has
\begin{align}
 &\forall x\,(x=0\lor\exists y\,x=Sy),\tag{Cov}\label{ax:Cov}\\
 &(0=1)\to\bot,\tag{Sep}\label{ax:Sep}
\end{align}
and the following contextual rule for \emph{literal equations}:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\seq E(0,\vec p)\qquad
       \Gamma,E(z,\vec p)\seq E(Sz,\vec p)}
      {\Gamma\seq E(t,\vec p)}.
 \tag{EI}\label{rule:EI}
\]
Here $E$ is one equation of numerical terms. The eigenvariable $z$ is
absent from $\Gamma,t,\vec p$ and all other fixed parameters. Retained
assumptions in $\Gamma$ may be arbitrary quantified formulas. This rule
is equivalently the usual universally closed equation-induction schema.
Induction for other formulas is not primitive.

\section{The key numerical construction}
\label{sec:bootstrap}

\subsection{A sign and a selector from direct computation}

Define
\[
 \sigma(n):=J(n;u.1).
\]
Since $u$ is unused, the two iterator equations immediately give
\[
 \sigma(0)=0,\qquad \sigma(Sn)=1.
 \tag{Sign endpoints}\label{eq:sign}
\]
Now define the ternary selector
\begin{equation}
 C(n,a,b):=W\bigl(B(B(\sigma(n),Sa),b)\bigr).
 \label{def:C}
\end{equation}
Every symbol on the right is an explicit abbreviation in $0,B,J$.

\begin{lemma}[Selector endpoints]
\label{lem:C}
The three computation schemas and equality reasoning alone prove
\[
 C(0,a,b)=a,\qquad C(Sn,a,b)=b.
\]
No induction, coverage or separation is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two calculations are
\begin{align*}
 C(0,a,b)
 &=W(B(B(0,Sa),b))
  =W(B(S(Sa),b))=W(Sa)=a,\\
 C(Sn,a,b)
 &=W(B(B(1,Sa),b))
  =W(B(0,b))=W(Sb)=b.
\end{align*}
Each displayed contraction is literally (B), or a $J$ computation
establishing \eqref{eq:sign}.
\end{proof}

The binary operation performs two jobs. On its zero input it constructs
a successor of its second input; on a successor input it returns the
predecessor and ignores its second input. Nesting these two behaviors
produces an unrestricted selector. This is the missing two-input
operation which the fixed-seed impossibility results do not permit.

\subsection{Predecessor is immediate}

Put
\begin{equation}
 P(n):=C(n,0,W(n)).\label{def:P}
\end{equation}
Then direct computation gives
\[
 P(0)=0,\qquad P(Sa)=W(Sa)=a.
 \tag{Predecessor}\label{eq:P}
\]
There is no predecessor bootstrap through parity, period-three
trajectories, halving, or a pre-existing comparison function.

Applying $P$ to $Sa=Sb$ proves successor injectivity.
Applying $\sigma$ to $Sa=0$ gives $1=0$, so separation proves
$Sa\ne0$. Coverage now also has the equivalent form
\[
 \forall x\,(x=0\lor x=S(Px)).\label{eq:predecessor-coverage}
\]

\subsection{Loading an arbitrary initializer}

Fix an arbitrary finite update $h(v,\vec p)$ and an arbitrary seed $a$.
All their passive parameters remain fixed. Define
\begin{align}
 H(u)&:=C(u,Sa,S(h(Pu,\vec p))),\label{def:H}\\
 V(n)&:=J(Sn;u.H(u)),\label{def:V}\\
 I(n,a;v.h)&:=P(V(n)).\label{def:I}
\end{align}
Rename the bound $u$ away from the parameters before expanding these
abbreviations. The extra initial iteration loads $Sa$. Thereafter every
state has the form $Sv$, so each update decodes $v$, applies $h$, and
returns its successor.

\begin{lemma}[Successor-state invariant]
\label{lem:invariant}
Equality reasoning, the three computation schemas and literal-equation
induction prove
\[
 V(n)=S(P(V(n))).\tag{Inv}\label{eq:Inv}
\]
Neither coverage nor separation is used in this proof.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At zero, $V(0)=H(0)=Sa$, so both sides are $Sa$ by
\eqref{eq:P}. For the step, assume $V(n)=S(PV(n))$. The iterator
equation, that hypothesis, the successor selector equation and
$P(Sx)=x$ give
\begin{align*}
 V(Sn)&=H(V(n))=H(S(PV(n)))\\
      &=S(h(P(S(PV(n))),\vec p))
       =S(h(PV(n),\vec p)).
\end{align*}
Thus applying $S\circ P$ to $V(Sn)$ returns $V(Sn)$, proving the
next equation invariant. Only one literal equation is inducted on.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary initialization]
\label{thm:I}
The abbreviation \eqref{def:I} satisfies, throughout the domain,
\[
 I(0,a;v.h)=a,\qquad
 I(Sn,a;v.h)=h[I(n,a;v.h)/v].
\]
The recovery uses no unbounded search or coding of arbitrary functions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The zero equation is $P(V(0))=P(Sa)=a$. The calculation in the
induction step of Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant}, now using its established
conclusion at arbitrary $n$, gives
$V(Sn)=S(h(PV(n),\vec p))$. Applying $P$ gives the successor equation.
All statements are internal equalities with free numerical parameters.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[An alternative boundary for the same compiler]
Coverage and equality reasoning also suffice to prove
Theorem~\ref{thm:I} without equation induction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Coverage of $u$ and the selector endpoints show
$H(u)=S(P(H(u)))$: the two outputs are $Sa$ and
$S(h(Pu,\vec p))$. Since $V(n)=H(J(n;H))$ by (JS), the same
identity holds for $V(n)$. The previous calculation now applies.
\end{proof}
Thus the short initializer construction can be proved in either of two
weak fragments. The certificate evidence uses the equational-induction
version, which makes its independence from coverage explicit.

\section{Actual equality and quantifier-free induction}
\label{sec:reflection}

It would be insufficient to construct a Boolean expression which happens
to test equality in the standard model. The following internal argument,
adapted from the supplied equality-reflection proof
\cite{choice,closure}, establishes actual equality on the whole domain.
Every function used here is already a term from Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap}.

Define
\[
 x+y:=I(y,x;u.Su),\qquad d(x,y):=I(y,x;u.Pu).
\]
Four elementary equation inductions give
\begin{equation}
 0+y=y,\quad Sx+y=S(x+y),\quad
 d(Sx,Sy)=d(x,y),\quad d(Sa,a)=1.
 \label{eq:elementary}
\end{equation}
For the third, induct on $y$: its base is $P(Sx)=x$ and its step
applies $P$ to the induction equality. The fourth then follows by
induction on $a$. The first two follow immediately by induction on the
second argument of addition. In particular $Sy+r=y+Sr$.

Coverage, separation and \eqref{eq:sign} prove
\[
 \sigma(u)\in\{0,1\},\quad
 \sigma(u)=0\ \longleftrightarrow\ u=0,
 \qquad W(u)=0\ \longleftrightarrow\ u=1.
 \label{eq:tests}
\]
The last claim follows by coverage: $W(0)=1$, while
$W(Sv)=v$. These are finite constructor cases, not induction.

\begin{lemma}[Residual identity]
\label{lem:residual}
The source proves, for arbitrary $x,y$,
\begin{equation}
 C\bigl(\sigma(d(x,y)),x,y+d(x,y)\bigr)=x.
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Hold $x$ fixed and induct on $y$ using this single equation. At zero,
$d(x,0)=x$ and $0+x=x$, so both selector branches are $x$.
For the step let $r=d(x,y)$ and $r'=Pr=d(x,Sy)$. If $r'=0$, the
new selector returns $x$. Otherwise coverage writes $r'$ as a
successor. Coverage of $r$ cannot give $r=0$, since then $r'=0$;
so $r=Sr'$. The induction equation therefore gives $y+r=x$.
Now $Sy+r'=y+Sr'=y+r=x$, exactly the nonzero branch of the next
instance. All case splits use coverage; the induction formula remains
a literal equation.
\end{proof}

Consequently
\begin{equation}
 d(Sa,b)=1\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad a=b.
 \label{eq:residual-reflection}
\end{equation}
The forward implication substitutes $x=Sa,y=b$ in
\eqref{eq:residual}, obtaining $b+1=Sa$, hence $Sb=Sa$ and
$b=a$. The reverse implication is the last identity in
\eqref{eq:elementary}. Thus
\begin{equation}
 e(a,b):=\sigma(W(d(Sa,b)))
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
 e(a,b)=0\ \longleftrightarrow\ a=b,
 \qquad e(a,b)\in\{0,1\}.
 \label{eq:e}
\end{equation}
Decidability of numerical equality follows; it has not been assumed.

For a fixed quantifier-free formula $F$, externally construct
\begin{align*}
 b_{s=t}&=e(s,t),& b_\bot&=1,\\
 b_{A\land C}&=C(b_A,b_C,1),&
 b_{A\lor C}&=C(b_A,0,b_C),&
 b_{A\to C}&=C(b_A,b_C,0).
\end{align*}
External structural induction on $F$, with finite Boolean cases in the
source, proves $b_F\in\{0,1\}$ and $b_F=0\leftrightarrow F$.
For implication, when $b_A=0$ the antecedent holds and its characteristic
is $b_C$; when $b_A=1$ the antecedent is false and the implication is
true. No excluded middle for quantified formulas is involved.

To obtain induction for $F(n,\vec p)$ under any retained $\Gamma$,
translate its base and its step through this equivalence, apply
equation induction to $b_F(n,\vec p)=0$, and translate back.
This proves full contextual quantifier-free induction in the current
term language before any appeal to a general primitive-recursion compiler.

\section{Every fixed primitive-recursive description}
\label{sec:compiler}

The initialized scalar iterator of Theorem~\ref{thm:I} has no separate
counter argument in its update. We therefore account for the usual
counter-dependent primitive-recursion rule, instead of silently assuming
it. All constructions in this section are finite descriptions, with
their numerical correctness proved internally by the quantifier-free
induction just obtained.

Addition and truncated subtraction are already available. Multiplication
is $x\cdot y=I(y,0;u.u+x)$. Comparisons are constructed from the
faithful equality test and subtraction. A bounded first-search operation
is a scalar iteration on its candidate index: for a fixed Boolean
predicate with characteristic $b(k,\vec p)$, with zero meaning true,
put
\[
 R_b(u,\vec p):=C(b(u,\vec p),u,Su),\qquad
 \mu_{\leq N}b:=I(SN,0;u.R_b(u,\vec p)).
\]
Its state advances until the first true candidate, then stays there.
If a witness exists at or below $N$, the result is its least value;
otherwise the result is $SN$. This is an iteration on the index alone;
it does not presuppose a pairing of an index and an accumulator.

For complete explicit coding details and the internal proof invariants,
see Appendix~\ref{app:compiler}. In particular one obtains a numerical
pairing $\langle a,b\rangle$ and projections $\pi_0,\pi_1$ with
\[
 \pi_0\langle a,b\rangle=a,\qquad
 \pi_1\langle a,b\rangle=b
\]
internally at arbitrary $a,b$. There is no restriction to standard
numerals. These identities support the usual counter-and-state compiler.

Suppose the earlier-stage terms $g(\vec p)$ and $h(k,v,\vec p)$ have
already been compiled. Define
\[
 U(w,\vec p):=
 \big\langle S\pi_0(w),h(\pi_0(w),\pi_1(w),\vec p)\big\rangle,
\]
\[
 f(n,\vec p):=
 \pi_1 I\bigl(n,\langle0,g(\vec p)\rangle;w.U(w,\vec p)\bigr).
\]
Equation induction proves that the first projection of this state is
$n$. Using that invariant, the two projection equations prove
\[
 f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p).
\]
Zero, successor, projections and composition compile directly. External
induction on each finite PR description completes the construction.
The source proves the equations of the actual compiled terms. It does
not merely possess standard-model functions with the desired values.

\section{Both interpretations and both numerical composites}
\label{sec:biinterpretation}

\subsection{The ordinary target}

By full first-order $\ipra$ we mean intuitionistic first-order equality
logic with the symbol of each finite primitive-recursive description,
its defining equations, successor injectivity, successor nonzero, and
induction for every quantifier-free formula. Descriptions, rather than
extensionally identified standard functions, specify the signature.
Numerical predecessor coverage follows by quantifier-free induction.

\subsection{From the new theory to iPRA}

Interpret $0$ as zero and set
\begin{equation}
 B(n,b)=
 \begin{cases}
 Sb,&n=0,\\
 Pn,&n\ne0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:Btarget}
\end{equation}
This is a fixed primitive-recursive function. Choose its target description
explicitly by $B(0,b)=Sb$ and $B(Sn,b)=n$, with the step equal to
the counter projection. In the displayed case definition $S,P$ are
target successor and predecessor. Interpret each $J$ description
as ordinary primitive recursion starting at zero and ignoring the
counter argument in its update. Its update is the recursively translated
finite body. The target proves (B), (J0), (JS), coverage and separation,
and it admits literal-equation induction. Moreover $B(0,x)=Sx$, so
source successor translates to target successor.

\subsection{From iPRA to the new theory}

Use the whole source domain, actual equality, the abbreviation $Sx=B(0,x)$
and the compiler of Section~\ref{sec:compiler}. Successor injectivity
and nonzero were proved after \eqref{eq:P}; all PR equations are proved
for their compiled terms; Section~\ref{sec:reflection} gives every
target induction instance. Ordinary connectives and quantifiers translate
identically in these ordinary presentations.

\subsection{The source composite is internally the identity}

The crucial source operation is $B$. In the source, coverage gives
\begin{equation}
 B(n,b)=C(n,Sb,Pn).
 \label{eq:Bcharacterization}
\end{equation}
At zero both sides are $Sb$. At $n=Sa$, both sides are $a$.
More directly, the reverse compiler applied to the specified target
$B$ description yields a term $\widehat B$ with
$\widehat B(0,b)=Sb$ and $\widehat B(Sa,b)=a$. Coverage compares
these endpoints with the original source $B$ and proves
$\widehat B(n,b)=B(n,b)$ throughout the domain. This does not assume
that compiled target descriptions for $C$ or $P$ are already identical
to the source abbreviations.

For a fixed $J$ body, its double translation satisfies the same zero
and successor equations as the original $J$. At every earlier-stage
operation the corresponding equality has already been proved. Equation
induction on the equality of the two $J$ values therefore proves their
identity for arbitrary counters and parameters. This is an external
induction on the finite description stages and an internal equation
induction at each iterator stage.

\subsection{The target composite is internally the identity}

Each target primitive-recursive symbol and its double translation have
the same base and step equations, after substituting the established
identities for its earlier descriptions. Equation induction proves
uniqueness of the recursively defined value. Projections and composition
are immediate. The same external induction on descriptions establishes
the composite for every target symbol.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-domain bi-interpretability]
\label{thm:ordinary-bii}
The ordinary theories $\TB$ and $\ipra$ are bi-interpretable.
In both directions the domain formula is $x=x$, interpreted equality
is actual equality, and the definable isomorphism from the original
structure to its double interpretation has graph $x=y$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding two internal composite proofs show that the identity
preserves every operation throughout the domain. Ordinary logic is
unchanged, so induction on finite formula syntax gives every formula
composite. The identity relation is internally total, single valued, injective and surjective. These are the required definable isomorphisms.
\end{proof}

The finite proof translations preserve genuine falsity. Consequently the
two theories are equiconsistent. This is a metatheoretic consistency
equivalence; neither theory is claimed to prove its own consistency.

\section{A native presentation deriving its ordinary logic}
\label{sec:native}

The ordinary presentation is easiest to inspect. To make explicit the
alternative allowed in the question, we now give a complete native
presentation using the inherited Choice-DI logical mechanism
\cite{choice}. This version does not separately postulate all the
ordinary logical rules or the two ordinary boundary sentences.

\subsection{Syntax, contexts and every primitive inference}

Numerical syntax and the three computation schemas are exactly those
of Section~\ref{sec:ordinary}. Formulas and reusable context clauses are
\[
 A::=(s=t)\mid\D_{x,y}A,\qquad
 \langle A;y.F(y)\rangle\quad(y\notin\FV(A)).
\]
The two bound coordinates of $\D$ are distinct. An ordinary retained
assumption $F$ abbreviates the constant clause
$\langle K;v.F\rangle$, with $v$ absent from $F$, where
$K$ is the fixed computation theorem $J(0;u.0)=0$.
Besides the computation schemas, the primitive patterns are
\begin{gather*}
 \frac{\Gamma\seq A}{\Gamma\seq F(t)}
 \quad\mathsf{Use}\quad
 (\langle A;y.F(y)\rangle\in\Gamma),\\[4pt]
 \frac{\Gamma\seq s=t\quad\Gamma\seq F(s)}{\Gamma\seq F(t)}
 \quad\mathsf{Tr},\\[4pt]
 \frac{\Gamma\seq\D_{x,y}F(x,y)\quad
       \Gamma,\langle F(a,0);y.F(a,y)\rangle\seq C}
      {\Gamma\seq C}
 \quad\mathsf{DE},\\[4pt]
 \frac{\Gamma,F(t,\eta_\varepsilon(z))\seq F(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\seq\D_{x,y}F(x,y)}
 \quad\mathsf{DI}_\varepsilon,
 \qquad \eta_0(z)=0,\quad\eta_1(z)=z.
\end{gather*}
The case choice $\varepsilon=0$ is allowed for every matrix. The
progress choice $\varepsilon=1$ is allowed only when the immediate
matrix $F$ is a literal equation. In DI, $z$ is absent from the retained
context, the witness $t$, and every fixed parameter. In DE, $a$ is
absent from the context, conclusion and fixed matrix parameters. All
substitutions avoid capture, and displayed local assumptions or clauses
are discharged. These are the entire primitive logical and nonlogical
inventories of $\CB$.

The intended readings are
\[
 \D_{x,y}F(x,y)\ :=\
 \exists x\bigl(F(x,0)\to\forall yF(x,y)\bigr),
 \qquad
 \langle A;y.F\rangle\ :=\ A\to\forall yF.
\]
Native falsity will be the designated equation $0=1$.

\subsection{The arithmetic bootstrap is available natively}

Weakening, fresh renaming, capture-free substitution and cut for ordinary
assumptions are admissible by induction on derivations. The restriction
to an equation root is preserved by numerical substitution.

Reflexivity needs a small adjustment from the old scalar proof. Put
$r=B(St,0)$. Schema (B) proves $r=t$. Use this same equation as
both premises of transport with motive $F(v)\equiv(v=t)$. The
conclusion is $t=t$. Symmetry, transitivity and numerical congruence
then follow from the usual explicit equality motives.

Progress DI with an unused existential coordinate, followed by DE and
Use of a supplied base, gives contextual literal-equation induction.
Case DI with an unused coordinate gives numerical cases for every
native formula:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\seq F(0)\qquad\Gamma\seq F(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\seq F(t)}.
 \tag{NC}\label{rule:NC}
\]
Here $z$ is fresh in the usual sense. In both constructions the unused
coordinate prevents the existential elimination from changing a
preselected witness or a passive parameter.

The direct computations of $\sigma,C,P$ and the equation-induction
proof of arbitrary initialization now apply natively, before ordinary
coverage has been reconstructed. There is no circular use of the
ordinary logical interface.

\subsection{Gates, selection, and every ordinary connective}

For a numerical control $v$, define a formula gate externally by
\[
 \G_v(s=t):=(C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t)),\qquad
 \G_v(\D_{x,y}A):=\D_{x,y}\G_vA.
\]
Rename bound coordinates away from $v$. Case DI, equality and binder
congruence prove
\[
 \seq\G_0A,\qquad \G_{Sw}A\dashv\vdash A,
 \qquad A\dashv\vdash\D_{u,y}\G_yA
\]
when $u,y$ are fresh for $A$. Binder congruence follows by opening the
old clause, case-introducing the new matrix at that same witness,
converting its base, using the old clause at the successor, and
converting back.

Define selection between formulas by
\[
 \M_v(s=t,r=q):=(C(v,s,r)=C(v,t,q)),
\]
and recursively, with aligned fresh binders,
\begin{align*}
 \M_v(\D_{x,y}A,\D_{x,y}F)&:=\D_{x,y}\M_v(A,F),\\
 \M_v(E,\D_{x,y}F)&:=\D_{x,y}\M_v(\G_yE,F),\\
 \M_v(\D_{x,y}A,E)&:=\D_{x,y}\M_v(A,\G_yE),
\end{align*}
where $E$ is an equation. The maximum input binder depth decreases in
each recursive call. Padding and congruence prove
$\M_0(A,F)\dashv\vdash A$ and
$\M_{Sw}(A,F)\dashv\vdash F$.

The complete logical macro inventory can now be chosen as
\begin{align}
 A\Rightarrow_*F&:=\D_{u,z}\M_z(A,F),\nonumber\\
 A\land_*F&:=\D_{u,z}\G_z\M_{Pz}(A,F),\nonumber\\
 \exists_*x\,A(x)&:=\D_{x,y}\G_yA(x),\nonumber\\
 A\lor_*F&:=\exists_*x\,\M_x(A,F),\nonumber\\
 \forall_*x\,A(x)&:=\D_{u,z}\G_zA(Pz),\nonumber\\
 \bot_*&:=(0=1).\label{eq:macros}
\end{align}
The variable $u$ is unused; all other newly bound coordinates are fresh.
These are syntax definitions, not trusted new proof rules.

For implication introduction, the zero selector retains the antecedent
and every successor selector supplies the consequent. Its elimination
opens the clause and uses it at $1$. For conjunction introduction, NC
and the selector endpoints give $\M_v(A,F)$ for arbitrary $v$ from
both operands. For projections, the zero gate supplies the antecedent;
indices $1,2$ give controls $0,1$ through $P$ and recover the operands.
Existential introduction and elimination use the successor gate at $1$
and the fresh witness supplied by DE. Disjunction introduction chooses
witness $0$ or $1$. For elimination, first discharge each branch as
an implication to the common conclusion; NC combines the implications
at arbitrary selector controls, then existential elimination applies it.
Universal introduction uses $P(Sz)=z$ and a fresh-parameter proof.
Universal elimination opens its true-base clause at $St$, obtaining
$A(P(St))$, hence $A(t)$.

Under $0=1$, selector congruence gives
$s=C(0,s,t)=C(1,s,t)=t$ for every $s,t$. External induction on
formula syntax using case DI then gives every native formula. This
is falsity elimination. NC proves
$x=0\lor_*x=S(Px)$, using the zero disjunct at zero and the
predecessor computation in the successor case. Thus ordinary numerical
coverage is derived as well.

\subsection{The formula composites}

Each native inference is sound in the ordinary realization $\TB$:
case DI uses coverage, progress DI uses equation induction under its
conditional base, and the remaining rules have their indicated
intuitionistic meanings. Conversely, the macros above derive every
ordinary logical rule, coverage, and equation induction. The translation
of separation is the identity implication from $0=1$ to itself.

The key native composite is
\[
 \D_{x,y}A\dashv\vdash
 \exists_*x\bigl(A(x,0)\Rightarrow_*\forall_*y A(x,y)\bigr).
\]
Forward, DE retains the conditional clause at a fresh witness. Under
its base assumption, Use gives an arbitrary instance; introduce the
universal, implication and existential macros. Backward, eliminate the
macro existential at a fresh witness and use case DI: the retained base,
macro implication and universal elimination provide the successor
instance. The ordinary realizations of \eqref{eq:macros} have their
ordinary connective meanings by the selector endpoints and coverage.
For falsity use the explicit ordinary separation bridge. External
induction on finite formulas proves both formula composites; clauses
correspond to $A\Rightarrow_*\forall_*yF$.

Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:ordinary-bii}, these arguments prove
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. They also state exactly where native logical
rules encode obligations that the ordinary presentation lists separately.

\section{Why the logical boundaries cannot simply be deleted}
\label{sec:boundaries}

\begin{proposition}[Coverage is independent of the equational fragment]
The three computation schemas, separation, and literal-equation
induction do not prove ordinary numerical coverage.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the classical structure $\mathbb N\times\mathbb N$. Interpret
$0$ as $(0,0)$ and $B$, and every staged $J$, coordinatewise from
their standard meanings. Thus $S(a,b)=(a+1,b+1)$. The computation
schemas hold and $0\ne1$ holds, but $(0,1)$ is neither zero nor
a successor, so coverage fails.

Every term is coordinatewise. At fixed parameters an equation $E(n)$
therefore has the form $E_1(n_1)\land E_2(n_2)$. Suppose its zero
instance and its global successor step hold. Both $E_1(0)$ and
$E_2(0)$ hold. Fix the second coordinate at zero: the global step
shows $E_1(k)\to E_1(k+1)$. Standard external induction gives all
$E_1(k)$. Fixing the first coordinate at zero gives all $E_2(k)$
in the same way. Thus every equation-induction instance holds in
the product. The argument applies at every fixed parameter tuple;
the contextual rule is also sound because its eigenvariable is absent
from the retained context. Classical structures are in particular
one-node intuitionistic models.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Separation is independently necessary here]
The three computation schemas, coverage and equation induction do not
prove the ordinary sentence $(0=1)\to\bot$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The singleton numerical structure satisfies all numerical equalities,
coverage and every equation-induction instance. In its ordinary
one-node logic $\bot$ is false and $0=1$ is true, so separation fails.
\end{proof}

These are exact independence statements about the displayed ordinary
presentation. They do not rule out all alternative interpreted domains
or logical calculi. In particular the native calculus supplies the
corresponding logical behavior through its paired rules and designated
contradiction. Neither model is a global proof-checker lower bound.

\section{Further research questions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item \textbf{Can the arithmetic simplification shrink the entire kernel?}
  Find a coherent sorted representation of $B$ and $J$ that shares the
  native binder-opening machinery without introducing a larger dispatch
  or a hidden normalizer. Count the whole program against the corrected
  comparator envelope, with identical public-interface requirements.
\item \textbf{Can the three computation schemas be reduced meaningfully?}
  An apparent merger must count its matcher, recursion declaration
  syntax, and binding checks. Merely placing equations in one conjunction
  does not reduce the implementation obligation.
\item \textbf{Which two-input operations suffice with zero-seeded iteration?}
  The fixed-seed obstructions permit all unary helpers and still exclude
  arbitrary initialization. Our $B$ crosses that boundary with the lowest
  possible nonunary arity. A useful classification would distinguish
  genuinely two-input reset operations from binary operations still
  bounded by one chosen coordinate.
\item \textbf{What is the weakest useful induction interface here?}
  The initializer needs just the successor-state equation invariant, or
  alternatively coverage. Full arithmetic strength later uses equation
  induction to reflect actual equality. Can those uses be fused into a
  smaller independently checked rule without admitting full HA induction?
\item \textbf{Can the whole equivalence be mechanically checked?}
  Formalize staged term substitution, the symbolic certificate checker,
  the bounded pairing compiler and both interpretation composites in
  Lean or Rocq. Source-level logical rules and all nonstandard-domain
  claims should remain explicit.
\end{enumerate}
