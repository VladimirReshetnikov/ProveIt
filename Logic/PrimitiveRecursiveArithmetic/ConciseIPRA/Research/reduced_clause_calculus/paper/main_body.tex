\begin{abstract}
This report gives a strict local simplification of the newest raw-clausal
presentation in ConciseIPRA. Delete the direct root-composition case of
its evaluation rule. Reflexivity and the remaining zero-evaluation case
derive the deleted inference through an identity frame, while successor
evaluation is unchanged. The resulting calculus has exactly the same
sequents on exactly the same language. It is therefore equiconsistent
and bi-interpretable with full first-order intuitionistic primitive
recursive arithmetic, with whole numerical domains, actual equality,
and numerical composite isomorphism graph $x=y$.

The complete unchanged-interface checker decreases from 3046 to 2999
normalized Python bytes, from 853 to 848 significant tokens, and from
993 to 986 AST nodes. An explicit compiler preserves each conclusion
and replaces $k$ root steps in an $N$-node proof by an $(N+2k)$-node
proof. A supplemental local lower bound shows that the three-node
replacement is optimal in the pure reduced arithmetic fragment.

The clause presentation and the identity-frame deletion are both
already present, separately, in the incoming reports. This deliverable
integrates them and verifies the exact matched saving; it does not
claim a new foundational breakthrough, an absolute minimum, or a
repository-wide checker-size record. The complete axiomatization,
interpretation argument, inherited arithmetic proofs, executable
verifier, and proof compiler are supplied.
\end{abstract}

\path{Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA}~\cite{repo}.
Its README and all five incoming archives were examined. The archive

This matters because the incoming material is stronger than the

The immediate control is the newest raw-clausal checker
\path{code/clausal_eval.py} inside
\path{ipra_raw_clausal_2026-11-11.zip}~\cite{rawclause}. It uses
five proof instruction families: reflexivity, Eval, clause use,
paired elimination, and paired introduction. Eval itself has three
cases. Call this calculus $\Cold$.

The proposed calculus $\Cred$ keeps the same numerical terms,
formulas, contexts, binding discipline, and proof format, and deletes
one of those three cases. It combines the raw clause interface with
the identity-frame argument already supplied in
\file{ipra_equality_from_iteration_2026-11-11.zip}~\cite{identity}.
The result is a smaller integrated presentation, not a claim that
either component theorem is new.

\begin{theorem}[Exact local simplification]
For every finite clause context $\Gamma$ and every well-scoped raw
formula $F$,
\[
          \Gamma\vdash_{\Cred}F
          \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
          \Gamma\vdash_{\Cold}F.
\]
Both translations leave every end sequent---its terms, formula,
and context clauses---literally unchanged. Consequently the ordinary realization of $\Cred$
is bi-interpretable and equiconsistent with full first-order $\ipra$.
\end{theorem}

There are still five serialized instruction families. If the two
evaluation branches are counted as separate mathematical schemas,
the displayed inventory has six schemas rather than the control's
seven. The introduction schema retains its explicit mode test.
No encoding-independent minimum follows from either counting convention.

\section{The complete proposed axiomatization}
\label{sec:spec}

\subsection{Terms, formulas, clauses, and ordinary readings}

There is one numerical sort. Terms are raw finite expressions
\[
     t ::= x\mid 0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]
The iterator binds $u$ only in its body $h$, not in its counter $n$
or initializer $a$. The body may have passive numerical parameters
and may contain further iterators. Every substitution in the main
calculus is ordinary capture-avoiding substitution. In particular,
$I(0,a;u.h)$ is not definitionally the same term as $a$.

The formula language is
\[
     F ::= (s=t)\mid D_{x,y}F,
     \qquad \bot_*:=(0=S0).
\]
The distinct coordinates $x,y$ are bound. A context is a finite
reusable list of clause occurrences
\[
      [A;y.B(y)],\qquad y\notin\FV(A).
\]
The clause binds $y$ only in its consequent. Clauses are context
objects, not additional formula constructors. Their ordinary
intuitionistic readings are
\begin{align*}
 (D_{x,y}B)^\circ
   &=\exists x\bigl(B^\circ(x,0)\to\forall y B^\circ(x,y)\bigr),\\
 [A;y.B]^\circ&=A^\circ\to\forall y B^\circ.
\end{align*}
Equality has its actual numerical meaning. These readings define an
interpretation; they do not add primitive inference rules.

Put $H=(0=0)$. An ordinary assumption $F$ is an abbreviation for
the constant clause
\[
          \ord(F)=[H;v.F],\qquad v\notin\FV(F).
\]
Thus a local context written $\Gamma,F$ below means
$\Gamma,\ord(F)$, and its use is derived by clause use from $H$.

\subsection{Every primitive rule}

The two arithmetic evaluation cases are
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash a=t\qquad
       \Gamma\vdash I(0,a;u.h)=r}
      {\Gamma\vdash r=t}\quad\VZ,
 \tag{V0}\label{rule:v0}
\]
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash I(c,a;u.h)=t\qquad
       \Gamma\vdash I(Sc,a;u.h)=r}
      {\Gamma\vdash r=h[t/u]}\quad\VS.
 \tag{VS}\label{rule:vs}
\]
In particular, the major premise of VS is about the entire predecessor
iterate. It is not an initializer equality. These two cases are
serialized by the single instruction tag Eval.

The other four instruction families are exactly
\[
           \frac{}{\Gamma\vdash s=s}\quad\Refl;
 \tag{R}\label{rule:refl}
\]
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash B(t)}\quad\mathsf U,
 \qquad [A;y.B(y)]\in\Gamma;
 \tag{U}\label{rule:use}
\]
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)\qquad
       \Gamma,[B(b,0);y.B(b,y)]\vdash F}
      {\Gamma\vdash F}\quad\mathsf E;
 \tag{E}\label{rule:elim}
\]
\Needspace{10\baselineskip}
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta_{B,t}(z))\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B(x,y)}\quad\mathsf I,
 \tag{I}\label{rule:intro}
\]
where
\[
 \eta_{B,t}(z)=
 \begin{cases}
   z,& B\text{ is a literal equation and }t\syn0,\\
   0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The symbol $\syn$ means literal identity of syntax. An equation
under a binder is not a literal equation. All instances are well
scoped, and witnesses and clause targets may be arbitrary terms.

In E the parameter $b$ is fresh for $\Gamma,F$ and all passive
parameters of $B$. In I the parameter $z$ is fresh for $\Gamma,t$
and all passive parameters of $B$. The displayed local assumptions
are discharged. Context entries remain reusable inside each branch.

This is the entire primitive inventory. Computation equations,
symmetry, transitivity, congruence, successor injectivity, numerical
coverage, ordinary logical connectives and their rules, and arithmetic
induction are derived. There is no first-class $Q$ formula or separate
hypothesis instruction.

\subsection{Elementary structural facts}

Refl proves $H$ in every context. U applied to $[H;v.F]$ then
proves the ordinary assumption $F$. Weakening and injective fresh
renaming follow by induction on derivations. They leave the mode
of I unchanged.

Ordinary-assumption cut is also admissible: to remove an occurrence
$[H;v.F]$ supplied with a proof of $F$, replace each use of that
clause by the supplied proof, weakened into the local context.
Freshen eigenvariables first. This is induction on finite proof
syntax. Arbitrary numerical substitution into proofs is not presumed
here: it may change a nonliteral witness into literal zero and
therefore change the primitive introduction mode.

\section{The deleted rule and its exact replacement}
\label{sec:frame}

The control additionally permits
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash s=r}
      {\Gamma\vdash r=t}\quad\Root.
 \tag{Root}\label{rule:root}
\]
The following inherited identity-frame argument removes it without
using any equality laws in advance~\cite{identity}.

\begin{lemma}[Identity-frame derivation]
Let $W(s)=I(0,s;u.0)$. From $p:s=t$ and $q:s=r$ one derives
$r=t$ by
\[
 \boxed{\quad
   \Root(p,q):=\VZ\bigl(p,\VZ(q,\Refl(W(s)))\bigr).
 \quad}
\]
Both supplied premise proofs occur once.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Refl gives $W(s)=W(s)$. Applying V0 with major premise $q:s=r$
gives $W(s)=r$. Applying V0 again, now with major premise $p:s=t$,
gives $r=t$. Every step has the same retained clause context. The
term $W(s)$ is well scoped whenever $s$ is. No computation equation,
symmetry, induction, logical rule, or proof-substitution theorem has
been invoked.
\end{proof}

For example, the abstract root step $x=y,\ x=z\vdash z=y$ expands as
\[
 \begin{array}{rll}
 1.&W(x)=W(x),&\Refl;\\
 2.&W(x)=z,&\VZ\text{ from }x=z\text{ and line 1};\\
 3.&z=y,&\VZ\text{ from }x=y\text{ and line 2}.
 \end{array}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Literal preservation of every sequent]
The identity map on language and contexts gives the equivalence
$\Gamma\vdash_{\Cold}F\iff\Gamma\vdash_{\Cred}F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on a $\Cold$ proof tree. Replace each Root node by the
identity-frame derivation and copy every other rule, translating
its subproofs recursively. At E and I the same local clause and
the same scope are introduced, so the induction hypothesis applies
under exactly the required context. The reverse translation is
inclusion: every rule of $\Cred$ is a rule of $\Cold$.
\end{proof}

The three control evaluation cases are disjoint on raw finite syntax.
For zero evaluation, $I(0,a;u.h)$ has $a$ as a proper subterm, and
therefore cannot equal $a$ literally. For successor evaluation,
$I(Sc,a;u.h)$ has one more successor node than the required source
$I(c,a;u.h)$, and therefore cannot literally equal it. Their counters
also distinguish the two computation cases. Thus the compiler can
identify Root by its literal matching condition without changing
the classification of a retained computation step.

\begin{corollary}[Exact compiler overhead]
If a control proof has $N$ inference nodes and $k$ Root nodes,
the displayed tree compiler produces exactly $N+2k$ nodes.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each removed node becomes two V0 nodes and one Refl node. Its two
premise subtrees occur once, and all other nodes are copied.
\end{proof}
This is a count of inference nodes, not certificate bytes. The new
Refl label contains $W(s)$, so the byte overhead depends on the
size of the original source term. The standalone verifier does not
run this compiler or trust it; it checks the resulting certificate.

\subsection{A local optimality bound for the replacement}

The following additional observation bounds the arithmetic gadget;
it does not bound the number of axioms or the size of a verifier.

\begin{proposition}[Three new nodes are necessary]
Take distinct free variables $x,y,z$ and treat supplied proofs
$p:x=y$ and $q:x=z$ as the only zero-cost opaque leaves. In the
pure raw fragment with Refl, V0, and VS, every proof tree of $z=y$
contains at least two Eval nodes and one Refl node in addition
to the supplied leaves. The identity-frame construction attains
this bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The last inference cannot be $p$, $q$, or Refl: their conclusions
are respectively $x=y$, $x=z$, and a syntactically reflexive
equation. It is therefore an Eval. Its minor premise has an
iterator-headed left side and right side $z$, because Eval
carries the minor's right side to the conclusion's left side.
That minor cannot be $p$ or $q$, whose left side is $x$; and it
cannot be Refl, since a raw iterator term is not the variable $z$.
Hence the minor itself ends in a second Eval.

Follow minor-premise edges from that Eval. The finite tree must
eventually reach a leaf with iterator-headed left side. Neither
opaque supplied leaf has that shape, so this leaf is a Refl
instance. This proves the lower bound. The three-node expansion
above supplies the matching upper bound.
\end{proof}
The restriction to this fragment is essential. Preprovided derived
lemmas, additional inference rules, primitive cut, different term
quotients, and a different certificate representation are outside
the statement. It is not a global minimality claim.

\section{Recovering computation and ordinary equality}
\label{sec:equality}

All constructions in this section work under an arbitrary retained
clause context. Here Root always denotes the derived three-node
construction, never an added primitive.

For $p:s=t$ define
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}(p)=\Root(\Refl(s),p):t=s.
\]
For $p:s=t$ and $q:t=v$ define
\[
 \operatorname{Trans}(p,q)=\Root(q,\operatorname{Sym}(p)):s=v.
\]
Both iterator computation equations have direct proofs:
\begin{align*}
 I(0,a;u.h)=a
    &\quad\text{by }\VZ(\Refl(a),\Refl(I(0,a;u.h))),\\
 I(Sc,a;u.h)=h[I(c,a;u.h)/u]
    &\quad\text{by }\VS(\Refl(I(c,a;u.h)),
                             \Refl(I(Sc,a;u.h))).
\end{align*}

\begin{lemma}[Congruence without root composition]
For every fixed finite term template $h(u)$, a proof of $s=t$
gives a proof of $h(s)=h(t)$ using only Refl, V0, and VS.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $A=I(0,s;u.h)$ and $B=I(S0,s;u.h)$, and let $p:s=t$.
Construct
\begin{align*}
 z_s&=\VZ(\Refl(s),\Refl(A))&&:A=s,\\
 c_s&=\VS(z_s,\Refl(B))&&:B=h(s),\\
 z_t&=\VZ(p,\Refl(A))&&:A=t,\\
 \VS(z_t,c_s)&&&:h(s)=h(t).
\end{align*}
The last minor has left side $B=I(S0,s;u.h)$ and the required
predecessor iterate is literally $A$. This uses no earlier
congruence or transitivity principle. Capture-avoiding body
substitution handles all occurrences of $u$, including nested ones.
\end{proof}

Given a second premise $h(s)=r$, derived Root applied to it and
the congruence equation yields the crossed one-template rule
\[
               s=t,\ h(s)=r\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=h(t).
\]
Applying that rule twice gives transport in an arbitrary literal
equation template:
\[
               s=t,\ a(s)=b(s)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a(t)=b(t).
\]
The template is needed only in this derived construction, as an
iterator body. Primitive certificates have no equality-template
argument.

\section{Logical completeness and the full iPRA interpretation}
\label{sec:bridge}

\subsection{The missing case rule is derived}

The introduction in Section~\ref{sec:spec} uses progress only for
an equation matrix at literal witness zero. All numerical case
introductions are nevertheless available. The only missing
primitive case has matrix $E(x,y)$ an equation and witness $0$.
Put $c=I(0,0;u.0)$. Its syntax is not zero, and computation
proves $c=0$.

Suppose the desired case premise is
$\Gamma,E(0,0)\vdash E(0,Sz)$. Under $E(c,0)$, equation
transport gives $E(0,0)$; ordinary-assumption cut inserts the
given premise; transport back gives $E(c,Sz)$. Primitive I
at witness $c$ uses its case mode and concludes the desired
$D_{x,y}E(x,y)$. No compound-formula transport is needed here.

Transport through a compound $D$ formula then follows by external
structural induction on the formula: eliminate the source $D$
to obtain its clause, introduce the target $D$ by the just-derived
case rule, translate the base by the induction hypothesis, use
the clause at $Sz$, and translate its conclusion back. Only a
strictly smaller formula is used in the inductive transport.

\subsection{The omitted closure binder is definable}

For a fresh unused coordinate $w$, write
\[
                  q_yB(y):=D_{w,y}B(y).
\]
It has the following derived interfaces:
\begin{itemize}
\item From $\Gamma,B(0)\vdash B(Sz)$ derive $q_yB$ by case
      introduction with unused witness coordinate.
\item From $q_yB$ and $B(0)$ derive $B(t)$ by E and U.
\item From a retained clause $[B(0);y.B(y)]$ derive $q_yB$
      by case introduction, using that clause at $Sz$.
\end{itemize}
The last property is the eta expansion of the opened clause.
For a literal equation $E(y)$, I with unused witness $0$
derives $q_yE$ from $\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)$. With the
base equation and closure elimination this is contextual
equation induction.

These constructions give the explicit comparison to the repository's
native Eq/$Q$/$D$ calculus: leave terms and equations unchanged,
translate $D$ recursively, and translate $Q_yB$ as $q_yB$.
Ordinary assumptions become constant clauses. In a translated
native elimination branch, the opened clause eta-expands to the
translated $Q$ assumption, and ordinary-assumption cut inserts
the translated branch proof. Conversely a clause created by E
is represented by a native $Q$ assumption, while a constant
clause is represented by its ordinary formula assumption. U
becomes respectively native QE or ordinary assumption use.
The remaining rules are computation, equality, and the same
introductions or their derived forms.

This clause comparison is inherited from the two clausal
reports~\cite{clausal,rawclause}. The exact same-sequent theorem
of Section~\ref{sec:frame} makes it applicable without modifying
any interpretation map. On Eq/$D$ formulas the inclusion followed
by translation is literally the identity. In the other direction,
the native equivalence
\[
                   Q_yB(y)\dashv\vdash D_{w,y}B(y)
\]
gives the formula round trip by structural induction.

\subsection{The ordinary arithmetic theory}

Let $T_I$ be intuitionistic first-order logic with actual equality
and genuine falsity. Each fixed finite iterator body is elaborated
into an ordinary function symbol. If $h(u,\vec p)$ is an earlier
term, introduce $i_h(n,a,\vec p)$ with the two equations
\[
 i_h(0,a,\vec p)=a,\qquad
 i_h(Sn,a,\vec p)=h(i_h(n,a,\vec p),\vec p).
\]
The other axioms are coverage and separation,
\[
 \forall x\,(x=0\lor\exists y\,x=Sy),\qquad
 0=S0\longrightarrow\bot,
\]
and contextual induction for every literal equation. There are
no function variables, and no total universal evaluator for
codes of primitive recursive programs.

The inherited native interpretation derives ordinary intuitionistic
logic, numerical coverage, and explosion, and proves exact
equivalence with $T_I$. The detailed logical and arithmetic
reference proofs are reproduced in the appendices. In particular,
the ordinary theory is not just the iterator equations interpreted
in a singleton algebra: the falsity bridge and numerical cases
are part of the established native interpretation.

Every primitive rule of $\Cred$ is sound in $T_I$ under the readings
in Section~\ref{sec:spec}. V0 and VS follow from the computation
equations and equality. U and E are ordinary intuitionistic
eliminations. The case mode of I uses zero/successor coverage;
the progress mode uses induction for the literal equation.
The reverse interpretation follows from the recovered native
calculus and its ordinary logical macros.

\subsection{From scalar arithmetic to standard iPRA}

Here $\ipra$ is full intuitionistic first-order logic with equality,
one function symbol for every finite primitive recursive definition,
its defining equations, successor injectivity and separation,
and induction for quantifier-free formulas with parameters.
This is the repository's target theory, not only its equational
fragment.

The inherited scalar bridge proceeds in a noncircular order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Construct a scalar predecessor $P$ and prove $P0=0$ and
      $P(Sn)=n$ using literal-equation induction and numerical cases.
\item Construct truncated subtraction and a faithful Boolean
      characteristic of actual equality at arbitrary domain elements.
\item Reflect each fixed quantifier-free formula into an equation
      and obtain its induction instance.
\item Construct pairing and projections, then compile each fixed
      primitive recursion into scalar iteration.
\end{enumerate}
These are inherited mathematical results~\cite{erasure,forward,progress};
the corresponding source proofs are included unchanged.

For clarity, let $\pi,L,R$ be the derived pairing and projections.
An ordinary recursion
\[
 f(0,\vec p)=g(\vec p),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec p)=h(n,f(n,\vec p),\vec p)
\]
is represented by the finite scalar term
\begin{align*}
 V(n,\vec p)
   &=I\bigl(n,\pi(0,g^*(\vec p));
       u.\pi(SL(u),h^*(L(u),R(u),\vec p))\bigr),\\
 f^*(n,\vec p)&=R(V(n,\vec p)).
\end{align*}
Equation induction proves $L(V(n,\vec p))=n$; the projection
equations then prove both defining equations for $f^*$. Pairing
is used only after its scalar construction is available.

\subsection{Both composites and equiconsistency}

Interpret each fixed scalar iterator in $\ipra$ by ordinary
primitive recursion with an update that ignores its counter.
Interpret each primitive recursive definition in the other
direction by the scalar compiler. Both domain formulas are
$x=x$, and equality is actual equality.

Equation induction proves uniqueness for each fixed iterator
and for each fixed general primitive recursion. External
induction on a finite description therefore proves, internally
in the appropriate theory, that each double-translated numerical
operation equals the original operation. Thus the numerical
composite isomorphism has graph $x=y$, on the whole domain,
including nonstandard elements.

The native logical comparison supplies both formula round trips.
For $D$ the key equivalence is its ordinary reading
\[
 D_{x,y}B\dashv\vdash
 \exists_*x\bigl(B(x,0)\Rightarrow_*\forall_*y B(x,y)\bigr),
\]
where the starred connectives are the derived native macros.
The forward proof opens the clause and specializes it under the
base assumption; the backward proof uses the supplied universal
in case introduction. Structural induction handles all formulas.

Finally the identity translation $\Cred\leftrightarrow\Cold$
changes neither composite. It also preserves the designated
contradiction $0=S0$. Translating derivations of contradiction
in both directions proves equiconsistency. This proves the
requested bi-interpretability, rather than merely agreement
of term values on standard numerals.

\section{The complete checker and executed validation}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{Public certificate contract}

The top-level JSON object contains exactly \file{goal} and
\file{proof}. Its goal is a closed Eq/$D$ formula. The mathematical
calculus supports open sequents; the public executable checks
closed theorems. Both compared programs use this same contract.

Zero is the integer $0$. Negative integers name free parameters
introduced by proof branches. Positive integers are local bound
indices starting at $1$. Successor is \file{[0,t]}, iteration is
\file{[n,a,h]}, equality is \file{[s,t]}, and $D$ is \file{[B]}.
The expected sort distinguishes a successor term from an equality
formula. Only an iterator body gains one bound coordinate; a
$D$ body gains two. Every proof-supplied term and formula is
validated in its actual scope.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Tag & Certificate form & Operation\\
\midrule
\file{r} & \file{["r",term]} & Reflexivity\\
\file{v} & \file{["v",major,minor]} & V0 or VS\\
\file{u} & \file{["u",index,target,premise]} & Clause use\\
\file{e} & \file{["e",goal,major,branch]} & Paired elimination\\
\file{i} & \file{["i",goal,witness,branch]} & Paired introduction\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The opening operation inserts only terms scoped at the current
free-parameter level; their free coordinates are negative.
Consequently insertion beneath a binder requires no renumbering
of these free coordinates. The opening traversal still increments
the protected bound depth and removes the opened bound index.
The freshness discipline is enforced by assigning one new
negative level per E or I branch and checking its conclusion
back at the old scope.

A structural invariant of successful derivations is that every
returned result is a well-scoped formula. Refl establishes it;
U uses a well-scoped retained clause; E and I validate their
conclusions and preserve branch scope; Eval checks matching
equation and iterator shapes. A nonsuccessor numerical counter
cannot pass the VS match: if it is a list of length two, the
term invariant already forces its head to be $0$.

The executable rejects malformed input, failed matching, invalid
scopes, and mismatched goals. It uses explicit failure rather
than \file{assert}, so Python's optimization flag does not remove
certificate checks. As with any finite interpreter, resource
limits may reject very large valid proof trees; no bounded
runtime can accept all finite certificates of unbounded size.

\subsection{Measurement procedure and exact diff}

The primary measure parses the complete module, removes its module
docstring (neither compared checker contains function docstrings),
applies \file{ast.unparse}, appends
one line feed, and counts UTF-8 bytes, significant Python tokens,
and AST nodes under CPython 3.12.14. Gzip-9 size is reported
separately as information: it falls from 990 to 979 bytes.

The source change removes the control's test \file{if b != s:}
and makes its iterator matching unconditional. All surrounding
validation and the four other instruction families are
byte-for-byte the same. The archive includes the exact normalized
diff, both complete sources, and a script that recomputes all
measurements. This controlled deletion is the basis of the
simplicity claim; it does not depend on renaming variables,
changing the certificate encoding, or omitting validators.

\subsection{Verification performed}

The executed main-suite evidence records:
\begin{itemize}
\item 32 inherited valid certificates and 5 targeted additional
      certificates, translated and checked in both directions;
\item 24 inherited invalid certificates, all rejected;
\item four original certificates that exercise the deleted branch:
      accepted by the control, rejected by the reduced checker
      before translation, and accepted after translation;
\item 260 command-line executions of the proposed source and its
      normalized source, under ordinary Python and Python \file{-O};
\item 112 additional command-line runs from the unchanged inherited
      test suite;
\item identical end formulas, no surviving Root nodes, compiler
      idempotence, and the exact $N+2k$ node count.
\end{itemize}
The cases exercise actual use of an induction hypothesis, nested
binders, capture-avoiding witnesses, closure specialization,
zero-source equality, nested numerical templates, malformed
values and scopes, wrong antecedents, and an iterator-counter
attack. These tests check implementations and specific proof
transformations. They are not a proof-assistant verification
of every certificate or of the bi-interpretation theorem.

\section{Other simplifications tested and exact limits}
\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{The zero-normal single-evaluation alternative}

A companion combines the same clause interface with the existing
zero-normal single-Eval arithmetic~\cite{identity,zero}. Contract
$I(0,a;u.h)$ to $a$ during substitution and write the smart
iterator as $\mathcal I(c,a;u.h)$. Its only evaluation schema is
\[
 \frac{\mathcal I(c,a;u.h)=t\qquad I(Sc,a;u.h)=r}
      {r=h\langle t/u\rangle},
\]
where angle brackets denote normalized substitution. Refl plus
this rule recovers equality and computation by the one-step
identity frame $I(S0,s;u.u)$. The same three clause operations
complete the five-family presentation.

This is a cleaner arithmetic interface, but canonical syntax
and normalization must be counted. The companion's complete
checker measures 3050 bytes, 871 tokens, and 1011 AST nodes.
A matched canonical control retaining Root measures 3107,
879, and 1023. Thus Root deletion improves that interface too,
but the 2999-byte raw presentation remains smaller under the
chosen primary measure.

Its proof translation must repair an introduction whose
nonliteral witness normalizes to zero. The raw alias
$I(0,0;u.0)$ disappears. The surviving canonical alias
$I(S0,0;u.0)$, together with equation transport and
ordinary-assumption cut, supplies the required case-mode proof.
The companion includes that translation and a mode-sensitive
test; it is not silently treating primitive I as stable under
arbitrary normalization.

\subsection{The smaller compact codecs are not surpassed}

The repository's 516-byte profile uses a different guarded
language and a backwards-consumed instruction stream~\cite{compact}. It
already combines source-selected equality and closure
specialization and does not contain the three-case Eval
interface being reduced here. It does not validate the complete
integer/list input tree or enforce its public-goal grammar.
Its paired strict version validates that tree and the closed
guarded public-goal grammar, and measures 1376 bytes.
Even that strict version remains smaller than this report's
unrestricted raw Eq/$D$ checker.

There is also a closer comparison: the incoming identity-frame
report's Eq/$Q$/$D$ checker has 2996 normalized bytes, 954 tokens,
and 1019 AST nodes~\cite{identity}. The new clause checker is
three bytes larger, although it uses 106 fewer tokens and 33
fewer AST nodes. This is another reason to claim a matched
improvement of the raw clause presentation, rather than a
minimum even over every transparent checker.

The exact source files and a contract audit were checked.
These differences explain the comparison boundary; they do
not turn the matched 3046-to-2999 saving into a global record.
The relevant conclusion is strictly local: a complete program
for the same full raw public language and certificate format
has become smaller in all three primary coordinates.

\subsection{Why no absolute simplest claim is made}

Rule counts depend on how syntax, normalization, schemas,
and proof instructions are divided. Source size additionally
depends on the implementation language and public contract.
This report proves an explicit deletion and measures its
unchanged-interface consequence. It neither proves a lower
bound over all bi-interpretable theories nor establishes that
the current repository-wide source-size frontier has been
beaten. The original request for an absolute simplest theory
therefore remains open.

\section{Further research questions}
\label{sec:further}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Formalize the whole clause interface.}
      The repository formalizes the native arithmetic bridge
      and the three-case Eval calculus. A useful next target
      is a Lean development of clause contexts, their scope
      operations, the two-case calculus, and both complete
      interpretation composites. The written proof here is
      short enough that this is a concrete verification project.
\item \textbf{Translate the reduction into the compact protocol.}
      The old guarded codec has no corresponding Root branch.
      A meaningful global size comparison needs a new compiler
      and an implementation using one common fully specified
      public contract, rather than comparison of unmatched
      source lengths.
\item \textbf{Improve global certificate overhead.}
      The local three-node gadget is optimal in its stated
      arithmetic fragment. A proof DAG might share zero-frame
      reflexivities or earlier lifts across several root
      steps. Bounds on total nodes and serialized bytes for
      such a compiler remain separate questions.
\item \textbf{Settle successor rigidity.}
      The newest raw-clausal report proves that the restricted
      rule $f(0)=0,\ f(Sz)=S(f(z))\Rightarrow f(t)=t$
      implies contextual induction for every zero equation
      $q(n)=0$. Whether it recovers actual equality reflection
      and all equation induction in the scalar language is
      still open in the audited material. Predecessor and
      pairing cannot be imported before the missing bootstrap
      is supplied.
\item \textbf{Find a substantive arithmetic deletion.}
      The numerical theory already has strong compression.
      A more significant advance would remove an arithmetic
      obligation, a term constructor, or an entire logical
      operation while preserving both interpretation
      composites and reducing a complete matched checker.
      No such further deletion is claimed here.
\end{enumerate}
