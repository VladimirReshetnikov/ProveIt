\section{Executable implementation and honest size comparison}

\subsection{The new calculus and its matched control}

The selected mathematical verifier is \path{code/checker.py}. Its comparison
program \path{code/control.py} has the same terms, formulas, clauses,
computation axioms, Use, full formula Tr, Open, scope checks, JSON interface,
and acceptance convention. The control takes an explicit term in Intro;
the candidate instead calls the deterministic witness selector. The formulas
are raw finite syntax: no normalization or external solver is hidden in the
trusted boundary.

The complete certificate format is in \path{code/FORMAT.txt}. Numerical zero
is the integer $0$; a free parameter is a negative level; positive indices
are de Bruijn bound variables. The numerical constructors are $[0,t]$ for
successor and $[n,a,h]$ for iteration, binding only in $h$. An equation is
$[s,t]$ and a paired formula is $[B]$, whose matrix binds two variables.
The formula validator enforces the guard at every atomic paired matrix.

In this encoding a literal naming matrix is
\[
 \bigl[\,[1,0,3],\ [1,0,r],\bigr],
\]
provided $r$ validates in the parent scope with no extra bound slots. The
index $3$ is the witness $x$ beneath the iterator's state binder; the row
and state occupy the nearer two slots. Internally bound indices of $r$
remain legal. The scope check rejects references to the matrix's witness,
row, or state even when those references are nested inside other iterators.

These measurements count the entire acceptance program, including parsing,
syntax, binding, scope, rule checking, and the final verdict. They use
CPython 3.12.14, removal of actual docstrings, \texttt{ast.unparse}, and one
final line feed. Significant tokens exclude whitespace, indentation,
comments, and end markers; AST nodes are counted by \texttt{ast.walk}.
The programs import only Python's standard library. Untrusted proof builders
and audit scripts are outside both checker measurements.

\textbf{The new mathematical calculus is not smaller under this matched
checker-size metric.} It saves the independent witness datum at each
introduction; recognizing the fixed naming exception costs more source in
this implementation. The comparison is included to prevent the interface
theorem from being mistaken for an achieved software minimum.

\subsection{What the executable checks establish}

The primary suite constructs ten accepted deterministic certificates and
eleven control certificates, including an original proof using a compound
iterator as its witness. It supplies an explicit deterministic proof of
that identical conclusion. It also embeds each of the ten deterministic
proofs into the control by restoring its inferred witness fields.

The most relevant structural fixture exhibits all three stages:
an introduction valid before scope extension, a rejected unchanged replay
after a newer parameter is inserted, and the successful naming repair.
Other positives use an equation induction hypothesis and a compound case
base assumption. Eighteen negative certificates attack guards, pattern
scope, bound-variable capture, witness escape, packet arities, and equality
premises. The suite performs 78 standalone executions across ordinary and
optimized Python, with five direct binding regressions.

An independently written targeted audit adds three positive and three negative
fixtures, twelve additional ordinary/optimized executions, and five direct
checks. It exercises full formula transport beneath one and two guarded
$D$ binders with nested iterator binders in the substituted term. It also
rejects an opened parameter hidden in the unused body of a zero iterator;
raw syntax cannot erase that escape.

The deterministic-to-control elaborator is complete for accepted finite
certificates in this format. The opposite direction is established by the
general mathematical proof and illustrated by an explicit translated pair;
this archive does not claim a complete implemented compiler in that direction.
All executed results are included as JSON. Finite tests cannot establish the
universal bi-interpretation theorem, and no new proof-assistant verification
is asserted.

\subsection{A separate smaller implementation of the inherited calculus}

The incoming native control performs a public-formula grammar traversal and
then a scope traversal. The selected refinement checks grammar during the
first scope traversal, using its existing depth parity as the formula/term
distinction. After that first traversal, a local phase flag is zero, and
every subsequent proof operation is the inherited operation.

The general idea of sharing these traversals already occurs in the
repository~\cite{scope}; the present change applies it to the newer
continuation-fused control. It is an implementation refinement of an existing
calculus. It does not implement the deterministic introduction of this paper.

The refined complete source is 15.9\% shorter in normalized bytes. It retains

Its native public-goal validator must also be distinguished from complete
validation of every raw proof field.

\paragraph{Universal equivalence argument.}
The inherited traversal starts at depth $d=0$ and enters a list of length
$r$ by adding $5-r$ to the depth. Formula lists have length $1$ or $2$;
numerical lists have length $1$ or $3$. A formula $D$ preserves even parity,
an equation changes even to odd, and both numerical constructors preserve
odd parity. Thus $d\bmod2$ already is the separate validator's sort flag.
During the initial pass the admissible list lengths are precisely
\[
                  r\in\{1,2+(d\bmod2)\}.
\]
Term integers must be exactly $0$ or odd integers at least $3$. The compact
test \texttt{not a or a > 1 == a \% 2} expresses exactly this on actual
integers, and a type test excludes booleans and floats.

The first-pass rejection path is forced to fail even at formula depth, where
the ordinary scope traversal would otherwise have a neutral scalar fallback.
Induction on the finite goal tree proves that this pass succeeds exactly when
both old passes succeed, and then returns the unchanged goal. A valid formula
is a nonempty list and cannot equal the initial scalar context sentinel.
Therefore the initial invocation necessarily clears its local phase flag
before reading a proof packet. All recursive invocations start with flag zero.
Their traversal and continuation expressions are exactly the old ones.
Induction on the consumed packet suffix proves equality of successful traces.
The full argument and finite checks are in \path{implementation/}.

\paragraph{The separate 506-byte compact profile.}
The archive also records a two-byte change to the inherited 508-byte
guarded compact module: replace $k-1$ by $\mathord\sim(-k)$. Python integer
complement gives $\mathord\sim(-k)=k-1$ for every integer, so all scopes
and accepted traces remain the same. It has 506 bytes, 267 tokens and
303 AST nodes, compared with 508, 267 and 302. This is arithmetic
source compression only, and actually adds one AST node. It supplies no
new mathematical simplification and no public-goal validator. We do not
present this two-byte saving as a breakthrough or as the implementation of
the new theory.

\section{Reproduction and deliverable map}

The archive contains the standalone report source and compiled PDF, the
editable component sources, the new checker and matched control, accepted
and rejected certificates, proof elaboration examples, reproducible
measurements, exact inherited comparison sources, the attributed arithmetic
bridge, and a checksum manifest. It does not contain nested copies of older
delivery archives or an unbuilt proof-assistant project.

Run from the extracted directory:
\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
python3 verify_manifest.py
python3 reproduce.py
python3 build_report.py
python3 code/checker.py < code/examples/accepted/arbitrary_witness_translated.json
\end{lstlisting}
The standalone \path{ipra_deterministic_introduction.tex} also builds directly
with pdfLaTeX; it has no external input files or graphics. The modular builder
regenerates it from the editable sources and the normalized checker listing.
Compilation disables shell escape and requires standard TeX Live packages.
The Python verifiers use only the standard library. Measurements are tied to
the stated Python version. All theoretical verifier claims concern ideal
finite executions with sufficient resources; a concrete process may exhaust
memory or recursion limits on a valid large proof.
