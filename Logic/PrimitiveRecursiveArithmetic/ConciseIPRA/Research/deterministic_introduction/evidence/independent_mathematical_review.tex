\subsection{The structural bootstrap for a deterministic newest witness}

Let $\Delta$ be an ordered numerical scope. The primitive introduction
chooses its witness from the \emph{parent} scope, before adjoining its fresh
step variable. It chooses the outer-scoped $r$ when its matrix is literally
$N_r(x,y)=C(y,0,x)=C(y,0,r)$, and otherwise chooses the last parameter of
$\Delta$, or $0$ if $\Delta$ is empty. The mode remains equation progress
versus compound cases, independently of the selected witness.

Call a derivation algebraic if its only nodes are computation axioms, Use,
and full formula transport. Algebraic derivations admit arbitrary injective
renaming and numerical or clause weakening by a direct induction. They do
not contain the context-sensitive introduction rule.

\paragraph{Algebraic cut.}
Suppose $\Gamma\vdash A$ has an algebraic derivation. Then from an arbitrary
new-calculus derivation of $\Gamma,A\vdash F$ one may construct a derivation
of $\Gamma\vdash F$. Induct on the receiving derivation and replace a Use
of the distinguished constant assumption clause by an algebraically weakened
copy of the donor proof. Removing a clause does not change the ordered
numerical scope, so it does not change any selected witness. The other
clauses and all numeric scopes remain intact. Under a binder, only the
algebraic donor is weakened; no general weakening theorem is being assumed.
The donor must not use the assumption being cut.

\paragraph{Unconditional names.}
For every outer-scoped $r$, the rule's naming exception proves $D N_r$ in
every context. Its step target is literally $C(Sz,0,r)=C(Sz,0,r)$, which
has an algebraic proof by zero computation and transport. Its local
assumption is unused. After opening the name at fresh $e$, the new clause
gives $e=r$ algebraically: its zero antecedent follows from computation,
Use at $1$ gives the unit equation, and both endpoints compute to $e,r$.

\paragraph{Full weakening.}
State the induction in its strong form: every derivation over $(\Delta,\Gamma)$
can be transported along an arbitrary injective numerical-scope embedding
$j:\Delta\hookrightarrow\Delta'$ and into any target clause context
containing $j\Gamma$. Induct on the height of the \emph{original} derivation.
Computation, Use, and transport are immediate. At Open, map its eigenparameter
to a fresh target parameter and apply the induction hypotheses to its two
proper premises.

At an original introduction, let $w$ be its old selected witness, after
applying $j$, and let $B$ be its mapped matrix. If $B$ is a naming matrix,
use the unconditional name proof and ignore the old branch. Otherwise prove
$D N_w$ in the enlarged outer context and open it at a new last parameter
$e$. Its clause supplies $e=w$ and $w=e$ algebraically. The introduction
of $D B$ in this scope must now select $e$.

Choose a fresh step parameter $z$. Apply the induction hypothesis only to
the original proper introduction branch, embedding its original step variable
as $z$. Enlarge that branch context by the name clause, the new ordinary
assumption $B(e,\eta_B(z))$, and, temporarily, the original ordinary
assumption $B(w,\eta_B(z))$. The induction hypothesis yields the original
target $B(w,Sz)$ in this context.

There is an algebraic proof of $B(w,\eta_B(z))$ from the name clause and
$B(e,\eta_B(z))$: Use retrieves the latter, and full formula transport along
$e=w$ gives the former. Algebraic cut therefore removes the old assumption
from the transformed branch. Transport along $w=e$ changes its conclusion
to $B(e,Sz)$. Deterministic introduction at the newest parameter $e$ now
proves $D B$, and Open discharges the name. Its final conclusion and retained
outer clauses contain no $e$.

This step invokes the height induction only on a strictly smaller original
branch. It invokes algebraic cut on the newly transformed proof, but algebraic
cut was established independently and has no height restriction. Thus the
enlarged proof constructed in this step is never fed back into the weakening
induction hypothesis.

\paragraph{General ordinary-assumption cut and completeness.}
After full weakening, ordinary-assumption cut follows by the standard
derivation induction, inserting weakened donor proofs at the corresponding
Use nodes. Every unrestricted old introduction is then simulated by naming
its supplied witness $t$, opening the name at fresh newest $e$, transporting
the new local assumption from $e$ to $t$, cutting the translated old branch,
transporting its result back to $e$, introducing at $e$, and discharging the
name. A naming-matrix goal is instead its unconditional theorem. No general
term substitution into a proof is needed at any point in the bootstrap.

The construction relies on full canonical-formula transport. Substituting a
term-only equality rule here would require a new proof that the cut donor is
stable, because its conversion of $B(e,\eta)$ can have a compound formula.
