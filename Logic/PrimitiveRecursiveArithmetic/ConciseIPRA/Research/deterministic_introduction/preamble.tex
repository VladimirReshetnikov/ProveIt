\begin{abstract}
The current ConciseIPRA proposals already reduce arithmetic and full
intuitionistic logic to unusually small proof interfaces. We prove a further
restriction of the guarded presentation: a paired introduction need not carry
a witness term, parameter index, or witness-choice bit. Its witness is read
from a fixed literal naming matrix when that pattern occurs; otherwise it is
the newest numerical parameter, with zero as the empty-scope default.

The restriction preserves every open consequence, not merely the closed
theorems. The main issue is that numerical-context weakening can change the
automatically selected witness. We resolve it by proving algebraic cut first,
then repairing weakening by an induction on the original derivation, and only
afterwards deriving general cut and unrestricted introduction. Composition
with the pinned guarded/native and native/iPRA bridges gives whole-domain
bi-interpretability, actual equality, identity numerical composite graphs
$x=y$, and equiconsistency with full first-order iPRA.

This is a reduction in independent inference data, not a claim of a globally
smallest axiomatization. Complete executable checkers and their measured costs
are included. Separate, small implementation refinements of the inherited
compact checkers are identified as such; their sizes are not attributed to
the new mathematical calculus. No new Lean or Rocq verification is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result, comparison, and the meaning of ``simpler''}

The comparison is pinned to ProveIt commit
\texttt{11814771b7490dfbea0ed5fcf0f5b23586334764}, under
\path{Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA}. It includes the
seventy-three filed packages described by that snapshot's README and the six
archives then present in \path{docs/incoming}. References~\cite{repo,incoming}
identify the exact sources. The delivered provenance file records their
identities and hashes.

The mathematical change is narrow and explicit. A previous introduction
contains an arbitrary term $t$ supplied by the proof author. Our introduction
contains no such datum. It obtains $t$ by a deterministic operation on the
matrix and the ordered numerical context. Both versions have the same two
computation schemas and the same four logical/equality inference families.
We do not count a restriction on an existing rule as deletion of another rule.

Three different comparisons must be kept distinct:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Inference interface.} The new rule deletes the independent
      witness field, including the special choice of a parameter index.
\item \textbf{Matched complete checker.} Recognizing the naming pattern is
      part of the trusted implementation. Its cost is measured against an
      otherwise matched unrestricted-witness checker below; it is not free.
\item \textbf{Repository source-size frontier.} The incoming archives already
      contain a 508-byte compact checker and a 780-byte version with its
      native public-goal validator. The coordinate minima 256 tokens,
      276 AST nodes, and 508 bytes belong to different programs with different
      contracts. They are attained sizes, not proved lower bounds.
\end{enumerate}

Accordingly, the mathematical result should be judged as a proved restriction
of witness choice. A numerical saving obtained by changing Python diagnostics
or integer expressions is a separate implementation result. Neither proves
that no simpler iPRA presentation exists.

\paragraph{What is inherited.}
The scalar iterator, paired binder $D$, guarded formula grammar, full formula
transport, clause contexts, ordinary logical reconstruction, and arithmetic
compiler all come from the pinned repository. Existential naming through
equations is also standard and appears in earlier proposals. The new theorem
is that a literal naming exception and a deterministic newest-parameter
default suffice for \emph{all} introductions, including the structural
admissibility argument required by that default.

\paragraph{Verification boundary.}
The universal equivalence result below is a mathematical proof. The code is
checked by executable certificates, adversarial examples, and comparison
with the matched source calculus. Those checks test implementations; they
are not a proof-assistant verification of the metatheorem. The repository's
existing downstream Lean bridge is cited precisely, and is not presented as
a formalization of this new rule.
