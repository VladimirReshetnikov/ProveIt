\section{The complete presentation and its equivalence proof}
\label{sec:deterministic}

The new calculus $\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}$ is defined here without relying on
an informal notion of a proof step. Its numerical scopes are finite ordered
lists of distinct parameters. Retained clauses are a separate finite list;
adding or deleting a clause does not alter the parameter list. The displayed
schemas range over finite, well-scoped syntax, with capture-avoiding
substitution and fresh bound names. There is no computation normalization in
syntax, no external theorem oracle, and no implicit equality decision.

\subsection{The inherited guarded calculus}

Terms are the raw terms
\[
 t ::= a\mid 0\mid S(t)\mid I(n,r;u.h),
\]
where only $u$ is bound in $h$. No computation normalization is built into
substitution. Put $C(v,r,s)=I(v,r;u.s)$ with $u$ fresh for $s$, and put
$H=(I(0,0;u.0)=0)$. A canonical formula is either an equation or $D_{x,y}B$,
where $B$ is compound or, if $B$ is an equation, has the literal shape
\[
                  I(y,0;u.h)=I(y,0;v.k).                 \tag{G}
\]
The bound variables $x,y$ are distinct. The state variables $u,v$ are bound
only in their respective update terms. Updates may depend on $x,y$ and all
outer parameters. Capture-avoiding substitution for outer parameters preserves
this grammar. The designated contradiction is $0=S0$.

A retained clause $\langle A;y.B(y)\rangle$ denotes
$A\to\forall y\,B(y)$; $y$ is absent from $A$. An ordinary assumption $A$
is the constant clause $\langle H;v.A\rangle$ with unused $v$. Every context
admits the two computation axiom schemes
\[
 I(0,r;u.h)=r,\qquad
 I(Sn,r;u.h)=h[I(n,r;u.h)/u].
\]
Besides these, there are four inference families:
\paragraph{Use.} For a retained $\langle A;y.B(y)\rangle$, infer
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash B(t)}.
\]
The numerical target $t$ is any term in the current numerical scope.
\paragraph{Transport.} For a canonical formula template $F(v)$, infer
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}{\Gamma\vdash F(t)}.
\]
\paragraph{Open.} With $b$ fresh for the outer context and conclusion, infer
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B\qquad
       \Gamma,\langle B(b,0);y.B(b,y)\rangle\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash A}.
\]
\paragraph{Unrestricted introduction (comparison calculus only).} Infer
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta_B(z))\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B},\qquad
 \eta_B(z)=\begin{cases}z,&B\text{ an equation},\\0,&B\text{ compound}.\end{cases}
\]

In Intro, $z$ is fresh for $\Gamma,t$ and all outer matrix parameters.
This is precisely the raw canonical guarded-core presentation of the pinned
repository, with full formula transport; it is not the later term-only
transport kernel. Its ordinary reading is
\[
 D_{x,y}B\;:=\;\exists x\bigl(B(x,0)\to\forall y\,B(x,y)\bigr).
\]
The previous guarded-core report supplies its translations and both composites
with the native calculus and hence with full first-order iPRA. Those results
are inherited here, rather than reasserted as a new proof from scratch.

\subsection{A witness determined by syntax and scope}

For an outer-scoped term $r$ define the literal naming matrix
\[
 N_r(x,y):=C(y,0,x)=C(y,0,r).                            \tag{Name matrix}
\]
Both updates are state-independent. In particular $r$ contains neither
matrix variable $x,y$ nor either freshly introduced state variable. Every
such matrix satisfies (G). Recognition is literal modulo bound-variable
renaming; it is not equality up to provability or computation.

\paragraph{An explicit numerical scope.}
Keep the ambient finite list of distinct numerical parameters separate from
the retained clause context. Write it as $\vec a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$.
Open appends its fresh witness to this list in its branch. Introduction appends
its step eigenparameter in its branch. Additional clauses do not by themselves
change the numerical scope. Define
\[
 \omega(B,\vec a)=
 \begin{cases}
 r,&B\text{ is literally }N_r\text{ with }r\text{ outer scoped},\\
 a_n,&B\text{ is not such a matrix and }n>0,\\
 0,&B\text{ is not such a matrix and }n=0.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{DW}
\]
The newest parameter is read from the \emph{parent} scope, before the fresh
step eigenparameter is allocated. A successful naming match takes precedence.
The theory $\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}$ has the same syntax, axioms, Use, Tr and
Open, and has only this introduction:
\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(\omega(B,\vec a),\eta_B(z))
              \vdash B(\omega(B,\vec a),Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B}.                           \tag{DI-d}
\]
Its certificate has a matrix and one branch proof, with no witness field.


\paragraph{Complete inventory.}
The new presentation consists of the stated term/formula grammar, the two
computation axiom schemas, Use, full formula Tr, Open, and DI-d. It has no
additional primitive hypothesis, reflexivity, weakening, cut, coverage,
successor, ordinary connective, or induction rule. Ordinary assumptions are
retrieved by Use with the fixed computation theorem $H$. The unrestricted
introduction displayed earlier defines only the comparison calculus
$\mathcal G$ and is not an extra rule of $\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}$.

\subsection{The structural bootstrap}

\paragraph{The structural problem.}
Changing the ambient numerical scope can change the selected witness. Thus
ordinary weakening is not justified by a blind rule-by-rule replay. In
particular, one must not use general cut, whose proof needs weakening of the
cut donor, to prove weakening of this calculus. The following bootstrap uses
a restricted cut first.

\begin{lemma}[Algebraic proofs and restricted cut]
Call a proof algebraic if it uses only the computation axioms, Use and Tr,
with no introduction or Open. Such proofs admit injective parameter renaming
and arbitrary extensions or reindexings of their available clauses and
parameters. Moreover, an ordinary assumption in an arbitrary
$\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}$ proof can be cut against an algebraic donor proof.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the first assertion, induct on the algebraic proof. Its rules have no
scope-dependent choice and no newly bound eigenparameter. For the second,
induct on the proof receiving the cut. A Use of the deleted constant clause
is replaced by the algebraic donor, extended and renamed by the first
assertion. At DI-d and Open, freshen the local variable and continue the
induction in the extended branch. The numerical parameter list is unchanged
when the ordinary-assumption clause is deleted, so DI-d selects exactly the
same witness before and after the deletion. This proves restricted cut
without general weakening of proofs containing DI-d.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Reflexivity and symmetry]
For every term $r$, $r=r$ is derivable using only the zero computation and Tr;
from $s=t$ the equation $t=s$ is derivable with the same resources.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $q=I(0,r;u.0)$. The computation equation $q=r$, used as both premises of
Tr with template $F(v)=(v=r)$, gives $r=r$. From $s=t$ transport $s=s$ with
template $F(v)=(v=s)$ to obtain $t=s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Every term has a name]
For every well-scoped term $r$, $\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}$ proves
$D_{x,y}N_r(x,y)$. Opening this theorem at a fresh parameter $b$ supplies a
context in which $b=r$ is derivable without any use of an unrestricted witness.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The deterministic naming alternative reads $r$ from the matrix. It asks for
\[
 \Gamma,N_r(r,z)\vdash N_r(r,Sz).
\]
The conclusion is literally
$C(Sz,0,r)=C(Sz,0,r)$, so reflexivity proves it independently of the local
induction assumption. This is a permitted progressive introduction on a
guarded equation; no numerical induction is needed in its proof.

Open the resulting theorem. Its clause is
$\langle N_r(b,0);y.N_r(b,y)\rangle$. Both terms in $N_r(b,0)$ compute to
$0$, so the antecedent follows from the two zero computations and Tr.
Use at $1=S0$ gives $C(1,0,b)=C(1,0,r)$. The successor computation for a
state-independent update gives $C(1,0,b)=b$ and $C(1,0,r)=r$. Symmetry and Tr
therefore give $b=r$. All these computations are literal instances of the
two stated axioms. The extracted equation and its symmetric form have
algebraic proofs from the opened clause.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weakening and injective renaming for determined introduction]
Let a finite $\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}$ derivation be given. Embed its parameters
injectively into any larger ordered parameter list, and embed its clauses,
after that renaming, into any larger clause context. There is an effectively
constructed derivation of the correspondingly renamed conclusion in the
larger context and scope.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the height of the \emph{original} derivation, with the parameter
and clause embeddings universally quantified. Shared computation, Use and
Tr cases are immediate. At Open, choose a receiving fresh witness, extend
the embeddings, and apply the induction hypotheses to its proper premises.

Consider a DI-d node with matrix $B$ and original selected witness $w$.
Let $B'$ and $w'$ be their images under the given injective parameter map.
Literal naming matches are preserved and reflected by such maps: bound
slots cannot become free parameters or conversely, and a free parameter
cannot become a fixed numeral or a term constructor. If $B=N_r$, the new
selected witness is exactly the renamed $r=w'$, and the branch induction
hypothesis gives the rule directly. If the default selected witness is also
unchanged, the same direct construction works.

Suppose, therefore, that $B'$ is not a naming matrix and its new default
witness differs from $w'$. Prove $D N_{w'}$ by the naming lemma and open it
at a receiving fresh parameter $e$, which is now newest. In this branch its
name clause gives the algebraic proof $e=w'$. We will introduce $D B'$ at
$e$; this is exactly the witness DI-d now selects. Let $v$ be its fresh step
parameter, and write
\[
 A_{\rm old}=B'(w',\eta_{B'}(v)),\qquad
 A_{\rm new}=B'(e,\eta_{B'}(v)).
\]
Apply the weakening induction hypothesis to the original DI-d node's
\emph{proper branch}, mapping its old step variable to $v$. Use a receiving
context containing the desired outer clauses, the name clause, and both
$A_{\rm old}$ and $A_{\rm new}$ as ordinary assumptions. This supplies
$B'(w',Sv)$ there. This use of the induction hypothesis is legitimate even
though $e$ and $v$ are newly allocated receiving parameters: the induction
statement quantifies over all embeddings of the smaller original proof.

From $A_{\rm new}$, transport along the algebraically proved $e=w'$ to
obtain $A_{\rm old}$. This is an algebraic donor. Restricted cut removes the
extra $A_{\rm old}$ assumption, giving $B'(w',Sv)$ with only the intended
local assumption $A_{\rm new}$. Algebraic symmetry and one further Tr give
$B'(e,Sv)$. Apply DI-d at newest $e$ and discharge its name by Open.

Every recursive call was on a proper subderivation of the original proof.
The newly built naming and transport proofs were never inputs to that
recursive weakening transformation. Restricted cut was already established.
Thus the construction is well founded and has no circular appeal to general
cut or weakening. The same argument includes a changed closed-scope default
$w=0$, in which case the added name is $N_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ordinary-assumption cut]
General cut of an ordinary assumption is admissible in
$\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the proof receiving the cut, replacing uses of the deleted constant
clause by the supplied donor. The theorem now supplies the donor's weakening
and fresh renaming at every insertion site. Deleting only a clause leaves
the numerical parameter list unchanged, so existing determined introductions
keep their selections.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The fully determined introduction is complete]
For every finite canonical clause context $\Gamma$ and canonical formula $A$,
in each ambient numerical scope,
\[
 \Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}}A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal G}A.
\]
Both transformations are effective and preserve the entire end sequent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The forward direction regards the selected witness as the witness of the
old Intro rule. For the converse, induct on the original $\mathcal G$ proof.
At an old introduction with a literal naming matrix $N_r$, ignore its
supplied witness and branch and use the independent naming theorem $D N_r$.
At any other old introduction, translate its proper branch by induction,
prove and open $D N_t$ for its old witness $t$, and let $b$ be this new
fresh parameter. In the context containing the opened name and the new ordinary
assumption $B(b,\eta_B(z))$, transport along $b=t$ produces
$B(t,\eta_B(z))$. General weakening and ordinary-assumption cut insert the
translated old branch, giving $B(t,Sz)$. Transport along $t=b$ gives
$B(b,Sz)$. The newest parent parameter is $b$, and
$B$ is not a naming matrix, so DI-d now selects exactly $b$. Close that
introduction and the name opening. The structural theorem and general cut
were proved before this comparison, so the simulation has no circularity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Full bi-interpretability and equiconsistency]
The ordinary realization of $\mathcal G_{\mathrm d}$ is bi-interpretable
and equiconsistent with full first-order iPRA. Both numerical domains are
whole, equality is actual equality, and both numerical composite maps have
graph $x=y$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The new comparison is the identity on all terms, formulas, clauses and
ordinary readings. Compose it with the pinned guarded/native and native/iPRA
interpretations described in Section~\ref{sec:bridge}. Their existing formula
round-trip derivations transfer without changing their statements. Neither
numerical domain nor numerical map changes. The contradiction $0=S0$ is
preserved, giving equiconsistency.
\end{proof}
Arbitrary numerical substitution is admissible through the exact comparison;
unchanged certificate replay after substitution is not claimed.

\subsection{Why the qualifications matter}

\paragraph{Why the root-selected guarded basis is useful.}
The simulation changes the chosen witness from an arbitrary term $t$ to a
parameter $b$. It is essential that this does not change the assumption of
the branch. The older rule that chooses progress exactly when the witness
is literal zero cannot simply be substituted into this argument: at $t=0$
it would use $B(0,z)$, whereas at $b$ it would use $B(b,0)$. The guarded-core
mode is independent of the witness. The substitution-stable diagonal-guard
basis also has this property and supports the same naming construction.

\paragraph{What the newest-parameter proof adds.}
The stronger theorem does remove the index field. Its extra mathematical
cost is exactly the preceding structural proof: ordinary numerical weakening
may require elaboration through a new name and algebraic transports. The
executable fixtures include a proof valid before scope extension, its failed
naive replay in a larger scope, and a repaired proof obtained by naming the
old selected parameter anew.

\paragraph{No implicit equality decision.}
Testing $B=N_r$ is a fixed syntactic match, including the absence of the new
bound names from $r$. Replacing that by ``some row names the witness'' would
need extra row data or proof search; a universal rule about a provable naming
row would reintroduce an equality-proof premise. The narrow pattern is
sufficient for completeness and is the simpler mathematical specification.
