\begin{abstract}
The current zero-normal scalar presentation of intuitionistic primitive
recursive arithmetic uses an equality identifying a preceding iterate to
evaluate the next iterate. This note replaces that premise by equality of
the initial values:
\[
 a=b,\quad I(Sc,a;u.h)=r
 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
 r=h\langle I(c,b;u.h)\rangle.
\]
Zero-counter iteration remains a syntax convention. The replacement and
the current rule derive one another using reflexivity and arithmetic
inferences alone; no induction or logical rule is needed for that
equivalence. The complete calculus still has five schemes. It retains the
same numerical terms, formulas, scopes, and normalized substitution, so the
whole-domain bi-interpretation with full first-order iPRA is preserved.
The checker and proof-certificate comparisons are deliberately modest:
the rule change ties the original implementation in one direct comparison
and saves two normalized bytes and two tokens in a second matched
implementation. Its standard computation certificates omit one repeated
predecessor iterator. This is a verified local improvement, not a proof of
absolute simplicity or a new compact-checker record.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result, comparison, and exact limits}

The requested target is full first-order intuitionistic primitive recursive
arithmetic: all intuitionistic first-order formulas are allowed, while
numerical induction is restricted to quantifier-free formulas. It is not
enough to obtain the same standard numerical functions, the same equational
theorems, or an equiconsistency statement without both interpretation round
trips. The result here preserves the entire consequence relation of the
native scalar clause calculus.

The pinned repository describes sixty-one research packages, a fully
formalized native scalar bridge to iPRA, and several increasingly small
arithmetic interfaces~\cite{baseline}. Its latest commit formalizes
reflexivity plus the uniform successor-step rule in
\file{NativeZeroStep.lean}. The five-scheme clause version is supplied in
\file{Research/uniform_step}; the clause presentation itself is a written
mathematical development, not one of that file's Lean theorems
\cite{nativezero,uniform}.

\begin{theorem}[Main local reduction]
Let $C_{\mathrm{Step}}$ be the zero-normal five-scheme clause calculus in
the pinned \file{uniform_step} package, and let $C_{\mathrm{Seed}}$ be the
calculus explicitly specified below. For every well-scoped finite clause
context $\Gamma$ and formula $A$,
\[
 C_{\mathrm{Step}}:\Gamma\vdash A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{\mathrm{Seed}}:\Gamma\vdash A.
\]
The end formulas and contexts are literally unchanged. Consequently the
ordinary realization of $C_{\mathrm{Seed}}$ is equiconsistent and
bi-interpretable with iPRA, with both numerical domains whole, equality
actual, and both numerical composite isomorphisms given by $x=y$.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{What is new relative to the inspected baseline.}
The new item is the fusion of initializer replacement with successor
evaluation in one crossed rule. The earlier \file{initializer_equality}
package has initializer replacement and a separate successor-computation
axiom. The \file{counter_evaluation} package fuses evaluation with equality
of counters in a different recursor language whose body binds both the old
counter and the state~\cite{initializer,counter}. Here the iterator is the
existing scalar iterator, its body binds one state variable, and the only
arithmetic axiom is reflexivity. The case $c=0$ supplies equality reasoning;
the general case supplies computation. No literature-wide priority claim
is made.

\textbf{The size verdict.}
The deliverable includes the original checker, a matched shared-walker
control, and the candidate. The candidate's smaller overall source comes
mostly from sharing syntax validation and substitution traversal. The
mathematical rule change contributes only the small residual saving in
that implementation. The original helper-based implementations tie.
Neither result beats the historical compact-checker frontier. Keeping
these distinctions visible is necessary to assess whether the change is
worth adopting under the repository's proof-checker-size metric.

\textbf{Assessment against the requested target.}
The construction meets the equiconsistency and bi-interpretability
requirements and gives a concrete alternative arithmetic interface.
It does \emph{not} establish a convincing new minimum for complete
proof-checker size. The positive results are the exact rule equivalence,
the smaller standard computation certificates, and a very small local
source saving. A more substantial simplicity improvement remains open.

\textbf{Verification status.}
The new equivalence is proved in writing and supported by executable
translations and certificate checks. The package does not claim a new
Lean or Rocq verification. The inherited scalar-to-iPRA bridge is cited
at its pinned repository source; its Lean build was not rerun in this
environment. All new checker and certificate comparisons below are generated
from the included files rather than inferred from instruction counts.

\section{Initializer evaluation: a smaller arithmetic interface}

The new rule compares an equality directly with an iterator's seed.
The current Step rule instead compares it with the entire preceding
iterate.  Both rules give exactly the same arithmetic consequences,
already without induction or any logical inference.  On the same
syntax and certificate protocol, the change also shortens the canonical
certificate for each successor computation with a syntactically nonzero
predecessor counter: its first premise becomes
the reflexivity of the seed, rather than the reflexivity of a whole
predecessor iteration.

This is a modest local simplification.  It retains the same five-rule
logical presentation, changes neither the numerical language nor its
normalization, and does not claim a globally minimal proof checker.
The separately reported whole-module measurements quantify its small
implementation improvement; the proof below establishes the mathematical
equivalence independently of those measurements.

\subsection{Unchanged syntax and normalized substitution}

Use raw scalar terms
\[
 t::=x\mid0\mid St\mid I(n,a;u.h),
\]
where $u$ binds only its occurrences in $h$.  Let $N$ be the baseline's
zero-only normalizer: normalize every child recursively, then replace
an iterator whose counter is literally zero by its normalized
initializer.  Do not unfold a successor iteration.  The numerical terms
of the calculus are the fixed points of $N$.

For already normal arguments use the abbreviation
\[
 K(c,a;u.h):=
 \begin{cases}
 a,&c\text{ is literally }0,\\
 I(c,a;u.h),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The counter, initializer, and body have all already been normalized.
Write $h\langle b\rangle:=N(h[b/u])$, with capture-free substitution.
All numerical substitutions in formulas and clauses have this same
normalizing convention.  The inherited coherence equations are
\[
 N(Nt)=Nt,\qquad
 N(t[\sigma])=N\bigl(N(t)[N\circ\sigma]\bigr).
\]
They follow by structural induction, lifting substitution under every
binder.  In particular, normalized substitution is associative and
introduces no variable that was absent from the term and substitution
images.  No new normalization operation is added by the present proposal.

\subsection{The arithmetic rule}

Retain reflexivity and replace Step by
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash a=b\qquad
       \Gamma\vdash I(Sc,a;u.h)=r}
      {\Gamma\vdash r=h\langle K(c,b;u.h)\rangle}
       \quad\mathrm{SeedEval}.\quad}
 \tag{SeedEval}
\]
The same $c,a,h$ occur in the displayed iterator.  The seed equality
does not change the counter, update body, or any passive parameter.
In a checker, all three are read from the second premise.  The first
premise's left side is compared directly with $a$.

For comparison, the baseline has
\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash K(c,a;u.h)=t\qquad
       \Gamma\vdash I(Sc,a;u.h)=r}
      {\Gamma\vdash r=h\langle t\rangle}
       \quad\mathrm{Step}.\tag{Step}
\]
Thus the new rule has not simply discarded a necessary equality
check.  Its conclusion is changed at the same time: it iterates from
the equal seed $b$ before performing the final update.  Both rules
coincide literally when $c=0$:
\[
 a=b,\qquad I(1,a;u.h)=r
       \quad\vdash\quad r=h\langle b\rangle.
 \tag{ZStep}
\]
The following elementary derivations use only this shared zero-counter
instance and reflexivity.

\subsection{A two-step equality pivot}

For a normal term $x$, put $J_x:=I(1,x;v.v)$.  Given
$p:x=y$ and $q:x=z$, apply ZStep to $q$ and the reflexivity of $J_x$;
then apply it to $p$ and the result:
\[
 \frac{x=y\qquad
       \displaystyle\frac{x=z\qquad J_x=J_x}{J_x=z}\ \mathrm{ZStep}}
      {z=y}\ \mathrm{ZStep}.
 \tag{Pivot}
\]
This works also when $x$ is literally zero and under arbitrary retained
assumptions.  Taking one premise reflexive gives symmetry.  Given
$x=y$ and $y=z$, first reverse the former, then pivot at $y$;
this gives transitivity.  These facts require no induction, numerical
cases, separation, or logical connective.

\subsection{Congruence in two zero steps; crossed transport in four}

Let $p:s=t$ and let $h(v)$ be any fixed normal numerical template,
possibly with passive parameters and nested iterators.  Set
\[
        H_s:=I(1,s;v.h(v)),\qquad
        h_s:=h\langle s\rangle,\qquad h_t:=h\langle t\rangle.
\]
First use ZStep on the reflexivities of $s$ and $H_s$ to obtain
$H_s=h_s$.  Reuse that result as the second premise of ZStep with
first premise $p$.  It gives
\[
 \frac{s=t\qquad
       \displaystyle\frac{s=s\qquad H_s=H_s}{H_s=h_s}\ \mathrm{ZStep}}
      {h_s=h_t}\ \mathrm{ZStep}.
 \tag{Cong}
\]
The reuse of the first result as the minor premise is what makes
congruence take two primitive steps.  It is unnecessary to derive
both $H_s=h_s$ and $H_s=h_t$ and pivot them.

Now suppose also $q:h_s=r$.  Pivot the derived $h_s=h_t$ with $q$.
The result is
\[
       s=t,\qquad h\langle s\rangle=r
           \quad\vdash\quad r=h\langle t\rangle.
 \tag{Tr}
\]
It uses the two zero steps in (Cong) and the two zero steps in (Pivot):
four primitive SeedEval instances in total.  The argument is valid for
every fixed numerical term template and every retained context.
The same macro is valid in the old Step arithmetic, whose zero instances
are identical. It is also present in the newly filed clause developments
and is not claimed here as a new transport principle.

\subsection{Every computation equation is derived}

Zero computation is the definition of $K$.  For successor computation,
write
\[
                 P:=K(c,a;u.h),\qquad B:=I(Sc,a;u.h).
\]
Apply SeedEval to the reflexivities of $a$ and $B$:
\[
                  \frac{a=a\qquad B=B}{B=h\langle P\rangle}
                         \ \mathrm{SeedEval}.
 \tag{Comp}
\]
This is exactly the baseline successor computation equation.  Unlike
the Step version, its major premise does not contain $P$.

\subsection{SeedEval is derived from Step, without induction}

Suppose the Step arithmetic has the two SeedEval premises
\[
                     p:a=b,\qquad q:I(Sc,a;u.h)=r.
\]
Use (Cong) in the fixed initializer template
$v\mapsto K(c,v;u.h)$, with the hole variable $v$ fresh for the
counter and update body.  It gives
\[
                     K(c,a;u.h)=K(c,b;u.h).
\]
Step with this equality as its first premise and $q$ as its second
premise gives exactly
$r=h\langle K(c,b;u.h)\rangle$.
Thus one SeedEval instance is simulated by two zero Steps and one generic
Step.  At $c=0$, the original instance is already a zero Step and needs
no expansion.

In ordinary arithmetic this is the same proof in familiar terms:
equal seeds give equal iterates by congruence in the seed argument;
successor computation then evaluates the displayed final step.
No induction proving agreement of two different update bodies is used:
the body $h$ stays syntactically fixed throughout the argument.

\subsection{Step is derived from SeedEval, without induction}

Conversely suppose the SeedEval arithmetic has the two Step premises
\[
              p:P=t,\qquad q:B=r,
       \qquad P=K(c,a;u.h),\quad B=I(Sc,a;u.h).
\]
The derived computation (Comp) gives $B=h\langle P\rangle$.
Apply Pivot with $q$ as its first premise and this computation as its second to obtain
\[
                          h\langle P\rangle=r.
\]
Apply the four-step crossed transport (Tr) in the body template $h$
along $p:P=t$.  Its result is $r=h\langle t\rangle$, the desired
Step conclusion.  The displayed expansion uses one computation SeedEval,
two pivot applications, and four transport applications: seven primitive SeedEval
instances.  Again the case $c=0$ is already a shared zero instance
and can be translated directly.

It follows that the two arithmetic fragments derive exactly the same
equations from every set of retained equations.  More strongly, the
translations can be inserted into arbitrary larger derivations while
leaving every boundary formula unchanged.  Each new inference is a
finite derived inference of the other fragment.  None of these
equivalences invokes induction, case distinction, or any logical rule.

\subsection{The complete logical and nonlogical specification}

Use formulas and their ordinary readings
\[
 A::=(s=t)\mid D_{x,y}B(x,y),\qquad
 (D_{x,y}B)^*:=\exists x\bigl(B(x,0)^*\to\forall y B(x,y)^*\bigr).
\]
The designated contradiction is $0=S0$.  A retained clause is
$[A;y.B]$, with reading $A^*\to\forall y B^*$; the displayed $y$
binds only the body $B$.  Write
\[
             \operatorname{Assm}(A):=[0=0;y.A]
             \qquad (y\notin\operatorname{FV}(A)).
\]
The five primitive schemes are precisely:
\begin{align*}
 \mathrm{Refl}:\quad&\Gamma\vdash t=t;\\[1mm]
 \mathrm{SeedEval}:\quad&
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash a=b\qquad\Gamma\vdash I(Sc,a;u.h)=r}
      {\Gamma\vdash r=h\langle K(c,b;u.h)\rangle};\\[2mm]
 \mathrm{Use}:\quad&
 \frac{[A;y.B]\in\Gamma\qquad\Gamma\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash N(B[t/y])};\\[2mm]
 \mathrm{DE}:\quad&
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B\qquad
       \Gamma,[N(B[b/x,0/y]);y.N(B[b/x])]\vdash A}
      {\Gamma\vdash A};\\[2mm]
 \mathrm{DI}:\quad&
 \frac{\Gamma,\operatorname{Assm}(N(B[t/x,\eta/y]))
           \vdash N(B[t/x,Sz/y])}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B}.
\end{align*}
All displayed formulas and terms are normal and well scoped.
In DE, $b$ is fresh for the outer context, conclusion, and passive matrix parameters.
In DI, $z$ is fresh for the outer context, witness $t$, and passive
parameters, and
\[
 \eta=
 \begin{cases}
 z,&B\text{ is a literal equation and }t\text{ is literally }0,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Outer clauses are weakened when entering a fresh-variable scope.
The chosen witness and the outer conclusion are checked in the outer
scope before opening the branch.  There are no further primitive
equality, computation, successor, logical, or induction axioms.
Use with the reflexivity of zero supplies the ordinary hypothesis rule
for an encoded assumption.

\subsection{Exact full-theory equivalence and bi-interpretation}

Call the old five-scheme calculus $C_{\mathrm{Step}}$ and the new one
$C_{\mathrm{SeedEval}}$.  They have literally the same terms, formulas,
clauses, contexts, substitution, and freshness conditions.  Replace
each arithmetic inference in a derivation by its finite expansion
above, and retain every logical inference unchanged.  Structural
induction on derivations gives, for every finite clause context
$\Gamma$ and formula $A$,
\[
 C_{\mathrm{Step}}:\Gamma\vdash A
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{\mathrm{SeedEval}}:\Gamma\vdash A.
 \tag{Exact}
\]
The end formulas are identical, not merely provably equivalent.
No witness is re-encoded or normalized differently, so the DI mode is
unchanged.  No new term-normalization theorem or new body-congruence
argument is required for this passage between the two calculi.

The baseline's ordinary scalar realization is therefore also the
ordinary realization of the new calculus.  Its inherited
bi-interpretation with full first-order intuitionistic primitive
recursive arithmetic composes with the identity presentation map
in (Exact).  Both numerical domains remain whole, numerical equality
remains actual equality, and the same provable identity graphs $x=y$
witness both composite isomorphisms.  Formula round trips and all
quantified consequences are inherited, since every sequent of the
same native language is preserved.  The contradiction $0=S0$ is
unchanged, so equiconsistency follows as well.

The new mathematical result is an explicit equivalence of arithmetic
inference interfaces.  The already established scalar-to-iPRA bridge,
zero-normal syntax, clause logic, and witness-directed introduction are
used with attribution; they are not claimed as new constructions.

\subsection{An exact certificate-size improvement}

Consider the standard tree certificate for one successor computation,
with target $B=h\langle P\rangle$ as above.  Its Step version has
the proof tree
\[
              \operatorname{Step}(\operatorname{Refl}(P),
                                   \operatorname{Refl}(B)),
\]
whereas SeedEval uses
\[
              \operatorname{SeedEval}(\operatorname{Refl}(a),
                                   \operatorname{Refl}(B)).
\]
The number of proof-constructor nodes is unchanged.  Count one node
for a numerical constant or variable and one for each constructor.
If $c\ne0$ syntactically, then
\[
 |P|=1+|c|+|a|+|h|,
 \qquad
 |P|-|a|=1+|c|+|h|.
\]
That is the exact reduction in supplied numerical syntax nodes in
this proof tree.  When $c=0$, $P=a$ and the saving is zero.
The public goal is identical in both certificates, so its size
cancels in the comparison.

For the report's tagged compact JSON terms
$I(c,a;u.h)=[\texttt{"i"},c,a,h]$, with no optional whitespace,
write $\ell(t)$ for the serialized term's UTF-8 length.  For nonzero
$c$, replacing the reflexivity of $P$ by that of $a$ saves exactly
\[
                         8+\ell(c)+\ell(h)
\]
bytes in the proof field: the iterator's list/tag/separators contribute
eight bytes, and the counter and body are no longer repeated there.
The saving is unbounded as the body grows, although it is asserted
only for these standard computation certificates.

This is not a claim that every SeedEval proof is shorter than every Step
proof of the same result.  General proof translations can expand
derivations, retained clauses can provide short references, and a DAG
certificate format may share subterms.  The precise established
advantage is removal of the repeated predecessor-iterator syntax from
the canonical two-reflexivity computation proof, together with the
separately measured complete-checker reduction.

\section{The full intuitionistic bridge}

\subsection{Why a change in the arithmetic rule is enough}

The preceding exact-sequent theorem is stronger than a standard-model
soundness check. A proof may have arbitrary retained clauses, use a
quantified formula as an assumption, discharge an assumption, or introduce
a fresh numerical parameter. Replacing one arithmetic inference by its
finite expansion leaves all its boundary formulas unchanged. It therefore
fits inside every one of those proof contexts. The induction restriction
and every eigenvariable condition remain the original ones.

The bridge being used has three layers:
\[
 C_{\mathrm{Seed}}\ \equiv\ C_{\mathrm{Step}}
 \ \equiv\ T_{\mathrm{eq}}\ \simeq\ \ipra.
\]
Here the first equivalence is this note's new theorem. The second is the
existing clause/native-logic translation, with the usual zero-normal
translation. The last is the repository's whole-domain bi-interpretation.
$T_{\mathrm{eq}}$ uses ordinary intuitionistic equality logic, the scalar
iterator computations, numerical coverage, separation of $0$ from $S0$,
and induction for literal equations under arbitrary retained assumptions.
Those are theorems or interpretations of the native presentation, not
additional primitive rules appended to the five-scheme calculus.

\subsection{The logical interface is explicit}

For orientation, the conditional closure of a native formula can be
represented using the one public binder:
\[
 \widehat Q_y B(y):=D_{x,y}B(y),\qquad x\notin\operatorname{FV}(B).
\]
Its ordinary reading is equivalent to $B(0)\to\forall y B(y)$, since the
domain has the closed inhabitant $0$. Given this closure and a proof of
$B(0)$, DE opens the retained clause $[B(0);y.B(y)]$; Use at $t$ proves
$B(t)$. Thus there is no missing primitive universal-elimination rule.

For equation progress, suppose
$\Gamma,\operatorname{Assm}(E(z))\vdash E(Sz)$, where $E$ is a literal
equation and $z$ is fresh. DI at dummy witness $0$ derives
$\widehat Q_y E(y)$. Combining this with a base proof and closure
elimination gives equation induction. This explains exactly where the
arithmetic induction strength resides. It is not induction for arbitrary
quantified formulas.

The case form of closure introduction can use the normal closed term
\[
 q:=I(1,0;u.u).
\]
It is not the literal $0$, while a single derived computation proves
$q=0$. At the dummy witness $q$, DI selects case mode. The inherited
case-recovery argument also handles an atomic matrix whose intended
existential witness is $0$; the derivation and its freshness details are
given in the supplied \file{uniform_step_theory.tex} reference.

The other intuitionistic connectives have the existing native macros.
Let $\operatorname{sel}(v,A,B)$ be the recursively constructed formula
which is $A$ at $v=0$ and $B$ at a successor, and let
$\operatorname{gate}(v,A)$ be true at zero and equivalent to $A$ at a
successor. These formula constructions are obtained from the term
selector $I(v,a;u.b)$ and extended through the native binders. With the
existing parity term $\operatorname{par}$, one may use:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
 \bot&:=&0=S0,\\
 A\to B&:=&\widehat Q_y\operatorname{sel}(y,A,B),\\
 A\land B&:=&\widehat Q_y\operatorname{gate}
                  (y,\operatorname{sel}(\operatorname{par}(y),A,B)),\\
 \exists x\,A&:=&D_{x,y}\operatorname{gate}(y,A(x)),\\
 A\lor B&:=&\exists v\,\operatorname{sel}(v,A,B),\\
 \forall x\,A&:=&A(0)\land\widehat Q_y A(y).
\end{array}
\]
Their introduction and elimination rules and both logical round trips
are part of the pinned native-logic bridge~\cite{baseline,bridge}. The
selected written foundational sources are included with this archive.
The Seed equivalence preserves those derivations exactly, so it does not
need to assume a new truth predicate, a function-variable sort, or
classical excluded middle.

\subsection{The two numerical interpretations}

In the interpretation into iPRA, $0$, $S$, and equality have their usual
meanings. For each fixed finite body $h(u,\vec p)$, the expression
$I(n,a;u.h)$ denotes the primitive recursive function obtained by iterating
that body $n$ times from $a$, with $\vec p$ held fixed. The symbol describes
one finite primitive recursive definition. There is no internal universal
evaluator for arbitrary primitive recursive programs.

In the interpretation from iPRA, each primitive recursive function
description is compiled into the already constructed scalar arithmetic.
The existing bridge first constructs predecessor, then a faithful equality
test, reflection of quantifier-free formulas, pairing, and a compiler for
primitive recursion. These steps ensure the interpretation works on the
entire internal domain, including nonstandard elements. They do not rely
on agreement only at standard numerals. In particular, induction for
equations transfers to induction for arbitrary quantifier-free formulas
after that reflection theorem is available.

Composing either interpretation with the other returns the original
numerical functions up to provable equality, with numerical identity map
$x\mapsto x$. Formula round trips return provably equivalent formulas.
The native and ordinary round-trip statements are present in the pinned
\file{NativeBiInterpretation.lean} and \file{BiInterpretation.lean}
sources~\cite{bridge,ordinarybridge}. Since the new presentation map is
the identity on all terms, formulas, and domains, it does not alter those
maps or their proofs.

\subsection{Equiconsistency means equivalence of contradiction proofs}

A Seed proof of $0=S0$ translates to an old native proof and then to an
iPRA proof of falsity. Conversely, an iPRA proof of falsity translates
through the inherited bridge and then to Seed. Hence
\[
 C_{\mathrm{Seed}}\vdash 0=S0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \ipra\vdash\bot.
\]
This proves equiconsistency between the two presentations. It is not a
claim that either theory proves its own consistency.

\section{The standalone checker and its trust boundary}

\subsection{Concrete numerical and formula syntax}

The main checker is a single standard-library Python module. It accepts a
JSON object with exactly the fields \file{goal} and \file{proof}; the goal
must be a closed, well-formed zero-normal formula. Its syntax is:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
JSON object & Meaning\\
\midrule
\file{0} & Numerical zero.\\
Positive integer $j$ & The $j$th enclosing numerical binder, counted from one.\\
Negative integer $-j$ & Rigid free-variable level $j$ in the current scope.\\
\file{['s',t]} & Successor.\\
\file{['i',c,a,h]} & Iterator; only its body binds one variable.\\
\file{['=',s,t]} & Equation.\\
\file{['d',B]} & Paired binder; in $B$, index 1 is $y$ and index 2 is $x$.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The printed examples use Python-like single quotes for readability; the
actual certificates use JSON double quotes. A normal iterator never has
literal counter zero. Opening a binder can expose such a counter, so
opening recursively contracts the new zero iterators.

Each internal clause is a pair $(A,B)$: $A$ is in the current scope and
$B$ is under one additional bound variable. At a new eigenvariable scope,
the fresh parameter receives the next unused negative level. Existing
negative levels stay fixed. This representation implements the usual
weakening of the old context without physically shifting all of its
free-variable names.

\subsection{Certificate constructors}

The five instructions are direct encodings of the five schemes:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
Certificate & Checked inference\\
\midrule
\file{['r',t]} & Reflexivity of a validated normal term.\\
\file{['x',p,q]} & Seed, with both premise conclusions synthesized.\\
\file{['u',j,t,p]} & Use retained clause $j$, proving its antecedent with $p$,
and instantiate its body at validated target $t$.\\
\file{['e',A,p,q]} & DE: $p$ proves a paired formula, and $q$ proves outer
goal $A$ under the fresh witness clause.\\
\file{['i',D,t,p]} & DI: $D$ is a validated paired goal, $t$ a validated
witness, and $p$ proves the prescribed successor instance under the
appropriate assumption.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The goal and witness of a branch are validated in the outer scope before
the new parameter is introduced. This prevents a witness or conclusion
from depending on the supposedly fresh parameter. Clause indices are
exact integers, not Boolean values or negative Python indices.

\subsection{The two-line mathematical change}

In the unchanged helper-based upstream checker, the old arithmetic tail is
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
require(s == iterator(n, a, h))
return ['=', r, opening(h, t)]
\end{lstlisting}
The candidate replaces it by
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
require(s == a)
return ['=', r, opening(h, iterator(n, t, h))]
\end{lstlisting}
This version has exactly the same measured normalized size as its control.
It makes the mathematics particularly easy to audit: the first premise
changes from a predecessor-iterate equality to a seed equality, and the
missing predecessor iterator moves into the conclusion.

The main delivered implementation shares a single typed traversal between
syntax validation and normalized opening. In that implementation the smart
constructor is expressed inline, so the same rule change saves the pair
of parentheses needed around a conditional expression in the old
comparison. That is the source of the two-byte and two-token matched
improvement. It is a small syntax-level consequence of a different valid
rule, not evidence of a major decrease in the mathematical content.

\subsection{Soundness and completeness of the acceptance interface}

The proof of checker soundness is induction on a successful call to
\file{derive}. Reflexivity validates its term. Use obtains a scoped
antecedent/body pair from an already valid context. For Seed, the induction
hypotheses ensure that both synthesized equations are valid and normal;
pattern matching identifies the second equation's successor iterator,
the explicit comparison identifies its seed, and normalized opening
constructs exactly the displayed conclusion. DE and DI implement their
fresh-variable premises in the enlarged scope and compare the resulting
formula with the required branch target.

The same induction gives the normality and scope invariant for every
returned formula. The traversal checks all children before contracting a
zero iterator; an erased child cannot conceal a malformed term. Its use
of exact integer and list types excludes Boolean indices and non-syntax
objects. In the opener, inserted terms have only rigid free levels at the
outer scope; locally bound indices remain protected by the depth counter.
No arbitrary open bound-index tree is inserted without this invariant.

Conversely, encode any finite derivation of the five schemes recursively
using the five displayed certificate constructors, choosing consecutive
negative levels for its eigenvariables. Every rule check then succeeds.
This supplies completeness for the specified finite syntax and proof
objects. The public verifier restricts this general internal statement to
closed theorems with empty context.

The trusted checker contains no \file{assert}, \file{eval}, or
\file{exec}. Explicit exceptions reject. Standard Python resource limits
still apply to a concrete run: stack or memory exhaustion is rejection or
an unfinished computation, not an accepted proof. The mathematical
acceptance relation is understood with unbounded finite resources; no
claim is made that every derivation fits one process's default recursion
limit.

\subsection{What executable validation establishes}

The included translator reconstructs a source proof's conclusion at each
node and replaces arithmetic instructions by the derived macros proved
above. Its output is checked again by the independently selected target
checker. Tests cover numerical computation, equality symmetry,
transitivity and transport, clause use, paired introduction and
elimination, induction that actually uses its hypothesis, fixed passive
parameters, and nested numerical binders. Negative certificates exercise
bad indices, malformed syntax, invalid seed matches, wrong conclusions,
and eigenvariable escape. Public-process tests also run with
\file{python -O}, where Python assertion checks are disabled.

Finite tests can catch errors in the implementation and the translators;
they do not replace the universal written equivalence proof. The exact
counts and measurement environment appear in the generated evidence table
below and in \file{evidence/validation.json}.

\section{A further structural reduction that did not win on size}

An independently useful alternative factors every successor iteration
into an opaque one-step application. Introduce a suspended numerical
application $U(a;u.h)$ with the intended reading $I(1,a;u.h)$, and normalize
only by
\[
 I(0,a;u.h)\longrightarrow a,
 \qquad
 I(Sn,a;u.h)\longrightarrow U(I(n,a;u.h);u.h).
\]
No reduction is performed on $U$. The arithmetic rule then becomes
\[
 \frac{a=b\qquad U(a;u.h)=r}{r=N(h[b/u])}.
\]
It has no arbitrary counter. Every old Step becomes one application of
this rule after factorization, and the same zero-step pivot recovers
equality. The two unconditional rewrite rules are orthogonal. A direct
positive-integer weight proves termination, including duplicated bodies:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 w(0)=w(x)&=1,&w(St)&=w(t)+1,\\
 w(U(a;u.h))&=w(a)+w(h)+1,\\
 w(I(n,a;u.h))&=(w(n)+1)(w(a)+w(h)+1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The successor factorization decreases this weight by exactly $w(a)>0$;
the zero rewrite decreases it as well. Every constructor is monotone in
its child weights. Normalization is therefore total, confluent, and
substitution coherent. It never executes an iterator body. The supplied
notes give both presentation maps, both round trips, and the formulation
that retains the old term grammar by using $I(1,a;u.h)$ as an abbreviation
instead of a new constructor.

The complete implementation is the decisive qualification. In the shared
forward format, the explicit-$U$ checker measured 2,902 bytes, 803 tokens,
and 864 AST nodes, against 2,804, 758, and 815 for its Step control.
Its normalization and extra constructor cost more than the simplified
arithmetic match saves. It is therefore included as a proved structural
alternative, not as the recommended checker-size improvement.
The standalone source, matched control, measurement script, and 83 checked
positive certificates are supplied in \file{notes/unit_experiment/}.

This outcome also explains why moving computation into syntax is not
automatically a simplification. The syntax constructor, its normalization,
its scope handling, and its validation must all remain inside the cost
boundary. Deleting an inference rule while omitting those costs would
give the wrong comparison.

\section{Further research questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A larger mathematical reduction.}
Can the arithmetic interface be reduced beyond reflexivity and one
evaluation scheme while retaining a small, explicitly counted syntax
layer? The all-false interpretation of atomic equations obstructs simply
deleting reflexivity from the unchanged clause calculus. Relocating it to
an initial universal clause still leaves a reflexivity axiom to count.

\item \textbf{Proof sharing and initializer evaluation.}
Can an initializer-oriented arithmetic kernel reduce certificate size for
broader families of proofs, including nontrivial preceding-state equalities,
without the seven-step translation overhead? The exact tree-certificate
saving should also be compared against DAG certificates and explicit
subterm tables, with those decoders counted.

\item \textbf{Partial normalization with a real net gain.}
Is there a useful normalization between zero contraction and full numerical
evaluation that reduces both the arithmetic interface and the complete
trusted implementation? Suspended application gives a clean equivalence
and a primitive recursive normalizer, but the measured implementation here
does not meet that stronger target.

\item \textbf{Narrower induction principles.}
The existing successor-rigidity approach derives induction for zero
equations, but its faithful equality-reflection bootstrap remains a
separate obligation. Can it be completed without importing general
equation induction at an intermediate step? Counter-aware recursion gives
a simpler predecessor, but does not by itself discharge the reflection
argument. This note does not claim a solution.

\item \textbf{Formal verification and a common benchmark.}
Add the SeedEval constructor and its equivalence to the existing
\file{NativeZeroStep} development, then connect the same proof to the
formalized clause layer. Separately, benchmark the compact, goal-directed,
and forward checkers under explicit common contracts for syntax validity,
open sequents, certificate sharing, and error behavior. A lower bound for
one encoding should not be presented as an absolute mathematical lower
bound for all presentations of iPRA.
\end{enumerate}
