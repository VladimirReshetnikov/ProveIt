# Seeded crossed uniqueness: a six-family normalized calculus for iPRA

Prepared as a mathematical research note, 12 November 2026.

## Status and scope

This note proves a change of primitive basis, not a new absolute minimum and not a claim to beat the current checker envelope. Recursion uniqueness as an alternative to induction is classical. The potentially useful contribution here is narrower: **one arbitrary-seed, crossed uniqueness rule can supply both equality reasoning and equational induction**, so that neither equality transport nor an induction-hypothesis rule is primitive. In the resulting native calculus, paired introduction always uses cases and never tests the syntactic shape of its matrix or witness.

The language is the repository's scalar iterator language, represented in full computational normal form. Normalization is a required part of the trusted specification and must be included in any complete checker size comparison. This is not a rule-count or byte-count win over every current proposal.

For the scalar uniqueness -> equational induction bootstrap, this note uses and audits the existing written theorem in:

- Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/Research/scalar_uniqueness/report.tex,

The source was fetched in full to alternative/scalar_uniqueness_report.tex. Its central dependencies and algebraic constructions are spelled out below. No claim of a new proof-assistant verification is made.

## 1. Syntax and definitional computation

Raw terms are

  t ::= x | 0 | S(t) | I(n,a;u.h),

where u is bound in h only. Terms have one numerical sort. A template f(z) means a fixed finite term with the indicated free numerical variable; it is not a variable ranging over functions.

Computational conversion is generated by

  I(0,a;u.h) -> a,
  I(S n,a;u.h) -> h[I(n,a;u.h)/u].

Let nf be full normalization for these equations. Every formula and term in the calculus is stored canonically up to this normalization and alpha equivalence, and every substitution is capture-avoiding substitution followed by nf. Thus, for example,

  C(v,a,b) := I(v,a;u.b)

satisfies C(0,a,b) = a and C(Sv,a,b) = b definitionally, for symbolic v as well as numerals. Also I(1,a;u.h) is definitionally h[a/u]. Here 1 means S0.

This normalizer is total on finite well-scoped terms. One way to establish this is structural normalization by evaluation. To evaluate an iterator term, first normalize the counter and seed; traverse the finite successor prefix of the normalized counter, applying the total function supplied by the structurally smaller body at each successor. A neutral counter is retained as an iterator with a reified body. The structural induction on the source term supplies totality of the body's semantic function; recursion through a counter value is recursion on its finite syntax. The usual renaming, substitution, and environment-composition lemmas establish

  nf(nf(t)[sigma]) = nf(t[sigma]).

The existing repository formalization supplies this exact normalization layer (Tm.nbe, Tm.full, and Tm.full_subst_congr). The theory is not silently quotienting terms by all provable arithmetic equalities; only the two displayed computational equations are definitional.

Formulas are

  A ::= (s=t) | Q_y A | D_{x,y} A.

Q binds y; D binds x and y. Contexts are finite reusable lists of formulas. There is no implicit equality logic, induction, substitution-of-equals instruction, or classical rule.

The ordinary readings are

  Q_y B(y) := B(0) -> forall y B(y),
  D_{x,y} B(x,y) := exists x (B(x,0) -> forall y B(x,y)).

The designated contradiction is 0=1.

## 2. Complete primitive rule inventory

All displayed expressions are normalized as in Section 1.

### Hypothesis (Hyp)

  Gamma |- A, if A occurs in Gamma.

### Reflexivity (Ref)

  Gamma |- t=t.

### Conditional closure elimination (QE)

  Gamma |- Q_y B(y)       Gamma |- B(0)
  ------------------------------------
              Gamma |- B(t)

### Paired elimination (DE)

  Gamma |- D_{x,y} B(x,y)       Gamma,Q_y B(b,y) |- A
  --------------------------------------------------
                      Gamma |- A

The numerical eigenparameter b is fresh for Gamma, A, and the passive parameters of B.

### Paired introduction by cases (DIc)

  Gamma,B(t,0) |- B(t,Sz)
  -----------------------
  Gamma |- D_{x,y} B(x,y)

The eigenparameter z is fresh for Gamma, the witness t, and all passive parameters of B. This rule treats equation and compound matrices alike. It has no progress mode.

### Seeded crossed uniqueness (U)

  Gamma |- f(0)=a       Gamma |- f(Sz)=h(f(z))
  ------------------------------------------------
           Gamma |- I(n,a;u.h(u))=f(n)

The displayed f and h are fixed finite numerical templates. The eigenparameter z is fresh for Gamma, a, n, h, and all passive parameters of f. In particular h has no active counter argument: it may depend on passive numerical parameters but not on z. The recurrence premise has no induction hypothesis.

There are six rule families. There are no separate computation axioms, equality transport rules, or equation-induction rules.

## 3. Structural admissibility

Weakening, alpha-renaming, capture-avoiding substitution followed by normalization, and assumption cut are admissible by induction on derivations, freshening eigenparameters first. Every rule is stable under these operations. The normalization-substitution coherence equation above handles the canonical syntax.

This is a useful difference from introductions dispatched by “literal witness = 0”: no substitution can change the mode of DIc or U because neither rule has such a mode test. The U recurrence binder is always fresh and distinct from the update binder.

No numerical induction is used in this metatheoretic induction on finite derivations.

## 4. Numerical cases before equality

The following rule is derivable before proving any equality property beyond Ref:

  Gamma |- A(0)       Gamma |- A(Sz), z fresh
  -----------------------------------------
                 Gamma |- A(t).

To see this, use DIc with an unused witness to derive D_{w,y}A(y). Its premise under A(0) is the given successor branch, weakened by A(0). Open this D formula at a fresh witness b, obtaining Q_y A(y), and use QE with the given A(0). The result A(t) is independent of b, so DE discharges the opening.

A related conditional introduction rule is also derived:

  Gamma,A(0) |- A(Sz)
  ------------------
    Gamma |- Q_y A(y).

DIc gives the D formula with unused witness, and DE exports its Q assumption itself by Hyp. These derivations do not invoke equality transport or equation induction.

## 5. Equality from U

All the following are explicit finite derivations. Equality is being derived from the six primitive rules, not borrowed from ambient first-order logic.

### 5.1 Symmetry

Suppose Gamma |- s=t. In U take

  f(z)=s,  a=t,  h(u)=u,  n=0.

The initial premise is s=t. The recurrence premise is s=s, supplied by Ref. The conclusion normalizes to t=s.

### 5.2 Congruence in any numerical template

Suppose Gamma |- s=t, and let k(u) be any finite numerical template. In U take

  f(z)=I(z,s;u.k(u)),  a=t,  h=k,  n=1.

The first premise normalizes to s=t. The recurrence premise normalizes on both sides to

  k(I(z,s;k)),

and is Ref. The conclusion normalizes to

  k(t)=k(s).

Applying the symmetry just derived gives k(s)=k(t). This includes templates that duplicate, omit, or place their argument underneath iterator binders, with capture avoidance. It also includes arbitrary passive parameters.

### 5.3 Common-source joining

Suppose Gamma |- s=t and Gamma |- s=u. Define

  f(z)=C(z,s,u),  h(v)=v.

We claim Gamma proves the recurrence f(Sz)=f(z), which normalizes to

  u=C(z,s,u).

Derive it by the numerical cases rule from Section 4:

- At z=0 it is u=s, obtained by symmetry of the second given equation s=u.
- At z=Sv it is u=u, supplied by Ref.

Now apply U with seed a=t and target n=1. Its initial premise is f(0)=t, i.e. s=t. Its conclusion normalizes to

  t=u.

This derivation uses numerical cases but no numerical induction, no cancellation, no successor injectivity, and no pre-existing equality transitivity.

### 5.4 Transitivity

From s=t and t=u, first derive t=s by symmetry. Apply common-source joining to t=s and t=u. The result is s=u.

### 5.5 The repository's one-term crossed transport

From s=t and k(s)=r, numerical congruence gives k(s)=k(t). Join the two equations with common source k(s) to obtain r=k(t). Thus the existing crossed transport rule is derived exactly.

### 5.6 Full formula transport

For every formula template F(v),

  Gamma |- s=t, Gamma |- F(s)  ==>  Gamma |- F(t)

is derived by structural induction on F.

- For an equation p(v)=q(v), use numerical congruence, symmetry and transitivity.

- For F(v)=Q_y B(v,y), work temporarily under B(t,0). The induction hypothesis applied to t=s changes B(t,0) to B(s,0). QE at Sz changes Q_y B(s,y) and B(s,0) to B(s,Sz). The induction hypothesis for s=t changes this to B(t,Sz). Conditional Q introduction from Section 4 gives Q_y B(t,y).

- For F(v)=D_{x,y}B(v,x,y), open the given D formula at a fresh b, retaining Q_y B(s,b,y). Introduce D_{x,y}B(t,x,y) with witness b. Under its temporary base B(t,b,0), reverse transport of the matrix gives B(s,b,0); QE at Sz gives B(s,b,Sz); forward matrix transport gives B(t,b,Sz). DIc closes the desired D formula, which does not mention b, and DE discharges b.

Only the structurally smaller matrix B is used in the two binder cases. There is no circular appeal to transport in the whole formula.

## 6. Ordinary intuitionistic logic is recovered before induction

The usual ordinary logical interface of the repository is now available, because it requires computation, actual equality reasoning, the Q/D case rules, and Ref, all established above. It does not require equation progress.

For completeness, the macros can be given explicitly. Replace a Q formula inside a macro by its equivalent D formula with unused first variable. On equations define

  G_v(s=t) := C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t),

and extend G through D binders. The zero instance of a gate is provable; every successor instance is the original formula definitionally. A formula selector M_v(A,B) is formed by selecting equation endpoints with C, recursively through aligned D prefixes. Pad the shallower formula by the equivalent wrapper D_{w,y} G_y A until prefixes align. Then M_0(A,B) is equivalent to A and M_{Sv}(A,B) to B.

Let p(z)=I(z,0;u.C(u,1,0)). Only its finite computations p(1)=1 and p(2)=0 are needed for conjunction elimination. With fresh unused variables, define

  bottom_* := (0=1)
  A ->_* B := D_{w,z} M_z(A,B)
  A and_* B := D_{w,z} G_z M_{p(z)}(A,B)
  exists_* x A := D_{x,y} G_y A
  A or_* B := exists_* v M_v(A,B)
  forall_* x A(x) := A(0) and_* D_{w,z} G_z A(z).

The ordinary intuitionistic introduction and elimination rules follow using the derived cases rule, QE, DIc, DE, and formula transport. No excluded middle is added. The constructions are those of the repository's induction-independent logical interface; the primitive-rule changes above do not alter them.

Explosion is particularly transparent: under 0=1, apply numerical congruence to v |-> C(v,s,t), obtaining arbitrary s=t by normalization. Formula-structural induction using DIc and conditional Q introduction gives every compound formula. Coverage n=0 or exists m n=Sm follows by numerical cases, using the ordinary macros and Ref.

This yields ordinary intuitionistic first-order logic with actual numerical equality, scalar computation, coverage, and 0!=1, before any use of equational induction.

## 7. Recovering existing scalar uniqueness

From a premise Gamma |- f(Sz)=h(f(z)), apply U with a=f(0). Its initial premise is Ref. Its conclusion is

  I(n,f(0);h)=f(n).

By symmetry,

  f(n)=I(n,f(0);h).                                      (UR)

This is exactly the unconditional scalar uniqueness principle proved sufficient in the existing scalar_uniqueness report. It is essential that h does not depend on z. No higher-order function quantification or simultaneous recursion has been introduced.

## 8. Why UR gives equation induction: dependency audit

The existing written scalar bootstrap is applicable now: Sections 4--6 already supplied its ordinary logical and equality background without using UR or equation induction. The following is its dependency order.

### 8.1 Zero-equation induction by retraction rigidity

UR gives I(n,0;u.Su)=n by comparing the template f(z)=z to the successor update.

For a fixed term e(x), assume in a retained context Gamma

  e(0)=0, e(e(x))=e(x), e(S(e(x)))=S(e(x)).

Set h(u)=e(S(e(u))) and r(n)=I(n,0;h). The equations make e(r(n)) and r(n) have the same seed and autonomous update h, so UR gives e(r(n))=r(n). The third equation then makes r have update S, whence UR gives r(n)=n. Therefore e(n)=n. This is retraction rigidity and uses no induction.

For a decidable property A(n) with a term e(n) equal to n when A(n) and to 0 otherwise, a proved base A(0) and step A(z)->A(Sz) give the three retraction identities: A(e(n)) holds in both cases; apply the true-case equation to e(n), then apply the step at e(n). Rigidity and formula transport give A(n).

For A(n) equal to q(n)=0, coverage and separation decide A(n), and

  e(n)=C(q(n),n,0)

is the required term. Thus UR gives induction for all zero equations.

### 8.2 A scalar predecessor without induction

The explicit construction in the existing report has no product-state recursion.

Define C(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b), Z(v)=C(v,1,0), addition a+b=I(b,a;u.Su). Common-update uniqueness proves the commutative monoid laws of addition and iterator rotation.

Define the clocks and ramp

  N(n)=I(n,0;u.Z(u)),
  j(u)=Z(u)+2N(u),
  M(n)=I(n,0;u.j(u)),
  g(u)=u+N(M(u))+1,
  G(n)=I(n,0;u.g(u)).

Coverage gives the finite ranges of N and M. Direct finite cases prove

  M(g(u))=Z(M(u)).

Common-update uniqueness then gives M(G(n))=N(n), and further fixed updates give

  G(2n)=3n,
  G(3n)=3G(n)+N(n).

With H(u)=C(N(u),G(u),u), one obtains

  H(2u)=3u, H(1)=1, H(3u)=3H(u).

Put E(x)=I(x,1;u.2u), L(x,y)=N(I(y,E(x);H)). Rotation and the preceding equations give

  L(0,y)=1,
  L(Sx,0)=0,
  L(Sx,Sy)=L(x,y).

Set

  B_m(u)=C(L(Su,m),u,Su),
  W(t,m)=I(t,0;u.B_m(u)).

Common-update uniqueness gives W(St,Sm)=S W(t,m), then W(n,n)=n and W(Sn,n)=n. Finally,

  P(m)=I(m,0;u.C(L(SSu,m),u,Su))

satisfies P0=0 and P(Sm)=m: at parameter Sm its update is exactly B_m, by the L recurrence, so P(Sm)=W(Sm,m)=m. Successor injectivity follows by congruence with P.

Every recurrence comparison here is unconditional; no predecessor, pairing, equality test, or equation induction is assumed early.

### 8.3 Faithful equality reflection without equation induction

Define d(a,b)=I(b,a;u.Pu), sg(a)=C(a,0,1). UR gives

  d(Sa,Sb)=d(a,b), d(a,a)=0, d(0,a)=0.

A constant-recurrence consequence of UR says f(Sz)=f(z) implies f(n)=f(0).

A diagonal descent principle follows. If a scalar term q(x1,...,xr) is unchanged by simultaneous successor of all coordinates, and is zero when any coordinate is zero, it is identically zero. Coverage first gives q(Px1,...,Pxr)=q(x1,...,xr). Apply constant recurrence to

  k |-> q(d(x1,k),...,d(xr,k)),

and evaluate at k=x1.

Apply this principle to Boolean xor terms to obtain

  Z(d(Sn,a)) = sg(d(a,n)),                              (B1)
  Z(d(d(x,a),d(b,a)))
     = or(Z(d(x,a)),Z(d(x,b))),                         (B2)

where or(p,q)=C(p,q,1) on Boolean arguments. All zero-coordinate boundaries are checked by coverage and the monus equations. Also or(sg(u),sg(v))=sg(u+v).

The explicit scalar accumulator

  mu(n,a)=d(n,d(n,a)),
  phi(n,a,b)=mu(n,a)+mu(d(n,a),d(b,a))

satisfies

  phi(0,a,b)=0,
  phi(Sn,a,b)=phi(n,a,b)+sg(d(a,n)+d(b,n)).

For the first summand, split d(Sn,a) into zero and successor and use P(d(Sn,a))=d(n,a). B2, B1 and the sign-or identity then give the displayed accumulator step.

Let m(a,b)=a+d(b,a). Four numerical cases give

  m(a,b)=m(Pa,Pb)+sg(a+b).

Consequently the two terms

  F(n)=m(d(a,n),d(b,n))+phi(n,a,b),
  G(n)=m(d(b,n),d(a,n))+phi(n,a,b)

both have identity update. At n=b their values agree because d(b,b)=0 and m(c,0)=m(0,c)=c. Their constant values therefore agree, proving

  a+d(b,a)=b+d(a,b).

Thus

  Delta(a,b)=sg(d(a,b)+d(b,a))

is Boolean and satisfies Delta(a,b)=0 iff a=b. The forward implication follows because both differences are zero and the preceding identity then says a=b; the reverse follows from d(a,a)=0. This is equality on the whole numerical domain.

### 8.4 General equation induction

Given a base s(0)=t(0) and a contextual step s(z)=t(z) -> s(Sz)=t(Sz), translate both through the equivalence

  s(n)=t(n) iff Delta(s(n),t(n))=0.

Apply zero-equation induction from Section 8.1 and translate back. This gives induction for every literal equation in arbitrary retained first-order contexts.

The audit found no circular invocation of a primitive recursive equality test, scalar predecessor, pairing, general recursion compiler, or the equation induction being recovered. The substantial scalar bootstrap is inherited and attributed, not a new contribution of the U rule.

## 9. The converse interpretation and exact equivalence

The six-rule calculus is sound in the repository's native iPRA-equivalent calculus:

- Hyp, Ref, QE, DE, DIc are already available.
- Definitional computations are provable equalities there, and replacing a formula by its normal form preserves derivability.
- U is derived by literal-equation induction on

    I(z,a;h)=f(z).

  Its base uses f(0)=a and symmetry. Its successor step uses iterator computation, congruence of the induction equation, and the given recurrence f(Sz)=h(f(z)). The target is the stated U conclusion.

Conversely, translate a native derivation to normal forms. Computation axioms become Ref; native equality transport is Section 5; cases are primitive; and Section 8 supplies equational induction, hence native Q introduction by progress. In more detail, from Gamma,E(z) |- E(Sz), work under E(0) and use recovered equation induction to derive E(Sw) for fresh w; conditional Q introduction from Section 4 yields Q_y E(y). Every native rule therefore has a finite translation.

This proves equality of all corresponding normalized native sequents, not only coincidence of closed numerical theorems. Through the repository's native scalar-to-iPRA bridge it gives equiconsistency and bi-interpretability. The numerical domain is unchanged, actual equality is derived, and the numerical composites have identity graph x=y. Formula round trips use the already established native ordinary-logic macros.

The result is conditional only in the normal mathematical sense that it composes the explicitly cited written scalar-uniqueness and native scalar bridge theorems; it is not a substitute for a new Lean verification of their composition.

## 10. Exact claims suitable for the final report

Proved in this note:

1. The arbitrary-seed crossed U schema, Ref, and numerical cases derive all equality reasoning.
2. Numerical cases themselves follow from DIc, DE and QE without equality or induction.
3. U then yields existing scalar UR, and the existing written scalar bootstrap yields all contextual equation induction.
4. U is derived in iPRA by equation induction, so the resulting native calculus is equivalent on all open sequents after normalization.
5. The six primitive rule families are stable under substitution and have no matrix-shape or literal-witness dispatch.

Not established here:

1. A complete checker below 253 significant tokens, 275 AST nodes and 506 normalized bytes, or a coordinatewise improvement on any later incoming files.
2. An absolute complexity minimum.
3. A proof-assistant formalization of this new fusion.
4. Historical novelty of recursion uniqueness, or a claim that moving computation into syntax removes its implementation cost.

A candid deliverable can call this an equality-and-induction fusion or a new primitive-basis proposal. It must report an implemented checker's actual full size and any failure to beat the current envelope.
