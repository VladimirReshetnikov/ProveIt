SCOPE ARGUMENT FOR THE POSITIVE COMPOSITIONS
------------------------------------------
At check(a,g,k), the parameter floor k equals minus the number of locally
retained assumptions, beginning at zero. The opener closes over that
call's k. A major subproof receives the same g and k, and its local updates
cannot change the caller's k. A continuation lowers k by one and appends
one assumption. An input accepted under the old floor cannot contain the
new negative eigenparameter at a meaningful numerical position.

This is exactly the inherited lexical-scope scheme. A finite canonical
proof is compiled by alpha-renaming each branch's eigenparameters to
local negative integers. The nested proof's private labels may be reused
after it returns. The arity decoder and certified-zero computation rules
are otherwise unchanged. Ordinary alias qualifications remain unchanged.

The loop variants use an IMMUTABLE tuple for g. An in-place list update
would leak assumptions from a major subproof into its caller and is not
an acceptable implementation. Both supplied tested loop files use tuples.

The generic traversal in those loops retains the inherited qualification
that nonempty malformed strings can rely on the actual Python recursion
limit to reject. The 562-byte explicit-list program and 554-byte
zero-product-list program do not have that qualification.

FINITE VERIFICATION
-------------------
check_local_composition.py uses the inherited named proof producer with
a lexical-floor wrapper, without importing a candidate checker. Its 31
cases include arithmetic, genuine contextual equation induction, nested
quantifiers, compound transport, and targeted rejection controls. Three
profiles, ordinary and optimized Python: 186 full CLI expectations passed.
The two tuple-loop profiles passed another 124 expectations on those
same cases. See local_composition_validation.json and
local_loop_validation.json. These finite runs are not formal soundness
proofs or Lean/Rocq verification.

OTHER EXPERIMENTS, NOT SELECTED
-----------------------------
long_names.py removes the global floor by allowing negative free names
and taking a fresh negative integer larger in magnitude than a decimal
encoding of the present sequent. It has not received a complete alias
audit or new producer, grows names rapidly, and is NOT a certified kernel.
It does not improve metrics (566 bytes, 272 tokens, 317 AST).

axiom_leaf*.py implement a direct computation leaf instead of computation
supply. The mathematical normalization is ordinary-assumption cut:
replace each use of the supplied equation by its computation leaf.
The void version returns [generated_equation].index(goal), so mismatch
raises. The boolean version propagates failure explicitly. These programs
need a producer with no allocation at computation leaves and have NOT
been replayed against such a producer. Their best byte size is 599,
above the frontier, so they are not selected candidates.

OTHER INVESTIGATED ROUTES
------------------------
An opaque-object eigenparameter in the old flat stream cannot be named
by future JSON packets, so a complete system would need scoped proof
opening or an environment. That obstruction and an additional float
collision counterexample were independently established by the corpus
agent. They are not a new positive construction.

Direct definitional computation would remove an arithmetic dispatcher,
but a correct normalizer needs binder-aware substitution under protected
slots, neutral counters, and a reflexivity closure. The current opener
only fills the exposed single hole; it cannot substitute an inner state
slot while preserving and lowering outer de Bruijn slots. No smaller
complete normalizer was obtained.

Hypothesis-only major elimination is already ruled out in the supplied
adaptive-seed draft: it loses the canonical closed Q_y(0=0) theorem.
That failed route must not be represented as a novel discovery here.
