Conditional versus progressive closure: exact criteria
=====================================================

Status and scope
----------------
These are written mathematical observations, not a new smaller iPRA
axiomatization or a machine-checked proof. They explain exactly when the
conditional and progressive readings of an atomic binder coincide. The
standard y=1 obstruction and anchored-progress introduction are already
present in the inspected corpus; neither is claimed as new. The criteria
and existential distinction below may be useful as a synthesis or as a
screening test for new matrix grammars. No literature-wide novelty claim is
made.

Fix full first-order intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic T=iPRA,
with usual intuitionistic equality and genuine falsity. Write 1=S(0).
Let E(y,p) be a literal equation, with p a finite tuple of passive
parameters. All formulas below may retain p; their universal closures are
theorems whenever the displayed formulas are said to be theorems. Define

  a := E(0,p),
  U(E) := forall y E(y,p),
  C(E) := a -> U(E),
  P(E) := forall y (E(y,p) -> E(S(y),p)).

The argument uses two established facts of iPRA, in exactly these roles:

  (i) a or not a (decidability of numerical equality);
  (ii) P(E) -> C(E) (contextual induction for the equation E).

It uses no excluded middle for quantified formulas or for an existential
statement. The corpus supplies the equality-reflection/decidability bridge
in the inherited equality_reflection.tex. Do not transplant the argument
into a weakened scalar core before that bridge is established.

1. Exact pointwise criterion
---------------------------
Theorem. T proves

  (C(E) <-> P(E)) <-> (not E(0,p) -> P(E)).                 (1)

Equivalently, for every retained context Gamma,

  Gamma |- C(E) <-> P(E)
       iff
  Gamma |- not E(0,p) -> P(E).

Proof. First, P(E) -> C(E) is equation induction under an assumed base a.
It remains to characterize C(E) -> P(E).

Forward: assume C(E) -> P(E) and not a. Under a, obtain falsity and hence
every E(y,p); discharging a gives C(E). Apply the assumed implication to
obtain P(E). This direction needs neither decidability nor induction.

Reverse: assume not a -> P(E) and C(E). Decide a.
* If a holds, C(E) gives U(E). For arbitrary y, U(E) gives E(S(y),p), so
  E(y,p) -> E(S(y),p), and therefore P(E).
* If not a holds, use the assumed implication directly.
Discharge C(E). Combining this implication with P(E) -> C(E) proves (1).
All case splits are on the one decidable numerical equation a.

A related useful identity, also provable intuitionistically using only
decidability of a, is

  C(E) <-> (not a or U(E)).                               (2)

For C(E) -> the disjunction, decide a: its false branch gives not a, and
its true branch gives U(E). Either disjunct conversely implies C(E).

Meaning of (1). A true-base guard is sufficient but not necessary. The
exact condition is that a false base must itself suffice for progress.
This says nothing about the checker cost of recognizing the family of
matrices satisfying that semantic condition.

2. Examples and the sharp obstruction
-------------------------------------
2a. True base. If T proves E(0,p), then not E(0,p) implies every formula,
including P(E). Thus (1) specializes to the familiar guarded equivalence.

2b. Constant false base. For E(y):=(0=1), both C(E) and P(E) are theorems,
while the base is false. This already shows that a true base is not
necessary.

2c. Nonconstant false-base example using only the scalar iterator. Put

  Csel(v,a,b) := I(v,a;u.b),
  H(y) := Csel(y,0,1),
  E(y) := (H(y)=1).

Computation gives H(0)=0 and H(S(z))=1. Hence T proves not E(0), and it
also proves E(S(z)) without an induction hypothesis. Consequently P(E)
and C(E) are both theorems. E is not constantly false: T proves E(1).
The ordinary standard interpretation is the predicate y>0.

2d. Existing sharp obstruction. For E(y):=(y=1), the base 0=1 is false,
so C(E) is a theorem. P(E) is false: instantiate its universal quantifier
at y=1, use 1=1, and obtain 2=1, contradicting successor injectivity and
separation. Thus T proves not P(E). This is precisely a failure of the
right side of (1).

This example is NOT new. It is explicitly recorded in
  baseline/incoming/ipra_deterministic_auxiliary/reference/
    guarded_implementation.tex
in the paragraph explaining why the unrestricted raw atomic D calculus
does not have the claimed guarded formula composites.

2e. Why decidability is an actual hypothesis. For arbitrary predicates in
intuitionistic logic, the converse implication in (1) need not hold.
Take a two-world Kripke frame w<v with constant natural-number domain.
At w force E(1), but not E(0) or E(2); at v force E(n) for every n. Force
no other E(n) at w. Then at w:
* C(E) is forced, since every future world forcing E(0) forces all E(n);
* not E(0) is forced nowhere, so not E(0) -> P(E) is forced vacuously;
* P(E) is not forced, since E(1) holds at w and E(2) does not.
This is only a logical counterexample for an arbitrary predicate E, not
an iPRA model of numerical equality. It identifies the role of equality
decidability and prevents an unjustified extension to arbitrary formulas.

3. Exact existential criterion and witness changes
-------------------------------------------------
Now let E(x,y,p) be an equation, and abbreviate

  C_x := E(x,0,p) -> forall y E(x,y,p),
  P_x := forall y (E(x,y,p) -> E(x,S(y),p)),
  D_C := exists x C_x,
  D_P := exists x P_x,
  F   := exists x not E(x,0,p).

Theorem. T proves

  (D_C <-> D_P) <-> (F -> D_P).                           (3)

Proof. D_P -> D_C follows by existential elimination and equation
induction at the selected witness.

For the remaining direction, suppose D_C -> D_P and F. Select a witness
x with not E(x,0,p). That false base gives C_x by explosion, hence D_C,
and therefore D_P.

Conversely, suppose F -> D_P and D_C. Select x with C_x and decide the
single equation E(x,0,p).
* If it is false, x witnesses F; apply F -> D_P.
* If it is true, C_x gives forall y E(x,y,p), hence P_x; use x as the
  existential witness for D_P.
This proves D_C -> D_P, completing (3).

The same proof can be summarized by the intuitionistically valid form

  D_C <-> ((exists x not E(x,0,p)) or
           (exists x forall y E(x,y,p))),

where (2) and distribution of exists over a finite disjunction are used.
The second disjunct always implies D_P. No decision of F is used.

Pointwise criterion versus existential criterion. The condition

  forall x (not E(x,0,p) -> P_x)

is sufficient for (3), but is not necessary. The witness establishing
D_P after a false-base witness is found may be a different number.

Concrete scalar example. Set

  E(x,y) := (Csel(x,1,y)=1).

At x=0, the matrix is identically 1=1. Therefore C_0 and P_0 are both
theorems, and T proves both D_C and D_P. In particular their equivalence
is a theorem. At x=1 the matrix is y=1. Thus not E(1,0) holds while P_1
is refutable. The pointwise condition fails at x=1 even though the
existential binders are equivalent. This is an explicit reason not to
silently pass from a Q-level necessity statement to a D-level one.

4. Base-conjunction normalization
--------------------------------
For any equation E(x,y), first consider the quantifier-free matrix

  A(x,y) := E(x,0) and E(x,y).

Then T proves, pointwise in x,

  A(x,0) <-> E(x,0),
  C(A_x) <-> C(E_x),
  P(A_x) <-> C(E_x).                                     (4)

For the second equivalence, under E(x,0) the statements forall y A(x,y)
and forall y E(x,y) are equivalent. For the third:
* C(E_x) -> P(A_x): from A(x,y) obtain E(x,0); C(E_x) then supplies
  E(x,S(y)), and the same base gives A(x,S(y)).
* P(A_x) -> C(E_x): assume E(x,0), hence A(x,0), and use quantifier-free
  induction on A to obtain forall y A(x,y), then forall y E(x,y).
Alternatively apply (1) to a literal-equation representation of A:
if its base fails, every A(x,y) fails, so progress is vacuous.

A can be represented by one literal equation inside iPRA. For example,
let delta(s,t) be a primitive-recursive Boolean disequality test with
values 0 and 1 and with delta(s,t)=0 iff s=t. For E(x,y)=(s(x,y)=t(x,y)),
the equation

  delta(s(x,0),t(x,0)) + delta(s(x,y),t(x,y)) = 0          (5)

represents A(x,y), since a sum of naturals is zero exactly when both
summands are zero. Alternatively use an injective primitive-recursive
pairing function pi and the equation

  pi(s(x,0),s(x,y)) = pi(t(x,0),t(x,y)).                  (6)

Equations (5) and (6) are iPRA representations, not new primitive
function symbols or unproved reflection axioms for the scalar basis.
The already-established arithmetic compiler can elaborate their fixed
primitive-recursive descriptions into scalar terms.

Consequently exists x P(A_x) and the old ordinary exists x C(E_x) are
equivalent, with the same witness. This gives a systematic matrix
normalization beyond the special case of a literally true base.

Limit. This is not yet a smaller proof kernel. Counting the reflection
or pairing construction, the normalized matrix may be much larger than
the existing selector guards. Moreover its direct inference-rule form
is the corpus's OLD anchored DI:

  Gamma, E(t,0), E(t,z) |- E(t,S(z))
  --------------------------------
             Gamma |- D E.

For compound matrices it retains only the base assumption. This rule is
explicitly displayed as 'Old DI' in inherited_choice_basis.tex, so
anchored progress must not be presented as a novel simplification.

5. The proposed disjunction E(y) or E(0) does not work
----------------------------------------------------
Let B(y):=E(y) or E(0). Its base is equivalent to E(0), and C(B) is
always a theorem: under E(0), every B(y) follows from the right disjunct.
Thus this operation destroys the conditional content rather than
preserving C(E). For E(y):=(y=0), C(E) is false, but C(B) is true.
It also does not force progress when the base is false: for E(y):=(y=1),
B reduces to the original counterexample.

The correct disjunctive normalization is instead

  G(y):=not E(0) or E(y),

equivalently E(0)->E(y), using decidability of E(0). Its base is provable,
and C(G), P(G), and C(E) are equivalent. This is again a true-base guard
at the level of quantifier-free formulas, requiring an atomic reflection
if a literal-equation induction interface is demanded.

6. Consequences and non-consequences for restricted induction
------------------------------------------------------------
After equality reflection is available, every equation can be encoded by
a zero-test equation, so induction restricted to s(y)=0 suffices for full
equation induction. That observation does not supply the missing
bootstrap for a new scalar theory whose only induction is already
zero-test induction. The present notes do not prove such a bootstrap.

Likewise, induction restricted to W(s)=W(t) is equivalent to general
equation induction whenever W has a provably defined left inverse R:
congruence wraps the premises, restricted induction gives the wrapped
conclusion, and applying R cancels W. The transparent iterator wrapper
W(s)=I(0,s;u.0) has this property by computation; that mechanism is
already used in protected/guarded proposals. Taking W=S requires a
separate proof of successor cancellation (or a left inverse), and that
proof cannot simply be assumed in the weakened arithmetic.
