OPEN SEQUENTS WITH A BUILT-IN NEUTRAL TERMINAL
============================================

Status: a small certificate-interface refinement of the existing calculus.
It deletes no mathematical axiom or inference rule. It is not a solution
below all three current compact source-size minima.

1. Files and measured result
----------------------------
checker_sequent458.py: 265 significant tokens, 297 AST nodes, 458 normalized
UTF-8 bytes. The matched incoming checker_bytes460.py has 266 / 299 / 460.
All measurements use CPython 3.12.14, ast.unparse plus one final newline.
The new checker improves all three coordinates of THAT matched control.
It improves only the byte coordinate of the componentwise compact frontier
239 / 255 / 460; the separate old token and AST records remain smaller.

2. Precisely changed statement and input contract
------------------------------------------------
The existing checker reads a whole JSON stack. Its last element, consumed
first, is [A, k]. For closed public theorems, k is the literal integer 0.
The remaining elements are the existing four-field packets [s,u,t,m].

The new checker changes only that header to [Gamma, A, k], where Gamma is a
finite list of assumptions. The public contract for this report is:

  * k is the literal integer 0;
  * every member of Gamma and the goal A is the 1-shifted canonical encoding
    of a CLOSED public guarded formula of the upstream calculus U;
  * packets use the same integer/list contract and ordinary resource
    convention as the matched compact checker.

The ordinary reading of [Gamma,A,0] is the sequent Gamma |- A, or equivalently
(the conjunction of Gamma) -> A, with the empty conjunction interpreted as
truth. Numerical parameters do NOT occur free in the initial Gamma or A.
Bound variables under D and iteration remain allowed. In particular, a run
accepting [ [0=1], 0=1, 0 ] certifies (0=1)->(0=1), not the assertion 0=1.

As with the compact baseline, the public grammar is a promise; this module
does not validate it. The new context-closedness promise is essential to
freshness. No statement about raw out-of-contract contexts is claimed.

The public formulas are equations, and nested paired binders D whose final
atomic matrix has the guarded form I(y,0;u.h)=I(y,0;u.j). Ordinary Q formulas
may still occur as auxiliary formulas inside a proof, as upstream permits.

3. Exact local simulation
------------------------
Write C(g,a,k) for the incoming 460-byte checker body, with the packet reader
held as an explicit common state. Write S(g,a,k) for the new checker body.
Their only internal source difference is the terminal membership test:

       old:  i(a) in g + [Computation]
       new:  i(a) in [0, *g, Computation].

The recursive body, opener, order of evaluations, source selection,
introduction mode, floor decrement, and packet consumption are unchanged.

For every finite list g, the invariant is

                S(g,a,k)  ~  C([0]+g,a,k).

Both sides have equal remaining packet stacks. The opener does not consult
g, so its calls have exactly equal values, exceptions, and effects. The
terminal lists are literally equal. If the call continues, major recursive
calls preserve the invariant. If the newly produced hypothesis is H, the
continuation contexts satisfy

        [0] + (g + [H]) = ([0] + g) + [H].

They therefore preserve the invariant too. All other expressions are equal.
Induction on finite evaluation prefixes gives the same verdict, the same
packet consumption, and the same floor values, even for rejected inputs
where the root header does have the required list shape. There is no
assumption about the truth of Gamma in this simulation.

The old root call C([0],A,0) corresponds exactly to S([],A,0), with no change
to any proof packet. Thus the compiler from every old theorem certificate
merely changes its final header [A,0] to [[],A,0]. The included executable
compiler performs exactly that transformation and no proof search.

4. Soundness and completeness on the public contract
-----------------------------------------------------
Soundness follows from the upstream local soundness theorem for C with
arbitrary in-scope assumptions. If S(Gamma,A,0) succeeds, the simulation
gives C([0]+Gamma,A,0). The scalar formula 0 is the auxiliary truth 0=0,
so the upstream soundness induction yields Gamma |- A. Each initial
assumption is closed, so the unchanged negative fresh names occur in none
of them. On guarded public formulas the auxiliary and ordinary readings
are equivalent, by the upstream guarded-source theorem.

Completeness for open public sequents is the parameterized form of the
existing canonical-certificate compiler. If U proves Gamma |- A, compile
that derivation into C with initial context [0]+Gamma. The compiler's
induction on derivations already carries a context; adding the fixed closed
Gamma changes none of its scope arithmetic. Hypothesis leaves close by
membership, computation leaves and source-selected eliminations are the
same, and introductions and D eliminations use the same fresh-name schedule.
The branch-aware terminal convention remains necessary: a node closed early
must not leave the discarded subproof packets for a caller. This produces a
certificate for C([0]+Gamma,A,0), hence for S(Gamma,A,0) by the simulation.

Equivalently, one can retain the canonical compiler's finite-context
parameter throughout its usual proof rather than specialize it initially to
[0]. No new arithmetic or logical admissibility result is required.

5. Relation to iPRA and the extent of the improvement
-----------------------------------------------------
The ordinary realization is the SAME guarded native calculus U, now
presented as finite-context consequences. Its closed theorems, obtained by
Gamma=[], are exactly the old closed theorems. The ordinary realization
therefore inherits the established whole-domain bi-interpretation with
full first-order intuitionistic PRA; equality is actual equality and both
numerical composites have graph x=y. The sequent readout uses the existing
intuitionistic conjunction and implication macros and adds no axiom.
Consistency is unchanged because a proof of contradiction with empty Gamma
is exactly a proof in the old presentation.

The code reduction is a change of interface, not a mathematical reduction
of the axiom basis. It replaces a fixed top-level initial context by a
context argument and moves the same neutral truth into the terminal test.
It is legitimate only when an accepted object is explicitly read as a
sequent. It must never be advertised as accepting its consequent alone.
No theorem of absolute minimality is claimed. The finite-program frontier
is not a proven lower bound on possible implementations.

6. Header-only variants and why they are secondary
--------------------------------------------------
If the original body is left unchanged and only its initial call is changed
to c(*q()), the normalized byte count is 455. Similarly, changing the
239-token stream program's final call to c(*loads(input())) gives 238 tokens
(262 AST nodes, 545 bytes). These variants have a canonical root context
beginning with the explicit administrative entry 0. They move the neutral
seed into the certificate grammar and do not establish completeness for
every literal context representation lacking that seed. Their accurate
comparison must charge or disclose that additional representation convention.
They are not the preferred general-context construction above.

7. Evidence
-----------
verify_sequents.py executes the matched old checker, the candidate, and an
explicit old-body context adapter. Thirty inherited fixtures and eight
additional context/scope cases give 212 executions in ordinary and -O
Python, with zero failures. Cases whose stated verdict is rejection test
the supplied certificate; an invalid certificate need not mean its sequent
is unprovable (particularly under an inconsistent context).

The additional semantic false-sequent checks are [] |- 0=1 and [0=0] |- 0=1.
Both are rejected. Other checks cover identity at a false assumption,
transport using two assumptions, absent proof premises, and a future
parameter in the goal. These tests support, and do not replace, the proof.

An exact normalized-AST scan of 3314 parseable extracted Python files found
no duplicate of checker_sequent458.py. That is a finite corpus check, not
a claim of mathematical novelty or an exhaustive history search.
