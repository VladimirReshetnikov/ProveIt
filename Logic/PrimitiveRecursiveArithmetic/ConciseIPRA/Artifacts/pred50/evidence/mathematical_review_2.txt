INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF THE 50-NODE PREDECESSOR
==========================================

Reviewed math_reduction/pred50.tex and math_reduction/pred50.py.

Conclusion: the new predecessor argument is mathematically sound relative
to the explicitly listed inherited parity and ramp identities. This review
does not independently reconstruct all of those inherited identities, and
it is not a formal proof-assistant verification.

The key new factor-map identity H0(3a)=3H0(a) uses the inherited equation
R(3a)+N(a)=3R(a). For b=N(a), Boolean interpolation gives
C(b,c+b,0)=C(b,c,0): at b=0 it is c+0=c, while at b=1 both sides are zero.
Distribution of fixed multiplication by 3 through the selector has the
same two-endpoint proof. Thus the new H0 homogeneity argument neither
cancels a summand nor assumes that every even element has a half.

For the comparator, iterator rotation is provable by literal-equation
induction. The proof of I(y,3a;H0)=3I(y,a;H0) holds a fixed throughout its
induction on y. Consequently the recurrence K(Sx,Sy)=K(x,y) follows from
H0(2a)=3a and Z(3a)=Z(a), without any hidden variable-parameter induction.
The base equations K(x,0)=0 and K(0,Sy)=1 follow respectively from the
nonzero powers of two and the absorbing state H0(1)=H0(0)=0.

The saturation proof has the correct dependency order. The two-parameter
identity W(St,Sm)=S W(t,m) is first proved by induction on t with m fixed.
Only after that theorem is obtained is t instantiated by m to prove
W(m,m)=m. This avoids the common invalid maneuver of changing a parameter
of an active induction hypothesis. The recurrence for the intermediate
P54 then identifies it with W on successor inputs.

The final selector identity C(Z(v),a,b)=C(v,b,a) is proved by equation
induction with an unused hypothesis. It turns C(K(Su,m),u,Su) directly
into C(O(Su,m),Su,u), deleting the terminal zero-test occurrence without
changing the numerical update. Equality of the resulting outer iterators
is itself an equation-induction theorem, so no extra body-extensionality
rule is silently assumed.

The Python syntax builder uses fresh binder identifiers and expands every
macro. Its separate de Bruijn conversion and scope checker count the same
50 constructor occurrences and leave only n free. The stated metric is
therefore an expanded arithmetic-term count, not a DAG count or a complete
proof-checker source count. The latter distinction must remain explicit.
