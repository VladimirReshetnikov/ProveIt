SIX-RULE WEAK-HEAD CHECKER
========================

Run: python3 code/head_checker.py < certificate.json

Input has exactly these keys:
  {"variables": n, "context": [A0,...], "goal": A, "proof": hint}
n is an exact nonnegative integer, not a Boolean. Context and goal are
well-scoped native formulas, already weak-head normal at every equation
endpoint. Formula binders Q and D remain available. Every raw term hint is
scope- and sort-checked before any computation can erase part of it.

Term JSON:
  ["z"]                 zero
  ["v", i]              de Bruijn variable index i
  ["s", t]              successor
  ["i", n, seed, body]   scalar iterator; binds index zero only in body

Formula JSON:
  ["=", s, t]           equality
  ["q", B]              one bound variable in B
  ["d", B]              y=index zero, x=index one in D_{x,y} B

Weak-head conversion leaves S(t) untouched. For an iterator it normalizes
the counter's head; at zero it continues with the seed, at S(k) it continues
with the raw update after substitution of I(k,seed,body), and at a neutral
counter it stops without entering the seed or body. Equations normalize
both endpoints; formulas recurse through Q and D.

Hypothesis and literal reflexivity are automatic: their proof hint is
ignored. This is the stated interface, not validation of unused hints.
Otherwise the available hints are:

  ["vs", j, t, u, p, q]
    The goal has endpoints v,w. The exact nonnegative integer j identifies
    a free variable in scope. p proves v_j = W(t); q proves W(u) = v.
    Require w = W(u[t/v_j]), using the RAW supplied t and u. This substitution
    does not remove the variable from scope and does not recursively rewrite
    inside the replacement. t may contain v_j.

  ["qe", B, t, p, q]
    B is a canonical formula with one additional variable. p proves Q B;
    q proves normalized B(0). The goal is normalized B(t), with raw t.

  ["de", B, p, q]
    B is a canonical formula with two additional variables. p proves D B.
    q proves the shifted goal under fresh variable zero, with Q B as the
    first hypothesis and all previous hypotheses shifted. The original
    goal cannot mention the fresh variable.

  ["di", t, p]
    Goal is D B. t is a RAW well-scoped witness. A fresh z is added.
    If B is an equation and raw t is literally ["z"], the local assumption
    is normalized B(t,z); otherwise it is normalized B(t,0).
    p must prove normalized B(t,Sz). Previous hypotheses are shifted.

Acceptance is normal termination, exit code zero, and exactly ACCEPT on
standard output. Rejection has nonzero exit code. Explicit requirements
remain active under python3 -O. Physical stack, time and memory limits can
reject otherwise valid large finite derivations.

FULL-NORMALIZATION CONTROL
==========================
code/full_checker.py uses precisely the same input structure and raw hints,
with full conversion at equality endpoints. Thus its canonical formula
language differs by the specified conversion strategy. The checked inference
families and all scope/error/CLI logic are matched. This is a matched control
written for this experiment, not an incumbent minimum.

COMPACT PROFILES
================
The separately included compact programs implement the inherited guarded
calculus, not the six-rule weak-head calculus. They assume exact integer/list
trees and a canonical closed guarded public goal; they do not validate this
public grammar. Their small byte counts must not be presented as arbitrary
JSON/native-formula validation counts. The report specifies their framing
and records the byte-only result separately from token and AST minima.
