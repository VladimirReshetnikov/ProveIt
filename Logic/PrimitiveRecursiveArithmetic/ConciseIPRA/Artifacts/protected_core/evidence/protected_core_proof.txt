# Protected atoms make the guarded and source-selected kernels compatible

## Scope and status

This is a new compatibility argument for two existing reductions in ProveIt
commit `1517d9d79ce33f631e0f82960c849427cbba23bf`:

- `Research/guarded_core/sections/guarded_core.tex`;
- `Research/source_dispatch/proofs/zero_source_admissibility.tex` and
  `Lean/ConciseIPRA/NativeZeroSource.lean`.

It does **not** prove that the raw identity-language composition preserves
all native sequents. In particular it does not prove zero-source symmetry
from an arbitrary retained hypothesis `0=t`. The new public atom grammar
and the specified translation are essential. The theorem is a written proof,
not a Lean/Rocq verification.

The conclusion is a whole-domain, actual-equality bi-interpretation with the
inherited iPRA presentation, on the public language specified below. A raw
administrative checker can implement a more permissive syntax. Its soundness
outside that public language follows from the uniform inherited reading;
completeness and formula composites are asserted only on the public language.
Any advertised complete-input source-size metric must state whether public
syntax validation is included. A raw kernel byte count alone does not certify
the public grammar or prove bi-interpretability of every administrative tree.

## 1. Terms, protected atoms, and public formulas

Terms are unchanged:

\[
 t ::= x\mid 0\mid St\mid I(n,a;u.h).
\]

Only `u` in `h` is bound by I. All substitutions are capture-avoiding;
all new variables mentioned below are fresh. A term is **I-headed** if its
outermost constructor is I. This is a syntactic condition, not a claim that
its numerical value is nonzero. In particular, an I-headed term may denote
zero. Numerical substitution preserves I-headedness.

Put

\[
 W(t):=I(0,t;u.0),\qquad C(v,a,b):=I(v,a;u.b).
\]

These are abbreviations. Every W(t) and C(v,a,b) is I-headed, including
W(0). A **protected equation** is `s=t` with both s and t I-headed.

Public formulas are generated by:

1. every protected equation;
2. `D_{x,y} B` when B is a compound public formula;
3. `D_{x,y} B(x,y)` when B is an equation of the literal shape

\[
 I(y,0;u.h)=I(y,0;u.k).
 \tag{Guard}
\]

The bodies h,k may depend on x,y, passive parameters and their state variable.
There is no requirement that they be constant updates. This is the same
guard condition used in the existing guarded-core proposal. Rule 3 makes
every atomic matrix under D protected and gives it a provable zero instance.

The public false formula is

\[
                 \bot_p := W(0)=W(S0).
\]

It has its ordinary false numerical meaning. It is **not** the bare
administrative equation `0=S0`.

Retained closure clauses have the form

\[
               \langle A;y.B(y)\rangle,
               \qquad y\notin\operatorname{FV}(A).
\]

For public contexts A,B are public formulas. A public assumption F is the
constant clause `\langle H;v.F\rangle`, where v is unused and H is any fixed
protected theorem, for example `W(0)=W(0)`. This notation does not add a
primitive implication connective.

The proof implementation also uses **auxiliary numerical equations** with
unrestricted endpoints. For example `W(t)=t` is a computation equation and
need not be a protected public formula. These auxiliary judgments are
available inside derivations. Public formulas and public end contexts obey
the grammar above. No arbitrary auxiliary hypothesis `0=t` is part of the
public theorem statement. Administrative context facts may of course be
retained after they have been derived.

## 2. Raw rules and the stable subcalculus H

The raw administrative kernel has ordinary equation atoms, arbitrary D
matrices, retained Q-style clauses, the two computation equations

\[
 I(0,a;u.h)=a,\qquad
 I(Sn,a;u.h)=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u],
 \tag{I0,IS}
\]

and the following rule patterns:

\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash B(t)}
 \quad\text{when }\langle A;y.B(y)\rangle\in\Gamma,
 \tag{Use}
\]

\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash s=t\qquad\Gamma\vdash F(s)}
      {\Gamma\vdash F(t)}
 \quad s\text{ is not literally }0,
 \tag{Tr}_{\ne0}
\]

\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B\qquad
       \Gamma,\langle B(a,0);y.B(a,y)\rangle\vdash F}
      {\Gamma\vdash F},
 \tag{DE}
\]

\[
 \frac{\Gamma,B(t,\eta_B(z))\vdash B(t,Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash D_{x,y}B},
 \qquad
 \eta_B(z)=
 \begin{cases}z,&B\text{ is a literal equation},\\
               0,&B\text{ is compound}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{DI}_{\mathrm{root}}
\]

In DE, a is fresh for the retained context, conclusion and passive matrix
parameters. In DI, z is fresh for the retained context, witness and passive
matrix parameters. Displayed local resources are discharged. Ordinary
hypothesis lookup is the constant-clause abbreviation described above.
The instruction-level empty-array padding used by compact implementations
is a spelling of a fixed true equation and has no extra mathematical force.

For the completeness proof, use the subcalculus **H**:

- D introductions and D conclusions have the public guard grammar;
- ordinary external assumptions and clauses have public formulas;
- transport is restricted further: its source s must be **I-headed**;
- computation equations remain available as auxiliary equations;
- auxiliary equations may occur in the equational manipulations below.

Transport motives may be auxiliary numerical equations or public formula
templates, provided the displayed public instances are well-formed. In the
proofs below every relevant template is stable under numerical substitution.

The further restriction on transport is the key technical device. The
condition `s is not literally 0` is not preserved by substituting zero for
a variable, but the condition `s is I-headed` is preserved. Therefore
weakening, renaming, capture-avoiding numerical substitution and ordinary
assumption cut are admissible in H by direct induction on derivations.
Clause-use substitution is likewise direct. Rename eigenparameters first.
No source-zero admissibility theorem is used in this structural induction.

Every H derivation is accepted by the combined raw rule system: every
I-headed source is nonliteral-zero, and every guarded public introduction
is a root-determined raw introduction. We do not assert the reverse
translation on arbitrary administrative judgments.

## 3. Safe equality inside H

All equality operations in this section use only I-headed transport sources.

### Reflexivity and congruence

For arbitrary t, computation gives `W(t)=t`. Use this equation as both
premises of transport with motive `v=t` to obtain `t=t`. Its transport
source W(t) is I-headed even when t is literally zero.

From `p=q` with p I-headed, symmetry follows by transporting `p=p` with
motive `v=p`. The resulting reverse equation is available, but is used as
a transport major only when its source is I-headed. Transitivity through
an I-headed middle term is immediate.

From `p=q` with p I-headed, arbitrary unary numerical congruence
`A(p)=A(q)` follows by transporting the right side of the reflexivity
equation `A(p)=A(p)`. When A has a fixed I root, the resulting equation has
two protected endpoints and may safely be reversed or composed.

### Joining two I-headed terms that equal zero

Suppose p,q are I-headed and `p=0`, `q=0` are available. Congruence of W
along `p=0` gives `W(p)=W(0)`. Computation gives `W(p)=p`. Transport along
the latter equation, with the former as minor, yields `p=W(0)`. Do the
same for q. Reverse `q=W(0)` at source q and compose through W(0). This
proves `p=q`. Every transport source is I-headed; literal zero is never
used as a transport source. Call this derived construction **zeroJoin**.

### Guards and selectors

For a protected equation E=(s=t), put

\[
 G_vE := C(v,0,s)=C(v,0,t).
\]

At zero, its two endpoints compute to zero, so zeroJoin proves G_0E.
At a successor, its two endpoints compute to s and t. All four relevant
terms are I-headed. Forward and reverse safe rewrites therefore prove

\[
               \vdash_H G_0E,\qquad G_{Sz}E\dashv\vdash_H E.
 \tag{Endpoints}
\]

The hypothesis that E is protected is used in the reverse conversion:
the reverse computation equations are then used only at I-headed sources.

For two protected equations U=(s=t), V=(r=q), the selector

\[
 M_v(U,V):=C(v,s,r)=C(v,t,q)
\]

is protected and satisfies `M_0(U,V) <-> U` and `M_{Sz}(U,V) <-> V`
by the same safe endpoint reasoning. These are interderivability statements
under arbitrary additional retained public assumptions.

## 4. Padding, cases, and induction for protected equations

Define

\[
 L(E):=D_{a,v}G_vE,
 \qquad
 P(A):=\begin{cases}L(A),&A\text{ is an equation},\\A,&A\text{ is compound}.
          \end{cases}
\]

The new variables a,v are unused in E. P(A) is always compound.

### Padding

For every protected equation E,

\[
                       E\dashv\vdash_H L(E).
\]

Forwards, under E use atomic progress introduction of `D_{a,v}G_vE`:
its successor target follows from E by the successor endpoint conversion;
ignore its local induction hypothesis. Backwards, eliminate D, supply the
theorem G_0E to its opened clause, use the clause at 1, and remove the guard.
Thus P(A) is uniformly interderivable with A for every public A.

### Numerical cases

For every stable public template A(y), the rule

\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash A(0)\qquad\Gamma\vdash A(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash A(t)}
 \tag{NC}
\]

is admissible, with z fresh for Gamma and the fixed parameters. If A is
compound, introduce `D_{a,y}A(y)` by the compound case rule with unused a,
then eliminate it and use the opened clause at t. If A is a protected
equation, apply the already proved compound case rule to L(A(y)) and use
padding at its endpoints. No equation induction is used.

### Implication between protected equations

For protected U,V define

\[
                 U\Rightarrow_p V:=D_{a,w}L(M_w(U,V)).
\]

The outer matrix is compound. The selector endpoints, padding and case
introduction give implication introduction; DE and clause Use at 1 give
elimination. These proofs use only the safe equality lemmas above.

### Contextual induction

For every equation E(y) whose two outer term constructors are fixed I's,

\[
 \frac{\Gamma\vdash E(0)\qquad\Gamma,E(z)\vdash E(Sz)}
      {\Gamma\vdash E(t)}
 \tag{EI}_p
\]

is admissible in H. Here is the bootstrap with its structural issue explicit.
Set `J(y)=G_yE(y)` and

\[
                      \Phi(y):=J(y)\Rightarrow_p E(Sy).
\]

Under Gamma, prove Phi(0) by ignoring its local J(0) assumption, using
E(0), and applying the supplied step proof **specialized at zero**. This
specialization is valid because we are in H: every source in that proof
is I-headed and remains I-headed after substitution. For fresh w, prove
Phi(Sw) using `J(Sw) -> E(Sw)` and the supplied step specialized at Sw.
The retained context in both branch proofs is Gamma, independent of the
case variable. NC therefore gives Phi(z). Under J(z), implication
elimination yields E(Sz), hence J(Sz).

The matrix J(y) has the required literal guard shape. Atomic progress DI
introduces `D_{a,y}J(y)` with unused a. Its DE clause, the theorem J(0),
and Use at Sw give J(Sw), hence E(Sw). Finally NC for E combines these
successor instances with the supplied E(0) and concludes E(t).

No arbitrary nonzero-source derivation was substituted in this argument.
The I-headed subcalculus is precisely what prevents the circularity that
would otherwise occur here.

## 5. Protected transport and binder congruence

### Recovering numerical replacement from boxed equality

Suppose `W(s)=W(t)` is available and U(v) is a term template with fixed I
root. The computation `W(s)=s`, by numerical congruence, gives

\[
                       U(W(s))=U(s).
\]

Its source W(s) is I-headed and both displayed endpoints are I-headed.
Likewise obtain `U(W(t))=U(t)`. Congruence along `W(s)=W(t)` gives
`U(W(s))=U(W(t))`. Reverse the first link and compose all three through
I-headed intermediate terms. The result is

\[
                         U(s)=U(t).
 \tag{Protected replacement}
\]

For a general old term template a(v), use U(v)=W(a(v)). Thus boxed
equality of s,t replaces arbitrary occurrences of s by t inside boxed
equation endpoints, without ever transporting from the literal zero term.
Two endpoint replacements recover equational transport for protected atoms.

### Binder congruence

Suppose matrices B,C are uniformly interderivable under the same fixed
public context, with their displayed bound parameters treated as fresh.
If both are compound, `D B` and `D C` are interderivable by the ordinary
case construction: open the old clause, introduce the new binder by cases,
convert its local base to the old base, use the old clause at the successor,
and convert the result back.

If B,C are both permitted guarded atomic matrices, the same conclusion
holds by a different construction. Open `D B` at a. Its zero instance
B(a,0) is a theorem by computation and zeroJoin. Use the opened clause
to obtain B(a,Sz), convert to C(a,Sz), and introduce `D C` by atomic
progress while ignoring C(a,z). Reverse B,C for the other direction.

There is no unsupported appeal to congruence between a guarded atomic
matrix and an arbitrary compound matrix. The cross-category comparison
needed for the round trip is proved separately below.

External induction on public formula syntax now proves replacement along
boxed equality inside every stable public formula template: use protected
endpoint replacement at atoms and the preceding two binder-congruence
constructions at D. Numerical substitution preserves both root categories
and literal guard shape, so the induction applies under binders.

## 6. Translation from the inherited Eq/D clause calculus

Use the existing Choice-DI calculus C from
`certified_zero/reference/inherited_choice_basis.tex`. It has the same
terms, arbitrary equation atoms, arbitrary D matrices, the same retained
clauses, full transport, case introduction at every matrix, and progress
introduction at literal equations. Its full iPRA bridge is inherited.
The current Q/D native presentation is equivalent to this clause interface;
an object Q_yB can also be represented by a D with unused witness coordinate.

Define the structural translation

\[
 T(s=t):=W(s)=W(t),\qquad
 T(D_{x,y}B):=D_{x,y}P(TB).
 \tag{T}
\]

Translate clauses pointwise:
`T\langle A;y.B\rangle=\langle TA;y.TB\rangle`.
All introduced padding variables are fresh. T preserves numerical free
variables and commutes with capture-free numerical substitution. Every
translated binder matrix P(TB) is compound, so it is a public H formula.

Every C derivation translates effectively into H:

- **Computation:** the unchanged auxiliary computation has an I-headed
  left side. Safe congruence of W gives exactly its boxed translation.
- **Use:** translated clauses and T's substitution law give the rule directly.
- **Transport:** its translated equality major is W(s)=W(t); Section 5
  supplies replacement inside the translated formula template.
- **Case DI:** its target has compound matrix P(TB). Unpad its local base,
  insert the translated old step proof, and pad the result.
- **Progress DI:** the old equation matrix E translates to protected T(E).
  Introduce the required D with compound matrix P(T(E)) by cases. Unpad
  its local base to T(E(t,0)); use protected contextual induction with the
  translated old progress premise; pad the resulting T(E(t,Sz)). Use a
  separately fresh induction variable, keeping the outer case parameter fixed.
- **DE:** the new opened clause is
  `\langle P(TB)(a,0);y.P(TB)(a,y)\rangle`. The translated old minor expects
  `\langle TB(a,0);y.TB(a,y)\rangle`. Replace each use of the expected clause
  by padding the antecedent, using the available clause at the same term,
  and unpadding its conclusion. The witness freshness condition is unchanged.

This proof does not need source-zero symmetry on an unboxed equation. Every
new arithmetic transport major has an I-headed source.

## 7. Both composites

Let J include H's public formulas literally into the old C syntax.
All H rules, including auxiliary computation reasoning, are valid C rules.

### Old composite

In C, ordinary full equality proves `s=t <-> W(s)=W(t)`. Padding is
interderivable with its argument, and C has binder congruence for arbitrary
matrices by case introduction. Structural induction therefore gives

\[
                    A\dashv\vdash_C JT(A)
\]

for every old C formula A, uniformly in retained contexts.

### Protected atomic composite

If B is the public equation s=t, then s,t are I-headed. Computation links
`W(s)=s`, `W(t)=t` can be used forward and reversed, with all relevant
sources I-headed. Thus

\[
                 s=t\dashv\vdash_H W(s)=W(t).
\]

This is where public protection is needed. No analogous claim is made for
the arbitrary administrative atom `0=t`.

### Binder composite, including the guarded atomic case

Assume inductively that `B <-> T(JB)` uniformly at its displayed parameters.
Write B' for T(JB). We must compare

\[
                         D B\quad\text{and}\quad D P(B').
\]

Forwards, open D B at a. Introduce D P(B') by compound case DI. Unpad its
local base, convert B'(a,0) to B(a,0), use the opened source clause at Sz,
convert B(a,Sz) to B'(a,Sz), and pad.

Backwards, open D P(B') at a. If B is compound, use case introduction of
D B: convert its local B(a,0) to B'(a,0), pad, apply the opened clause,
unpad and convert the successor result back.

If B is a guarded atomic matrix, B(a,0) is an unconditional theorem by
computation and zeroJoin. Convert and pad that theorem, use the opened
clause at Sz, then unpad and convert to B(a,Sz). This successor proof is
independent of the local B(a,z) hypothesis, so atomic progress DI introduces
D B. Close DE. This explicitly proves the required cross-category atomic
composite rather than assuming an invalid general binder congruence.

Structural induction yields

\[
                     B\dashv\vdash_H TJ(B)
\]

for every public H formula. The conversions are uniform under contexts
and commute with numerical substitution; they extend to retained clauses
by replacing each Use locally with the two endpoint conversions.

J and T leave the entire numerical sort and its terms unchanged. Both
numerical composites therefore have the literal identity graph x=y.
Equality is actual equality in the ordinary realization: boxing is an
explicit definitional presentation because I0 proves W(x)=x. There is no
quotient, standard-numeral restriction, hidden coding domain or reflection
assumption. Composing these translations with the inherited C/iPRA bridge
gives the required bi-interpretation. The public contradiction W(0)=W(1)
corresponds in both directions to ordinary 0=1, giving equiconsistency.

## 8. Why the raw combined checker is sound

The raw kernel permits unguarded atomic D matrices. Its rules are nevertheless
sound for the uniform ordinary reading inherited from C:

\[
 \rho(s=t):=(s=t),
\]

\[
 \rho(D_{x,y}B)=
  \exists x\bigl(\rho(B(x,0))\to\forall y\rho(B(x,y))\bigr).
 \tag{Raw reading}
\]

Read retained clauses as anchored implications
`rho(A) -> forall y rho(B(y))`. Atomic progress introduction is an inherited
Choice-DI progress rule: under a temporary zero instance, literal-equation
induction proves every instance, giving the displayed anchored closure.
Compound case introduction is an inherited case rule and is sound by
numerical coverage. DE is ordinary existential elimination. Clause Use,
computation and equality transport have their immediate inherited meanings.
The source restriction only removes transport instances. Administrative
padding denotes truth. Thus every successful raw proof translates into C
and then into iPRA under this uniform interpretation; a separate progressive
interpretation is unnecessary for the compatibility theorem.

Thus for every public Gamma and public A:

1. an H proof is a raw combined proof;
2. a raw combined proof translates to C under literal inclusion and then
   has a sound iPRA reading;
3. applying T to that C proof and then the public H composite converts it
   back to an H proof of the same public sequent.

This soundness/completeness sandwich proves that the raw combined kernel
and H have exactly the same **public** consequences. It does not supply
formula round trips for arbitrary noncanonical administrative trees.

## 9. The bootstrap obstacle and the honest measurement boundary

The naive composition would copy the old guarded proof of equation
induction and specialize its supplied step at zero. That is unjustified
in the nonzero-source calculus: an allowed source variable can become the
forbidden literal zero term. Similarly, unrestricted atomic padding can
require inserting a wrapper around a literal zero endpoint. These are real
gaps, not notational details. Protected atoms plus the I-source subcalculus
avoid both without invoking the old unrestricted zero-source theorem.

The measured compact source improvement belongs to the raw combined kernel.
Its public mathematical theory has the protected grammar above. A claim
that the executable accepts exactly canonical public goals must also count
and run a validator for that grammar. If the selected comparison convention
instead treats canonical input as a documented contract, say so explicitly
and apply the same convention to every compared program. Do not identify
a raw successful-execution test suite with a proof of the public-language
bi-interpretation, and do not claim absolute minimality from the small finite
source improvement.
