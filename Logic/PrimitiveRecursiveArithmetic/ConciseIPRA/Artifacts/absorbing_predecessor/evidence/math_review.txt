Review scope and findings:

1. Native and ordinary theories.
The displayed native rule inventory is the inherited eight-schema kernel:
Hyp, I0, IS, one-term crossed Tr, QE, DE, DI by cases, and QI for literal
equations. Its variable freshness conditions, discharge conventions and
restriction on induction matrices are consistent. The ordinary realization
includes coverage and the necessary falsity bridge explicitly. Its use of
full first-order logic does not extend induction to arbitrary formulas.

2. Logical macros.
The recursive selector and the Q/D logical macros agree with the inherited
basis. The raw terminal gate used for exact size counts is explicitly
distinguished from the general selector-based gate. The one-binder universal
macro has a derivable zero antecedent and specializes at Sx using P(Sx)=x.

3. New arithmetic proof.
The short-ramp proof uses equational Boolean interpolation rather than
specializing an active induction hypothesis. The absorbing factor scaling
identity is cancellation-free. Rotation, scaling, zero-test identities and
the comparator recursion are valid internally. The saturation proof first
establishes a two-variable conjugacy with its passive parameter fixed and
only then substitutes on the diagonal. Weighted parity is used through its
selector equivalence, not through the false numerical equality T=N.

4. Interpretation bridge.
The compiler and reflection/pairing dependencies are identified as inherited
and supported by the included reference chapter. The new predecessor is
internally equal to each inherited implementation by literal-equation
induction. Term and formula replacement therefore preserve both round trips
on the whole domain. The argument does not infer nonstandard correctness
from numerical samples or silently replace actual equality by a quotient.

5. Exact formula sizes.
For the specified sum family, the initial equation has 4n+4 nodes. Each
paired binder with terminal gate adds seven, and each predecessor
substitution adds p-1. This gives (p+10)n+4, hence 67n+4 versus 60n+4.
The paper properly limits this to the stated raw translation family.

6. Restricted lower bound.
A unary term with at most one iterator is nondecreasing on positive inputs:
the sole iterator has iteration-free counter, initializer and update, giving
exactly the cases analyzed in the paper. The raw-tree identity
|t|=1+3j+s implies a term of at most six nodes has at most one iterator.
The values c(1)>0 and c(2)=0 therefore require at least seven nodes. The
30-node corollary is correct for the fixed outer-selector template with the
specified 21-node ramp and is not asserted for arbitrary factor maps.

7. Claim boundaries.
The paper explicitly states that the requested complete-checker frontier
improvement was not achieved. It distinguishes constructor nodes from
checker tokens, AST nodes, bytes, proof length and runtime. It claims neither
global minimality of the 50-node predecessor nor new proof-assistant
verification. The inherited controls and the new contributions are separated.
