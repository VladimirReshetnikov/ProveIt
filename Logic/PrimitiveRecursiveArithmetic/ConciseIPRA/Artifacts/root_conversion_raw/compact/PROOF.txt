A COMPOSITION OF STRICT OPENING AND EAGER MODE SELECTION
======================================================

Status
------
This is a one-byte implementation improvement of an existing presentation,
not a new arithmetic axiom or logical rule. It does not meet the stronger
request for a checker below every observed complexity coordinate.

The construction is the exact source of
  Artifacts/head_conversion/code/compact477.py
at ProveIt commit 31e1e9220868788a85ca5fea351d7ab4097cfa4a, with its unique
expression
  bool(m or 3 % len(a))
replaced by
  any((m, 3 % len(a))).
The latter selector is the already proved refinement appearing separately
in Artifacts/checked_reductions/checkers/compact477.py. The two incumbent
477-byte programs differ; neither is the composed 476-byte program.

Public contract
---------------
The public goal is a canonical closed guarded Eq/D formula in the source
repository's scalar-iterator encoding. Precisely, its named public grammar
is the least class P0 with these three clauses:
  (i) every equation s = t is in P0, without an iterator-head restriction;
  (ii) D_{x,y} B is in P0 when B is compound and belongs to P0;
  (iii) D_{x,y} (I(y,0;u.h) = I(y,0;v.j)) is in P0.
The state binders u and v in (iii) are independent; h and j may mention
x, y, their own state, and outer numerical parameters. Public formulas
contain no Q. A delivered goal has no free numerical parameters. This is
Definition cert:def:U of the pinned article's ch11_cert.tex; the same
language is P0 in Definition guard:def:public of ch03_guard.tex. Auxiliary
derivations may use all native Eq/Q/D formulas, including unguarded atomic
matrices. The source restriction in elimination is s not literally 0,
not an iterator-only restriction. The smaller public subgrammar P_W with
iterator-headed equation endpoints is available but is not required here.

The proof data are finite trees of
exact integers and lists. A complete input is one JSON array ending in the
pair [goal, 0]; preceding entries are four-field instruction records
[major, source, target, template], read from right to left. Administrative
major 0 of older certificates is changed to [] by convert.py; numerical
zeros inside terms and formulas are left unchanged. The initial retained
resource is []. Unused prefix records are permitted.

The 476-byte module assumes the stated public-language and input-alphabet
contract. It is not an arbitrary-JSON validator, and does not itself reject
every input outside that contract. Off-contract regression examples are
diagnostics only. Its acceptance convention is normal exit and exactly
ACCEPT followed by LF. A thrown exception is rejection. As for its controls,
the completeness theorem uses ideal unbounded mathematical execution;
fixed Python recursion and memory bounds impose practical limits.

Operational theorem
-------------------
Every successful run of checker476.py is a successful run of the strict
opening control477.py on the same input, with identical consumed records,
goals, contexts, and scope floors at corresponding steps.

Proof. At a reached selector, put e = 3 % len(a). The new program first
evaluates m and e to form a tuple, and then takes any of that tuple. If the
new run is successful, e was defined. For every JSON value m and every
defined value e,

              bool(m or e) = any((m, e)).

This is equality of Boolean values, not an identification of m with a bit.
The expression e uses only len and integer remainder and has no side
effects. Thus replacing the new selector by the old one yields the same
selected bit and the same state, even if the old selector short-circuits.
The allocation k := k - 1 happens after the selector in both programs.
The subsequent hypothesis opening and both endpoint openings use the same
new floor. The recursive major check happens before the selector and uses
the same old floor. Induction over a finite successful execution, including
its recursive calls, proves the theorem. No reasoning about random tests
or mathematical soundness of the control is needed for this simulation.

Consequently soundness for the public guarded theory is inherited from
control477.py. This implication holds even on raw inputs outside the
theorem contract whenever a successful new run is considered; the inherited
mathematical interpretation is asserted only on the specified contract.

Canonical completeness theorem
------------------------------
Every theorem of the same canonical closed guarded scalar theory has a
certificate accepted by checker476.py. Hence both programs present the
same public theorem set, and the established full iPRA bi-interpretation
of that public theory is unchanged.

Proof. Use the source repository's canonical certificate compilation for
the strict-opening control. On such a trace, every reached selector has
a nonempty list as a. Genuine equations, paired formulas D and auxiliary
closures Q have arities 2, 1 and 3. During introduction a is replaced by a
canonical matrix instance, again a nonempty formula. A check of the neutral
administrative goal [] returns from the initial membership test and never
reaches a selector. Surplus resources formed by tripling an equality or
closure major are retained administrative annotations; the canonical
compiler does not use them as proof goals. Thus len(a) is positive at every
reached selector, and e is always defined.

The operational proof therefore runs in both directions for these
canonical certificates. Every control trace provided by compilation is
accepted by the composition with its public goal and consumed records
unchanged. This proves completeness for canonical encodings. It does not
claim identical acceptance of all raw administrative spellings.

The strict opener of the control is inherited, rather than newly proved
here. Its canonical adequacy relies on the invariant that actual images
are numerical terms well scoped at the current floor (allocated rigid
parameters are allowed); its extra even-depth image checks all succeed
on canonical compiled traces. Its successful openings have the same sorted
reading as the older short-circuit opener after identifying an empty-list
zero alias with scalar zero. See the pinned head_conversion audit files
named in provenance.json. The present composition does not alter that
opener, its sentinel, or the order of its checks.

Readable expansion
------------------
readable_checker.py expands each expression in checker476.py while
preserving the order relevant to scopes and reads:
  (1) validate the current goal and check retained membership;
  (2) read a packet and recursively prove its major at the old floor;
  (3) build its computation, opened-major or introduction hypothesis;
  (4) evaluate the eager selector, then allocate the fresh parameter;
  (5) open and append the new hypothesis;
  (6) eagerly open the source endpoint and then the target endpoint;
  (7) choose the backward-elimination goal and open its successor hole;
  (8) recurse on the new goal and enlarged context.
Both successful check functions return a truthy value (True at membership,
and a one-element list at continuation), which justifies the readable
version's explicit major check and subsequent major * 3.

verify.py tests 66 inherited cases against the control, the compact
composition and its readable expansion, in normal and optimized Python:
396 actual CLI executions, zero failed expectations. Positive cases cover
the arithmetic computations, transport, guarded induction, D introduction,
Q use and D elimination. Negative cases include future-variable capture,
context leakage, a false scalar sentinel, and 0 = 1. Surplus-arity and
unused-data cases preserve their explicitly labelled inherited behavior.
These checks substantiate the implementation expansion and the source
edit; they do not replace the universal simulation and compilation proofs.

No new Lean or Rocq verification is claimed for the Python module.
