# Bridge synopsis: the guarded public calculus and full first-order iPRA

This is an independently checked synopsis of the existing bridge, not a claim
that the bridge is new in this package. Source: pinned repository
12197d86f63e9ec73b2d91399c4de2a8cb0077ee, Article chapters ch01_base.tex and
ch03_guard.tex. Especially labels base:thm:bridge, base:prop:termrt,
base:thm:logic, guard:def:T, guard:thm:public, guard:thm:aux.

## A. State the actual calculus, including its public language

Terms are x, 0, S t, I(n,a;u.h), binding u in h only. Substitution is ordinary
capture-free numerical substitution. Write C(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b), with u fresh.

Auxiliary formulas are equations, Q_y B and D_{x,y}B. The *public* formulas
have Eq and D only. A D matrix that is itself an equation must literally be

    I(y,0;u.h) = I(y,0;v.k),

with y the second variable bound by that D. A compound D matrix is any public
formula. An unbound equation at the root need not be guarded.

The six rule families are:
- Hyp: a retained assumption.
- I0: I(0,a;u.h)=a.
- IS: I(Sn,a;u.h)=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u].
- SE: from M(s,t;F) and F(s), infer F(t), where
  M=Q_v F(v) if s is literally zero, and M=(s=t) otherwise.
- Open: from D_{x,y}B and a derivation of A under Q_y B(b,y), infer A,
  with b fresh for the outer context, A, and the parameters of B.
- Intro: from B(t,eta) |- B(t,Sz) in an arbitrary outer context, infer
  D_{x,y}B, where eta=z for a literal equation matrix and eta=0 for a
  compound matrix. z is fresh for the outer context, t, and the parameters
  of B.

Intermediate formulas may be arbitrary auxiliary formulas, even when the
goal is public. This is essential. Restricting every intermediate formula to
the public grammar makes the source restriction and the root-selected
introduction incompatible without an additional repair.

The contradiction is the public equation 0=S0. A claim about full iPRA refers
to the theory of *public* sequents. It must not be extended to arbitrary
unguarded auxiliary D formulas. The repository explicitly proves that such an
extension fails.

## B. The two bridge spans

The clean bridge has two spans:

    guarded public U <-> native N <-> ordinary T_eq <-> full first-order iPRA.

The native N is the unrestricted Eq/Q/D kernel: the two computation laws,
equality transport, closure elimination, D elimination, D introduction by
cases, and equation progress. One can use either full transport or the
equivalent crossed one-term rule. The ordinary theory T_eq is intuitionistic
first-order logic with actual equality, the same iterator terms, and:
1. the two computation equations;
2. numerical coverage x=0 or exists y x=Sy;
3. 0=S0 -> bottom;
4. induction for literal equations, with arbitrary parameters.

Successor injectivity and all quantifier-free induction are theorems of T_eq.

## C. Ordinary readings and logical macros

The native ordinary reading is:

    (s=t)^o := s=t
    (Q_y B)^o := B^o(0) -> forall y B^o(y)
    (D_{x,y}B)^o := exists x (B^o(x,0) -> forall y B^o(x,y)).

It leaves the numerical domain whole. There is no quotient and equality is
actual equality.

The following is an explicit finite definition of the formula selector
Sel(c,A,B), not a postulated conditional connective. Bound variables are
always renamed fresh. It is a recursion first on A, and then on B when A is
an equation:

    Sel(c, s=t, r=w) :=
        C(c,s,r)=C(c,t,w)

    Sel(c, s=t, Q_y B) :=
        Q_y Sel(c, C(y,0,s)=C(y,0,t), B)

    Sel(c, s=t, D_{x,y}B) :=
        D_{x,y} Sel(c, C(y,0,s)=C(y,0,t), B)

    Gate(c,B) := Sel(c,0=0,B)

    Sel(c,Q_y A,B) := Q_y Sel(c,A,Gate(y,B))
    Sel(c,D_{x,y}A,B) := D_{x,y} Sel(c,A,Gate(y,B)).

The binder depth of unmatched operands cannot simply be padded with a vacuous
D: D_{x,y}A with both variables unused is a truth, not A. The gate in this
definition is what makes the padding correct.

Define parity N(n)=I(n,0;u.C(u,1,0)), where 1=S0. Then

    bottom_* := 0=S0
    A implies_* B := Q_y Sel(y,A,B)
    A and_* B := Q_y Gate(y,Sel(N(y),A,B))
    exists_* x A := D_{x,y} Gate(y,A)
    A or_* B := exists_* x Sel(x,A,B)
    forall_* x A := A(0) and_* Q_x A.

These are all finite native formulas. Q can subsequently be eliminated by
the public translation below. The conjunction uses the rows y=1 and y=2:
N(1)=1 and N(2)=0, while its row at y=0 is a theorem because of Gate.

The native calculus derives:
- Sel(0,A,B) equivalent to A;
- Sel(Sz,A,B) equivalent to B;
- Gate(0,A) is a theorem;
- Gate(Sz,A) equivalent to A;
- Q_y Gate(y,A) equivalent to A, when y is fresh;
- all introduction and elimination rules for the six displayed macros,
  with the usual eigenvariable conditions.

For example implication introduction is D/Q case introduction using the
zero row A and successor row B; implication elimination specializes at 1.
Existential introduction chooses the D witness; existential elimination
opens that witness and uses the gate closure at 1. Explosion first obtains
every equation from 0=S0 by transport in C(-,s,t), and then inducts over
formula structure. These are intuitionistic derivations. No excluded middle
for arbitrary formulas is introduced.

Writing phi* for replacement of ordinary connectives by these macros:
N proves exactly the native translations of T_eq consequences, and

    (A^o)* is interderivable with A in N;
    T_eq proves phi <-> (phi*)^o.

The first is proved by induction on A. For Q, its expansion is
B(0) implies_* forall_*y B; use Q introduction in one direction and QE in
the other. For D, combine that Q equivalence with the existential
introduction/elimination rules. The second is a structural induction using
the displayed logical rules and the selector laws.

## D. Public translation and its round trips

For an equation E=(s=t), use the zero guard

    gamma_z E := I(z,0;u.s)=I(z,0;u.t).

Put L(E)=D_{a,z} gamma_z E, with fresh a,z, and define Pad(E)=L(E) for an
equation, Pad(A)=A for compound A. The exact public translation is

    T(s=t)=s=t
    T(Q_y B)=D_{a,y} Pad(TB), with a fresh
    T(D_{x,y}B)=D_{x,y} Pad(TB).

Every translated matrix is compound, and every atomic D matrix created by
L is literally zero-guarded. Thus T maps every native formula to the public
grammar without adding term constructors. Padding satisfies Pad(A) equivalent
to A; for an equation this follows because gamma_0 E is the theorem 0=0,
and gamma_1 E is E by computation.

The U-to-N comparison is not obtained by declaring every auxiliary formula
to have the ordinary reading. A safe intermediate soundness reading treats
atomic D matrices progressively, and case matrices conditionally; it agrees
with the ordinary reading on public formulas because every guarded atomic
matrix has a provable zero row.

The established public-language theorem gives:
1. N derivations Gamma |- A translate effectively to U derivations
   T(Gamma) |- T(A).
2. N interderives A and T(A), for every native A.
3. U interderives B and T(B), for every public B.
4. U and N derive exactly the same public sequents.

These are the two formula round trips for the public interpretation. They
are stronger than an external equivalence of consistency assertions.

## E. Arithmetic and the term interpretations

The hard inherited arithmetic fact is that scalar iteration plus equation
induction defines a predecessor P satisfying, throughout every model,

    P(0)=0, P(Sn)=n.

It is not enough to test this on standard natural numbers. The pinned article
has an equational proof; the incoming P45 package composes two existing
improvements of the concrete term. The checker reductions do not change P.

With P define truncated subtraction a dotminus b = I(b,a;u.P(u)).
Let K(u)=C(u,1,P(u)), and epsilon(a,b)=K(Sa dotminus b). T_eq proves

    epsilon(a,b)=0 iff a=b.

Hence atomic equality is decidable in T_eq. For every quantifier-free
ordinary formula phi, build a numerical term b_phi with b_phi=0 iff phi.
Using "zero means true", one may take

    b_bottom=1
    b_(s=t)=epsilon(s,t)
    b_(A and B)=C(b_A,b_B,1)
    b_(A or B)=C(b_A,0,b_B)
    b_(A implies B)=C(b_A,b_B,0).

The equivalence for implication uses the decidability of the relevant
quantifier-free formulas, not arbitrary excluded middle. Equation induction
on b_phi=0 proves all quantifier-free induction instances.

The article constructs numerical pairing terms pair, fst, snd and proves
fst(pair(a,b))=a, snd(pair(a,b))=b. One explicit choice is

    pair(a,b)=2^(a+b)+a
    fst(w)=w dotminus 2^lg(w)
    snd(w)=lg(w) dotminus fst(w),

where lg is the scalar-iteration saturation of the binary logarithm. Pairing
need not be onto: the domain of interpretation remains the whole numerical
domain, and pairs serve only as internal recursion states.

Compile a counter-aware recursion by

    step_g(w)=pair(S fst(w), g(fst(w),snd(w)))
    R(n,a;i,u.g)=snd(I(n,pair(0,a);w.step_g(w))).

Equation induction proves fst(I(n,pair(0,a);step_g))=n, and hence the ordinary
primitive recursion equations for R. Define sigma from each finite PR
function description by projection, composition, and this R construction.
This is an external recursion on finite syntax. It is not a universal
internal evaluator for codes of all primitive recursive functions.

In the other direction tau maps a scalar iterator to the ordinary primitive
recursive function with base a and step h ignoring its counter, with all
free variables treated as parameters. Both sigma and tau fix numerical
variables and 0,S. Tau commutes with substitution up to provable equality;
sigma does so literally.

Both term round trips are proved internally:
- T_eq proves sigma(tau(t))=t, by structural induction on scalar terms.
  The iterator case uses uniqueness of iteration, itself equation induction.
- iPRA proves tau(sigma(r))=r, by induction on each PR function description.
  At a primitive recursion, the compiled term and the original function
  satisfy the same base and step equations. Quantifier-free induction in
  their equality proves their equality. Structural induction extends the
  result to all terms.

Thus the domain comparison map in both directions is literally x=y. It is
total, bijective, and compatible with all operations; no proper definable
subdomain or quotient is used.

## F. Final precise assertion

For a public U formula B put B^flat = tau(B^o).
For an iPRA formula phi put phi^sharp = T((sigma(phi))*).
The inherited bridge and the two round trips yield

    Gamma |-U B iff Gamma^flat |-iPRA B^flat,
    Delta |-iPRA phi iff Delta^sharp |-U phi^sharp,

with public/native formula round trips and numerical identity composites.
In particular U derives 0=S0 iff iPRA derives bottom.

The continuation and eager-aggregation checker changes preserve this same
public theorem language, so this bridge applies to the new checking
presentations unchanged. There is no new consistency assumption, no appeal
to the standard model to justify all models, and no additional induction
schema.
