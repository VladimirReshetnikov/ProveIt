INDEPENDENT MATHEMATICAL REVIEW

The direct variable-source recovery of crossed transport was reviewed from
two directions. The reviewed proof uses initialized scalar I, not the
successor-seeded scalar R: the selector endpoints must normalize literally
before equality transport is available.

The following obligations were checked:

1. Numerical cases are derived without equality transport, using dummy
   witness 1 and closure elimination. No general atomic case at witness
   zero is needed for that preliminary construction.
2. The two-row witness package uses only the given transport premises at
   its selector endpoints. Its introduction branch ignores the new
   assumption, so zero-witness progress mode is harmless.
3. One fresh-name elimination supplies h(b)=r and b=t. One variable
   substitution gives r=nf(h(t)); b does not occur in the final sequent.
4. Normalization/substitution coherence covers iterator bodies and
   duplication or erasure of occurrences.
5. The newest-variable refinement does not use unrestricted target
   weakening. Its translation invariant protects internally introduced
   sources while initial parameters and clauses are extended.
6. The reverse translation is a special case of unrestricted transport.
   General numerical substitution becomes admissible afterward.
7. The raw/canonical bridge treats unrestricted case introduction and
   progress separately; a witness may normalize to zero.
8. Reductions under update binders use derived iterator-body extensionality
   from equation induction in the raw theory.
9. Composing exact comparisons with the pinned native/iPRA bridge preserves
   whole numerical domains, actual equality, and both identity composites.

No mathematical defect was found in these arguments. This is a written
review and executable regression evidence, not a Lean/Rocq verification.
No claim of a new global checker-size minimum is made.
