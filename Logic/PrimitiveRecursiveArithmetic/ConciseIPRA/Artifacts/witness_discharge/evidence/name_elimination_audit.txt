Independent audit: equality-aware name elimination

The audited rule is:
  Gamma |- D_{x,y} B(x,y)
  Gamma, [B(b,0); y.B(b,y)] |- b=t
  Gamma, [B(b,0); y.B(b,y)] |- F
  ------------------------------------------------
  Gamma |- N(F[t/b])
where b is fresh for the outer context and the matrix's passive parameters,
N is full computation normalization, and the final normalized formula is
b-free. Both branch proofs live under the SAME local clause and numerical
scope. The term t may contain b; substitution is one simultaneous pass,
not a recursive rescan of inserted t. This is sound: within the existential
scope use ordinary equality replacement on b=t and F, then existentially
eliminate b because the resulting formula is b-free. Finite normalization
conversions are provable in iPRA. No freshness condition t-free-of-b is needed.

Ordinary D elimination is the t=b special case: the equality branch is
Refl(b), and the F branch is required to have b-free result. Thus the rule
does not weaken the existing logical interface.

Numerical cases are available without ANY equality inference. From a
proof of A(0) and a proof of A(Sz) for fresh z, introduce D_{x,y} A(y)
at witness 0, ignoring the local Intro hypothesis even when the equation
progress guard selects A(z). Open by ordinary D elimination and Use the
exported clause at an arbitrary target u. Its antecedent is A(0).
This is a direct proof construction, not substitution into an already
proved derived rule. It avoids assuming general numerical substitution.

Transport packet. Define C(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b) and
  p(y)=I(y,0;u.C(u,1,0)).
Then full normalization gives p(1)=1 and p(2)=0. Given supplied premises
  Gamma |- s=t,      Gamma |- h(s)=r,
with fresh x,y and h a fixed finite numerical template, put
  B(x,y) := C(y,0,C(p(y),h(x),x))
              = C(y,0,C(p(y),r,t)).
The three endpoints normalize literally to
  B(x,0) = (0=0), B(x,1) = (x=t), B(x,2) = (h(x)=r).
To introduce D B at witness s, prove B(s,Sz) using numerical cases on
p(Sz). At zero its normalized equation is h(s)=r; at a successor it is
s=t. Both are the supplied premises. The local Intro hypothesis is unused,
so it does not matter whether s is literal zero. In the Name scope, Use
at 1 gives b=t and Use at 2 gives h(b)=r, both from Refl(0) for B(b,0).
Name then concludes h(t)=r. This derives uncrossed one-term transport.

Equality algebra now follows without circularity. For symmetry of s=t,
take h(v)=v and minor s=s, obtaining t=s. Transitivity of s=t and t=u
follows by one such transport with major t=u and minor t=s, then symmetry.
Two applications give transport in both sides of an equation. Full formula
transport follows by the usual external induction on the Eq/D grammar,
after recovering unrestricted case introduction as below.

Missing atomic case at witness zero. Full normalization destroys closed
nonliteral zero aliases, so the old raw alias I(0,0;u.0) cannot be reused.
Instead use Z(x,y):=C(y,0,x)=0. Intro at witness 0 proves D Z by Refl(0),
ignoring its hypothesis. Its opened clause at 1 proves b=0. To simulate
an equation case-introduction at witness 0, introduce at the variable b
(hence case mode), translating its base and successor equations along b=0
using the now-derived atomic transport, and discharge b by ordinary DE.
The final D formula contains no b. Thus both old introduction modes can
be recovered before asserting general numerical substitution admissibility.

The route to equivalence is therefore noncircular:
  Name -> ordinary DE -> numerical cases -> packet transport -> equality
  algebra -> missing atomic case repair -> both introductions -> native
  full-normal calculus -> inherited raw native/iPRA bridge.

The rule is a genuine strengthened elimination interface, not ordinary DE
alone. The complete trusted normalizer and scoping checks must be included
in checker measurements. Full normalizing substitution can be expensive;
termination is external (e.g. the System T embedding), not a uniform total
normalizer theorem assumed inside iPRA. Both interpretation composites are
provably identity, not literally identical when raw terms are compared.
