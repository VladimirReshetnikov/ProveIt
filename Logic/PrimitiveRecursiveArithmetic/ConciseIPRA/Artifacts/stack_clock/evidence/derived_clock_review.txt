1. State relation
-----------------

Let P be the list of unread proof packets after the public conclusion has
been removed, and let N be its initial length. In the candidate the floor
is the expression f(P)=len(P)-N. In the control the floor is the mutable
integer k initially0, decreased once by every proof call.

At every corresponding point after r packet pops,
    k=-r=len(P)-N=f(P).
This equality is independent of arbitrary unused prefix records. Such a
prefix increases both len(P) and N by the same amount and cancels exactly.

The initial N is computed from the parsed list; it is not a certificate
promise or an unchecked claimed length. No theorem prover or proof search

2. Why the goal scan moves before pop
------------------------------------

The control reads/unpacks the packet, then scans its goal while k still
has its old value, and only then decrements k. A packet pop alone does not
change the control's k.

In the candidate a packet pop changes f(P) immediately. Therefore the scan
MUST happen before pop. Its result b=i(a) is computed with f(P)=old k. The
subsequent pop makes f(P)=old k-1, exactly the control's new floor.

For JSON-built data the opener is pure: it traverses lists, compares and
maps scalars, builds lists, and raises an exception on unsupported values.
It never pops P or mutates an input tree. Thus moving the scan past the
packet read does not alter any successful result or later packet value.
If either the scan or packet read/unpack fails, the candidate and control
both reject. Their error timing/exception text need not agree.

3. Why the phantom terminal entry can be deleted
-----------------------------------------------

The old terminal tuple contains its newly assigned floor k_new. This entry
can never equal the result of the preceding successful goal scan.

A successful scan without a supplied image returns either a list or a
retained scalar. A list does not equal an integer in JSON/Python built-in
semantics. Every retained scalar is an int/bool: parity with the integer
depth rejects a float, while unsupported types fail earlier. A retained
negative scalar is at least k_old. A retained zero normalizes to1, and a
positive scalar is also not k_new. Since k_new=k_old-1<k_old, no scalar
result equals k_new.

The default image is the checker function, which cannot be successfully
opened; hence a missing-image path cannot manufacture a contrary scalar.

Consequently deleting only that membership entry leaves the terminal
verdict unchanged. Its old assignment side effect is already supplied by
the candidate's pop through f(P).
