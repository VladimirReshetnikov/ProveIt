RAW SYNTAX

For a scope with n free parameters and local binder depth d:

  0          zero
  -1..-n     free parameters; -n is newest (fallback zero if n=0)
  1..d       local bound indices; 1 is nearest
  [0,t]      successor
  [n,a,h]    iterator I(n,a;u.h); u has index 1 in h

Formula:
  [s,t]      equation
  [B]        D_{x,y} B; x has index 2 and y has index 1 in B

The raw term grammar does not perform normalization. The term test checks the
literal zero tag of a successor, including when a substitution would erase or
repair malformed input. An iterator's unused body is still validated.

A context entry (base,body) is [base;y.body], with the bound clause variable
at index 1 in body. base=[] means no antecedent. Assumption A is ([],A)
with the clause variable unused. Contexts and scopes are explicit in the
in-process API, and both are empty at a standalone checker's entry point.

TARGET PROOF NODES

  ["u",index,premise]
    Use the indexed clause at the newest parameter, or at zero in empty scope.
    premise proves its antecedent; it is ignored if the clause has none.

  ["c",counter,seed,body]
    Derive the iterator computation equation at counter 0 or a literal
    successor [0,n]. Every term component is validated.

  ["v",goal,major,minor]          (equation_back.py)
    goal must be an equation. major proves b=t with b the newest parameter.
    minor proves goal[t/b]. Conclude goal.

  ["v",term,major,minor]          (crossed_back.py)
    term is a term h. major proves b=t with b newest; minor proves h[t/b]=r.
    Conclude r=h. r is not substituted, and may contain b.

  ["a",target,branch]
    Append fresh b=-scope-1 and the assumption b=target. The branch derives F.
    Conclude F[target/b]. target is over the original scope.

  ["e",goal,major,branch]
    major proves D B. Append fresh b and [B(b,0);y.B(b,y)]. The branch proves
    goal, which must be over the original scope. Conclude goal.

  ["i",goal,branch]
    goal is D B. Choose the newest parameter w (zero in empty scope).
    Append fresh z. The assumption is B(w,z) if raw B is diagonal, and B(w,0)
    otherwise. The branch proves B(w,Sz). Conclude goal.

Diagonal means that B is an equation and each side is an iterator whose
counter and seed are the bound y: both raw iterator prefixes are [1,1].
This is tested before the witness is inserted.

LIBERAL SOURCE PROOF DIFFERENCES

  ["u",index,term,premise]        specialization uses the supplied term
  ["v",goal,major,minor]          goal may be compound; source may be any
                                  current free parameter, not just newest
  ["i",goal,witness,branch]       introduction uses the supplied witness

All other nodes are identical. Source and target use the same raw syntax.

STANDALONE ENVELOPE

  {"goal": <closed native formula>, "proof": <proof node>}

Exactly these keys are required. A successful run prints ACCEPT and exits 0.
Validation or execution errors print REJECT and exit 1. The checkers are
standalone; they import only standard-library json and sys.

The compilers require well-formed source contexts when invoked through their
open-sequent API. Their default command-line entry point validates a closed
source certificate. Output is a checked target certificate. convert(proof,
context,scope) in compile_crossed.py can also postprocess an accepted equation
certificate directly; provide its actual context and scope for open proofs.
