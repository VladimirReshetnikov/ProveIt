1. Core conclusion
------------------
The new initial resource [1,0] is mathematically legitimate. Under the
parametric raw-tree reading already proved in the article, scalar 1 is a
new constant c, scalar 0 is the arithmetic zero, and [1,0] is exactly c=0.
The new public spelling 1 for zero is therefore justified by a genuine
fixed axiom, not merely by assigning two raw spellings the same meaning.
The resulting theory is a conservative definitional extension of the
inherited native theory. Its public c-only reduct is definitionally
equivalent to the native theory with zero renamed c.

This is a verified implementation-level presentation change, not a new
arithmetic-strength theorem or a new logical basis.

2. Soundness, including nesting
------------------------------
Article ch11_cert.tex, proposition cert:prop:reserved ('Reserved
coordinates as constants', approximately lines 4270--4320), establishes
that the raw native proof protocol is sound when scalar 1 means an
arbitrary c_1 and scalar 2 an arbitrary c_2, rather than identifying them
with zero. The proof explicitly covers transport, computation, closure
specialization, witness discharge, the two introductions, and the
zero-instance supply argument.

The candidate changes the initial context from neutral truth alone to
neutral truth together with c_1=0. The invariant is now derivability from
this additional closed assumption. Weakening and the local-floor proof
apply unchanged: c_1=0 contains no rigid negative eigenparameter and no
unbound slot. The numerical floor still starts at k=0; its relation to
context length is irrelevant because this checker passes k explicitly.

At EVERY meaningful term position the depth is at least 3. Consequently
scalar 1 is in the protected constant band, is never the opening hole,
and remains the same constant when copied beneath further D, Q, I, or S
syntax. In particular c cannot accidentally turn into a witness or state
variable below binders. Existing dummy-slot conventions are unchanged.

The extra raw constant c_2 may still occur inside certificates; it is
absent from the new canonical public syntax. Soundness may assign it any
number, or specialize it to zero after the run. It adds no public axiom.

3. Exact definitional-equivalence argument
-----------------------------------------
Let T be the ordinary realization of the inherited complete native
presentation. Let T+ be its extension by a constant c and the closed
axiom c=0; equation induction is allowed to mention c, as an ordinary
parameter. Let L_c be the public language in which c replaces zero.

(i) Interpret T+ in T by c |-> 0 and identity on all other symbols and
on the numerical domain. The extra axiom becomes 0=0; any induction
instance mentioning c becomes the corresponding old instance at 0.
Every derivation translates, so T+ is conservative over T and is
consistent iff T is consistent.

(ii) Interpret T in T+ by the inclusion. The composite on T is literally
identity. The other composite sends c to 0; T+ proves c=0, so term
congruence and formula Leibniz prove that every formula agrees with its
composite translation. This is a definitional equivalence on the same
numerical domain, with actual equality and identity return map x=y.

(iii) Restricting to L_c loses nothing: the auxiliary zero is definable as
c. Equivalently, the public theory is T with its zero symbol renamed c.
The scalar iterator, its two computation schemes, coverage, separation,
and equation induction are transported through c=0. Compose this
identity-domain equivalence with the repository's inherited full
bi-interpretation of T and iPRA. Both numerical round trips remain x=y.

No new consistency assumption, quotient, nonstandard domain, or stronger
induction is involved.

4. Completeness and compilation caveat
--------------------------------------
For a canonical public formula A in L_c, replace c by 0 and use the
inherited completeness theorem to obtain a native derivation. In T+,
formula Leibniz under c=0 yields A iff A[c:=0], including all nested
binders. More concretely, the inherited native calculus extended by the
constant c and the fixed assumption c=0 derives A whenever its zero
translation is a theorem. Its proof-to-packet completeness construction
works with an arbitrary retained context and with constant symbols,
because 1 is retained by every meaningful opening. Hence the candidate
has a certificate for A.

Do NOT implement this argument by replacing every numeric 0 in a packet
by 1: packet zeroes also select instruction branches and closure
specialization. Source-selected transport reserves literal raw 0 for QE.
Instead use the extended native derivation/Leibniz argument, or compile a
proved equality-conversion boundary using the inherited derived
zero-source transport. The retained axiom itself is [1,0], and the internal
zero remains 0.

The public alphabet test v <= (d%2 & a), with v=1, means exactly that d is
odd and a is odd; k=0 and a<d additionally force a positive in-scope
scalar. Thus 1 is the public zero constant and 3,5,... are the protected
slot coordinates of the inherited padded representation. The array arity
check still separates Eq/D formulas from S/I terms. Lexical typing still
rejects booleans, floats, strings, nulls, and objects anywhere in input.

One inherited presentation caveat: the article's completeness proofs are
explicitly for canonical encodings, whose definition forbids references
to administrative dummy slots. The printed goal grammar P^ed allows odd
in-scope coordinates without a separate dummy-slot occurrence check.
The article identifies this predicate with a canonical grammar. I have

inherited issue, unchanged by replacing 0 with 1. Avoid strengthening the
claim to a new theorem about every raw alias; state the public canonical
syntax and inherited contract precisely. No counterexample was established.

5. Novelty boundary
-------------------
The article already contains 'Uniform zero and constructed majors'
(ch11_cert.tex ~740--920): canonical zero is encoded by scalar 1, and the
opener is CHANGED so that retained raw 0 is normalized to 1. That is a
prior uniform-zero implementation and must be cited.

The article's reserved-coordinate obstruction later explains that the
UNCHANGED raw opener treats 1 as an independent constant and cannot prove
[1,0] from its old initial context. It explicitly says that a presentation
could add an identification or normalize spellings, provided the cost and
correctness are counted.

The specific fixed-resource implementation [0,[1,0]], together with a
strict positive-odd public leaf grammar and unchanged raw opener, was not
found in the fetched unified article. Therefore a narrow novelty claim is
supported: this is a measured implementation of the article's suggested
identification repair, replacing per-opening zero normalization by one
fixed equality resource. Do not claim that the idea of uniform zero or
adding an identification was absent from prior work.

6. Further guard deletion
--------------------------
Admitting raw 2 by deleting '& a' would require a second independent
constant identification (e.g. c_2=0); c_1=0 alone does not justify it. The
parametric-reading theorem proves that those constants are independent
in the old raw kernel. Admitting even protected slot coordinates also
introduces alternative spellings of bound variables, whose proof-level
identification requires a separate argument. There is no justified free
further deletion of the oddness test.

7. Sharpened audit of the inherited dummy-slot issue
--------------------------------------------------
After discussion with independent_logic, there is a concrete parametric
countermodel to the stronger completeness claim for all P^ed-admitted raw
goals. Modify the sorted reading so that the SECOND dummy slot introduced
by every unary TERM (successor) constructor has a fixed arbitrary value
Delta, rather than zero. Keep the first successor dummy and the iterator
counter/seed dummy at zero. Keep the actual successor and iterator as the
standard arithmetic operations. The terms still denote primitive recursive
functions with the additional parameter Delta.

The raw tree [5], read at root term depth 3, denotes S(Delta): its child
is read at depth 7 and scalar 5 is the second successor dummy. The tree
[0] denotes S(0), and [1] denotes S(c_1).

The raw protocol's soundness proof extends to this reading:
* Openings preserve an internal successor dummy because it is never an
  exposed hole and never becomes an outer bound slot. Thus semantic
  substitution, equality transport, Q elimination, D elimination, and
  the introductions are unchanged. Fresh numerical parameters are negative
  and independent of Delta. Equation induction with Delta as a fixed
  parameter is valid in standard arithmetic.
* Zero-computation adds one zero-valued iterator dummy, not a changed
  successor dummy; hence the zero-instance-supply proof remains valid.
* In successor computation, the left predecessor appears below two extra
  successor dummy slots. If the update uses its state, the generated
  recursive image [s,u,t] must pass validation at the root term depth.
  Its predecessor s is then read below only the iterator's zero dummy.
  Any reference of s to the newly added SECOND successor dummy would be
  out of scope in that recursive image and cause rejection. Thus all
  successful state-using computations agree in their predecessor readings.
  If the update does not use its state, no recursive image is needed and
  I(S(n),a;u.h)=h for every n, so an arbitrary value Delta in the
  predecessor does not affect soundness.

Therefore every accepted old-protocol raw goal is valid for arbitrary
Delta; for the alias candidate this is true under c_1=0. The old validator
admits [[5],[0]], and the new validator admits [[5],[1]]. Both have the
intended all-dummies-zero reading S0=S0, but under the modified reading
at Delta=1 they say S1=S0 and are false. Thus no certificate exists for
them. This explains precisely why the established completeness theorem
must be stated for canonical encodings (which never reference dummy slots),
not every raw goal passing the loose parity/depth validator.

This argument is a new audit result relative to the fetched article; it
has not been machine-formalized. The scalar constant-alias repair itself
is valid and does not cause this inherited defect. The deliverable must
not silently strengthen the inherited canonical-image completeness theorem.
