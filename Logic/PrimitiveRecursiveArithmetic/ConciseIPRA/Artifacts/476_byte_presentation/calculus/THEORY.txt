THREE RULE FAMILIES ON RAW TERMS, WITH DIRECT SUBSTITUTION
=======================================================

Result and scope
----------------
The following calculus P has three rule families, uses raw scalar iterator
terms, and is closed under arbitrary capture-avoiding numerical substitution
by a direct induction on derivations. It is equivalent, on the same Eq/D
sequents, to the native kernel of ConciseIPRA. The equivalence uses the
already established diagonal clause calculus KPrf, with an explicit bridge
between its ordinary clauses and the uniform clauses used here.

This is a new combination of existing ingredients and a restriction of the
evaluation-discharge operator. It does not establish a checker below the
current repository envelope. The matched split diagonal implementation is
slightly larger than the fused one, while the older witness-dependent fused
checker is smaller than both. See evidence/metrics.json.

1. Syntax, assumptions, and the complete rule inventory
-----------------------------------------------------
Terms are x, 0, S(t), I(n,a;u.h), with only u bound in h. Terms are raw:
there is no implicit normalization or conversion test. Formulas are s=t and
D_{x,y}B. Only x and y are bound by D. Its ordinary first-order reading is

  D_{x,y}B := exists x (B(x,0) -> forall y B(x,y)).

A uniform clause [Delta;y.C(y)] has Delta empty or the singleton {A}; A has
no occurrence of the clause-bound y. Its reading is forall y C(y) if Delta
is empty, and A -> forall y C(y) otherwise. Contexts are finite lists of
such clauses. An ordinary assumption A is [empty;v.A] with v unused.
Every initial context contains the fixed reflexivity clause

  R := [empty;y.y=y].

The contradiction is 0=S(0). There are no other logical or nonlogical axiom
schemes, and no implicit equality, computation, conversion, cut, weakening,
or induction rules. The following are the three primitive rule families.

(U) Use. For a retained clause [Delta;y.C(y)] and an arbitrary term t,
from a proof of each member of Delta infer C(t). In the empty case this is
a rule with no premises. In particular R yields t=t.

(I) Diagonal introduction. From

  Gamma, B(t,eta) |- B(t,Sz)

infer Gamma |- D_{x,y}B, where z is fresh for Gamma, t and the free
parameters of B. Put eta=z exactly when B is literally

  I(y,y;u.h) = I(y,y;v.k),

and eta=0 otherwise. The matrix test precedes witness instantiation. The
step terms h and k may mention x, y and the other free parameters.

(E) Evaluation with discharge. From

  Gamma |- D_{x,y}B,
  Gamma,[{B(b,0)};y.B(b,y)] |- E,
  Gamma,[{B(b,0)};y.B(b,y)] |- F,

infer Gamma |- rho(E,F), provided rho(E,F) is defined and b is fresh for
Gamma, the free parameters of B, and the result rho(E,F). The parameter b
may occur in E and F separately. The partial operator rho has exactly the
following three, pairwise disjoint, positive patterns:

  rho(s=t, I(0,s;u.h)=r) = (r=t);

  rho(I(c,a;u.h)=t, I(Sc,a;u.h)=r) = (r=h[t/u]);

  rho(D_{p,q}C, F) = F.

It is undefined on every other pair. The last row is the only identity
case. In particular, an unmatched equation E does not select identity.

2. Why the partial operator matters
----------------------------------
The inherited E3 operator returns F whenever neither arithmetic pattern
matches. That total operation is not covariant under substitution. For
example let E be x=0 and let F be I(0,0;u.0)=0. Before replacing x by 0,
the total operator returns F. Afterwards its first arithmetic row returns
0=0. These are different raw formulas.

The present partial operator is undefined on that original pair. The
required covariance law is only for a defined row:

  if rho(E,F)=A, then rho(E[sigma],F[sigma])=A[sigma].

For the first row, substitution preserves the zero counter and the common
seed. For the second, it preserves the common counter, seed and body;
capture-avoiding substitution commutes with h[t/u]. For the third, a
compound formula retains its D constructor. Each row therefore stays
defined and produces precisely the substituted conclusion. Definedness
need not be reflected: an undefined pair may become defined.

The diagonal test is also preserved and reflected by substitution for free
variables. The tested y is bound, and capture avoidance prevents an image
of a free variable from becoming that bound occurrence. The two outer
iterator constructors and their counters and seeds are otherwise unchanged.

3. Direct structural theorems
----------------------------
Theorem. If Gamma |-P A and sigma is an arbitrary capture-avoiding
substitution of terms for the free numerical variables, then
Gamma[sigma] |-P A[sigma]. Context weakening and injective renaming are
also admissible.

Proof. Induct on the given finite derivation. Use translates the retained
clause and its zero or one antecedent proofs; substitution commutes with
specialization. At introduction, first rename z away from the images of
sigma. Preservation and reflection of the diagonal test gives the same
mode after substitution. The induction hypothesis translates the branch.
At discharge, first rename b away from the images of sigma, extend sigma
by b -> b in both branches, and apply the induction hypotheses. The
covariance law for the selected positive rho row gives the required result.
Since b does not occur in the original result and is fresh for sigma, it
does not occur in the substituted result. The same induction proves
weakening; its only extra step is freshening z and b away from the added
context. Renaming is the variable-valued special case. No cut or
normalization theorem is used.

4. The correct clause bridge
----------------------------
Let K be the existing diagonal clause calculus KPrf. Its clauses are
[A;y.C(y)], always with one antecedent, and its ordinary assumption is
[0=0;v.A]. Its rules are Refl, EvalZero, EvalSucc, Use, Elim and the same
diagonal introduction. EvalZero and EvalSucc form one rule family.

Let V be the uniform-clause version of K: replace explicit Refl by R and
allow clauses with no antecedent; keep the two standalone evaluations,
ordinary Elim, Use and diagonal Intro. This V is the semantics implemented
by control_split.py. The following two translations establish its precise
relationship with K.

(a) V to K. For a uniform clause c, pack(c) retains its consequent and
uses its existing antecedent if present, or 0=0 otherwise. Translate the
context entry by entry, except that the distinguished R is simulated
directly by K's Refl. A Use of an empty-antecedent clause is a K Use with
the missing antecedent supplied by Refl(0). The clause introduced by Intro
then becomes exactly K's ordinary-assumption clause. The clause introduced
by Elim already has one antecedent, so it is unchanged. Every other rule
is literally the corresponding K rule. This gives a direct induction.

(b) K to V. Carry through the induction a relation between source and
target contexts: R belongs to the target, and every source clause has a
target clause with the same consequent whose antecedent is either the same
source antecedent or empty. Translate Refl by Use of R. Translate a Use
by the corresponding target clause; if its antecedent is empty, discard
the translated antecedent proof, and otherwise use it. This invariant
extends at Intro because its target ordinary assumption is empty, while
the source ordinary assumption has antecedent 0=0. It extends at Elim
with the identical nonempty antecedent B(b,0). The arithmetic rules are
unchanged. Fresh renaming of bound variables makes all extensions valid.

Therefore, for any Eq/D formulas Gamma and A,

  V (R :: ordinary_assumptions(Gamma)) A
      iff K (ordinary_assumptions_K(Gamma)) A.

It would be incorrect to identify the two context datatypes without this
argument. The existing formal theorem kprf_iff_nprf_of_qfree now identifies
these sequents with those of the native kernel NPrf.

5. Equivalence of the three-family calculus P and V
------------------------------------------------
Theorem. P and V derive exactly the same formulas from every uniform
clause context containing R.

P to V: translate Use and Intro literally. For a discharge, translate its
major and its two branch proofs. In either arithmetic row apply the
corresponding standalone evaluation in the opened context and then apply
Elim. In the compound row ignore E and apply Elim directly to F. The
freshness condition on rho(E,F) is exactly what Elim requires.

V to P: first derive T := D_{x,y}(0=0) in every P context. Its matrix is
not diagonal, so its introduction premise is 0=0 |- 0=0; Use of the local
ordinary assumption proves that premise. This proof needs no Refl.

Ordinary Elim is now a derived P rule. Given its major and branch F,
derive T in the branch as auxiliary E and use the compound identity row
of discharge. Standalone EvalZero and EvalSucc are derived as well: use T
as the major, open it at a fresh b, weaken the two arithmetic premises into
that context, and use their positive rho row. The new b does not occur in
the arithmetic result. Weakening is legitimate by Section 3. Use and Intro
already belong to P. An induction on V derivations replaces each evaluation
and elimination by these fixed macros. Both directions leave the end
formula and the original context unchanged.

The executable compiler translations.py realizes these two translations
for the concrete proof format, with explicit maps for clause indices and
rigid free levels. Dummy discharges allocate extra free levels; the compiler
renames branch eigenparameters accordingly. It rechecks every produced
closed certificate using the target's standalone checker.

6. Bi-interpretation and equiconsistency
--------------------------------------
Combining Sections 4 and 5 with kprf_iff_nprf_of_qfree gives

  P (R :: ordinary_assumptions(Gamma)) A iff NPrf Gamma A

for Eq/D sequents. The existing unQ translation, replacing Q by D with an
unused witness variable, connects this with all native formulas. The
ordinary realization and the existing native/iPRA bridge consequently
apply without modification: both number domains are whole, equality is
actual equality, and both composites have the identity graph x=y. The
term and formula translations are exactly the inherited ones; only proof
rules and their certificates have changed. In particular the designated
contradiction translates to 0=S(0), giving both directions of consistency
equivalence.

This relies on the repository's established arithmetic and logical bridge,
including predecessor, equality reflection, quantifier-free induction,
pairing, primitive-recursion compilation, and the formula round trips. It
does not claim a new proof of those results or a new Lean verification of
the Python program.

7. Implementation contract and evidence
--------------------------------------
The standalone checker reads one JSON object with exactly the keys goal
and proof, checks a closed Eq/D goal, and prints ACCEPT only after checking
the proof. Zero is integer 0, free rigid levels are negative integers,
bound de Bruijn indices are positive, successor is [0,t], iteration is
[n,a,h] with its last child binding one variable, equality is [s,t], and
D is [B] binding two. Numerical booleans are rejected, as are floats,
strings, dictionaries, malformed arities and escaping free or bound
variables. Every returned formula and every supplied term witness is
validated. In Use, the unused proof field must be JSON null when its
clause has no antecedent. The inherited control treats that field as an
ignored annotation; the matched split control has the same stricter null
requirement as the new checker.

The proof nodes are ['u', clause_index, term, antecedent_proof],
['i', matrix, witness, step_proof], and
['e', major_proof, auxiliary_proof, result_proof]. The split control uses
['v', left_proof, right_proof] for evaluation and ['e', major, branch] for
ordinary elimination. The context-length convention allocates a fresh
rigid level at each introduced clause and checks that it does not escape.

verify.py constructs 16 positive and 26 negative closed certificates,
checks both exact-goal compiler directions on every positive example,
runs all 42 examples through both the readable and normalized command-line
checkers normally and under Python -O (168 process runs), and checks nine
free-parameter substitutions and twenty
diagonal-selector substitution pairs. Two positive examples actively cite
their introduced hypothesis and are rejected by the old witness-dependent
checker on the same proof trees; another test records the removal of its
unmatched-equation identity case. Witness-erasing evaluation and witness
escape are tested separately. These are finite regression checks, not a
substitute for the universal proofs above.

8. Measurements and limitations
------------------------------
On CPython 3.12.14, measuring each complete literal standalone
*_normalized.py profile by ast.unparse plus one LF, with no exclusions:

  new fused diagonal checker:        678 tokens / 727 AST / 2087 bytes;
  matched split diagonal checker:    688 tokens / 735 AST / 2128 bytes;
  inherited witness-dependent E3:    643 tokens / 695 AST / 1892 bytes.

The fusion saves 10 tokens, 8 AST nodes and 41 bytes against its matched
split control. It is larger than the inherited E3 checker and much larger
than the current compact frontier. No claim that this meets the requested
global simplification target is made. Parser, validator and acceptance
output are included; proof generators and evidence scripts are not part of
the trusted checker. The normalization and metrics scripts are supplied.
The readable counterparts carry leading explanatory docstrings; the measured
profiles omit those docstrings and formatting only, are independently
runnable, and import no helper from the archive. The new normalized profile
passes the same 42 certificates in both ordinary and optimized execution.
