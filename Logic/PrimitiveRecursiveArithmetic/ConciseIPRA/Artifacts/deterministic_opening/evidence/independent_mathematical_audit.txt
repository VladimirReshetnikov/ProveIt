DEFINITION AUDITED

Let H be 0=0. From a proof of D_{x,y} B(x,y), open the usual fresh parameter b
and the clause [B(b,0); y.B(b,y)]. Normalize C0=B(b,0) and J=B(b,1). Set rho=t
if C0 is literally H and J is literally b=t. Otherwise set rho=b. From a
branch proof F, export nf(F[rho/b]), provided this formula is outer scoped.
The branch context and the matrix are the same in both modes. All displayed
substitutions are simultaneous and capture avoiding.

SOUNDNESS

In the distinguished case, reflexivity gives H and clause use at 1 gives
b=t. Equality substitution yields F[t/b]. Normalization is justified by the
unconditional iterator computations in the ordinary reference theory.
The outer-scope condition makes existential elimination legitimate.

In the default case the substitution is the identity. Hence the outer-scope
condition requires that the normalized result contain no free b; this is
ordinary existential elimination. The selected t may itself contain b.
No occurs check is mathematically needed: b=t(b) justifies simultaneous
substitution, while the final b-freedom condition enforces witness discharge.

RECOVERY OF ORDINARY ELIMINATION

If the branch result F already contains no b, then nf(F[rho/b])=F for either
selected rho. Therefore ordinary paired elimination is recovered with no
equality premise and without knowing which selection case occurs.

EQUALITY RECONSTRUCTION

Use the normalized selector C(v,a,c)=nf I(v,a;u.c), where u is unused in c,
and parity P(y)=nf I(y,0;u.C(u,1,0)). Its only required parity facts are the
literal computations P(1)=1 and P(2)=0. Given s=t and nf h(s)=r, let

 B(x,y) :=
 C(y,0,C(P(y),h(x),x)) = C(y,0,C(P(y),r,t)).

Choose x,y fresh for all passive data. Direct normalization gives

 B(x,0) = (0=0),
 B(x,1) = (x=t),
 B(x,2) = (nf h(x)=r).

To introduce D B at witness s, numerical cases applied to v=P(Sz) prove
the successor row C(v,h(s),s)=C(v,r,t): its zero case is the supplied
nf h(s)=r, and its successor case is the supplied s=t. This proof ignores
the introduction's temporary assumption, so it is valid in either the case
or progress mode, including s literally zero.

Open the resulting D B at b. Its first row forces rho=t. Clause use at 2
provides F=(nf h(b)=r). Exporting F gives nf h(t)=r by normalization and
substitution coherence. This is uncrossed one-term transport.

Symmetry follows by taking h(w)=w and using s=s as the minor premise.
Crossed transport follows by symmetry of the uncrossed conclusion. Full
equation transport follows by the usual two one-term transports. Transport
through D follows by formula-depth induction using ordinary elimination,
case introduction at a fresh witness c, and the transport induction
hypothesis on the matrix. None of these proofs assumes the equality rule
being reconstructed.

FULL-NORMALIZATION AND BRIDGE CHECK

Both scalar computation rewrites are unconditional equations of the

The comparison preserves every canonical end sequent, including arbitrary

LIMITS AND IMPLEMENTATION OBLIGATIONS

This audit does not establish a global proof-checker size record. The
deterministic mode test and normalizer must be counted in full. A trusted
checker must verify the major premise, open the correct matrix at a fresh
parameter, verify the branch in that exact extended context, perform the
chosen simultaneous substitution, normalize it, and reject an escaping
witness. For bounded-fuel normalization, fuel exhaustion is rejection.
Uniform normalization is effective but need not have a primitive-recursive
bound over all descriptions; no object-level evaluator or new totality
principle may be inferred from it.
