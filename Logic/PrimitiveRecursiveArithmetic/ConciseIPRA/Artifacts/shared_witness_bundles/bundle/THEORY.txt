# Shared-witness certificates remove deep head conversion

This is an auxiliary mathematical proposal, not a measured frontier improvement.
The public theory has precisely the native formula language. Finite bundles are
allowed inside proof certificates only. This distinction avoids an unnecessary
formula-roundtrip claim for an enlarged public language.

Let the ordinary scalar terms be x, 0, S(t), I(n,a;u.h). The public formulas
are s=t, Q_y B, and D_{x,y} B. The auxiliary grammar used inside a proof
replaces D by the finite-bundle binder

    D_x [ y_1.B_1(x,y_1), ..., y_m.B_m(x,y_m) ]

read as

    exists x. conjunction_i (B_i(x,0) -> forall y_i. B_i(x,y_i)).

The singleton case is native D, and Q_y B retains its native reading
B(0) -> forall y B(y). A bundle elimination opens one fresh b and
adds every Q_y_i B_i(b,y_i). Bundle introduction uses one common witness t
and, separately for each i, a derivation B_i(t,S z_i) from B_i(t,0), or the
equation-progress hypothesis B_i(t,z_i) when B_i is a literal equation.
The z_i are fresh. Empty bundles are allowed as exists x truth, though
they are unnecessary for the argument.

These auxiliary rules are sound under their displayed ordinary readings
in iPRA. Native D rules are singleton cases. The public derivability
relation admits a finite auxiliary proof but requires that every bundle
in its final formula (and initial context) be a singleton. No claim that
the proof calculus is complete on arbitrary auxiliary bundle sequents is
needed. The completeness theorem below concerns exactly the native public
sequents, and yields an identity interpretation there.

Now take Refl, crossed variable substitution, QE, DE for bundles, bundle
introduction, and reversible root computation of equation endpoints. The
variable-substitution rule is

    b=t, h(b)=r  ==>  r=h(t)

where b is a variable. Reversible root computation permits either scalar
iterator equation at the root of one endpoint, in either direction. It
does not enter iterator counters, seeds, or bodies.

## Derivation of full transport

Given s=t and h(s)=r, define C(y,a)=I(y,0;u.a), with u fresh in a.
It has root reductions C(0,a)->0 and C(S z,a)->a. Introduce the common
witness x=s in the two-item bundle

    D_x [ y. C(y,x)=C(y,t), y. C(y,h(x))=C(y,r) ].

For the first component, expand s=t at both endpoint roots to obtain
C(S z,s)=C(S z,t). For the second do the same to h(s)=r. Each derivation
ignores its local base/progress hypothesis, so either mode is sound and
the literal spelling of s is irrelevant.

Choose all displayed auxiliary names outside the ambient numerical scope,
the context, and the two premise derivations. Open the bundle at fresh b.
Both zero rows are 0=0 by root computation,
so reflexivity plus expansion supplies their bases. Specialize both
clauses at S 0 and contract endpoint roots. This yields b=t and h(b)=r.
Crossed variable substitution gives r=h(t); eliminate the fresh b.

The proof uses only root conversions. The existing scalar BH_3 proof in
ch04_comp.tex uses a parity packet and computation at counter depths up
to three because its single matrix must carry three rows. Here separate
matrices share the witness, so no parity or predecessor code is needed.

More precisely, the construction adds 21 inference nodes to the two input
proof trees: 12 root conversions, 2 closure eliminations, 2 hypothesis
uses, 2 reflexivity steps, one bundle introduction, one bundle elimination,
and one VSub. Each input proof occurs once (with its hypothesis indices
weakened). All 12 conversions are at depth zero. This is a node-count
statement about the displayed compiler, not a constant bound on the bytes
of its substituted terms.

Only one- and two-matrix bundles are needed for the public completeness
theorem. Restricting the auxiliary grammar to those two sizes preserves
the theorem: the native embedding uses singletons, and transport uses a
pair. The reference implementation accepts arbitrary finite bundles
because the uniform list implementation is straightforward. Empty and
larger bundles add no public consequences by the soundness/completeness
argument below.

With full transport recovered, each raw computation equation follows
from reflexivity and a root expansion. The native kernel therefore embeds
on public sequents. Conversely, read every auxiliary bundle as the
displayed iPRA existential conjunction. Soundness gives an iPRA proof of
the ordinary reading of any accepted public sequent. The inherited
native completeness theorem then gives a native proof of that same
public sequent. This establishes exactly the native consequence relation,
without assuming that arbitrary bundle formulas can be eliminated inside
this proof calculus.

## Complete specification of the seven rule families

Judgments are Gamma |- A in an explicitly declared finite numerical scope
X. Every free variable of Gamma and A belongs to X. Gamma is an ordered
finite list of auxiliary formulas. Terms and formulas are identified up
to alpha conversion, and substitution is simultaneous and capture-avoiding.

1. Hyp: select an entry of Gamma.
2. Refl: infer t=t for any term in scope X.
3. VSub: from b=t and u=v infer v=u[t/b], where b is a variable in X.
   The right endpoint v is not substituted by this rule. There is no
   restriction on whether b occurs in v. Soundness follows by equality
   substitution in the left endpoint and symmetry.
4. HC0: in an equation, replace either endpoint by one scalar iterator
   root contractum, or by a supplied redex whose one root contractum is
   that endpoint. The two root reductions are I(0,a;u.h)->a and
   I(S n,a;u.h)->h[I(n,a;u.h)/u]. Nothing under the endpoint root is
   normalized or reduced.
5. QE: from Q_y B and B(0) infer B(t), for any t in scope X.
6. DE*: from D_x[y_i.B_i] and a derivation of A in scope X+b with all
   Q_y_i B_i(b,y_i) appended to Gamma, infer A in scope X. The same
   b is used for every matrix; b is outside X and is not free in A.
7. DI*: choose one term t in scope X for the entire finite bundle. For
   each matrix B_i choose a fresh z_i outside X and one of two modes.
   In case mode derive B_i(t,S z_i) from Gamma,B_i(t,0). In progress
   mode derive B_i(t,S z_i) from Gamma,B_i(t,z_i), and require that B_i
   be literally an equation. Infer D_x[y_i.B_i]. Each branch has scope
   X+z_i. The branches are independent, so their eigenvariable names
   may be reused. Empty bundles have no branches and an arbitrary
   well-scoped witness t, for example 0.

These are all the rules. In particular there is no ordinary equality
transport axiom, general congruence rule, general induction rule, hidden
normalizer, separation axiom, or primitive intuitionistic connective rule.
They are recovered on the public language through the native equivalence.

For checking a theorem, the public context is empty; free variables of
the public goal are treated as parameters and universally closed in its
ordinary reading. For an open sequent theorem, take X to contain the free
variables of its context and conclusion. Numerical parameters can be
renamed injectively to avoid every eigenvariable used by the certificate.

## Why soundness uses only iPRA

Refl, VSub and HC0 use ordinary equality and the two iterator equations.
QE is implication elimination followed by universal elimination. DE*
is existential elimination, followed by conjunction elimination; freshness
of the shared b is exactly its eigenvariable condition.

For a DI* component in case mode, assume B_i(t,0). The branch gives every
successor instance, and numerical coverage gives every instance. Thus
Q_y_i B_i(t,y_i). This uses coverage and the case branch, not induction on
the potentially compound B_i. In progress mode the matrix remains a
literal equation after substituting the witness, so quantifier-free
induction derives Q_y_i B_i(t,y_i). Form the finite conjunction of these
closures and introduce the existential with the common t. The empty
conjunction is truth. Every finite auxiliary derivation therefore has an
ordinary iPRA reading, uniformly also under arbitrary initial assumptions.

## The public equivalence theorem, including both composites

Let N be the native scalar calculus of the pinned repository. Let H be
the public theory whose proofs are these auxiliary certificates.

Theorem: for every native public context Gamma and formula A,

    Gamma |-H A   iff   Gamma |-N A.

Left-to-right follows from auxiliary soundness and the repository theorem
native_iff_pra (NativeBiInterpretation.lean). Right-to-left is by induction
on the eight constructors of NPrf (Native.lean):

- Hyp, QE, and native DE are respectively Hyp, QE, and singleton DE*.
- Each iterator computation is Refl at its right-hand side followed by
  HC0 expansion of the left endpoint.
- NPrf.trOne is the two-component construction proved above.
- NPrf.dICase is singleton DI* in case mode.
- NPrf.qIInd: introduce a singleton bundle with an unused witness variable
  and witness 0 in progress mode, open it at a fresh b, return the added
  Q_y E(y) hypothesis, and discharge b. The resulting formula contains no b.

Weakening and injective renaming of auxiliary derivations are admissible
by induction on their finite trees (fresh eigenvariables are renamed as
needed). This justifies reusing the two input derivations in the branches
of the transport construction. The sample compiler explicitly shifts
hypothesis indices when inserting the local base assumption; it does not
assume that literal proof-tree reuse preserves those indices.

The two interpretations between H and N are the identity on every term
and every public formula. Their numerical domains are the whole domain,
their equality is actual equality, and both composites are literally the
identity. Composing these identity interpretations with the repository's
native-to-iPRA and iPRA-to-native interpretations gives H <-> iPRA. The
formula roundtrips are exactly the inherited native_roundTrip and
pra_native_roundTrip, transported across the proved consequence equivalence.
The numerical roundtrip graph remains x=y. Thus the required
bi-interpretability is stronger than a standard-model equivalence or
equiconsistency claim. Contradiction is the public equation 0=S(0), so
equiconsistency also follows immediately.

This is a written mathematical proof depending on the pinned native
bridge. It is not a new Lean or Rocq formalization, and executing sample
certificates does not replace that proof.

## Artifact evidence

bundle_checker.py implements the preceding grammar and rules with explicit
syntax validation, named binders, alpha-equivalence, simultaneous
capture-avoiding substitution, numerical scopes, and a native public-goal
gate. Its inputs are JSON proof objects, not ordinary first-order proof
strings. It never evaluates supplied Python or code strings.

bundle_examples.py constructs root_congruence.json, equation_induction.json,
induction_then_transport.json, and empty_bundle_auxiliary.json. The first
recovers successor congruence from I(0,1;u.u)=1 through a nonvariable
transport source. The third first proves I(n,0;u.u)=0 by genuine equation
progress and then transports it under successor using a two-matrix bundle.
It exercises hypothesis-index weakening inside a nested induction proof.
The empty bundle is exercised only as an auxiliary object; a nonnative
empty-bundle public goal is rejected.

The reproducible test script also rejects a nonvariable source passed
directly to VSub, a wrong root expansion, 0=S(0), Boolean variable codes,
an inconsistent common witness, case mode substituted for the needed
induction step, a missing bundle component, and eigenvariable escape.
Additional checks cover simultaneous replacement and capture avoidance.

Measurements on CPython 3.12.14 are recorded in bundle_results.json.
They count the entire standalone reference module, including its module
docstring. This reference is much larger than the repository's compact
frontier. The mathematical result is the elimination of positive-depth
head conversion in a certificate language with shared-witness bundles,
not a global reduction of checker size.

## Limits

This is a legitimate new proof interface and a mathematical simplification
of the root-conversion boundary, but not yet a smaller standalone checker.
An implementation must count finite-bundle typing, common-witness scope,
and the traversal of multiple matrices. It must also retain enough
equation induction: cases alone are insufficient. No token/AST/byte
minimum or absolute optimality is claimed.
