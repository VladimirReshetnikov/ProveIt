SYNTHESIZED-CONCLUSION EXPERIMENT

Purpose
=======
Test whether removing the submitted goal and synthesizing a well-formed
conclusion from a proof algebra can eliminate the native raw-goal validator.
The executable synth.py is complete and standalone. Its input is one JSON proof
tree. Its output is one canonical Eq/Q/D formula, in the encoding below. Normal
completion is acceptance; an exception is rejection. There is no external
decoder, axiom table, goal validator, or generator in the trusted module.

This experiment DOES NOT improve the current checker-size frontier. Normalized
measurements: 875 significant tokens, 1027 AST nodes, 2145 UTF-8 bytes, 739
deterministic gzip-9 bytes. These are empirical sizes, not impossibility bounds.

Encoding
========
A numerical term is an integer variable, [] for zero, [t] for successor, or
[n,a,h] for I(n,a;u.h). Negative integer -j names rigid proof parameter j.
Nonnegative integers are de Bruijn indices for numerical binders. Only the
third child of an iterator binds one variable. At scope n with d local binders,
the allowed variables are -n,...,-1 and 0,...,d-1.

A formula is [w,s,t], where w is a finite list with entries 1 and 2. The entries
list the formula's outer-to-inner binders: 1 means Q and 2 means D. The terminal
equation is s=t, whose terms have sum(w) local binders. For D_{x,y}, y has the
smaller de Bruijn index and x the larger one. This grammar is exact, without
dummy padding slots or aliases. Every formula in the native grammar has one
such structural representation modulo numerical parameter names.

The printout is a syntax code, not a separate semantic declaration: the complete
checker builds and validates every constructor that can enter the printout.

Primitive proof constructors
============================
[0,j]              Hypothesis g[j].
[1,c,a,h]          I(0,a;u.h)=a; c is validated but otherwise unused.
[2,c,a,h]          I(S(c),a;u.h)=h[I(c,a;u.h)/u].
[3,h,p,q]          If p proves s=t and q proves h(s)=a, return a=h(t).
[4,p,q,t]          If p proves Q_y B and q proves B(0), return B(t).
[5,p,q]            If p proves D_{x,y}B, prove q under fresh b and hypothesis
                   Q_y B(b,y); return q's conclusion after checking that b
                   does not occur freely in it.
[6,t,B,p]          Prove p under fresh z and hypothesis B(t,0); require its
                   conclusion to be B(t,S(z)), and return D_{x,y}B.
[7,E,p]            E is a literal equation. Prove p under fresh z and E(z);
                   require E(S(z)), and return Q_y E(y).

The counter operand c in constructor 1 can be removed in a routine interface
cleanup; it was retained to keep the two computation constructor layouts
uniform. This does not affect any mathematical claim or the negative size
assessment. There is no unrestricted formula induction.

Substitution lemma
==================
sub(a,t,d) opens the variable of index d in a numerical term a, replacing it
by the closed-in-the-local-binders term t and decrementing larger indices.
It increases d exactly in the iterator body. Negative rigid names are retained.
Every replacement t is checked in the current proof scope with zero surrounding
local binders. Hence t contains no free nonnegative index, so insertion needs
no lifting. Its internally bound indices remain protected by its own iterator
binders. Structural induction proves typing and the ordinary substitution law.

opened([w,s,t],*xs) repeatedly applies this lemma at the highest indices of
the outermost formula binder. Full opening of D takes x followed by y; partial
opening at x leaves a Q binder at y. Full opening of Q removes its one binder.

Soundness invariant
===================
Fix scope n. Assume g is a list of native formulas well formed over rigid
parameters -n,...,-1. Every successful check(p,g,n) returns a well-formed native
formula A over those same parameters, and the native eight-rule kernel proves
g |- A. Prove this by induction on the successful finite execution tree.

The hypothesis and computation cases are immediate. The transport and Q-use
cases check exactly the required syntactic premise equalities, and their
outputs have the native rule's conclusion. Both introduction constructors type
the supplied template and witness at the old scope BEFORE entering n+1; the new
parameter -(n+1) is therefore fresh for them and g. The equation guard of QI is
checked explicitly. For D elimination, the major is a typed D formula and the
continuation has the correct Q hypothesis at the new parameter. Calling formula
on the synthesized result at scope n rejects any free occurrence of -(n+1).
Therefore the native eigenvariable condition holds. None of these arguments
uses a promise about a separately supplied goal.

Completeness invariant
======================
Every finite native derivation g |- A, after injectively renaming its active
parameters to -n,...,-1, compiles recursively to a proof tree whose synthesized
conclusion is exactly the canonical encoding of A. Each native rule has the
corresponding constructor. Fresh parameters are renamed to -(n+1) on entry;
the usual eigenvariable side condition guarantees that DE's export check
succeeds. The supplied premise templates are ordinary canonical terms and
formulas. Shared subderivations may be duplicated, since proof length is not
being minimized. The root theorem set is therefore precisely the closed native
theorem set. The existing native/iPRA bi-interpretation applies unchanged.

Evidence and limits
===================
verify_synth.py runs 14 cases in normal and optimized Python, for 28 isolated
executions. The successful cases cover all eight constructors, equation
induction that uses its induction hypothesis, closure use, D elimination that
uses its major, and compound case introduction. Negative cases cover an
escaping eigenparameter, a wrong induction step, a future witness, a free
public parameter, Boolean contamination, and an unknown constructor. All 28
outcomes agree with expectations. Finite tests support the implementation
account; they do not establish the structural metatheorems.

No Lean/Rocq verification of this new Python implementation is claimed. The
prototype is intentionally transparent rather than extensively source-golfed.
It shows that a genuine synthesis interface is possible, but it retains typing
for user-supplied iterator bodies, witnesses, and introduction matrices. Merely
removing the externally supplied goal does not remove those costs. A stronger
constructor algebra might share them more efficiently; this experiment gives
no lower bound against such a design.
