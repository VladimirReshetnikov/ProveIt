## Invalid apparent savings

1. Moving `(k := k - 1)` inside the true arm of `... if s or len(a) == 2 else 0` fails to allocate a fresh successor variable in compound introductions. It is not an equivalent optimization.
2. Choosing progress from the generated hypothesis's arity alone confuses an opened witnessed closure (arity three) with a compound Q matrix introduced by cases (also arity three). The latter must not receive equation induction. Public Eq/D syntax does not exclude Q from auxiliary proof trees.
3. Giving the opener access to the next fresh variable before validating the major and introduction witness weakens the future-witness check and is not a legitimate scope simplification.
4. Replacing `[q()]` by `q()` in the comprehension and wrapping each supplied packet in a list saves two characters but admits an empty proof packet. The result is an empty list that the acceptance driver can ignore; moreover recursive major calls become falsy rather than rejecting. Enforcing nonemptiness costs at least the apparent saving in the simple implementation.
5. Returning the proved goal rather than a success marker would eliminate a conjunction at major use, but the original goal must survive the mutable `a` rewrites in introduction/elimination. The straightforward preservation cost exceeds the saved expression.
