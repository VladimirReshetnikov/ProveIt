INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF COVERAGE WITHOUT SUCCESSOR CANCELLATION

Reviewed source:
  arithmetic_work/coverage_without_cancellation.md

Conclusion:
  The displayed proof derives numerical coverage from the listed self-seeded
  computation, epsilon, separation, and literal-equation induction axioms.
  It does not use successor injectivity, numerical coverage, or a hidden
  restriction to standard elements. The derived equation is a right-inverse
  equation; cancelling its leading successor without a further axiom would
  be invalid.

Detailed checks:

1. Successor-nonzeroness is derived before Boolean operations are used.
   With D(c,a)=F_(v.a)(c), Z(c)=D(c,0), a putative Sn=0 equates Z(Sn)=0
   with Z(0)=1. This contradicts the explicit separation axiom 0!=1.

2. The proposed Boolean conjunction uses no distinction between 1 and 2.
   For b=0, D(b,epsilon(c,0)) is a successor, so its equality-to-zero test
   is false. For b=1, it is epsilon(c,0), and its equality-to-zero test
   is c. This uses only 0!=1 and successor-nonzeroness. Disjunction by
   De Morgan is valid on these two-valued terms. Range and reflection of
   epsilon make numerical equality decidable, so the characteristic-function
   recursion for arbitrary fixed quantifier-free formulas is constructive.

3. Equation induction legitimately yields the quantifier-free induction
   instances used here: replace a fixed quantifier-free invariant A(n) by
   its previously constructed source characteristic equation chi_A(n)=1.
   All terms remain staged source terms; no newly introduced graph-defined
   update operation is smuggled into F.

4. The zero-protected update B_a(v)=epsilon(v,0) OR epsilon(Sv,a),
   H_a(v)=D(B_a(v),v), gives H_a(0)=0 and U(0,a)=1 for every a.
   U(j,a)=Sj OR S U(j,a)=a is proved with a fixed passive parameter a.
   In the first branch, U= Sj is nonzero; a failed hit advances to S²j,
   and a successful hit freezes. In the second branch the hit remains
   true and the update fixes the state. No cancellation is involved.

5. The separate lemma U(j,a)!=0, although true, is not needed for the last
   invariant proof. The clock branch supplies nonzeroness locally, and
   the hit branch freezes even if the current state were zero. Omitting
   that auxiliary induction makes the written argument shorter.

6. Specialization to j=m, a=S²m happens only after the fixed-a invariant
   is proved. Both alternatives imply S U(m,S²m)=S²m. The update now fixes
   that state; two (exactly two) computation steps show U(S²m,S²m)=U(m,S²m).
   Thus S pstar(S²m)=S²m. There is no meta-level loop with nonstandard
   length and no use of injectivity to identify the state itself.

7. The final invariant C(n)=(n=0 OR n=1 OR S pstar(n)=n) is quantifier-free.
   Its successor step has three valid cases. In the final case, substitute
   m=pstar(n) into the already universal right-inverse theorem, and use
   Sn=S²pstar(n). This is ordinary capture-free term substitution into a
   proved theorem, not induction with a varying passive parameter.

8. Actual coverage n=0 OR exists m n=Sm follows by ordinary existential
   introduction in the latter two cases (witnesses 0 and pstar(n)). All
   reasoning is internal to the whole numerical domain.

Additional sound but nonessential observation:
  The same assumptions rule out fixed points of S. Let h(v)=epsilon(v,0).
  Boolean range and 0!=1 prove h(v)!=v for every v. If Sa=a, the recursor
  equations force F_h(a)=h(F_h(a)), contradiction. This does not establish
  successor injectivity and should not be presented as doing so.

Further lemma supplied by arithmetic_basis and independently checked:
  The predecessor of 1 is unique. Since S has no fixed points, 2!=1;
  successor-nonzeroness also gives 2!=0. Put h(v)=not_B epsilon(v,2)
  and V(n)=F_h(n). Then h(0)=1, V(0)=2, V(1)=h(2)=0, and h has
  range {0,1}, excluding 2. Derived coverage therefore proves
  V(n)=2 iff n=0. If Sn=1, then h(V(n))=V(Sn)=V(1)=0. The Boolean
  truth table gives V(n)=2, hence n=0. This is a boundary cancellation
  theorem only; the general step for arbitrary targets remains open.

FINITE-PREFIX RIGIDITY ADDENDUM (independent review of the TeX extension)

  For each externally fixed standard k>=1, the theory proves
  Sx=bar(k) iff x=bar(k-1), with x ranging over the whole domain.
  The arithmetic agent's displayed proof has been checked as follows.

  A. The external induction data are correctly chosen: distinct numerals
     0,...,k+1, and predecessor uniqueness delta_j for every 1<=j<k.
     The k=1 start needs only the previously proved distinctness of 0, 1, 2.

  B. The finite update h_k sends i to i-1 for 1<=i<=k-1, sends k+1 to k-1,
     and sends every other element (including 0 and k) to k. The disjointness
     of these guards follows from the carried numeral distinctness.

  C. The threshold Boolean b_r is correctly false exactly when the target
     value is below r. For a guard v=i, the target i-1 is below r exactly
     when i<=r; for v=k+1, target k-1 is below r only when r=k. Thus the
     displayed finite disjunction is correct. The source composition
     t_r=D(not_B b_r,t_(r-1)) increments exactly for true b_r. Exactly k
     external term-construction steps yield the intended finite numeral.
     No arithmetic order, finite-case function recursor extension, or
     variable-k iteration is used.

  D. Its markers q0=k+1 and q_j=k-j for 1<=j<=k are distinct. The image
     of h_k omits q0 and h_k(v)=q_j iff v=q_(j-1) for 1<=j<=k. This also
     holds at k=1, where the first table row is empty, q0=2, and q1=0.

  E. A_k(0)=q0 follows from h_k(0)=k; exactly j fixed computations give
     A_k(bar(j))=q_j for 0<=j<=k. The proof of A_k(n)=q0 iff n=0 is a
     legitimate quantifier-free induction (or can use established coverage).

  F. For j<k, the external j-induction uses only already proved delta_j.
     In the successor case, A_k(Sm)=q_j iff A_k(m)=q_(j-1) iff m=j-1,
     while Sm=j has the same condition by delta_j. The zero case has two
     false sides. Hence no future delta_k is used circularly.

  G. From Sx=k, recursor computation and A_k(k)=q_k give A_k(x)=q_(k-1),
     hence x=k-1. For k=1 this uses the established q0 equivalence directly.

  H. Extending distinctness to k+2 is correct: equality to a positive j<=k
     reduces by delta_j to k+1=j-1, contradicting the old distinctness.
     Equality to 0 contradicts successor-nonzeroness; equality to k+1
     contradicts the general no-fixed-point lemma. This closes the outer
     external induction.

  No change is needed in this proof. Its finite source constructions and
  finite derivations are parameterized at the metalevel. They do not yield
  a single internal formula quantifying over the numeral index k, and no
  arbitrary-target successor cancellation is inferred.

Limits:
  No proof of pstar(Sn)=n without cancellation was obtained, and no complete
  countermodel of all F schemas plus induction was constructed. In particular
  a branching successor graph by itself would not be an adequate countermodel.
  The review is written mathematical verification, not Lean/Rocq verification.
