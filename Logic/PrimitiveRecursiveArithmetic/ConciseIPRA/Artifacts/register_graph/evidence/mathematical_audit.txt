INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE REGISTER-ITERATOR BASIS
==============================================

This note records mathematical checks separately from executable tests.
The primary checker uses counter-aware J(n;i,r.h). The scalar variant
J(n;r.h) has the same constant-update bootstrap below.

1. STEP GRAPH IS EXACTLY THE TWO COMPUTATION RULES PLUS COVERAGE

Put v=J(n;i,r.h) and e=h[n/i,v/r]. The graph is

  J(Sn;i,r.h)=b <-> ((e=0 and b=Sv) or e=Sb).

Its right-to-left direction gives both conditional computation rules:

  e=0  => J(Sn;i,r.h)=Sv;
  e=St => J(Sn;i,r.h)=t.

For functional coverage, take h to be the constant term a, n=0, and
b=W(a):=J(1;i,r.a). Reflexivity supplies the graph's left side. Its
right side implies a=0 or a=S(W(a)). Hence ordinary successor coverage
is derived, not separately assumed.

Conversely, from the two conditional computations and coverage, split e
into 0 or St. If e=0 and J(Sn)=b, computation gives b=Sv. If e=St,
computation gives b=t, so e=Sb. This proves the graph's left-to-right
direction; its other direction is the two computations. This equivalence
uses no induction, separation, or successor injectivity.

The analogous full graph for arbitrary n is equivalent to J0 plus this
step graph. Its right-to-left direction gives J0 and both computations;
its left-to-right direction applied to J(n;0)=J(n;0) gives coverage.
Thus a single full graph is possible, but it has a larger logical body.

2. THE SUCCESSOR ABBREVIATION IS NOT CIRCULAR

S(a) means the finite term J(a;i,r.0). The equations below use this
abbreviation, not an additional primitive successor symbol. Finite term
bodies are not quantified numerical descriptions. In an interpretation
into iPRA, J_h is defined by ordinary primitive recursion, with update
returning state+1 for command zero and predecessor(command) otherwise.
The fixed zero program then represents ordinary successor by equation
induction. The definition of the target primitive-recursive function uses
iPRA's own successor; it does not presuppose the source abbreviation's
correctness.

3. PREDECESSOR AND SELECTION REQUIRE NO INDUCTION OR COVERAGE

Let f(n,a)=J(n;i,r.a), 1=S0. Direct computation gives

  f(0,a)=1,    f(Sn,Sa)=a,    f(n,0)=Sn.

The last identity is the definition of S. In particular f(Sn,0)=S^2 n
does not require an induction or even the nonconstant zero-command rule.

Define

  Z(x)=f(x,1), W(x)=f(1,x), D(x)=f(x,Sx), P(x)=W(D(x)).

The exact endpoint identities are

  Z0=1,       Z(Sx)=0;
  W0=2,       W(Sx)=x;
  D0=1,       D(Sx)=Sx;
  P0=0,       P(Sx)=x.

Each is a finite endpoint calculation. In particular predecessor is
available before induction and before any arbitrary numerical case split.
Successor injectivity follows by applying P. If Sx=0, applying Z gives
0=1, so SEP gives successor separation.

The shortened positive gate and selector are

  G(c,a)=P^2 f(Sa,Zc),
  C(c,a,b)=P^2 f(Sa,G(c,S^3 b)).

At c=0, Zc=1, so G(0,a)=P^2(0)=0. At c=Sn, Zc=0, so
G(Sn,a)=P^2(S^2 a)=a. Consequently C(0,a,b)=a and C(Sn,a,b)=b:
its inner command is respectively 0 or S^3 b, and the counter Sa is
visibly a successor in both cases. No claim about arbitrary c is needed
to prove these endpoint schemas.

4. THE COUNTER-AWARE PR COMPILER

For a fixed desired step h(u,v) and initializer a, use the finite term

  R(n,a;u,v.h) = J(Sn;i,r. S(C(i,a,h(Pi,r)))).

Its update is always visibly a successor, so only the load computation is
used. At the first update, old counter i=0 gives command Sa, hence loads a.
At the next update, old counter i=Sn gives command
S(h(P(Sn),R(n,a;h)))=S(h(n,R(n,a;h))). Thus

  R(0,a;h)=a,
  R(Sn,a;h)=h(n,R(n,a;h)).

These are finite endpoint derivations, using neither induction nor
coverage. They compile arbitrary fixed primitive-recursive definitions
directly. All substitutions into h are simultaneous and capture avoiding.

5. THE SCALAR COMPILER ALSO CHECKS OUT

With a scalar J(n;r.h), set

  H(v)=S^3 C(Pv,a,h(P^2v)),
  K(n)=J(Sn;v.H(v)),
  I(n,a;h)=P^2 K(n).

One positive-load computation gives K(n)=S^2 q(n) for an explicit term
q(n). Hence P(K(n)) is visibly a successor. The initial state is 1, and
P1=0, so K0=S^2a. At the next update selection at the positive argument
P(K(n)) gives K(Sn)=S^2 h(P^2K(n)). Applying P twice proves the two
ordinary scalar-iterator equations. Again no induction or coverage is
used. This is the same-tagging variant, not the counter-aware compiler.

6. BOTH COMPOSITES NEED AND HAVE AN INTERNAL UNIQUENESS PROOF

The numerical domain is the whole domain in each direction and equality
is actual equality. Recovery of PR computations is not by itself a proof
of bi-interpretability; the source J operator must also be recovered.

Suppose two fixed J recurrences have the same initial value and the same
instruction body, up to already proved equalities of smaller term bodies.
Induct on the literal equation asserting equality of their nth states.
Their current instructions are equal under that induction hypothesis.
Coverage splits the common instruction into zero or a successor. The two
conditional computations give equal next states in the respective cases.
This proves recurrence uniqueness internally, with passive parameters
fixed throughout the induction.

The iPRA interpretation of J satisfies these same source recurrences, so
this uniqueness proof supplies the J-side numerical composite. Ordinary
PR recursion uniqueness similarly supplies the other numerical composite.
With ordinary first-order logic preserved, formula composites follow by
structural induction, and the domain isomorphisms have graph x=y.

To recover quantifier-free induction from literal-equation induction one
still needs the standard internal equality-characteristic argument. The
new finite bootstrap supplies P, C, all PR computations, coverage, and
separation before that argument is used. Thus importing that established
completion lemma is not circular. It is a genuine proof dependency and
must be cited or reproduced; finite evaluations do not replace it.

7. CLAIM BOUNDARY

The grammar removes the independent successor and initializer fields;
the counter-aware version pays for a second bound update coordinate.
The graph explicitly accounts for command decoding and numerical cases.
The immediate bootstrap and direct PR compiler are mathematical benefits.
Neither fewer named schemas nor fewer primitive term constructors proves
a smaller standalone proof checker. The supplied readable checker is
deliberately complete and much larger than the current compact frontier.
