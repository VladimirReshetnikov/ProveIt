FINAL FOCUSED REVIEW: CANONICAL PREDECESSORS
10 November 2026

An independent assistant review examined whether the finite-case right
predecessor P supplies an inductive canonical subset C(n): P(Sn)=n.

Proved without successor injectivity:
  T(n) := P(Sn)
  S(T(n)) = Sn
  T(T(n)) = T(n), by applying P to the first equation
  T(0) = 0.

Thus C is the fixed-point set of a retraction and contains zero. Its
successor closure would, by the available quantifier-free induction,
give T=id and hence successor injectivity. Conversely injectivity
immediately gives T=id and successor closure.

The proposed clock-intertwining proof does not establish that closure.
For v != 0, S(h_{Sn}(v)) stops when Sv=Sn, whereas h_{S^2n}(Sv) stops
when S^2v=S^2n. The contrary case S^2v=S^2n and Sv!=Sn makes the two
expressions S^2v and Sv, distinct by fixed-point exclusion. Thus this
route needs cancellation, at least on the visited states, or a new
internally proved orbit invariant. No such invariant was established.

This is a precise gap analysis, not an independence or impossibility
proof. It does not change the stated limits of the main result.
