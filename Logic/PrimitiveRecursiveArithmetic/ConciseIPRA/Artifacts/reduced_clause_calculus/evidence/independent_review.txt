Review status: no mathematical or proof-translation gap found in the local

1. IDENTITY FRAME AND SEQUENT EQUIVALENCE

The retained V0 rule is a=t, I(0,a;u.h)=r => r=t. Set W(s)=I(0,s;u.0).
Given p:s=t and q:s=r, V0(q,Refl(W(s))) concludes W(s)=r, and V0 with
p and that conclusion gives r=t. This is exact syntactic matching and uses
neither ordinary substitution on proofs nor a previously derived equality
law. W(s) is well scoped if s is. Each input subtree occurs once.

All other rules preserve their original contexts, scopes and formulas.
Structural induction on a baseline derivation therefore compiles all clause
sequents, including arbitrary retained contexts, with literally identical
end formulas. Every retained inference is a baseline inference. This proves
both directions of consequence equivalence. Both formula and term maps are
identity maps; composing this with the existing baseline bi-interpretation
is legitimate, including its internal formula round trips and identity
numerical composite graphs.

Rule-count boundary: there are still five serialized instruction families,
and six mathematical schemas if the two Eval branches are counted separately.
DI also retains its explicit syntax-directed mode test. Saying 'four rules'
or claiming the bare five-tag count is an encoding-independent minimum would
be incorrect.

The exact proof-node overhead is N_new=N_old+2k, where k counts baseline
Root nodes selected by the Eval dispatch. No constant certificate-byte bound
follows: the inserted frame contains the derived source term s, which can be
large compared with the source instruction.

2. DISPATCH OVERLAPS AND REVERSE TRANSLATION

No retained computational Eval instance can be selected as Root by the
baseline. A zero case would require I(0,a;u.h) to be literally a, impossible
for a finite raw syntax tree. A successor case would require
I(S c,a;u.h)=I(c,a;u.h) literally, hence S c=c, likewise impossible.
This explains both exact reverse inclusion and idempotence of the compiler.

The compiler correctly tests baseline b==s before changing a v node, matching
the original evaluator's priority. In e and i branches it reconstructs exactly
the original extended clause context and fresh negative variable level.
Compiled premises are recursively preserved, so each added frame matches in
the intended local scope. The compiler is outside the trusted checker and
both source and target are checked by separate standalone checkers.

3. TYPING AND BINDING AUDIT

The public contract requires exactly keys goal/proof and a closed Eq/D goal.
term excludes booleans by exact type(a) is int. A successor [0,a] checks
both entries, so a boolean False cannot masquerade as a numeric zero tag.
Derived equality premises in v must unpack as pairs; D formulas fail that
unpacking. The second left term must unpack as an iterator triple. If its
counter is nonzero, its valid-term invariant and length-two check force it
to be a successor. Incorrect shapes raise exceptions, and the public entry
point converts them to rejection. No assertion is used, so optimization
does not remove checks.

The derivation invariant is that results and context entries are well scoped.
All explicit goal/witness/reflexivity inputs are validated. Negative levels
are free variables and positive indices are locally bound. Substituted terms
have no free positive indices; copying them under a binder therefore does not
require lifting them. opening tracks the one iterator binder and the two D
binders. E verifies its final target in the old scope before opening a fresh
variable; I verifies witness data in the old scope. These checks enforce
eigenvariable non-escape and absence from old context and passive parameters.

As for the baseline, the mathematical completeness statement is naturally
read with unbounded finite resources. A particular CPython process has
recursion, time and memory limits and can reject an otherwise valid oversized
certificate. Tests are evidence for the implementation, not a replacement for
the syntactic invariant and mathematical proof.

5. FULL-NORMALIZATION ALTERNATIVE: COST AND BOUNDARY

Normalize both I(0,a;h)->a and I(Sn,a;h)->h[I(n,a;h)/u], on finite scalar
syntax with well-founded finite body descriptions. A term-level termination
argument can be supplied by translating to the usual strongly normalizing
first-order primitive-recursion rewrite system. The uniform evaluator is not
primitive recursive; closed descriptions can have arbitrarily high primitive
recursive growth. No uniform total evaluator may be silently postulated
inside iPRA.

On the quotient/canonical syntax, computations disappear from the rule table,
but equality still needs reflexivity and a transport principle. With the three
clause rules this is Refl + Tr + U + E + I: still five families. A one-template
Tr needs the template in the certificate, validation of that extra binder,
and two normalized instantiations. All normalization, including substitutions
that expose new redexes, belongs in the trusted checker and size comparison.
The current term-inferred VS case cannot simply remain unchanged after full
normalization, because its identifying successor-iterator expression has
already contracted. It would need supplied syntax or a different rule.
There is no demonstrated smaller complete checker from this route here.

6. A PRECISE BOUNDARY FOR THE SR0 RESEARCH QUESTION

The incoming raw-clausal report proves that successor rigidity SR0 implies
zero-equation induction. The canonical-recursors report supplies a model of
zero-equation induction with two-point fibers over nonstandard numbers and
singletons over standard numerals. At nonstandard counters c its identity
iterator is I(c,a;u.u)=(pi(a),0).

That model does NOT validate SR0. Fix a nonstandard c, and take the finite term
template f(a)=I(c,a;u.u). In the model, f(0)=0 and f(Sa)=S f(a) for every a,
because standardness is successor invariant and the iterator resets the label
to zero. But f(b,1)=(b,0)!=(b,1) for nonstandard b. It violates exactly the
conclusion of SR0. Thus the existing zero-induction countermodel cannot settle
whether SR0 recovers full equation induction. This observation is a direct
check of that known model, not a completed SR0 axiomatization.

A potentially useful SR0-specific target: it would prove predecessor from
any finite scalar term P satisfying the unconditional equations P(1)=0 and
P(SSn)=S(P(Sn)), by applying SR0 to f(n)=P(Sn). Existing scalar predecessor
proofs do not establish that recurrence without more general iteration
uniqueness or equation induction, so importing them would remain circular.
