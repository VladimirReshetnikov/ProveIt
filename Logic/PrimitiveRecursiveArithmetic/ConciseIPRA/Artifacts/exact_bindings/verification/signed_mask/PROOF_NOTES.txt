EXACT CANONICAL MASK INTEGRATION

This note proves the new syntax and operational claims. Arithmetic and logical
adequacy of the unchanged native calculus is inherited from ConciseIPRA at
e88ebf229bb61bd870bb229b9c97caaa1e044e6e. The note does not assert a new
arithmetic axiom deletion or a global checker-size optimum.

1. Canonical public trees

The numeral zero is the integer 1. A variable in slot j, counting from zero,
has integer code 3+2j. A unary term [t] is successor. A ternary term [n,a,h]
is the iterator. An equation is the pair [s,t], and a unary formula [B] is
the paired binder D. The term/formula sort determines the meaning of a unary
array. A root goal is a closed formula.

The compact uniform traversal introduces administrative slots even where the
mathematical syntax introduces no variable: successor prepends two dummy
slots; the iterator's counter and seed each prepend one dummy slot; its body
prepends one genuine accumulator slot; D prepends two genuine slots. Equality
changes from formula sort to term sort without adding a numerical slot.
Canonical variable occurrences may refer only to genuine slots.

Let n be the number of slots currently in scope, and let U be the subset of
genuine slots. Define the positive integer

    A = 1 + sum(2**(j+1) for j in U).

Its bit zero represents numerical zero. Its other set bits represent exactly
the permitted variable codes. The signed mask is M=-A at formula sort and
M=A at term sort. Initially M=-1. For a term scalar a, canonical admissibility
is exactly: a is positive and odd, and bit a//2 of A is one.

2. The update identity

The four constructor updates are:

    D:          -A -> -(4A+3)
    successor:   A ->   4A-3
    equality:   -A ->   A
    iterator:    A ->   2A-1  in counter/seed,
                              2A+1  in body.

Thus both unary constructors use the same expression 4M-3. A non-unary
formula is an equation and uses -M. A non-unary term is an iterator and uses
2M+2[j=2]-1, with j the child index. These are precisely the expressions
in the extended opener. The sign records the syntactic sort only during
public validation; that invariant is not required for arbitrary proof data.

3. Exact public validation

At the beginning of a root call, the scope floor is k=0. The inherited arity
test permits array lengths 1 and 2 at even traversal depth d (formulas), and
lengths 1 and 3 at odd depth (terms). Its scalar guard is strengthened by
the indicated mask bit. At formula positions d=2n; at term positions
d=2n+3. Consequently a genuine scalar code automatically satisfies the
existing upper bound a<d, and its lower bound k<=a excludes negatives.
The factor d & (a % 2) excludes scalars at formula positions and excludes
even integers at term positions. The additional bit test excludes exactly
dummy-slot references and references beyond the available variables.

An induction on public trees, using the update identity, proves:

    The first opener call is defined exactly on the canonical closed
    Eq/D encoder image, and on that image its return value is the input.

Failure paths consult the invalid default image (the checking function in
the byte profile, a complex scalar in the node profile) and raise; malformed
array shapes likewise fail. The whole-input lexical test is unchanged and
rejects every parsed JSON value outside the exact integer/list alphabet,
including unused entries. The mask proof assumes that alphabet; the reader
enforces it before the root opener is reached.

4. Preservation during proof checking

After public validation the entry-reservation profiles make k negative and
never restore it to zero. The guard `k or ...` therefore bypasses the mask
bit test in every subsequent opener call. The added mask computations are
integer additions, multiplications, comparisons, and sign changes; they are
defined on every finite auxiliary tree. They have no side effects and do
not change the opener's returned tree or its original failure behavior.
Resetting the mask when an image is opened is harmless in this phase.

There is one essential qualification. The mask is KEYWORD-ONLY. The existing
core calls i(*a,t,2) to open a paired binder. A unary a supplies three
positional arguments, as required. An equation a supplies four and must
raise. Making the mask a fourth positional argument destroys this arity
check and admits a proof of 0=S0. The explicit keyword-only marker in the
final checkers preserves the old three-positional-argument interface. Every
new internal recursive call passes its mask by keyword.

The command and certificate interface, allocation order, retained context,
computation frame, and recursive proof-checking function are unchanged from
the corresponding scope_phase source. Induction over ideal finite execution
therefore proves equality of accepted raw certificates between each new
program and its source program preceded by the canonical predicate. The
predicate-wrapped controls shipped in controls/ express exactly that claim.

5. Consequences for the arithmetic presentation

The source completeness theorem supplies a finite certificate for every
canonical encoding of a native theorem. The exact mask admits every such
encoding. Conversely, every accepted goal is canonical and the unchanged
source checker certifies its native reading. Thus the new checker accepts
exactly the canonical encodings of native theorems (with existential
quantification over finite certificate supplies).

The native language, rules, formula interpretation, and arithmetic
interpretation are unchanged. In particular, the inherited whole-domain,
actual-equality bi-interpretation with full first-order iPRA, including both
formula round trips and identity numerical comparison graphs, applies.
The new mask is proof-certificate syntax validation, not an object-language
axiom. This is a smaller complete realization of the exact canonical input
contract. It does not beat the compact cross-contract envelope or establish
an absolute simplicity minimum.

6. Scope of the evidence

verify.py compares the exact grammar against the upstream explicit
availability-list predicate, compares proof-phase openings against the
unmodified source, and runs the final complete programs and matched exact
wrappers on inherited positive and adversarial certificates with ordinary
and optimized Python. The finite evidence detected the positional-parameter
mistake and verifies its repair on the supplied cases. It supplements,
rather than replaces, the preceding universal invariants.
