EXACT ENCODER-IMAGE ENTRY POINT
=============================

The standalone file code/checker_exact.py contains both the complete
587-byte inference core and the exact closed-goal predicate. It imports
only the standard JSON module. Every byte of this file is charged in its
reported measurements: 581 tokens, 641 AST nodes, 1424 normalized UTF-8
bytes, and 631 gzip-9 bytes on CPython 3.12.14 / zlib 1.3.2.

1. The syntax and the availability mask
-------------------------------------
The abstract numerical terms have the constructors variable, zero,
successor, and scalar iteration I(n,a;u.h). The abstract formulas have
equations and the paired binder D_{x,y} B. Zero is encoded by the integer
1. A reference to position j is encoded by 3+2*j. The arity codec uses
padding positions in addition to genuine numerical binders.

An availability mask is a finite sequence of Booleans, with the nearest
position first. A scalar is accepted as a term precisely if it is 1, or
if it equals 3+2*j for an in-range position j whose mask bit is True.
Booleans and floats are not Python integers for this test: type(a) is int
is required. Negative references are not admitted in a closed public goal.

The recursive mask transitions are:

  successor child:          (False,False) + mask;
  iterator counter or seed: (False,) + mask;
  iterator body:            (True,) + mask;
  equation term children:   the same mask;
  D matrix:                (True,True) + mask.

The term predicate permits only unary and ternary arrays. The formula
predicate permits only binary equations and unary D arrays. Thus an
integer/list tree has no ambiguous constructor at a known term/formula
position. A term scalar either denotes zero or selects one uniquely
specified genuine variable position.

Lemma. formula(A,()) holds exactly when A is the canonical encoding of a
closed abstract Eq/D formula.

Proof. Prove the more general term/formula statement simultaneously for
every mask. In the scalar term case the test gives exactly zero or an
available genuine variable, and the encoding/decoding are inverse. At
successor the two codec positions are padding, so neither can be named;
the remaining positions correspond in order to the previous mask. At
iteration the first two children each have one padding position, whereas
the body has the one newly bound accumulator. The inductive hypotheses
therefore apply with exactly the displayed masks. Equation adds no genuine
or padding slots to its term arguments. D introduces two genuine variables.
All remaining arities and types fail. These cases prove both directions,
as well as uniqueness of decoding. The empty root mask is precisely the
closed-formula case. QED.

Consequently [[3],[1]] and [[5],[1]] are rejected: the left successor
argument selects its first or second padding position. In contrast
[[[7],[7]]] is admitted: D supplies genuine positions which lie below the
two successor padding positions. The term [1,1,3] is admitted because its
last child names the iterator's genuine accumulator. A 3 in its counter
or seed instead selects padding and is rejected.

2. Soundness and completeness on the exact public language
--------------------------------------------------------
The inference core's AST is identical to code/checker_bytes587.py. Its
input remains the full parsed JSON stack, with the public goal last and
the same four-field terminal packets before it. The whole-input alphabet
gate is unchanged. The only extra action before its root call is the
test that the input is a nonempty list and its final member satisfies
the exact predicate.

If the exact checker accepts, the whole-input gate has admitted an
integer/list tree, the lemma supplies a canonical closed native formula,
and the inherited arbitrary-input soundness of the inference core proves
its ordinary reading. No promised public syntax remains in this statement.

Conversely, take the canonical encoding A of any closed native theorem.
The canonical-completeness theorem for byte587 supplies a finite accepted
integer/list certificate for A. The extra predicate succeeds by the lemma
and changes neither packet consumption nor inference state. The same
certificate is accepted by checker_exact.py.

Thus the exact entry point recognizes precisely the canonical encodings
of native theorems. The established ordinary realization of the native
calculus and its bi-interpretation with full first-order iPRA are unchanged.
There is no assertion that every noncanonical alias is provable, since
these aliases are outside the exact public language.

3. Evidence and limitations
---------------------------
verify_exact.py checks the inference-core AST identity and the sole import,
13 targeted grammar cases, and 102 isolated ordinary/-O executions. All
canonical terminal positives remain accepted, including an explicit
zero-computation whose body uses its bound accumulator; padding aliases
are rejected before proof checking. These tests support the implementation.
They do not prove the universal semantic obstruction for the second
successor padding slot, which is a separate mathematical result.

The exact entry point is deliberately readable and makes no complexity
record claim. No end-to-end Lean or Rocq verification is claimed. As for
the inherited core, mathematical completeness concerns arbitrarily large
finite executions rather than a fixed physical resource bound.
