Verdict
-------
The two obstruction theorems and the no-interpretation corollary are correct
with the scope stated in the note. They do not supply a smaller iPRA theory,
do not establish a checker-size lower bound, and do not disprove any proposed
equiconsistency statement. They show that this particular proposed theory
cannot meet the mandatory bi-interpretability requirement.

Theory and model scope
----------------------
Fix the pure simply typed lambda calculus with one ground type o, arrow
types, application, abstraction, and no constants. The numerical type is
N_o=(o->o)->o->o. Every first-order numerical function symbol is represented
by a closed lambda term N_o^k->N_o; arbitrarily complicated intermediate
simple types are allowed. All first-order variables range over numbers.
This is not a first-order theory whose variables range over arbitrary lambda
syntax, arbitrary polymorphic functions, or all elements of all simple types.

Interpret o by {0,1}, arrows by full finite function sets, and the numerical
first-order domain by the denotations of the actual Church numerals only.
That domain has exactly three elements: zero, positive odd, positive even.
The full semantic carrier of N_o is larger; it must not be substituted for
this three-element numerical subalgebra.

Closure proof
-------------
For each closed M:N_o^k->N_o and each tuple of numeral denotations, choose
Church numeral representatives. The term M applied to those representatives
is a closed pure term of type N_o. Strong normalization and eta-long normal
forms give lambda f x. f^n x: a normal ground term in the context
f:o->o,x:o is either x or f applied to another such term. Therefore M maps
the three-element domain to itself. Its set denotation respects equality of
representatives, so this operation is well-defined.

This closure proof is valid for N_o. It is not asserted for N_tau at every
compound type tau, where additional closed normal inhabitants can exist.
The separate arbitrary-tau predecessor theorem does not require closure or
canonicity and is unaffected by that distinction.

Axioms and induction
---------------------
Beta-eta equations are valid by ordinary set-model soundness. Successor is
the total map 0->1, 1->2, 2->1. Hence every nonzero element is a successor,
zero is not a successor, and S(0)=S(2) although 0 and 2 are distinct.

Full numerical first-order induction holds, including formulas with arbitrary
parameters and every numerical symbol in the infinite signature. For any
parameter assignment, any formula defines a subset P of the three-element
domain. If 0 is in P and P is closed under S, then 1 and 2 are in P. This
argument covers all subsets, not only subsets already known to be definable.
The one-world Kripke model is a model of the intuitionistic theory as well.

An independent enumeration of all eight subsets reproduced the induction
claim. The finite Python verification checks this model and finite examples
of the general power identity; it does not verify all lambda terms. The
closure theorem and the arbitrary-cardinality statement are proved on paper.

Interpretation obstruction
---------------------------
An ordinary first-order interpretation with a fixed finite tuple domain in
this model has a finite domain, and taking a definable subdomain or quotient
cannot enlarge it. Finitely many source sorts, parameter choices, and finite
disjoint unions of such tuple domains still produce finite sets. If an
interpretation requires parameters, its existence condition must be proved
by the source theory and therefore holds in this model.

Every classical model of iPRA has a nonempty numerical domain, an injective
total successor, and a zero omitted from its image. On a finite set a total
injective endomap is surjective, which contradicts the omitted zero.
Consequently no such interpretation of iPRA in the Church theory exists,
and in particular there is no bi-interpretation of the required kind.

Do not infer non-equiconsistency from this finite-model argument. In an
external mathematical sense both theories can of course be consistent; an
equiconsistency statement needs a specified metatheory and is not settled
here. Failure of bi-interpretability already excludes the candidate.

Arbitrary fixed simple-type predecessor
----------------------------------------
Interpret all ground types by two-element sets. For a fixed simple type tau,
its full semantic carrier has q>=2 elements. Let m=q-1 and
L=lcm(1,...,q). Every endomap f on q elements satisfies f^(m+L)=f^m, since
after m iterations every orbit is on a cycle of length dividing L. Thus
Church m and Church m+L have equal denotations at N_tau.

The endomap f(i)=min(i+1,q-1), with initial point 0, distinguishes Church
m-1 and Church m+L-1: their values are q-2 and q-1. A denoted function
P:N_tau->N_tau cannot map equal arguments to unequal outputs, so no closed
pure simply typed P can compute predecessor on every numeral at this fixed
type. Both tested input numerals are positive; no convention for pred(0)
is needed for the contradiction.

At q=2 this is the particularly short argument Church 1=Church 3 while
Church 0 differs from Church 2.

Literature and claim limits
---------------------------
Same-type Church numeral predecessor nondefinability is established
literature, also following from the stronger characterization of numerical
functions by extended polynomials (Schwichtenberg). The package should not
claim this nondefinability theorem as a new discovery. The explicit three-
element arithmetic model, including full formula induction and the
finite-dimensional interpretation consequence, is a self-contained analysis
of the proposed simplification. No claim of historical novelty is needed.

This does not exclude polymorphism, varying Church types, untyped combinatory
calculi, additional arithmetic constants or recursors, a different numeral
representation, or a newly introduced infinite syntax domain. Their added
mechanisms require a separate soundness and bi-interpretability proof and
must be included in any complexity accounting.
