New schema
  I_h(n,h(a,p),p) = h(I_h(n,a,p),p).
This is seed shift / commutation with the iterator's own update, not a
general fusion or naturality law between two distinct updates.

Strengthened ultrapower lemma

Fix a term descriptor h and parameter representatives p_i in A for a tuple
p in B=A^N/U. Set f_i(x)=h^A(x,p_i), f=h^B(-,p). For each integer j define
  H_j([a_i]) = [ f_i^(max(0,i+j))(a_i) ].
This gives an actual function B->B, not merely a chosen point orbit.
Well-definedness in a: if [a_i]=[b_i], then a_i=b_i on a U-large set, and
finite repeated application of the same f_i preserves equality on that
set, independently of how large its finite exponent is.

The functions satisfy:
1. H_(j+1)=f o H_j, since the needed exponent identity holds on a cofinite
   set of indices. A single cofinite set works for every seed at fixed j.
2. H_j o f=f o H_j by coordinatewise commutation of finite powers with f_i.
3. If f(a)=a, then f_i(a_i)=a_i on a U-large set, so every finite iterate
   fixes a_i there, and H_j(a)=a.

Choose a presenting descriptor and parameter representatives once for each
actual presented function f. It is unnecessary for the extension lemma to
claim independence of this choice: the iterator assignment is made once for
the actual map. Old represented functions retain their old iterators. For
a genuinely new map, use finite powers on standard counters and H_j on
each nonstandard counter chain, indexed from an externally chosen origin.
This validates computation, fixed-point preservation and seed shift.
Cross-extensionality follows exactly from assigning once per actual map.

The final draft also asserts independence from the presenting descriptor.
That stronger statement is correct: equality of two presented unary maps in
B is the first-order formula forall u h(u,p)=g(u,q). Los's theorem gives a
U-large set of coordinates on which the two coordinate functions agree for
ALL coordinate inputs, hence their finite iterates of coordinate-dependent
length agree as well. Uniformity in the coordinate input is the key point.

Every old Shift/FP/Ext axiom is first-order with all parameter tuples bound,
so old-map reuse is valid even at new parameter tuples in the ultrapower.
Later expansions and the elementary tower preserve every scheme. Native
substitution has the same cross-extensional proof as the earlier model.

Strengthened seed

Use the nonnegative lexicographic cone in Z^2. Let J(n,a)=a+n and let
d(q,z)=(q,2z), so K(n,a)=a+d(n). Both operations are total on the cone.
d(0)=0 and d(Sn)=S^2(d(n)), hence K has the computation equations of S^2.
J(n,Sa)=S(J(n,a)) and K(n,S^2a)=S^2(K(n,a)), proving initial Shift.
S and S^2 have no fixed points, so initial FP is vacuous. The updates are
distinct, so initial cross-Ext is immediate.

At c=(1,0), K(c,0)=(1,0), while J(c,c)=(2,0). Thus E(n):K(n,0)=J(n,n)
fails. E(0) follows from computation. For the step one can use only
computation plus Shift:
  J(Sn,Sn)=S(J(n,Sn))=S^2(J(n,n)),
  K(Sn,0)=S^2(K(n,0)).
Congruence yields E(n)->E(Sn). Alternatively J commutativity and the
computation equation derive the needed seed shift for J in this seed theory.

The seed's commutative cancellative addition, J(n,0)=n, all computation,
FP/Shift/Ext schemes, and the bad E(c) inequation persist through the tower.

Scope

The strengthened construction shows that preservation of fixed seeds and
commutation with one's own update, even with canonical successor iteration
and a commutative cancellative additive monoid, do not imply equation
induction under the ordinary realization. It is not a lower bound on checker
size or a proof against all unrelated bi-interpretations.

The stronger cross-update fusion law is deliberately not covered. In this
model the term g(a)=J(a,a) semiconjugates S to S^2, but fusion for that g
would imply the failed equation K(n,0)=J(n,n). This is a useful explanation
of the exact boundary, not a defect in the claimed seed-shift theorem.
