# Binary-tree arithmetic: a precise alternative, not a size improvement

## Status and relation to the corpus

This gives a fully specified ordinary first-order arithmetic theory on finite
binary trees and a detailed proof route for bi-interpretability with iPRA. It
is not a claim to improve the 244-token / 265-AST / 477-byte coordinatewise
frontier. No checker for it has been implemented or measured. The underlying
tree-arithmetic idea is standard, and the existing corpus explicitly mentions
binary-tree domains as an uncompleted avenue:
`Artifacts/uniform_predecessor/evidence/arithmetic_research_status.txt`, item 2.
The corresponding article mention is in `chapters/ch10_obs.tex`, near line 3721.
The specific theory and inverse coding argument below were not found in the
inspected corpus.

## Theory BT

There is one sort. Terms are variables, a constant `e`, a binary constructor
`N(s,t)`, and a paramorphic term

    R(t; a; u,v,p,q.h).

Only `u,v,p,q` are bound in `h`; no variable is bound in `t` or `a`.
The intended domain is the finite full ordered binary trees, with leaf `e`.
The two computation schemes, for every term body `h`, are

    R(e; a; u,v,p,q.h) = a,
    R(N(s,t); a; u,v,p,q.h)
      = h[s/u, t/v, R(s;a;h)/p, R(t;a;h)/q].

The only other nonlogical axiom is

    e != N(e,e).

There is a structural induction rule for every quantifier-free formula A(x),
with parameters and side assumptions Gamma:

    Gamma |- A(e)
    Gamma, A(u), A(v) |- A(N(u,v))
    --------------------------------
    Gamma |- A(t)

Here u,v are distinct and fresh for Gamma, t, and the parameters of A. The
formula A may use all intuitionistic connectives, but no quantifier.

### Logical rules

Use full single-conclusion intuitionistic first-order natural deduction.
Contexts are finite lists of formulas; assumptions are reusable, so weakening,
exchange, and contraction are implicit. The rules are assumption; conjunction
introduction and its two eliminations; disjunction's two introductions and
case elimination with two branches discharging their respective assumptions;
implication introduction discharging its premise and modus ponens; falsum
elimination; universal introduction at a variable fresh for the undischarged
context and universal elimination at any term; existential introduction at
any term and existential elimination with a fresh eigenvariable and the
usual no-escape condition; equality reflexivity and capture-avoiding
substitution in any formula. Negation is A -> falsum. There is no excluded
middle, double-negation elimination, choice, or quantifier induction.

## Constructor properties are derived

Write 1=N(e,e). Define

    H(x)=R(x;e;u,v,p,q.u),
    T(x)=R(x;e;u,v,p,q.v),
    Z(x;a,b)=R(x;a;u,v,p,q.b).

Then H(N(s,t))=s, T(N(s,t))=t, Z(e;a,b)=a, and
Z(N(s,t);a,b)=b by computation alone. Consequently constructor injectivity
is derived by applying H and T to an equality of nodes. If e=N(s,t), apply
Z(-;e,1) to obtain e=1, contradicting separation. Thus every node is nonempty.

Coverage is also derived, by structural induction on the quantifier-free
formula

    x=e OR x=N(H(x),T(x)).

Its induction hypotheses are unused. Therefore e and N really are an
exhaustive disjoint injective constructor pair in every model of BT.

## Unary naturals inside BT

Define

    F(x)=R(x;e;u,v,p,q.Z(u;q,1)),
    U(x) :<=> F(x)=e,
    S(x)=N(e,x).

F(e)=e and F(N(u,v))=Z(u;F(v),1). Using coverage of u and separation,

    U(e),
    U(N(u,v)) <=> (u=e AND U(v)).

The intended U-elements are the unary trees. The equations above, not an
appeal to standard-model finiteness, are what is used internally.

Every primitive recursive function on U is denoted by a BT term. Zero,
successor, projections, and composition are immediate. For primitive
recursion f(0,z)=g(z), f(n+1,z)=h(n,f(n,z),z), use R on the unary input;
in its node branch ignore the left child and its recursive value and use
`h(v,q,z)`. Type preservation U(f(...)) follows by structural induction
conditioned on U of the input, with U of the parameters retained.

Quantifier-free induction on U follows from structural induction applied to

    U(x) -> A(x).

At a node U(N(u,v)) implies u=e and U(v), so the right-child induction
hypothesis supplies A(v), and the numerical step supplies A(S(v)). This
argument works for any quantifier-free formula of the entire BT language,
including formulas comparing arbitrary trees; it is stronger than merely
checking the translated original iPRA syntax. Thus iPRA is interpreted in BT
with domain U and actual equality.

## BT inside iPRA

Code e by 0 and N(s,t) by

    pi(a,b)=2^a(2b+1).

Every positive n has a unique such pair (a,b), found by primitive-recursive
bounded valuation and division. Both a and b are strictly below n. The
constructor is injective, disjoint from 0, and exhausts positive numbers.

Each fixed paramorphism R has a primitive-recursive interpretation by
course-of-values recursion on n: at n=0 return a; at n=pi(i,j) evaluate the
fixed translated h at i,j and the already computed values at i,j. A finite
table of previous values supplies the course-of-values recursion. The
translation is defined externally by induction on the finite term syntax;
no uniform total evaluator for all primitive-recursive programs is assumed.

The computation equations and separation are iPRA theorems. Structural
induction becomes quantifier-free strong numerical induction. For each
fixed quantifier-free formula A, iPRA proves decidability and gives a
primitive-recursive characteristic term. The bounded conjunction
"A(k) for all k<n" is therefore represented by a quantifier-free equation,
and ordinary quantifier-free induction proves strong induction. Since the
two child codes are smaller than the node code, the translated structural
rule follows. This interprets BT in iPRA using the whole numerical domain
and actual equality.

## Explicit inverse coding in BT

All arithmetic in this section is on U, using the PR operations just
constructed. Define the unary-valued tree code

    c(e)=0,
    c(N(s,t))=pi(c(s),c(t))

by one R term. Structural induction proves U(c(t)). Define unary inverse
pairing functions a(n),b(n) for positive n, assigning any fixed values at
zero. Their usual arithmetic identities hold in the interpreted iPRA.

Lists of arbitrary trees are represented by e and N(item,rest). Head and
tail are H and T. Define drop(k,L) by unary recursion, with drop(0,L)=L and
drop(k+1,L)=T(drop(k,L)), and get(L,k)=H(drop(k,L)).

Define a table of decoded values in reverse order:

    Tab(0)=N(e,e),
    Tab(n+1)=N(
      N(get(Tab(n), n-a(n+1)), get(Tab(n), n-b(n+1))),
      Tab(n)).

Here subtraction is unary truncated subtraction. The child-index bounds
a(n+1),b(n+1)<=n ensure that every lookup is in range. Put
d(n)=H(Tab(n)). These are genuine fixed BT terms, because Tab uses primitive
recursion on a unary argument with an arbitrary-tree-valued state.

The table lemma is

    k<=n -> get(Tab(n),n-k)=d(k).

It is proved by unary quantifier-free induction on n with k as a parameter.
At the step split k=n+1 versus k<=n, a decidable arithmetical case distinction;
the head case is immediate, and the tail case uses the induction hypothesis.
This proves the recursive equations

    d(0)=e,
    d(pi(a,b))=N(d(a),d(b))

for unary a,b.

For an elementary proof of c(d(n))=n that avoids using strong induction for
arbitrary-tree predicates, define MapC on lists by

    MapC(e)=e,
    MapC(N(u,v))=N(c(u),MapC(v)),

using R and ignoring the recursive result for u. Define the expected unary
list E(0)=N(0,e), E(n+1)=N(n+1,E(n)). Prove by unary induction

    MapC(Tab(n))=E(n).

In the step, get commutes with MapC (a separate unary induction on its
index), so the two child codes are a(n+1),b(n+1), and their paired code is
n+1. The tail is exactly the induction hypothesis. Applying H gives
c(d(n))=n. Finally structural induction, using the displayed d equations,
proves d(c(t))=t. Thus c and d are internally proved inverse definable maps
between all trees and the unary domain U.

## Both bi-interpretation round trips

For the BT round trip the isomorphism is t |-> c(t), with inverse d.
Every primitive symbol is respected: e and N by the defining coding
equations, and each R term by structural induction and uniqueness of its
two recursion equations. An induction on formulas gives the round trip for
all intuitionistic formulas, including both quantifiers, because c,d are
an internal bijection. There is no appeal to double negation.

For the iPRA round trip, unary trees coded with pi are

    u(0)=0,  u(n+1)=2u(n)+1,  hence u(n)=2^n-1.

The isomorphism from the original numerical domain to the interpreted
unary-tree subset is n |-> 2^n-1. Its inverse on that subset is the
primitive-recursive binary-length function. The U characterization proves
internally that these maps are inverse on exactly the required domain.
The arithmetic defining equations and primitive-recursion uniqueness show
that all function symbols are respected. Formula induction then proves
the second full round trip.

Consequently the two theories are bi-interpretable and equiconsistent.
The graph of either isomorphism is explicitly definable; it need not be
the identity graph, which the user's current request does not require.

## Costs and limitations

The gain is conceptual: pairing and projections are structural and the
scalar predecessor construction disappears. The price is one constant,
one binary constructor, a recursion binder with four bound variables,
two computation schemes, one separation axiom, and two recursive induction
hypotheses. Standard intuitionistic first-order logic remains explicit.
Those costs cannot be omitted from a checker comparison. This does not
establish a smaller axiomatization by the stipulated proof-checker metric,
and should not be presented as a breakthrough below the current frontier.
