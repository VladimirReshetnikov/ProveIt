2. WHY POSITIVE ROOT FLOORS ARE REJECTED UNDER THE GUARDED CONTRACT

Every closed canonical guarded public formula contains a literal numerical
zero. For a closed bare equation, a closed term must contain zero: follow
the counter or initializer of an iterator, or the argument of a successor,
until a leaf is reached. For a formula with D binders, every innermost
guarded atomic matrix has a literal-zero iterator initializer.

Every such zero is reached by the initial opener at odd depth. Equality
adds three to the depth; S, I and D add respectively four, two and four.
Thus the term-constructor and D shifts preserve parity. The initial scan
is eager over all list children, so none of these zero leaves is skipped.

If k>0, the leaf zero fails k <= 0 < d. Since d is odd, the fallback calls
i(c,d=3), where c is the default image, a Python function object. The
attempt to multiply that function by zero raises TypeError. Therefore the
root scan rejects before any proof packet is consumed.

3. WHY NONPOSITIVE ROOT FLOORS ARE HARMLESS

For k=-m <= 0, the initial scope additionally declares the m numerical
parameters -m,...,-1. No assumptions involving them are supplied: the
retained initial context is still exactly [0]. The inherited decoder and
soundness invariant extend by treating those coordinates as arbitrary
rigid free parameters. Every subsequent fresh parameter is strictly below
the current floor, hence fresh for this initial supply as well.

An accepted derivation therefore proves the same closed public G in an
empty logical context with extra free parameters. Their values are
irrelevant to G, or may be uniformly replaced by zero in the ordinary
realization. It follows that G is a theorem of that realization. Conversely
every old certificate is reproduced exactly with k=0. This proves equality
of public theorem sets, not equality of the two raw serialization languages.

This argument is a written extension of the inherited decoder theorem. It
is not a new end-to-end mechanization of the Python operational semantics.
The conventional sufficient-resources qualification is shared with the
incumbent checker.

5. AUDIT OF THE MATHEMATICAL SPLIT AXIOM

Work over ordinary intuitionistic first-order logic with equality, the
scalar computation equations, literal-equation induction, and 0 != S0.
Put C(v,a,b)=I(v,a;u.b), Z(n)=C(n,S0,0). The repository's independently
equational predecessor theorem supplies P(0)=0 and P(Sn)=n without using
coverage or logical case distinction.

One literal-equation induction proves

  n = C(Z(n), S(P(n)), 0).

The zero instance is immediate. At Sx, the zero-test computation gives
Z(Sx)=0, and P(Sx)=x reduces the right side to Sx. No disjunctive
induction invariant or coverage assumption is needed.

Consequently the quantifier-free schema

  n=0 OR Z(n)=0

implies ordinary coverage n=0 OR exists x(n=Sx): in the second branch use
the displayed identity and choose x=P(n). Conversely coverage gives the
schema by Z(Sx)=0. The two ordinary theories therefore have exactly the
same consequences, by the identity translation, and their existing iPRA
bi-interpretation transfers. This is a real simplification of the
non-equational arithmetic axiom, not a smaller measured complete checker.

The companion Boolean-range schema Z(n)=0 OR Z(n)=S0 is equivalent as
well: Split gives it by Z(0)=S0, and the displayed identity gives n=0 in
the Z(n)=S0 branch. Preserve 0 != S0 explicitly in the ordinary basis.
