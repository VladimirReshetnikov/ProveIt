INDEPENDENT MATHEMATICAL REVIEW: A 57-NODE SCALAR PREDECESSOR
Prepared during this research run, 12 November 2026.

VERDICT

The new upper-ramp construction and the reversed-comparison predecessor
are correct in the inherited ordinary realization T_eq. All induction
invariants can be single literal equations. The argument does not assume
predecessor, successor injectivity, pairing, a division algorithm, or
arbitrary quantifier-free induction. Boolean case distinctions use numerical
coverage and the selector equations already available in T_eq/full native.
If a stronger claim in the pure equational EqPrf fragment is wanted, the
case distinctions should be replaced by its proved two-point equational
rule; that additional formal presentation is not supplied by this review.

NEW TERMS

Primitive terms are x, 0, S(t), I(n,a;u.h). Only u in h is bound.
All macro expansion uses fresh bound variables, avoiding capture.

  C(n,a,b) = I(n,a;u.b), with u absent from b
  Z(n) = C(n,1,0)
  N(n) = I(n,0;u.Z(u))
  J(v) = I(v,2;w.Z(w))
  M(n) = I(n,0;v.J(v))
  R(n) = I(n,0;u.S(C(M(u),S(u),u)))
  H(u) = C(N(u),R(u),u)
  E(x) = I(x,1;u.2u)
  L(x,y) = N(I(y,E(x);u.H(u)))

Here 2u means u+u, with a+b=I(b,a;v.S(v)); it is a macro, not an
additional constructor. The new proposed predecessor is

  P(m) = I(m,0;u.C(L(m,S(u)),S(u),u)).

1. CLOCK AND RAMP

The three fixed computations are J(0)=2, J(1)=0, J(2)=1. Thus M follows
the order 0,2,1,0 on standard numerals, but no general modulo theorem is
needed in the internal proof.

The inherited parity terms give N(n) in {0,1}, N(Sn)=Z(N(n)),
N(n+2)=N(n), N(2n)=0, N(3n)=N(n), and b+Z(b)=1 for b=N(n).
These are obtained before predecessor and injectivity, as in the baseline.

Prove the single equation M(R(n))=N(n) by equation induction.
The base is immediate. In the step retain the induction equation and
write b=N(n). If b=0, the R update moves by two successors. Therefore
M advances twice, 0 -> 2 -> 1, agreeing with N(Sn)=1. If b=1, the R
update moves by one successor, and M advances 1 -> 0, agreeing with
N(Sn)=0. This uses only the three displayed fixed computations of J.

Consequently

  R(Sn) = R(n)+1+Z(N(n)),
  R(n+2) = R(n)+3.

The second follows by applying the first twice and using
Z(b)+Z(Z(b))=1 at the Boolean b=N(n). Equation induction on n gives
R(2n)=3n. A separate equation induction gives

  R(3n)+N(n)=3R(n).

Its successor step needs no cancellation. Put b=N(n), use N(3n)=b,
and note R(3n+3)=R(3n)+4+Z(b). The new left side is
R(3n)+4+2Z(b). The new right side is 3R(n)+3+3Z(b).
At b=0, the induction equation is R(3n)=3R(n), and both sides add 6.
At b=1, the induction equation is R(3n)+1=3R(n); substitution turns
the new right side into R(3n)+4, exactly the new left side.

2. FACTOR MAP AND COMPARATOR

The preceding equations yield

  H(2u)=3u,  H(1)=1,  H(3u)=3H(u).

For the last, N(3u)=N(u). At parity zero the ramp equation with the
zero summand gives R(3u)=3R(u); at parity one both selectors retain
their numerical argument. This does not assume an even/odd decomposition
u=2v or u=2v+1 of an arbitrary number.

The inherited rotation and scaling proofs now transfer literally:

  I(Sy,a;H)=I(y,H(a);H),
  I(y,3a;H)=3I(y,a;H),
  I(y,1;H)=1.

Each is proved by equation induction with a and all other parameters fixed.
Therefore L has the required comparator equations at every argument:

  L(0,y)=1,
  L(Sx,0)=0,
  L(Sx,Sy)=L(x,y).

Its intended <= meaning motivates the term but is not used to prove the
following predecessor identities.

3. REVERSED-COMPARISON SATURATION

Define

  A_m(u)=C(L(m,u),S(u),u),
  W(t,m)=I(t,0;u.A_m(u)).

The comparator recurrences and the selector identity
C(e,Sa,Sb)=S(C(e,a,b)) give

  A_(Sm)(Su)=S(A_m(u)),
  A_(Sm)(0)=1.

Equation induction on t with m fixed then proves

  W(St,Sm)=S(W(t,m)).

The base is the second displayed A equation. The successor step applies
the first A equation to the induction equation at the same fixed m.
Equation induction on n now proves W(n,n)=n, using the already universally
proved shift law with t=m=n. This is substitution into an established theorem,
not changing the parameter of an active induction hypothesis.

A separate single-equation induction proves L(n,n)=1: the base is
L(0,0)=1 and the successor step is the comparator's diagonal recurrence.
It follows that

  W(Sn,n)=A_n(W(n,n))=A_n(n)=C(1,Sn,n)=n.

For P(0)=0 use the zero computation. For the successor equation define

  V(t,m)=I(t,0;u.C(L(Sm,Su),Su,u)).

The comparator recurrence gives its update pointwise equal to A_m(u).
An equation induction on t at fixed m proves V(t,m)=W(t,m), without an
extra primitive extensionality rule. Finally

  P(Sm)=V(Sm,m)=W(Sm,m)=m.

Thus the internal primitive equations of predecessor hold. In particular,
injectivity of S is a consequence afterward, not an assumption above.

4. INDEPENDENT RAW TREE COUNTS

Count one node per variable occurrence, zero, successor, or iterator,
count the three iterator children, and do not charge binder labels.
Expand every macro with no sharing and no normalization.

  Z(x): 5    N(x): 8    J(x): 10    M(x): 13
  R(x): 21   H(x): 31   E(x): 9     L(x,y): 49
  P(m): 57

In detail, L(m,Su) has 50 nodes. The outer P adds I, m, 0 (three nodes)
and the selector adds its I, Su and u (four nodes), giving 50+3+4=57.
The 58-node variant using L(SSu,m) has one additional successor occurrence.
The baseline lower-ramp predecessor has 75 nodes in the same convention,
so 57 is an 18-node or 24-percent reduction of this derived term.
This is not a lower bound or an optimality proof for all predecessor terms,
nor is it a reduction of the number of primitive axioms.

written internal derivation; finite evaluations do not establish validity
in nonstandard models, and no new Lean/Rocq formalization is claimed here.
