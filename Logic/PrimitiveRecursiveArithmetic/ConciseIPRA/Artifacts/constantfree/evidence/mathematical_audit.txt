Date: 2026-11-12
Reviewer role: math_breakthrough, independently of the author
Scope: 543-line mathematical section, including the 2-J/3-J local bounds.

Conclusion: no mathematical error found in the stated theorem or bounds.
This is a written mathematical review, not formal proof-assistant verification.
It does not establish a smaller complete proof checker.

1. Anchors and the constant terms

The theory is defined explicitly by CF0, CFstep, CFsep and CFEI for every
anchor a. Consequently using 0_a=Z(a) while proving Z constant is not
circular: these are the actual schemes of the new theory, and a separate
iPRA interpretation later establishes their satisfiability and strength.

Fixing a, K(0)=1 follows from CF0. At old counter 0, the increment branch
gives K(1)=2. The equation U(n)=1 is progressive because its next
instruction K(U(n)) is 2=S1 and therefore loads 1. One literal-equation
induction gives U(t)=1. For B(n)=J(n;i,r.r), the first instruction is
1=S0 and therefore B(1)=0. Hence Z(t)=B(U(t))=0_a. This proof uses no
separation, predecessor, equality reflection or general PR compiler.

2. Canonical zero and parameter discharge

Z is internally constant on the entire domain. The formula x=Z(x) is
inhabited by Z(a) for any arbitrary a and defines at most one element.
The explicit Param rule is sound in nonempty-domain first-order logic and
is necessary if the checker enforces a separate empty numerical scope.
Adding c=Z(c) is thus an ordinary conservative definitional extension.
No closed numerical term is falsely asserted to exist.

3. Body extensionality and syntax aliases

Literal-equation induction suffices to identify two J computations with
equal initial values and pointwise equal instructions. The forward graph
direction supplies a transition disjunct for the first actual output;
transport and the reverse graph direction give that same output for the
second. No successor injectivity is silently needed here.

For a completely strict first-order exposition, derive substitution
aliases by external induction on the substituted body syntax, using this
extensionality lemma at the outer J. Ordinary substitution into function
symbol arguments remains primitive first-order substitution. This is a
clarification of the written proof, not a missing arithmetic axiom.

4. Register bootstrap and primitive recursion

All displayed equations check algebraically:
q(0)=1, q(Sv)=0; W(0)=2, W(Sv)=v; D(0)=1, D(Sv)=Sv;
p(0)=0, p(Sv)=v. Coverage follows from CFstep at f(1,v), not from
induction or an unstated natural-number domain assumption. The G and C
selector constructions return the claimed arbitrary numerical branches.

The compiled primitive recursion uses only load instructions. At old
counter 0 it loads b; at old counter Sn it loads h(n,r). Its displayed
defining equations follow directly from CFstep and the proved selector
and predecessor equations. No unproved pairing theorem is used.

5. Actual equality reflection

The residual equation C(d(x,y),x,y+d(x,y))=x has a valid equation-induction
proof. In its nonzero successor case, coverage and p(Sv)=v identify
r=S(r'), which reduces the old invariant to y+r=x and establishes the
new branch. The implication d(Sa,b)=1 => a=b uses the already derived
successor injectivity. The detector e(a,b)=q(W(d(Sa,b))) is Boolean and
reflects actual equality. Therefore the Boolean formula translation
correctly recovers all contextual quantifier-free induction.

6. Both interpretations and their composites

J_h translates to a fixed primitive-recursive operation for each finite
body descriptor; no universal evaluator is asserted. Under this reading
U=1, Z=0, and J(n;0)=n+1, validating the anchored axioms and induction.

In the reverse direction, the unique definable zero allows the PR
compiler to be applied in the conservative extension with c. The wrapper
exists z (Zero(z) and A_z) is intuitionistically equivalent to the standard
compositional interpretation by definable graphs, including for implication
and universal quantification, because Zero is inhabited and unique.

Operation round trips follow by external induction on finite descriptors
and internal literal-equation induction. The numerical domains are whole,
equality remains actual equality, and x=y is the graph of each composite
isomorphism. No quotient equality, restricted cut, or merely standard-model
equivalence has replaced the claimed bi-interpretation.

7. The local size bounds

The 2-J bound for constant one and 3-J bound for constant zero are valid
for fully expanded terms under ordinary standard-number semantics. A
one-J term has both a valuation giving 1 and a valuation giving 0. In a
term with at most two J occurrences, the root counter is either a variable
or a one-J term, hence can be made zero; the root then returns 1. Thus it
cannot denote constant zero. U(x) and Z(x) attain the stated bounds.
These bounds concern raw numerical term occurrences only, not an
axiomatization metric or the repository's complete-checker frontier.

Optional observation: once the anchor theorem is established, the unary
operation S*(t)=J(t;i,r.Z(r)) is parameter-free and equals every S_a(t) by
body extensionality. This does not change the present axioms or size claim.

ADDENDUM: DEFINING S* FROM THE OUTSET

The parent proposed taking S*(t)=J(t;i,r.Z(r)) as the successor macro
in the original schemes, with z_a=Z(a), 1_a=S*(z_a), 2_a=S*(1_a).
I independently checked this revision: it is valid and noncircular.

Z is a raw finite term containing only J and variables; its definition
does not use successor. Thus S* is also a finite raw term. CF0 and the
revised graph give K(z_a)=1_a and K(1_a)=2_a. The equation U(n)=1_a
is progressive for S* because its next instruction is 2_a=S*(1_a),
which loads 1_a. Revised CFEI proves U(t)=1_a. B(1_a)=z_a follows
from the load branch at z_a, and hence Z(t)=z_a follows as before.

Body extensionality is then proved by equation induction using this
same successor macro. Since Z(r)=Z(a) for all r, it proves
J(t;i,r.Z(a))=J(t;i,r.Z(r))=S*(t). This recovers exactly the
initialized register-graph interface used in the arithmetic bootstrap.
The statement f(n,0)=Sn in that bootstrap is now justified by this
extensionality lemma, rather than being a literal syntactic definition.

For the iPRA interpretation, first prove U=1 and Z=0 directly from
the ordinary primitive-recursive defining equations of J using ordinary
successor and equation induction. Then S*=J(n;0)=n+1 follows. Only
after those steps are the revised anchored axioms verified; no use of
the target axioms is needed to establish their own validity.

Proof certificates must use the revised raw S* expansions consistently.
Old trees expanding S_a(t)=J(t;i,r.Z(a)) are extensionally equal but
not syntactically identical to S* and must not silently replace them.
