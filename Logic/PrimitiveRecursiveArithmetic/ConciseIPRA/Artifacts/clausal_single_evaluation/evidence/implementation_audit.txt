1. IMPLEMENTED CALCULUS

The selected acceptance module implements closed Eq/D theorems of the
zero-normal clausal calculus with five patterns: reflexivity, uniform
successor evaluation, clause use, paired elimination, and witness-selected
paired introduction. It has no direct root-equality branch, no zero-Eval
branch, no first-class Q formula, and no separate equality-only context
parameter. The arithmetic packet still supplies h as the iterator update
body; calling it entirely template-free would be imprecise.

The goal-oriented packet constructs the successor redex I(S(c),b;u.h) and
its canonical predecessor I(c,b;u.h) from c,b,h. The constructor shape is
therefore not separately matched after inference. This is a legitimate
certificate representation of the single inference scheme, and its code
is fully counted. The matched raw and zero controls use their natural
packets with an explicit comparison source and root/computation dispatch.

2. TYPING AND FRESHNESS

Every recursive prove call validates its goal before the reflexivity
shortcut. Eval validates c,b,h through the constructed minor equation and
t through the major equation. Booleans are excluded by exact int tests.
Noncanonical iterators are excluded recursively. Clause indices are exact
integers in range; negative indices cannot exploit Python list indexing.

Numerical witnesses at a use site contain no free positive bound indices.
Their free parameters are negative levels. This explains why insertion
under local binders needs no shifting of free variables. The opener still
increases its distinguished index beneath an iterator body and beneath D.
A witness may contain internally bound variables, which remain bound.

The fresh negative level -n-1 is available only in the branch. The DE goal
and DI witness are validated at scope n before scope is extended. Replaying
a proof under a larger context preserves the meanings of earlier negative
levels. No detected path permits the fresh witness to escape.

A well-typed reflexive goal closes regardless of the accompanying unused
proof payload. The same policy is used by both matched controls. Therefore
claim full validation of goals and every used rule input, not validation
of all irrelevant payload bytes. Acceptance does not rely on assert.

3. MATHEMATICAL TRANSLATION EVIDENCE

The supplied reproducibility program checks 66 source certificates with
the archived raw CE5 checker: 32 original examples, 32 distinct round-trip
certificates of those examples, and two explicit witness-collapse cases.
It translates them to the zero-normal forward UE calculus and verifies
the normalized conclusions. It then checks the selected goal-oriented
certificates and the matched zero-normal control, and replays translated
proofs in the independently supplied tagged Eq/Q/D E2 verifier.

The witness-collapse repair is necessary when a nonzero raw witness
normalizes to literal zero. The compiler uses the surviving alias
I(S(0),0;u.0), transports the old zero-instance assumption to witness zero,
translates the branch, transports its conclusion back, and applies case
introduction at the alias. One dedicated case consumes the old base,
so the context-replacement path is exercised rather than merely present.

The complete portable run reports 70 accepted examples, 20 rejected
malformed/scope examples, and 180 public process checks, half under
ordinary Python and half under python -O. An independent audit of the
same source found no soundness flaw and additionally exercised 17
malformed/type/scope cases plus valid UE and nested-binder opening.
These are executable checks of finite instances, not a formal verification
of all Python inputs or a new Lean/Rocq proof.
