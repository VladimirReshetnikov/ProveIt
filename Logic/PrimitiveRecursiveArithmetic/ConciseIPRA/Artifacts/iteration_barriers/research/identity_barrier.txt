IDENTITY AND NUMBERING LAWS DO NOT REPLACE EQUATION INDUCTION

Theorem (relative model construction).
Let T_std be intuitionistic first-order logic with equality, the scalar term
constructors 0, S, I(n,a;u.h), both iterator computation schemas, coverage
  x=0 or exists y (x=S y),
separation 0 != S 0, successor injectivity, and the induction rule for every
equation t(n,params)=kbar with k any fixed standard natural number, arbitrary
parameters, and arbitrary side assumptions not containing the eigenvariable.
Add the schemas
  I(n,a;u.u)=a,
  I(n,kbar;u.S u)=S^k(n)       (each fixed standard k).
The second schema includes the numbering/eta law I(n,0;u.S u)=n.
Then T_std still does not derive induction for all literal equations. The
countermodel's iterator depends on the actual update function, so this remains
true with full extensionality in that function. This initial theorem is a
nonderivability result in the stated language. The equivariant refinement in
automorphism_barriers.txt supplies the additional bi-interpretability obstruction.

Proof.
Take a nonstandard model M of PA expanded by the usual definitions of all
primitive recursive functions. Write St(M) for its external standard part.
Let
  D = (St(M) x {0}) union ((M minus St(M)) x {0,1}).
Projection pi:D->M forgets the label. Interpret 0 as (0,0), and define
S_D(m,i)=(S_M(m),i). Every standard numeral has one lift, every nonstandard
element two. Standardness and nonstandardness are preserved by successor;
all nonzero elements have predecessors of the same label. Thus actual equality,
successor injectivity, separation, and coverage all hold. Every nonstandard
successor chain in D has external order type Z. The projection is surjective.

Let H be the class of actual functions h:D->D for which some unary function
f:M->M, primitive recursive with parameters in M, satisfies pi o h = f o pi.
Such an f is unique. Iteration It_f is well defined independently of the chosen
primitive recursive description: PA proves that extensionally equal updates
give equal iterations, using induction in the base model M.

For h in H and initializer a in D, define I_D(k,a,h)=h^k(a) at a standard
counter k. For each nonstandard counter chain C=(n_j)_{j in Z}, with
n_{j+1}=S_D(n_j), let
  r_j = It_f(pi(n_j),pi(a)),   B_j=pi^{-1}(r_j).
The nonempty finite sets B_j have at most two members, and h maps B_j into
B_{j+1}. A compatible bi-infinite path b_j exists. Indeed every finite interval
has a compatible path by choosing an element at its left endpoint and pushing
forward; compactness of the product of the finite B_j, or the finite inverse
limit theorem, supplies a path on all of Z. Define I_D(n_j,a,h)=b_j.

Make the following prescriptions before choosing the remaining paths.

1. If h=id_D, choose b_j=a, for every initializer and every nonstandard
   counter chain. Its projection is correct because It_id(m,pi(a))=pi(a).
   This validates every identity-iterator instance.

2. If h=S_D and a=kbar_D is a standard numeral, choose
     b_j=S_D^k(n_j).
   Its projection is It_S(pi(n_j),kbar)=pi(n_j)+k, and S_D(b_j)=b_{j+1}.
   This validates all standard-seed equations, including eta at k=0.

3. Fix a nonstandard b in M and set a=(b,0). For h=S_D, prescribe at seed a
   the path
     b_j=(pi(n_j)+b,0),
   and at seed S_D(a) the path
     b_j=(pi(n_j)+S_M(b),1).
   These first coordinates are nonstandard, so both labels exist. Each path
   is compatible because S_D preserves labels. The two seeds are distinct,
   are both nonstandard, and therefore are not covered by prescription 2.
   Also S_D is distinct from id_D. Consequently none of the prescriptions
   conflicts with another.

Choose arbitrary compatible paths for every remaining triple (h,a,C).
For all n,a,h the resulting operation satisfies
  I_D(0,a,h)=a,
  I_D(S_D n,a,h)=h(I_D(n,a,h)),
  pi(I_D(n,a,h))=It_f(pi(n),pi(a)).
No cycle compatibility remains to be checked: successor has no cycles in M
or D; different standard and nonstandard successor chains never meet.

Interpret nested scalar terms recursively, giving a body its actual function
on D before applying I_D. Induction on terms proves
  pi([[t]]_D,rho)=[[t]]_M,pi(rho).
The interpretation on the right is a parameterized primitive recursive function,
so every body encountered belongs to H. Since I_D was chosen on actual
functions, two extensionally equal bodies receive exactly the same iterator
value. This also handles descriptions of id_D and S_D other than u and S u.
There is no circular choice on syntax and no failure of substitution.

For any fixed standard k and any valuation,
  [[t]]_D=kbar_D iff pi([[t]]_D)=kbar_M,
because the fiber over kbar_M is a singleton. If in D the base and the universal
step of an induction instance t(n)=kbar hold, surjectivity of pi and the displayed
projection identity transfer them to the corresponding primitive recursive
predicate in M. Induction in M gives the conclusion for every first coordinate,
and the singleton fiber reflects it back to D. For a rule under arbitrary side
assumptions, fix a valuation satisfying those assumptions; the same reasoning
applies, without projecting the assumptions themselves. Thus all numeral-equation
induction rules are valid.

Finally take
  E(n) : I(n,S a;u.S u) = S(I(n,a;u.S u)).
The base E(0) reduces on both sides to S a. The two successor computation laws
and congruence prove E(n)->E(S n), without induction. For a nonstandard counter
n, however, prescription 3 gives label 1 on the left and label 0 on the right,
although the first coordinates agree. Hence E(n) fails. The universal equation-
induction rule therefore cannot be derived in T_std. QED.

Relation to the repository.
This strengthens the two-fiber countermodel in ConciseIPRA chapter 9,
theorem ctr:thm:zeroind, by satisfying identity iteration and all standard-seed
numbering equations. It answers negatively the literal proposal that the identity
iterator law, even together with eta, repairs numeral-equation induction. It does
not settle every proposed uniqueness or extensionality scheme. By itself the
nonderivability argument does not exclude interpretations on a different domain.
The separate note automorphism_barriers.txt upgrades an equivariant version of
this model to a parameter-free non-bi-interpretability theorem; it does not
exclude a one-way interpretation or arbitrary parameterized ones.

compatibility of the prescribed paths and the preserved induction argument.
