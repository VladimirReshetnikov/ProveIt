MIRRORED GUARDED CHECKERS AND EXECUTABLE EXAMPLES

Run with CPython 3.12.14 for the pinned measurements:

    python3 reproduce.py

No third-party Python packages are required. The script regenerates the
named proof certificates, runs the standalone checkers in ordinary and -O
mode, audits the named derivations, and writes evidence/metrics.json and
evidence/validation.json. Subprocess exit status zero together with exactly
ACCEPT followed by a newline is acceptance. An exception is rejection.

TRUSTED PROGRAMS

Each code/checker_*.py file is a complete, separate proof checker importing
only Python's JSON parser. checker_tokens.py uses a global fresh-name supply;
checker_ast.py and checker_bytes.py use lexical local scopes. Certificates
for the two supply conventions are not interchangeable. readable_checker.py
is an independent standalone expansion of the local-scope program.

strict_checker.py is the complete local byte checker plus exact public-goal
syntax validation. It rejects administrative zero aliases in dummy slots,
unallocated names, Q-root public goals, and atomic D matrices not in the
mirrored shape. Its entire source is measured. It is larger than the derived
baseline checker equipped with the same general Eq/D and term validation;
the report does not claim a reduction in the size of strict goal validation.

No checker imports a proof producer, normalizer, theorem database, or another
checker. The files under tests/ and reference/ and reproduce.py are untrusted.

PUBLIC SYNTAX AND PROOF RESOURCES

The numerical constructors are 0, successor, and scalar iteration I.
Object formulas are equations and D_{x,y}B. When B is a literal equation it
must be a = I(y,a;u.h), with literal repeated a and arbitrary update h; a
may itself depend on x and y. Compound D matrices use the case rule, and
atomic matrices use progress. There is no certificate mode switch selecting
the two rules. Contexts additionally contain Q_yB closure resources generated
by D elimination. The report specifies their ordinary anchored reading and
the sound interpretation of administrative raw aliases.

The raw codec is the inherited arity representation: unary arrays are D in
formula position and successor in term position, binary formulas are
equations, and ternary clauses encode Q by repeating the body. The depth
increment is 5 minus array length. A protected numerical binder occupies two
integer coordinates; root coordinates 0,1,2 denote zero semantically. Public
canonical goals use 0 and odd coordinates of actual binders only. Dummy
slots inserted below successor or iterator counter/initializer contain no
canonical variable. The strict mirrored guard check compares its initializer
to the left term with one outer protected slot inserted, including beneath
nested term binders.

EXAMPLES AND LIMITS

The generated positive proofs cover both computations, a zero initializer,
reflexivity, mirrored DI and DE, a nonvacuous witness, compound case
introduction, implication elimination, derived equation induction, the
contextual zero-source bootstrap, and a derived literal-zero transport.
Every positive named derivation is expanded through actual computation
normalization and audited: there are no primitive transports from literal
zero, and every atomic D subformula in every goal or transport motive has
the mirrored guard. Q resources are permitted in this audit.

The contextual bootstrap proves the same-left implication (0=1) => (0=2)
using the variable-left invariant n=I(n,1;u.Su). The normalized zero-transport
example contains 507 instructions and uses 13 mirrored atomic introductions
and 9 compound case introductions. These are worked derivations, not a
claim that finite testing proves the general interpretation theorem.

tests/normalizer.py is an inherited untrusted construction library. The
mirrored_cases.py module replaces its equation induction and numerical case
derivations by the new canonical constructions. Only equation-valued
transport templates are exercised. Its frozen general Q/D transport
branches are not advertised as a complete executable normalizer for the new
calculus; the report's general theorem is mathematical.

Two controls have deliberately different results by profile. A former
case/progress-marker negative control is now accepted by the compact cores:
its final Q_y(I(y,0;u.u)=0) is true, and the matrix root selects induction
regardless of its old mode marker. The strict wrapper rejects this Q-root
public goal. Similarly, a valid computation using a dummy-slot zero alias
is accepted by the cores but rejected as noncanonical by strict_checker.py.
These distinctions are explicit in evidence/validation.json.

COMPARISON PROVENANCE

The frozen checker sources and auxiliary producers come from
VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt at commit
1517d9d79ce33f631e0f82960c849427cbba23bf, under
Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/Research/.
The new programs combine source_dispatch's source-selected elimination
with guarded_core's root-selected introduction. The report proves their
compatibility for the new mirrored object language; the numeric source
changes alone do not establish that theorem.

source_strict_derived.py is a comparison derivative, not an upstream file:
it adds the same canonical term and Eq/D validator to source_dispatch's
byte program, without the new mirrored-matrix restriction.

Measurements remove actual module/function/class docstrings and comments,
then apply ast.unparse and one final newline. The outputs include
significant Python tokens, AST nodes, normalized UTF-8 bytes, raw bytes,
SHA-256, and gzip level-9 sizes with mtime=0. gzip is information only.
