INDEPENDENT REVIEW: RAW FRESH ALIASES AND BACKWARD VARIABLE SUBSTITUTION
Pinned comparison: 36d37fc1071fe289371f6bcd7dcfa0eac593a5a6

STATUS
The proposed two-rule equality interface is sound in ordinary intuitionistic
first-order equality and recovers reflexivity, symmetry and arbitrary formula
transport without computation, normalization, induction or cut. The derivations
below use Back only at the newest active numerical variable. Alias must be
counted as an additional primitive structural/equality rule. This does not prove
that variable-only substitution in the unchanged raw calculus suffices.

RULES
Alias: from Gamma, b=t |- H infer Gamma |- H[t/b], with b fresh for Gamma,t.
Back:  from Gamma |- b=t and Gamma |- H[t/b] infer Gamma |- H.
All substitutions are simultaneous, capture avoiding, and act on free occurrences.
Back may be restricted to the newest active numerical parameter b.

DERIVATIONS
1. Refl(t): introduce fresh b=t, use that hypothesis as H, then Alias exports
   t=t. No computation axiom is used.
2. Symmetry: given s=t, introduce fresh b=s. Back at b=s with H=(b=t)
   gives b=t. Back at b=t with H=(t=b), using Refl(t), gives t=b.
   Alias at b=s exports t=s.
3. Transport: given s=t and F(s), first use (2) to obtain t=s. Introduce
   fresh c=t. Back at c=t with H=(c=s) gives c=s. Back at c=s with H=F(c),
   using F(s), gives F(c). Alias at c=t exports F(t).
Every introduced alias is chosen fresh for the context, input terms and template.
Consequently all displayed substitutions are literal identities of raw syntax.
The Back source is the currently innermost alias throughout each branch.

SOUNDNESS
Back is ordinary equality substitution used in the reverse direction. Alias
is validated by substituting t for its fresh b throughout the branch derivation,
then discharging the reflexive hypothesis t=t. This is soundness into the
ordinary reference theory, so using that theory's substitution metatheorem here
does not presuppose substitution in the new calculus. For the reverse translation,
replace each primitive reference transport by (3). All other rules are copied.
General substitution, weakening or cut of the new calculus can then be obtained
from the equivalence, or proved by a generalized renaming/parameter compiler;
they must not be silently assumed in a restricted newest-variable presentation.

FRESHNESS NEGATIVES FOR IMPLEMENTATION TESTS
- If b may already occur in Gamma, let Gamma={b=S0}, t=0 and let the Alias
  branch conclude H=(b=S0) by hypothesis. Incorrect discharge would yield
  Gamma |- 0=S0, despite Gamma being consistent in the ordinary natural numbers.
- If b is allowed in t, take t=S(b), H=(b=S(b)), and the Alias hypothesis itself.
  Incorrect discharge yields S(b)=S(S(b)), false in the ordinary natural numbers.
Validating t and Gamma in the outer numerical scope, and H in the scope with
one new final parameter, enforces both requirements structurally.

NOVELTY COMPARISON
Current Article, chapters/ch06_disch.tex, subsection "Substitution for a parameter
on raw terms" (label disch:sec:param), uses Param: variable substitution combined
with joining at a common left side for nonvariable sources. Its Fresh aliases
lemma (disch:lem:alias) discharges b=a only when b is absent from the exported
formula; it derives that operation using computation, root joining and logic.
The new primitive Alias instead exports H[t/b] for arbitrary H.
The same chapter's VSub theorem (disch:thm:vsub-transport) assumes full normal
forms and recovers transport by a naming packet with literal normalized rows.
The proposed raw interface makes no such normalization assumption.
No earlier literal Alias/Back rule pair was found in the current TeX corpus.
This is repository-relative novelty, not a literature-wide priority claim.
