## A precise obstruction to successor-only transport

This statement was checked against the article's references to
"successor-headed" terms. The passages in `ch03_guard.tex` around lines
1101, 1279, and 1465 concern *iterator-headed or successor-headed* terms and
the removal of literal-zero sources, not a restriction to successors alone.
The subsection "Successor sources constructed from predecessors" in
`ch10_obs.tex` concerns the counter of a computation packet, not a
successor-only equality rule. I did not find this particular reading in
those sections. This is a local novelty check, not a claim that no equivalent
observation occurs anywhere in the full corpus or literature.

An apparent code simplification would encode the computation counter directly
as zero or a successor term, avoiding the present conditional wrapping. Its
eager leaf construction is safe only if nonzero transport sources are also
successor-headed. That restriction is incomplete with the inherited oriented
computation axioms.

Consider the rule inventory with hypotheses, the two equations

    I(0,a;u.h)=a
    I(S(n),a;u.h)=h[I(n,a;u.h)/u],

the native logical rules, and transport allowed only when its major source
is literally `S(t)`. Use the following reading over the standard natural
numbers:

- zero and successor have their usual meanings;
- every iterator expression has value zero;
- the atomic formula `s=t` is true iff the value of `s` is zero;
- closures and paired binders have the same ordinary/progressive readings
  used in the source calculus.

Both computation axioms are true because their left side is an iterator.
Every allowed transport major is false, so transport is sound vacuously.
Hypotheses and the binder introduction/elimination rules are sound by their
usual semantic arguments. Cases and equation progress are sound because the
numerical domain is the standard natural numbers; their soundness does not
require the atomic relation to be actual equality.

However, `S(0)=S(0)` is false in this reading. Thus the restricted calculus
does not even derive that reflexivity instance. Any viable constructor
restriction must change the computation axioms, the equality representation,
or another part of the calculus as well. It cannot inherit the usual bridge
merely by asserting that every term has a successor-coded name.

### Exact theorem and contextual soundness proof

Let `N_S` be the eight-rule native calculus of the article, except that every
instance of transport has source syntactically `S(r)`. The conclusion holds
for both the original ordinary transport rule

    s=t, F(s) / F(t)

and the one-term crossed form

    s=t, h(s)=u / u=h(t).

In both cases reflexivity is **not** an additional primitive axiom. Then
`N_S` is strictly incomplete for the open native consequences of `NPrf`:
indeed `NPrf` proves `S(0)=S(0)` in the empty context whereas `N_S` does not.
The same argument works with witnessed progress in place of direct closure.

For a valuation `v` of the free variables, let term evaluation be the usual
evaluation of variables, zero, and successor, with every iterator assigned
zero regardless of its arguments. The evaluation/substitution lemma follows
by structural induction; the iterator case is constant on both sides.
Interpret an equation `s=t` by `eval_v(s)=0`; interpret `Q_y B` as
`B(0) -> forall y B(y)`, and `D_{x,y} B` as
`exists x (B(x,0) -> forall y B(x,y))`. These are meta-level Boolean truth
conditions over the standard natural numbers. This is a proof-system reading,
not an ordinary model with actual equality.

Prove by induction on derivations that if all assumptions of `Gamma` are
true at `v`, every derivable conclusion is true at `v`.

1. Hypothesis is immediate. Each computation axiom is true because its left
   side is an iterator and hence evaluates to zero.
2. A transport major with source `S(r)` would have to assert
   `eval_v(r)+1=0`. This is false for every valuation. Therefore the soundness
   implication is vacuous, independently of its minor premise, template,
   and whether the conclusion is crossed.
3. Closure elimination is the direct use of implication and universal
   instantiation in the stated reading.
4. Paired-binder elimination chooses a witness for its existential premise.
   Extend `v` by this witness at the fresh variable. The old assumptions and
   the conclusion retain their truth values by freshness; apply the induction
   hypothesis to the branch with the opened closure assumption.
5. For introduction by cases, fix the value of the supplied witness term.
   Assuming the zero instance, the zero row is already true. Every other
   natural number is `S(n)`; apply the induction hypothesis to the premise
   with its fresh index variable assigned `n`. Thus all rows are true, giving
   the conditional closure and then its existential witness.
6. For direct equation progress, assume the zero instance. Freshness makes
   the premise yield `E(n) -> E(n+1)` for every standard natural number,
   with `Gamma` unchanged. External induction on the standard `n` proves
   every row. Thus the conditional closure is true. The witnessed-progress
   variant adds the value of its supplied witness term to this argument.

This proves contextual soundness. The sentence `S(0)=S(0)` is false because
its left side evaluates to one. In the unrestricted native calculus it is
derived by transporting the computation `I(0,S(0);u.0)=S(0)` along itself,
using the equation template `F(v) := (v=S(0))`. Hence the asserted strict
incompleteness follows.

### Limit of this obstruction

This theorem rules out deletion of equality sources while preserving the
native theorem set, including the allowed top-level public equation
`S(0)=S(0)`. It does **not** prove that no different guarded public language,
no different interpretation of equality, or no different interpreted domain
can be bi-interpretable with iPRA. For example, iterator-headed gated atoms
all happen to be true in this reading, so it does not refute completeness
on a fully gated translation image. It must not be advertised as a universal
barrier to successor-only presentations.

## Other cautions

- Replacing `3 % len(a)` by `len(a)-1` is unsafe: auxiliary closure matrices
  of arity three must select case introduction, not progressive introduction.
  Public formulas omit `Q`, but arbitrary certificates may introduce it as
  an auxiliary matrix.
- A context-length scope bound has two phases (entry and the fresh parameter
  introduced by a continuation). Collapsing these bounds loses eigenvariable
  freshness. The article already documents counterexamples to that shortcut.
- Fold fusion or dinaturality is an interesting mathematical direction, but
  no proof was obtained that a smaller such principle recovers contextual
  equation induction. It should remain a research question.
