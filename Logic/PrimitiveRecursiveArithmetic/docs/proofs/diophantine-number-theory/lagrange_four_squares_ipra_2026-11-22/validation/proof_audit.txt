FOUR SQUARES IN iPRA — INDEPENDENT MATHEMATICAL AND LOGICAL AUDIT
================================================================

Scope and conclusion
--------------------
The prime-seed / parity-first descent / bounded factor-loop construction is
sound. It proves the witnessed form

  iPRA |- forall n, S0(n)^2 + S1(n)^2 + S2(n)^2 + S3(n)^2 = n,

for explicitly primitive recursive natural-valued S0,...,S3. Universal closure
is taken only after quantifier-free induction with parameters. No induction on
an existential representation predicate, no least-counterexample argument, no
unbounded minimization, no choice, and no non-finite excluded middle is needed.

1. Ambient arithmetic and primitive recursive conventions
---------------------------------------------------------
Use the definitional presentation of iPRA with a function symbol and defining
equations for each primitive recursive function, intuitionistic first-order
logic, and induction for quantifier-free formulas. Natural equality, truncated
subtraction, order, parity, quotient/remainder, finite sums/products, bounded
search, and finite tuple encodings are primitive recursive. Define division by
zero as 0 so all functions are total; the proof only uses positive divisors.

Signed integers are a notational convenience for primitive recursive signed
codes over N. One may use sign-and-magnitude or normalized difference pairs;
addition, multiplication, absolute value, remainder, and exact division all
have explicit primitive recursive implementations. Tuples may be encoded by
any primitive recursive pairing with primitive recursive projections. There
is no integer or finite-sequence quantifier outside the natural-number sort.

A bounded formula used below has a primitive recursive characteristic bit.
Finite conjunction is a product of bits, finite disjunction a recursively
accumulated Boolean OR, and bounded existential witnesses are obtained by a
scan with a hit flag and stored first witness. Standard elimination and
introduction facts for these folds have quantifier-free induction proofs.
Thus a bounded universal invariant is represented by the equality of its
primitive recursive characteristic bit to 1. This does not authorize
induction on arbitrary quantified formulas: it is specific to the explicit
bounded formulas appearing here.

2. The bounded elementary number theory needed for the seed
----------------------------------------------------------
Prime(p) is the primitive recursive condition p>=2 and that no d with
2<=d<p divides p. Divisibility is a remainder-zero test, with its usual
positive-divisor quotient witness. For any q>=2 the bounded search for the
least d in [2,q] dividing q succeeds because q itself does. Its result p is
prime: if 2<=e<p and e|p, then e|q, contradicting the recorded failure of all
earlier candidates. This needs no unique factorization theorem.

Euclid's prime-divisor lemma can be proved by a fixed-length extended Euclidean
algorithm. Given prime p and 0<a<p, initialize (r0,r1)=(p,a), together with
coefficient pairs expressing r0 and r1 as integer combinations of p and a.
If r1>0, perform the usual quotient/remainder update

  (r0,r1) -> (r1, r0 - floor(r0/r1)*r1),

and update coefficients by the same integer linear operation. If r1=0, freeze.
Every active step strictly decreases r1, so p+1 steps suffice. The invariant
that every d<=p divides both current remainders iff it divides p and a is a
finite conjunction of decidable divisibility equivalences. Each equivalence
is immediate from r0=q*r1+r2. The coefficient identities are quantifier-free.
At the end r0=g>0, r1=0, g|p and g|a. Primality and 0<a<p give g=1. Hence
u*p+v*a=1 for computed signed u,v. If p|a*b, multiply this identity by b to
obtain p|b. Reducing a modulo p gives, for arbitrary integers a,b,

  p | a*b  ->  p|a or p|b.

The disjunction is a decidable split on a mod p. The remainder decreases
within a known natural bound; extended coefficients are obtained by primitive
recursion. No coefficient magnitude bound is required for primitive
recursiveness, but (p+1)^(p+2) is a harmless coarse bound for p+1 updates.

3. Constructive seed for an odd prime
------------------------------------
Let p=2h+1 be odd prime, h>=1. For i,j in {0,...,h}, form the two lists

  A_i = i^2 mod p,
  B_j = (-1-j^2) mod p.

Both lists are injective. Indeed A_i=A_j implies p|(i-j)(i+j), hence p|i-j
or p|i+j. Since |i-j|<=h<p, the first case gives i=j. Since 0<=i+j<=2h=p-1,
the second gives i+j=0, again i=j=0. B-list injectivity is the same argument.

Scan all (h+1)^2 pairs for A_i=B_j. This is a total bounded scan returning a
hit bit and first pair. If the bit were 0, injectivity of each list plus no
cross-list collision would make their concatenation L a list of p+1 distinct
residues, all in {0,...,p-1}. Put

  C(r) = sum_(k=0)^p [L_k=r].

Pairwise distinctness gives C(r)<=1 for each r<p. Counting the same finite
incidences in the two orders gives

  p+1 = sum_(k=0)^p sum_(r=0)^(p-1) [L_k=r]
      = sum_(r=0)^(p-1) C(r) <= p,

a contradiction. Finite-sum rearrangement is proved by distributivity and
quantifier-free induction, not by an infinite or set-theoretic counting
principle. Splitting on the computed bit leaves bit=1, so the stored pair
(a,b) is an actual witness. This is not double-negation elimination for an
arbitrary proposition; it is elimination of one impossible case of a known
0-or-1 natural number.

Set x=(a,b,1,0) and m=(a^2+b^2+1)/p. Divisibility was just established. The
numerator is positive, hence m>=1. Also

  a^2+b^2+1 <= 2h^2+1 <= 2h^2+h = hp,

because h>=1. Consequently 1<=m<=h<p and N(x)=mp. This sharper upper bound
is convenient, though m<p is all descent needs. Handle p=2 by (1,1,0,0).

4. Euler product with conjugation: signs and divisibility
--------------------------------------------------------
For x=(a,b,c,d) and r=(r,s,t,u), put

  z0 =  a*r + b*s + c*t + d*u,
  z1 = -a*s + b*r - c*u + d*t,
  z2 = -a*t + b*u + c*r - d*s,
  z3 = -a*u - b*t + c*s + d*r.

These are the components of x times the conjugate of r. Direct polynomial
expansion and cancellation proves

  N(z) = N(x) N(r), where N(a,b,c,d)=a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2.

If x_i congruent r_i mod m, then modulo m, z0 is N(r) and the other three
components vanish by pairwise cancellation. Thus if m|N(r), every z_i is
divisible by m. The quotient y=z/m then satisfies

  m^2 N(y) = N(x) N(r).

With N(x)=mp and N(r)=mk, cancellation of positive m^2 yields N(y)=pk.
All cancellations occur in coded integer/natural arithmetic and are
constructively valid because m>=1.

5. Descent: treat even m before taking centered residues
-------------------------------------------------------
Maintain 1<=m<p and N(x)=mp for a prime p. If m=1, freeze.

EVEN CASE. If m>1 is even, N(x) is even. Since z^2 and z have the same
parity, the number of odd coordinates is even. Pair the four coordinates
into same-parity pairs. An explicit three-way algorithm is: pair a with b
if their parity agrees; otherwise pair a with c if their parity agrees;
otherwise pair a with d. The last pair has the needed parity, because if
a differed in parity from all three others the total parity would be odd.
The remaining pair automatically has the same parity. Write the reordered
tuple as (a,b,c,d). Define

  y = ((a+b)/2, (a-b)/2, (c+d)/2, (c-d)/2).

These four integer quotients are exact. Expansion gives

  N(y)=N(x)/2=(m/2)p.

Set m'=m/2. Then 1<=m'<m<p. In particular, the troublesome even-modulus
equality case of a centered-residue proof never arises.

ODD CASE. If m>1 is odd, choose each centered residue r_i congruent x_i mod m
with |r_i|<m/2, equivalently |r_i|<=(m-1)/2. Define it explicitly from
t_i=x_i mod m in [0,m-1]: take r_i=t_i when t_i<=(m-1)/2 and r_i=t_i-m
otherwise. Since N(r) congruent N(x)=0 mod m, write N(r)=mk with computed
natural k. The size bound gives

  mk=N(r)<=4*((m-1)/2)^2=(m-1)^2<m^2,

hence k<m. If k=0 then N(r)=0, so each r_i=0, and every x_i is divisible by
m. Writing x=m*w gives m^2 N(w)=mp and therefore m N(w)=p. This makes m a
proper divisor of p, impossible because 1<m<p. Decidability of k=0 therefore
gives k>=1. Apply the product of Section 4 and divide all four components by
m. Its norm is pk, so (m',x')=(k,z/m) has 1<=m'<m<p.

The exact order of cases is essential: a naive centered-residue estimate for
even m gives only k<=m and needs additional work. The parity branch above
removes that issue completely.

6. Fixed primitive recursive descent and its exact invariant
------------------------------------------------------------
For p prime initialize the seed state (m0,x0). Define a total step function
by the three branches above, with harmless arbitrary defaults on malformed
inputs (all quotients/remainders are total). Iterate it exactly p times by
ordinary primitive recursion on the loop counter. For prime p the invariant

  1<=m_t<p,
  N(x_t)=m_t p,
  m_t<=max(1, m0 dotminus t)

is quantifier-free in the primitive recursive function language. All needed
facts about primality are carried by the parameter condition PrimeFlag(p)=1.
Prove the invariant by quantifier-free induction on t. If m_t=1 the state
freezes. If m_t>1 the step just proved has m_(t+1)<=m_t-1, establishing the
rank bound. Since m0<p, at t=p the bound forces m_p=1. Taking absolute
values of x_p gives a natural four-square representation P(p) of p.

Alternative rank invariant, if convenient for mechanization:

  (m_t=1) or (m_t+t<=m0),

together with positivity and norm identity. The first branch is stable;
the second strictly decreases m at each active step. It yields the same
terminal conclusion without arithmetic involving max and truncated minus.

Intermediate register bounds are elementary. Since N(x)=mp<p^2, every
|x_i|<p. In the odd branch |r_i|<p and every raw product coordinate has
absolute value <4p^2. The final quotient coordinates again have absolute
value <p by their norm. In the even branch raw sums/differences have
absolute value <2p. These bounds can be recorded if finite array/register
encodings require explicit natural bounds; no unbounded computation is
hidden in the tuple notation.

7. All natural numbers: no appeal to unique factorization
---------------------------------------------------------
For n=0 return (0,0,0,0). For n>=1 initialize remainder q0=n and tuple
v0=(1,0,0,0), so q0*N(v0)=n. At each of exactly n stages:

  - if q=1, freeze;
  - if q>1, find the least p in [2,q] dividing q;
  - compute the prime representation P(p) by the bounded construction above;
  - set q'=q/p and v'=v*P(p) by the ordinary Euler/quaternion product.

The bounded least-divisor lemma makes p prime. Because p>=2 and q>1,
1<=q'<q. Euler's identity preserves q'*N(v')=q*N(v)=n. Quantifier-free
induction on t proves

  1<=q_t<=n,
  q_t*N(v_t)=n,
  q_t<=max(1, n dotminus t).

After n steps q_n=1. Thus N(v_n)=n, and coordinatewise absolute values give
the promised S_i(n). This construction uses no infinitude of primes, no
factorization uniqueness, no induction on a formula asserting existence of
a representation, and no well-founded recursion whose primitive recursive
status would need separate justification.

If an implementation uses the conjugating Euler product in every outer
step, its norm identity remains valid and the same proof works; ordinary
quaternion multiplication is not essential.

8. Formalization risk checklist and resolutions
----------------------------------------------
(a) A Lean proof in Nat/Int of an existential four-square theorem is not by
itself a proof in iPRA. Keep the constructive program and QF induction audit
explicit; distinguish external Lean arithmetic checking from an internal
iPRA derivation or compiler correctness theorem.

(b) Avoid importing Mathlib's four-square theorem to justify the algorithm.
It may serve as an external comparison only. The proof above is independent.

(c) The modular seed requires Euclid's lemma or a substitute. It is not
enough merely to assert distinct square residues; include the bounded
Euclidean argument or a previously established iPRA lemma.

(d) Claiming "bounded induction" without Booleanization is potentially
ambiguous. State the specific characteristic functions or the general
finite-formula compilation lemma and its QF induction proof.

(e) Do not make the seed search partial or leave its failure branch undefined.
Return a flag and default tuple. Correctness establishes that the failure
branch is unreachable on valid prime inputs.

(f) Primitive recursive descent should be a fixed-count iteration, not a
while-loop justified solely by an informal statement that m decreases.

(g) A prime factorization function with ordinary recursive calls at n/p
can be primitive recursive after a course-of-values coding, but the fixed
n-stage factor loop avoids requiring that separate explanation.

(h) No claim of feasible or polynomial-in-bitlength complexity is justified
by these broad numeric loop bounds. Primitive recursiveness is the intended
resource guarantee.

(i) The final natural coordinates satisfy S_i(n)<=n, since S_i(n)^2<=n and
a<=a^2 for every natural a (separate a=0 from a>=1). Thus the existential
corollary may explicitly bound all four witnesses by n.

Independent verdict: the plan is complete at the human proof level, provided
the report includes the elementary arithmetic/finite-counting support above
and labels any unimplemented Lean obligations honestly.
