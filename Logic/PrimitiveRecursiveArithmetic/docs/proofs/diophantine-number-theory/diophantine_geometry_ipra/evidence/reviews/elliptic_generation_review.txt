A. Local congruence certificates
A primitive integral vector reduces to a unimodular residue vector:
a common divisor of m and all residues divides all original coordinates.
This works for composite m and is stronger than merely requiring a nonzero
residue vector. Polynomial congruence preservation is finite arithmetic.
The mod-9 obstruction for X²+Y²=3Z² is correct: mod 3 first forces X,Y
to be multiples of 3; then 3Z²=0 mod 9 forces Z a multiple of 3. The
stated two nonzero mod-3 vectors are exactly (0,0,1),(0,0,2).
No local-to-global converse is assumed.

B. Smoothness condition
For the homogeneous cubic Y²Z-X³-AXZ²-BZ³, the Z derivative at [0:1:0]
is 1. In the affine chart, singularity gives y=0, A=-3x² and B=2x³,
so 4A³+27B²=0. The stated nonzero condition therefore suffices for
nonsingularity. The denominator and enumeration theorems correctly
remain valid without the nonsingularity hypothesis.

C. Square/cube denominators
With x=a/q reduced and q>0, T=a³+Aaq²+Bq³ satisfies gcd(T,q)=1
because T=a³ modulo q and gcd(a,q)=1. Consequently T/q³ is reduced.
For reduced y=c/e, c²/e² is also reduced. Cross multiplication plus
coprimality gives e²=q³ and c²=T. This conclusion is valid also for
y=0: a zero reduced numerator forces denominator 1, hence q=e=1.
The positive-integer factorization lemma then proves q=d²,e=d³
from 2 v_p(e)=3 v_p(q): every exponent in q is even and the exponent
in e is three times its half. All factorization inputs are positive,
all loops are bounded, and the code's d=isqrt(q) is justified by the
proved perfect-square property. Taking b=c preserves the sign of y.
Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of the reduced denominator of x
and the positive square root d; it is not merely uniqueness up to sign.

D. Bounded elliptic search
H(x)=max(|a|,d²) because gcd(a,d)=1. Thus the loops
1<=d<=floor sqrt(T), -T<=a<=T are complete. A square root b0 of W
must be coprime to d: any prime common to them divides a³ modulo p,
then divides a, contradicting gcd(a,d)=1. This also handles b0=0,
which forces d=1. Different (a,d) give different reduced x coordinates,
and the only sign duplicate is b0=0, explicitly handled.
The upper bound W<=(1+|A|+|B|)T³ and root bound CT² for T>=1,C>=1
are correct. The stated output count is an upper bound even after
filters and zero-sign deduplication. The only point at infinity is O.

E. Finite generation
The descent rank r(Q)=h(Q) dotminus T is nonnegative. In every active
step h(Q)>T and h(D(Q))<h(Q), so r decreases by at least 1; once it is
zero the stopped state remains fixed. Therefore exactly
h(P) dotminus T stages suffice, including the zero-fuel case. The
invariant P=m^k Q+sum_i a_i r_i is preserved when the OLD state's
index i(Q) receives the increment m^k and then k increases. Final Q
has height <=T and is found in the supplied S by bounded equality
search. Coefficients are indeed nonnegative, and their stated bound
sum_{j<=h(P)}m^j also bounds the coefficient m^k of the terminal
small point. D, i and all group operations are fixed primitive
recursive functions, so their numerically fuelled iteration is
primitive recursive even if code lengths grow. The argument does
not choose preimages from an unspecified quotient.
The sufficient inequality 2h(D(P))<=h(P)+T correctly implies strict
decrease when h(P)>T.

Requested precise clarifications in the theorem/pseudocode:
1. Change 'finite lists R=..., S=...' to 'finite lists OF VALID GROUP
   ELEMENTS'. This matters formally: group identities can only be
   applied to valid r_i, and all output generators must lie in the
   represented group. Completeness of S alone does not explicitly
   assert validity of every element of R. The intended meaning is
   clear, but it should be a written hypothesis.
2. In an active step first save j=i(Q) and Q'=D(Q) from the old Q;
   then update a_j by m^k, replace Q by Q', and increase k. Current
   prose can be read as replacing Q before computing i(Q), which
   would use the wrong representative. The displayed invariant
   unambiguously uses the old index, so this is a wording fix.
Optional precision: item 2's selector condition should say 'for a
VALID P with h(P)>T', since validity is the actual induction invariant.

F. Foundational strength
Section 01's trial-division infrastructure is sufficient for the
square/cube exponent argument. Prime-divisor existence is a bounded
least-divisor calculation; exponent counts and product uniqueness
are bounded finite statements with Boolean characteristic functions.
The descent proof uses quantifier-free state identities and rank
inequalities. No unrestricted induction, minimization, choice, real
height, general Mordell-Weil theorem, or universal PR evaluation is
used. The manuscript correctly separates this supplied-data
finite-generation theorem from a construction of such data for all
elliptic curves.
