1. Coordinate change and discriminant
M has determinant p0 and the displayed adjugate really is
[[1,0,0],[-p1,p0,0],[-p2,0,p0]].  The transformed form is
U(ell V+mW)+aV²+bVW+cW² with the stated coefficients.  Its polar determinant
is -2(am²-b ell m+c ell²), and also p0² det(S_q); thus r!=0 follows.
The input hypothesis is explicitly a supplied rational point, so seed
existence is not smuggled into an unbounded search.

2. Certificate identities
Both identities were expanded independently.  In the first, the s²
coefficient is am²-b ell m+c ell² and the st,t² coefficients cancel.
In the second, the t² coefficient is r and the s²,st coefficients cancel.
They correctly exclude simultaneous projective zeros of L and R, including
ell=0 or m=0.

3. Parametrization and tangent
F=(-R,sL,tL) is nonzero on every nonzero parameter.  Substitution in UL+R
is exactly zero.  When the inverse-transformed point has (v,w)!=0, the
identity F(v,w)=L(v,w)(u,v,w) is valid and L(v,w)!=0 follows from r!=0.
The exceptional point has inverse [-m:ell], which is nonzero and maps to
[-r:0:0].  The recovery of all parameters with L=0 handles ell=0 explicitly;
there is no lost direction or duplicate tangent.  The stated tangent is
correct because the gradient at [1:0:0] is (0,ell,m).

4. Heights and gcd cancellation
The bound |ell|+|m| <= 2CP follows termwise from the original coefficients;
therefore the raw image coordinates are <=3CP S².  The subtle lower bound
is sound: primitive (s,t) gives gcd(sL,tL)=|L| (also for L=0), hence the
content g of F equals gcd(|R|,|L|).  The certificate identities imply
g|r s² and g|r t², so Bezout for coprime s²,t² yields g|r.  With
T0=max(|R|,S|L|), either certificate gives |r|S²<=D T0; substituting
T0=g H([F]) and g<=|r| permits cancellation of positive |r|.  Applying
the adjugate to a primitive representative of the final image gives
H([F])<=2P H(Phi(T)), proving the displayed K=2PD lower bound.
No assumption that projective coordinates remain primitive is made.
D>=1, A>=1, K>=2 follow from the hypotheses.

5. Counts
For N>=2, the union bound over ALL d=2,...,N is valid (overcounting is harmless).
The finite rational sum bound 1/4 + sum_{d=3}^N 1/[d(d-1)]
=3/4-1/N is correct even for N=2.  It implies G(N)>=N²/4+N, hence
4G(N)>=N².  N=1 is correctly separated.
The upper parameter-box count is ((2S+1)²-1)/2=2S²+2S<=4S² for S>=1.
Here S=floor sqrt(KB)>=1 when B>=1.  Thus N_q(B)<=4KB.
For the lower bound, maximal N with AN²<=B satisfies N>=1 for B>=A,
and B<A(N+1)²<=4AN²<=16A N_q(B).  The claimed weak inequality follows.
The n+1 parameters [k:1], k=0,...,n, are distinct and have height <=n
for n>=1.  Quantitative infinitude is therefore proved by an explicit list.

6. Examples
The circle inverse [Z+X:Y] gives the original point multiplied by
2(Z+X); the only zero inverse pair is [-1:0:1].  Content of the
quadratic triple divides 2 and is exactly 2 for both-odd parameters,
otherwise 1.  The height formula (s²+t²)/epsilon is correct.
The conic X²+Y²=3Z² is obstructed by two modulo-3 deductions, equivalently
by lack of a unimodular solution modulo 9.  The text correctly warns that
it DOES have an F3 projective point, so a mod-3 no-point certificate is
not being asserted.

7. iPRA strength
All algorithms use fixed-degree polynomial operations, bounded loops,
gcd/Bezout, finite counts, and characteristic functions of bounded
predicates.  The supplied formulas avoid universal PR evaluation, real
analysis, choice, algebraic closure, and arbitrary formula induction.
The current text sufficiently identifies count/union-bound/telescoping
invariants as quantifier-free after coding.  An eventual object-theory
formalization must still provide the indicated arithmetic infrastructure;
this chapter's mathematical proof does not by itself establish an already
kernel-checked iPRA derivation.  The parent report's status distinction
should make that explicit, as planned.
