MATHEMATICAL AUDIT: CONSTRUCTIVE MORDELL-WEIL IN iPRA
Date: 2026-11-15

1. Scope and status

The general Mordell-Weil theorem states finite generation of A(K) for an
abelian variety A over a number field K. A uniform constructive theorem that
produces a finite generating list from arbitrary elliptic-curve coefficients
would solve the currently open general algorithmic Mordell-Weil problem.
No impossibility or independence theorem is asserted here. The defensible
general result in this package is relative to explicit descent-cover data,
together with unconditional special examples proved separately.

Current official source:
Magma Handbook, Mordell-Weil Groups and Descent Methods,
https://docs.magma-maths.org/v2.30-1/ArithmeticGeometry/EllipticCurvesOverQAndNumberFields/mordell-weil.html
The introductory paragraphs explicitly state the open algorithmic problem.
The rank/group commands report booleans distinguishing proved results from
lower bounds or potentially incomplete subgroups. Retrieved 2026-11-15.

Primary explanatory source:
Andrew V. Sutherland, MIT 18.782, Lecture 25 (2013), pp. 1-2,
https://www.ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-782-introduction-to-arithmetic-geometry-fall-2013/e011c006f6aeb95083197457da90598d_MIT18_782F13_lec25.pdf
The notes identify the ineffective bound on the heights of quotient
representatives as the reason the ordinary descent proof is not an algorithm.

2. Finite ambient labels do not produce a representative list

An injection E(Q)/2E(Q) into a finite explicitly computed group provides a
bound on the possible number of classes. It does not automatically decide
which labels are attained. Selmer elements may describe locally soluble
coverings without rational points. Actual rational representatives must be
supplied or their existence established by a separate constructive argument.

A general computability illustration is decisive. Let f_e(t) be 1 if machine
e halts within t steps and 0 otherwise. This is primitive recursive in e,t,
and its image is contained in {0,1}. A uniform algorithm returning finitely
many input times whose values cover its entire image would decide halting:
simulate through the largest returned time. Thus subfiniteness of an
enumerable image is not uniform listability of that image.

3. Exact abstract finite descent

For a fixed PR-presented commutative group, let h(P) be a natural-valued
height and L(B) a PR finite list covering all points of height at most B.
Suppose a finite list R and a PR selector return i,Q with

    P = R_i + 2Q,       2 h(Q) <= h(P) + C.

Take the final generating list R ++ L(C). Whenever h(P)>C the excess
e(P)=h(P)-C satisfies e(Q)<=floor(e(P)/2); in particular h(Q)<h(P).
Stopped descent using h(P)+1 units of fuel terminates within that fuel.
An optimized bound is bitlength(max(h(P)-C,0)). A t-step trace gives

    P = sum_{j=0}^{t-1} 2^j R_{i_j} + 2^t Q_t,
    h(Q_t) <= C.

The total digit coefficient sum is 2^t-1, and the terminal coefficient is
2^t. Encoding the finite state, its trace, and this equality uses PR
operations and quantifier-free induction. No minimal counterexample,
unbounded search, excluded middle for a nondecidable predicate, or real
number completeness is needed.

4. Verified elliptic height bounds

Let E: y^2=x^3+A x+B with A,B integers and nonzero discriminant. Put
S=1+|A|+|B|. Write affine points in normalized denominator form

    P=(a/d^2,b/d^3),       R=(c/e^2,w/e^3),
    d,e>=1, gcd(a,d)=gcd(c,e)=1.

Define H(P)=max(|a|,d^2), H(O)=1. The denominator-square/cube normal form
follows prime by prime: a negative valuation of x forces 2v(y)=3v(x),
so v(x) is even and v(y) is three times half v(x).

The curve equation gives |b|d <= sqrt(S) H(P)^2 and
|w|e <= sqrt(S) H(R)^2. The proof can avoid square roots by squaring
both nonnegative sides and using integer comparisons.

For x(P) != x(R), x(P-R) is represented by N/D, where

 N = a^2 c e^2 + a c^2 d^2
     + A(a d^2 e^4 + c d^4 e^2)
     + 2B d^4 e^4 + 2b w d e,
 D = (a e^2-c d^2)^2.

Each of the first two terms is at most H(P)^2 H(R)^2 in absolute value.
The two A terms contribute 2|A| times that product, the B term contributes
2|B|, and the last term contributes at most 2S. Consequently

 max(|N|,|D|) <= 4S H(P)^2 H(R)^2.

The package's larger constant K=4S^2 is therefore valid. The exceptional
cases P=O, P=R, P=-R are handled by

 T_R = max(K H(R)^2, H(2R), 1),
 H(P-R) <= T_R H(P)^2.

If R=O, the same inequality is immediate (choose T_O>=1). If y(R)=0,
the two coincident-x exceptional cases coincide and the same bound applies.

For duplication, use homogeneous binary forms

 F(X,Z)=X^4-2A X^2 Z^2-8B X Z^3+A^2 Z^4,
 G(X,Z)=4X^3 Z+4A X Z^3+4B Z^4.

The Sylvester determinant is nonzero for nonsingular E. It equals Delta^2
with Delta=-16(4A^3+27B^2), though the explicit determinant formula is not
needed if the nonzero resultant and Bezout identities are supplied.
The adjugate gives degree-three integral forms U,V,U',V' satisfying

 UF+VG = R0 X^7,       U'F+V'G = R0 Z^7,      R0 != 0.

Let L>=1 bound both sums of absolute coefficients appearing on the left.
For primitive X,Z, the common divisor g=gcd(F(X,Z),G(X,Z)) divides |R0|.
The identities give max(|F|,|G|)>=|R0| max(|X|,|Z|)^4/L. Therefore

    H(P)^4 <= L H(2P).

The formulas include O and 2-torsion: evaluate them on projective x
coordinates, so x(O)=(1:0). There is no need to divide by y at these points.
Dividing both identities, their four coefficient vectors and R0 by any
common positive divisor preserves the proof and can substantially reduce
L. The paper uses this optional normalization. For E1, its displayed
normalized identities have R0=16 and L=36; the resulting T=16 and C=12
are correct.

5. Verified bit-height conversion and bounded selector

Let h(P) be the least n with H(P)<=2^n. Thus h(P)=0 when H(P)=1.
For a supplied finite list R, put

 D0 = L max_i T_{R_i} >= 1,
 d0 = least n with D0<=2^n,
 C = d0+2.

If P=R_i+2Q, the preceding inequalities imply

    H(Q)^4 <= D0 H(P)^2.

For h(Q)>0, H(Q)>2^(h(Q)-1), whence

    4h(Q) <= d0+2h(P)+3 <= 2h(P)+2d0+4,
    2h(Q) <= h(P)+C.

The case h(Q)=0 is immediate. All these conclusions can be proved with
integer exponent comparisons; real logarithms are only mnemonic notation.

In particular h(Q)<=h(P)+C. Consequently any existential cover witness Q
can be found by bounded search through the complete finite list of points
with H(Q)<=2^(h(P)+C). This turns a proved actual-cover assertion into a
PR selector. It does not establish the actual-cover assertion itself.

An improved selector does not need the height search. If T is affine with
reduced rational x-coordinate u/v (v>0), the x-coordinate X of any Q with
2Q=T is a root of

 v X^4 - 4u X^3 - 2A v X^2 - (8Bv+4Au) X + (A^2 v-4Bu).

This is exactly vF(X,1)-uG(X,1). Include X=0 if the original constant is
zero, and strip all factors X^k. For each nonzero reduced rational root
m/n of the remaining polynomial, the rational-root theorem gives

    |m| divides |c_k|,       n divides v,

where c_k is its nonzero constant and n>0. Enumerate the finitely many
signed m and positive n within these bounds, test coprimality and the
polynomial equation, then test whether X^3+A X+B is a rational square.
A reduced nonnegative rational is a square iff its numerator and
denominator are integer squares; both square tests admit bounded PR
search. Try both y signs and verify the full group equation 2Q=T.
If stripping leaves a nonzero constant polynomial, there are simply no
nonzero roots. Nonsingularity prevents a common zero of F and G, but the
final group-equation check also protects soundness at exceptional points.

For T=O, enumerate O and the points (x,0) where x is a rational root of
the monic cubic x^3+A x+B; its rational roots are integral, and the same
zero-stripping and divisor enumeration handles B=0. This yields an exact
PR list of all halves of T, and a PR decision procedure for T in 2E(Q).
The procedure for a supplied representative list checks the finitely many
targets P-R_i and returns the first successful half. Its failure value is
allowed for arbitrary input lists; the proved cover hypothesis excludes
failure on the lists to which the theorem is applied. Pointwise
2-divisibility is decidable, while universal coverage remains an additional
obligation.

6. Logical and formalization cautions

(a) Generic algorithms must be treated as a schema for fixed PR function
symbols. There is no total universal PR evaluator for all PR program codes.
For elliptic curves, use fixed programs with numeric A,B and list parameters.

(b) A finite tuple of data is not automatically a decidable certificate of
all its universal correctness, coverage, or height properties. Those
properties must be hypotheses or must have separate mathematical proofs.

(c) If the tuple includes iPRA proof codes, checking a derivation is PR and
combining accepted derivations into a new derivation can be PR. This is a
syntactic metatheorem. It does not justify an internal iPRA principle
'checker accepts an arbitrary proof => the proved universal statement is
true'; that would require a reflection principle.

(d) A Lean theorem about mathematical groups or a Lean proof of PR
algorithms is not automatically an object-language iPRA derivation. Keep
the distinction between Lean metatheory, exact algebraic lemmas, and the
qf-induction arithmetic translation visible. A completed Lean outline is
not a claim that all arithmetic-geometry dependencies are formalized.

(e) General rank/group algorithm status is not an unprovability statement.
Do not assert that Mordell-Weil is known independent of iPRA or that an
unconditional constructive proof is impossible.

Audit conclusion: the supplied-cover elliptic corollary, with the explicit
height constants above and bounded extraction, is mathematically coherent.
The unresolved general step is producing actual rational quotient
representatives uniformly from arbitrary curve data.

FINAL MATHEMATICAL REVIEW

Sections 1-6 (files 01_foundations, 02_descent, 03_elliptic,
04_unconditional) were reviewed. The frozen excess invariant, bit-length
fuel, coefficient bounds, denominator normalization, inverse translation,
resultant cancellation, rational halving and supplied-cover theorem were
checked. Two minor preliminary proof slips were reported and verified
corrected: Euclid now initializes with ordered arguments; the Z^r test
uses the exact odd-coordinate identity
(2q+1)^2+1-2q^2=2(q+1)^2. The equation label and Sha macro issues were
also corrected.

The unconditional square-area argument was checked in full. Primitive
normalization preserves square area; the Pythagorean parametrization
uses only coprime square splitting; the four factors
m,n,m+n,m-n are pairwise coprime. With their roots r,s,t,u,
the actual paper's simpler descending triangle

    ((t+u)/2, (t-u)/2, r, s/2)

is integral because t,u are odd and s is even. Its area and Pythagorean
equations expand exactly, its legs are coprime, and its hypotenuse r is
strictly smaller than the original m^2+n^2. The validity-plus-decreasing-
hypotenuse invariant is quantifier-free and reaches contradiction after
exactly the initial hypotenuse, without least-counterexample reasoning.

The rational-point-to-area-one triangle map is correct for every y!=0,
including negative rational coordinates, because absolute values make
the side lengths positive while preserving area 1. Denominator clearing
produces an integral square-area triangle. The remaining four points,
their Klein-four table, rational scalings by u!=0 and coordinatewise
finite products are correct. The paper accurately says that these
scaled products are powers of one rational isomorphism class and do not
settle arbitrary twists, positive rank or general number fields.

No remaining mathematical error was found in the reviewed written
arguments. This is a written mathematical review, not a claim that the entire paper is
already formalized. The native Lean files prove their explicitly stated
interfaces; the full object-language iPRA derivation and elliptic
classification bridge remain the formalization work described by the
paper. In particular the compiled native descent theorem's axiom report
includes propext and Quot.sound, and should not be described as an
axiom-free object-language iPRA proof.
