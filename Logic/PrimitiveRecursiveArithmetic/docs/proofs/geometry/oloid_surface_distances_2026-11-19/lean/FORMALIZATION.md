# Formalizing the distance table in Lean 4

`SurfaceDistance.lean` defines the original convex hull and its frontier, the continuous competitors, length as extended nonnegative variation, the ten spatial and developed points, both gate sets, the exact answer for each sign pattern, and the 45 rational enclosure targets. Index 0 in Lean is P1 in the paper. The proposed final theorems have statements `ExactTable` and `NumericalTable`. The generic order and variation lemmas have proof scripts. The geometric lemmas and the certificate-soundness theorem below remain to be implemented. No Lean compiler or mathlib checkout with compiled dependencies was available to test these scripts.

## 1. The closed chamber

Let J be [−1/2,1], let Q be [0,1] × J as a subtype of ℝ², and take X(s,c) = chart s c, U(s,c) = flat s c. Set C = surface ∩ {y ≥ 0} ∩ {z ≥ 0} and D = U(Q). Show the following propositions in this order.

1. The image of X is C. In a support direction n = (−c, √(1−c²), √(1+2c)), both disks attain their supporting values at endA c and endB c. Every point of the segment between those end points therefore lies on the surface. Prove exhaustiveness by the fixed-x projected sweep in the supplied oloid archive, or use the support-functional classification in the accompanying paper. Symmetries of the convex hull give all four sign copies. To prove injectivity, the outward support plane for an interior ruling singles out its two end points, and equality in the disk support inequality forces any second representation to have the same end points when both weights are positive. At a circle point only the representation of weight zero or one is possible; its arc is injectively parametrized. X is a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space and hence a homeomorphism onto C.
2. Derive the planar boundary tangent in the arclength coordinate t, where c = cos(t), 0 ≤ t ≤ 2π/3. Put u = tan(t/2)/√3. The derivative of flatA with respect to t is

   ( (1−u²)^(3/2)/(1+3u²), u(3+u²)/(1+3u²) ).

   Its squared norm is one by polynomial expansion. The second component increases since its derivative with respect to u is 3(1−u²)²/(1+3u²)². Its first component is positive on the open interval. The boundary arc is thus the graph of a convex function f on [0,W]. The other arc is the graph of H−f(W−x). Convexity bounds f by its endpoint chord and gives a vertical gap of at least √3 between the graphs. The closed region between them is convex. The two end-point maps along its lower and upper boundaries are strictly ordered in x, including at the extreme rulings. Hence different ruling segments are disjoint: their four endpoints do not alternate on the two boundary graphs. An analytic alternative compares the two straight-line graphs on their common horizontal interval, first checking the order at its two ends by convexity. Every point of the region belongs to one segment: as the continuous family advances, the segments separate the two vertical sides; at a fixed test point the orientation with respect to the moving segment changes sign between the extreme rulings, and the ordered convex-boundary intersections put the point on the segment at a zero. This proves that U is a homeomorphism onto the stated D. At the vertical sides use the boundary segment directly. An alternative surjectivity proof uses the Jordan curve theorem or invariance of domain on the parameter interior followed by compactness.
3. Identify the loci of coordinate zeros. The equation z = s √(1+2c)/(1+c) = 0 gives s=0 or c=−1/2, so its developed image is gateZ. The equation y = (1−s) √(1−c²) = 0 gives s=1 or c=1 on J and gives gateY. Both are compact unions of two nonempty images of compact intervals.

## 2. Length on the closed chamber

Reparametrize c at the ends when proving differentiability. Put u = sin(v), v ∈ [0,π/2] and c = (1−3u²)/(1+3u²). For the opposite end point the corresponding u is cos(v)/√(1+3sin²(v)) and its complementary square root is 2sin(v)/√(1+3sin²(v)). These formulas remove endpoint square-root singularities. For the planar arc use its continuous derivative above and the fundamental theorem of calculus, extended from the open interval by continuity. Signed sine and cosine expressions give regular one-sided derivatives and smooth continuations in v as required.

Prove that the two first fundamental forms agree. Specifically, write X = A+s(B−A) and U = a+s(b−a) and check equality of each Gram matrix entry for the two derivatives in (s,v), using the identities in the paper. The two spatial coordinate derivatives have rank two, except at (s,v) = (1,0) and (0,π/2). The rank formula in the supplied archive is an alternative, upon identifying the two regular angle charts. In particular, s=0 or 1 alone does not force a loss of rank.

Here is a route to the all-continuous-curves statement that does not require a theorem restricting geodesics to piecewise smooth paths. Let T = U ∘ X⁻¹. Fix a regular parameter w and a number δ > 0. On a small convex parameter neighborhood, the differences of X between any two points differ from DX(w) applied to their parameter difference by at most η times the norm of that difference. Use the mean-value inequality on the segment and continuity of DX, and apply the same argument to U. Write m>0 for the smallest singular value of DX(w), obtained by minimizing on the compact unit circle. Since ‖DU(w)v‖=‖DX(w)v‖, the ratio of the two chord lengths is bounded above by (m+η)/(m−η), in either direction. Choose η so this ratio is at most 1+δ. Half-rectangles and corner rectangles are also convex and the estimate holds on them.

For a continuous curve in C with a compact regular subarc, split the time interval using a Lebesgue number for the inverse images of these neighborhoods. On every finer partition, the local chord estimate holds term by term. Triangle inequality, the definition of variation, and δ ↓ 0 give the developed endpoint bound. For a curve regular except possibly at its two ends, apply this to closed subintervals approaching both ends: endpoint images converge, and the original subinterval variation is bounded by the full variation.

For a general subarc, remove the two exceptional point values by finite induction. Among the exceptional values met in its open time interval, take the earliest encounter, allowing an encounter at time zero with that same value. More formally intersect the inverse image of the finite exceptional set with the closed time interval after discarding values that occur only at the endpoints, and take its minimum; this minimum exists by compactness. Take the last occurrence of its value. The piece before the first encounter has no interior exceptional point, and the piece after the last encounter meets one fewer exceptional value in its interior. Ignore the intervening loop when estimating the displacement of the endpoints. The triangle inequality and additivity of variation give the endpoint bound on the full interval. Repeat for every pair of times and sum over any finite partition to obtain variation(T ∘ g) ≤ variation(g). Apply the same proof to T⁻¹ to get equality. The argument also covers infinite variation. The spatial absolute-value fold contracts each ambient Euclidean chord since ||a|−|b|| ≤ |a−b|, so the developed folded competitor satisfies `BoundsChords` on every subinterval.

## 3. Realizing and bounding routes

For distinct signs the corresponding (continuous) coordinate has a zero by the intermediate-value theorem. Pass that time, or the two times in their actual order, to `one_hit` or `two_hits`. If the two times agree the middle chord has zero length. When signs agree use `BoundsChords` at 0 and 1. This gives the lower bound for every continuous competitor.

The route infima are attained. Use the real-valued Euclidean chord sums on the compact gate sets and their finite products, then convert the minimizing value to extended nonnegative real distance. Convexity of D puts every chord in D. Lift it through T⁻¹ and use the signs of the current sheet until a gate is reached. Reflect the appropriate coordinate for the next piece. At the shared end the coordinate is zero, so the pasting lemma gives a continuous curve in the original surface. Its length is the chord sum by the two-sided variation equality, invariance under reflection, and `split_variation`. Apply `attained_intrinsic`. Prove this once for arbitrary sample parameters in the specified ranges, then discharge the 10 × 10 finite specialization with arithmetic.

## 4. Certifying the numerical inequalities

Use scaled integers for intervals with a positive denominator, and define `Contains i x` by the two endpoint inequalities. For each operation in geometry.py return interval data plus a soundness proof; integer square roots use the floor-square bracket. Lift the alternating cosine and arctangent bounds and the positive log-series tail bound to these operations. Machin's identity can be proved using the tangent addition formulas on the indicated principal branches. For the cosine series bound, after the first term the absolute terms decrease on [0,2π/3], so the first omitted term bounds the supplied high-order truncation. Handle both endpoints by the special formulas. The result must assert membership of the exact planar point for every requested dyadic gate parameter.

Use a finite tree of bisections for each allowed word. For a gate parameter interval [l,r], its entire arc is within 5(r−l)²/8 of its endpoint chord, proved by the second derivative norm estimate and linear interpolation remainder. Add both endpoint coordinate errors using convexity of the error ball. For each planar link and any integer direction d with ‖d‖ ≤ S, its length is at least its displacement dotted with d/S. After summing these affine bounds, the coefficient of a gate point is the entering direction minus the outgoing direction. The minimum on an endpoint chord is the smaller endpoint value, with an additional error bounded by the coefficient norm times the tube radius. This is the soundness lemma for a leaf. A displayed dyadic crossing tuple gives an upper bound by the Euclidean triangle inequality with outward-rounded square roots.

Port the independent replay already implemented by `code/verify.py` and use `data/trees.json` as input. The preorder symbol '/' bisects the longest parameter interval, breaking ties at the first coordinate. Symbols 'm' and 'w' compute supporting directions at the midpoint or at the advertised witness clamped into that leaf. Each branch covers both closed child boxes. Each such leaf proves a lower bound for every parameter in its box. The extra contact tree for P4–P10 also admits 'i', asserting range inclusion in the stated contact rectangle. Its other leaves prove a strict bound above a feasible length, forcing every minimizing pair of contacts inside that rectangle. Distinguish this containment statement from a lower-bound certificate. A small parser can return a finite tree with a proof that the stream is exhausted; tree induction proves the checker sound. Generate all permitted words directly from the two sign pairs. Check the upper witness against the advertised upper numerator. These computations imply the corresponding inequalities in `NumericalTable`. Replay does not require formalizing the optimizer or proving its termination.
