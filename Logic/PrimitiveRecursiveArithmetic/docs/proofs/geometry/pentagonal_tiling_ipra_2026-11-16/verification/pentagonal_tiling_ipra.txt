Abstract
This report reconstructs as much as possible of the mathematical content of the current
Wikipedia article “Pentagonal tiling” inside a deliberately weak and constructive setting: intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic (iPRA). The central method is to replace unrestricted
real-plane objects by finite exact certificates. Coordinates lie in rational quadratic fields or small
explicitly isolated algebraic extensions; every loop is bounded; every sign test is reduced to
integer arithmetic; and an infinite periodic tiling is compressed to a finite cell on a lattice torus.
Complete constructive arguments are given for a strengthened obstruction to a Euclidean
regular-pentagon tiling, the pentagon angle sum, the general Type 1 half-turn construction,
exact Type 1 and Type 3 hexagonal subdivisions, a full 12-pentagon Type 15 lattice cell,
the rigid Type 14 algebra and a six-copy local patch, three congruent nonconvex subdivision
families, duality facts for valence-five uniform tilings, the dodecahedral {5, 3} spherical tiling, the
degenerate {5, 2} case, and the arithmetic criterion for all regular hyperbolic {5, n} tilings. The
fifteen systems of angle equations in the article are also reduced to explicit linear consequences
of the 540◦ angle sum.
A companion exact checker verifies four periodic Euclidean cells, including all edge subdivisions
and reverse pairings, exact lattice areas, a complete finite
√ set of separating-axis tests, the Type 14
nested radicals, nonconvex subdivisions, and a Q( 5) dodecahedron certificate. A no-sorry
Lean blueprint specifies the path to closed derivations of type Prf PRAAx. The report is explicit
about what is not proved: in particular Rao’s full 371-case classification trace, the 24-isohedral
classification, the all-orders nonperiodic rotational construction, and the irregular hyperbolic
catalogue are not imported as axioms.

Formal status. The human proofs below are constructive and finitary. The shipped
Python checker passes all exact assertions. The Lean file contains no sorry, admit, or new
axiom, but Lean/Lake were unavailable in the delivery environment, so it is not claimed
compiler-checked. None of the main geometric results is falsely presented as an already
completed object Prf PRAAx derivation.

Scope, interpretation, and main results

The source article ranges from elementary angle arithmetic to large classification theorems, infinite
nonperiodic constructions, and Euclidean, spherical, and hyperbolic geometry. These statements
do not all have the same logical shape. A finite incidence statement about a dodecahedron is
naturally arithmetical. A periodic tiling with algebraic coordinates can be compressed to a finite
torus certificate. By contrast, “every real convex pentagon that tiles the plane belongs to one of
fifteen families” quantifies over arbitrary real parameters and an infinite tiling; that sentence is not
literally a first-order formula of iPRA until one specifies codes for reals, tilings, and their correctness.
Accordingly, this report uses the following constructive reading.
Definition 1.1 (Certified polygon and certified tiling). A certified algebraic polygon is a finite code
for its coordinates, together with exact algebraic sign information sufficient to decide all orientation,
equality, and length comparisons used in the proof. A certified periodic tiling is a finite list of such
polygons, two lattice vectors, finite edge-pairing data, and finite nonoverlap witnesses satisfying the
verifier of definition 3.1.
This is not an arbitrary weakening chosen to make the problem easy. It is the right form for
iPRA: primitive recursive arithmetic can manipulate any fixed finite algebraic certificate, and it
can prove a uniform theorem about a primitive-recursive certificate checker. The infinite family of
translates is generated by the two integer lattice parameters, while global coverage is reduced to a
finite arrangement inside one fundamental cell.

1.1

Results proved here

The principal completed results are as follows.
R1. A regular pentagon cannot tile the Euclidean plane, even if T-junctions are permitted. The
local equation would be 3k + 5ℓ = 10, with k ≥ 1, which is impossible in N.
R2. Every certified Type 1 convex pentagon tiles periodically. Two copies joined by a half-turn
form a centrally symmetric hexagon, and every such hexagon tiles by two translations.
R3. Exact periodic cells are provided for a rational Type 1 pentagon, a regular hexagon split
into two Type 1 pentagons, and a regular hexagon split into three Type 3 pentagons.
√
R4. The rigid Type 15 tile is reconstructed over Q( 3), with exact angles and side ratios, and
twelve copies are certified in one lattice cell.
R5. The rigid Type 14 nested-radical formulas and a six-tile local patch are proved exactly. A
full torus certificate is not claimed for Type 14.
R6. The angle equations for all fifteen convex types yield explicit linear identities compatible
with a 540◦ pentagon angle sum.
R7. The dual of an edge-to-edge valence-five tiling is pentagonal; primal isogonality implies
dual isohedrality, and the three listed uniform-dual angle sequences follow from the formula
360◦ /p.
R8. Equilateral triangles, squares, and regular hexagons are divided into respectively three, four,
and six congruent nonconvex pentagons by exact constructions.

1.2

Current status of the exhaustive convex classification

Rao’s 2017 manuscript reports an exhaustive search with 371 angle-condition cases and a backtracking
search that finds no family beyond the known fifteen [2]. On the author’s current publication page,
the work is still listed under “Manuscripts/Submitted,” with links to the arXiv version, a draft,
slides, and preliminary code [3]. The present report therefore does not silently treat the classification
as a small checked lemma. Section 11 gives a concrete certificate-import program and identifies the
additional issue that the published statement quantifies over arbitrary real pentagons and infinite
tilings.

The target theory: intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic

2.1

The ProveIt object calculus

The checked ProveIt presentation used as the formal target is ordinary first-order iPRA: one
numerical sort, a function symbol for every primitive recursive function, defining equations for
those symbols, successor axioms, intuitionistic natural deduction with equality, and induction for
quantifier-free formulas. In the repository, the relevant types are
PR(k),

PRAAx,

and a final closed theorem has the shape

The existing derivation library supplies arithmetic, integers as differences, order, pairing, bounded
search, powers, division, and finite linear algebra. The intended final endpoints are actual derivation
values, not merely semantic truths in Lean.

Proof. The first dual-coordinate interval of the translate is the interval of Pj shifted by the integer

any endpoint of the first polygon interval and any endpoint of the second unshifted interval is
bounded by the global width w1 . Hence |m| ≤ w1 , and the integer bound follows. The second
coordinate is identical. All maxima and minima are finite folds using exact order.
For each of the four shipped periodic cells, both ceilings are at most 2. Thus all possible overlaps
are covered by the 25 shifts in [−2, 2]2 . This is a derived bound, not a visually chosen search radius.

3.3

Constructive soundness

Theorem 3.3 (Finite certificate soundness). If a periodic certificate passes the checks of definition 3.1, then the lattice translates

form a monohedral tiling of the plane: tile interiors are disjoint and their union is the plane.
Proof. Congruence follows from the common cyclic squared-distance matrix. For convex polygons,
the separating-axis theorem says that two interiors are disjoint if some normal to an edge separates
their projection intervals. The certificate supplies such a normal for every translate allowed by
lemma 3.2; all farther translates are excluded by the dual coordinate intervals. Therefore the entire
infinite family has pairwise disjoint interiors.
It remains to prove coverage without appealing to countable additivity or a classical measure
theorem. Work in the fundamental parallelogram with opposite sides identified. First use the
dual-coordinate bound to list every translated tile that can meet the cell. Intersect all of those
translated tile edges with one another and with the parallelogram boundary. Sort the finitely
many intersection parameters exactly and split the edges. These segments form a finite planar

arrangement. Each arrangement face is a polygon whose relative interior is either inside one tile or
inside none; it cannot cross a tile boundary by construction. Triangulate every face by choosing its
least-coded vertex and forming a fan.
Shoelace additivity is an integer-polynomial identity: internal directed edges cancel in reverse
pairs. Since tile interiors are disjoint, every covered arrangement triangle occurs once. The
certificate’s area equality therefore says that the sum of the areas of uncovered arrangement triangles
is zero. Every nondegenerate triangle has strictly positive exact area, so bounded induction over
the face list shows there is no uncovered two-dimensional face.
Could a lower-dimensional slit remain? No. The tile images on the torus are closed polygons,
so an uncovered point would have an uncovered neighborhood; in the finite arrangement that
neighborhood contains a nondegenerate open face, already excluded by the area argument. The
reverse-subedge pairing is an explicit combinatorial check of the same boundary gluing: every tile
edge has material on the opposite local side after passage to the torus, and cell edges are identified
with their translates. Hence the quotient is covered. Translating the cell covers the plane.
Every construction used in this argument is a bounded list operation, rational/algebraic sign test,
or quantifier-free induction. The proof is therefore a direct blueprint for a theorem of iPRA.
Remark 3.4. The companion Python program verifies the concrete hypotheses and records edge
pairings, exact separating axes, and translate instances. It does not pretend to be the object iPRA
soundness proof. In Lean, the finite arrangement theorem must be proved once and then instantiated
by each evaluated certificate.

Universal Euclidean lemmas

4.1

Pentagon angle sum

Angles are most convenient in rational turns: a full turn is 1, a half turn is 1/2, and 108◦ = 3/10.
For general algebraic diagrams one can instead use oriented ray codes and a finite angle-addition
table.
Theorem 4.1 (Constructive pentagon angle sum). The interior angles of a convex pentagon sum
to three half-turns, equivalently 3π = 540◦ .
Proof. Let the vertices be A, B, C, D, E in boundary order. Draw the diagonals AC and AD. Convexity puts them inside the pentagon, and they divide it into the three triangles ABC, ACD, ADE.
The three triangle angle sums give 3π. At A, C, D, the triangle angles partition the corresponding
pentagon angles; at B, E they are unchanged. Summing and cancelling gives the result.
In the coded proof, “inside” is the common sign of five determinants. The partition of an angle
is a finite orientation statement. The triangle angle sum may be represented by the exterior-turn
identity, avoiding analytic trigonometry. The derivation is a fixed finite calculation.

4.2

Regular pentagons: a stronger local obstruction

The usual one-line argument says that 108◦ does not divide 360◦ . That formulation tacitly assumes
an edge-to-edge vertex. A general polygon tiling may contain a T-junction, where the chosen point
lies in the interior of another tile edge. The following arithmetic argument covers both cases.
Theorem 4.2 (No Euclidean tiling by regular pentagons). No locally finite tiling of the Euclidean
plane by congruent regular pentagons exists, even when the tiling is not edge-to-edge.

4.3

Centrally symmetric hexagons tile

Lemma 4.3 (Parallelohexagon translation lemma). Let a simple convex hexagon have successive
edge vectors
u, v, w, −u, −v, −w.
Then it tiles the plane by translations generated by
U = u + v,

V = v + w.

Proof. Translate the edge with vector u by V = v + w; it meets the opposite edge with reversed
boundary orientation. Translate the edge with vector v by V − U = w − u, and translate the
edge with vector w by −U . Thus all six directed boundary edges pair under the lattice ZU + ZV .
The shoelace area of the hexagon equals | cross(U, V )|; this follows by expanding both expressions
and cancelling opposite-edge terms. Convexity gives nonoverlap with neighboring translates, and
theorem 3.3 gives coverage. More elementarily, the three edge pairings glue one hexagon to a torus
fundamental polygon.

The general Type 1 construction

The article’s Type 1 condition is
B + C = 180◦ ,

A + D + E = 360◦ ,

where the second equation follows from the first and theorem 4.1.
Theorem 4.4 (Every certified Type 1 pentagon tiles). Let P be a simple convex algebraic pentagon
whose consecutive angles B, C satisfy B + C = π. Then two congruent copies of P form a centrally
symmetric hexagon, and P tiles periodically.

16 November 2026

Proof. Rotate P by a half-turn about the midpoint of the side joining the B- and C-vertices. The
two copies share that side with opposite orientation. At one endpoint, an exposed edge of the
original and the corresponding exposed edge of the rotated copy meet with total interior angle
B + C = π, so they are collinear and merge into one boundary segment. The same happens at
the other endpoint. Starting from eight exposed edges, these two mergers leave six. The half-turn
exchanges opposite vertices and edges, so the resulting hexagon has edge vectors u, v, w, −u, −v, −w.
Convexity of the original and the exact orientation conditions certify that the interiors of the two
copies do not overlap. Apply lemma 4.3.
On coordinate codes, half-turn, midpoint, determinant, and collinearity are all primitive recursive.
Thus this is a uniform constructor from a certified Type 1 pentagon to a periodic certificate.

7.1

Finite quotient duality

For an edge-to-edge periodic tiling, take a finite torus quotient. Place a dual vertex in each primal
face and join dual vertices across each primal edge. This is a finite incidence transformation.
Theorem 7.1 (Valence five gives pentagonal duals). If every primal vertex has valence five, then
every face of the dual is a pentagon. If the primal symmetry group is transitive on vertices, the dual
symmetry group is transitive on faces. More generally, k primal vertex orbits give k dual face orbits.
Proof. A dual face traces the primal faces incident around one primal vertex. There are exactly
five, so its boundary has five dual edges and vertices. The dual construction commutes with every
incidence-preserving symmetry: a symmetry sending primal vertex v to w sends the dual face around
v to the dual face around w. Therefore vertex orbits and dual-face orbits are in bijection. The proof
is a finite permutation argument on the torus quotient.

Lemma 7.2 (Dual angle at a regular p-gon). At the dual vertex placed at the center of a regular

11

The exhaustive classification and its constructive boundary

11.1

What Rao’s manuscript establishes

Rao’s manuscript states that only the fifteen known convex pentagon families can tile the plane,
without assuming periodicity. Its architecture has two large parts: a reduction to finitely many sets
of vertex types, and a backtracking search through 371 angle-condition representatives [2]. This is
strong evidence and is the source of the article’s completeness claim. It is not, however, already a
theorem of the checked ProveIt object calculus.

11.2

Why “just run the program” is not enough

Three distinct obligations must be separated.
1. Analytical reduction. One must constructively justify the passage from an arbitrary infinite
tiling by an arbitrary real convex pentagon to a positive-density or otherwise finitely controlled
configuration. Classical compactness, limit extraction, or arbitrary real choice cannot be
silently used in iPRA.
2. Finite enumeration. The list of vertex-condition cases must be generated by a primitiverecursive program with a proof that it is complete.
3. Backtracking certificates. Every rejected branch needs an exact contradiction certificate;
every surviving branch must map to one of the fifteen families.
22 of 28

Pentagonal tilings in iPRA

16 November 2026

Furthermore, first-order iPRA cannot quantify directly over all real numbers. A literal constructive classification must choose one of the following formulations.
Certified-algebraic classification. Quantify over finite algebraic coordinate codes and a certified
tiling witness.
Primitive-recursive-name classification. Quantify over indices of primitive-recursive Cauchy
names and primitive-recursive tiling enumerators, with explicit moduli and coverage predicates.
Finite-patch consequence. State that every sufficiently coherent finite patch certificate forces
one of the fifteen equation systems.
The first is the most directly compatible with this report. It is weaker than an unrestricted
theorem about all real pentagons, but it is an honest arithmetical sentence.

11.3

A checkable import design

A future archive should contain:
1. a canonical finite encoding of each of the 371 representatives;
2. rational row-reduction or Farkas certificates for every infeasible linear angle system;
3. an explicit search tree whose nodes record boundary states and whose children exhaust every
local placement;
4. for each terminal failure, a small exact witness: angle overflow, forbidden edge incidence,
length inconsistency, or previously dominated state;
5. for each surviving branch, a derivation of one of the fifteen family equation systems;
6. a verified checker and a separate soundness theorem in Prf PRAAx.
The finite search itself is plausible in iPRA: a very large numeral and a very long bounded
derivation are still primitive recursive. The delicate part is the uniform analytical reduction from
arbitrary real-plane tilings. Until that reduction and the complete trace are present, this report
records the classification as open rather than importing it as an axiom.
Corollary 11.1 (Conditional “no convex aperiodic-only polygon”). Assume the full convex classification and assume a periodic certificate for one member of each of its fifteen families. Then no
convex polygon tiles the plane only aperiodically.
Proof. Any convex polygon that tiles belongs to one of the fifteen families, and every family has a
periodic tiling witness. Therefore the polygon admits at least one periodic tiling. This does not say
that all of its tilings are periodic.

Conclusions and further research

The broad lesson is that much of elementary and computational tiling geometry is compatible
with very weak constructive arithmetic once the statement is phrased in proof-relevant finite
form. Periodicity is especially helpful: a whole-plane tiling becomes a finite torus complex, and
exact area, edge pairing, and separation reduce global geometry to bounded arithmetic. The

The remaining work falls into four distinct programs.
1. Complete object derivations. Implement the generic certificate checker and arrangement
soundness as actual Prf PRAAx values, beginning with the rational Type 1 cell and then
Type 15.
2. Complete the convex-family witness library. Convert standard diagrams for Types 2
and 4–13 into exact algebraic periodic certificates. In particular, finish the Type 14 lattice

3. Classification trace. Obtain a stable complete Rao search trace, replace every numerical

4. Uniform geometry generators. Formalize Coxeter reflection matrices and cyclotomic root

nonperiodic pentagonal tilings.
A particularly useful near-term theorem would be a reusable, checked “periodic algebraic polygon
certificate” library in ProveIt. It would apply not only to pentagons but to periodic tilings, polyhedral
nets, finite-element meshes, and lattice packings throughout the repository.

Reference checker design notes

The exact checker is intentionally straightforward rather than optimized. Its main procedures are:
√
1. normalize and operate in a + b d;
2. compare signs by lemma 2.1;
3. check congruence by all entries of cyclic squared-distance matrices;
4. check convexity by five turn signs;
5. derive a finite overlap radius from dual lattice widths;
6. search the finite translate box and apply exact separating axes;
7. split edges at all translated certificate vertices lying on them;
8. pair every directed subedge with a reverse lattice translate;
9. compare shoelace area with lattice determinant;
10. verify the ordered-quartic Type 14 algebra, full distance congruence, convexity, local separation,
and dodecahedral incidence; and
11. emit exact rational linear-combination witnesses for all fifteen angle rows.
These operations are all primitive recursive once rational and list encodings are fixed. The
program is therefore not merely an informal numerical experiment; it is a readable reference
