# Euclidean geometry in intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic

**Research package — 22 November 2026.**

This package develops a broad Euclidean-geometry portfolio through explicit
finite coordinates, constructive witnesses, and arithmetic proofs suitable for
intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic (iPRA). The report contains
**117 labeled results**, counted once per label. Its final inventory
records the different roles of geometric theorems, arithmetic foundations,
families with finite input data, observation results, and metatheoretic results.

Start with **`report.pdf`** for the complete human-readable arguments and
formalization plan. **`report.tex` and `chapters/`** contain its editable LaTeX
source. The package also contains Lean proof candidates, exact computation
scripts, certificate outputs, and pinned source provenance.

The human proofs are not a claim that the whole portfolio has received new
Lean kernel checking. The exact formalization status is explained below and
recorded in `lean/build_status.json`.

Statements retain their actual hypotheses. For example, noncollinear triangles
carry a nonzero determinant, circle intersections retain discriminant signs,
directed-ratio theorems retain nonzero denominators, and polygon or polyhedral
arguments use the finite orientation, decomposition, or cellulation data
specified in the report. Algebraic angle pairs express turns and angle
equalities without presupposing an analytic inverse-trigonometric function.

## Two representations, with different purposes

### Exact geometry over the coded field K

An exact scalar is finite rational polynomial and isolating-interval data.
These records represent the real algebraic numbers. Equality, order, arithmetic,
nonnegative square roots, and the required polynomial-root operations have
explicit bounded algorithms in the human development. Invalid records receive
specified defaults, and theorem translations retain their validity conditions.

Points are tuples of scalar codes. A geometric assertion becomes a formula
about natural-number codes, with primitive-recursive operations and
quantifier-free induction. A proof about ordinary real numbers in Lean's
ambient logic would be a different assertion; the intended formal target here
is a derivation in the repository's **object theory of iPRA**.

The field K includes algebraic numbers beyond those obtainable by straightedge
and compass. The construction-obstruction chapter restricts the initial frame
to rational coordinates and studies the smaller class generated by successive
quadratic extensions. Thus existence of a coded cube root and impossibility
of constructing that length by the designated geometric operations are
compatible conclusions.

### PR rational observations for circle measurement and volumes

The circumference constant `π_g` is represented by one specified
primitive-recursive recipe returning nested rational intervals of width at
most `2^(-n)` at requested precision `n`. It is **not introduced as another
element of K**. The circle, cone, and sphere formulas mean equality of these
observation names, proved at every requested numerical precision.

The report constructs actual finite polygonal or prism enclosures and supplies
their error bounds. The circle's algebraic brackets have width at most
`2·2^(-k)` for `π_g`; the cone and ball volume brackets have widths at most
`6r²h·2^(-k)` and `12R³·2^(-k)`, respectively. An explicit rationalization
schedule turns them into the requested rational observations. This establishes
the displayed measurements without a general measure or completeness axiom.

`π_g` is geometric π when π is defined through the unit-circle circumference.
An identification with an independently defined analytic sine, integral, or
other transcendental function is an additional theorem, not an implicit step
of this package.

## What the general elimination theorem says

For each **fixed first-order ordered-field formula**, the report gives a
primitive-recursive Boolean decision procedure and a human iPRA proof of its
equivalence with the numerical translation. The elimination argument records
specialized zero coefficients, degree drops, signed remainder queries,
occurring sign patterns, witnesses for existential cases, and counterexamples
for rejected universal cases. Universal quantification is justified directly
by finite-cell coverage.

This is a formula-by-formula metatheorem schema. It explains why a further
fixed semialgebraic geometric theorem has a route into the arithmetic
interpretation. It is not an internal truth predicate or a soundness assertion
for arbitrary arithmetic, and it does not replace the individual geometric
proofs in the report. Families whose polygon size, construction length, or
precision varies receive their own finite-list invariants and induction
arguments.

There is **no universal PR evaluator for arbitrary PR program codes** being
assumed. A rational observation name is a specified recipe, and the algorithms
for scalar records, finite construction lists, polynomial arithmetic, and
bounded program simulation each have their own fixed definitions and bounds.

The final real-name equality example is a **metatheoretic computability
obstruction**. It constructs a uniformly PR family of observation names whose
zero test would decide halting. The impossibility of every total computable
classifier is not counted as a newly kernel-checked iPRA geometric derivation.
It explains why the exact algebraic comparison procedure cannot simply be
extended to arbitrary PR real names.

## Reproducing the computational checks

Run the following from the directory containing this README. Use Python 3.10
or later and the dependencies in `requirements.txt`, which pins SymPy 1.14.0
and Matplotlib 3.10.8. SymPy is used by the circle and observation diagnostics;
Matplotlib is needed only to regenerate the illustration. The
polynomial-normalization and solid-coordinate checkers use the Python standard
library. The main verification command is:

```sh
python3 code/verify.py
```

It runs all four exact check suites and saves their logs under `verification/`,
together with `verification/status.json`. All four suites were executed
successfully during preparation of this package. Individual commands are
also available:

```sh
python3 code/check_certificates.py --output certificates/polynomials.json
python3 code/check_circle_identities.py
python3 code/check_limits.py
python3 code/check_solids.py --output certificates/solids.json
```

The checks have different evidential scopes:

| Check | What it establishes | What still comes from the proofs |
|---|---|---|
| Integer-polynomial normalization | Equality of complete coefficient dictionaries; therefore a universal identity under every assignment in every commutative ring | Geometric meanings, order conditions, nonzero denominators, existence and uniqueness, and the object-theory derivation |
| Symbolic circle diagnostics | The displayed polynomial or denominator-cleared rational identities, sometimes reduced under explicitly supplied polynomial relations | Sign branches, root selection, geometric order, cancellation hypotheses |
| Observation diagnostics | Universal algebraic recurrence identities and exact outward rational checks of the implemented finite precision stages | The all-stage induction, explicit modulus, geometric containment, and equality of observation names |
| Solid-coordinate checker | Exact supporting planes, incidences, and regularity properties of the five supplied finite examples, using rational arithmetic in Q(√5) | The general counting theorem and any global uniqueness or rigidity theorem |

Polynomial normalization is not numerical sampling: it expands and compares
exact integer coefficients. The implementation is nevertheless an ordinary
Python program, not a formally verified proof checker. Finite example checks
and finite precision diagnostics have the narrower scopes stated above; their
success must not be promoted into a universal theorem by extrapolation.

## Rebuilding the PDF

The supplied figures are already included. A reasonably complete TeX Live or
equivalent installation with pdfLaTeX, `latexmk`, AMS packages, Latin Modern,
TikZ, and the packages listed in the preamble can rebuild the report. The main
command regenerates the theorem inventory before compilation:

```sh
bash build_report.sh
```

Once the inventory is current, the underlying LaTeX command is:

```sh
latexmk -pdf -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error report.tex
```

To regenerate the illustration, `python3 code/make_figures.py` additionally
requires Matplotlib. Figure generation is independent of the proof and exact
certificate checks.

After rebuilding the report and running the checks, recreate the release with:

```sh
python3 code/package_release.py
```

This refreshes `SHA256SUMS` and writes the ZIP and a standalone PDF into an
`output/` directory beside the package directory. Temporary build products and
Python bytecode are excluded from the archive.

## Lean status and the concrete build route

**The 42 new candidate declarations were not compiled in the preparation
environment.** Lean/Lake and the compiled dependency cache were unavailable.
The candidate module contains 40 generic integer-coordinate declarations and
two universally closed versions. Their intended conclusion type is
`Γ ⊢[PRAAx] φ`, using the repository's proof-producing `ipra_ring` and
`ipra_zle` machinery.

The geometry-syntax module specifies PR symbols and translated predicates,
but the full algebraic scalar backend is **not instantiated**. The complete
root/sign algorithms, representation laws, number-field certificates,
construction witnesses, observation routines, and full theorem portfolio
remain to be turned into checked object-theory derivations. The absence of
`sorry` and new `axiom` declarations in the candidate sources does not establish
that they elaborate or that the intended claims have been kernel checked.
See `lean/README.md` for the detailed module map and completion sequence.

The dependency pin is exact:

| Dependency | Version |
|---|---|
| ProveIt | `bbb85238dac533fc4b7b84a750cca864fe35fed5` |
| Lean | `leanprover/lean4:v4.32.0` |
| Mathlib, as pinned by that checkout | `81a5d257c8e410db227a6665ed08f64fea08e997` |

Prepare a ProveIt checkout at that commit, with its matching toolchain and
dependencies installed. In that checkout, build the required prerequisites:

```sh
lake build IPRADerivations.Core.IntCertificates IPRADerivations.Core.Definitions
```

Then, from this package directory, run the included build helper:

```sh
python3 lean/build_in_proveit.py --repo /absolute/path/to/pinned/ProveIt
```

`lean/build_in_proveit.py` checks the commit, relevant source cleanliness,
toolchain, and prebuilt dependencies. It compiles only the new packet modules
into a separate `lean/build/` directory and records `build.log` and
`result.json`. It does not install tools, fetch dependencies, build upstream
modules, or modify the upstream checkout. A successful result would establish
compilation of those modules; it would still not complete the unimplemented
algebraic backend or formalize every report theorem.

## Explicit limits of the deliverable

The package does not establish unrestricted geometry over arbitrary subsets
of a complete continuum, a decision procedure for arbitrary computable-real
equalities, or general measure and integration theorems. Its finite polygon
and polyhedral arguments retain the stated certificates. The Platonic-solid
results prove the five admissible face–vertex types and give exact regular
examples; they do not add a full global rigidity or combinatorial uniqueness
classification for every polyhedron of a type. The Desargues result retains
its explicit homogeneous representative conditions.

Circle squaring is not proved impossible here: that conclusion requires the
separate transcendence argument for π. The circle-measurement observations do
not supply that argument. The proof report, executable checks, and Lean
blueprint are intended to make both the established mathematical content and
the remaining formalization work concrete and reproducible.
