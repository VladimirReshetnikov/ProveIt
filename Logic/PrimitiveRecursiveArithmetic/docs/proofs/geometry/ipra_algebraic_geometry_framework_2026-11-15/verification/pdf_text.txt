Abstract
The existing ProveIt iPRA packets develop finite contracts, demand-driven observations,
relational finite-use composition, stable outputs, exact arithmetic, and several Diophantine
constructions. This report adds a specifically algebraic-geometric layer. Mathematical objects
are represented by natural-number codes with proof-relevant equality certificates; quotient rings
use explicit ideal-combination witnesses; localizations use explicit annihilating exponents; and
finite affine covers are carried by unit-ideal certificates rather than by quantification over prime
ideals. The central result is an explicit primitive-recursive Zariski gluing algorithm for modules
on a finite principal-open cover. It works over rings with zero divisors and separates certificate
verification from certificate production.
This provides a finitary semantics for affine schemes, finite affine atlases, quasi-coherent
module data, vector bundles, and finite Čech complexes. As a developed case study, the two-chart
atlas of P1 gives a primitive-recursive normal form for the Čech cohomology of every O(d). The
construction extends to the standard cover of Pn : the complex decomposes by exponent vectors,
mixed-sign summands have an explicit contracting homotopy, and only the all-nonnegative
and all-negative summands survive. Thus the familiar cohomology table is reduced to finite
combinatorics suitable for iPRA.
The archive contains complete human-readable proofs, exact Python validation, source-level
Lean interfaces, and a dependency-ordered route to actual PrfPRAAx terms. The Lean outlines
are not claimed compiler-checked, and no general effective Siegel, Faltings, Mordell–Weil, or
singular Puiseux theorem is claimed.

1

Scope, thesis, and proof status

1.1

What the requested formalization should mean

The phrase “formalize algebraic geometry in iPRA” can be understood in several inequivalent
ways. The strongest and least useful interpretation would demand a single natural number coding
an arbitrary scheme, an internal set of all of its points, and a universal evaluator for every
algorithm mentioned in a textbook. That interpretation is neither necessary nor compatible with
the foundational discipline of primitive recursive arithmetic.
The interpretation adopted here is presentation-relative and theorem-relative:
1. Fix a class of finite codes: polynomials, matrices, finite presentations, local fractions, overlap
maps, and certificates.
2. Fix each primitive-recursive operation before it appears in a theorem. There is no evaluator for
arbitrary encoded programs.
3. Express equality and side conditions through finite witnesses whose checkers are primitive
recursive.
4. Replace an infinite geometric object by the finite algebraic presentation needed by the theorem:
an affine presentation, a finite atlas, a finite cover, a finite Čech complex, or finitely many
observations of a represented object.
5. Prove the finite correctness statement with quantifier-free induction and the ordinary intuitionistic logical rules of iPRA.
A completed formalization is not merely a Lean theorem about standard natural numbers. In the
repository’s notation, its endpoint is a term
Prf PRAAx [] φ,
where φ is the coded first-order formula expressing the advertised result. The existing bridge
pra_iff_native may then transport that derivation to the concise native calculus.

1.2

The central thesis

The topological point-set definition of a scheme quantifies over prime ideals and arbitrary open sets.
Most concrete arguments, however, consume much less information. They use finitely presented rings,
standard opens D(f ), localizations, finitely many overlap maps, and finitely many compatibility
equations. This finite algebraic skeleton is enough for a large body of algebraic geometry.
The central thesis is therefore:
For iPRA, the right primitive notion is not an internally completed prime spectrum. It
is a certified finite affine atlas together with proof-relevant localization and descent data.
The conventional scheme is the external mathematical interpretation of that atlas. The internal
arithmetic proof manipulates only its finite codes and certificates. This is analogous to working with
a finite presentation of a group rather than with a completed set containing all group elements, but it
additionally records the local-to-global structure that distinguishes geometry from bare commutative
algebra.

1.4

What is and is not proved

The ordinary mathematical proofs of the power-cover lemma, localization calculus, principal-cover
gluing, and the projective Čech computations are complete in this report. Their algorithms are
implemented over exact rings in the Python companion and checked on finite test suites, including
a zero-divisor example over Z/12Z.
The supplied Lean files are blueprints. They contain host-level structures, definitions, and named
target propositions, together with the exact object-theory endpoint. The preparation environment
had no Lean executable or local ProveIt dependency closure, so no new compilation claim is made.
The final conversion of the principal theorems to PrfPRAAx remains an engineering task, although
the required primitive recursions and quantifier-free invariants are specified.
The framework does not manufacture a general height bound, a complete rational-point list, a set of
Mordell–Weil quotient representatives, or an exact branch selector for arbitrary Cauchy coefficients.
It makes such data composable and verifiable when supplied; it does not erase the mathematics
needed to produce them.

2.3

Difficulties recorded by theorem-specific packets

The theorem packets identify several logically distinct obstacles.
1. No universal PR evaluator. An inductive Lean datatype of PR programs does not give an
iPRA function evaluating arbitrary program codes. Every evaluator in this package is fixed
syntactically, or a finite trace is supplied and checked.
2. No induction on infinitary predicates. Cauchy convergence, sheafhood over all opens, or
ideal equality by unbounded search cannot be the induction formula. Finite prefix folds and
explicit witnesses replace them.
3. Exact choice can be discontinuous. Singular Puiseux branches and arbitrary Cauchy
coefficient zero tests remain outside a uniform exact interface. Relational chart data or supplied
separation witnesses are required.
4. Cardinality does not locate points. The Faltings and Siegel packets stress that a bound
on the number of rational or integral points is not a height cutoff and does not terminate
enumeration.
5. Descent requires actual representatives. The Mordell–Weil packet can consume a finite
list covering a quotient but does not manufacture rational representatives from abstract Selmer
data.

6. Native Lean is not object iPRA. Several packets check host-level finite arithmetic but do

The geometric framework below resolves none of the genuinely missing height or representative

localizations, finite covers, gluing, and projective cohomology without silently using classical quotient
types, prime spectra, arbitrary choice, or induction beyond iPRA.

2.4

Why the Wikipedia checklist is useful but insufficient

The user-specified overview divides algebraic geometry into polynomial zero sets, affine and projective
varieties, computational methods, and the modern scheme/sheaf viewpoint. Each item requires a
different iPRA representation:
• polynomial zero sets use finite polynomial evaluation and bounded point searches;
• affine geometry uses finitely presented coordinate rings;
• projective geometry uses homogeneous presentations and finite standard charts;
• computational geometry uses certificate-checked reductions rather than an assumed terminating
universal algorithm;
• schemes and sheaves use finite affine atlases and descent equalizers rather than an internally
completed topology of prime ideals.
The rest of the report supplies one common architecture for these representations.

3

A general framework: certified presentations and finite descent

3.1

The five-layer architecture

A reusable embedding of mathematics into iPRA should make the transition from ordinary mathematical language to arithmetic explicit. The following five layers are sufficient for the examples in
this report and subsume the earlier finite-contract methodology.
Layer 1: numerical representation.
Every datum is a natural-number code. Lists, trees, signed integers, polynomials, matrices, and
finite diagrams have primitive-recursive constructors and bounded accessors. Malformed inputs
are handled by total default values; a separate predicate Valid(x) states the intended domain.
Layer 2: proof-relevant equality.
An object code need not have a canonical normal form. Equality is witnessed by a code
w satisfying EqW(x, y, w). The checker is primitive recursive, and fixed primitive-recursive
transformers produce witnesses for reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and congruence.
Layer 3: static algebra and finite diagrams.
Operations are fixed PR functions on codes. A finite diagram consists of finitely many objects,
arrows, and compatibility witnesses. There is no quantification over an open-ended universe of
programs or objects.
Layer 4: observations, covers, and descent.
A theorem asks only for a finite observation: a bounded coefficient identity, a local section on
a finite cover, a finite Čech class, or a finite error bound. Local answers are composed using
explicit compatibility and gluing certificates.

Layer 5: object-theory reflection.
The finite checker and constructor are represented by PR terms. Their loop invariants are
quantifier-free formulas. The result is assembled as a term of type Prf PRAAx Γ φ.
The architecture is deliberately asymmetric. A witness may certify a correct output even when no
uniform procedure can discover that witness from the raw input. This is not a defect: it isolates the
actual mathematical producer theorem.

Algebraic structures without quotient types

A certified ring is a certified setoid equipped with PR operations 0, 1, +, −, · and witness transformers
for congruence. Its ring laws are finite families of equality witnesses. A certified module similarly
supplies addition, negation, scalar multiplication, and their congruence data.
This formulation accommodates several common representations:
• rational numbers as numerator/positive-denominator pairs with cross-multiplication witnesses;
• projective points as primitive coordinate tuples with scaling witnesses;
• quotient rings as polynomial codes with ideal-combination witnesses;
• localization fractions with annihilator witnesses;
• Cauchy names observed only to a finite precision, with quantitative closeness witnesses;
• chain complexes whose equality is coefficientwise certificate equality.
The key gain is uniformity. Once an operation has a congruence transformer, all later constructions
can use it without deciding a quotient equality or selecting a canonical representative.

3.4

Finite diagrams and descent objects

A finite diagram has a natural-number bound r on its objects and finitely coded arrows. Source,
target, identity, and composition are fixed PR functions. Equality of arrows is proof-relevant. A
diagram-valued construction supplies object and arrow codes together with finite commutativity
witnesses.
For geometry, a cover is represented by a finite index set i < r, local objects Ai , overlap objects Aijα ,
and restriction maps. A descent datum is a tuple si with equality witnesses between its restrictions
on each overlap, plus cocycle witnesses where needed. A gluing contract consists of
(a) a PR constructor producing a candidate global code;
(b) PR constructors for the restriction-equality witnesses;
(c) a PR uniqueness transformer taking local equality witnesses to a global one.
The principal-open case will supply all three explicitly. More general finite sites can take them as
verified primitives or reduce them to principal refinements.

3.5

Exact, relational, and approximate outputs

The framework has three output modes.
Exact certificate mode.
A quotient identity, localization equality, or matrix equation is returned with an exact finite
witness. This is the main mode of the algebraic geometry developed below.
Relational finite-use mode.
A producer supplies a finite relation of possible charts, branches, or refinements. A bounded
consumer uses the repository’s finite-use viability construction. The final output must be
branch-invariant or quantitatively stable.
Approximation mode.
A represented infinite object is queried only to finitely many precisions. A modulus, apartness
witness, or error DAG determines the demanded input precision. Exact algebraic conclusions

Certified Zariski descent in iPRA

13/51

may not be inferred without exact separation data.
These modes can be mixed. For example, a singular variety may be covered by a finite relation of
candidate charts, while each chosen chart carries exact polynomial and localization certificates. The
finite-use framework handles chart choice; the certified atlas handles algebraic gluing.

3.6

The no-universal-evaluator discipline

Nothing above licenses a PR function that evaluates arbitrary encoded PR programs. There are
two sound implementation patterns:
1. the theorem fixes a particular function code built from the constructors of PR;
2. the input contains a finite execution trace and a fixed checker verifies each transition.
A degree parameter, number of variables, cover size, or matrix dimension may be an ordinary
numerical input to one fixed evaluator. What is forbidden is replacing that evaluator by a universal
interpreter whose termination or primitive recursiveness has not been established.

4.1

Polynomial codes

Fix a number n of variables. A monomial is a vector α = (α0 , . . . , αn−1 ) ∈ Nn , encoded by a finite

the required sorting/collection procedure has been represented.

a global normal form and extends directly to coefficients in a certified ring.

4.6

Gröbner bases as optional certificates

A Gröbner basis is valuable for producing ideal witnesses and normal forms, but the foundational
interface should not assume that a uniform unbounded Buchberger run is a primitive-recursive
oracle. Three safe modes are available.
Verification mode.
The input contains a proposed basis, representations of its elements in the original ideal,
reduction traces for the original generators, and reductions of all relevant S-polynomials to
zero. A fixed checker verifies Buchberger’s criterion and every identity.
Fuelled mode.
A fixed Buchberger evaluator runs for a supplied bound and returns its finite trace. Soundness
of a successful output is proved; failure to finish within the bound makes no completeness claim.
Bounded-family mode.
For a family with a separately proved degree/size bound, the bound becomes the fuel for a
total primitive-recursive constructor.
This separation matters for computational algebraic geometry. Ideal membership certificates,
Hilbert-function tables, elimination identities, and finite standard-monomial bases can all be checked
in iPRA even when their general-purpose discovery algorithm is not part of the theorem.

8.2

Finite principal-overlap atlases

Pairwise intersections of affine opens need not always be one principal open. The practical extension
replaces fij by a finite family fijα , each giving a principal overlap piece. The data include:
• a unit-ideal or relative-cover certificate that the pieces cover the intended intersection;
• a localization isomorphism on every piece;
• compatibility witnesses on double refinements;
• cocycle witnesses on triple refinements.
The principal-cover gluing theorem proves that chartwise constructions independent of the chosen
piece descend to the whole intersection. Refinement coherence follows from uniqueness. Thus the
additional indexing is finite bookkeeping rather than a new logical principle.

8.3

The internal geometric language

A finite atlas supports the following notions without constructing an internal prime spectrum.
Regular function.
A tuple ai ∈ Ai with localization equality witnesses ϕij (ai ) = aj on overlaps. The principal-cover
theorem glues such tuples whenever one chart is itself presented by a finite principal cover.

Morphism.
A chartwise finite family of presented ring maps in the contravariant direction, together with a
finite target-chart cover and overlap commutativity witnesses. Relational finite-use composition
may choose among several valid target charts; the induced map on an invariant observation is
choice-free.
Open subobject.
A finite family of principal elements on each chart with compatibility certificates under overlap
maps. Union and intersection are computed by concatenation and products of principal
elements.
Closed subobject.
A finite ideal presentation on each chart, stable under localization and equipped with overlap
identities. A global finitely presented closed subscheme is represented by finitely many local
relation lists.
Point over a presented ring R.
A compatible family of certified ring maps Ai → R on a selected chart cover, or more commonly a

coordinates are a compact specialized presentation of the same data.
These are theorem-relative representations. A statement requiring only chartwise equations never
needs to enumerate the topological points of the scheme.

8.4

Atlas morphisms and refinement

A refinement replaces a chart Ai by a finite principal cover. Its certificate contains the unit-ideal
relation and the localization maps. A common refinement of two finite atlases is formed by taking
finite pairwise intersections and then principal refinements. All index bounds are primitive recursive
in the atlas sizes.
Proposition 8.2 (Refinement invariance). Suppose a finite atlas observation is defined by compatible
local data and has uniqueness under the principal-cover equalizer. Then its value is invariant, with
an explicit equality witness, under finite principal refinement.
Proof. Pull the data to the common refinement. Both candidate values have equal restrictions on
every refined principal piece. Apply principal-cover uniqueness chart by chart and then on the outer

constructor.
This proposition supplies the practical replacement for proving equality of two constructions on all
open sets.

8.5

Base change and fiber products

For presented affine rings, tensor products have explicit presentations. If A ← R → B are finitely
presented, then A⊗R B is presented by the combined variables and relations, together with equations
identifying the two images of the generators of R. The construction is primitive recursive on the
finite presentations.

Base-changing an atlas along a presented morphism applies this tensor construction chartwise and
base-changes each localization certificate. Fiber products of finite atlases are obtained by the finite
product of chart index sets. Overlap cocycles are inherited by functoriality of localization and
tensor-product maps. Thus many finite-limit constructions in algebraic geometry fit the same
certificate language.

8.6

Properties as finite certificates

Some geometric properties have direct finite witnesses on a chosen atlas:
• finite presentation: already part of every chart and morphism code;
• separatedness: a finite presentation/certificate for each affine diagonal or, in common cases,
affine pairwise intersections with multiplication-map surjectivity witnesses;
• smoothness of a fixed relative dimension: a finite principal cover on which specified Jacobian
minors are invertible, together with identities certifying the relations and rank;
• finite locally free module of rank d: local bases and inverse matrices on a finite principal cover;
• proper projective presentation: a closed homogeneous presentation inside a fixed projective
space.
These certificates are sufficient conditions with soundness proofs. The framework does not assert
that a uniform primitive-recursive algorithm discovers such certificates for every input presentation.

8.7

Conventional semantics

Externally, Definition 8.1 is interpreted by the standard gluing theorem for schemes. The finite ring
maps determine open immersions of affine schemes, and the inverse and cocycle witnesses provide
the usual gluing datum. Every internal chartwise theorem is sound for the resulting conventional
scheme because its algebraic restriction maps are exactly the localization maps used by the structure
sheaf.
A full formal equivalence between the finite-atlas category and an appropriate category of quasicompact schemes with chosen atlases would be valuable, but it is not required for individual iPRA
theorems. Each theorem can state its atlas hypotheses explicitly and prove the desired finite
observation directly.

Homogeneous coordinates and points

For a field or domain with a suitable exact presentation, a projective point may be represented by

representation are complementary:
• homogeneous coordinates are compact for point enumeration and height arguments;
• affine charts are natural for local rings, functions, sheaves, Jacobians, and descent;
• a point with xi invertible maps to chart i by the ratios xj /xi ;
• two chart descriptions agree by the certified transition maps.
A theorem may use a finite relational choice of a nonzero coordinate. If its final output is projectively

primitive representative over Z can choose the least nonzero coordinate deterministically.

Projective morphisms

A tuple of homogeneous forms F0 , . . . , Fm of one common degree defines a projective morphism on
the locus where they do not vanish simultaneously. A finite certificate for a global morphism on a
projective subscheme consists of:
(a) relation witnesses showing that the image satisfies the target homogeneous equations;
(b) a finite principal-cover certificate that the Fj generate the unit ideal on each source chart, or
equivalent local nonvanishing certificates;
(c) chartwise rational formulas and overlap commutativity witnesses.
Composition substitutes homogeneous forms and multiplies degrees. The relation and base-locus
certificates transform by finite polynomial identities. Height upper bounds are obtained from
coefficient norms exactly as in the Diophantine packet; lower bounds still require gcd/resultant or
base-locus information and are not produced by the atlas formalism alone.

10.4

Jacobian and smoothness certificates

For a closed affine presentation A = R[X]/(g1 , . . . , gs ), the Jacobian matrix is a finite matrix of
formal partial derivatives. A certificate that a chosen d × d minor is invertible on a principal open
consists of a localization inverse witness. A finite list of such minors and a unit-ideal cover certificate
proves that the required rank condition holds everywhere on the chosen atlas.
This gives an iPRA-friendly sufficient interface for smoothness in standard complete-intersection
situations. It does not assert a uniform algorithm deciding smoothness of arbitrary presentations.
The input may carry a Gröbner/elimination certificate producing the relevant Jacobian ideal, or the
minor cover may simply be part of the theorem hypotheses.

10.5

Divisors and line bundles

A Cartier divisor on a finite atlas is represented by local nonzerodivisor fractions gi and unit
transition witnesses gi /gj ∈ O× (Ui ∩ Uj ). The associated line bundle uses those units as rank-one
transition matrices. Tensor product adds divisor data and multiplies transition units; dualization
inverts them on overlaps.
For projective space, the hyperplane divisor gives O(1), and O(d) has transition factor (Xj /Xi )d . All
integer powers are finite localization terms. The projective-line and projective-space computations
below therefore require no abstract Picard group.

10.6

Hilbert functions and finite graded certificates

A homogeneous Gröbner certificate can provide a finite monomial ideal of leading terms. For a fixed
degree D, the standard monomials of degree D are a bounded finite set, and coefficient reduction
proves a spanning or basis statement for the degree-D quotient. Hence the Hilbert function at a
specified degree is a finite iPRA observation.
A claim about the eventual Hilbert polynomial needs an additional stabilization certificate: a bound
D0 , a polynomial P , and finite algebraic data proving the relevant recurrence or graded resolution

30/51

for all D ≥ D0 . The framework checks such a certificate; it does not infer an unbounded stabilization
theorem from a finite table.

10.7

Elimination, images, and fibers

For a presented morphism, an elimination certificate can describe equations for a scheme-theoretic
image or a fiber. Soundness is again finite: substitute the morphism tuple and verify ideal
membership. Completeness of the proposed elimination ideal requires a Gröbner basis, resultant, or
other finite certificate appropriate to the family.
This division of labor is useful in iPRA:
• polynomial substitution and certificate verification are generic;
• the domain-specific algorithm produces the elimination certificate;
• the geometric theorem consumes only the certified presentation of the resulting image or fiber.

10.8

What part of algebraic geometry is thereby accessible?

The finite-atlas language directly supports substantial fragments of:
• affine and projective varieties/schemes of finite presentation;
• regular and rational maps on certified domains;
• finite morphisms and finite module algebras;
• closed immersions, fiber products, and base change;
• vector bundles, line bundles, and finite Čech complexes;
• Jacobian calculations and chartwise smoothness certificates;
• exact finite-field point counting by bounded enumeration;
• rational/integral point searches once an external height bound is supplied;
• graded pieces, Hilbert-function observations, and certificate-relative elimination.
The framework is intentionally less ambitious about arbitrary non-quasi-compact schemes, unbounded
sites, derived categories as completed universes, and existence theorems whose proofs provide no
primitive-recursive quantitative data. Those topics can still have finite theorem-specific observations,
but they are not forced into a single universal code.

11.5

iPRA implementation

A Laurent polynomial code stores a lower exponent, an upper exponent, and a finite coefficient
array, or equivalently a sorted sparse list. Negative exponents are encoded as signed integers. The
normal-form constructor scans the finite support once and writes each coefficient to one of three
output lists according to the decidable comparisons e ≥ 0 and e ≤ d.
A quantifier-free invariant after processing a support prefix states:
(i) the input prefix equals the sum of the partial coboundary and partial residual;
(ii) the chart-zero list contains only nonnegative exponents;
(iii) the chart-one list contains only nonnegative u-exponents;
(iv) the residual contains only exponents d < e < 0.
At the end, coefficientwise uniqueness is proved by a bounded scan of the common finite support
interval. No induction formula quantifies over polynomials or cohomology classes.

• finite maps from exponent vectors to coefficient tuples;
• coefficientwise splitting into all-nonnegative, all-negative, and mixed classes.
All are primitive recursive. To prove (13), an object-iPRA development can avoid abstract matrices.
For each output component and each deletion index, a bounded case split checks whether the deleted
index is i∗ , below it, or above it. A finite accumulator records the paired terms and their signs. The
induction formula is an equality of natural-number-coded finite sums.

12.6

Executable matrix check

The companion program independently constructs the finite matrices of d and h for every proper
nonempty negative-index set in dimensions 1 ≤ n ≤ 6. It verifies dh + hd = I over the integers in
240 mixed-sign cases. This is not a proof for all n, but it checks the exact sign convention and the
finite-index implementation used by the Lean plan.

12.7

Conventional cohomology

The standard cover has affine finite intersections, and O(d) is quasi-coherent. The conventional
comparison theorem therefore identifies the finite Čech calculation with H q (PnR , O(d)). The Stacks
Project gives the same exponent-vector decomposition and insertion homotopy. The new foundational
point is that the entire calculation itself is finite, explicit, and admits a direct route to quantifier-free
induction.

13.2

Integration with Diophantine geometry

The existing Diophantine packet represents rational projective points, heights, polynomial maps,
conics, local congruence obstructions, and finite descent. The atlas layer extends that work in four
ways.
1. Local equations. A projective variety can be dehomogenized on finitely many charts, and
local algebraic manipulations can be glued or compared by localization witnesses.
2. Rational maps with domains. A denominator-nonvanishing condition is represented by a
principal open. Several formulas for the same rational map are compared on overlaps rather
than forced into one global syntactic expression.
3. Line bundles and sections. Height constructions often use homogeneous forms or divisors.
Transition factors and local sections can now be represented and checked on a finite atlas.
4. Local-to-global obstructions. Congruence, real, or p-adic local certificates can be attached
to chart data, while exact algebraic descent witnesses handle the finite overlaps.
A bounded rational-point search on a projective scheme proceeds as before: enumerate primitive
coordinate tuples up to a supplied height, check homogeneous equations, and quotient by projective
scaling. The atlas does not create the height bound. It provides the geometric operations needed to
state and verify local computations around the enumerated points.

Certified Zariski descent in iPRA

13.3

38/51

Faltings and Siegel

The Faltings and Siegel packets correctly distinguish a finite cardinality statement from an effective
height cutoff. The present framework sharpens the interface but does not remove that obstruction.
A positive theorem can have the form:
Given a presented projective curve X, a height bound B, and a certificate proving that
every rational point has height at most B, the bounded enumeration returns a complete
list; every local chart computation and overlap equality is certified by the atlas.
The bounded enumeration, chart conversion, and completeness-from-bound implication are primitive
recursive. The production of B for general high-genus curves is the missing arithmetic theorem. A
count bound, a finite-dimensional span bound, or a point-count estimate over finite fields is not a
substitute.
For Runge-type integral-point problems, explicit pole data and a supplied separation/height estimate
can be attached to the finite projective compactification. This may simplify the exact bookkeeping
of points at infinity, divisors, and local functions, but the quantitative Runge inequality remains the
substantive producer.

13.4

Mordell–Weil descent

The Mordell–Weil packet has an explicit finite-height descent conditional on actual representatives
of the quotient by doubling. A finite group scheme, isogeny, or Selmer computation can now be
presented chartwise with exact localization certificates. This is useful for verifying the descent maps
and local images.
It still does not follow that a finite abstract Selmer class has a rational point representative. The
atlas framework can verify a supplied representative and its compatibility; it cannot create a rational
point from a purely cohomological class without an additional theorem.

13.5

Puiseux expansions and singularities

For exact polynomial coefficients, Newton polygons, finite truncations, and branch equations can be
encoded as finite algebraic certificates. A singularity may be covered by finitely many candidate
charts or blow-up charts; relational finite-use composition can avoid an arbitrary global branch
label.
For arbitrary Cauchy coefficient names, the exact vanishing tests that distinguish singular strata
remain discontinuous. The correct interfaces are:
• a supplied exact algebraic stratum or Newton-polygon certificate;
• a derivative/separation witness giving a regular local branch;
• finite root clusters and branch-invariant observations;
• approximate solution germs with residual bounds.
The finite atlas does not turn approximation into exact equality.

Certified Zariski descent in iPRA

13.6

39/51

Analytic number theory packets

Chebotarev, Siegel–Walfisz, Bombieri–Vinogradov, and related packets require uniform analytic
estimates, exceptional-zero control, field arithmetic, and explicit error terms. Finite algebraic
geometry can organize number fields, finite covers, discriminants, and Frobenius data, but it does
not supply the missing estimates. The no-universal-evaluator and finite-observation disciplines
continue to apply.

13.7

Further algebraic-geometry targets

The framework gives a clear route to several additional iPRA projects.
1. Blow-ups of finitely presented affine schemes. Use the standard finite charts of the Rees
algebra and localization transition maps.
2. Grassmannians. Use Plücker coordinates, finitely many standard minors, and explicit
transition matrices.
3. Finite morphisms and normalization certificates. Present the target algebra as a finite
module with multiplication tables and verify integral equations.
4. Bézout in zero-dimensional certified intersections. Combine resultants or a homogeneous
Gröbner certificate with finite quotient-rank calculations.
5. Riemann–Roch for explicitly presented curves. First formalize divisor/line-bundle Čech
complexes and finite-dimensional normal forms; any general theorem still needs quantitative
algebraic inputs.
6. Cohomology of toric varieties. Fans are finite combinatorial objects, affine toric charts are
monoid algebras, and line-bundle cohomology often reduces to finite lattice-region calculations.
7. Finite descent for vector bundles. Transition matrices and principal-cover gluing already
provide the base layer for explicit projective-module calculations.
The common theme is that the geometric theorem should expose a finite atlas, a finite complex, or
a finite certificate rather than conceal infinitary semantics behind an unexamined object code.

14.1

The formal endpoint

The repository distinguishes three formal systems that must not be conflated. Lean’s ambient type
theory is used to define syntax and metatheorems; the ordinary first-order presentation of iPRA has
primitive-recursive terms PTm, axioms PRAAx, and derivations Prf; and the smaller native scalar
core has its own derivation relation. The checked bridge is of the form

{Gamma : List (Fm PTm n)} {phi : Fm PTm n} :
(Gamma |- [PRAAx] phi) <->
NPrf (Gamma.map NFm.ofPRA) (NFm.ofPRA phi)

Consequently a host theorem such as

The source files in this archive are blueprints. No Lean executable or complete repository dependency

contain no axiom, sorry, or admit; unfinished results are named targets, not asserted theorems.

14.2

Two formalization layers

The proposed development has two deliberately separate layers.
Host certificate layer. A CertSetoid contains numerical codes, a Boolean validity predicate, a
proof-relevant relation eqWxyw, and explicit witness transformers for reflexivity, symmetry, and
transitivity. A certified map carries equality witnesses to equality witnesses. Certified rings and
modules add congruence transformers for their operations. This layer is useful for testing the
interfaces and for proving generic metatheorems, but its quantifiers live in Lean rather than in the
object theory.
Object-iPRA layer. Every numerical operation is replaced by one fixed PR code. Its application
is a PTm; every checker and invariant is an FmPTmn; and all correctness theorems are values of
PrfPRAAx. A theorem schema may fix the definitions of a presented ring or module externally
and remain uniform in ordinary numerical parameters. It must not evaluate arbitrary codes for
primitive-recursive programs, since there is no primitive-recursive universal evaluator for all such
codes.

14.3

Codecs and totalization

All objects of the internal theory are natural numbers. The first module should therefore supply
fixed primitive-recursive codecs for:
• signed integers and finite tuples;
• lists with length, bounded lookup, update, append, map, fold, maximum, and least bounded
search;
• exponent vectors, monomials, sparse polynomials, and Laurent polynomials;
• finite matrices and bit-mask subsets of {0, . . . , n};
• ideal-combination witnesses, localization witnesses, cover certificates, and descent data.
Every operation is total. Malformed input receives a default output together with a failure flag.
Theorems assume the finite validity predicate, so no partial function is hidden in the object language.
The basic reconstruction lemmas have the shape
Valid(x) = 1 −→ decode(encode(x)) = x,
and bounded access lemmas are proved by quantifier-free induction on the list prefix.

15.5

Negative checks and nonclaims

The validation deliberately does not infer an unbounded theorem from a successful finite test.
Specifically:
• testing finitely many rings does not prove the power-cover theorem for every presented ring;
• a bounded point search does not prove absence of points above its cutoff;
• a successful certificate checker does not guarantee that a certificate producer exists for every
true semantic statement;
• native Lean propositions, once added, would not by themselves be PrfPRAAx values;
• the standard Čech computation is not by itself a formalization of all derived-functor sheaf
cohomology inside iPRA.
No claim is made here of general effective Siegel or Faltings, uniform Mordell–Weil generators,
exact singular Puiseux selection from arbitrary Cauchy coefficients, decidable equality of arbitrary
represented reals, or a primitive-recursive universal evaluator.
