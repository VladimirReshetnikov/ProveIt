ARITHMETIC REVIEW FOR THE FINAL REPLAY

Reviewed source: verify_stdlib.py, with its use by verify_offshell.py.

The engine represents all five-variable ordinary numerators, and all
six-variable off-shell numerators, by homogeneous-length exponent tuples.
Every constructor in the supplied computations preserves that length.
Polynomial addition combines equal exponent tuples, multiplication adds
tuples coordinatewise, and coordinate differentiation decreases exactly
one exponent and multiplies its integer coefficient by that exponent.
The off-shell derivative additionally sends d to v and v to w, applying
these polynomial operations without invoking analytic differentiation.

Exact division is valid in either dimension. Graded lexicographic order
is a well-order on the finite set of monomials of degree at most d. On
each subtraction, the current leading monomial disappears, and all new
terms have smaller graded lexicographic order. No degree increases.
There are binomial(d+N,N) possible monomials, with N=5 in the ordinary
engine and N=6 in the off-shell run. This supplies a finite primitive-
recursive bound for the apparent while loop in either configuration.
If a leading monomial is not divisible by the divisor's leading
monomial, exact divisibility is impossible; returning None is sound.
If the remainder vanishes, the accumulated quotient satisfies P=Q*f.

The divisors S, D, and s have leading coefficients +1 or -1. In the
off-shell configuration the divisor d is a single variable, again with
leading coefficient +1. Integer quotient operations therefore remain
exact. Coefficientwise division by two is performed only when every
coefficient is even, and each such iteration decreases its finite
denominator exponent. No generic polynomial gcd is used.

The acceptance checks and critical exponent/divisor validations use
explicit exceptions. They remain effective with Python optimization
enabled. The final suite is replayed with python3 -O; the independent
nonzero controls must still be rejected in that run.

Each script is run in a fresh subprocess by run_all.py. In particular,
the off-shell script's intentional reconfiguration of the global ring
dimension and derivation cannot leak into any other checker.

This review explains the finite algebraic invariants. It is not a
machine-checked verification of the Python implementation or interpreter.
