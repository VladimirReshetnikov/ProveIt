LEAN FORMALIZATION STATUS AND COMPLETION PLAN
============================================

1. What is supplied, and what has actually been checked
------------------------------------------------------

GeometryCore.lean and PascalConic.lean are concrete candidate Lean source.
They contain proofs written with ordinary Mathlib tactics, without proof
holes or new axiom declarations. They have NOT been compiled: no Lean or
Lake executable was present in the authoring environment. They must not be
described as kernel-checked files or as completed formalizations.

The mathematical coordinate identities are established in the accompanying
paper and checked independently by the package's exact polynomial verifier.
The 20 signs in the fixed six-by-six determinant's Laplace expansion were
also compared with the Leibniz determinant on ten exact integer matrices.
These checks do not replace a Lean compilation.

The build metadata targets Lean/mathlib v4.32.0, the versions declared by
the inspected ProveIt revision
0bba41d70f061442357cae0f6de055434a33fb09.

With an installed Lean toolchain, run from this directory:

    lake update
    lake build

The first command downloads dependencies. The build may need generous
memory for the six generic column-replacement ring proofs. Heartbeat and
recursion limits are explicitly raised in the source. If normalization is
too costly, replace these six flat proofs by shared determinant
multilinearity and alternating-column lemmas, or by a proof-producing sparse
polynomial normalization tactic. This is an implementation change, not a
change in mathematical assumptions.

2. Scope of the candidate Lean layer
-----------------------------------

The code proves algebraic statements in Lean's ambient logic over a
commutative ring. Cancellation theorems additionally assume NoZeroDivisors.
The intended arithmetical instance is the integer ring.

The main declarations are:

  DesarguesPascal.desargues_identity:
    the universal degree-twelve identity.

  DesarguesPascal.det_zero_of_kernel:
    the three scalar adjugate identities and a supplied nonzero coordinate
    imply vanishing of a three-by-three determinant.

  DesarguesPascal.desargues_of_center:
    a supplied nonzero center incident with the three vertex joins implies
    the determinant collinearity of the three side-intersection vectors.

  DesarguesPascal.desargues_converse_det:
    nonzero triangle determinants and determinant collinearity imply the
    determinant concurrence of the three vertex joins.

  DesarguesPascal.explicit_axis:
    the cross product of an apart pair of output points is a nonzero line
    incident with all three output points.

  DesarguesPascal.pascal_bracket_identity:
    the Pascal determinant equals the difference of the two bracket
    products printed in the paper.

  DesarguesPascal.pascal_veronese_identity:
    the Pascal determinant equals the six-by-six Veronese determinant,
    with row order (x^2,y^2,z^2,xy,xz,yz).

  DesarguesPascal.column_identity0 through column_identity5:
    replacing column j by the linear combination of all columns with
    coefficients q multiplies the determinant by q_j.

  DesarguesPascal.det6_zero_of_kernel:
    a supplied nonzero coefficient of q and the six equations Mq=0 imply
    det(M)=0. This is the fixed-dimensional adjugate argument expressed
    through determinant multilinearity.

  DesarguesPascal.pascal_of_conic:
    six points on an arbitrary nonzero homogeneous quadratic have Pascal
    determinant zero. Smoothness, irreducibility, a rational
    parametrization, and any nonzero coordinate minor are unnecessary.

The namespace is DesarguesPascal for every declaration above; the file
names specify organization, not additional namespaces.

Apart and Apart6 carry finite disjunctions identifying a nonzero coordinate.
No excluded-middle step is used to manufacture those witnesses in the
generic-ring theorems. For integers the required finite tests are decidable
and primitive recursive.

These algebraic theorems permit zero join/meet vectors. A zero vector is
not a projective point or line. The geometric wrapper must include the
nonzero and apartness hypotheses stated in the paper; it must not silently
interpret every cross product as a projective object.

3. An ambient Lean theorem is not an iPRA derivation
--------------------------------------------------

The files do not yet construct terms of the form

    Gamma |- [PRAAx] phi

in ProveIt's object-language deduction type. An ordinary Lean theorem over
Int, even one proved by `ring`, is not by itself a proof that iPRA derives
its arithmetization. The remaining work is to formalize the elementary
translation supplied in the paper. It requires no new mathematical
principle; it does require explicit syntactic proof terms.

The repository files inspected for the bridge are:

  Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/PR.lean
  Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/Syntax.lean
  Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/
      NativeBiInterpretation.lean
  Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/
      TeqReflect.lean

In namespace LeanProofs.ConciseIPRA, PR.lean defines:

  PR       primitive-recursive function symbols of a fixed arity;
  PTm      first-order terms;
  PRAAx    their defining equations, successor axioms, and quantifier-free
           induction;
  PRA.qfInd  the derived proof constructor for quantifier-free induction.

PR is generated by zero, successor, projections, composition and primitive
recursion. This is a first-order theory of natural numbers, not a theory
quantifying over arbitrary real coordinates, arbitrary rings, or arbitrary
functions.

4. Concrete object-theory implementation tasks
---------------------------------------------

Task A: implement the finitely many arithmetical terms.

Define addition, multiplication, truncated subtraction, integer comparison,
and the fixed finite tuple operations as PR symbols. The usual pair coding
is optional: keeping every signed integer as two separate natural variables
is particularly simple here. There is no need for a universal evaluator for
arbitrary PR programs.

Represent the integer p-n by (p,n). Define:

  (p,n) + (q,m) = (p+q,n+m)
  -(p,n)       = (n,p)
  (p,n)(q,m)   = (pq+nm,pm+nq)
  (p,n) =Z (q,m)  iff  p+m=q+n.

Every equality displayed here is a quantifier-free formula of PRAAx.
No quotient type is needed inside the object theory. Prove congruence for
=Z directly from natural addition and multiplication.

Task B: derive the needed arithmetic laws within PRAAx.

Use PRA.qfInd for natural addition and multiplication laws, together with
the elementary comparison and cancellation laws. All induction formulas
can remain quantifier-free: auxiliary parameters are free variables, and
fixed finite conjunctions of invariants are quantifier-free. This gives
the commutative-ring identities for the signed-pair operations and the
integer cancellation rule

  a !=Z 0,  a*b =Z 0  |-  b =Z 0.

Do not import Int.mul_eq_zero from the ambient library as an object-theory
axiom. Its arithmetical counterpart must be derived from PRAAx. One direct
proof normalizes each pair by truncated subtraction, splits the decidable
sign cases, and uses cancellation/positivity for natural products. The
necessary sign comparisons are primitive recursive.

Task C: reify the fixed coordinate formulas.

Build PTm terms for dot, cross, the 3-by-3 determinant, the six monomials
of the Veronese map, the 6-by-6 determinant, and the three side meets.
All dimensions are fixed. The 6-by-6 determinant is a fixed sum of 720
signed monomials, or the fixed 20-term block expansion used in the code.

Desargues with a supplied center has 42 natural coordinate variables:
seven vectors, three signed coordinates each, two naturals per coordinate.
Pascal with a supplied quadratic has 48 natural coordinate variables:
six point vectors and six signed coefficients. Form the corresponding
quantifier-free implications and then universally close them.

Task D: produce syntactic equality derivations.

There are two clear implementation routes.

  D1. Reify the short algebraic proof in the paper. Derive the cross-product
      identities and determinant identities by repeated substitution of
      the signed-ring laws, then compose them with equality transitivity.
      Every derivation is finite and has the fixed coordinate terms as
      parameters.

  D2. Implement a proof-producing polynomial normalizer. The input language
      only needs integer constants, variables, addition, subtraction and
      multiplication. It is much smaller than PR itself. For each rewrite,
      return a PRAAx proof built from the already proved signed-ring laws.
      Normalize both sides of the identities and compose the results.

For D2, mere equality of coefficient arrays in ambient Lean is insufficient:
the output must be a Prf term proving the signed-pair equality. Polynomial
evaluation and normalization are primitive recursive, but this does not
authorize a reflection principle for the whole object theory. No such
reflection or consistency principle is needed.

Task E: add the finite logical and cancellation steps.

For Desargues, introduce the coordinate witness for the center, use the
three scalar adjugate equalities to derive det(J)*o_i=0, cancel o_i, and
substitute in the Desargues identity. For Pascal, use the supplied nonzero
coefficient q_j. The six conic equations make the column-replaced
determinant zero; its column identity gives q_j*det(M)=0; cancellation and
the Pascal identity conclude the theorem. This is intuitionistic reasoning
with finite disjunction elimination only.

Task F: construct the projective outputs.

Cross products already give explicit integer representatives of every
join and meet. Nonzero tests and comparisons of the three candidate
pairwise crosses give a primitive-recursive choice of an axis. When all
pairwise crosses vanish, choose a perpendicular to a supplied nonzero
point as described in the paper. Include all input validity predicates
in the object-language statement. Do not use an unspecified projective
quotient or an existential choice of a representative.

Task G: transport to the native ConciseIPRA calculus.

Once the ordinary object-theory derivations h have been constructed,
NativeBiInterpretation.lean supplies the exact bridge:

  (Gamma |- [PRAAx] phi)
      <-> NPrf (Gamma.map NFm.ofPRA) (NFm.ofPRA phi).

Thus apply pra_iff_native.mp to h. Its hypotheses are the actual
object-theory proof, not an ambient assertion that phi is true. No new
semantic completeness or reflection theorem is necessary.

5. Verification gates for a completed formalization
--------------------------------------------------

First compile both candidate files and address any elaboration or performance
issue. Inspect the dependency axioms of the principal theorems with
`#print axioms`. Next compile Tasks A--F in a new namespace imported from
ConciseIPRA.PR, checking the final types are Prf/PRAAx statements. Finally
apply the native translation in Task G and build against the recorded
ProveIt revision. At that point one can accurately claim a completed Lean
formalization of the iPRA derivations. This package does not claim that
these gates have already been passed.
