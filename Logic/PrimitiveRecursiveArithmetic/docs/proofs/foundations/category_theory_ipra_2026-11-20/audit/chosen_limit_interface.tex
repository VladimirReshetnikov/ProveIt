\paragraph{An exact object-language interface for a chosen limit.}
For a fixed finite shape $J$, let $D_i(p)$ and $D_u(p)$ be the object and
arrow terms of a parameterized diagram, and let $\operatorname{Diag}(p)$
be its arithmetic validity formula.  Encode a cone's components by a
single tuple code $c$, with PR projections $c_i$.  The formula
\[
\begin{split}
 \operatorname{Cone}(p,x,c)\;\equiv\;& O_C(x)
 \wedge\bigwedge_{i\in\operatorname{Ob}J}H_C(x,D_i(p),c_i)\\
 &\wedge\bigwedge_{u:i\to j\in\operatorname{Arr}J}
 E_C\bigl(x,D_j(p),D_u(p)c_i,c_j\bigr)
\end{split}
\]
contains only number variables.  The conjunctions have fixed finite length;
they are syntax, not an induction principle.  A chosen limit interface is
three fixed PR terms: $L(p)$, a projection tuple $P(p)$ with components
$\pi_i(p)$, and $m(p,x,c)$.  Its laws are iPRA derivations of the following
formulas, universally closed over the displayed numerical variables:
\[
 \operatorname{Diag}(p)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Cone}(p,L(p),P(p)),
\]
\[
\begin{split}
 \operatorname{Diag}(p)\wedge\operatorname{Cone}(p,x,c)
 \longrightarrow\;&H_C(x,L(p),m(p,x,c))\\
 &\wedge\bigwedge_i E_C\bigl(x,D_i(p),
          \pi_i(p)m(p,x,c),c_i\bigr),
\end{split}
\]
\[
\begin{split}
 &\operatorname{Diag}(p)\wedge\operatorname{Cone}(p,x,c)
 \wedge H_C(x,L(p),v)\\
 &\qquad\wedge\bigwedge_i E_C(x,D_i(p),\pi_i(p)v,c_i)
 \longrightarrow E_C(x,L(p),v,m(p,x,c)).
\end{split}
\]
For an empty shape, $c$ and $P(p)$ are empty tuple codes and the empty
conjunction is true; these laws reduce to the chosen-terminal-object laws.
No predicate is assumed decidable.  In Lean, $L,P,m$ are terms of type
\texttt{PTm} of the appropriate arities, the formulas are built by
\texttt{Fm} conjunction, implication, and universal closure, and each law
is a field of type $\Gamma\vdash[\mathrm{PRAAx}]A$ or its closed equivalent.
This representation permits a primitive recursive mediator without a
function-valued input: a cone of a fixed finite diagram is a numerical tuple.
