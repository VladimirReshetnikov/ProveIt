\section{Stable observations, shrinking clouds, and finite consumers}\label{sec:observations}

\subsection{Exact choice erasure}

Let \(R(x,q,a)\) be a bounded total relation and \(O(x,q,a)\) an observer.

\begin{theorem}[Exact stable-observer elimination]\label{thm:stable-exact}
Assume \iPRA\ proves:
\begin{align*}
 P(x,q)&\longrightarrow \exists a<B(x,q)\;R(x,q,a),\\
 P(x,q)\wedge R(x,q,a)\wedge R(x,q,b)&\longrightarrow O(x,q,a)=O(x,q,b).
\end{align*}
Let \(a_*(x,q)\) be the least bounded valid candidate.  Then \iPRA\ proves
\[
 P(x,q)\longrightarrow R(x,q,a_*)
\]
and
\[
 P(x,q)\wedge R(x,q,a)\longrightarrow O(x,q,a_*)=O(x,q,a).
\]
Thus \(O(x,q,a_*)\) is a canonical primitive-recursive output even when no witness is intrinsically canonical.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Booleanize \(R\) and apply least bounded search.  Totality implies that the found flag is one, so search soundness gives \(R(a_*)\).  Instantiate exact stability with \(a_*\) and an arbitrary valid \(a\).  The internal choice has disappeared from the public theorem: changing the search order or producer changes the hidden witness but not the observed value.
\end{proof}

A typical example is a symmetric polynomial in an unordered root list.  The list order is arbitrary, but elementary symmetric functions are stable observers.

\subsection{Diameter-stable observations}

Exact equality is often unnecessary.  Suppose a rational-code pseudometric \(d\) and a bound \(\delta(x,q)\) satisfy
\[
 P(x,q)\wedge R(a)\wedge R(b)
 \longrightarrow d(O(a),O(b))\le\delta(x,q).
\]
The least valid candidate again gives a canonical program output.  The theorem now says that it is within \(\delta\) of every valid realization, not that all realizations are equal.

\begin{theorem}[Approximate choice erasure]\label{thm:stable-approx}
Under bounded totality and the diameter hypothesis, \iPRA\ derives
\[
 P(x,q)\wedge R(x,q,a)
 \longrightarrow d(O(x,q,a_*),O(x,q,a))\le\delta(x,q).
\]
If a downstream observer is uniformly stable with modulus \(m\), the composite error is obtained by the corresponding demand transformation and triangle inequality.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Search soundness proves \(R(a_*)\); instantiate the diameter theorem at \((a_*,a)\).  For composition, request enough upstream precision that the observer's modulus turns the upstream diameter into the downstream tolerance, and combine local error inequalities.  All sums and comparisons are rational-code arithmetic.
\end{proof}

\subsection{From clouds to a regular name}

Pointwise approximate answers do not automatically form a coherent sequence.  Let \(C_q\) be a nonempty finite cloud of valid observations at level \(q\).  Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item \(\operatorname{diam}(C_q)\le\delta_q\);
  \item there exists at least one compatible edge between \(C_q\) and \(C_{q+1}\), and every valid edge \((u,v)\) satisfies \(d(u,v)\le\mu_q\);
  \item a primitive-recursive tail modulus \(T(k)\) certifies
  \[
    \sum_{q=T(k)}^{m-1}\bigl(\delta_q+\mu_q+\delta_{q+1}\bigr)\le 2^{-k}
    \quad\text{for every }m>T(k).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
The finite sums are exact rational-code inequalities; a geometric or telescoping closed form normally supplies the tail certificate.

Choose any primitive-recursive valid representative \(a_q\in C_q\), for example the least one.  Independent choices at adjacent levels need not form an edge.  Nevertheless, select for the proof one valid edge \((u_q,v_{q+1})\).  Then
\begin{align*}
 d(a_q,a_{q+1})
 &\le d(a_q,u_q)+d(u_q,v_{q+1})+d(v_{q+1},a_{q+1})\\
 &\le \delta_q+\mu_q+\delta_{q+1}.
\end{align*}
The edge is used only existentially in the proof; the output algorithm may keep choosing least representatives.

\begin{theorem}[Shrinking-cloud name extraction]\label{thm:shrinking}
Under assumptions (a)--(c), the subsequence
\[
 r_k=a_{T(k)}
\]
is a regular Cauchy name in the sense that, for \(\ell\ge k\),
\[
 d(r_k,r_\ell)\le 2^{-k},
\]
provided \(T\) is nondecreasing.  All values and the modulus are primitive recursive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For consecutive levels, use the preceding three-term estimate.  For \(n<m\), repeatedly apply the triangle inequality to obtain
\[
 d(a_n,a_m)\le
 \sum_{q=n}^{m-1}(\delta_q+\mu_q+\delta_{q+1}).
\]
Set \(n=T(k)\) and \(m=T(\ell)\); monotonicity gives \(m\ge n\), and the tail certificate bounds the sum by \(2^{-k}\).  The finite telescoping proof is a bounded loop with a QF rational-inequality invariant, so it is admissible in \iPRA.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why a diameter estimate alone is not enough}

Even if \(\delta_q\to0\), the centers of the clouds may oscillate.  Motion control is required.  Conversely, edge motion without shrinking diameter does not make independent choices close.  The three-term budget in \cref{thm:shrinking} isolates the exact information needed: within-level ambiguity, one-step motion, and the next within-level ambiguity.

\subsection{Metastability as a final interface}

A metastable statement has the form
\[
  \forall k,F\;\exists n\le B(k,F)\;
  \forall i,j\in[n,F(n)]\ d(x_i,x_j)\le 2^{-k}.
\]
For fixed \(k,F\), the witness \(n\) is a bounded search problem.  Booleanize the finite window condition and use \cref{thm:least-search}.  This produces a valid finite-use contract.

It is generally invalid to infer a global Cauchy modulus \(N(k)\) from metastability alone.  A finite consumer, however, only supplies finitely many control functions \(F\) and tolerances.  The framework may propagate these requests backward and search the required windows.  Thus metastability is not a failure: it is a different, weaker interface that composes with finite consumers.

\begin{barrier}[Metastability is not a modulus]\label{barrier:metastability}
There is no general uniform transformation taking an arbitrary computable metastability bound for a sequence to a computable Cauchy modulus for that sequence.
\end{barrier}

\begin{proof}[Reason]
Computable sequences can converge without a computable modulus.  For each fixed finite control function, a finite stable window may still be found effectively.  A uniform transformation would turn these window procedures into a modulus and contradict the known computability separation.  In the present framework the conclusion is operational: retain the metastable contract unless an explicit tail argument such as \cref{thm:shrinking} is supplied.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stable labels under a gap}

Suppose finitely many true scores \(s_i\) have a unique minimum \(s_{i_*}\) and a certified gap
\[
 s_j-s_{i_*}\ge\gamma>0\qquad(j\ne i_*).
\]
If approximations \(\tilde s_i\) have error at most \(\varepsilon\) with \(2\varepsilon<\gamma\), then the least approximate minimizer is \(i_*\).  Indeed,
\[
 \tilde s_j-\tilde s_{i_*}
 \ge (s_j-\varepsilon)-(s_{i_*}+\varepsilon)
 \ge\gamma-2\varepsilon>0.
\]
This elementary estimate is the generic guarded-label theorem.  It applies to root matching, eigenvalue ordering, pivot choice, chart choice, nearest-cell selection, and sign determination.  At \(\gamma=0\), the exact label is removed from the general interface rather than chosen discontinuously.
