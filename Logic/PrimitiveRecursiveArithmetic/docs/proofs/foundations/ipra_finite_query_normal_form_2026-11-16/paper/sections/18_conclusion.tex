\section{Conclusion}

The large collection of iPRA proof packets has already discovered the right
broad philosophy: mathematics must be presented through finite computation,
certificates, moduli, and stable observations.  The next advance is not another
name for a contract graph.  It is a common object-arithmetic normal form that
connects those designs to the checked \(\Prf\,\PRAAx\) backend.

Finite-query normal form supplies that connection.  It fixes the consumer's
query, packs all finite evidence into one bounded code, verifies it by one fixed
reflected checker, selects the least accepted code by checked bounded search,
and extracts the theorem inside iPRA.  Mixed-radix master codes flatten arrays,
paths, tables, and fixed proof graphs.  Whole-path certificates remove hidden
finite choice.  Cross-certificate stability removes unnecessary coherent
representatives.  Explicit gaps guard exact labels.  Boundary premises isolate
the deep mathematics that no generic compiler can produce.

The proposal is intentionally conservative in its formal claims.  Its human
proofs show that the architecture is sound and broadly applicable; its exact
reference programs test the finite algorithms; its Lean files mark a short,
specific route to kernel checking.  The decisive criterion for future work is
unchanged: a completed application ends with a declaration whose type is
literally an iPRA derivation, and its axiom audit says exactly what the
metatheory used.
