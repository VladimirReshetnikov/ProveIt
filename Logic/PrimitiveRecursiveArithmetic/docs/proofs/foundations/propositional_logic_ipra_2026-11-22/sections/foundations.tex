\section{Scope, meaning, and verification status}\label{sec:scope}

The starting point is the article \emph{Propositional logic}, together with its
mathematically relevant links concerning formulas, truth functions, natural
deduction, Hilbert systems, analytic tableaux, sequent calculi, Boolean algebra,
SAT, and intuitionistic propositional logic. The source map in the appendix
distinguishes these topics from supporting developments such as interpolation
and Horn satisfiability. Historical and philosophical claims are outside our
proof inventory. All mathematical proofs below are supplied here; a reference
is not being used as a substitute for a proof.

There are two different logical levels. At the \emph{object level}, \CPC\ is a
calculus whose rules include classical reasoning, while \IPC\ is the
intuitionistic propositional calculus. At the \emph{metalevel}, the theory in
which we justify operations on their finite syntax is \ipra. The assertion
that a Boolean valuation gives $p\vee\neg p$ the value one is an equation
between primitive recursive numerical functions. It is not an assertion of
$P\vee\neg P$ for an arbitrary arithmetic proposition $P$.

\paragraph{What is established.}
The paper gives constructive, human-readable proofs with algorithms and
primitive recursive bounds. Each algorithmic assertion has a numerical
formulation using codes, and every induction is either quantifier-free or
explicitly reducible to quantifier-free induction by bounded reflection.
This constitutes a non-formalized proof in the stipulated theory. The
accompanying Lean files are a formalization starting point and a detailed
implementation plan. They are \emph{not} a completed, kernel-checked
formalization of all these arithmetized theorems. The supplied validation
record states exactly which programs were executed and whether Lean was
available. Executable tests corroborate implementations; they do not establish
universal mathematical correctness.

\paragraph{A useful uniform contract.}
Many results take the following stronger form than bare decidability. There
are explicitly described primitive recursive functions $D,W$ and primitive
recursive predicates $\mathrm{Good},\mathrm{Positive},\mathrm{Negative}$ such that
\[
 \ipra\vdash \forall x\bigl(\mathrm{Good}(x)\to
 [(D(x)=1\wedge\mathrm{Positive}(x,W(x)))\vee
  (D(x)=0\wedge\mathrm{Negative}(x,W(x)))]\bigr).
\]
The witnesses are proofs, valuations, clause refutations, or finite Kripke
models. Thus a negative answer has explicit mathematical content.

\section{The arithmetic base and finite data}\label{sec:base}

\subsection{An explicit presentation of iPRA}

The language has one sort of numbers, equality, $0$, successor $S$, and a
function symbol for each finite primitive recursive definition. The initial
definitions are zero, successor, and projections; composition and primitive
recursion form new definitions. With the recursion variable first, the defining
equations are
\[
 f(0,\vec x)=g(\vec x),\qquad
 f(Sn,\vec x)=h(n,f(n,\vec x),\vec x).
\]
The nonlogical axioms are these equations, $Sx=Sy\to x=y$,
$Sx=0\to\bot$, and the quantifier-free induction scheme, with parameters,
\[
 Q(0,\vec z)\wedge\forall n\,[Q(n,\vec z)\to Q(Sn,\vec z)]
       \ \to\ \forall n\,Q(n,\vec z).
\]
The logical rules are ordinary intuitionistic first-order natural deduction
with equality: assumption, introduction and elimination for $\wedge,\vee,\to$,
falsehood elimination, quantifier introduction and elimination with their
usual fresh-variable conditions, reflexivity, and equality substitution.
There is no unrestricted excluded-middle rule, double-negation elimination,
choice axiom, or unbounded minimization principle.

This agrees with the definition inspected in ProveIt's
\texttt{ConciseIPRA/PR.lean}. Its syntax is called \texttt{PR},
\texttt{PTm}, and \texttt{Fm}; its nonlogical theory is \texttt{PRAAx}; a
derivation is \texttt{Prf PRAAx}. We use this interface rather than changing
the underlying arithmetic theory.

\subsection{Bits, bounded quantifiers, and bounded witnesses}

A bit is a number equal to zero or one. For bits put
\[
 \overline b=1\mathbin{\dot-}b,\quad b\mathbin{\&}c=bc,\quad
 b\mathbin{|}c=1\mathbin{\dot-}(1\mathbin{\dot-}b)(1\mathbin{\dot-}c),
 \quad b\Rightarrow_b c=\overline b\mathbin{|}c.
\]
Here $\dot-$ is truncated subtraction, defined by primitive recursion.
The equality test $[x=y]$ and comparisons are primitive recursive and have
their familiar arithmetic specifications. Their specifications follow by
quantifier-free induction on predecessor, subtraction, and comparison.

\begin{lemma}[Finite Boolean reflection]\label{thm:bounded-reflection}
For every fixed bounded arithmetic formula $R(\vec x)$ built from primitive
recursive predicates, there is a primitive recursive bit $\chi_R(\vec x)$
such that \ipra\ proves
\[
 \chi_R=1\ \leftrightarrow\ R,\qquad
 \chi_R=0\ \leftrightarrow\ \neg R,\qquad R\vee\neg R.
\]
If $R(i,\vec x)$ is such a predicate, there is a primitive recursive
$\operatorname{first}_R(B,\vec x)\le B$ which is the least witness below $B$
when one exists, and is $B$ otherwise.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Atomic equality and order have characteristic bits. For conjunction,
disjunction and negation use the bit operations above. For implication use
$\Rightarrow_b$; its correspondence with the logical implication is justified
by splitting on the already proved bit for its antecedent. This uses
decidability of the particular bounded formula, not excluded middle for an
arbitrary proposition. For bounded quantification define
\[
 \operatorname{All}(B)=\prod_{i<B}\chi_R(i),\qquad
 \operatorname{Some}(B)=1\mathbin{\dot-}\prod_{i<B}(1\mathbin{\dot-}\chi_R(i)).
\]
The empty product is one. Define $w(0)=0$ and
\[
 w(k+1)=
 \begin{cases}
 w(k)&w(k)<k,\\
 k& w(k)=k\text{ and }\chi_R(k)=1,\\
 k+1& w(k)=k\text{ and }\chi_R(k)=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The simultaneous invariant says $w(k)\le k$, that $w(k)<k$ gives a true
instance there, and that every instance at $i<w(k)$ is false. Encode the last
assertion by its bounded product, and use quantifier-free induction on $k$.
The three clauses of the recursion establish the induction step directly.
It follows that $\operatorname{Some}(B)=1$ exactly when $w(B)<B$ and that
$w(B)$ is a witness in this case. The analogous least-failure argument gives
the universal-quantifier clause. These constructions also establish the
zero cases and the claimed disjunction. A structural induction on the
\emph{fixed} syntax of $R$ completes the compilation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The permitted induction convention]\label{thm:bounded-induction}
Induction on any fixed bounded formula is derivable in \ipra: apply
quantifier-free induction to $\chi_R(n,\vec x)=1$ and use reflection.
Finite case distinctions, a least member of a nonempty bounded set, and
bounded simultaneous choices are therefore legitimate.
\end{corollary}

This convention matters throughout the paper. A phrase such as ``for every
entry of this table'' always has a displayed finite table as its bound.
We never apply induction to a formula containing an unbounded existential
claim such as ``there exists a proof''. Instead a proof-producing algorithm
is constructed, and the induction invariant says that its current output
passes the primitive recursive proof checker.

\subsection{An entirely numerical representation}

One possible pairing, compatible with a construction already present in the
repository, is
\[
 \pi(a,b)=(a+b)^2+a.
\]
It is injective: if $s=a+b$, then $s^2\le\pi(a,b)<(s+1)^2$, so $s$ is the
integer square root; then $a=\pi(a,b)-s^2$ and $b=s-a$. For arbitrary input
$z$ the proposed decoder uses $a=z\mathbin{\dot-}s^2$ and
$b=s\mathbin{\dot-}a$ before testing that $\pi(a,b)=z$. Integer square root
is a bounded search below $z+1$. Its uniqueness and these inverse equations
are proved with bounded arithmetic. Numbers outside the image are detected
by re-encoding their proposed components. Code lists by $[]:=0$ and
$a::t:=1+\pi(a,t)$. The tail of a nonempty coded list is smaller than its
code. Membership, length, lookup with a default, append, and bounded scans
are consequently primitive recursive by a fuel counter no larger than the
input code. This is an intentionally simple coding, not a space-efficient
one.

For arrays of a specified length $r$, whose entries are below $B$, it is more
convenient to use base $B+1$ digits and retain $r$ explicitly. Subsets of an
$m$-element set are bit masks below $2^m$. Finite relations are bit masks
on a Cartesian product. Ordered tuples, matrices and proof annotations are
combinations of these encodings. All tests mentioned below include validity
checks on their codes; ill-formed inputs return a fixed default and are
excluded from correctness statements by a validity hypothesis.

\begin{lemma}[Finite ascending closure]\label{thm:finite-closure}
Let $U$ be a coded universe with $N$ elements. Suppose a primitive recursive
operator $F$ maps subsets of $U$ to subsets, is monotone, and satisfies
$X\subseteq F(X)$. Then
\[
 X_0=\varnothing,\quad X_{i+1}=F(X_i)
 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad X_N=X_{N+1}.
\]
The resulting set is the least $F$-closed set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every unequal consecutive pair adds at least one previously absent element,
so $|X_{i+1}|\ge |X_i|+1$. If equality occurs, determinism makes all subsequent
sets equal. If equality has not occurred before stage $N$, the cardinality at
stage $N$ is at least $N$, hence that set is all of $U$ and is fixed as well.
Cardinalities are sums of bits. The argument is an induction on a bounded
arithmetic invariant. If $Y$ is closed, induction using monotonicity gives
$X_i\subseteq Y$, proving leastness.
\end{proof}

\section{Formulas, substitution, and Boolean semantics}\label{sec:syntax}

\subsection{Syntax and primitive recursive recursion on formulas}

The primitive formulas are
\[
 A::=\bot\mid p_i\mid(A\wedge A)\mid(A\vee A)\mid(A\to A),\quad i\in\N.
\]
Define $\neg A:=A\to\bot$, $\top:=\bot\to\bot$, and
$A\leftrightarrow B:=(A\to B)\wedge(B\to A)$. Formulas are finite trees.
The size $|A|$ counts their primitive nodes. The finite support
$\operatorname{Var}(A)$ is the duplicate-free list of atom indices occurring
in $A$; $\operatorname{Sub}(A)$ is its duplicate-free list of subformulas.
For a finite context $\Gamma$, take unions over its entries.

For definiteness use codes
\[
 \ulcorner\bot\urcorner=0,\quad \ulcorner p_i\urcorner=5i+1,\quad
 \ulcorner A\circ B\urcorner=5\pi(\ulcorner A\urcorner,\ulcorner B\urcorner)+t_\circ,
 \quad (t_\wedge,t_\vee,t_\to)=(2,3,4).
\]
The tags are disjoint. Both child codes are strictly smaller than the parent
code. To evaluate a recursively defined operation on a formula of code $a$,
fill a table of values at all codes $0,\ldots,a$; an invalid code gets a
default. A primitive recursion on the table index does the job. This is
course-of-values recursion implemented by ordinary primitive recursion,
not an extra recursion principle. If an operation constructs new formulas,
store their numerical codes in the same table; no a priori elementary
bound on those numerical values is needed for primitive recursion.

\begin{theorem}[Unique parsing and effective syntax]\label{thm:syntax}
Formula validity, constructor recognition, child extraction, size, support,
subformula closure, and simultaneous substitution are primitive recursive.
Constructor cases are unique, and substitution satisfies
\[
 A[\mathrm{id}]=A,\qquad (A[\sigma])[\tau]=A[\lambda i.\sigma(i)[\tau]].
\]
Only the finitely many values of a substitution at atoms in the input are
needed. A coded finite table supplies them; unlisted atoms are left unchanged.
To represent the composition as a total substitution with that default,
use the union of the two explicit domains and substitute into each of the
first table's images; outside that union both substitutions act identically.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The residue modulo five determines the constructor. The inverse pairing test
checks the binary arguments, and their smaller codes allow the validity table
to be computed from left to right. Support and subformulas are built by
finite unions. Substitution copies each connective and replaces each variable
by its table entry. The two identities hold at variables and falsehood and
are preserved by each binary constructor. Formally the induction invariant
is the conjunction of the claimed equations for all valid subcodes up to the
current index, expressed as a bounded product. This explains exactly how
structural induction is compiled into the permitted numerical induction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Truth tables with finite support}

Fix a duplicate-free support list $V=(v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1})$ covering all formulas
under discussion. A valuation is a mask $a<2^n$; its $j$th digit is the value
of $p_{v_j}$. Define $\operatorname{ev}(a,\bot)=0$, evaluate atoms by lookup,
and use $\&,|,\Rightarrow_b$ at the three binary constructors.

\begin{theorem}[Locality, substitution, and congruence]\label{thm:eval-subst}
Evaluation is a primitive recursive bit. Valuations agreeing on
$\operatorname{Var}(A)$ give the same value to $A$. For a substitution $\sigma$,
\[
 \operatorname{ev}(a,A[\sigma])
   =\operatorname{ev}(b,A),\qquad b(i)=\operatorname{ev}(a,\sigma(i)).
\]
Pointwise equivalent formulas may be replaced inside any formula context.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a variable the assertions are the definition of lookup and of $b$.
At falsehood both sides are zero. At a binary constructor the two induction
hypotheses identify its argument bits, hence also the result of its specified
Boolean operation. The same reasoning on a formula with one marked hole
proves replacement. All valuation tables involved are finite; enlarge the
support by the finitely many variables in the substituted formulas first.
\end{proof}

Put $\operatorname{Sat}_\Gamma(a)=\prod_{B\in\Gamma}\operatorname{ev}(a,B)$.
Define
\[
\begin{split}
 \Gamma\models_b A&\ :\!\Longleftrightarrow\
   \prod_{a<2^n}(\operatorname{Sat}_\Gamma(a)\Rightarrow_b
                         \operatorname{ev}(a,A))=1,\\
 \operatorname{SAT}(A)&=1\mathbin{\dot-}\prod_{a<2^n}(1\mathbin{\dot-}\operatorname{ev}(a,A)),\\
 \operatorname{TAUT}(A)&=\prod_{a<2^n}\operatorname{ev}(a,A).
\end{split}
\]
The choice of a larger covering support changes none of these properties:
restricting a row preserves its evaluations, and extending a smaller row by
zeroes supplies an inverse witness. These are finite numerical definitions
of satisfiability, validity, consequence and equivalence.

\begin{theorem}[Truth-table decision with witnesses]\label{thm:truth-decision}
Satisfiability, tautologicity, equivalence, and finite consequence are
primitive recursively decidable in \ipra. A failed tautology or consequence
test returns a countervaluation; a successful satisfiability test returns a
satisfying valuation. A formula is exactly one of contradictory, tautological,
or contingent. Furthermore,
\[
 \Gamma\models_b A\ \leftrightarrow\
 \models_b(\bigwedge\Gamma)\to A,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TAUT}(A)=1\ \leftrightarrow\ \operatorname{SAT}(\neg A)=0.
\]
Here the empty conjunction is $\top$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use bounded reflection and least search on the $2^n$ rows. A failed product
has a zero factor, and the corresponding row is the required witness. The
three-way classification uses the two decidable bits ``some row is one''
and ``some row is zero''. They cannot both be zero because there is at least
one row, even when $n=0$. The two displayed equivalences follow row by row
from the definition of Boolean implication and negation. This also proves
that finite consequence is reflexive, monotone in premises, and transitive:
on a row satisfying the original premises, successively apply the relevant
implication tests.
\end{proof}

With formulas stored in postorder, one row takes a scan of their nodes;
the truth-table procedure uses $2^n$ such scans. This is a bound on Boolean
operations. A concrete bit-machine implementation adds polynomial coding
overhead; no claim of unit-cost arithmetic on arbitrary integers is needed.

\section{Natural deduction and constructive classical completeness}\label{sec:nd}

\subsection{The two calculi and their proof checkers}

Contexts are finite lists, with unused and repeated assumptions permitted.
The rules of \IPC\ are the following; all premises have the indicated common
context, and $A,\Gamma$ denotes context extension:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \dfrac{A\in\Gamma}{\Gamma\vdash A}\quad
 \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash\bot}{\Gamma\vdash A}\quad
 \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash A\quad\Gamma\vdash B}{\Gamma\vdash A\wedge B}\quad
 \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash A\wedge B}{\Gamma\vdash A}\quad
 \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash A\wedge B}{\Gamma\vdash B},\\[5pt]
 \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash A\vee B}\quad
 \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash B}{\Gamma\vdash A\vee B}\quad
 \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash A\vee B\quad A,\Gamma\vdash C\quad B,\Gamma\vdash C}
       {\Gamma\vdash C},\\[5pt]
 \dfrac{A,\Gamma\vdash B}{\Gamma\vdash A\to B}\quad
 \dfrac{\Gamma\vdash A\to B\quad\Gamma\vdash A}{\Gamma\vdash B}.
\end{gathered}
\]
\CPC\ adds $\Gamma\vdash A\vee\neg A$. Equivalently one may add the
classical case rule with branches $A,\Gamma\vdash C$ and
$\neg A,\Gamma\vdash C$; excluded middle followed by disjunction elimination
derives that rule, and its two disjunction-introduction branches derive
excluded middle.

A proof is a finite tree whose nodes contain a rule tag, a context, a
conclusion, and its child proofs. The checker tests the exact local shape,
assumption membership, matching contexts, and the recursive validity of
the children. This yields primitive recursive bits
$\operatorname{Check}_I(d,\Gamma,A)$ and
$\operatorname{Check}_C(d,\Gamma,A)$. For computationally shared proofs,
a list of earlier-node references is an equivalent representation: every
reference must be strictly smaller than the current node index. Expanding
a well-formed such list into a tree is a primitive recursive operation.
Write $\Gamma\vdash_I A$ as an abbreviation for the existence of a checked
proof, and likewise for $C$. These existence statements themselves need
not be used as induction formulas.

\begin{theorem}[Structural proof transformations]\label{thm:structural}
Both calculi admit primitive recursive proof transformations for weakening,
exchange, contraction, simultaneous substitution, and cut. In particular,
\[
 \Gamma,A\vdash_* B\ \leftrightarrow\ \Gamma\vdash_* A\to B,
 \qquad *=I,C.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For weakening, recursively change every node's ambient context and retain
its discharge assumptions. Membership remains valid; each other local
rule is unchanged. Exchange and contraction follow because membership is
insensitive to order and repetition. For substitution replace every formula
at every node; the syntax identities of Theorem~\ref{thm:syntax} show that
each rule instance remains an instance, including excluded middle in $C$.
For cut, given proofs of the needed hypotheses from $\Delta$, traverse the
proof from $\Gamma$, replace each hypothesis leaf by its supplied proof,
and rebuild the rules. Under a discharged assumption extend the assignment
of hypothesis proofs by the new hypothesis leaf and weaken the old ones.
This handles implication introduction and both disjunction branches without
variable capture, since propositional syntax has no binders. The output
checker succeeds by a bounded induction over the traversal. The forward
deduction implication is implication introduction. For the reverse, weaken
a proof of $A\to B$ to $A,\Gamma$ and apply it to the assumption $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Boolean soundness and consistency]\label{thm:bool-sound}
For every proof code $d$,
\[
 \operatorname{Check}_*(d,\Gamma,A)=1\ \Longrightarrow\ \Gamma\models_b A,
 \qquad *=I,C.
\]
Consequently no checked closed proof of $\bot$ exists in either calculus.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a covering support for all formulas in the proof. Verify at each proof
node and each of the finitely many valuation rows that truth of its context
implies truth of its conclusion. Assumptions and the two conjunction
projections are immediate. Conjunction introduction multiplies two ones;
disjunction introduction gives a one when either input is one. In
disjunction elimination, the disjunction's bit determines a true branch,
and the corresponding child invariant yields the conclusion. For
implication introduction, split on the antecedent bit; when it is zero the
implication is true, and when it is one use the premise under the extended
context. Implication elimination is the two-bit truth table. Falsehood
elimination has no row with true falsehood. Excluded middle has bit one
because its input bit is either zero or one. This establishes every rule
using finite bit cases. Induction on the proof traversal, with all row
conditions packed into a bounded product, finishes the proof. The empty
context is true on its unique zero-variable row but $\bot$ is false there.
\end{proof}

Consistency here is consistency of the two \emph{propositional} proof
checkers. It is not a proof of $\operatorname{Con}(\ipra)$ or of any stronger
arithmetic theory.

\subsection{A proof constructor from each truth table}

For a valuation $a$ on $V$ let $L_a$ consist of $p_i$ when that atom's bit is
one and of $\neg p_i$ when it is zero.

\begin{lemma}[The signed evaluation lemma]\label{thm:signed-eval}
There is a primitive recursive procedure giving an \IPC\ proof
\[
 L_a\vdash_I A\quad\text{if }\operatorname{ev}(a,A)=1,
 \qquad
 L_a\vdash_I\neg A\quad\text{if }\operatorname{ev}(a,A)=0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a variable use its literal assumption. For falsehood prove
$\bot\to\bot$ by assumption and discharge. For a true conjunction obtain
both conjuncts recursively and introduce conjunction. For a false
conjunction choose a false conjunct by a bit test; assuming the conjunction,
project that conjunct and contradict its recursively obtained negation.
For a true disjunction introduce a recursively proved true disjunct. For a
false disjunction both disjuncts are false, so an assumed disjunction gives
falsehood in either branch.

For a true implication $B\to C$, either $C$ is true, when its recursive
proof can be weakened under $B$, or $B$ is false, when its recursive
negation contradicts an assumed $B$ and falsehood elimination gives $C$.
Discharge $B$. For a false implication the bits of $B,C$ are respectively
one and zero. Obtain $B$ and $\neg C$ recursively; an assumed $B\to C$
then yields $C$ and falsehood, hence its negation. Every recursive choice
is controlled by a computed bit. The proof does not use excluded middle
as an \IPC\ rule.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive classical completeness]\label{thm:cpc-complete}
For finite $\Gamma,A$,
\[
 \Gamma\models_b A\quad\leftrightarrow\quad \Gamma\vdash_C A.
\]
More precisely, a primitive recursive algorithm returns either a checked
\CPC\ proof of the sequent or a valuation satisfying $\Gamma$ and falsifying
$A$. Correctness of this disjunction is provable in \ipra.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the finite consequence test fails, return its least failing row. Suppose
it succeeds. For each valuation row $a$, work under $\Gamma,L_a$. If $A$
is true there, weaken the signed evaluation proof of $A$. If $A$ is false,
success of the consequence test means some $B\in\Gamma$ is false. Find its
least index, obtain $\neg B$ by the signed evaluation lemma, combine with
the context assumption $B$, and infer $A$ by falsehood elimination.
Thus every full literal context has a proof of $A$.

Now combine the two rows differing in the last atom by the classical case
rule, discharging that atom's positive and negative literal. Combine the
resulting pairs for the preceding atom, and continue through all $n$
atoms. The final proof has context $\Gamma$. This is a full binary recursion
of depth $n$, equivalently a bottom-up pass on a table with fewer than
$2^{n+1}$ nodes. A proof of each leaf was constructed, not merely asserted
to exist. Hence the entire pass is primitive recursive and its correctness
invariant is the bounded conjunction of the checker bits of its entries.
The reverse implication is Boolean soundness.
\end{proof}

For an explicit generous certificate bound, let $s$ be the total primitive
node count of $\Gamma,A$ and put $n=|V|$. Count proof-rule nodes, temporarily
ignoring the bit length of formula labels. The signed evaluation recursion
uses at most $20^{s+1}$ nodes: each constructor combines at most two previous
proofs with fewer than twenty local rule applications. A leaf in the
completeness construction and its context copies fit below
$10\cdot20^{s+2}$. All merging nodes fit below an additional
$10\cdot2^{n+1}$. Thus
\[
 B_C(s,n)=100\cdot2^{n+1}\cdot20^{s+2}
\]
is a valid generous rule-node bound. Formula labels are input subformulas,
their negations and literal contexts; their string lengths are bounded by
a polynomial in the input string length. Their numerical tree codes can
instead be bounded by iterating the specified constructor functions. Either
encoding yields a primitive recursive bound on the complete certificate.

\begin{corollary}[Classical transformations by rebuilding proofs]\label{thm:cpc-rebuild}
If a proposed primitive recursive transformation preserves Boolean truth,
then finite truth tables and Theorem~\ref{thm:cpc-complete} produce \CPC\
proofs of its equivalence. In particular, tautological rules, equivalent
replacement, contradiction tests, and semantic simplifications have
primitive recursive proof certificates.
\end{corollary}

For example, the source article's formula
$p\to((q\vee r)\to(r\to\neg p))$ is false at $p=1,q=0,r=1$:
$q\vee r=1$, $\neg p=0$, $r\to\neg p=0$, and both remaining antecedents
are one. The countervaluation certifies its nonderivability in either
calculus. In contrast, the modus ponens sequent $p,p\to q\vdash q$ has a
single implication-elimination step and needs no classical rule.
