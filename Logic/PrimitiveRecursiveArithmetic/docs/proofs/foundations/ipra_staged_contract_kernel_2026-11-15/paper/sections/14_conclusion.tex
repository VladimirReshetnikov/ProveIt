\section{Conclusion}

The iPRA proof packets have already found the right broad strategy: replace completed objects by finite
observations, replace arbitrary existence by finite certificates, compute demands before running a trace,
use relations where exact choice is unstable, and expose nonuniform mathematical inputs.  The remaining
bottleneck is proof production at the checked object-language boundary.

The staged contract kernel addresses that bottleneck with a deliberately small construction.  Local
theorems may keep their natural arities and contexts.  The compiler specializes them into one global frame
using the checked substitution theorem, aligns assumptions by weakening, and composes them by simultaneous
cut.  Bounded recursion is packaged once through quantifier-free induction.  The host traverses a fixed graph
and emits concrete syntax, avoiding any universal evaluator in iPRA.

Around this kernel, the report supplies a coherent library of human theorems: bounded Booleanization,
least search, trace reflection, demand closure, relational viability, stable branch erasure, resource ranks,
and height/list conversion.  These theorems explain both the positive reach of iPRA and the unavoidable
boundaries.  Exact sign, reciprocal, root labels, and eigenspaces need separation.  Finite cardinality needs
height data before it becomes enumeration.  Ineffective constants remain explicit.  Deep producer theorems
are not replaced by a representation trick.

The most useful next repository step is therefore narrow and testable: compile the supplied generic Lean
kernel against \texttt{ConciseIPRA}, implement the Boolean fold and bounded-search modules, and migrate one
complete application---the cubic bisection example is suitable---all the way to a literal
\(\Prf\,\PRAAx\) conclusion and native export.  Once this vertical slice is checked, the same compiler can
serve the much larger algebraic, analytic, topological, categorical, and Diophantine developments without
reopening the structural proof-engineering problem in every packet.
