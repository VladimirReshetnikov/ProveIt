\section{Scoped Certificate Realization}
\label{sec:scr}

\subsection{Contracts}

Fix an outer arity \(n\), a local context \(\Gamma\) of formulas in
\(\Fm\,\PTm\,n\), and an outer goal \(G\).  A one-certificate SCR contract
consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*)]
\item a formula \(V\in\Fm\,\PTm\,(n+1)\), usually quantifier-free, whose
      variable \(0\) denotes a certificate and whose remaining variables are
      shifted query parameters;
\item an \emph{extractor}
\[
       V\;::\;\Gamma^{\up}\quad\turnstile\quad G^{\up};
\]
\item optionally, a \emph{producer}
\[
       \Gamma\quad\turnstile\quad \exists c\,V(c).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The superscript \(\up\) denotes the checked de Bruijn shift.  The goal has no
free occurrence of the certificate because it lives in the outer scope.

\begin{theorem}[Scoped realization]
\label{thm:seal}
Every contract with a producer yields \(\Gamma\turnstile G\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the existential-elimination constructor of \(\Prf\) to the producer and
the extractor.  Its eigenvariable is exactly the locally scoped certificate.
No term denoting the certificate is formed, and no choice principle is used.
\end{proof}

This theorem is logically elementary; its architectural consequence is not.
A noncanonical object may be used arbitrarily many times inside the extractor,
may parameterize further local producers, and may be compared with another
locally opened certificate.  It simply cannot escape into \(G\) unless the
output interface explicitly exposes it.

\subsection{A missing producer becomes an exact theorem boundary}

Even without a producer, the extractor has objective mathematical content.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional closure]
\label{thm:conditional}
From the extractor one obtains
\[
   \Gamma\turnstile
   \bigl(\exists c\,V(c)\bigr)\to G.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Introduce the implication assumption, eliminate that existential, and use the
extractor after weakening by the shifted implication assumption.  This is the
proof implemented by \code{Contract.conditional} in the supplied Lean kernel
candidate.
\end{proof}

Thus a missing height theorem, modulus, cover, or separation bound does not
force the rest of the packet to remain informal.  The finite verifier and
extractor can be proved now, while the exact missing producer is visible in the
conclusion's antecedent.

\subsection{Dependent composition}

Real mathematical pipelines are dependent: a certificate chosen at one stage
changes the formula verified at the next.  Suppose
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &\Gamma\turnstile\exists x\,A(x),\\
  &A(x),\Gamma^{\up}\turnstile\exists y\,B(x,y),\\
  &B(x,y),A(x),\Gamma^{\up\up}\turnstile G^{\up\up}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the second producer is allowed to mention the first local certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Dependent sealing]
\label{thm:dependent-seal}
The three derivations above compose to \(\Gamma\turnstile G\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Eliminate the existential over \(x\).  In the resulting local context,
eliminate the existential over \(y\).  The third derivation gives the shifted
goal, after which the two existential-elimination rules close in reverse
order.  All context shifts are those already handled by \(\Prf.\mathsf{exE}\)
and \(\Prf.\mathsf{shift}\).
\end{proof}

Iterating this argument realizes any finite acyclic dependency graph.  Unlike
a global certificate pack, it does not require an a priori numerical bound for
each internal witness.  Only a witness that must be decoded by a variable-
horizon object computation needs a finite code; fixed proof-level dependencies
can remain as nested existential scopes.

\subsection{Relational rather than functional interfaces}

A useful mathematical component is often a total relation rather than a
canonical function.  Let \(R(q,c)\) be a decidable certificate relation.  SCR
represents totality by
\[
   \forall q\;\exists c\;R(q,c)
\]
only at the point where a theorem needs it, and composes downstream consumers
under the local assumption \(R(q,c)\).  This is stronger constructively than
silently postulating a global function \(c(q)\): it preserves all theorem
consequences invariant under the relation without asserting a selector.

When a concrete function is later needed, there are two legitimate upgrades.
Either a direct primitive-recursive producer term is supplied, or
Section~\ref{sec:selection}'s bounded selector is invoked with an explicit
cutoff.  There is no third route.

\subsection{Contract families and fixed queries}

A theorem packet normally carries a family of contracts indexed by an outer
query \(q\): a requested precision, degree, height, or finite diagram.  In the
object theory, \(q\) is simply a tuple of natural-number terms.  At the Lean
metalevel, a contract family is a function returning fixed formulas and proof
terms.  Specialization to arbitrary query terms is performed by
\code{Prf.subst}; the proof does not have to be rebuilt for numerals only.

This distinction avoids two common errors.  First, fixed-query reasoning is not
merely a theorem for each standard numeral: the open derivation can still be
universally closed over \(q\).  Second, specializing a fixed program family is
not an object-level universal evaluator.  Lean constructs the target syntax
before the iPRA proof begins.
