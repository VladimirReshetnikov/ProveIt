\section{Relation to the repository's prior frameworks}
\label{sec:comparison}

The proposal is best understood as a logical refactoring of existing strong
ideas, with one new organizing analysis.

\subsection{Finite-query normal form}

FQNF packs all evidence for an explicit finite query into one bounded natural
certificate, verifies it with a fixed PR checker, and selects the least
accepted code.  This is an excellent serialization and reflection normal form
when all bounds are available and a named computation is desired.

SCR changes the default.  Proof-local witnesses remain separate existential
scopes; a master code is created only when variable-horizon decoding or a
named output requires it.  Therefore SCR can formalize a conditional theorem
from an unbounded existential producer while FQNF must first obtain a code
cutoff.  Under explicit bounds the two are intertranslatable: fuse SCR scopes
to obtain FQNF, or open one FQNF certificate and treat its fields as SCR
contracts.

\subsection{Certified bounded interaction programs}

CBIP supplies a typed language of bounded interactions, finite strategies,
relational viability, and stable witness erasure.  SCR adopts these
mathematical mechanisms but makes ordinary existential scope the primitive
logical operation.  The interaction language becomes an optional front end;
its compiler target is a small collection of \code{Prf.exE},
\code{Prf.trans}, trace, strategy, and observer combinators.  This reduces the
amount of a large DSL that must be trusted or proved sound before applications
can migrate.

\subsection{Native clause programs}

Native clause programs use the checked \(\KPrf\) calculus as an intermediate
representation, with checked cut, substitution, and export.  SCR is compatible
with that backend.  A local quantifier-free arithmetic subgraph can carry a
\(\KPrf\) proof and export it to ordinary iPRA at the interface.  Existential
sealing and rich logical conclusions are most naturally organized in
\(\Prf\;\PRAAx\), which remains the canonical endpoint.

\subsection{Observation graphs and staged contract kernels}

Proof-carrying observation graphs already emphasize finite traces, backward
demand, relational witness clouds, and stable observations.  Staged contract
kernels emphasize specialization by \code{Prf.subst}, context alignment by
\code{Prf.weaken}, and composition by \code{Prf.trans}.  SCR combines these
with a missing criterion: whether a certificate escapes.  That criterion
selects among existential sealing, observer erasure, and bounded naming.  It
also supplies a direct proof of the conditional theorem when a producer is
missing.

\subsection{What is novel, and what is not}

Existential elimination, least bounded search, and invariant induction are of
course standard logical tools.  The contribution claimed here is the
repository-level synthesis:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item a static escape classification tied to exact \(\Prf\) constructors;
\item a scoped alternative to mandatory global certificate packing;
\item dependent composition without selectors for finite proof DAGs;
\item a uniform boundary-normal-form theorem for incomplete packets;
\item a single trace/strategy/observer architecture explaining when finite
      codes are genuinely necessary;
\item an integration plan that reuses the current checked arithmetic and makes
      open bridge transport first-class.
\end{enumerate}
The novelty is architectural and proof-theoretic rather than a claim to a new
intuitionistic logic.
