\section{Reusable mathematical representation templates}
\label{sec:templates}

SCR is not a single coding of all mathematics.  It is a disciplined way to
connect domain-specific finite presentations to the same proof-producing
kernel.  This section records the templates that cover most current packets.

\subsection{Finite algebra and exact linear algebra}

A polynomial, matrix, finite module map, or chain complex is represented by a
bounded coefficient table.  A certificate may contain row operations,
cofactor expansions, a null vector, an ideal combination, a multiplication
table, or a basis change.  The validator checks coefficientwise identities by
bounded sums and products.  The extractor uses the checked semiring, integer,
and finite-linear-algebra library.

Typical contracts include:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathsf{PolyId}(p,q,c) &\Rightarrow p=q,\\
\mathsf{BezoutCert}(a,b,u,v,g) &\Rightarrow ua+vb=g,\\
\mathsf{InverseCert}(A,B) &\Rightarrow AB=BA=I,\\
\mathsf{SpanClosed}(F_1,\ldots,F_D) &\Rightarrow
        \#\mathsf{Solutions}\le D.
\end{array}
\]
The last line is a cardinality theorem, not an enumeration cutoff.  To list
solutions, add a height or complete-list producer.

Symbolic identities should be proved by reusable object tactics only after the
tactic's output is a checked derivation.  Until then, a coefficient table plus
a reflected equality checker is the conservative interface.

\subsection{Integers, rationals, and cleared inequalities}

Integers are represented by differences of naturals, as in the checked
library.  A rational is a signed numerator and positive denominator, with
cross-multiplication used for equality and order.  Every analytic or geometric
packet should state denominator positivity in its validator and clear
fractions before invoking iPRA arithmetic.  Division is an interface operation
with a nonzero or positive-denominator guard, not an ambient field axiom.

A rational error bound \(|x-y|\le a/b\) is encoded after multiplying by the
positive common denominator.  This keeps all induction invariants
quantifier-free and permits exact audit of strict versus non-strict estimates.

\subsection{Real numbers and functions}

A usable real name is a fixed primitive-recursive rational sequence \(r(k)\)
with an explicit modulus, preferably in the normalized form
\[
      |r(k)-r(\ell)|\le 2^{-n}
      \quad\text{whenever }k,\ell\ge m(n).
\]
The name code is fixed at specialization time; iPRA quantifies over the natural
precision arguments, not over arbitrary functions.  Equality is an apartness
or two-sided approximation relation at requested precision.  A continuous
function is represented by an evaluator on names together with a precision
transformer and a proof that equivalent inputs give equivalent outputs.

There are four legitimate output levels.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]
\item a fixed named real with a uniform modulus;
\item a name conditional on supplied constants or moduli;
\item a finite-precision cloud with a diameter theorem;
\item a finite-consumer metastable window.
\end{enumerate}
Classical convergence without a computable rate normally yields only the last
two levels in iPRA.

\subsection{Integration, infinite series, and asymptotics}

An integral theorem is compiled to finite quadrature plus an error certificate:
partition data, sample values, local oscillation bounds, and a tail estimate if
the domain is unbounded.  A series uses a partial sum term and a remainder
bound.  An asymptotic statement uses a primitive-recursive threshold
\(N(\eps,\text{parameters})\) and an explicit remainder inequality.

If the packet has an exact symbolic antiderivative or telescoping identity,
the finite algebra core may discharge most of the work.  If it relies on a
classically asserted dominated-convergence or compactness argument, the
missing domination, tail, or finite-cover modulus must be made a producer.
The framework can verify a proposed modulus; it does not conjure one.

\subsection{Finite probability}

A finite law is a list of natural weights \(w_i\), a positive total
\(W=\sum_i w_i\), and a rational or integer observable.  Expectations are
represented in cleared form, so linearity, finite Fubini, variance identities,
Markov, and Chebyshev avoid division.  The recently checked
\code{IPRADerivations/Probability} modules illustrate the desired endpoint:
literal \(\Prf\;\PRAAx\) derivations for finite laws.

Countable or continuous laws require approximation contracts: truncation and
tail bounds, quadrature data, or a coupling at requested precision.  A sample
path over a finite horizon is a trace certificate; almost-sure asymptotics
must be replaced by a quantitative finitary probability bound or by an
explicit rate producer.

\subsection{Combinatorics and finite structures}

Graphs, hypergraphs, words, tilings, permutations, and incidence structures
are bounded tables.  Local constraints are reflected bits; global validity is
a failure sum.  Pigeonhole, parity, descent, and finite sums should be imported
from \code{IPRADerivations}.  A finite search theorem should return a complete
certificate or a countercertificate, not rely on a host exhaustive search as
an axiom.

An infinite combinatorial object is represented by coherent finite patches:
for every requested radius \(r\), a patch code \(p_r\) satisfies a local
checker, and a transition certificate relates \(p_r\) to \(p_{r+1}\).  One
fixed PR producer gives a genuine name; mere existence of every finite patch
without coherence gives only a family of finite theorems.

\subsection{Topology and geometry}

At a fixed mesh, a space is observed by a finite simplicial or cubical complex.
A map is represented by a strong carrier or a finite cloud of possible target
simplices.  A selection certificate chooses one simplex per source vertex.
The carrier theorem proves that every selection is simplicial and any two
selections are contiguous; an explicit prism operator gives a chain homotopy.
The induced homology map is therefore an exact observer and the selection can
be sealed.

Metric and manifold claims additionally require approximation data connecting
the finite complexes to the presented space: moduli of continuity and
injectivity, chart radii, finite covers, or separation data.  A Jordan or
dimension theorem may have a completely finite combinatorial core while still
lacking this producer.  SCR records the division precisely.

Exact Euclidean/projective geometry over rational or constructible coordinates
usually reduces to cleared polynomial identities plus nondegeneracy guards.
A branch-dependent construction---angle bisectors, eigenaxes, oriented roots---
uses the exact-label gate of \cref{sec:observer}.

\subsection{Finite categories, diagrams, and gluing}

A fixed finite category is a table of objects, morphisms, sources, targets,
identities, and composition, together with finite associativity and unit
checks.  Functors and natural transformations are tables with local equations.
Finite limits, colimits, adjunctions, and Kan-extension calculations become
finite universal-property certificates: proposed cones/cocones and mediating
arrows, with uniqueness checked by enumeration under explicit hom-set bounds.

For algebraic geometry, affine charts, localizations, and \v{C}ech diagrams are
finite presentations.  Equality in a localization or quotient is witnessed by
an exponent, ideal combination, or module relation.  Glue representatives
locally, prove compatibility on overlaps, and seal the representatives after
showing the global observer is invariant.  General schemes or unbounded
covers are not silently encoded by one finite atlas; the theorem states the
presentation class to which it applies.

\subsection{Diophantine geometry and finite descent}

For a polynomial system \(F(\vec x)=0\), a height bound \(H\) produces a finite
box and hence an exhaustive enumerator.  A complete-list certificate contains
the accepted points and either the full box scan or a compressed exclusion
certificate.  Soundness and completeness inside the box are primitive
recursive.  If a theorem proves every solution has height at most \(H\), SCR
seals the height certificate and exports finiteness or the exact list.

Descent data---covering curves, Selmer representatives, local tests, and
rank-decreasing measures---are finite certificates when their code ranges are
explicit.  A uniform Mordell--Weil theorem additionally needs a producer for a
complete covering list and termination bounds.  The verifier/producer split
allows all finite descent arithmetic to be formalized without overstating that
uniform result.
