\begin{abstract}
The \repo\ repository now has three unusually strong ingredients: a checked
ordinary presentation of intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic
(iPRA), a growing library of literal \(\Prf\;\PRAAx\) arithmetic derivations,
and checked translations to a small native calculus and to a separate
Foundation formalization.  It also has a much larger collection of research
packets whose human arguments are finite and constructive but whose Lean files
usually stop before constructing the requested object-theory derivation.
The bottleneck is therefore not another foundational presentation.  It is a
proof-producing interface between finitary mathematics and the canonical
judgment
\[
        \Gamma\turnstile A.
\]

This report proposes \emph{Scoped Certificate Realization} (SCR).  A finite
mathematical component is represented by a quantifier-free validator
\(\Valid(q,c)\), a local extractor proved under the temporary assumption that
\(c\) is valid, and a producer for \(\exists c\,\Valid(q,c)\) when one is
available.  Producer and extractor are joined by ordinary existential
elimination.  The certificate is thus hidden at precisely the scope where it
is noncanonical.  A bounded least search is used only when static
\emph{certificate escape analysis} shows that the certificate itself, rather
than a certificate-independent consequence, must occur in the public output.
This distinction eliminates unnecessary choice terms, avoids global master
certificates, and gives conditional theorems an exact boundary formula instead
of a vague missing lemma.

The generic results proved here include: scoped and dependent certificate
composition; a boundary-normal-form theorem for unresolved producers; an
optional bounded selector built from the repository's checked search theorem;
a one-induction trace-reflection theorem for adaptive and relational finite
computations; fixed-shape finite-strategy reflection for bounded quantifier
alternation; exact and approximate observer-erasure theorems; and a disciplined
classification of uniform, pointwise, conditional, and unavailable claims.
We give detailed applications to exact rational bisection, colliding algebraic
branches, finite carrier maps in topology, and height-bounded Diophantine
enumeration.  We also give a concrete route from the existing closed
Foundation bridge to open sequents.

The archive contains complete human proofs of the generic mathematics, a
no-placeholder Lean candidate for the small structural kernel, detailed Lean
targets and a dependency-ordered implementation plan, a machine-readable graph
schema, and exact standard-library reference programs with regression tests.
The Lean candidate was not compiled in the artifact runtime because Lean was
not installed; no new kernel-checked theorem is claimed.  Deep producer gaps---
for example a general height bound for Faltings or Siegel, an absent convergence
modulus, or a missing separation estimate---remain explicitly visible rather
than being disguised as proof-engineering problems.
\end{abstract}
