Scoped Certificate Realization
A General Proof-Producing Framework for Mathematics in
Intuitionistic Primitive Recursive Arithmetic

A repository-grounded research report for the ProveIt project
Baseline: f444b76e8480ebea128c15b4a59f158e14f478cc

This report develops a reusable method for turning finite, quantitative mathematical arguments
into literal derivations of the checked judgment Γ ⊢PRAAx A. Its central device is certificate
escape analysis: auxiliary witnesses remain under the existential scope in which they are needed,
and a bounded least selector is introduced only when a certificate itself must escape into the
externally visible theorem.

Abstract
The ProveIt repository now has three unusually strong ingredients: a checked ordinary
presentation of intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic (iPRA), a growing library of literal
Prf PRAAx arithmetic derivations, and checked translations to a small native calculus and to
a separate Foundation formalization. It also has a much larger collection of research packets
whose human arguments are finite and constructive but whose Lean files usually stop before
constructing the requested object-theory derivation. The bottleneck is therefore not another
foundational presentation. It is a proof-producing interface between finitary mathematics and
the canonical judgment
Γ ⊢PRAAx A.
This report proposes Scoped Certificate Realization (SCR). A finite mathematical component is
represented by a quantifier-free validator Valid(q, c), a local extractor proved under the temporary
assumption that c is valid, and a producer for ∃c Valid(q, c) when one is available. Producer and
extractor are joined by ordinary existential elimination. The certificate is thus hidden at precisely
the scope where it is noncanonical. A bounded least search is used only when static certificate
escape analysis shows that the certificate itself, rather than a certificate-independent consequence,
must occur in the public output. This distinction eliminates unnecessary choice terms, avoids
global master certificates, and gives conditional theorems an exact boundary formula instead of
a vague missing lemma.
The generic results proved here include: scoped and dependent certificate composition; a
boundary-normal-form theorem for unresolved producers; an optional bounded selector built from
the repository’s checked search theorem; a one-induction trace-reflection theorem for adaptive
and relational finite computations; fixed-shape finite-strategy reflection for bounded quantifier
alternation; exact and approximate observer-erasure theorems; and a disciplined classification
of uniform, pointwise, conditional, and unavailable claims. We give detailed applications to
exact rational bisection, colliding algebraic branches, finite carrier maps in topology, and heightbounded Diophantine enumeration. We also give a concrete route from the existing closed
Foundation bridge to open sequents.
The archive contains complete human proofs of the generic mathematics, a no-placeholder
Lean candidate for the small structural kernel, detailed Lean targets and a dependency-ordered
implementation plan, a machine-readable graph schema, and exact standard-library reference
programs with regression tests. The Lean candidate was not compiled in the artifact runtime
because Lean was not installed; no new kernel-checked theorem is claimed. Deep producer gaps—
for example a general height bound for Faltings or Siegel, an absent convergence modulus, or
a missing separation estimate—remain explicitly visible rather than being disguised as proofengineering problems.

1.1 The canonical endpoint
The correct endpoint for new work in this part of ProveIt is not a semantic claim that a host
program terminates, nor a theorem in Mathlib about ordinary natural numbers. It is an inhabitant
of the deeply embedded judgment
Prf PRAAx Γ φ,

written

Γ ⊢PRAAx φ.

The current ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/PR.lean defines primitive recursive symbols PR(k),
terms PTm(n), and the axiom family PRAAx. The latter contains the defining equations for
projection, composition, and primitive recursion, the two successor axioms, and induction for
quantifier-free formulas. The logic in Deduction.lean is intuitionistic natural deduction with
equality, scoped simultaneous substitution, and the structural theorems
Prf.subst,

Prf.weaken,

Prf.trans,

Prf.shift.

These are not mere conveniences. They are the generic proof compiler already present in the
repository: substitution specializes a mathematical contract, weakening aligns local contexts, and
Prf.trans performs simultaneous cut.
The checked native scalar core remains valuable as a compact backend. The checked native
bi-interpretation module proves both
NPrf ∆ A

⇐⇒

∆.map(toPRA) ⊢PRAAx A.toPRA,

Γ ⊢PRAAx φ

⇐⇒

NPrf Γ.map(ofPRA) (ofPRA(φ)).


The five-rule clause calculus KPrf additionally has checked substitution, cut, and export to NPrf.
SCR therefore treats KPrf as an optional compressed intermediate representation, not as a competing
foundation. The portable public interface remains Prf PRAAx.

1.2 The checked arithmetic substrate
The IPRADerivations library has advanced beyond a toy arithmetic core. At the audited baseline
it contains object-theory derivations for semiring algebra, cancellation, integers as differences, order
and truncated subtraction, pairing and projections, powers, parity, bounded sums and products,
descent, pigeonhole, permutations, quotient and remainder, gcd and Bézout, bounded search, finite
Fubini, and finite linear algebra. The relevant engineering lesson is that a packet should import
these results at arbitrary terms and under local hypotheses, not reproduce them in a private host
model.
Two checked tools are especially central here.
(i) Core/Basic.lean supplies open quantifier-free induction
⊢PRAAx ϕ(0, ⃗x)
ϕ(z, ⃗x) ⊢PRAAx ϕ(Sz, ⃗x)
⊢PRAAx ϕ(n, ⃗x)
with all parameters explicit, together with instantiation of closed open-formula derivations at
arbitrary terms.

(ii) Core/Search.lean defines the least bounded zero µf (N, x), returning the sentinel N when no
zero occurs, and proves inside iPRA its bound, correctness, and minimality. Thus a certificate
can be canonically named once—and only once—an actual numerical cutoff and a reflected
verifier are available.

1.3 The checked bridge and its remaining asymmetry
The Foundation–Concise bridge translates closed theorems in both directions. Its implementation is
already more general internally than the public API suggests. In the Concise-to-Foundation direction,
toFoundation_provable_of_axioms transports an arbitrary Prf derivation with a finite local
context under any assignment of free variables to Foundation terms. The public wrappers then close
the free variables and specialize to an empty context. In the reverse direction, hilbertToConcise
is uniform in the images of free variables, but theoryToConcise is exposed only for closed theory
consequences. Section 14 explains how to finish the open-sequent interface without changing either
object theory.

1.4 The packet layer and the actual gap
The proof-packet catalogue explicitly warns that the filed archives were not rerun, compiled, or
checked during placement. Many packets have strong finite mathematics and exact reference
programs, yet their Lean files are described as scaffolds, blueprints, or targets. The recurring gap
has two different causes and they must not be conflated.
Proof-production gap. The finite objects, bounds, and local lemmas are available, but no term
of type Prf PRAAx Γ A has been assembled. This is the gap addressed by SCR.
Producer gap. The theorem requires data not supplied by the argument: a uniform height bound,
a complete list of covering representatives, an explicit convergence modulus, a separation gap, a
finite subcover, or an effective exceptional constant. No generic compiler can manufacture such
mathematics. SCR isolates it as a producer boundary.
For example, the filed Faltings and Siegel packets explicitly stop at missing height information; the
Mordell–Weil packet does not give a uniform generator algorithm; exact axis selection is restricted by
a constructive obstruction; and several analytic packets state results only for fixed names equipped
with moduli. A trustworthy framework must make those distinctions visible in its final theorem
type.

1.5 Prior framework work
The repository already contains several ambitious interfaces: finite observations, certified finitary
presentations, proof-carrying observation graphs, finite-query normal form (FQNF), certified bounded
interaction programs (CBIP), and native clause programs. Their shared achievements are substantial:
fixed finite queries, reflected checkers, whole-path certificates, relational witness clouds, stable
observations, and explicit producer boundaries. Their shared unresolved point is also clear from
their status files: the generic object-theory compiler remains largely a human proof or Lean blueprint.
SCR does not discard those ideas. It changes the logical normal form beneath them. A global least
certificate is no longer the default. The default is a locally scoped existential, eliminated by the

2 Design constraints
A general iPRA framework is useful only if it respects the theory’s actual proof rules and the
repository’s actual interfaces. The following constraints are therefore part of the specification, not
after-the-fact cautions.
Principle 2.1 (Object proof, not host truth). A Python test, a terminating Lean function, a theorem
in the host logic, and an inhabitant of Γ ⊢PRAAx A are four different artifacts. Host computation
may generate fixed syntax or test examples; every reusable mathematical conclusion must end in a
literal object derivation.
Principle 2.2 (Specialize first). There is no appeal to a total object-level universal evaluator for
arbitrary primitive-recursive programs. A theorem compiler is a metatheoretic function which receives
a fixed syntax tree and emits a fixed PTm, Fm, and proof term. Its soundness is proved by structural
recursion in Lean. The resulting object theorem speaks only about the specialized symbols.
Principle 2.3 (Finite query before induction). Precision, height, mesh, horizon, matrix size,
diagram shape, and all other consumer demands are fixed as explicit natural-number parameters
before the object induction is formed. The induction invariant is an equation, inequality, or Boolean
combination of such atoms, hence quantifier-free.
Principle 2.4 (No hidden choice). An existential proof is not silently converted into a primitiverecursive selector. A selector is introduced only with an explicit finite cutoff or a direct constructive
term. Otherwise the witness remains under existential elimination.
Principle 2.5 (No hidden compactness). Any use of compactness is replaced by the finite datum
actually consumed: a net, cover, Lebesgue-number certificate, mesh, finite complex, truncation index,
or metastable window. The theorem records which producer supplies that datum.
Principle 2.6 (No unstable names). At a collision or singularity, one may export a cluster, a
symmetric invariant, a quotient class, or a diameter-controlled cloud. One may export a specific root,
eigenvector, chart, axis, or branch only after proving a uniqueness or separation premise sufficient
to stabilize that name.
Principle 2.7 (Uniformity is a theorem). The following claims are distinct and must carry different
types:
(a) one internal primitive-recursive producer works uniformly;
(b) a conclusion follows from an explicit producer hypothesis;
(c) for each fixed external name and supplied modulus there is an iPRA derivation schema;
(d) only finite observations or a metastable form are presently justified.
The report never upgrades one status to another by prose.

2.1 Why quantifier-free induction is enough for the framework
The apparent obstacle is that a trace assertion often has the form “every stage below N is valid,”
which is a bounded universal formula and therefore not quantifier-free in the object syntax. The
solution is not to induct on that formula. Instead, define a primitive-recursive failure accumulator
bad(0) = 0,

bad(k + 1) = bad(k) + fail(k),

where fail(k) ∈ {0, 1}. The invariant
bad(k) = 0 −→ I(state(k)) = 1
is quantifier-free. At a successor, bad(k + 1) = 0 yields both bad(k) = 0 and fail(k) = 0, using the
checked arithmetic of nonnegative sums. This is the recurring compilation pattern: bounded logical
structure is represented by a primitive-recursive certificate and a quantifier-free reflection invariant.

2.2 Three meanings of “general”
The framework is general in three precise, compatible senses.
(G1) Logical generality. Contracts compose in arbitrary finite contexts using only the checked
structural rules of intuitionistic natural deduction.
(G2) Computational generality. Any fixed primitive-recursive validator, state transformer, finite
strategy checker, or observer can be inserted once its defining equations and local reflection
lemmas are supplied.
(G3) Mathematical generality. Algebraic, analytic, probabilistic, topological, categorical, and
Diophantine objects are represented by the finite observations actually used by a theorem,
with domain-specific producer obligations separated from the generic verifier.
It is deliberately not a universal internal interpreter for arbitrary mathematics, and it does not
claim that every classical theorem has a uniform iPRA realization.

3.1 Contracts
Fix an outer arity n, a local context Γ of formulas in Fm PTm n, and an outer goal G. A one-certificate
SCR contract consists of:
(C1) a formula V ∈ Fm PTm (n + 1), usually quantifier-free, whose variable 0 denotes a certificate
and whose remaining variables are shifted query parameters;
(C2) an extractor
V :: Γ↑ ⊢PRAAx G↑ ;
(C3) optionally, a producer

Γ

⊢PRAAx

∃c V (c).

The superscript ↑ denotes the checked de Bruijn shift. The goal has no free occurrence of the
certificate because it lives in the outer scope.

Theorem 3.1 (Scoped realization). Every contract with a producer yields Γ ⊢PRAAx G.
Proof. Apply the existential-elimination constructor of Prf to the producer and the extractor. Its
eigenvariable is exactly the locally scoped certificate. No term denoting the certificate is formed,
and no choice principle is used.
This theorem is logically elementary; its architectural consequence is not. A noncanonical object
may be used arbitrarily many times inside the extractor, may parameterize further local producers,
and may be compared with another locally opened certificate. It simply cannot escape into G unless
the output interface explicitly exposes it.

3.2 A missing producer becomes an exact theorem boundary
Even without a producer, the extractor has objective mathematical content.
Theorem 3.2 (Conditional closure). From the extractor one obtains
Γ ⊢PRAAx ∃c V (c) → G.

Proof. Introduce the implication assumption, eliminate that existential, and use the extractor

3.3 Dependent composition
Real mathematical pipelines are dependent: a certificate chosen at one stage changes the formula
verified at the next. Suppose
Γ ⊢PRAAx ∃x A(x),
A(x), Γ↑ ⊢PRAAx ∃y B(x, y),
B(x, y), A(x), Γ↑↑ ⊢PRAAx G↑↑ .
Here the second producer is allowed to mention the first local certificate.
Theorem 3.3 (Dependent sealing). The three derivations above compose to Γ ⊢PRAAx G.
Proof. Eliminate the existential over x. In the resulting local context, eliminate the existential over
y. The third derivation gives the shifted goal, after which the two existential-elimination rules close
in reverse order. All context shifts are those already handled by Prf.exE and Prf.shift.
Iterating this argument realizes any finite acyclic dependency graph. Unlike a global certificate
pack, it does not require an a priori numerical bound for each internal witness. Only a witness that
must be decoded by a variable- horizon object computation needs a finite code; fixed proof-level
dependencies can remain as nested existential scopes.

3.4 Relational rather than functional interfaces
A useful mathematical component is often a total relation rather than a canonical function. Let
R(q, c) be a decidable certificate relation. SCR represents totality by
∀q ∃c R(q, c)
only at the point where a theorem needs it, and composes downstream consumers under the local
assumption R(q, c). This is stronger constructively than silently postulating a global function c(q):
it preserves all theorem consequences invariant under the relation without asserting a selector.
When a concrete function is later needed, there are two legitimate upgrades. Either a direct
primitive-recursive producer term is supplied, or Section 7’s bounded selector is invoked with an
explicit cutoff. There is no third route.

3.5 Contract families and fixed queries
A theorem packet normally carries a family of contracts indexed by an outer query q: a requested
precision, degree, height, or finite diagram. In the object theory, q is simply a tuple of natural-number
terms. At the Lean metalevel, a contract family is a function returning fixed formulas and proof
terms. Specialization to arbitrary query terms is performed by Prf.subst; the proof does not have
to be rebuilt for numerals only.
This distinction avoids two common errors. First, fixed-query reasoning is not merely a theorem
for each standard numeral: the open derivation can still be universally closed over q. Second,
specializing a fixed program family is not an object-level universal evaluator. Lean constructs the
target syntax before the iPRA proof begins.

4 Certificate escape analysis
The central static analysis of SCR asks what part of a certificate appears in the public theorem.
There are three classes.
Definition 4.1 (Escape classes). Let c satisfy a contract V (q, c).
Sealed. The final formula contains no data computed from c. The certificate is eliminated directly
by theorem 3.1.
Observed. The final interface uses only an observation o = O(q, c), and the proof establishes that
all valid certificates give the same observation, or observations within a stated diameter.
Named. The actual code c, its leastness, or a label depending on a particular representative occurs
in the output. A direct producer or bounded selector is mandatory.

4.1 The escape theorem
Theorem 4.2 (Escape analysis). For a finite contract graph whose final goal is in the outer scope:
(i) every sealed certificate can be removed by nested existential elimination, without a code bound;
(ii) every exactly observed certificate can be replaced by its single-valued output relation, without
selecting a representative;

Scoped Certificate Realization for iPRA

7

(iii) a named certificate can be exported only from a supplied term producer or from a proved
bounded search.
Proof. Part (i) is repeated application of theorem 3.3. For part (ii), define
Out(q, o) :⇐⇒ ∃c [V (q, c) ∧ O(q, c) = o].
Existence follows by opening a valid certificate and introducing its observation. If o, o′ satisfy Out,
open both certificates; observer independence yields o = o′ . All consumers formulated through Out
therefore compose relationally. Part (iii) is a syntactic scope fact: an outer term cannot contain
the eigenvariable introduced by existential elimination. To name it one must already have an outer
term, or construct one by a total bounded operation whose correctness is proved.
The theorem does not assert an impossibility result for every conceivable encoding. It states the exact
proof obligations for this natural-deduction interface. Any purported third method of exporting a
local eigenvariable will manifest either as an unproved choice axiom or as an omitted bound.

4.2 Why a global master certificate is not the default
Packing all witnesses in one natural number is sometimes valuable. It permits one reflected checker,
one bounded search, and compact serialization. It also has costs:
• every component needs a numerical range even when it is proof-local;
• changing one producer changes the global radix and checker;
• a least global code imposes a canonical order unrelated to the mathematics;
• relational and quotient objects are prematurely forced into named representatives;
• the final proof term reflects a monolithic verifier rather than the logical dependency structure.
SCR therefore treats packing as a derived backend. If every internal certificate has a proved bound,
nested scopes can be fused into one mixed-radix code; the resulting theorem is an FQNF-style
master certificate. Conversely, a master certificate can always be opened once and its fields used in
nested SCR scopes. The two representations are extensionally intertranslatable when bounds exist,
but the scoped form remains meaningful when they do not.

4.3 Sharing a certificate without leaking it
A certificate may feed many downstream lemmas. Open it once, prove a conjunction of all required
consequences in the same scope, and eliminate it after the last consumer. In Lean this amounts to
constructing a local theorem under V :: Γ↑ and using Prf.andI or a downstream context. There is
no need to select the certificate merely to avoid duplicating a proof. At the metalevel, a named
Lean definition can share the derivation term.

4.4 When observed becomes named
Observer independence proves that a relation has at most one output, not that there is a primitiverecursive output term. If downstream mathematics accepts an existentially unique output relation,
the observation remains relational. If the API insists on a term o(q), one still needs either a

direct observation producer or a bound from which an accepted certificate can be selected. This
distinction is especially important for exact real numbers: a unique real specified by a predicate is
not automatically a primitive-recursive Cauchy name with a modulus.

5 The proof-producing compiler
5.1 A small program language
For exposition, an SCR program over context Γ and goal G is built from the following constructors.
These are a metalevel organization of existing proof rules, not new object-language syntax.
Local proof. Store an actual derivation Γ ⊢PRAAx G.
Specialize. Substitute arbitrary terms for the free query variables.
Supply. Replace each formula in a local context by a derivation of it, using Prf.trans.
Seal. Combine Γ ⊢PRAAx ∃c V (c) with a continuation V, Γ↑ ⊢PRAAx G↑ .
Boundary. When the producer is absent, retain ∃c V (c) as an assumption or implication antecedent.
Loop/strategy/observer. Invoke one of the reusable arithmetic reflection modules developed in
later sections; each invocation itself ends in an ordinary Prf derivation.
A finite directed acyclic graph can be converted to such a program by reverse topological traversal.
A node with proof-only outputs is cut into its consumers. A node with a certificate output wraps
the remaining consumers in one existential scope. Boundary nodes become formulas in the outer
context.
Theorem 5.1 (SCR compiler soundness). Let P be a well-scoped finite SCR program. Suppose
every local node carries an actual derivation of the judgment required by its constructor, and every
arithmetic reflection node has its stated object-theory proof. Then structural recursion on P constructs
a term
compile(P ) : ΓP ⊢PRAAx GP .
No host proposition about evaluator correctness is used as an object axiom.
Proof. The local-proof case returns the stored term. Specialization is Prf.subst. Supply is
Prf.trans; weakening aligns supercontexts. Seal is Prf.exE. A boundary is a hypothesis and
may be moved to the right by repeated Prf.impI. The remaining constructors return the already
supplied reflection derivation. Every recursion is on the finite host syntax of P , so termination is
metatheoretic and does not require a universal object evaluator.

5.2 Boundary normal form
Let B1 , . . . , Br be the unresolved producer formulas reachable from the goal. Compilation produces
B1 , . . . , Br , Γ ⊢PRAAx G.
Repeated implication introduction gives
Γ ⊢PRAAx B1 → · · · → Br → G.

9

Alternatively, a finite conjunction can be used. The context form is better for incremental discharge
because Prf.trans can supply each boundary independently.
The boundary set is dependency exact: it contains exactly the unresolved nodes used by the chosen
program after dead-node elimination. It is not claimed logically minimal—one boundary may in
principle follow from the others. Such minimization is ordinary theorem proving and should not be
hidden inside the compiler.

5.3 DAG sharing and proof size
Flattening a large graph to one Boolean checker is one way to control proof size. SCR provides
another. Local proof constructors are named Lean definitions; contexts record interfaces rather than
expanding proofs; and Prf.trans composes them. Variable-length arithmetic work is compressed
by a primitive-recursive trace plus one induction, while fixed logical work is shared by the host
proof term. A later optimization may compile a connected subgraph to KPrf, use KPrf.cut and
KPrf.subst, and export it through the checked bridges. This changes proof representation, not
theorem meaning.

5.4 Compositional correctness under substitution
A frequent source of packet failure is proving a lemma only at generic variables and then informally
using it at complex terms. Every SCR node is required either to be stated at arbitrary terms or
to be an open theorem specialized by Prf.subst. Since substitution commutes with all formula
constructors and the calculus proves closure under substitution, the compiler has the following
property.
Proposition 5.2 (Naturality). If P compiles to Γ ⊢PRAAx G, then for every simultaneous term
substitution σ, the specialized program compiles to
Γ[σ] ⊢PRAAx G[σ].
Proof. Apply Prf.subst to the compiled derivation. Equivalently, prove the claim by induction on
the program and use the checked substitution laws under binders in the seal case.

The arithmetic compiler needs a uniform way to turn a finite verification condition into quantifier-free

quantifier in the syntax, whereas the induction scheme of PRAAx accepts only formulas recognized
by Fm.QF. SCR therefore compiles bounded logic to primitive-recursive data and proves reflection

6.1 Reflected atoms
A reflected atom over n variables consists of a formula A, a bit term bA , a proof that bA is Boolean,
and derivations connecting its two values with A:
⊢PRAAx bA = 0 ∨ bA = 1,
bA = 1 ⊢PRAAx A,
A ⊢PRAAx bA = 1.
Equality, order, divisibility with a supplied quotient, and all polynomial identities used by the packet
layer have such characteristic terms in the checked arithmetic library or can be constructed from
them. Negation is represented by complement, conjunction by multiplication of bits, and disjunction
by the sign of their sum. Each combinator carries object proofs.
This is intentionally a specialization-time interface. A Lean structure may hold (A, bA , πA ), and a
recursive Lean function may combine these structures. It emits a fixed term and a fixed derivation.
No natural number inside iPRA is asked to interpret arbitrary syntax.

7.1 The selector theorem
Let V (q, c) be a reflected quantifier-free validator and let B(q) be an explicit natural-number cutoff.

The cutoff is not cosmetic. Without it, µ is not the repository’s total bounded-search symbol, and

7.2 Selection policy
The compiler applies theorem 7.1 only under one of the following escape conditions.
(S1) The certificate code itself occurs in the goal.
(S2) A downstream variable-horizon computation must decode the code after its existential scope
would otherwise close.
(S3) The public API explicitly promises a canonical least representative.
(S4) A certificate-independent observer is needed as a term and no direct primitive-recursive observer
producer is available.
None of these applies to an ordinary proof-local witness. In particular, selecting the least root
isolation, least triangulation, least finite cover, or least descent representative is usually unnecessary.
It introduces bounds and arithmetic obligations without strengthening the theorem.

7.4 Height versus cardinality
A statement “there are at most D solutions” gives a cardinality bound, not a bound on their
natural-number codes. It cannot justify enumeration below D. To obtain a named complete list one
needs either:
• a height bound H from which a finite code box is computed;
• an explicit complete list certificate; or
• a direct producer that enumerates all solutions.
This is the decisive distinction in the Faltings, Siegel, Mordell–Weil, and finite-algebra packets. The
generic list verifier is easy; the producer of H or of the exhaustive list is the deep theorem.

7.5 Canonicalization is an additional theorem
A least code is canonical relative to an encoding, not necessarily invariant under the mathematical
equivalence relation. If public output is supposed to be a canonical quotient representative, the
proof must additionally show:
(i) every equivalence class has an accepted representative below the cutoff;
(ii) the selected code is equivalent to the input object;
(iii) equivalent inputs yield equal selected codes, or the API explicitly accepts encoding-dependent
normalization.
Otherwise the proper output is an invariant observer or a witnessed setoid, not a silently normalized
representative.

8.1 Normalized trace data

9 Finite strategies for bounded quantifier alternation

9.1 Strategy tables
For each existential node, enumerate the preceding universal history in mixed radix and store one
bounded response in a table. A strategy code s then has primitive-recursive accessors movej (s, h).

(ii) for every complete universal history, the matrix bit is true after inserting the stored moves.

9.2 Completeness for finite decidable games
When all move bounds are explicit and the leaf matrix is decidable, backward induction computes a
winning bit and a least winning response at each existential node. Because the alternation shape is
fixed, this is a primitive-recursive program family. The object proof uses nested bounded search
and quantifier-free reflection.
Theorem 9.2 (Finite strategy completeness, schema). For each fixed bounded alternation shape α,
iPRA proves that if the bounded formula Φα (q) holds, then an accepted finite strategy code exists.
Equivalently, the formula is interderivable with the existence of an accepted strategy certificate.
Proof architecture. Induct metatheoretically on α. At an existential node, retain the formula’s
witness in local scope and combine it with the recursively obtained sub-strategy; if a named table
is desired, use the explicit move and table bounds to select the least accepted combined code. At
a universal node, construct a table of sub-strategies for all finitely many challenges. This table
construction is a primitive recursion on the challenge bound whose invariant states, in reflected form,
that all completed entries verify. Since α is fixed, all table accessors and verifiers are specialized
syntax. A complete Lean proof should first implement the ∀∃ case and then abstract the recurring
fold.
The completeness schema is a formalization target rather than a claimed kernel-checked declaration
in this archive. Its human proof uses only the bounded search and trace lemmas already isolated
above.

• a small checker for a positive strategy;

10 Stable observers, quotients, and exact-label gates
Many mathematical objects have valid finite presentations but no natural canonical presentation:
a polynomial’s roots may be permuted, an eigenspace may have many bases, a manifold may
have many atlases, and a homotopy class may have many simplicial representatives. SCR treats
presentation choice as a local certificate and exports only proved-invariant observations.

10.1 Exact observer erasure
Let V (q, c) validate certificates and let O(q, c) be a term-valued observer. Suppose iPRA proves
V (q, c) ∧ V (q, d) −→ O(q, c) = O(q, d).
Theorem 10.1 (Exact observer erasure). The relation
Out(q, o) :⇐⇒ ∃c [V (q, c) ∧ O(q, c) = o]
is single-valued. Every consumer invariant under equality of o can be composed with a sealed producer
for V without selecting a certificate.
Proof. Open witnesses c, d for two outputs. Observer independence gives O(q, c) = O(q, d); the
defining equalities then give o = o′ . For a consumer, open one valid certificate, introduce its output
relation, apply the consumer, and close the scope.
For root sets, elementary symmetric polynomials are exact observers. For a finite matrix, the
characteristic polynomial and traces of powers are exact observers under change of basis. For a finite
chain map induced by a carrier, the homology map is an exact observer once any two selections are
proved contiguous and the prism homotopy is verified.

10.2 Quantitative clouds
Exact equality is often too strong. Let d be an encoded rational metric or seminorm and suppose
V (q, c) ∧ V (q, d) −→ d(O(q, c), O(q, d)) ≤ δ(q).
Then valid certificates form an output cloud of diameter at most δ. Downstream operations carry
moduli which transform input diameters into output diameters. Composition is by arithmetic on
explicit error budgets.
Theorem 10.2 (Diameter composition). Suppose F has modulus m:
dX (x, x′ ) ≤ m(q, ε) → dY (F x, F x′ ) ≤ ε.
If a certificate cloud for x has diameter at most m(q, ε), then the image observer cloud has diameter
at most ε.

Proof. Open two valid certificates, apply their diameter premise, then the supplied modulus theorem.
Since the conclusion contains neither certificate, close both existential scopes.
This is the correct unconditional output at collisions. It supports finite precision consumers without
pretending to have a globally coherent branch.

10.3 The exact-label gate
Suppose candidate labels ℓ0 , . . . , ℓr−1 are separated by g > 0, and every valid observation lies within
e of one label. If 2e < g, two different labels cannot both explain valid observations.
Lemma 10.3 (Gap stabilization). In a metric encoded by rational inequalities, if
d(ℓi , ℓj ) ≥ g

(i ̸= j),

d(o, ℓi ) ≤ e,

d(o′ , ℓj ) ≤ e,

d(o, o′ ) ≤ δ,

and 2e + δ < g, then i = j.
Proof. If i =
̸ j, the triangle inequality gives g ≤ d(ℓi , ℓj ) ≤ e + δ + e < g, a contradiction. The
formal version clears rational denominators and uses the checked order library.
The gate yields an exact branch label only on a region with a proved positive gap. At the discriminant
locus the API falls back to a cluster or invariant. This resolves, in maximal constructive form, the
recurring exact-root, eigenvector, rotation-axis, and chart-selection difficulties.

10.4 Witnessed setoids
A general quotient interface consists of:
(a) a certificate type represented by natural codes;
(b) a reflected validity predicate V (c);
(c) a reflected equivalence witness E(c, d, w);
(d) proofs of reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity at the level needed by consumers;
(e) observer invariance under E.
No quotient representative is normalized unless a separate normalization contract is proved. This
is often smaller than coding a full equivalence relation as a decidable Boolean: equality may be
witnessed by an ideal combination, a chain homotopy, a coordinate transition, or a finite zigzag.
The witness itself is sealed after observer invariance has been used.

10.5 Coherent names across precision
To obtain a primitive-recursive Cauchy name rather than unrelated finite clouds, an application
must supply a coherence contract. A common sufficient form is
d(ok+1 , ok ) ≤ 2−k−c
for a fixed c, verified by a finite transition certificate. A summable motion budget gives a Cauchy
modulus by a finite geometric-tail estimate. If only finite-horizon compatibility is known, SCR
exports finite coherent paths or metastable windows, not an infinite name. Again, the missing tail
modulus is a producer obligation, not a logical inference.

11 Reusable mathematical representation templates
SCR is not a single coding of all mathematics. It is a disciplined way to connect domain-specific
finite presentations to the same proof-producing kernel. This section records the templates that
cover most current packets.

11.1 Finite algebra and exact linear algebra
A polynomial, matrix, finite module map, or chain complex is represented by a bounded coefficient
table. A certificate may contain row operations, cofactor expansions, a null vector, an ideal
combination, a multiplication table, or a basis change. The validator checks coefficientwise identities
by bounded sums and products. The extractor uses the checked semiring, integer, and finite-linearalgebra library.

11.2 Integers, rationals, and cleared inequalities
Integers are represented by differences of naturals, as in the checked library. A rational is a
signed numerator and positive denominator, with cross-multiplication used for equality and order.
Every analytic or geometric packet should state denominator positivity in its validator and clear
fractions before invoking iPRA arithmetic. Division is an interface operation with a nonzero or
positive-denominator guard, not an ambient field axiom.
A rational error bound |x−y| ≤ a/b is encoded after multiplying by the positive common denominator.
This keeps all induction invariants quantifier-free and permits exact audit of strict versus non-strict
estimates.

11.3 Real numbers and functions
A usable real name is a fixed primitive-recursive rational sequence r(k) with an explicit modulus,
preferably in the normalized form
|r(k) − r(ℓ)| ≤ 2−n

whenever k, ℓ ≥ m(n).

The name code is fixed at specialization time; iPRA quantifies over the natural precision arguments,
not over arbitrary functions. Equality is an apartness or two-sided approximation relation at

requested precision. A continuous function is represented by an evaluator on names together with a
precision transformer and a proof that equivalent inputs give equivalent outputs.
There are four legitimate output levels.
(R1) a fixed named real with a uniform modulus;
(R2) a name conditional on supplied constants or moduli;
(R3) a finite-precision cloud with a diameter theorem;
(R4) a finite-consumer metastable window.
Classical convergence without a computable rate normally yields only the last two levels in iPRA.

11.4 Integration, infinite series, and asymptotics
An integral theorem is compiled to finite quadrature plus an error certificate: partition data, sample
values, local oscillation bounds, and a tail estimate if the domain is unbounded. A series uses a
partial sum term and a remainder bound. An asymptotic statement uses a primitive-recursive
threshold N (ε, parameters) and an explicit remainder inequality.
If the packet has an exact symbolic antiderivative or telescoping identity, the finite algebra core
may discharge most of the work. If it relies on a classically asserted dominated-convergence or
compactness argument, the missing domination, tail, or finite-cover modulus must be made a
producer. The framework can verify a proposed modulus; it does not conjure one.

11.5 Finite probability
A finite law is a list of natural weights wi , a positive total W = i wi , and a rational or integer
observable. Expectations are represented in cleared form, so linearity, finite Fubini, variance identities,
Markov, and Chebyshev avoid division. The recently checked IPRADerivations/Probability
modules illustrate the desired endpoint: literal Prf PRAAx derivations for finite laws.

Countable or continuous laws require approximation contracts: truncation and tail bounds, quadrature data, or a coupling at requested precision. A sample path over a finite horizon is a trace
certificate; almost-sure asymptotics must be replaced by a quantitative finitary probability bound
or by an explicit rate producer.

11.6 Combinatorics and finite structures
Graphs, hypergraphs, words, tilings, permutations, and incidence structures are bounded tables.
Local constraints are reflected bits; global validity is a failure sum. Pigeonhole, parity, descent, and
finite sums should be imported from IPRADerivations. A finite search theorem should return a
complete certificate or a countercertificate, not rely on a host exhaustive search as an axiom.
An infinite combinatorial object is represented by coherent finite patches: for every requested radius
r, a patch code pr satisfies a local checker, and a transition certificate relates pr to pr+1 . One fixed
PR producer gives a genuine name; mere existence of every finite patch without coherence gives
only a family of finite theorems.

11.7 Topology and geometry
At a fixed mesh, a space is observed by a finite simplicial or cubical complex. A map is represented
by a strong carrier or a finite cloud of possible target simplices. A selection certificate chooses one
simplex per source vertex. The carrier theorem proves that every selection is simplicial and any two
selections are contiguous; an explicit prism operator gives a chain homotopy. The induced homology
map is therefore an exact observer and the selection can be sealed.
Metric and manifold claims additionally require approximation data connecting the finite complexes
to the presented space: moduli of continuity and injectivity, chart radii, finite covers, or separation
data. A Jordan or dimension theorem may have a completely finite combinatorial core while still
lacking this producer. SCR records the division precisely.
Exact Euclidean/projective geometry over rational or constructible coordinates usually reduces to
cleared polynomial identities plus nondegeneracy guards. A branch-dependent construction—angle
bisectors, eigenaxes, oriented roots— uses the exact-label gate of section 10.

11.8 Finite categories, diagrams, and gluing
A fixed finite category is a table of objects, morphisms, sources, targets, identities, and composition,
together with finite associativity and unit checks. Functors and natural transformations are tables
with local equations. Finite limits, colimits, adjunctions, and Kan-extension calculations become
finite universal-property certificates: proposed cones/cocones and mediating arrows, with uniqueness
checked by enumeration under explicit hom-set bounds.
For algebraic geometry, affine charts, localizations, and Čech diagrams are finite presentations.
Equality in a localization or quotient is witnessed by an exponent, ideal combination, or module
relation. Glue representatives locally, prove compatibility on overlaps, and seal the representatives
after showing the global observer is invariant. General schemes or unbounded covers are not silently
encoded by one finite atlas; the theorem states the presentation class to which it applies.

11.9 Diophantine geometry and finite descent
For a polynomial system F (⃗x) = 0, a height bound H produces a finite box and hence an exhaustive
enumerator. A complete-list certificate contains the accepted points and either the full box scan
or a compressed exclusion certificate. Soundness and completeness inside the box are primitive
recursive. If a theorem proves every solution has height at most H, SCR seals the height certificate
and exports finiteness or the exact list.
Descent data—covering curves, Selmer representatives, local tests, and rank-decreasing measures—
are finite certificates when their code ranges are explicit. A uniform Mordell–Weil theorem additionally needs a producer for a complete covering list and termination bounds. The verifier/producer
split allows all finite descent arithmetic to be formalized without overstating that uniform result.

12.1 What has genuinely been resolved
Four improvements go beyond renaming prior contract graphs.
(1) Choice is classified by escape, not by finiteness. Earlier normal forms tend to select a least
accepted global code whenever finite witnesses appear. SCR proves that a proof-local witness
needs no selector or bound at all. This materially reduces the obligations for noncanonical and
conditionally produced objects.
(2) The boundary theorem is already a final object theorem. A missing producer does not
block the verifier and extractor: they compile to (∃c V ) → G or a context theorem now.
(3) Dependent local witnesses compose without global packing. Nested existential elimination handles finite dependent DAGs directly. Whole trace coding is introduced only at
object-variable horizons.
(4) The open bridge is tied to the same contract interface. Local hypotheses and arbitrary
query terms are first-class, so a packet need not close every theorem merely to cross between

Scoped Certificate Realization for iPRA

24

repository formalizations.

12.2 What no framework resolves
A generic framework cannot prove a uniform height bound, discover a missing analytic rate, decide
a singular branch continuously, or turn a non-effective compactness theorem into a finite cover.
Its contribution is to reduce each application to a small, exact producer statement and to prove
everything downstream of that statement in a reusable way. This is a stronger result than an
informal claim of “formalizability”: it identifies the theorem whose absence is mathematically
decisive.

13.2 Colliding algebraic branches
Consider a polynomial family whose valid root certificates are ordered lists of isolating boxes. Away
from the discriminant, a positive separation gap allows stable ordering and exact labels. At a
collision, different valid certificates may permute or merge boxes.
A packet that exports “root number 2” everywhere is therefore asking for an unstable named
certificate. SCR instead uses the following layers.
(i) Validate a finite cloud of boxes and multiplicities.
(ii) Export symmetric observations—sum, product, power sums, resultant factors—by exact
observer erasure.
(iii) Export the union of boxes with a diameter/refinement theorem.
(iv) On a subdomain with a proved separation g, apply the gap lemma and recover exact labels.
The Python example uses all six permutations of the rational roots (−2, 1, 3). The first-root observer
is rejected as unstable, while sum, product, and sum of squares are certified as (2, −6, 14).
The same pattern applies to eigenvectors: the eigenspace projector or characteristic polynomial is
stable; a normalized eigenvector requires a simple eigenvalue and a sign/orientation convention with
an apartness guard. For rotations, a quaternion or rotation matrix is stable; an exact axis vector
requires exclusion of the identity/half-turn singular cases or an approximate axis cloud.

13.3 Finite carrier maps in topology
Let K, L be finite simplicial complexes and let C(v) be a nonempty finite set of vertices of L for
every vertex v of K. Assume the strong carrier condition: for each simplex σ of K, the union
S
v∈σ C(v) spans a simplex of L. A selection certificate chooses f (v) ∈ C(v).
The certificate validator is finite. Every valid selection is simplicial. For two selections f, g, the
vertices of f (σ) ∪ g(σ) lie in the same carrier simplex, so the maps are contiguous. Over F2 , the
standard prism operator is a finite chain homotopy between their induced chain maps. Therefore
their homology maps agree.
The selection is sealed and the induced homology map is an exact observer. No canonical vertex
choice is needed. A geometric theorem about an actual compact space additionally needs a producer
connecting a requested scale to K, L, C: a finite net, cover, and modulus. The combinatorial theorem
can be fully formalized while that approximation producer remains a boundary.

13.5 Finite-consumer stability without a global modulus
Suppose a downstream proof uses an approximating sequence only at finitely many precision requests
n1 , . . . , nr , perhaps chosen adaptively from earlier finite observations. A trace can record a common

added, and bounded search finds a window avoiding their union. The final consumer theorem is
valid even when no global Cauchy modulus is known.
This does not prove convergence of the entire sequence. It proves the exact finite-use statement
needed by the consumer. If later mathematics supplies a uniform modulus, the same consumer
contract upgrades to a uniform named real without changing its internal proof.

13.6 A finite categorical gluing example
Take a finite diagram of presented modules and a finite cover graph. A section certificate gives one
module element per chart; compatibility witnesses give equalities on each overlap. A gluing certificate
proposes a global presented element and local equality witnesses. All checks are finite combinations
of module relations. If two global proposals have the same restrictions and a finite separation
certificate proves injectivity of the restriction map, their observers agree. Local representatives and
equality witnesses are sealed.
The generic theorem is a finite descent theorem for the specified presentations. A claim about a
broad geometric category additionally needs a producer that turns each requested object and cover
into those finite presentations. This separation prevents a finite calculation from being mistaken for
an unrestricted scheme-theoretic theorem.

14.4 Round trips and reflection
Preservation in both directions does not by itself give an open-sequent equivalence. One also needs
formula round trips.
Concise round trip. The current bridge reports that the Concise-to-Foundation-to-Concise translation is exact. Expose this equality for open formulas and show it commutes with context maps.
Foundation round trip. The reverse round trip can replace a positive-arity zero symbol by a
composition of the nullary zero. Prove these terms equal in the Foundation theory, then prove
formulas equivalent by structural induction and equality substitution.
With local equivalences D ↔ DF C for every hypothesis and P ↔ P F C for the goal, use simultaneous
cut to replace the round-tripped context. This yields the desired open-sequent reflection theorem
up to the already documented zero-code normalization.
Primitive-recursive function codes require a structural induction. In the primitive-recursion constructor, compare the two interpreted iterators by qf induction on the recursion argument. This is
exactly the kind of theorem the bridge’s closed API previously avoided but the ordinary iPRA core
now supports.

14.5 Bridge acceptance tests
A convincing integration should include more than a closed sentence.
(B1) a two-variable equality theorem under a local equality hypothesis;
(B2) substitution of non-variable arithmetic terms after transport;
(B3) a theorem with a quantifier and one shifted local hypothesis;
(B4) a qf-induction theorem with parameters;
(B5) both round trips on a primitive-recursive symbol containing composition and recursion;
(B6) an SCR contract transported with its certificate hypothesis still open.
The last test is important: the open bridge should preserve the very local producer boundaries that
make the framework modular.

16 Relation to the repository’s prior frameworks
The proposal is best understood as a logical refactoring of existing strong ideas, with one new
organizing analysis.

Scoped Certificate Realization for iPRA

16.1 Finite-query normal form
FQNF packs all evidence for an explicit finite query into one bounded natural certificate, verifies it
with a fixed PR checker, and selects the least accepted code. This is an excellent serialization and
reflection normal form when all bounds are available and a named computation is desired.
SCR changes the default. Proof-local witnesses remain separate existential scopes; a master code is
created only when variable-horizon decoding or a named output requires it. Therefore SCR can
formalize a conditional theorem from an unbounded existential producer while FQNF must first
obtain a code cutoff. Under explicit bounds the two are intertranslatable: fuse SCR scopes to obtain
FQNF, or open one FQNF certificate and treat its fields as SCR contracts.

16.2 Certified bounded interaction programs
CBIP supplies a typed language of bounded interactions, finite strategies, relational viability, and
stable witness erasure. SCR adopts these mathematical mechanisms but makes ordinary existential
scope the primitive logical operation. The interaction language becomes an optional front end;
its compiler target is a small collection of Prf.exE, Prf.trans, trace, strategy, and observer
combinators. This reduces the amount of a large DSL that must be trusted or proved sound before
applications can migrate.

16.3 Native clause programs
Native clause programs use the checked KPrf calculus as an intermediate representation, with
checked cut, substitution, and export. SCR is compatible with that backend. A local quantifierfree arithmetic subgraph can carry a KPrf proof and export it to ordinary iPRA at the interface.
Existential sealing and rich logical conclusions are most naturally organized in Prf PRAAx, which
remains the canonical endpoint.

16.4 Observation graphs and staged contract kernels
Proof-carrying observation graphs already emphasize finite traces, backward demand, relational
witness clouds, and stable observations. Staged contract kernels emphasize specialization by
Prf.subst, context alignment by Prf.weaken, and composition by Prf.trans. SCR combines
these with a missing criterion: whether a certificate escapes. That criterion selects among existential
sealing, observer erasure, and bounded naming. It also supplies a direct proof of the conditional
theorem when a producer is missing.

16.5 What is novel, and what is not
Existential elimination, least bounded search, and invariant induction are of course standard logical
tools. The contribution claimed here is the repository-level synthesis:
(i) a static escape classification tied to exact Prf constructors;
(ii) a scoped alternative to mandatory global certificate packing;
(iii) dependent composition without selectors for finite proof DAGs;

Scoped Certificate Realization for iPRA

33

(iv) a uniform boundary-normal-form theorem for incomplete packets;
(v) a single trace/strategy/observer architecture explaining when finite codes are genuinely
necessary;
(vi) an integration plan that reuses the current checked arithmetic and makes open bridge transport
first-class.
The novelty is architectural and proof-theoretic rather than a claim to a new intuitionistic logic.

17 Limitations and conclusion
17.1 Limitations
This report does not provide a completed Lean implementation of Boolean reflection, trace coding,
strategy reflection, or the open bridge. It provides full human arguments, exact object-theory
endpoints, a no-placeholder structural kernel candidate, and a dependency-ordered path to those
proofs. The absence of a Lean executable in the artifact runtime prevented even the small candidate
from being elaborated, so no new kernel-checked theorem is claimed.
The framework does not prove the deep producer theorems identified in the packet catalogue.
In particular, it proves no general Faltings, Siegel, Mordell–Weil, effective Roth, Chebotarev, or
unrestricted analytic convergence result. It does not select exact branches through singularities,
derive moduli from arbitrary classical convergence proofs, or infer an enumeration cutoff from finite
cardinality.
The fixed-bounded-formula compiler is a schema indexed by host syntax. This is intentional.
Internalizing a universal interpreter would be a different and substantially harder arithmetization

Scoped Certificate Realization for iPRA

34

project, and a total universal function for all primitive-recursive programs is not the interface needed
by the applications.
Finally, ordinary Prf proof terms may still be large. Trace compression, shared Lean definitions,
and an optional KPrf backend address the main sources of blow-up, but empirical compilation will
determine which subgraphs should be flattened or translated.

17.2 Conclusions
The current ProveIt tree already contains the logical and arithmetic machinery needed for a
principled general framework. The missing step is to use that machinery at the right granularity.
A mathematical proof in iPRA should be organized around finite queries and local contracts.
Auxiliary certificates should remain existentially sealed unless something certificate-dependent must
escape. Exact invariant observations should replace arbitrary representative choices; approximate
clouds should replace unstable labels; a positive gap should be the explicit gate to exact naming.
Variable-length work should be represented by a whole trace or finite strategy and reflected by
a quantifier-free invariant. Missing height bounds, moduli, covers, or constants should appear as
formulas in the final theorem, not as prose in a “future work” section.
This organization produces a small, auditable compiler whose primitive steps are already checked:
substitution, weakening, simultaneous cut, existential elimination, and qf induction. It also explains
how the strongest ideas of the existing packet frameworks fit together. FQNF is the packed named
backend; CBIP is a bounded-interaction front end; KPrf is a compact qf backend; SCR is the
ordinary logical spine.
The immediate practical recommendation is therefore narrow: elaborate the supplied structural
kernel, implement the search and trace adapters against IPRADerivations, and migrate the four
pilots before designing another large proof-graph language. Success at those gates would turn
a substantial class of current “human-complete but uncompiled” packets into literal Prf PRAAx
derivations, while leaving the genuinely deep mathematical boundaries visible and honest.

A.1 One-certificate sealing
Let V ∈ Fm PTm (n + 1), G ∈ Fm PTm n, and Γ ⊆ Fm PTm n. The constructor
Prf.exE :

Γ ⊢PRAAx G

is exactly theorem 3.1. The side condition traditionally expressed as “the eigenvariable is not free in
G, Γ” is enforced by the types: G, Γ live at arity n, while V lives at arity n + 1. Consequently no
separate freshness proof is needed.

Weakening the original extractor inserts (∃V )↑ . Close existential elimination, then implication
introduction. This is why the source candidate’s proof is only three lines.

A.3 Boundary collection
Suppose a compiled continuation has context Br :: · · · :: B1 :: Γ. Repeated implication introduction
gives
Γ ⊢PRAAx B1 → · · · → Br → G,
with the order reversed relative to the context stack. If a user-facing order is required, use weakening
and hypotheses to prove the desired permutation, or compile a conjunction B1 ∧ · · · ∧ Br and
eliminate its fields. The context form is canonical for Lean because Prf.trans takes a family of
derivations for all members.
If a producer pi : Γ ⊢PRAAx Bi later becomes available, apply Prf.trans to replace that occurrence
while leaving the other boundaries as hypotheses. Thus packet completion is monotone: adding
producers does not change downstream extractors.

A.5 Trace reflection in arithmetic detail
Take Boolean failure terms, so every failure is 0 or 1. Define
b0 = 0,

bi+1 = bi + ei .

The checked natural-number arithmetic proves
bi + ei = 0 → bi = 0 ∧ ei = 0.
One proof uses bi ≤ bi + ei and ei ≤ bi + ei , then equality to zero and antisymmetry. Another
uses the zero-or-success decomposition: if either summand were a successor, the sum would be a
successor and hence nonzero. The first style reuses order lemmas; the second often gives a smaller
proof term.
The induction formula

Φ(i) = bi = 0 → I(si ) = 1



is qf because equality and implication preserve Fm.QF. At zero, s0 = init and b0 = 0; congruence
transports the initialization proof. At a successor, assume bi+1 = 0, split it, apply the induction
hypothesis to bi = 0, and instantiate local preservation at the current state and decoded action.
Defining equations for si+1 complete the congruence step.
If Trace(N, τ ) also contains a terminal check, put that check in eN and run the recursion for N + 1,
or conjoin it after the prefix invariant. The latter keeps the state horizon conventional.

A.6 Exact observers
Assume

V (c), V (d) ⊢PRAAx O(c) = O(d).

Define the output formula with an existential certificate. To prove single-valuedness, start from two
output hypotheses. Eliminate the first existential, then the second. The local context contains
V (c), O(c) = o, V (d), O(d) = o′ .
By independence, O(c) = O(d). Equality symmetry and transitivity give o = o′ . Since the result is
in the outer scope, close both existential eliminations.
The same proof works for an observed relation R(c, o) if pairwise valid certificates and their output
witnesses imply output equality. Thus the observer need not be a term; it may itself be a small
relational certificate.

A.7 Diameter and gap arithmetic
Suppose a rational metric is represented by a nonnegative numerator after a common positive
denominator is cleared. The triangle inequality has the form
D(x, z) ≤ D(x, y) + D(y, z).
Under the four assumptions of the gap lemma,
g ≤ D(ℓi , ℓj )
≤ D(ℓi , o) + D(o, o′ ) + D(o′ , ℓj )
≤ e + δ + e < g,
a contradiction by transitivity and irreflexivity of strict order. In object arithmetic, each rational is
a numerator/denominator record and the final strict inequality is cross-multiplied by the product of
positive denominators. All positivity assumptions belong to the validator.

A.8 Finite-strategy soundness
For a ∀x < B∃y < C(x) formula, a strategy table is simply a bounded array yx . Its verifier says
every digit is below C(x) and the matrix holds at (x, yx ). To prove the formula, introduce arbitrary
x, assume x < B, instantiate the verifier’s no-failure theorem at x, and introduce yx . For one more
universal level, table entries are subtables indexed by the preceding history. Fixed-shape soundness
is therefore a finite nesting of the same argument.
Completeness uses bounded search for a winning response at each existential history. A universal
node packages all finitely many recursively generated subtables. The proof that the package verifies
is a qf induction over the history enumeration. The table code bound is the product of explicit
move radices. No infinite strategy or function space occurs.

B.4 Substitution tests
Every record constructor needs simp lemmas showing that its terms and formulas commute with
simultaneous substitution. Tests should instantiate:
• a query variable by a nontrivial sum or product;
• a trace code by a paired term;
• a bound by a term containing the induction variable;
• an observer under two nested existential scopes.
These tests catch the most common de Bruijn errors before application proofs become large.
