\section{Stable observables: eliminating choice without coherent witnesses}
\label{sec:stable}

\subsection{The shortcut}

Backward viability is needed when local choices must be joined into a path.
Some applications have a simpler structure: many independent certificates may
exist, but every valid certificate determines essentially the same observable.
Examples include the sorted spectrum of a matrix, the root multiset of a
polynomial, the minimum value of a finite optimization problem, a determinant,
or a homology rank.  In this situation a coherent witness sequence is
unnecessary.

For each precision \(n\), let candidates satisfy
\(a<C(p,n)\) and a Boolean predicate \(\mathsf{Cert}(p,n,a)\).  Let
\(\Obs(p,n,a)\) be a rational observation in a coded pseudometric \(d\).
Assume nonemptiness and a direct cross-precision stability theorem
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{Cert}(p,n,a)\wedge\mathsf{Cert}(p,m,b)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d(\Obs(p,n,a),\Obs(p,m,b))\le E(p,n,m).
 \label{eq:pairwise-stability}
\end{equation}
for all bounded candidates.

Define \(s(p,n)\) as the least valid candidate and
\(x(p,n)=\Obs(p,n,s(p,n))\).

\begin{theorem}[Stable-observable choice erasure]
\label{thm:stable-erasure}
Under nonemptiness and \eqref{eq:pairwise-stability}, iPRA proves
\[
 d(x(p,n),x(p,m))\le E(p,n,m).
\]
If \(E(p,n,m)\le 2^{-r}\) whenever \(n,m\ge\mu(p,r)\), then
\(r\mapsto x(p,\mu(p,r))\) is a regular Cauchy name with the displayed
primitive-recursive modulus.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Least-search correctness proves that \(s(p,n)\) and \(s(p,m)\) are valid
certificates.  Substitute these two terms into the pairwise stability theorem.
No compatibility relation between the witnesses is used.  The regular-name
claim is immediate after substituting the modulus.
\end{proof}

This theorem is constructively stronger than choosing one infinite branch: it
shows that the branch is irrelevant to the observable.  It is also easier to
formalize.  The repository's checked combinator
\code{observableOfIndependentCertificates} already performs the object-theory
cut from two certificate derivations and a supplied stability derivation.  A
completed module needs only the bounded-search proofs and the concrete
stability inequality.

\subsection{Exact stability}

If \eqref{eq:pairwise-stability} has the exact conclusion
\(\Obs(p,n,a)=\Obs(p,n,b)\) at a fixed level, then the least selector is
extensionally canonical even when certificate codes are not.  This is useful
for finite invariants.  The proof does not require quotient types inside iPRA:
one proves an ordinary equality between observation terms.

\begin{corollary}[Exact certificate independence]
Suppose all valid certificates at input \(p\) have equal observations.  Then
any proof-producing certificate generator may be substituted for any other in
all theorems expressed solely through that observation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the equality form of \cref{thm:stable-erasure} to the two generated
certificates, then use equality substitution in the downstream formula.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite families and aggregate observables}

The same argument handles a finite family of independent witnesses.  A
certificate may contain several local pieces whose mutual compatibility is not
needed because the final aggregate is stable under replacing any component.
One proves replacement stability one component at a time and chains the
resulting inequalities.  For exact observables, repeated equality substitution
shows complete independence.

This yields a practical proof pattern:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item run or verify each finite producer independently;
\item extract its certificate by a trace theorem;
\item prove one local replacement estimate for the observable;
\item combine independent certificates by object-theory cut; and
\item export only the observable.
\end{enumerate}

The pattern avoids building a large synchronized state when no mathematical
synchronization is required.

\subsection{Decision rule: stability before viability}

For each nonunique construction, use the following order.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{S\arabic*.}]
\item Prove direct pairwise stability of the desired observable.  If successful,
      use \cref{thm:stable-erasure}; no paths are needed.
\item If only adjacent refinements are comparable, build finite paths with
      viability and use the shrinking theorem of \cref{sec:shrinking}.
\item If neither stability statement holds, retain the finite relation as the
      result and formulate downstream theorems relationally.
\item Demand an exact label only after proving a positive separation gap.
\end{enumerate}

This order often shortens proofs dramatically.  Root algorithms, for example,
may produce unrelated isolating-disk orderings at different precisions while
the induced root multiset is stable.  Sorting or matching individual roots is
then unnecessary until a separation theorem is available.
