\section{Stable observables and guarded quantitative mathematics}
\label{sec:observables}

A certificate is usually more detailed than the theorem's public output.
Different algorithms can produce different certificates for the same object.
The correct notion of coherence is therefore often a comparison theorem for an
\emph{observable} extracted from certificates.

\subsection{Independent-certificate comparison}

Let \(C(x,p,c)\) be a finite certificate predicate, \(O(c)\) a coded
observation, and \(D_p(u,v)\) a decidable closeness relation.  Suppose iPRA
proves
\begin{equation}
 C(x,p,c_1)\wedge C(x,p,c_2)
 \longrightarrow D_p(O(c_1),O(c_2)).
 \label{eq:independent-stability}
\end{equation}
Two producer nodes may generate \(c_1\) and \(c_2\) by entirely unrelated
bounded traces.  Once both certificate formulas have been derived, two cuts
with \eqref{eq:independent-stability} yield the observation theorem.  The
witnesses never have to be identified.

This is the abstract content of the checked
\texttt{observableOfIndependentCertificates} and
\texttt{twoLoopsStableObservable} combinators in the repository's
stable-observables packet.

\begin{theorem}[Witness-independent observation]
\label{thm:witness-independent}
If two trace nodes produce certificates \(c_1,c_2\) satisfying \(C\), and the
comparison theorem \eqref{eq:independent-stability} is available as an embedded
derivation, then the PCOG compiler produces an embedded derivation of
\(D_p(O(c_1),O(c_2))\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compile each trace node to its certificate formula using
Corollary~\ref{cor:trace-finalization}.  Use the local stability rule as the
next node in the proof graph.  Theorem~\ref{thm:proof-graph} composes the three
proofs.
\end{proof}

The theorem seems elementary, but it removes a major source of formalization
complexity.  One no longer needs to prove that pivot choices, root orderings,
finite covers, or reduction paths coincide.

\subsection{Regular rational balls}

A convenient observation type for real quantities is a rational ball
\((m,r)\), denoting all values within radius \(r\) of midpoint \(m\).  Validity
at precision \(p\) requires \(r\leq2^{-p}\).  Restriction enlarges the allowed
radius or simply reuses the same ball at a lower demand.

Operations have local contracts.

\paragraph{Addition.}
From balls \((m_x,r_x)\), \((m_y,r_y)\), output
\((m_x+m_y,r_x+r_y)\).  To achieve radius \(2^{-p}\), it suffices to request
both inputs at \(p+1\).

\paragraph{Multiplication.}
If \(|x|\leq M_x\), \(|y|\leq M_y\), then
\[
 |xy-m_xm_y|
 \leq M_xr_y+M_yr_x+r_xr_y.
\]
The demand transformer is computed from magnitude certificates and the target
\(p\).  Without magnitude data, multiplication does not have a uniform modulus
on all reals.

\paragraph{Inverse.}
If \(|x|\geq a>0\) and a ball for \(x\) has radius at most \(a/2\), then its
entire interval avoids zero and
\[
 \left|\frac1x-\frac1m\right|
 \leq \frac{2}{a^2}|x-m|.
\]
Thus the inverse node takes a rational apartness witness \(a\) and computes a
precision demand large enough for both avoidance and final error.  A mere
proof \(x\neq0\) is not accepted as a substitute.

\paragraph{Composition.}
If \(F\) has a certified modulus \(\omega_F(p,M)\) on a bounded region and
\(G\) produces inputs in that region, then the demand for \(F\circ G\) is the
composition of moduli.  The graph's reverse pass performs this calculation.

Every inequality can be cleared of denominators and expressed as a bounded
integer checker.  The analytic content lies in the local estimate, not in the
coding.

\subsection{Finite nets and approximate extrema}

Let \(X\) be represented by finite \(\varepsilon\)-nets \(N_p\), and let
\(f:X\to\Q\) have a certified modulus.  To approximate the infimum within
\(2^{-p}\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item request a net radius small enough that the modulus error is at most
\(2^{-(p+2)}\);
\item evaluate \(f\) at every net point to error \(2^{-(p+2)}\);
\item take the finite minimum of lower or midpoint bounds;
\item combine net and evaluation errors.
\end{enumerate}
All loops and minima are bounded PR operations.  No minimizer need be chosen.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-net infimum transfer]
\label{thm:finite-net-infimum}
Given PR finite-net producers, proofs of coverage, a certified modulus for
\(f\), and bounded evaluation certificates, the PCOG compiler constructs a
regular rational name for \(\inf f\) with an embedded error theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The reverse demand pass composes the target error with the modulus and
splits the remaining budget between net and evaluation errors.  A trace node
evaluates the finite table and maintains the invariant that the current
minimum bounds all processed values.  Coverage supplies, for every point, a
nearby net point; the modulus compares their values.  The terminal finalizer
combines the lower and upper inequalities.  Every universal statement over the
net is represented by a bounded fold.
\end{proof}

A coherent minimizer requires more.  If
\[
 f(x)\leq\inf f+\varepsilon\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d(x,x_*)\leq\rho(\varepsilon)
\]
with a quantitative uniqueness modulus \(\rho\), then all sufficiently good
finite minimizers form a shrinking cloud.  Theorem~\ref{thm:shrinking-viability}
constructs a coherent point name.  Without such a modulus, the infimum value is
the correct stable observable.

\subsection{Polynomial and matrix observables}

Finite algebra is especially well suited to observation nodes.

\paragraph{Polynomial identity.}
A coefficient list and a degree bound reduce identity to a finite conjunction
of coefficient equations.  A certificate can be a straight-line derivation,
a determinant, a remainder table, or a multiplication-closure table.  Its
checker is bounded.

\paragraph{Roots.}
The stable output is generally a multiset of isolating regions with
multiplicities.  Individual root labels require disjoint regions or a positive
separation bound.  Symmetric functions of roots are exact observables even at
collisions.

\paragraph{Symmetric matrices.}
Ordered eigenvalue lists are stable under perturbation, including repeated
eigenvalues.  Eigenvectors are not.  A gap certificate permits a stable
spectral projector or block count.  The repository's stable-observables packet
already develops this application at the human level; the PCOG framework
supplies the general composition and trace interfaces.

\paragraph{Determinants and norms.}
A determinant is a finite polynomial computation.  Nonvanishing used for
division needs an apartness or exact integer nonzero certificate.  Weighted
norms can convert common-zero questions on curves into degree bounds; a local
norm certificate then becomes a node feeding a finite counting argument.

\subsection{Metastability and finite windows}

A metastable statement has the form
\[
 \forall g\ \exists N\leq B(g,p)\ \forall i,j\in[N,N+g(N)]\;
 d(x_i,x_j)\leq2^{-p}.
\]
For each fixed coded \(g,p\), this is a finite observation theorem and can be
handled by bounded search.  It should not be silently converted into a global
Cauchy modulus; computable counterexamples show that such a conversion is not
available in general.

The graph therefore represents metastability as a finite witness-producing
node.  If an application later supplies a quantitative principle that turns
these witnesses into a modulus, that principle is a separate node with its own
proof.

\subsection{Exactness tiers}

PCOG outputs should be labeled by one of four tiers.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact code:} equality of finite data is proved.
\item \textbf{Exact observable:} witnesses may differ, but the exported finite
observable agrees exactly.
\item \textbf{Quantitative observation:} outputs agree within a stated rational
error with a PR modulus.
\item \textbf{Finite relation:} a bounded cloud contains all valid outputs and
compatibility is proved, but no stable collapse is claimed.
\end{enumerate}

Moving upward in this list requires an explicit theorem.  In particular,
finite relation to quantitative observation requires shrinking or comparison;
quantitative observation to exact observable requires a positive gap or
integrality/discreteness argument.
