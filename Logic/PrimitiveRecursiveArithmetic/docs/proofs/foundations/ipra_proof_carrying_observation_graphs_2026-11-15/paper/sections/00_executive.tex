\begin{abstract}
The ProveIt iPRA collection now contains a broad range of theorem-specific
encodings and several increasingly general finite-observation frameworks.  Its
most persistent formal bottleneck is not the absence of semantic algorithms:
it is the absence of a uniform, honest passage from finite algorithms and local
contracts to a completed derivation of the deeply embedded judgment
\[
   \Prf\;\PRAAx\;[]\;\Phi.
\]
This report develops a \emph{proof-carrying observation graph} (PCOG)
framework aimed at that bottleneck.  A PCOG is a fixed finite graph whose nodes
are exact primitive-recursive calculations, bounded traces with
quantifier-free invariants, finite relational witness clouds, stable
observations, or theorem-specific finalizers.  Every node carries a local
object-theory proof constructor.  A host-level compiler computes backward
precision demands, creates concrete primitive-recursive symbols for the fixed
graph, proves recursive components by one quantifier-free induction each, and
folds the resulting derivations by cut in topological order.  Because the graph
is staged into concrete syntax, the construction needs no total universal
primitive-recursive evaluator and no truth-to-provability reflection.

Complete human proofs are given for bounded Booleanization and least search,
finite-trace reflection, staged graph internalization, backward-demand
minimality, finite relational viability, shrinking finite-horizon selection,
stable-observable collapse under a positive gap, fixed-dimensional descent
fuel, simultaneous finite-window selection, and the equivalence between an
explicit height bound and a complete finite list for decidable coded objects.
These results repair several recurrent interface failures in the repository:
non-unique approximate witnesses are kept relational instead of being forced
through an unstable selector; exact branching is performed only with a
separation certificate; cardinality bounds are not confused with search
cutoffs; and bounded runs are converted into actual object derivations rather
than justified by ambient computation.

The framework does not manufacture the deep quantitative producers absent
from general Faltings, Siegel, Mordell--Weil, ineffective prime-distribution,
or singular-branch arguments.  It instead isolates those producers as precise
interfaces and proves that, once supplied, their verification, composition,
finite search, and theorem extraction are primitive recursive and suitable
for iPRA.  A dependency-ordered Lean plan is tied to the current
\texttt{ConciseIPRA} API and reuses the repository's already checked
\texttt{primitiveRecursionInvariant}, \texttt{primitiveRecursionAt}, and
stable-observation combinators.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Executive conclusions}

The central recommendation is to standardize future iPRA developments on the
following four-layer boundary.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{L\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Finite presentation.}  Every mathematical object is exposed only
through coded finite observations, bounded predicates, restriction maps, and
explicit precision or resource demands.
\item \textbf{Local proof package.}  Every operation supplies an actual
\(\Prf\;\PRAAx\) derivation of its local contract.  A host theorem, a Python
check, or a semantic Lean proposition is not accepted as a substitute.
\item \textbf{Staged graph compiler.}  A fixed graph is elaborated to concrete
primitive-recursive syntax.  Its recursive nodes are discharged by
quantifier-free induction; its acyclic dependencies are discharged by
proof-term composition.
\item \textbf{Observation theorem.}  The exported result is exact when a finite
certificate proves exactness, quantitative when a modulus is available, and
relational when uniqueness or a gap is unavailable.  No hidden choice is
inserted between these cases.
\end{enumerate}

The most important new theorem is the staged internalization theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:staged-internalization}).  It explains exactly why a general
framework can be broad without becoming a forbidden universal evaluator.  The
compiler is universal as a \emph{Lean proof constructor over a fixed finite
syntax tree}; the resulting arithmetic program is specialized to that tree.
There is no primitive-recursive function inside arithmetic that interprets
arbitrary encoded primitive-recursive programs.

The framework resolves five recurrent difficulties at the architectural
level.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.96\textwidth}{@{}p{0.25\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Recurrent difficulty & Resolution in this report \\
\midrule
Semantic algorithm but no embedded theorem & A trace package contains base,
step, and finalizer derivations and compiles to \(\Prf\;\PRAAx\) by the checked
primitive-recursion invariant rule. \\
Non-unique approximate witness & Carry the entire finite verified cloud and
use backward viability; select only a finite-horizon path by bounded search. \\
Unstable exact branch or sign & Export a relation or interval until a positive
separation gap is proved; then use the gap-collapse lemma. \\
Cardinality mistaken for effective search & Require a height/cutoff producer;
prove that a cutoff and a complete list are interconvertible, while a bare
cardinality bound is not used as one. \\
Changing precision and nested algorithms & Run a reverse demand pass, then a
forward verified trace, with one explicit invariant per recursive component. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The accompanying artifacts are intentionally auditable.  The Python module
implements the finite graph, viability, gap, window, height, and descent
algorithms over exact rationals; ten tests are recorded as passing.  The Lean
folder contains a concrete proof-graph core plus blueprint interfaces and a
milestone plan.  The report and theorem ledger never identify executable
examples with universal proofs.
