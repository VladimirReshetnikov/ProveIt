\section{Representation discipline: finite data first}
\label{sec:representation}

A framework for mathematics in iPRA succeeds or fails at its interfaces.  The
logic is intuitionistic and the only unbounded computational objects available
uniformly are primitive-recursive functions.  It is therefore misleading to
begin with an ambient real number, an arbitrary countable sequence, an
unspecified algebraic closure, or a choice of one representative from every
fiber.  The correct starting point is finite coded data together with the
properties that later computations are allowed to inspect.

\subsection{Codes and finite observations}

A \emph{code sort} is represented by a natural number together with a
quantifier-free validity predicate.  Tuples, lists with a declared length,
finite tables, integer and rational numbers, polynomials of bounded degree,
matrices of bounded dimensions, and finite simplicial data all fit this
pattern.  The choice of pairing function is immaterial once fixed, but every
read, write, length, and well-formedness operation must be primitive recursive
and accompanied by its defining equations.

\begin{definition}[Finite presentation]
A finite presentation of a mathematical object class consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a code predicate \(\Valid_X(x,a)\), where \(a\) is an observation or
precision request;
\item a restriction function \(\rho_X(x,a,b)\), defined when \(b\leq a\),
which lowers an observation at demand \(a\) to demand \(b\);
\item a bounded equality or compatibility predicate
\(x\equiv_{X,a}y\);
\item embedded iPRA proofs of identity, transitivity where needed,
restriction validity, and restriction coherence.
\end{enumerate}
All functions and predicates are represented by fixed primitive-recursive
symbols.
\end{definition}

The object being represented need not have a single canonical code.  In fact,
forcing canonicality is often the source of nonconstructive behavior.  The
framework uses two variants.

\begin{itemize}
\item A \emph{functional presentation} has a specified producer
\(P_X(z,a)\) and proves \(\Valid_X(P_X(z,a),a)\).
\item A \emph{relational presentation} has a finite bound \(B_X(z,a)\) and a
predicate \(R_X(z,a,c)\); the valid outputs are the finite cloud
\(\{c<B_X:R_X(z,a,c)\}\).
\end{itemize}

Functional output is convenient but relational output is the more general
primitive.  A functional node is the singleton-cloud special case.  A
relational node can later collapse to a function when an isolation or
uniqueness certificate is available.

\subsection{Quantitative names instead of completed infinities}

For metric objects one works with regular finite observations.  A rational
regular name for a real number is a primitive-recursive function
\(a:\N\to\Q\) with a proved modulus, for example
\[
   |a(p)-a(q)|\leq 2^{-p}+2^{-q}.
\]
The theorem exported to iPRA is a first-order statement about the numerator and
denominator codes returned by \(a\), not a quantification over an ambient set
of real numbers.  Other representations---nested rational intervals, dyadic
balls, polynomial jets, finite spectral lists, or finite covers---are often
better suited to a particular operation.

The key design rule is that every operation states its demand.  Addition of
regular names may ask both inputs at \(p+1\).  Multiplication needs magnitude
bounds in addition to precision.  Inversion needs a positive apartness
certificate such as \(|x|\geq 2^{-a}\), and its demand depends on \(a\).
Differentiation of a jet reads one extra coefficient.  A root deflation step
can require a separation bound and a longer coefficient prefix.  These are
mathematical hypotheses, not implementation details.

\begin{principle}[Demand is part of meaning]
A finite observation is meaningful only relative to the demand for which its
validity has been proved.  A node that consumes an observation must expose a
primitive-recursive transformer mapping its requested output demand to all
input demands needed by its local proof.
\end{principle}

This principle rules out a common informal step: ``take sufficiently accurate
approximations.''  In a PCOG, ``sufficiently'' is a concrete term computed by
the reverse demand pass.

\subsection{Apartness, equality, and guarded operations}

Constructive mathematics distinguishes equality from quantitative
separation.  A proof of \(x\neq0\) need not supply a computable lower bound on
\(|x|\).  Operations whose modulus blows up at zero must therefore be guarded
by explicit data.

For a rational name \(x_p\), an apartness certificate can have the form
\[
  \mathsf{Apart}(x,a) :\equiv |x_{a+2}|>3\cdot 2^{-(a+2)},
\]
together with a comparison theorem implying \(|x|\geq 2^{-(a+2)}\).  An
inverse node takes \(a\) as input, requests enough precision to keep the
approximating interval away from zero, and proves the standard rational error
estimate.  The operation is total on the coded pair \((x,a)\) satisfying the
guard; it is not asserted total on an unguarded real name.

The same pattern applies to:
\begin{itemize}
\item selecting a side of a root or an orientation of an axis;
\item dividing by a determinant, discriminant, Jacobian, or leading
coefficient;
\item isolating a spectral block;
\item choosing one analytic branch;
\item turning an approximate minimizer into an exact discrete label.
\end{itemize}

When a guard is unavailable, the framework retains a finite relation: both
sides, all nearby roots with multiplicity, the whole eigenspace block, or all
compatible branch codes.  This is not a failure to finish the construction;
it is the constructively correct output type.

\subsection{Certificates and producers}

A \emph{producer} constructs candidate data.  A \emph{verifier} checks a
bounded certificate.  In iPRA the two should be separated even when the same
algorithm happens to perform both tasks.

A certificate predicate \(\Cert_X(z,a,c)\) must be primitive recursive.  A
local soundness theorem has the form
\[
 \Cert_X(z,a,c)\longrightarrow \Valid_X(\Obs_X(c),a),
\]
and is supplied as an embedded derivation.  Completeness may be one of three
kinds.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Constructive completeness}: a PR producer returns a certificate.
\item \emph{Bounded exhaustive completeness}: a finite search bound is
supplied and every valid witness occurs below it.
\item \emph{Conditional completeness}: an external theorem supplies a
height, cover, modulus, or separation certificate; the framework verifies and
uses it.
\end{enumerate}

This distinction is critical for deep arithmetic geometry.  Once a valid
height bound is given, enumeration is routine and formalizable.  Producing the
bound is the deep theorem.  The framework should make the latter obligation
small and visible rather than obscure it inside an unverified search routine.

\subsection{Restriction coherence}

Suppose \(c_a\) is valid at precision \(a\) and \(b\leq a\).  A coherent
presentation proves
\[
 \Valid_X(c_a,a)\longrightarrow
 \Valid_X(\rho_X(c_a,a,b),b).
\]
If producers are not canonical, one additionally proves finite uniqueness:
any two valid observations at precision \(b\) are equal, equivalent, or close
in the exported observation metric.  This yields two useful modes.

\begin{description}
\item[Exact coherence.] Restrictions agree as codes or under a decidable
congruence.  This is typical of finite algebraic identities, modular lifting,
and exact table computations.
\item[Observable coherence.] Witnesses can differ, but their compressed
observations are within a stated error.  This is typical of roots, spectra,
minimizers, local analytic charts, and finite covers.
\end{description}

Observable coherence is strictly more flexible.  It avoids proving that two
algorithms made the same arbitrary internal choices.  The final theorem
compares what the user actually observes.

\subsection{First-order statement design}

A headline theorem must ultimately be a formula in the repository's syntax.
The following checklist prevents a mathematically attractive statement from
becoming impossible to internalize.

\begin{enumerate}
\item All infinite objects are represented by fixed PR names or families of
finite observations.
\item Every quantifier over a finite structure has an explicit bound or a
coded list.
\item Every analytic assertion includes a rational error target and a PR
modulus or a finite certificate from which one is derived.
\item Every use of division or exact branching names an apartness or gap
witness.
\item Every search has a cutoff whose provenance is part of the theorem.
\item Every recursive proof identifies one quantifier-free invariant.
\item The final Lean endpoint is written explicitly as a
\texttt{Prf PRAAx} type.
\end{enumerate}

Following this discipline often changes the theorem's surface form, but it
usually reveals a stronger computational result: not merely existence, but a
uniform finite procedure and a verifiable quantitative contract.
