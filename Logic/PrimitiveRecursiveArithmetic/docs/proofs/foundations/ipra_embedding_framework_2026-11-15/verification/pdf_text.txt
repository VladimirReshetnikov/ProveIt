A Certified Finitary Presentation Framework
for Mathematics in Intuitionistic Primitive Recursive
Arithmetic
A common architecture for finite algebra, represented analysis,
geometry, probability, number theory, and constructive obstruction audits
Prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov
15 November 2026

Abstract
The proof packets under Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic exhibit a stable positive
pattern and a stable family of failures. Finite exact algebra, bounded search, finite sums, grid
arguments, and explicit descents usually fit intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic once their
loop invariants are written as quantifier-free Boolean equations. Difficulties arise when a textbook
proof silently quantifies over arbitrary functions, invokes an unspecified limit or compactness
principle, chooses an exact branch at a degenerate input, hides an ineffective constant, or stops at
an ambient Lean theorem instead of producing an object-language derivation of type PrfPRAAx.
This paper develops a unified Certified Finitary Presentation (CFP) framework. A mathematical
object is presented by finite observations, primitive-recursive precision transformers, domain
and apartness certificates, and bounded computation traces. The central finite-trace reflection
theorem reduces correctness to a base equation, a quantifier-free step invariant, and a final checker
implication. Regular names, finite nets, nested isolating certificates, robust overlapping branches,
and height/covering certificates are then reusable modules rather than theorem-specific prose
conventions.
The framework yields fully explicit constructive replacements for several reported obstacles:
all-precision induction is replaced by a finite query table at a free precision parameter; exact
branch selection is replaced by an overlapping decision or by a decision under a supplied gap;
abstract compactness is replaced by primitive-recursive nets; exact roots and axes are represented by
coherent isolating data; and Diophantine finiteness is factored into a finite verifier plus the genuinely
mathematical production of a height or covering certificate. It also proves sharp boundaries: there
is no total universal primitive-recursive evaluator; a bound on the number of solutions does not
provide a search cutoff; and exact selections known to imply LLPO, LPO, or Markov principles
cannot be recovered by a change of encoding.
A detailed Lean plan is given against the existing declarations PR, PTm, PRAAx, PRA.qfInd, NFm,
NPrf, and pra_iff_native. The accompanying archive contains LaTeX and PDF, a machinereadable difficulty matrix, a Lean semantic prototype and object-language blueprint, and an exact
Python certificate kernel with passing regression tests. The Lean files are an explicit completion
plan, not a claim that the generic embedded reflection theorem has already been kernel-checked.

1

Scope, standard of proof, and snapshot

1.1

The target is an arithmetic derivation

The phrase “formalizable in iPRA” is used here in a strict sense. The final mathematical assertion
must be a first-order formula over natural-number codes. Its functions are fixed primitive-recursive
symbols, its logic is intuitionistic, and every use of induction has a quantifier-free induction formula.
A completed Lean endpoint therefore has the shape
Γ ⊢ [PRAAx] φ,
not merely an ambient theorem about Lean’s Nat, Rat, or Real. The latter can validate an algorithm
or an algebraic identity, but it does not by itself construct a derivation in the embedded theory.
The existing ConciseIPRA development supplies both sides of this boundary. Its ordinary
presentation has the types PR and PTm, the axiom family PRAAx, and the derived rule PRA.qfInd. Its
native scalar calculus has iterator terms, equation formulas, the two binders Q and D, and derivations
NPrf. The theorems pra_iff_native and native_iff_pra faithfully transport an already completed
arithmetic derivation. They are bridges, not automatic mathematics compilers.

1.2

Repository snapshot and audit scope

The public repository was inspected at main-branch commit
7d492fbfc5fee3b2b9dec0b2623b0b82effe05f2.

The proof-packet index at that snapshot records forty-eight delivered packets in algebra, analysis,
analytic and Diophantine number theory, combinatorics, geometry, probability, and topology. The
index also warns that packets were filed as delivered: placement did not rerun their code or compile
their Lean files. Some contain complete finite arguments; some contain uncompiled blueprints, explicit
sorry obligations, uninstantiated analytic contracts, or deliberately isolated impossibility results.
This paper is therefore not a blanket certification of the packets. It uses them as a large empirical
sample from which to extract common proof shapes and common failure modes. Statements about a
particular packet are limited to the paths listed in section B. The positive theorems proved below are
framework theorems: finite-trace reflection, regular-name calculus, robust branching, finite-net search,
and certificate transformations. They do not supply the missing deep analytic or arithmetic estimates
in a packet whose report explicitly leaves them open.

1.3

Three notions that must not be conflated

There are three useful but distinct levels of completion.
Definition 1.1 (Semantic implementation). A semantic implementation is executable code or an
ambient Lean theorem that computes the intended finite object and proves a proposition about its
ordinary meaning.
Definition 1.2 (Arithmetic proof architecture). An arithmetic proof architecture specifies naturalnumber codes, fixed primitive-recursive functions, exact formulas, induction invariants, bounds, and a
dependency-ordered route to an embedded derivation.
Definition 1.3 (Embedded completion). An embedded completion is an actual derivation in PR AA x ,
optionally transported to NP rf , with an axiom audit of the final Lean declaration.
A packet may be strong at the first two levels while incomplete at the third. The framework is
designed to make the transition from the second to the third mechanical enough that the remaining
gap is visible and localized.

What “general” can mean in a first-order theory

A textbook theorem often begins “for every continuous function” or “for every real random variable.”
Literal higher-type quantification is not available in first-order iPRA. There are three legitimate
replacements.
1. A fixed-evaluator schema: choose a primitive-recursive evaluator a(p, k) externally, add its
regularity or domain assumptions, and prove the resulting first-order formula uniformly in the
numerical parameters p.
2. A finite-data theorem: make a rational table, finite law, polynomial, graph, triangulation, or
certificate the numerical input. This is a single uniform first-order theorem.
3. A trace-relative theorem: input a finite computation or proof trace and prove that acceptance by
a primitive-recursive verifier implies the desired arithmetic conclusion. Trace production may be
specialized, while verification remains uniform.
The last form is central. It gives generic infrastructure without postulating a total universal
evaluator for all primitive-recursive programs, which is impossible by diagonalization (theorem 11.1).

1.5

Contribution and limits

The main contribution is a reusable normal form for positive proofs:
fixed numerical input and precision −→ bounded trace
−→ Boolean invariant −→ checked observation
The exact or asymptotic theorem is recovered only after the finite observation has been proved for an
arbitrary free precision parameter. This order is the logical discipline that prevents a hidden use of
completeness, countable choice, or induction on a quantified Cauchy assertion.
The framework resolves many formulation and compilation difficulties. It does not turn ineffective
constants into effective ones, produce rational representatives from abstract Selmer data, or uniformly
choose an axis at the identity rotation. In those cases it identifies a minimal positive theorem and
isolates the missing producer or the constructive obstruction.

2

Diagnosis of the existing proof packets

2.1

The successful pattern

Across the packets, the arguments most naturally internalized in iPRA share six properties.
1. Every object used by the calculation has a finite code: an integer, rational, finite list, polynomial,
matrix, finite probability law, triangulation, or bounded computation state.
2. Every loop has a displayed primitive-recursive bound.
3. Its invariant is an equality or inequality of coded finite objects. Bounded universal assertions are
compressed into Boolean folds.
4. Every analytic symbol is backed by a finite approximant and an explicit error estimate; no appeal
is made to an abstract completion axiom.
5. Every division, normalization, pivot, or chart carries a positive denominator or apartness certificate.
6. The desired theorem is obtained by introducing the precision variable only after the finite
calculation has been proved.
Finite polynomial identities are the model case. Normalize both sides to a coefficient list, verify
coefficient equality, and prove evaluation respects normalization by induction on the finite list. The
same structure appears in finite sums, product telescoping, exact rational probability, grid parity,
bounded point searches, and descent traces.

Representative packets

The diagnosis is especially visible in the following reports.
Euler–Rodrigues. The unrestricted exact unit-axis selector for arbitrary represented rotations
is reported to imply LLPO. The same packet obtains rational exact axes, separated exact axes,
arbitrary-precision approximate axes, and uniform quaternion data. This is the canonical example of
replacing a discontinuous selector by robust data rather than weakening arithmetic by an extra logical
principle.
Dirichlet and Kronecker approximation. Sharp closed endpoints, a real-valued denominator
bound, and density from negative irrationality alone lead respectively to LLPO, LPO, and a Markovtype principle. Strict inequalities with margins and positive separation moduli survive. Thus “add a

quantitative margin” is not cosmetic; it changes a non-realizable selection problem into finite rational
comparison.
Faltings, Siegel, and Mordell–Weil. These reports isolate missing height bounds or covering
representatives. Finite search below a supplied height is straightforward; producing the height is
the hard theorem. A complete point list and a height bound are mutually convertible in the coded
setting, while a mere cardinality bound is not. Mordell–Weil descent becomes constructive relative
to actual quotient representatives, but finite abstract quotient data do not automatically produce
rational points.
Prime distribution. Dirichlet and finite sieve inequalities can be made explicit. The full Siegel–
Walfisz and Bombieri–Vinogradov routes retain ineffective or exceptional-zero input. The correct
modular theorem checks a supplied zero-free/exceptional certificate and propagates its constants; it
must not pretend that verification also produces the certificate.
Central limit theorems. Finite rational laws and finite product spaces give exact sums and exact
independence. Infinite or continuous laws require tail, quantization, and continuity moduli. Rational
thresholds avoid undecidable comparison with an irrational normalized sum; transfer to a CDF metric
uses a continuity or anti-concentration estimate.

2.4

A unifying interpretation

These examples suggest that the fundamental object is not a “real number” or “continuous map” in
isolation. It is a bundle of finite interfaces:
code and domain guard
modulus

finite observations

bounded trace

error or residual

soundness certificate

The CFP framework makes this bundle explicit. Once it is explicit, the same arithmetic lemmas
support analysis, geometry, probability, and number theory, while the genuinely nonconstructive or
ineffective step is no longer hidden inside notation.

3

The arithmetic base and its usable normal forms

3.1

Ordinary iPRA

We use the repository’s ordinary first-order presentation. For each arity r, PRr contains symbols
generated from zero, successor, projections, composition, and primitive recursion. Terms PTmn are
built from n variables and these symbols. The theory PRAAx contains the defining equations, successor
nonzeroness and injectivity, and induction for quantifier-free formulas. Intuitionistic natural deduction
is provided by Deduction.lean.
A bounded algorithm belongs to the theory in two different senses. First, its output function is a
fixed PR symbol because bounded loops and finite folds are primitive recursive. Second, its correctness
must be proved. That proof is usually an instance of PRA.qfInd; primitive recursiveness alone never
implies correctness or truth.

3.4

Totalization and guarded laws

The natural-number universe has no partial functions. Every partial mathematical operation is
implemented by a total function plus a domain predicate. For example, rational division may return
zero when the divisor code is invalid or zero, while its theorem states
Rat(x) = 1 ∧ Rat(y) = 1 ∧ Nonzero(y) = 1 −→ x/y has the expected cross-multiplication law.
This is preferable to trying to make every term intrinsically well typed. The domain bits become fields
of GoodTable, and invalid behavior is irrelevant under the theorem’s antecedent.

A relation may be universal when a function cannot be

A total primitive-recursive evaluator U (e, x) enumerating all unary primitive-recursive functions cannot

fails to be primitive recursive is a uniform function that always produces a terminating trace for every

production.

4

Certified Finitary Presentations

4.1

Definition of a presentation

A Certified Finitary Presentation is a proof-oriented interface rather than a new foundational ontology.
Definition 4.1 (CFP object). A CFP object family with numerical parameter p consists of:
1. a decidable code-domain predicate Dom(p) = 1;
2. finite observations obs(p, k), where k is a precision, scale, truncation, or grid parameter;
3. a rational or integer error radius err(p, k);
4. a regularity, consistency, or residual contract relating observations;
5. primitive-recursive bounds on all finite tables and traces used to compute or verify an observation.
The evaluator is fixed at the meta-level, or its individual computation is supplied by a finite trace.
Definition 4.2 (Certified transducer). A certified transducer from presentations X to Y consists of a
total primitive-recursive output algorithm, a primitive-recursive precision transformer µ, a bounded
trace system, and a soundness theorem saying that valid input observations at the finitely many
precisions requested by µ imply the requested output observation and error bound.
The transducer need not consume a single sample. It may request an adaptive finite table: later
indices can depend on earlier rational comparisons. The complete run is still a bounded trace. Its
input contract records every query actually made, and its soundness proof is an induction on the trace
length.

4.3

Four theorem shapes

The framework distinguishes four normal forms.
Definition 4.3 (Finite exact theorem). A finite exact theorem is
∀p [Dom(p) = 1 → Check(p, A(p)) = 1],
where A and Check are primitive recursive.
Definition 4.4 (Effective approximation theorem). An effective approximation theorem is
∀p ∀k [Dom(p) ∧ InputContract(p, µ(p, k)) → OutputContract(p, k, A(p, k))].
All quantities in the matrix are finite rational statements.
Definition 4.5 (Certificate-relative existence theorem). A certificate-relative theorem is
∀p, c [Certificate(p, c) = 1 → Conclusion(p, decode(c))].
The verifier is uniform. A separate theorem may construct c.
Definition 4.6 (Obstruction theorem). An obstruction theorem shows that a stronger uniform selector
or modulus would realize a principle not validated by the intended constructive semantics, or would
compute a known noncomputable function. The positive replacement is stated in one of the preceding
forms.
These forms make status compositional. A deep theorem can be incomplete only at its certificate
producer while its finite verifier, error propagation, and formal bridge are finished and reusable.

4.5

Proof-producing versus proof-checking modes

There are two legitimate deployment modes.
Compiled mode. For a fixed theorem, the algorithm and all helper functions are represented by
fixed PR symbols. Its trace is not an input; primitive recursion computes it. This mode gives the
strongest uniform theorem and usually the smallest final statement.
Certificate mode. A finite trace, isolating region, factorization, covering list, or analytic constant
table is input. A small uniform checker validates it. This mode is appropriate when production is
expensive, theorem-specific, or presently unknown, and it is essential when no total universal producer
can exist.
A mature development should often support both: certificate mode first, then a producer theorem
for the cases where an explicit construction is available.

Represented numbers, functions, and analytic operations

6.1

Integers and rationals

An integer may be coded as a sign bit and magnitude, or by the standard zigzag map. A rational is a
pair (z, d) with z ∈ Z and positive d ∈ N. Reduction to lowest terms is optional. Equality and order
are checked by cross multiplication:
(z, d) = (w, e) ⇐⇒ ze = wd,

(z, d) ≤ (w, e) ⇐⇒ ze ≤ wd,

under d, e > 0. Addition and multiplication use the unreduced formulas. Absolute value, maximum,
powers, finite sums, and finite products are primitive recursive.
Clearing denominators is more than an implementation detail. It converts the correctness predicates
for rational algebra into integer equations and inequalities, which are directly suited to quantifier-free
induction. Every rational theorem should therefore identify the common positive denominator or state
its cross-multiplied form.

Why no completeness axiom is needed

Robust control flow, gaps, and charts

7.1

Why exact trichotomy is the wrong interface

A finite algorithm can compare rationals exactly. It cannot in general decide whether two arbitrary
regular names are equal, nor choose the sign of a name that may be zero. Many classical proofs
use such a choice only to select a formula, pivot, or coordinate chart. The constructive repair is to
make the charts overlap, or to require a positive gap when a genuinely exact decision is part of the
conclusion.

Theorem 7.2 (Exact branch under a supplied gap). Suppose a contract supplies d > 0 with |x − q| ≥ d.

Stable finite pivoting

Let v0 , . . . , vm−1 be rationally approximated candidate vectors, and let ri be a nonnegative residual or

Lemma 7.3 (Robust pivot). Suppose some exact residual satisfies ri ≥ d, every sampled residual r̃i

Proof. For the good index i, r̃i ≥ d − e. Maximality gives r̃j ≥ d − e. Hence rj ≥ r̃j − e ≥ d − 2e ≥ d/2.
The maximum scan invariant says that the stored index dominates all processed samples and is a
quantifier-free Boolean fold. No exact comparison of represented residuals is used.
This is the generic core of stable column selection in matrix arguments, choice of a nonzero
polynomial coefficient, denominator selection in projective coordinates, and finite chart selection on
compact domains.

7.5

Overlapping atlases

A constructive atlas should be designed so that every point has at least one chart with a quantitative
denominator bound, and the chart-selection tests have overlap. For example, two formulas valid
respectively when c > −3/4 and c < 3/4 cover all c ∈ [−1, 1]. Approximate c to error at most 1/16
and compare it to zero. Either selected chart has a fixed denominator margin. On the overlap both
are valid, so no proof of unique chart selection is needed.
The same design applies to:
• quaternion charts for rotations;
• affine charts of projective space;
• inverse trigonometric formulas;
• root formulas with competing dominant coefficients;
• contour decompositions and local power-series disks.
Transition identities on overlaps should be finite polynomial/rational certificates. This replaces
topological choice by a bounded rational decision.

7.6

Projective and homogeneous output

Normalization often creates an artificial discontinuity. A nonzero kernel vector has a continuous
projective class even when no canonical unit vector is available. A polynomial root can be returned
as an isolating disk or cluster before an ordering is chosen. A rotation can be returned as a unit
quaternion pair with vector part allowed to vanish at the identity. A line or conic can be represented
by homogeneous coefficients with a positive maximum-norm certificate, postponing division until a
chart has been selected.
Principle 7.4 (Delay normalization). Whenever a theorem is invariant under scaling, sign, permutation, or another finite symmetry, state and prove the invariant conclusion first. Add a canonical
representative only under a separation or orientation certificate.
This principle converts many apparent logical obstacles into ordinary finite algebra.

Partial operations as certified transducers

A guarded inverse transducer takes a name x, a positive rational d, and a proof that |x| ≥ d. Its

The operation itself remains total on all codes, but only the guarded theorem assigns it the intended
mathematical meaning.

Finite nets, search, and exact objects from nested certificates

8.1

Effective finite nets

Definition 8.1 (Certified net presentation). A bounded metric-object family has a certified net
presentation when there are primitive-recursive functions N (k) and net(k, i), i < N (k), together with
a theorem that every valid input object lies within 2−k of some listed point. The coverage theorem may
compute the index from a finite approximation, or accept it as a certificate.
For rational boxes, simplices, circles with overlapping charts, finite probability simplices, and
coefficient-bounded polynomials, the standard grids are primitive recursive. Abstract compactness is
not assumed; the finite net and its coverage modulus are data.
Theorem 8.2 (Finite-net transfer). Let f have modulus µ on a certified net domain. If x is a valid
named point and i < N (µ(k)) satisfies dist(x, net(µ(k), i)) ≤ 2−µ(k) , then
|f (x) − f (net(µ(k), i))| ≤ 2−k .
The corresponding arithmetic statement is provable in iPRA.
Proof. This is the defining implication of the modulus instantiated at the finite net point. In the
arithmetic proof, approximate both sides to a smaller error, use the finite coverage certificate, and clear
denominators. All queried precisions are computed from k, so fixed-precision compilation applies.

8.2

Approximate existence by bounded minimization

Let a nonnegative residual R(y) vanish exactly on desired solutions and have a known continuity
modulus. Scan a finite net and return the least index with minimal sampled residual.
Theorem 8.3 (Approximate witness from a net). Suppose a valid named x satisfies R(x) = 0, and
|R(y) − R(z)| ≤ L dist(y, z) on the domain. On a net of radius r, the bounded minimizer y∗ satisfies
R(y∗ ) ≤ Lr + 2e,
where e is the error in each sampled residual. The algorithm and bound are primitive recursive.
Proof. Choose a net point yi within r of x. Then R(yi ) ≤ Lr. Its sampled residual is at most Lr + e.
The selected sample is no larger, and converting its sample back to the exact residual adds another e.
The scan invariant records the least processed index with minimal rational sample. It is quantifier free
after a bounded comparison fold.
This theorem is often the strongest uniform constructive replacement for an exact minimizer,
eigenvector, axis, or zero. It gives a witness at every requested accuracy without asserting that the
selected witnesses converge.

Coherent nested certificates

An exact represented object can be obtained when the finite witnesses are coherent.
Definition 8.4 (Nested isolating certificate). A nested certificate is a primitive-recursive sequence of
rational balls Bk = B(ck , rk ) such that
rk ≤ 2−k ,

Bk+1 ⊆ Bk ,

and a finite checker proves that every Bk has the desired existence property. If uniqueness is needed, a
separation certificate says that at most one solution lies in Bk .
Theorem 8.5 (Nested balls produce a regular name). The center sequence of a nested certificate is a
regular name after a fixed precision shift. Every property stable under the certified residual bounds
holds of that name. If each ball contains at most one solution, the represented solution is unique in
the name equality relation.
Proof. For l ≥ k, nesting puts cl in Bk , hence dist(ck , cl ) ≤ rk + rl ≤ 2−k + 2−l . For arbitrary k, l,
use m = min(k, l) and the same estimate, or symmetrize. This is exactly the regular-name condition.
A residual bound tending with the displayed dyadic rate yields the all-precision exact property by
fixed-precision compilation. If two represented solutions lie in every isolating ball, the uniqueness
clause at any requested precision bounds their distance; the positive-rational gap argument proves
name equality.
Applications include polynomial roots via isolating disks, solutions of well-conditioned implicit
equations, normalized eigenvectors under a spectral gap, and limits of contractive iterations. The
crucial input is not the word “compact” but the explicit refinement and isolation checker.

8.4

Approximate minimizers without an exact minimizer theorem

On a finite net, a minimum always exists by bounded scan. With a uniform continuity modulus, these
minima form an approximate-infimum name:
mk =

min

i<N (µ(k))

f (net(µ(k), i)).

Comparing two net levels through a refinement or through the true pointwise infimum bounds |mk − ml |.
Thus the infimum value can often be named without choosing a minimizing point. An exact minimizer
requires additional coherence, a compact choice principle, or a quantitative uniqueness condition. This
distinction is useful in optimization, spectral estimates, and extremal geometry.

8.5

Finite covers

Suppose every net point has a certified neighborhood on which one member of a finite family of local
assertions holds. A Lebesgue-number-style result is constructive only when the neighborhood radii
have a positive finite minimum that is actually computed. The checker scans the finite list, verifies
every radius, takes their minimum, and proves coverage by the net-transfer theorem. There is no
appeal to an abstract open-cover compactness theorem.

Search bounds and finite lists

Let P (a, x) be decidable and let h(x) be a primitive-recursive height such that all codes of height at
most B have a primitive-recursive finite enumeration.
Theorem 8.6 (Height bound and complete list). The following data are primitively and intuitionistically interconvertible:
1. a bound H(a) with P (a, x) → h(x) ≤ H(a);
2. a finite list L(a) that is sound and complete for P (a, ·).
Proof. Given H(a), enumerate all codes of height at most H(a) and retain exactly those passing the
decidable predicate. Bounded-filter correctness is a Boolean-fold induction. Completeness follows from
the height implication.
Given L(a), take the maximum of the heights of its entries, with zero for the empty list. If P (a, x),
completeness gives membership of x in the list; finite maximum correctness gives the height bound.
No excluded middle or choice is used.
A bound on the number of solutions is absent from this equivalence. It does not locate the solutions
and supplies no stopping criterion; a precise computability obstruction is proved in theorem 11.2.

Reusable domain libraries

The framework is most useful when it becomes a small collection of shared object-language libraries.
This section describes the interfaces and the proof reductions they support.

9.1

Finite algebra and polynomial certificates

A multivariate integer polynomial is a finite list of coefficient/exponent pairs in a canonical order.
Addition and multiplication merge and convolve those lists. Normalization combines equal monomials
and removes zero coefficients. Evaluation is a finite fold.
Proposition 9.1 (Polynomial certificate reflection). There is a primitive-recursive checker
PolyId(P, Q, c) such that iPRA proves: if c is an accepted normalization certificate for P − Q, then
∀x

P (x) = Q(x).

The same method checks rational identities after multiplying by a certified positive common denominator.
Proof. The certificate lists every normalization step: sorting, combining equal monomials, and deleting
zeros. The checker verifies local list equations. Induction on the certificate length proves that evaluation
is preserved. Acceptance says the final list is empty, whose evaluation is zero. The induction invariant
is equality of two integer evaluations and is quantifier-free. Variables x remain free throughout and
are universally introduced at the end.
This library handles ring identities, determinant expansions, adjugate identities, trigonometric
polynomial identities in sin and cos, and the finite coefficient parts of power-series proofs. It also
separates two checks that are often conflated: a computer algebra system may produce the certificate,
while the small arithmetic checker proves its soundness.
Algebraic roots. A root should be represented first by an isolating rectangle or disk, a root count,
and a refinement procedure. Polynomial arithmetic, interval bounds, and a finite argument-principle or
subdivision certificate validate each region. Repeated roots are represented by clusters with multiplicity
data. An ordered list of individual roots is added only under pairwise separation. This resolves the
branch discontinuity at collisions without weakening the algebraic theorem.

Linear algebra and geometry

Matrices over coded rationals are finite lists. Matrix multiplication, transpose, determinant, minors,
adjugate, dot products, and cross products are finite polynomial operations. Fixed-dimension identities
are direct polynomial certificates; variable-dimension identities use fold invariants.
Nonsingular systems. Cramer’s rule and adjugate inversion are constructive under a determinant
apartness witness. The transducer carries a lower bound on |det A|, requests sufficient entry precision,
and proves the inverse error by theorems 6.5 and 6.6. Gaussian elimination is also possible, but every
pivot choice must use a robust lower-bound scan.
Singular systems and eigenvectors. An exact nonzero kernel vector is not a continuous function
of a singular matrix near rank changes. The unconditional output should be one of:
• a projective kernel certificate;
• a residual-small unit vector at requested precision;
• an exact normalized vector under a supplied minor or spectral gap;
• a coherent isolating subspace projector rather than a basis.
Finite-net minimization of ∥Av∥2 on a rational sphere chart gives the approximate version. A gap
promotes it to a stable exact name.
Euclidean and projective geometry. Points, lines, circles, conics, and incidences are homogeneous
coordinate codes. Classical incidence theorems often reduce to determinant or polynomial identities
after nondegeneracy denominators are recorded. Power-of-a-point, Pappus, Ceva, Menelaus, and
many circle theorems fit this pattern. The library should expose determinant certificates, signed-area
predicates, and chart transitions, not a large opaque geometry ontology.
Trigonometry and spherical geometry. Use regular names for sin and cos built from finite
series or a circle parameter, and state identities first as polynomial equations in a pair (c, s) satisfying
c2 + s2 = 1. Division-based tangent or half-angle forms require denominator gaps. Spherical theorems
should prefer vector dot and triple products; inverse angles are added via overlapping charts and
separation certificates.

9.3

Real and complex analysis

The shared analytic library should have four layers.
1. Regular names and interval arithmetic. This is the numerical kernel of section 6.
2. Finite sums, products, and series tails. Every named special function exports a truncation
algorithm, magnitude bound, and tail modulus.
3. Mesh calculus. Integration, differentiation, and changes of variable use finite mesh sums plus
local remainder moduli.
4. Local analytic data. A holomorphic chart contains coefficient names and a majorant on a larger
rational disk. Cauchy estimates and termwise operations become finite tail calculations.
A contour is a finite polygonal or piecewise rationally parametrized path. A residue theorem
instance needs a positive distance from the path to every pole, isolating disks for enclosed poles, local
Laurent coefficient certificates, and a finite winding-number computation. These are exactly the inputs
that a classical diagram tends to suppress.
A uniform convergence theorem must include a primitive-recursive modulus. A pointwise statement
without a modulus generally does not support interchange of limit, integral, or derivative in iPRA.
Dominated convergence is replaced by finite truncation plus a tail or uniform-integrability modulus.

Elementary and Diophantine number theory

Bounded trial division, Euclid’s algorithm, extended gcd, modular arithmetic, finite factorization,
valuations below a supplied bound, and Chinese remainder construction are primitive-recursive finite
algorithms. Their proofs use state invariants such as
ri = Aui + Bvi ,

gcd(ri , ri+1 ) = gcd(A, B),

encoded by integer equations and a bounded decreasing measure.
Diophantine geometry adds a search interface:
• a decidable point predicate;
• a height and enumeration of bounded-height codes;
• a height, descent, Runge, or covering certificate;
• a finite verifier proving that the certificate applies to the input.
The generic descent theorem can then be fully embedded even when the production of a global height
certificate remains open.

9.5

Analytic number theory

Analytic-number-theory proofs should be divided into a finite arithmetic core and an analytic input
contract.
Definition 9.2 (Analytic input certificate). An analytic input certificate is a finite code containing
explicit constants, regions, exceptional cases, truncation lengths, and rational inequalities sufficient for
the later finite estimate. Its checker validates every finite algebraic and numerical condition.
For a zero-free region, for example, the certificate records the region parameters, a finite cover
or local argument data, bounds on coefficients and tails, and any exceptional zero with a separation
estimate. The downstream prime-counting proof is a certified transducer from this data to an explicit
threshold. The theorem is valuable even before a uniform certificate producer is known: it states
exactly which quantitative input is missing and prevents an ineffective existence statement from
entering unnoticed.

9.6

Finite and effective probability

A finite rational law is a finite list of rational values and natural weights with positive total weight.
Expectation is an exact finite weighted sum. A product law is a mixed-radix enumeration; independence
is an identity of finite sums rather than an axiom.
Moments, characteristic functions at rational arguments, convolution, and events with rational
thresholds are finite calculations. A Berry–Esseen or Lindeberg proof is then a chain of polynomial/trigonometric approximation certificates, finite product telescoping, and rational smoothing
estimates.
An infinite or continuous law is admitted only through finite rational approximants with explicit
tail and quantization moduli. For weak convergence, a bounded-Lipschitz or characteristic-function
metric is usually more robust than CDF comparison. Transfer to CDFs requires a continuity or
anti-concentration modulus near the threshold. This cleanly separates the finite probabilistic theorem
from the presentation of the input law.

Combinatorics and topology

Finite combinatorics is a natural fit: graphs, words, partitions, tableaux, simplicial complexes, and
finite colorings are codes, while recurrences and injections are bounded traces. Generating-function
proofs should be compiled to coefficient identities before analytic notation is introduced.
Constructive topology uses subdivisions and parity certificates. Sperner, Brouwer-style approximate
fixed points, polygonal Jordan separation, and finite-dimensional degree arguments can be stated on
rational grids with explicit moduli. Exact points arise from nested cells only when coherence is proved.
Dimension and invariance results require careful negative statements, but their local combinatorial
certificates still fit finite-trace reflection.

Resolution of the principal reported difficulties

This section applies the common framework to the difficulties identified in the repository. “Resolved”
means that the logical and representation problem has a precise constructive replacement and a clear
embedded-proof route. It does not mean that every deep theorem-specific estimate has been proved.

10.1

Unbounded precision and quantifier-free induction

Problem. A report defines equality or convergence by a formula quantified over all precisions, then
attempts to use that formula as an induction invariant. The invariant is not quantifier free.
Resolution. Use theorem 5.4. Keep requested precision k free. Compute a finite query table
containing only the samples needed at that k. Encode all bounded conditions as Boolean folds. Prove
the finite accumulator theorem by PRA.qfInd. Introduce k universally at the end. Global name
regularity is used only to derive the finite GoodTable by universal elimination.
Consequence. Most explicit series, mesh integral, finite-product, regular-name operation, and
finite probability proofs need no stronger induction. A genuine problem remains only when no
primitive-recursive modulus or finite query bound is available.

10.2

Euler axes, eigenvectors, and discontinuous selectors

Problem. At the identity rotation every unit vector is an axis. A uniform exact unit axis requires a
discontinuous choice; the Euler–Rodrigues packet gives an LLPO reduction.
Resolution. Use a four-tier interface:
1. unconditional exact homogeneous quaternion data, allowing zero vector part at the identity;
2. unconditional axis-and-angle approximation at every requested precision, with no claim that the
axes converge;
3. exact projective or normalized axis under a positive separation of the rotation from the identity
or under a nonzero-minor certificate;
4. exact rational axis for rational proper orthogonal matrices, where a finite nonzero cofactor or
column can be found by exact comparison.
Robust pivoting proves the separated case; polynomial certificates prove the Rodrigues identities. The
unrestricted selector remains correctly marked obstructed.
This pattern extends to eigenvectors at multiple eigenvalues, singular kernel vectors, root ordering
at collisions, and orientation choices.

Dirichlet and Kronecker endpoints

Problem. Closed sharp endpoints or density from negative irrationality ask the arithmetic to decide
a boundary or extract positive information from a double negation. The corresponding packet derives
LLPO, LPO, or a Markov principle from several unrestricted formulations.
Resolution. State strict approximation with a displayed rational margin, or accept a positive
irrationality/separation modulus. Finite pigeonhole and grid arguments then return an exact rational
witness. For matrix Kronecker, use a finite compatibility certificate and an approximation-or-smallrelation alternative. If the separation modulus rules out the relation branch, the approximation branch
follows.
The framework explanation is uniform: finite search can decide rational boxes; it cannot decide
whether a represented boundary value is exactly attained. An overlap or gap removes the discontinuity.

10.4

Faltings and Siegel: finiteness versus a stopping rule

Problem. A classical theorem says that a rational- or integral-point set is finite, or gives a bound on
its cardinality. A constructive complete list requires a height cutoff. The Faltings and Siegel packets
correctly do not claim a general cutoff.
Resolution. Build and embed the following modular theorem:
HeightCertificate(C, H) = 1 → CompleteList(C, Search(C, H)).
The checker verifies the curve, certificate hypotheses, and all finite search steps. Conversely, a complete
list supplies a maximum height by theorem 8.6. Thus the exact missing mathematical object is a
height-certificate producer, not finite search or list verification.
For Runge families, Fermat descents, or other explicit cases, the producer is available and the
whole theorem can be embedded. For general Faltings or Siegel, the verifier and equivalence theorem
remain useful while the producer is honestly open. A cardinality estimate is recorded as different data
and is never used as a cutoff.

10.5

Mordell–Weil and covering representatives

Problem. A finite quotient or Selmer computation need not provide rational points representing
every relevant coset. Descent needs actual representatives to write P = 2Q + Ri .
Resolution. Define a covering certificate consisting of a finite rational point list R, validity proofs
for its entries, and a checked covering relation or a finite trace deriving the needed decomposition in
the target family. From this certificate, a bounded rational-halving scan constructs a selector and the
generic height descent is finite-trace reflection. The descent theorem is therefore unconditional relative
to a checked covering certificate. Producing such certificates uniformly for arbitrary curves remains
separate.
This factorization improves the theorem interface: an uncheckable statement “assume representatives exist” is replaced by a finite object whose use is fully verified.

10.6

Ineffective constants in prime distribution

Problem. An asymptotic theorem needs a threshold N (A, q) before the later variable x. A classical
proof may only assert existence of a constant or may hide an exceptional-zero dependence. Choosing a
new threshold after seeing each finite x proves the wrong quantifier order.

Resolution. Separate two modules:
ProduceAnalyticCertificate(D) = c,
VerifyAndPropagate(D, c, A) = N.
The second is a finite arithmetic theorem and should be embedded first. Its input code contains every
constant, exceptional object, separation margin, and local estimate used downstream. Its conclusion

When a primitive-recursive producer is later proved for a class of data, the composition theorem yields
the unconditional result for that class.
This resolves the formal architecture and prevents accidental ineffectivity. It does not manufacture
the missing number-theoretic estimate.

10.7

Ambient Lean versus embedded proof

Problem. Many packets provide compilable or plausible Lean theorems about native data but stop
before an object-language derivation.
Resolution. Use a three-stage artifact policy:
1. semantic reference implementation and exact tests;
2. object-language specification with fixed PR symbols, formulas, and QF invariants;
3. actual PrfPRAAx construction, followed by pra_iff_native and an axiom audit.
A status table must mark the highest completed stage for every main theorem. The generic tracereflection module reduces stage three to a small number of shared proofs rather than reimplementing

10.8

Root choice and the fundamental theorem of algebra

Problem. A classical FTA statement may return an unordered multiset of roots, while a formal
development asks for a continuously ordered root list. Collisions make such ordering discontinuous;
analytic existence may also be written without a computable radius or winding certificate.
Resolution. Return finite root clusters with rational isolating disks and multiplicities. Refine each
disk by a checked subdivision procedure. The centers yield names by theorem 8.5. Split and order
clusters only under a positive separation certificate. Polynomial reconstruction from clusters is a
finite coefficient certificate. Thus the algebraic content survives without a uniform labeling choice at
collisions.

10.9

Central limits and arbitrary probability spaces

Problem. The textbook theorem quantifies over measures and real random variables, and CDF
events may involve irrational thresholds or discontinuities.
Resolution. Use finite rational laws as the exact core. State the finite triangular-array estimate at
rational raw thresholds. Present more general laws by finite rational approximants with primitiverecursive moment-tail and quantization moduli. Prove convergence first in a robust metric; transfer to
CDFs with an explicit continuity or anti-concentration bound. The unrestricted qualitative theorem
becomes a schema for effective presentations, not a literal higher-type sentence.

Sharp boundaries and impossibility arguments

A general framework is trustworthy only if it explains what it cannot encode. The following barriers
show that several recurring difficulties are mathematical, not deficiencies of notation.

11.1

No total universal primitive-recursive evaluator

Theorem 11.1 (Diagonal obstruction). There is no binary primitive-recursive function U (e, x) such
that every unary primitive-recursive function f has an index e satisfying f (x) = U (e, x) for all x.
Proof. If such U existed, then

g(n) = U (n, n) + 1

would be primitive recursive. By universality, choose e with g(n) = U (e, n) for every n. At n = e,
U (e, e) = g(e) = U (e, e) + 1,
a contradiction.
Consequently, a first-order theorem cannot receive an arbitrary PR function code and evaluate
it by one total PR symbol. Fixed evaluator schemas and finite trace verifiers are not a temporary
workaround; they are the maximal uniform interface compatible with primitive recursiveness.

11.2

A cardinality bound does not yield a height bound

Theorem 11.2 (No uniform conversion from “at most one” to a cutoff). There is a primitiverecursive decidable predicate P (e, t) such that for every e at most one t satisfies P (e, t), but there is no
primitive-recursive function H(e) satisfying
P (e, t) → t ≤ H(e)
for all e, t.
Proof. Let P (e, t) say that Turing machine e, on blank input, halts for the first time exactly at step t.
Bounded deterministic simulation is primitive recursive, so P is primitive-recursive and decidable. A
machine has at most one first halting time.
If a primitive-recursive H bounded every possible first halting time, the halting problem would
be decidable: simulate machine e for H(e) steps. If it has not halted by then, the implication says it
never halts. This contradicts undecidability of the halting problem.
Thus even a uniform cardinality bound of one carries no search-stopping information. The obstacle
in the Faltings and Siegel packets is exactly the missing location/height data, not the finite filtering
algorithm.

11.3

Exact sign selection and LLPO

One standard form of LLPO says: for every binary sequence with at most one entry equal to one,
either all even entries are zero or all odd entries are zero. From such a sequence α, construct the
regular real name corresponding to

(α(2n) − α(2n + 1))2−n−1 .

n≥0

The at-most-one premise gives an explicit tail modulus. If a uniform procedure always decides x ≥ 0
or x ≤ 0, then:

• x ≥ 0 rules out an odd-position one;
• x ≤ 0 rules out an even-position one.
When the sequence is all zero, both conclusions are valid. Hence real sign selection realizes LLPO.
The exact-axis and sharp-endpoint obstruction arguments in the inspected packets reduce their
selectors to this sign principle. Replacing the real encoding or choosing a different normal form cannot
remove the obstruction. Overlapping branches avoid it because they do not decide which side of an
exact boundary contains zero.

11.4

Negative inequality is not positive apartness

Intuitionistically, ¬(x = 0) need not provide a rational d > 0 with |x| ≥ d. Algorithms for inversion,
normalization, or density need the latter. A principle converting every negative nonzeroness proof
into a positive modulus is a Markov-style principle for represented numbers. The framework therefore
treats apartness witnesses as data and never derives them from bare negation unless the input class
has a specific finite argument.
For rationals and algebraic numbers with an isolating certificate, positive apartness can be computed.
For arbitrary regular names, it cannot.

11.5

Ineffective existence cannot be repaired by a verifier

A finite verifier may prove that any supplied constant table implies an asymptotic estimate. This does
not imply a primitive-recursive function producing such a table. The difference mirrors proof checking
versus theorem search: local verification may be elementary even when uniform production is open or
noncomputable.
Quantifier order detects the issue. The useful statement is
∀D ∀A ∃N ∀x ≥ N E(D, A, x),
with N produced before x. A family of proofs of ∀D, A, x ∃Nx ≤ x E(D, A, x) is not a substitute. The
finite-trace framework preserves the former order by making the threshold an output of a certificate
producer.

11.6

Semantic truth is not automatic iPRA provability

A primitive-recursive function can have true arithmetic properties whose proofs require induction or
consistency strength beyond a particular weak theory. Therefore the slogan “the algorithm is PR, so
iPRA proves it” is false. The framework always asks for the local invariant derivations (T0)–(T2).
The finite-trace theorem composes proofs; it does not create missing mathematical invariants.

11.7

Maximal positive statement principle

When an unrestricted theorem meets one of these barriers, the goal is not to stop at “impossible.” One
should determine the maximal positive family:
• approximate witness at every precision;
• exact invariant or projective data;
• exact witness under a positive gap;
• certificate-relative theorem;
• theorem for rational or finitely presented inputs;
• obstruction showing why the omitted uniform selector is stronger.
The Euler–Rodrigues and Dirichlet–Kronecker packets already exemplify this pattern. The CFP
framework makes it a standard deliverable for every future packet.

12.5

Finite query tables for names

For a fixed evaluator symbol a, define formulas RegularA, EqualAB, and ApartAB. A theorem-specific

• every instantiated regularity inequality;
• every domain and magnitude bound;
• every finite relation between repeated samples;
• all externally supplied certificate checks.
The proof of RegularA->GoodTable=1 is separate from trace induction. Universal elimination is
applied at each generated index; a fold combines the facts. For adaptive queries, an induction on the
trace stage derives the next instance at the term computed by the current state.

12.7

Robust branch and finite-net modules

The overlapping branch theorem is a short embedded rational inequality proof. Its implementation
inputs codes a, e, q, d, checks 4e ≤ d, compares a with q + d/2, and returns a branch bit plus one of
two checked margin inequalities. The exact-gap theorem consumes an apartness certificate.
The finite-net module needs:
• grid generation and a finite coverage theorem for each standard domain;
• bounded argmin correctness;
• a Lipschitz transfer lemma;
• nested-ball regularity and uniqueness.
Coverage is domain-specific. Boxes and simplices should be implemented first; sphere/projective charts
can then reuse robust branch and normalization.

12.8

Height and complete-list module

Formalize theorem 8.6 generically over fixed symbols for the point predicate, height, and bounded
enumerator. The forward direction is a bounded filter theorem. The reverse direction is maximum-onlist correctness plus list completeness. This module provides a precise reusable endpoint for Faltings,
Siegel, integral-point, and finite-generation packets: they need only supply the theorem-specific height
or covering certificate.

12.9

Certificate mode and public formulas

A finite certificate language can be checked in ordinary PRAAx and then translated. If a smaller external
proof format is desired, the guarded public calculus in NativePublic.lean is relevant: equations are
wrapped by a gating, transport sources are protected, and the public translation is interderivable with
the native kernel on the intended language. A future proof compiler can emit public certificates while
the trusted Lean theorem proves their equivalence to NPrf.
This is an optimization, not the first milestone. The initial implementation should favor readable
ordinary arithmetic derivations.

Polynomial identity certificates

Consider

A certificate producer expands the left side, sorts monomials (2, 0), (1, 1), (0, 2), and returns coefficients

accepted finite certificate plus universal introduction of x, y.
For a rational identity, the checker also records a positive common denominator. The final statement
is the corresponding integer polynomial identity after multiplication. This is the recommended path
for matrix, trigonometric, and algebraic identities in the packets.

Further research questions

1. Generic object-language trace reflection. Can the proposed PRTraceSystem record be
implemented with sufficiently clean arity and substitution lemmas that theorem-specific modules
only supply base, step, and final derivations?
2. Verified certificate compiler. Can a small external compiler translate polynomial normalizations, rational error calculations, and finite loop annotations into public guarded certificates
whose checker is proved equivalent to NPrf?
3. Canonical code library. Which existing ProveIt encodings of Booleans, pairs, lists, integers, and
rationals should become canonical, so that proof packets stop rebuilding incompatible arithmetic
kernels?
4. Automatic precision synthesis. For expression trees with sums, products, guarded inverses,
roots, and finite folds, can Lean synthesize a monotone primitive-recursive precision transformer
and emit the corresponding error proof?
5. Adaptive query extraction. Can a theorem-specific semantic program be instrumented to
emit its finite query trace, after which a generic object-language checker proves that all queried
regularity instances suffice?
6. Constructive root clusters. Develop a complete isolating-cluster library for integer polynomials,
including multiplicity, subdivision certificates, and exact coefficient reconstruction, without
imposing a discontinuous global root order.
7. Projective linear algebra. Develop projective kernel, eigenspace projector, and singularvalue residual interfaces that give unconditional approximate outputs and exact outputs under
quantitative gaps. Compare their proof size with classical normalized-vector interfaces.
8. Effective compact domains. Formalize certified nets for rational boxes, simplices, spheres,
projective spaces, compact matrix groups, and finite probability simplices. Determine a minimal
common interface for coverage and refinement.
9. Analytic certificate formats. What finite certificate best represents zero-free regions, contour
bounds, exceptional zeros, or saddle-point estimates so that downstream analytic number theory
becomes a small verifier theorem?
10. Height-certificate taxonomy. Classify Diophantine methods—Runge, descent, Baker bounds,
Chabauty, Mordell–Weil sieve—by the finite certificate they can produce and the checker needed
in iPRA.
11. Witness extraction bounds. For restricted formula classes proved in ordinary or native iPRA,
obtain explicit primitive-recursive bounds on extracted witnesses and compare them with direct
CFP producers.
12. Obstruction automation. Create reusable reductions from exact sign, exact root ordering,
exact chart selection, or negative-only apartness to LLPO, LPO, or Markov principles. A packet
could then test a proposed classical formulation before investing in a large positive proof.
13. Proof-size engineering. Measure whether ordinary PRAAx certificates, native formulas, or
guarded public certificates give the smallest checker and proof terms for large finite mathematics.
14. Repository-wide status ledger. Can every proof packet expose a machine-readable record
following metadata/framework_schema.json, allowing automated reports of which fields—
producer, verifier, embedded proof, native export, axiom audit—are complete?
15. A substantial end-to-end pilot. A strong first pilot would combine regular names, robust

A.1

Finite exact endpoint

For input code p, algorithm A, and Boolean checker V :
Dom(p) = 1 → V (p, A(p)) = 1.

∀p

1. PR symbols for Dom, A, V ;

A.2

Regular-name endpoint

For fixed evaluator symbols a, b:
∀p Rega (p) ∧ Dom(p) → ∀k Close(b(p, k), Target(p), k).
Here Target must itself be expressed through finite observations, or the conclusion should be equality
of names. The proof at free k must list every queried input precision.

A.3

Approximate existential endpoint

Instead of ∃y P (p, y) in a higher-type completion, use
∀k ∃y < B(p, k)

Residual(p, y) ≤ 2−k .

A stronger computational form provides a fixed producer Y (p, k):
∀p, k

Dom(p) → Residual(p, Y (p, k)) ≤ 2−k .

Coherence of Y (p, k) is a separate theorem; without it no exact name is claimed.

A.4

Exact existential from nested certificates

Input or construct B(p, k), a nested family of finite regions, with checked existence and shrinking radius.
Define the output name from their centers. Prove regularity, residual convergence, and uniqueness if
supplied. The object-language theorem quantifies only over natural codes and precision.

A.5

Certificate-relative endpoint
∀p, c CertOK(p, c) = 1 → Conclusion(p, decode(c)).

The packet must state whether a certificate producer is:
• proved uniformly primitive recursive;
• proved for a restricted input class;
• executable but not embedded;
• assumed as explicit input;
• unknown or obstructed.

A.7

Review checklist

Before accepting a new packet, answer:
1. What is the exact first-order formula?
2. Which function symbols are fixed, and which data are numerical inputs?
3. Is any purported universal evaluator hidden in the notation?
4. What is the requested precision and its dependency graph?
5. Which denominators, pivots, or charts require positive margins?
6. What is the primitive-recursive loop/search bound?
7. What is the quantifier-free invariant, after Booleanization?
8. Are all compactness/existence uses replaced by nets, bounds, or nested certificates?
9. Is an ineffective constant being used as though it were produced?
10. Is the Lean result semantic, embedded, or merely a target proposition?
11. Has the final declaration been compiled and audited for axioms?
12. If the literal classical statement fails constructively, is the maximal positive replacement and
obstruction both recorded?
