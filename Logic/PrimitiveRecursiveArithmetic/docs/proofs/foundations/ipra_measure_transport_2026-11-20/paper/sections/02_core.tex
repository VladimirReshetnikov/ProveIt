\section{A finite arithmetic interface for continuous objects}
\label{sec:core}

\subsection{The base theory and rational coding}

We use first-order intuitionistic logic with equality, a natural-number
function symbol for each fixed primitive-recursive definition, its
defining equations, successor injectivity and nonzeroness, and induction
for quantifier-free formulas. This is the ordinary \ipra\ interface
\(\Prf\,\mathrm{PRAAx}\) used by the repository. Arithmetic quantifiers
are permitted in statements and assumptions. They are not silently
admitted into induction invariants.

Integers are coded by a sign and a magnitude, with a fixed normalization
of zero. A rational is a coded integer numerator and a positive natural
denominator. Invalid codes decode to a fixed default. We may use reduced
fractions for efficient execution, but equality and order are defined by
cross multiplication with positive denominators. Finite arrays have an
explicit length and a total default-valued lookup operation. Pairing,
lists, finite sums, products, maxima, bounded search, and powers are
primitive recursive. Every displayed rational inequality thus abbreviates
an arithmetic formula. Negative numerators never require subtraction in
the natural-number sort: use signed pairs or move their negative parts
to the other side.

For example, an inequality \(a/b\leq c/d\) with \(b,d>0\) means
\(ad\leq cb\) after interpreting the signed products. A finite conjunction
\(\forall i<N\,P(i)\), when \(P\) is given by a fixed
primitive-recursive test with its proved Boolean specification, has the
primitive-recursive Boolean accumulator
\[
 v(0)=1,\qquad v(i+1)=v(i)\mathbin{\wedge}\chi_P(i).
\]
The defining equations and quantifier-free induction establish the
accumulator's finite soundness. This is bounded Booleanization, not a
truth predicate for arbitrary formulas.

\subsection{Fix programs or supply a uniform clock}

There are two executable routes in this report.
In the \emph{fixed-program route}, an approximation program and its
moduli are specific primitive-recursive definitions before a theorem is
formed. Numerical parameters, precision, dimension, and array lengths
can still vary. In the \emph{clocked route}, a fixed universal transition
function is iterated for a specified primitive-recursive bound
\(T(a,k)\); the code \(a\) may vary, but this particular clock family is
fixed. Failure to halt within the bound is an explicit error output.
The hypothesis that a presentation is valid includes successful
evaluation at each demanded precision.

There is no total primitive-recursive evaluator for all
primitive-recursive programs with arbitrary code and input. If
\(U(e,n)\) were one, then \(n\mapsto U(n,n)+1\) would itself have an
index \(e\), yielding \(U(e,e)=U(e,e)+1\). A primitive-recursive
\emph{bounded} transition iterator does not contradict this argument.
Nor does an arithmetic relation saying that a finite halting trace exists.

For relational presentations, a variable-length batch must carry a
complete trace array or a common clock. From a collection of formulas
\(\forall i\,\exists t\,R(i,t)\), we do not simply infer, inside weak
arithmetic, a coded array of witnesses of arbitrary requested length.
All algorithms below either use fixed primitive-recursive functions or
receive precisely that finite execution evidence.

\subsection{Regular names and observations}

Let \(V_0\) be a rationally presented vector space or abelian group of
finite objects, with a rational-valued pseudonorm \(p\). Its finite
operations satisfy
\[
 p(0)=0,\quad p(x+y)\leq p(x)+p(y),\quad
 p(qx)=|q|p(x).
\]
For step functions, \(p\) is an exact finite weighted sum. The word
``pseudonorm'' allows different codes for the same finite function.

A regular \(p\)-name is a fixed or clocked producer \(u\) such that
\[
 p(u(k)-u(\ell))\leq 2^{-k}+2^{-\ell}\quad(k,\ell\in\mathbb N).
 \tag{R}\label{eq:regular}
\]
Its validity is an arithmetic formula about all finite outputs. For two
regular names define
\[
 u\simeq v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall k,\ell\ p(u(k)-v(\ell))\leq2^{-k}+2^{-\ell}.
 \tag{E}\label{eq:equiv}
\]
The completion is used externally to explain these names; internally we
use the corresponding formulas and finite witnesses. Reflexivity and
symmetry are immediate. For transitivity, insert a common approximant
\(v(m)\), obtain the desired bound plus \(2^{1-m}\), and eliminate the
extra error by a rational separation argument. Concretely, if the
desired rational inequality failed, its positive rational gap has a
primitive-recursively computable dyadic lower bound. Substituting a
larger \(m\) gives a contradiction. Only rational order is decided.

We write \(h(C)\) for the least natural \(j\) with
\(C\leq2^j\), for a nonnegative rational \(C\), taking \(h(C)=0\)
when \(C\leq1\). A search bounded by the numerator of \(C\) plus one
suffices, so \(h\) is primitive recursive. This simple shift converts
norm constants into precision demands.

\begin{lemma}[Regularization with an explicit rate]
Suppose finite objects \(a_n\) have a primitive-recursive nondecreasing
rate \(N\) satisfying
\[
 n,m\geq N(k)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p(a_n-a_m)\leq2^{-k}.
\]
Then \(u(k)=a_{N(k)}\) is a regular name. If the original rate is not
nondecreasing, replace it by the prefix maximum
\(\max_{j\leq k}N(j)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If \(k\leq\ell\), both selected indices are at least \(N(k)\).
Their distance is at most \(2^{-k}\), and hence at most
\(2^{-k}+2^{-\ell}\). The opposite ordering is symmetric. The prefix
maximum is defined by one bounded recursion and preserves the rate
property. No selection of an infinite subsequence is involved: there is
one explicit numerical function of \(k\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stable-observation lifting]\label{lem:stable-lift}
Let \(F:V_0\to W_0\) be a fixed finite algorithm with
\(q(Fx-Fy)\leq C p(x-y)\), where \(C\geq0\) is rational.
Then
\[
 (\widehat F u)(k)=F(u(k+h(C)))
\]
is a regular name, respects \(\simeq\), and gives the usual extension
of \(F\) to the represented completion. If a local implementation has
error \(\eta\), the output budget becomes \(C\epsilon+\eta\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the finite Lipschitz inequality and
\(C2^{-h(C)}\leq1\) to \eqref{eq:regular}, or to
\eqref{eq:equiv}. The same calculation proves equality of output names.
For a local implementation insert its exact finite target and apply the
triangle inequality. For uniqueness of the extended map, every input
name is approximated by its constant finite names, and the Lipschitz
bound forces agreement at arbitrary precision. This is a finite
approximation proof of the usual universal property, rather than an
appeal to an object-language set of all limits.
\end{proof}

The significant choice is \(p\). Pointwise evaluation is not an
\(L^1\)-continuous observation. Integration is. The supremum functional
is stable for a uniform norm, whereas general diagonal substitution
requires further control. An observation is assigned its correct norm
and is rejected from a chain of constructions when no suitable bound is
available. Later sections supply several bounds that make previously
unavailable observations accessible.

\subsection{The induction rule used for all finite algorithms}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded replay rule]\label{thm:replay}
Fix primitive-recursive functions \(s(a)\), \(F(a,i,z)\), and
\(B(a)\), and a quantifier-free formula \(I(a,i,z)\).
Suppose \ipra\ proves the initial and step clauses
\[
 I(a,0,s(a)),\qquad
 i<B(a)\wedge I(a,i,z)\ \longrightarrow\ I(a,i+1,F(a,i,z)).
\]
For \(z_0=s(a)\), \(z_{i+1}=F(a,i,z_i)\), it proves
\(I(a,B(a),z_{B(a)})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Primitive recursion defines the entire numerical iterate. Induct on
\(i\) with the quantifier-free invariant
\(i\leq B(a)\to I(a,i,z_i)\). The step splits the decidable cases
\(i<B(a)\) and \(i\geq B(a)\). At the boundary \(i=B(a)\), the
next antecedent is false; below it, use the supplied step clause and
the defining equation. Instantiate at \(i=B(a)\).
\end{proof}

This theorem is a proof rule with hypotheses that must actually be
proved. It does not say that every true assertion about a
primitive-recursive program is provable in \ipra. In finite summation,
the invariant records the current partial sum and its bound. In
dyadic refinement it records common denominators and the conserved
weighted sum. In the energy-window search it records either a found
window or accumulated strict energy increments. In a topological chain
calculation it records coefficient identities after processing a
bounded list of faces. All parameters, arrays, and target precisions
remain numerical.

For each fixed theorem, the resulting object statement has the form
\[
 \forall a\,\forall k\;\bigl(A(a,k)\to
       Q(a,k,\mathrm{out}(a,k))\bigr),
\]
or a finite-certificate variant with the actual input traces and
certificates as additional numerical parameters. Local assumptions
about validity may be instantiated finitely. They are not treated as
Boolean invariants unless they have separately been reduced to bounded
conditions. The Lean roadmap specifies the required local derivations
before this replay rule can be invoked at an analytic endpoint.
