                                                   Abstract
         The current ProveIt development has two assets that should now be joined rather than duplicated:
     a substantial kernel-checked library of ordinary iPRA derivations, and more than one hundred
     research packets that repeatedly reduce sophisticated mathematics to finite searches, traces, tables,
     error budgets, and certificates. The remaining bottleneck is not the absence of another coding
     of arithmetic. It is a compositional interface that turns theorem-specific finite evidence into
     open object-theory derivations, preserves local hypotheses and parameters, exposes unavailable
     quantitative producers without overclaiming, and transports the resulting theorem through the
     repository’s checked backends.
         This report develops KIFC-S, a sequent-polymorphic refinement of the earlier residualized
     KIFC-R framework. Its central object is the contract
                                                                                   
                      Ctr(A, φ) ≡ ∀∆ ∀ψ ∈ A ∆ ⊢ [PRAAx] ψ → ∆ ⊢ [PRAAx] φ .
                                                                 


     The first main theorem proves, constructively and without adding a rule to the logic, that

                                      Ctr(A, φ)   ⇐⇒       A ⊢ [PRAAx] φ.

     Although extensionally equivalent to an ordinary sequent, the contract presentation is a better
     compiler interface: assumptions have no privileged ambient context, local rules compose by ordinary
     function application and simultaneous cut, substitution is functorial, and residual boundary formulas
     become explicit typed inputs. This removes a recurrent source of context-order and weakening
     errors from earlier graph blueprints.
         A query-specialized graph theorem then computes the least backward proof slice for the requested
     sinks, collects only the boundary producers reachable from that slice, and composes local contracts
     into
                                             Ctr(RQ ++ Γ, ΦQ ),
     where RQ is the exact residual frontier. Supplying object-theory derivations of the residuals closes
     the result; leaving them open records the actual mathematical boundary. The theorem is extended
     to multi-query sharing, acyclic demand propagation, capped monotone cyclic demand systems,
     normalized one-loop traces, and finite relational viability.
         The report also integrates a two-sided certificate semantics for arbitrary fixed bounded quantifier
     alternation. Truth and falsity are represented by finite winning strategies whose primitive-recursive
     checkers are reflected in iPRA. This absorbs many uses of classical bounded reasoning without
     global excluded middle. A new coherence trichotomy separates three often-confused goals: finite-use
     elimination of nonunique witnesses, coherent path production from an explicit extension strategy,

      and regular-name production from diameter, motion, and tail data. Horizonwise existence alone is
      proved insufficient for the latter two.
          Complete human-readable proofs are supplied for the contract/sequent equivalence, contract
      composition, least residual frontiers, graph compilation, two-sided bounded strategies, normalized
      trace reflection, capped demand closure, viability, stable-observer elimination, guarded operations,
      finite-net transfer, and the coherence trichotomy. The archive includes source-oriented Lean modules,
      a dependency-ordered formalization plan, exact Python reference algorithms and tests, worked
      graph and strategy examples, and a compiled PDF. New Lean files are accurately labeled as
      uncompiled candidates or blueprints; inherited checked results are separated from new claims.
      General effective Faltings, Siegel, Mordell–Weil generation, unrestricted exact branch selection, and
      ineffective analytic constants are not asserted: their exact residual producer obligations are stated
      instead.

1     Executive result and recommended architecture
1.1    The problem after the current repository advances
The repository no longer needs a general argument that elementary finite mathematics can in principle
be encoded in primitive recursive arithmetic. It already contains a checked ordinary calculus, a checked
native scalar presentation, a checked bridge to the Foundation development, and a growing library of
actual derivations for arithmetic, bounded search, simultaneous recursion, finite sums, number theory,
linear algebra, geometry, probability, and combinatorics. At the same time, the research packets
display a stable family of obstructions:
    • an ambient Lean theorem is mistaken for an object-theory derivation;
    • a loop invariant contains bounded quantifiers and is therefore not eligible for quantifier-free
      induction;
    • a finite witness is chosen independently at each horizon although later consumers need compati-
      bility;
    • a represented nonzero input is treated as if it carried a positive quantitative margin;
    • an abstract finiteness or compactness statement is used as if it supplied an enumeration cutoff or
      a finite net;
    • a theorem-specific effective constant, height bound, separation gap, or representative system is
      hidden in host-language data;
    • a proof is proved only for closed sentences even though applications require hypotheses and
      arbitrary parameters.
The earlier KIFC-R report resolved most of the mathematical representation issues and proposed
an explicit residualized endpoint. The newer bounded-interaction packet isolated a small normalized
trace kernel and two-sided finite strategy certificates. The remaining unifying step is to make the
composition interface itself independent of a fixed ambient context.

1.2    The one new abstraction
For a finite list A of formulas and a formula φ, all with the same free-variable arity, define
                                                                                   
                            : ⇒ ∀∆ (∀ψ ∈ A ∆ ⊢ [PRAAx] ψ) → ∆ ⊢ [PRAAx] φ .
                  Ctr(A, φ) ⇐

A value of this type is not a semantic implication. It is a proof transformer whose input and output
are embedded derivations. The ambient context ∆ is universally quantified, so a contract can be
reused under any local hypotheses. The key fact is exactness:

                                  Ctr(A, φ)    ⇐⇒       A ⊢ [PRAAx] φ.

The forward direction instantiates the contract at A and supplies assumptions by the hypothesis rule.
The reverse direction is the checked simultaneous-cut theorem Prf.trans. Thus contracts do not
strengthen iPRA; they expose the correct higher-order API for composing its derivations.
This modest change has several consequences.
 (1) A local theorem no longer stores a proof tied to a particular ordering of permanent and residual
     assumptions. It stores a contract from the assumptions it actually consumes.


                                                    7
KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                                 2026-11-16


 (2) Derived facts can be inserted, reordered, deduplicated, or supplied from another backend through
     the same trans interface.
 (3) Substitution acts on contracts by applying Prf.subst to the underlying sequent, so open
     parameters are first-class.
 (4) Residual boundaries are ordinary formulas, not special host-language tokens. Closing a residual-
     ized theorem is simply contract application.
 (5) A graph generator can remain untrusted: it emits a fixed ordinary derivation, and Lean checks
     the result.

1.3     The compilation pipeline
For a fixed mathematical query, the recommended pipeline is:
      P1. Choose an observational statement. Replace completed infinitary objects by the finite
          observations actually consumed: exact algebraic data, rational intervals, finite distributions,
          bounded complexes, finite nets, finite lists, or stable quotient observables.
      P2. Separate producers from verifiers. A height cutoff, separation margin, covering radius,
          effective constant, or representative family is a producer. Its checker is usually primitive
          recursive; its existence may be the difficult theorem. Put each unavailable producer into a
          boundary formula.
      P3. Booleanize fixed bounded content. Equality, order, connectives, and bounded quantifiers
          are compiled to Boolean primitive-recursive terms with object-level reflection derivations.
      P4. Resolve finite alternation by strategies. For a fixed bounded formula, construct a
          canonical winning certificate for truth or falsity and reflect its checker. This gives local
          decidability without a global classical axiom.
      P5. Propagate finite demands before induction. Compute all precisions, horizons, and
          table sizes from the terminal query. Use capped finite closure for justified cyclic requests.
      P6. Normalize recursion. Collapse bounded loops, searches, and folds into one primitive-
          recursive trace with a quantifier-free invariant, or compile each already checked loop separately.
      P7. Handle nonuniqueness at the correct strength. Use backward viability for finite
          compatibility; erase choices through stable observables when possible; require an explicit
          extension strategy for a coherent path; require diameter, motion, and tail data for a regular
          name.
      P8. Compile the proof slice. Starting from requested sinks, compute backward proof reacha-
          bility, collect the least residual frontier, topologically compose local contracts, and export
          one open contract.
      P9. Close and transport. Supply object proofs of the residuals, recover the ordinary Prf PRAAx
          sequent, and only then use the checked native or Foundation bridge required by the consumer.

3     Logical substrate and admissible proof engineering
3.1   The object theory
Fix a number n of free numerical variables. The type PTm(n) contains terms built from variables
and primitive-recursive function symbols. A formula φ ∈ Fm(PTm, n) is interpreted only through the
derivability relation
                                           Γ ⊢ [PRAAx]φ.
The metatheory is Lean. The object theory is iPRA. This separation allows the metatheory to use
finite types, inductive syntax, quotient constructions, and normalization tactics while still exporting a
derivation whose leaves are exactly the axioms of iPRA.
The following derived operations are the backbone of the compiler.
Lemma 3.1 (Substitution). If Γ ⊢ φ and σ substitutes terms for all free variables, then

                                              Γ[σ] ⊢ φ[σ].

Proof. Induct on the given natural-deduction derivation. The only nontrivial cases are binders. Under
a binder, lift σ by mapping the newly bound variable to itself and shifting every old image. The syntax
library proves the required substitution identities. The axiom case follows because an open axiom is
universally instantiated by the axiom rule. This is the content of the current Prf.substWeaken and
Prf.subst theorems.
Lemma 3.2 (Simultaneous cut). If h : Γ ⊢ φ and, for every ψ ∈ Γ, one has kψ : ∆ ⊢ ψ, then ∆ ⊢ φ.

Proof. Induct on the list Γ. The empty case is weakening. For Γ = ψ :: Γ′ , first discharge ψ from h
by implication introduction, recursively cut all members of Γ′ , and finally apply modus ponens to
kψ . This is exactly the current theorem Prf.trans. Notice that no cut rule is added to the kernel;
simultaneous cut is derived in intuitionistic natural deduction.
Lemma 3.3 (Quantifier-free induction interface). Let I(z, ⃗x) be quantifier-free. If

                                ⊢ I(0, ⃗x)   and        I(z, ⃗x) ⊢ I(Sz, ⃗x),

then ⊢ I(z, ⃗x) as an open theorem, and hence every term instance I(t, ⃗u) is derivable.

Proof. Apply the PRAAx induction axiom to I, use implication introduction for the step, and universally
eliminate the resulting formula onto the open induction variable. The repository packages this as
PRA.qfInd and, in the derivation library, as LeanProofs.IPRA.ind. Term instances then follow from
substitution.

The compiler is designed so that every recursive correctness argument terminates at theorem 3.3. This
is why Boolean reflection and state packing are not optional optimizations: they are the mechanism
that makes the invariant quantifier-free.

3.2   Finite data inside one numerical sort
Every finite data type used by a fixed theorem is encoded by natural numbers together with primitive-
recursive projections and a bounded well-formedness predicate. There are several interchangeable
choices:
   • iterated pairing, with a fixed arity known at compilation time;
   • mixed-radix tuples when component bounds are available;
   • tables encoded by bounded products of radices;
   • finite lists represented by a length and a bounded tuple code.
No coding theorem requires exponentiation as a primitive operation: exponentiation, bounded products,
pairing, and table lookup are primitive recursive and therefore have symbols in the ordinary language.
A coding package for a type X consists of a predicate WFX (c), projections, constructors, and object-
level equations showing that projecting a constructed code returns its components. Equality of codes is
not automatically mathematical equality. When multiple codes represent the same object, an explicit
primitive-recursive equivalence relation c ∼X d and congruence theorems for all observations are part
of the interface.

3.3   Metalevel normalization is legitimate when it produces proof terms
The current algebraic derivation library illustrates a general pattern. The metatheory forms a quotient
of terms by provable equality, proves that the quotient is a cancellative commutative semiring, embeds
it in a ring of differences, and invokes a ring normalizer. The final theorem is recovered through
injectivity as an object-level equality derivation. The normalizer is not an extra axiom of iPRA; it is a
proof-producing metaprogram.
The same standard applies to every KIFC-Scompiler phase:
 (1) the metaprogram may inspect a fixed graph, calculate a topological order, allocate registers, and
     synthesize terms;
 (2) it must also synthesize or invoke derivations of all local equations and implications;
 (3) the exported theorem must have the embedded derivability type;
 (4) an axiom audit must show no theorem-specific opaque axiom or sorryAx.

4     Sequent-polymorphic contracts
4.1     Definition
Fix a free-variable arity n. All formulas in this section lie in Fm(PTm, n). A finite list A is read as an
interface of required formulas, not as a set of semantic truths.
Definition 4.1 (Open contract). For a list A and formula φ, define
                                                                   

                 Ctr(A, φ) :=                        ∆ ⊢ [PRAAx] ψ  −→ ∆ ⊢ [PRAAx] φ.
                                    Y            Y
                                             
                                ∆:List(Fm)   ψ∈A

Equivalently, in Lean-shaped notation,
def Contract (A : List (Fm PTm n)) (phi : Fm PTm n) : Prop :=
  forall Delta,
    (forall psi, psi in A -> Delta |- [PRAAx] psi) ->
    Delta |- [PRAAx] phi

The product notation is constructive: a contract is an operation that consumes derivations of its inputs
and returns a derivation of its output. It is not a claim that the formulas are true in the standard
natural numbers.

4.2     Exact equivalence with an ordinary sequent
Theorem 4.2 (Contract–sequent equivalence). For every finite list A and formula φ,
                                   Ctr(A, φ)     ⇐⇒         A ⊢ [PRAAx] φ.
The equivalence is uniform in the free-variable arity.

Proof. Suppose first that h : A ⊢ [PRAAx] φ. Let ∆ be arbitrary and suppose that for every ψ ∈ A a
derivation sψ : ∆ ⊢ [PRAAx] ψ is supplied. Apply simultaneous cut to h and the family s. The result
is ∆ ⊢ [PRAAx] φ, hence a contract.
Conversely, suppose that c : Ctr(A, φ). Instantiate its universally quantified context with ∆ = A. For
each ψ ∈ A, the hypothesis constructor gives A ⊢ ψ. Applying c to this family gives A ⊢ φ.
Both constructions use only the existing natural-deduction rules and the derived theorem Prf.trans.
In particular, Ctr adds no object-level axiom and no semantic soundness principle.

Corollary 4.3 (Closed contracts). Ctr([], φ) is equivalent to [] ⊢ [PRAAx] φ.
Corollary 4.4 (Singleton implication). Ctr([ψ], φ) is equivalent to both [ψ] ⊢ φ and ⊢ ψ → φ.

Proof. The first equivalence is theorem 4.2. Implication introduction and elimination give the second.


4.3   Identity, composition, and simultaneous replacement
The point of theorem 4.1 is not to change provability but to make the admissible composition laws
explicit in the type.
Lemma 4.5 (Identity). If φ ∈ A, then Ctr(A, φ).

Proof. Given ∆ and a supplier for every member of A, apply the supplier to φ and its membership
proof.
Theorem 4.6 (Simultaneous contract cut). Suppose

                               c : Ctr(A, φ),      sψ : Ctr(B, ψ)     (ψ ∈ A).

Then Ctr(B, φ).

Proof. Let ∆ prove every member of B. Each sψ turns that family into a derivation ∆ ⊢ ψ. Supplying
these derivations to c yields ∆ ⊢ φ. No induction on derivations is needed at this interface level; it has
already been packaged by simultaneous cut.
Corollary 4.7 (Unary bind). If Ctr(A, φ) and Ctr(φ :: A, χ), then Ctr(A, χ).

Proof. Supply the distinguished premise φ by the first contract and every member of A by identity.
Corollary 4.8 (Composition of local rules). Let ri : Ctr(Ai , φi ) for i < m, and let

                                     t : Ctr(φ0 :: · · · :: φm−1 :: B, χ).

Then there is a contract from A0 ++ · · · ++Am−1 ++B to χ.

Proof. Under an arbitrary target context, restrict the supplied family to each Ai , obtain every φi ,
retain the members of B, and apply t. The finite concatenation and membership injections are
metalevel bookkeeping; all logical work is contract application.

4.4   Assumption variance and normalization
Lemma 4.9 (Weakening the available interface). If every member of A belongs to B and Ctr(A, φ),
then Ctr(B, φ).

Proof. A supplier for B restricts along the inclusion A ⊆ B, after which the original contract applies.

Thus the assumption list is contravariant with respect to inclusion of required inputs: more available
inputs never invalidate a contract. This justifies stable deduplication, sorting, and insertion of
permanent hypotheses in generated code.
Proposition 4.10 (Permutation and duplicate invariance). If A and B have the same members,
ignoring order and multiplicity, then

                                      Ctr(A, φ)     ⇐⇒       Ctr(B, φ).

Proof. Apply theorem 4.9 in both directions. The theorem does not say that Lean’s list expressions
are definitionally equal; it supplies the proof transformer that makes their difference irrelevant to
derivability.

This is the first concrete improvement over a context-fixed conditional record. A generator may choose
a deterministic normalized order for provenance without forcing every local node proof to be stated in
that order.

4.5   Substitution naturality
Let σ : Fin(n) → PTm(m) be a simultaneous substitution. Write A[σ] for the list obtained by

Theorem 4.11 (Contract substitution). If Ctr(A, φ), then

                                            Ctr(A[σ], φ[σ]).

Proof. By theorem 4.2, obtain A ⊢ φ. The checked substitution theorem gives A[σ] ⊢ φ[σ]. Apply the
reverse direction of theorem 4.2.
Corollary 4.12 (Specialization). An open contract with parameters can be instantiated at arbitrary
primitive-recursive terms without reproving the graph or trace theorem.
This is the correct way to package theorem schemas. For example, a finite-sum contract may remain
open in the summation bound, the summand parameters, and a modulus; an application later
substitutes its concrete terms.

4.6   Conjunction and finite products
Lemma 4.13 (Pairing conclusions). If Ctr(A, φ) and Ctr(A, ψ), then Ctr(A, φ ∧ ψ).

Proof. Under any supplier for A, apply both contracts and conjunction introduction.

Iterating this lemma yields a contract for any fixed finite conjunction of sink conclusions. Conversely,
conjunction elimination yields a contract for each component. Therefore a multi-query compiler can
export either one conjunction or a list of separate contracts without changing logical strength.

4.8    Contracts as a thin proof category
For fixed arity, regard a finite formula interface as an object. A contract from A to each member
of B is a morphism A → B. Identity is supplied by hypotheses, and composition is simultaneous
contract cut. Associativity and unit laws hold extensionally because both sides send the same input
derivations to derivations related by the same admissible cuts. No quotient of proof terms is needed
for the compiler: it only needs the operations and their types.
A useful refinement is to attach a resource annotation r to a contract while leaving the proof field
unchanged. Demand composition then combines annotations, whereas logical composition remains
theorem 4.6. This strict separation prevents precision bookkeeping from leaking into the logic.

does not by itself provide a contract. It may use classical reasoning, unbounded choice, non-primitive
recursion, or facts about the standard model unavailable in iPRA. A contract contains the syntactic
derivation transformation. Semantic testing remains useful for finding errors in generated terms, but
it never fills the proof field.

    3. finite metalevel list operations whose output is checked through the generated derivation.
Everything else—graph traversal, residual normalization, strategy extraction, register allocation,
demand propagation—may be implemented by ordinary code without entering the trusted base.

5.1    Proof graphs
A fixed theorem is represented by a finite directed graph. The graph is metalevel data; its formulas
and local proofs live in the object-language interface.
Definition 5.1 (Contract graph). A contract graph consists of a finite node set V = {0, . . . , N − 1}.
Every node v has:

5.2   Backward query reachability
Fix a finite set Q ⊆ V of requested sink nodes. Define Reach(Q) as the least subset of V containing Q
and closed under proof parents:

                            v ∈ Reach(Q), u ∈ Pv        =⇒    u ∈ Reach(Q).

Because the graph is finite, Reach(Q) is computed by a bounded work-list recursion.
Lemma 5.2 (Reachability soundness). Every proof parent used by a reachable node is reachable.

Proof. This is the closure condition at termination of the work-list algorithm. At each iteration, all
parents of the removed work item are inserted into the marked set and work list. The loop terminates
after at most N new marks.
Lemma 5.3 (Reachability minimality). If S ⊆ V contains Q and is closed under proof parents, then
Reach(Q) ⊆ S.

Proof. Induct on the stage at which a node is marked. Sink nodes lie in S. If a node is marked as a
parent of an earlier reachable node, closure of S puts it in S. Hence every mark belongs to S.

5.6   Dead-code invariance
Theorem 5.9 (Unreachable extension invariance). Extend a graph by nodes that are not backward
reachable from Q, without changing the local data of old reachable nodes. Then Reach(Q), Front(Q),

Proof. The work-list traversal never visits the new nodes, because no reachable old node names them

This theorem is important for a growing repository: adding an unrelated theorem module must not
enlarge the assumptions of an existing compiled endpoint.

5.7   Recursive components
A cycle in the proof-parent graph cannot be resolved by topological composition alone. There are
three legitimate treatments.
  1. Trace collapse. A recursive program plus a QF invariant is compiled to one trace theorem,
     then represented as one acyclic node.
  2. Well-founded descent. A cycle whose recursive call decreases an explicit natural rank is
     compiled through a checked descent theorem and collapsed.
  3. Capped demand closure. Cycles in resource requests, rather than logical proofs, are solved
     by the finite fixed-point theorem of section 9. They do not create proof cycles after the resulting
     requests are fixed.
An unannotated logical cycle is rejected by the compiler. Treating it as a fixed point would add an
unsound circular-proof rule.

5.8   Residual provenance and canonical formulas
Every boundary record should contain:
  • a stable key;
  • the exact formula with free-variable arity;
  • the node and local theorem that introduced it;
  • the expected producer theorem or module;
  • a human description of its quantitative meaning;
  • whether it is an input hypothesis, a currently open theorem, or a theorem available in another
    backend awaiting transport.
The formula, not the description, enters the object context. A generated report should print the
normalized residual list before its conclusion.

5.9   Proof-producing versus verified metadata compilation
The graph traversal itself need not initially be verified in Lean. A metaprogram may compute Reach(Q),
Front(Q), and a schedule, then emit an ordinary proof term. A bug causes type checking to fail or

yields a theorem with extra explicit assumptions; it cannot prove a false formula unless the trusted

A later verified metadata development can prove theorems 5.3, 5.4 and 5.9 for the implementation.

5.10    Incremental compilation
For a stable graph, cache for each node:
    • a hash of its normalized formula and local proof source;
    • its parent and boundary keys;
    • the compiled local theorem name;
    • the query sets for which it was reachable.
A change invalidates the node and its forward dependents, not the whole library. The cache is a build
optimization only. The final theorem remains an ordinary Lean declaration with no dependence on
cache correctness.

6.1    Why bounded alternation deserves a uniform interface
After atomic predicates have been reflected to Boolean terms, a theorem often contains a fixed bounded

6.3   Truth and falsity certificates
For each fixed formula F , define two finite certificate types by structural recursion.

Formula       truth certificate T (F )                         falsity certificate F (F )
atom p        unit, checked by bp = 1                          unit, checked by bp = 0

Theorem 6.1 (Certificate reflection). For every fixed bounded formula F , there are primitive-recursive
predicates CheckT (F, t, ⃗x) and CheckF (F, f, ⃗x) and open object-theory derivations of

                                     CheckT (F, t, ⃗x) = 1 −→ F (⃗x),
                                    CheckF (F, f, ⃗x) = 1 −→ ¬F (⃗x).

There are also primitive-recursive canonical constructors certT and certF satisfying

                              [[F ]] = 1 −→ CheckT (F, certT (F, ⃗x), ⃗x) = 1,
                              [[F ]] = 0 −→ CheckF (F, certF (F, ⃗x), ⃗x) = 1.

Proof. Proceed by structural recursion on the fixed formula.
For an atom, use its supplied Boolean range and reflection derivations. For conjunction, the truth
checker verifies both subcertificates; the induction hypotheses and conjunction introduction prove the
formula. A falsity certificate carries a tag. A finite conditional selects the indicated subchecker; the
induction hypothesis gives ¬G or ¬H, each of which intuitionistically implies ¬(G ∧ H). Disjunction
is dual: a truth tag chooses one introduction rule, while falsity requires both negations and proves
(G ∨ H) → ⊥ by disjunction elimination.
For ∀i < B G(i), the truth certificate is a bounded table. Its checker is the product of the row checkers.
Bounded-fold reflection gives every row checker below B; the induction hypothesis gives every instance
G(i). The bounded universal formula used by the compiler is itself represented by its reflected fold or
by a fixed first-order bounded expansion, so the desired formula follows. A falsity certificate contains
i < B and a false subcertificate; instantiate the universal at i and contradict it.
For ∃i < B G(i), a truth certificate supplies a bounded witness and true subcertificate, followed by
existential introduction. A falsity table supplies ¬G(i) for every bounded i; existential elimination
then yields a contradiction from any alleged witness.
Canonical constructors inspect the Boolean values of finitely many subformulas. At conjunction and
universal nodes, collect every required subcertificate. At disjunction and existential nodes, bounded
least search chooses the first successful branch or witness. False constructors use the dual pattern.
Every loop has a quantifier-free invariant because subformula truth is represented by Boolean equality.
The construction is primitive recursive for each fixed syntax tree.

This is a theorem about one fixed bounded formula, not an axiom schema deciding arbitrary formulas.

6.5    Game interpretation
At a bounded universal node, the opponent selects an index and a truth strategy must answer with a
sub-strategy. At a bounded existential node, the prover selects a witness. Falsity strategies reverse the
roles. The certificate table is therefore a finite strategy tree. The checker need not explore all possible
plays dynamically: it verifies the finite tree by bounded folds.
For the example
                               ∀u < U ∃v < V (u) ∀w < W (u, v) P (u, v, w),
a truth certificate is a function table v(u) together with, for every u and w < W (u, v(u)), an
atomic truth certificate. A falsity certificate chooses one u < U and, for every v < V (u), chooses a
counterexample w(v) < W (u, v) with an atomic falsity certificate. Both objects are finite.

6.6    Contracts for strategy checkers
The reflection theorem should be exported as contracts:

                                       Ctr([CheckT (F, t, ⃗x) = 1], F (⃗x)),
                                    Ctr([CheckF (F, f, ⃗x) = 1], ¬F (⃗x)).

The canonical constructor theorem is a second contract from the Boolean value equation to checker
acceptance. A graph node may use either an externally supplied certificate or the canonical internal
constructor. The separation is useful when a compact external certificate is much smaller than
recomputation.

6.7    Negative translation at finite endpoints
Some classical finite proofs can be normalized to a bounded formula F . Once theorem 6.2 is available,
double-negation elimination for F is derivable:

                                                 ¬¬F −→ F.

Indeed, decide F ; the negative branch contradicts ¬¬F . This justifies a retained classical proof only
when its translated conclusion has been proved equivalent to such a fixed decidable endpoint and every
premise has an object-level translation. It does not justify a general classical logic oracle.

6.8    Complexity and proof size
A naive truth certificate for k universal quantifiers can have size equal to the product of their bounds.
This is unavoidable for arbitrary predicates if the certificate explicitly answers every challenge. Three
reductions are available:
    1. use a primitive-recursive response function plus a proof that its answers pass, rather than an
       extensional table;
    2. share repeated subcertificates through a trace or DAG code;
    3. choose a stronger local invariant that proves a whole family of rows by induction.
The checker remains bounded and primitive recursive. The compiler should record whether a strategy
is table, functional, or DAG-compressed, because their proof adapters differ.

6.9    Limits
Bounded strategy reflection does not decide an unbounded existential or produce a uniform rate of
convergence. It applies after every quantifier has an explicit primitive-recursive bound. If a theorem
supplies only “there exists a finite bound”, that bound is itself a residual producer. Similarly, a
cardinality bound on a Diophantine set is not a height bound and cannot be substituted for the
quantifier bound required by the strategy compiler.


7     Boolean reflection and bounded search
7.1    Boolean conventions
Use numerical Booleans 0, 1. Define primitive-recursive operations

                                      ¬b,       b ∧ c,       b ∨ c,        b ⇒ c,

by fixed case distinctions, and normalize every Boolean-producing term so that an object-level range
theorem proves b = 0 ∨ b = 1. Equality and order on natural numbers are primitive recursive; let

                                            eq(s, t),    lt(s, t),    le(s, t)

denote their Boolean codes. The basic reflection library proves, for example,

                            eq(s, t) = 1 ↔ s = t,               lt(s, t) = 1 ↔ s < t.

The right-hand relations are themselves expressed in the fixed arithmetic language, for instance using
truncated subtraction or bounded witnesses. Once proved, application modules use only the reflected
form.

7.2    Finite folds
For a Boolean predicate term p(i, ⃗x), define primitive-recursive folds

                    All(0, ⃗x) = 1,                          All(Sn, ⃗x) = All(n, ⃗x) ∧ p(n, ⃗x),
                   Any(0, ⃗x) = 0,                         Any(Sn, ⃗x) = Any(n, ⃗x) ∨ p(n, ⃗x).

These functions carry all bounded quantifier content in a numerical state.

Theorem 7.1 (Finite-fold reflection). Assume p(i, ⃗x) is Boolean-valued and reflects a fixed formula
P (i, ⃗x). Then iPRA derives

                                    All(N, ⃗x) = 1 ↔ ∀i < N P (i, ⃗x),
                                   Any(N, ⃗x) = 1 ↔ ∃i < N P (i, ⃗x).

Moreover the induction used to establish the fold equations has a quantifier-free matrix.

Proof. The executable state is only the accumulator Boolean. Its defining equations are instances of
primitive recursion. First prove by quantifier-free induction that the accumulator remains Boolean.
For the universal fold, prove the two implications externally to the loop invariant.
For left-to-right reflection, use ordinary induction on N at the proof-construction level. At zero the
bounded universal statement is vacuous. At SN , the successor equation and Boolean reflection of
conjunction yield both All(N ) = 1 and p(N ) = 1. Apply the induction hypothesis to the first and
predicate reflection to the second.
For right-to-left reflection, again use induction on N . The successor bounded universal supplies the
prefix and the endpoint. Reflect these to All(N ) = 1 and p(N ) = 1, then use the conjunction equation.
The existential fold is dual: at the successor stage, split a bounded witness into the cases i < N
and i = N , and use reflected disjunction. All finite case splits are intuitionistically valid because the
relevant numerical comparisons have decidable Boolean codes.
A direct Lean implementation may package the prefix assertions as formulas containing bounded
quantifiers, but it need not apply the PRAAx induction rule to those formulas. The only object
recurrence requiring induction is the quantifier-free equality that defines the fold. Reflection is then
assembled from the fold equations, comparison reflection, and natural-deduction reasoning.

7.5     Derived finite algorithms
The same search theorem yields a library of reusable combinators.
    • Bounded argmin. Search the least index attaining the least value in a finite table; comparison
      reflection supplies the predicate.
    • Duplicate-free enumeration. Scan a bounded box, append an item only if its verifier succeeds
      and no earlier appended item is equivalent.
    • Finite cover selection. Search a finite net for the first cell whose certified test covers the
      current query.
    • Pivot selection. Under a margin certificate asserting that some pivot exceeds a threshold,
      search the least such pivot. Without the threshold, do not claim an exact pivot.
    • Finite path successor. Given a viability table, search the least compatible viable successor.
Each derived algorithm inherits a QF invariant because every finite universal or existential subcondition
has first been replaced by a Boolean fold.


8     One-loop normalization and trace reflection
8.1     Certified iteration
A loop node is specified by:
    • a packed state code s;
    • an initializer s0 (⃗x);

    • an output projection O(s).
All numerical maps are primitive recursive. The state trace is the primitive recursion

Then iPRA derives I(i, S(i, ⃗x), ⃗x) for all open i, and in particular at the fuel term F (⃗x). If a terminal

Proof. Substitute the trace term into the invariant to obtain the QF formula

                                         J(i, ⃗x) ≡ I(i, S(i, ⃗x), ⃗x).

The base derivation is the supplied base proof followed by the zero computation equation for S. For
the step, assume J(i, ⃗x), use the supplied transition-preservation proof, and rewrite S(i + 1) by the

8.2    Why all precision must be fixed before induction
A common invalid pattern is to put into the invariant the assertion

                                 ∀q “the state is correct to precision q”.

This is not quantifier-free. Even when every individual precision computation is finite, the displayed
invariant is stronger than the induction rule permits.
The correct transformation is:
 (1) fix the terminal precision q∗ as an open parameter;
 (2) run the reverse demand pass to calculate every intermediate request from q∗ ;
 (3) allocate in the state only the finite observations required at those requests;
 (4) prove one QF invariant about this finite table;
 (5) after obtaining the open theorem for arbitrary q∗ , universally quantify over q∗ if the final theorem
     needs all precisions.
The universal quantifier is introduced after induction, not inside its matrix.

8.3    Collapsing nested bounded constructions
A fixed theorem may contain many loops, searches, and finite table passes. They can be combined
into one state recursion.
Theorem 8.2 (One-loop normal form). Every fixed program generated from primitive-recursive
assignments, finite conditionals, bounded loops, bounded searches, finite-table folds, and acyclic calls to
previously generated routines can be transformed into:
  (i) one primitive-recursive fuel term F (⃗x);
 (ii) one packed state s containing a program counter, local registers, and loop counters;
(iii) one primitive-recursive transition T ;
 (iv) primitive-recursive projections recovering the original outputs.
If every source loop has a QF invariant and every source command has a local preservation derivation,
the combined machine has a QF invariant and an object-level base and step derivation.

KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                                    2026-11-16


Proof. Because the source program is fixed, its control-flow graph has finitely many labels. Allocate a
numerical program counter pc < L. Allocate a fixed tuple of registers large enough to hold every live
local variable; registers not live at the current label are ignored. Replace each atomic command by
one branch of a finite selector on pc. A loop header tests its counter and jumps either to the body or
the successor label. A bounded search is its explicit (f ound, witness, index) loop. A finite table fold
is an index/accumulator loop.
Define a structural cost function in the metatheory. An atomic command costs one step; sequential

9     Capped cyclic demand closure
9.1   Why acyclic propagation is sometimes insufficient
Most precision graphs are acyclic: the output tolerance determines tolerances of its inputs. Some useful
interfaces, however, produce mutually dependent finite resource requests. A chart-refinement routine
may ask for coefficient precision determined by a separation estimate, while the verified separation
estimate itself depends on a finite coefficient precision. A relational matching step may enlarge both
the candidate horizon and the precision used to test compatibility. These are not legitimate unbounded
recursive definitions, but they can be sound when all coordinates have justified finite caps and all
updates are monotone.

is not invoked abstractly. We compute the fixed point by a bounded primitive recursion and prove

9.3   Overflow as a residual or a diagnostic
The compiler must not silently interpret saturation as success. It has two admissible policies.

     compile the downstream theorem conditionally on it.

justifies the final theorem.

9.5    Changing-parameter recursion
Some cycles are only apparent because a later phase changes a parameter. For example, a horizon h
may grow while precision q(h) is recomputed. The framework first chooses a single finite terminal cap
H, then treats (h, q) as coordinates of the same capped closure. This avoids induction hypotheses that
quantify over all future horizons. If no finite H is known, the theorem is not a finite-use result and
the missing horizon producer must be made residual.

9.6    Commutation with proof compilation
Demand closure is computed before term generation and forward proof scheduling. The closed
vector is substituted into every node’s local contract. Thus resource closure and proof compilation
commute in the following precise sense: compiling with the least closed vector yields the same terminal
formula as compiling with any larger adequate vector, except that the latter contains unused precision.
Monotonicity lemmas transport observations from the larger requests down to the requested tolerance.
The least vector is preferred for proof size and executable cost, not for logical strength.


10     Finite relational witnesses and backward viability
10.1    Why local choice is insufficient
Suppose a computation has finite candidate sets A0 , . . . , AH , validity predicates Vi (a), compatibility
relations Ei (a, b) ⊆ Ai ×Ai+1 , and a terminal acceptance predicate T (a) on AH . Pointwise nonemptiness
of every Ai does not imply the existence of a compatible path. Choosing the least valid candidate at
each level can enter a dead end.
The correct finite analogue of dependent choice is to remove dead ends before selecting anything.

10.6    Products and amalgamation
If a theorem needs several independent relational certificates at the same finite horizon, form the

11     A coherence trichotomy for nonunique finite witnesses
11.1    The recurring mistake
Suppose that for every horizon N there exists a finite compatible witness sequence x0 , . . . , xN . It is
tempting to speak immediately of “the sequence” or of a Cauchy name selected from these witnesses.
Constructively, three different conclusions must be separated:
  1. a finite consumer needs only a stable output at one requested horizon;
  2. a later theorem needs one coherent path whose prefixes agree;
  3. analysis needs a regular metric name with a known rate.
Each level requires strictly more data than the preceding level.

11.6    Applications
Polynomial roots. At collisions, unordered multisets or clusters are Level I observables. A
continuously labeled root path is Level II and requires separation or an explicit continuation rule. A
regular complex root name is Level III and requires quantitative motion/tail control.

Spectral theory. Spectral projectors and invariant subspaces are often Level I quotient observ-
ables. A normalized eigenvector path requires a gap and phase convention. Convergence of iterative
eigenvectors requires an explicit contraction rate.

Optimization. An approximate minimum value over a finite net is Level I. A coherent sequence of
minimizers requires tie-handling or a stable quotient. A convergent minimizer name requires error
bounds converting mesh and objective gaps into metric distance.

Probability. Finite rational laws and expectations are Level I. A coupling across truncation levels is
Level II. A limit distribution name with a prescribed metric rate is Level III and needs a quantitative
theorem such as a Berry–Esseen bound, not merely tightness or subsequential convergence.


12     Guarded operations, extensionality, and finite gluing
12.1    Nonzero is not positive apartness
In constructive arithmetic, the proposition x ≠ 0 does not generally provide an integer g such that
|x| ≥ 2−g . Algorithms for division, matrix inversion, Newton iteration, branch selection, and chart
changes need such quantitative data to choose a finite input precision.
A guarded operation therefore has two layers:
 (1) a total primitive-recursive program, defined on every code and permitted to return a default
     outside the intended domain;
 (2) a correctness theorem under a positive rational margin certificate.
For inversion, a typical contract is

                     |x| ≥ 2−g ,   |x̃ − x| ≤ 2−m    =⇒       invm,g (x̃) − x−1 ≤ 2−q

when m ≥ q + 2g + c. The exact constant depends on the rational coding. The backward demand
edge for inversion is therefore q 7→ q + 2g + c.

checker fails. Then the program is primitive recursive and iPRA proves

                   G(x, g) ∧ ValidX (x, d(q, g), a) −→ ValidY (f x, q, Runf (x, q, g, a)).

No correctness claim is made when G fails.

domain condition.

Let U0 , . . . , Ur−1 be a fixed finite cover. A gluing certificate contains local codes si , validity proofs on

12.6     Projective and quotient data
Many singular choices disappear when the output is represented invariantly:
   • a line is a primitive integer or rational vector modulo nonzero scaling, with equality witnessed
     by cross-multiplication;
   • a rotation axis is a projective vector or quaternionic relation, not a normalized signed unit vector
     at the identity;
   • an eigenobject at a repeated eigenvalue is a projector or invariant-subspace relation, not a chosen
     basis vector;
   • a divisor or sheaf section is represented by finite local data and explicit overlap witnesses.
Normalization is added only under a guard proving that a selected coordinate or norm is bounded
away from zero.

12.7     Exact branch data
Exact Puiseux coefficients or analytic branches require more than arbitrary Cauchy coefficients. A
sound interface supplies an exact algebraic presentation, a factor/splitting certificate, and a separation
or discriminant condition. Without these data, the general output is a finite relation of approximate
branches. Stable symmetric observations may still be exact; individual labels are guarded.


13     The checked algebraic backend
13.1     Why this backend matters
Many packets reduce a theorem to a finite polynomial identity but then stop at a semantic ring
proof. The current Derivations library closes this gap for a substantial class of identities. It derives
arithmetic laws in the embedded theory, quotients terms by provable equality, embeds the quotient
semiring into a ring of differences, performs metalevel ring normalization, and reflects the normalized
equality back to Prf PRAAx.
This architecture is exactly compatible with KIFC-S. An algebra node stores an output equation and
a proof-producing tactic invocation. The equation can then be consumed by later search, loop, or
observer nodes through ordinary equality substitution.

KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                             2026-11-16


13.2    Provable-equality quotient
Fix a context Γ. Define s ∼Γ t when Γ ⊢ s = t. Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity are derived in
the deduction system; congruence follows from equality substitution. Thus ∼Γ is a congruence for the
derived addition and multiplication symbols. The quotient QΓ is a cancellative commutative semiring
because the corresponding laws and cancellation are proved in iPRA.
Natural-number terms embed into the Grothendieck ring G(QΓ ). Injectivity of the canonical map
follows from additive cancellation. An integer term is represented by a pair (p, n) denoting p − n, and
equality of integer terms is translated to the natural equality

                                           p + n′ = p′ + n.

Consequently, if a polynomial identity holds in G(QΓ ), injectivity yields an equality in QΓ , which by
definition is an object-level derivation.

13.3    Sound use of metalevel tactics
The tactic ipra_ring does not assert that ring normalization is an axiom of iPRA. Its proof term has
the following shape:
 (1) map each object term to its class in QΓ and then to G(QΓ );
 (2) use Lean’s ring tactic to prove equality in this metalevel ring;
 (3) apply injectivity to recover equality of quotient classes;
 (4) unfold quotient equality to obtain Γ ⊢ s = t.
The current axiom-audit module reports no sorryAx. It lists only Lean’s standard metatheoretic
axioms for quotients, choice, and propositional extensionality; the object theorem still depends only
on PRAAx.

13.4    Algebra node interface
A generic algebra node can be represented by:

                            AlgNode(Γ, s, t, h),     h : Γ ⊢ [PRAAx]s = t.

Its operational output may be one side of the equation; its proof output is h. Common subclasses are:
  • polynomial normalization and factorization;
  • linear-combination certificates for ideal membership;
  • determinant and adjugate identities at fixed dimension;
  • integer-coordinate geometry identities;
  • finite combinatorial sum identities after recurrence expansion.
Not every algebraic theorem is a ring identity. Inequalities require order lemmas and often sums
of squares or explicit witnesses. Divisibility requires a witness term. Matrix statements of variable
dimension require loop invariants. The node interface is nevertheless useful: these stronger nodes may
call the ring backend for their local algebraic subgoals.

14     Domain embedding recipes
This section turns the general framework into reusable proof patterns. The recipes are not assertions
that every named deep theorem has now been proved. They identify the admissible statement, the
finite data, the object-theory core, and the remaining producer obligations.

14.1    Elementary and algebraic number theory
Divisibility, gcd, and congruence. Use primitive-recursive bounded search for quotients and
Bézout coefficients within explicit bounds. Divisibility is witnessed by a term k and an algebra equation
a = bk. Euclidean descent is a loop with the remainder as a decreasing rank. Congruence calculations
reduce to polynomial identities and divisibility witnesses.

Finite sums over divisors or primes. Primality and divisibility below n are bounded predicates.
A sum such as d|n f (d) is a primitive recursion over d < n + 1 with a Boolean guard. Prove the
                 P

summation recurrence by QF induction; prove identities by rearranging finite double sums through
explicit bijections or counting certificates. No infinite Dirichlet series is needed for a finite identity.




                                                    42
KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                                 2026-11-16


Asymptotic theorems.          State a theorem with explicit constants and threshold:
                                n ≥ N (k) −→ |A(n) − M (n)| ≤ E(k, n).
The verifier is finite arithmetic. The producer must supply N , constants, and any zero-free or contour
estimate in an effective form. An ineffective existence of constants is not silently converted to a PR
symbol.

14.2    Diophantine geometry
Height-bounded enumeration. A rational point is encoded by primitive integer tuples with
bounded numerator and denominator. For a supplied height bound H(C), enumerate the finite box,
test the equations, normalize signs/gcd, and remove duplicates by bounded search. Completeness
follows immediately from the height hypothesis.
Theorem 14.1 (Height/list equivalence for decidable presentations). Fix a Diophantine presentation
with a primitive-recursive point verifier and height. The following data are interconvertible by primitive-
recursive, object-verifiable constructions:
  (i) a bound H proved to dominate the height of every valid point;
 (ii) a finite complete duplicate-free list of all valid points.
The conversion from a list to a bound takes the maximum height (or zero for the empty list); the
conversion from a bound to a list is bounded enumeration and verification.

Proof. Given H, enumerate every canonical code of height at most H, retain exactly those satisfying
the verifier, and remove duplicates. Every retained item is valid by reflection. Every valid point has
height at most H, hence has a canonical code in the enumerated box and appears in the filtered
list. Conversely, given a complete list, take the maximum of the finitely many heights. Completeness
implies every valid point occurs and is bounded by that maximum. All operations are bounded folds
and searches, so their correctness is handled by section 7.

This theorem clarifies the Faltings/Siegel difficulty. The finite part of the proof is not the obstacle.
The obstacle is the uniform, effective, object-justified height producer.

Mordell–Weil descent. A Selmer class is not automatically a rational point on its covering. The
interface must return a finite relation of covering representatives with explicit local/global compatibility
certificates. Backward viability can amalgamate a finite descent tree once such representatives are
supplied. A general uniform representative producer remains a separate mathematical problem.

Runge and explicit special cases. When geometry yields an explicit height inequality, the
complete proof fits the framework: derive the inequality using algebra nodes and guarded estimates,
compute H, enumerate, and verify. This is why Runge-type cases can be fully constructive even when
general Siegel remains open.

14.3    Real and complex analysis
Real names.      Use dyadic rational approximants with a regularity contract
                                         |ak − aℓ | ≤ 2−k   (ℓ ≥ k),
or a relational cloud plus theorem 11.5. Addition and multiplication have explicit demand transformers.
Inversion, logarithm, square root, and inverse trigonometric functions require guards.

                                                     43

Integration. A Riemann or quadrature approximation is a finite rational sum. The input includes a
modulus of continuity or derivative bounds and a tail/error theorem. The terminal query determines
a mesh size. The object proof consists of the finite sum identity, local cell estimates, and a finite error
sum. An “integrable” proposition without a modulus is insufficient for a uniform PR integrator.

Power series. A series is consumed through a partial sum and a tail bound. Composition, dif-
ferentiation, or inversion requires explicit coefficient bounds and a radius/apartness guard. Formal
power-series identities are exact algebra nodes; analytic equality adds a finite tail estimate.

Contour arguments. Replace a contour by a rational polygonal path or a certified finite parametriza-
tion, and replace integrals by quadrature with explicit error. Winding numbers are finite integer
computations under a positive distance-from-pole guard. Residue theorems then separate into exact
algebraic residue identities and quantitative approximation estimates.

Gamma, zeta, and special functions. Choose one finite representation—Euler products, alternat-
ing series, Euler–Maclaurin sums, or contour quadrature—and attach explicit domain, truncation, and
error contracts. Functional equations become comparisons of two finite approximations with common
error budgets plus exact algebraic identities. Any hidden ineffective constant remains a producer
obligation.

14.4    Probability and limit theorems
A directly admissible probability object is a finite rational law. Expectations and moments are finite
sums. For triangular arrays or approximated laws, input data include tail, moment, and quantization
moduli. A central limit theorem suitable for iPRA states an explicit finite bound, such as a Berry–
Esseen inequality for rational variables with a displayed constant. CDF comparisons at discontinuity
points require anti-concentration or smoothing data. Weak convergence without a modulus is retained
as a finite-test or metastable contract.

14.5    Geometry
Coordinate algebra. Whenever possible, express the theorem as a polynomial identity in integer or
rational coordinates. The current ipra_ring backend already handles a meaningful class of triangle
identities.

Avoiding division. Use homogeneous coordinates, oriented determinants, squared distances, and
cross-multiplied ratio statements. Introduce a denominator only under a nondegeneracy/apartness
certificate.

Incidence and conics. A projective incidence theorem is a determinant identity plus rank/nonde-
generacy guards. Pappus, Pascal, Desargues, Ceva, and Menelaus are natural algebra-node targets
after suitable coordinate normalization.

Spherical and rotational geometry. Trigonometric quantities are finite names with error con-
tracts. Exact axes and inverse angles are guarded. At singular configurations, use rotation matrices,
quaternions modulo sign, or relations of admissible axes rather than a discontinuous selected vector.

KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                                  2026-11-16


14.6    Linear algebra and spectral theory
Fixed-dimension determinant, adjugate, Cayley–Hamilton, and characteristic-polynomial identities are
algebra nodes. Variable dimension uses loops over indices with QF matrix-entry invariants. Gaussian
elimination is totalized: pivot search consumes a certified rank/pivot margin, or the output is a relation
of row-reduction branches.
For symmetric spectral theory, an unordered multiset of eigenvalue intervals is stable through collisions.
Ordered labels require a separation certificate. Eigenspaces are represented by approximate projectors or
finite relations of bases; a normalized eigenvector requires a simple-eigenvalue gap and a sign/coordinate
normalization guard.

14.7    Combinatorics and finite topology
Finite combinatorial identities are particularly well suited to KIFC-S. Objects are bounded codes,
predicates are decidable, and counting is primitive recursion. Bijections can be supplied as mutually
inverse PR maps with object-level inverse equations. Recurrences use QF induction.
Topological theorems should first be stated for finite simplicial or cubical complexes. Chains, boundaries,
parity, homology over finite coefficients, and carrier maps are finite matrix calculations. Passage from
a represented continuous space to a finite complex requires an approximation/nerve producer with
explicit mesh and separation data. The finite theorem and the approximation theorem remain distinct
contracts.

14.8    Algebraic geometry and sheaf-like constructions
Use finite affine presentations, ideal-membership witnesses, localization equalities with explicit killing
exponents, finite principal-open covers, and Cech tuples. A section is local finite data plus overlap
certificates. Gluing follows theorem 12.4; quotient equality is witnessed algebraically rather than
decided for arbitrary represented reals. General geometric finiteness theorems still require effective
degree/height bounds before enumeration.

14.9    Classical finite arguments
A finite decidable argument may be classically discovered and then checked constructively. The best
route is certificate extraction: a classical search tool produces a finite witness, and iPRA verifies it. For
theorem schemas whose conclusions are negative or stable, a negative-translation route may also apply,
but it should be formalized as a separate proof translation and not assumed globally. Disjunction and
existential conclusions require explicit recoverability, normally supplied here by bounded decidability
and least search.


15     Open-sequent portability and backend integration
15.1    The canonical interface
New portable results should be stated first in the ordinary ConciseIPRA language as open contracts or,
equivalently, sequents A ⊢ [PRAAx] φ. This preserves hypotheses and free parameters and gives direct
access to the checked derivation library. Backend-specific translations occur only at the boundary
required by an application.




                                                     45
KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                                2026-11-16


15.2    Native scalar transfer is already context-sensitive
The checked theorem pra_iff_native states, for arbitrary finite contexts,

                               A ⊢ [PRAAx] φ      ⇐⇒        NPrf(Anat , φnat ).

Combining it with theorem 4.2 gives immediately:
Theorem 15.1 (Contract/native equivalence).

                                  Ctr(A, φ)    ⇐⇒        NPrf(Anat , φnat ).

Proof. Compose the contract–sequent equivalence with pra_iff_native.

Thus the compact native checker can verify a compiled contract after the ordinary theorem exists.
Native transfer should not be used as a substitute for constructing the ordinary theorem, because the
checked equivalence already provides the proper order of operations.

15.3    Substitution before translation
Suppose a generic contract contains free parameters and an application has concrete terms. First apply
theorem 4.11 in the ordinary language. Then translate the specialized sequent. This avoids proving a
separate backend theorem for every parameter instance and keeps variable-layout obligations localized.

15.4    Foundation transport
The current bridge already supports important closed endpoints and contains much of the syntax
translation needed for open results. The repository unification plan correctly isolates the remaining
task: preserve and reflect a finite open context while retaining the identities of free variables under
binders.
A complete open bridge should provide functions of the following mathematical shape:

                                 A ⊢ [PRAAx] φ −→ FoundationPrf(AF , φF ),
                            FoundationPrf(B, θ) −→ B C ⊢ [PRAAx] θC ,

with round-trip equivalences sufficient to recover the original formulas. The first direction can translate
a derivation under arbitrary term assignments. Reflection must assign the finitely occurring free
variables distinct fresh indices so their identities survive translation.
Principle 15.2 (Bridge obligation isolation). Until the open Foundation bridge is checked, an
application may use either:
   1. a closed exported theorem obtained by universally closing and discharging the ordinary contract;
      or
   2. a backend-specific local theorem whose exact premises and theory are stated separately.
It must not silently identify an ambient Foundation theorem with an ordinary Prf PRAAx contract.

15.5    Round-trip requirements
The open bridge needs four families of lemmas.
   1. Term round trip: every primitive-recursive code is provably extensionally equal to its translated
      round trip at arbitrary parameters.

                                                    46
KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                               2026-11-16


  2. Formula round trip: equality atoms, implication, and both quantifiers are preserved up to
     object-level biconditional.
  3. Context transport: translation commutes with list mapping, weakening, substitution, and
     binder shift.
  4. Sequent recovery: translated assumptions can be replaced by original assumptions through
     simultaneous cut and the round-trip biconditionals.
The principal formal risk is de Bruijn bookkeeping, not a missing mathematical principle. Therefore the
implementation should first register translation/substitution simp lemmas, then recurse on derivations.

15.6    Portable theorem packaging
A portable theorem artifact should expose:
  • the ordinary formula and contract;
  • a native corollary using theorem 15.1;
  • a Foundation corollary only when the relevant bridge declaration is checked;
  • the exact theory name for every exported endpoint;
  • a record of any closure operation used to eliminate local hypotheses.
This prevents accidental upgrades between iPRA, classical PRA, HA, PA, and stronger fragments.

15.7    Why open transport matters
Closed sentences are adequate for final published theorems but inefficient for a reusable library.
Arithmetic lemmas are naturally stated at arbitrary terms under local hypotheses; graph nodes
consume temporary invariants; residual producer theorems are often conditional. Closing every
intermediate result introduces avoidable quantifiers and repeated instantiation proofs. The contract
interface keeps these results open and makes the remaining backend transport requirement explicit.

16.1    Example A: a residual height-bounded enumerator
Let P (c, ⃗x) be a reflected predicate on canonical point codes, let h(c) be a primitive-recursive height,

The graph has nodes for box generation, predicate reflection, filtering, canonicalization, deduplication,

                                        c ∈ LH −→ P (c, ⃗x) = 1.
Completeness consumes the boundary formula
                         HeightBound(⃗x, H) :    P (c, ⃗x) = 1 −→ h(c) ≤ H(⃗x).
The query for soundness alone reaches no height boundary. The query for completeness reaches exactly
HeightBound. The combined query shares enumeration and filtering. The compiler exports
                           Ctr([HeightBound], Sound(LH ) ∧ Complete(LH )).

KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                              2026-11-16


This demonstrates query-minimal residuals: a verifier does not prove the existence of the cutoff it
checks.

16.2    Example B: exact rational bisection of y 3 + y = x
For rational x and an initial interval [a, b] with
                                          a3 + a ≤ x ≤ b3 + b,
run N bisection steps. The state stores the rational endpoints. At each step, compare the midpoint
polynomial with x, retaining the half interval containing a root. Monotonicity follows from
                            (v 3 + v) − (u3 + u) = (v − u)(u2 + uv + v 2 + 1),
and the second factor is positive for ordered rationals.
All comparisons are exact integer inequalities after clearing positive denominators. The loop invariant
is the conjunction of endpoint order, bracketing, and the denominator convention; after Booleanization
it is quantifier-free. The normalized trace kernel proves the invariant at the computed bound. A
final arithmetic contract proves interval width (b − a)/2N . There is no branch-separation residual
because equality at the midpoint may be handled by either closed half interval. The public output is
an interval, not a discontinuously selected sign branch.

16.3    Example C: a bounded challenge–response theorem
Suppose A(u, v, w, ⃗x) is reflected and all bounds are primitive recursive. Consider
                        F (⃗x) = ∀u < U ∃v < V (u) ∀w < W (u, v) A(u, v, w, ⃗x).
The strategy compiler constructs a truth certificate consisting of a response table v(u) and atomic truth
data for every legal challenge (u, w). A falsity certificate chooses one u, gives for every candidate v a
counterchallenge w(v), and supplies atomic falsity data. The two checkers are reflected by structural
contracts. A later theorem can consume F without knowing which finite strategy representation was
used.

16.4    Example D: a dead-end relation
Let levels be
                               X0 = {0, 1},    X1 = {0, 1, 2},     X2 = {2},
with edges 0 → 0, 1 → 1, 1 → 2, and 2 → 2 where types permit. Backward viability at horizon 2
removes 0 ∈ X1 , then removes 0 ∈ X0 ; only the branch beginning at 1 survives. Forward greedy choice
without viability could choose 0 and get stuck. The reference implementation checks that the least
viable path is (1, 2, 2).

16.5    Example E: cyclic precision requests

strictly decreases on every nonstationary update, so at most 17 rounds are needed. If a transformer

17     Resolution of the recurring difficulty reports
This section states each common obstruction in a form suitable for a formalization decision. “Resolved”
means the generic consumer or composition theorem is supplied; it does not mean that every theorem-
specific quantitative producer exists.

17.1    Ambient evidence versus an object proof
Failure mode. A Lean theorem about N, a successful Python computation, or a checked finite
certificate is cited as though it had type Prf PRAAx.

Repair. Every executable node ends in a local contract. Semantic code may calculate terms and
proof schedules, but the public endpoint is converted through theorem 4.2. Tests remain validation
only.

Status. Resolved architecturally. The remaining work for an application is to construct its local
object derivations.

17.2    Bounded quantifiers inside induction
Failure mode. An invariant says “for every table entry below the current index” or “there exists a
pivot”, and is therefore not syntactically quantifier-free.

Repair. Compile the bounded statement to a Boolean fold or a strategy checker. The invariant
contains an equality such as b(s) = 1, which is quantifier-free. Reflection is used outside or alongside
the induction.

Status. Generic human proof complete; checked implementation should extend the existing bounded-
sum/search library.

KIFC-S for iPRA                                                                               2026-11-16


17.3    All precisions inside one loop
Failure mode. A trace invariant quantifies over every requested precision.

Repair. Keep terminal precision as an open parameter, run reverse demand before the trace, allocate
only the finite observations requested by that parameter, prove one QF invariant, and quantify after
induction.

Status. Resolved for acyclic and justified capped-cyclic demand systems.

17.4    Hidden quantitative producers
Failure mode. A height cutoff, separation gap, effective constant, finite net, or representative
system is inserted as host-language data without a formula or proof.

Repair. Attach a boundary formula to the node that needs the data. Query reachability collects it
into the least residual frontier. A later producer contract closes it.

Status. Resolved as a status discipline. Existence of particular producers may remain open mathe-
matics.

17.5    A universal primitive-recursive evaluator
Failure mode. The framework assumes one total primitive-recursive function that evaluates every
coded primitive-recursive function.
Barrier 17.1. There is no total primitive-recursive universal evaluator for all unary primitive-recursive
functions.

Proof. If U (e, x) were such an evaluator, g(e) = U (e, e) + 1 would be primitive recursive. Let d code g.
Then g(d) = U (d, d) + 1 = g(d) + 1, contradiction.

Repair. Specialize fixed syntax at the metalevel, or use a clocked evaluator for a fixed instruction
set and explicit bound. The generated term is an ordinary finite composition/recursion tree.

17.6    Independent finite witnesses
Failure mode. Each stage or horizon chooses a witness independently, but the conclusion assumes
compatible choices.

Repair. Use backward viability for finite horizons. Then apply the coherence trichotomy: stable
observer for finite use, extension system for a path, and tail data for a regular name.

Status. Finite case resolved. Infinite coherence remains conditional on explicit producer data.

18.4    Normalized trace kernel
The included candidate deliberately requires theorem-specific normalization before invoking the generic
theorem. Its proof has five logical steps:
  1. call PRA.qfInd on the invariant-along-run formula;
  2. universally eliminate at the computed bound;
  3. apply the terminal implication;
  4. discharge the input guard by implication introduction;
  5. universally close the remaining input variable.
This is a better integration target than a large generic trace theorem that tries to rewrite every run
equation internally.

18.5    Boolean and bounded-strategy implementation
Start with fixed combinators, not a universal formula-code interpreter. A structure Reflected should
contain a formula, Boolean term, range derivation, and two implication directions. Constructors for
equality, order, conjunction, disjunction, and negation are followed by bounded allB and anyB folds.
The strategy compiler is a metalevel recursion over a fixed syntax returning:
  • certificate code layouts;
  • truth and falsity checker PR symbols;
  • canonical constructor PR symbols;
  • local reflection contracts assembled from the fixed combinators.
The generated module contains the fixed symbols and derivations; there is no object-level evaluation
of arbitrary syntax.

18.6    Graph compiler implementation
The metalevel graph structure carries names, formulas, parent indices, boundary records, and local
contracts. Compilation performs:
  1. validate indices and arities;
  2. compute backward reachability from the query;
  3. normalize and deduplicate boundaries;
  4. topologically sort reachable nodes after recursive components are collapsed;
  5. build one contract per reachable conclusion using Contract.trans;
  6. conjoin requested sinks;
  7. emit Contract.toPrf and provenance declarations.

18.7   Coherence interfaces
Do not define a record named merely Coherent with hidden semantic fields. Use three separate
structures:
  • StableObserver: finite viability and exact/metric/quotient stability contracts;
  • ExtensionSystem: a PR extension symbol and preservation/compatibility contracts;
  • RegularNameData: local motion bounds and a finite-tail contract.
The type distinction prevents a Level I theorem from being consumed where a Level III name is
required.

18.8   Foundation bridge plan
Implementation should follow the repository unification plan:
  1. prove extensional equality of Foundation PR codes with their round trips at arbitrary parameters;
  2. lift to terms and formulas;
  3. establish translation/substitution and translation/shift simp lemmas;
  4. preserve derivations under arbitrary term assignments;
  5. reflect finite free-variable contexts using fresh indices;
  6. recover original assumptions by round-trip biconditionals and Prf.trans;
  7. derive existing closed exports as corollaries where theorem types remain unchanged.

18.9   Acceptance tests
A framework theorem should be advertised as formalized only after:
 (a) its public formula and residual assumptions are printed;
 (b) its endpoint has type Prf PRAAx, NPrf, or a checked backend judgment;
 (c) no sorry, admit, theorem-specific axiom, or semantic oracle lies in the dependency closure;
 (d) #print axioms is recorded;
 (e) a clean build from the pinned snapshot succeeds;
  (f) generated source is deterministic from the graph and query;
 (g) reference algorithms agree with independent tests, reported only as tests;
 (h) native or Foundation transfer occurs after the ordinary theorem exists, unless the theorem is
     explicitly backend-specific.

19.5    Claims deliberately not made

  • an unrestricted exact selector for roots, eigenvectors, axes, or minimizers at collisions;

  • a height cutoff extracted from mere finiteness or cardinality;
  • effective general Faltings, Siegel, Roth, Mordell–Weil generation, Chebotarev, Siegel–Walfisz, or
    Bombieri–Vinogradov;
  • a compiled generic graph elaborator or completed Foundation open bridge.

19.6     Trust boundary
Logical soundness rests on Lean’s kernel, the ConciseIPRA syntax and deduction implementation, and
the imported checked theorem declarations. Metalevel generators, Python tests, JSON graph files, and
provenance tooling are outside the logical trusted base. A malformed generator can fail to compile,
produce an unintended explicit formula, or introduce unnecessary residuals; it cannot make the kernel
accept a false theorem without an unsound imported axiom.

A.1    Variable order
The proposed Lean library should fix conventions globally:
  • loop formulas: variable 0 is time/state focus, followed by state components and external
    parameters;
  • relation formulas: variable 0 is the current candidate, variable 1 the next or comparison candidate,
    followed by query and parameters;
  • observer formulas: two candidate blocks first, then query and parameters;
  • rational metric formulas: numerator/denominator components are grouped and accessed through
    named substitutions.
Named substitution helpers should be proved once. Ad hoc Fin.cases expressions are a major source
of binder errors.

A.2    Rational codes
A convenient exact rational code is a signed integer numerator and a positive natural denominator.
Normalization is optional for arithmetic, but equality is cross multiplication. Every theorem that
divides by a denominator carries its positivity proof. Dyadic codes may use a signed numerator and
exponent; comparisons are reduced to a common exponent.

A.3    Bounds
All bounds are exclusive unless explicitly stated. “Search below B” means candidates 0, . . . , B − 1. A
finite relation may totalize invalid out-of-range cells as false. This convention simplifies rectangular

B     Reusable proof templates
B.1    Template 1: deterministic finite algorithm
Input: fixed PR state, bound, QF invariant, base/step/finish contracts. Normalize theorem-specific
rewrites, invoke the trace kernel, specialize at the bound, and export an open contract.

B.2    Template 2: bounded alternating statement
Input: reflected atoms and explicit bounds. Generate truth/falsity certificate layouts, checkers,
canonical constructors, and reflection contracts. Export a local decision theorem or retain the checked
certificate as an input.

B.3    Template 3: residual producer
Input: a difficult quantitative theorem. State the exact formula it must deliver, its coding conventions,
and its parameters. Keep the consumer graph checked independently and close by contract cut when
the producer arrives.

B.4    Template 4: finite relation
Input: bounded state sets and compatibility. Compute backward viability, search the least viable
successor, and either return a finite path or erase choice through a stable observer.

B.5    Template 5: coherent path
Input: viable initial state and PR extension with preservation and compatibility contracts. Iterate by
primitive recursion and prove the open path invariant by QF induction.

B.6    Template 6: regular name
Input: finite observations, adjacent motion bounds, and a tail modulus. Prove finite triangle estimates
and sample at the modulus. Do not infer the tail modulus from metastability.

B.7    Template 7: guarded operation
Input: a total implementation, positive margin, demand transformer, and local estimate. Reflect
the guard, compute demand before the trace, prove the intended branch under the guard, and leave
behavior outside the public domain unspecified.

B.8    Template 8: finite net
Input: net generator, coverage certificate, modulus, and local verifier. Verify all net points, search a
covering cell for an arbitrary coded query, and transport the estimate with an explicit error budget.

B.9    Template 9: height-bounded list
Input: code box, verifier, canonicalization, height, and height-bound formula. Enumerate, filter,
deduplicate, prove soundness unconditionally and completeness under the height residual.
