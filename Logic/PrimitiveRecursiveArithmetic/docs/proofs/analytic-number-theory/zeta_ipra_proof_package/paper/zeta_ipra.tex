\vspace{1.1cm}
\begin{abstract}
We develop a substantial portion of the classical theory of the Riemann
zeta function by explicit finite arguments suitable for first-order
intuitionistic primitive recursive arithmetic. The constructions include
Dirichlet and Euler-product representations with moduli, a uniform
finite-block proof of divergence of the defining series on
$\Re s\le1$, an entire regular part obtained by finite Euler--Maclaurin
summation, all nonpositive and positive even integer values, Stieltjes
coefficients, the theta transformation and both standard functional
equations, the entire xi function, arithmetic Dirichlet series, a global Euler--Hasse series, integral
representations, simple trivial zeros, and a quantitative zero-free
boundary theorem. Every analytic limit is read through prescribed
rational approximations and primitive recursive error bounds.

The arithmetic assertions are uniform for coded rational inputs and are
schemas in each fixed certified primitive recursive real or complex
name. They do not postulate an arbitrary-real sort, choice, an unrestricted
identity theorem, or a universal primitive recursive program evaluator.
The package includes exact rational enclosure software and a detailed
Lean outline. The mathematical proofs are human-readable; the analytic
development has not been kernel checked and no completed iPRA proof
certificate is claimed.
\end{abstract}

The strongest uniform algorithmic statements concern coded rational inputs.
For nonrational inputs the exact interpretation and hypotheses are given
in Section~\ref{sec:foundations}. ``Meromorphic identity'' means equality
of specified local expressions after removing or clearing displayed
poles. It never assigns a finite value to $\zeta(1)$.

\textbf{Results not proved here.} We do not prove the Riemann hypothesis,
zero counting or zero-density theorems, infinitely many critical-line
zeros, a Hadamard product over all zeros, universality, the prime number
theorem, or irrationality of $\zeta(3)$. The Riemann hypothesis is a
conjecture on the reference pages; the other omissions are limitations
of this package, not assertions of unprovability in iPRA. We also do not
claim a uniform decision procedure for equality of arbitrary real names
or for whether an unspecified complex input lies on a domain boundary.

\section{Provenance, dependencies, and scope of verification}
The two requested web pages determine the selection of result families.
NIST DLMF is used to check standard normalizations, notably the signs in
the functional equations and special values~\cite{DLMF25_2,DLMF25_4,DLMF25_6,DLMF25_8}.
The quantitative derivations and their finite-precision organization are
given in the manuscript; a source citation is not used as a substitute
for an iPRA proof.

We inspected the repository's ordinary iPRA definition and native bridge,
and the human proof packages for calculus, gamma and the zeta functional
equation. The gamma gateway is reconstructed here with its actual finite
estimates, rather than imported as an unverified analytic axiom.
The other repository analytic packages contain proof plans and explicitly
report incomplete Lean formalizations; they are not treated as completed
formal dependencies. The provenance manifest lists the exact paths and
Git blob identifiers actually read. No repository file was changed and
no commit or pull request was created for this archive request.

The proof review checked the Euler--Maclaurin sign, Bernoulli convention,
Mellin normalization, gamma and theta constants, quantitative propagation,
exceptional eta denominator, and boundary lower bounds. It also checked
the distinction between Euclidean and rational $1$-norm errors in the
executable enclosures. The saved exact tests and build record are under
\texttt{verification/}. These are concrete verification records, while
the missing kernel proof remains explicitly listed in Section~\ref{sec:lean}.
