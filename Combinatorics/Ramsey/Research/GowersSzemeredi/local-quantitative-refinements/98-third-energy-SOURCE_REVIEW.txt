2. CANDIDATE-FAMILY SCREENING IN OPENAI/MATH

The repository catalogue and overview were reviewed, followed by selective
reading of mathematical source files. The four candidates below were screened
for a concrete question connected with ProveIt's research. This was not a
complete proof audit of any family's global headline theorem.

Family 159: quasipolynomial bounds for arithmetic progressions.

  Source directory:
  preprints/Quasipolynomial-Bounds-for-Arithmetic-Progressions-September-23-2026/

  Selected source files read:
  build/sections/00-introduction.tex
  build/sections/03-relative-detection.tex
  build/sections/04-scalar.tex
  build/sections/09-consequences.tex
  lean/docs/159.md (relative to the repository root)

The introduction and consequences were read fully; the relative-detection and
scalar material received selective mathematical inspection. The source reports
quasipolynomial Szemeredi bounds and consequences for sets with divergent sums
of reciprocals. Its consequences already discuss weighted divergence and
harmonic tails, so these were not treated as unexplored applications. This
family is close to ProveIt's Gowers--Szemeredi work, but its complete global
quantitative argument was not audited. The stated Lean scope does not certify
the whole quantitative bound. No new Szemeredi bound is asserted here.

Family 182: polynomial-difference avoidance.

The catalogue groups intersective polynomial differences, prime arguments, and
square differences. The selected branch is:

  preprints/A-power-saving-for-square-difference-free-sets-September-24-2026/

Its graph and tuple sections were read directly. They expose a local loss
between the p^(-1/2) single-edge spectral estimate and the p^(-1/8) whole-list
estimate. That precise interface was selected because it can be improved and
propagated through a complete finite construction without assuming the source's
global power-saving theorem for integer sets. The main paper addresses this
finite question. It does not audit the other two global branches of Family 182.

Family 169: elementary positivity of chromatic quasisymmetric functions.

  Source file read in full:
  preprints/Elementary-Positivity-of-Chromatic-Quasisymmetric-Functions-September-24-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex

The source reports a deterministic terminating map from a class of permutations
to partitions, yielding a positive expansion in the elementary symmetric-function
basis with q recording graph inversions. It does not claim e-unimodality in
that introduction. The announced route uses graph alphabets in a quantum torus,
positive Hall/scattering constructions, interval rotations, and packet
bijections. This offered an enumerative-combinatorics connection, but auditing
the foundational mechanism would require a separate project. The full proof
was not read, and no consequence of that headline theorem is used here.

Family 242: single-fold Diophantine representations.

  Source directory:
  preprints/Single-fold-Diophantine-representations-September-24-2026/

  Selected source files read:
  build/main.tex
  build/consequences.tex

The source reports representations of recursively enumerable sets with exactly
one complete auxiliary tuple when a solution exists. Its consequences derive
undecidability under an at-most-one-solution promise, including fixed-degree and
fixed-variable-count versions through specialization; the inspected consequences
do not numerically specify the resulting constants or polynomial. The elliptic,
power, and computation body files were not audited. A connection to Diophantine
research was considered, but no local question was selected without checking
that underlying uniqueness machinery.

The permutation-block architecture, index interaction, marked-cut positivity,
and list regrouping are inherited from this source and explicitly credited and
reproved in the new paper. The contribution is the whole-list spectral
replacement with geometric coefficients and optimal ordering, its exact
square-field specialization, the revised marking scale and depth, the
fixed-index subgroup extension, and the matching conditional-norm obstruction.

Telescoping products, Cauchy--Schwarz, spectral mixing, character orthogonality,
and Gram-matrix positivity are classical ingredients. The bibliography gives
relevant external mathematical context, including edge-exposure arguments of
Conlon, Fox, and Zhao. No claim that telescoping itself is new is intended.
Targeted searches in ProveIt did not locate an earlier version of the same
whole-list theorem; that bounded search does not establish global priority.

That report establishes the arbitrary-group second-energy threshold 3/4,
distinguishes hereditary infima from individual finite tests, and develops the
torsion-aware local Jensen route. Report 290 advances the indicator endpoint
classification and the next indicator gap. Report 291 develops the sharp
weighted gap and three-coset extremizers. Later intake includes further
rigidity, fourth-norm extremizers, finite saturation, and counterexamples to an
overly universal endpoint-lifting statement. These existing results were not
treated as new problems solved by the present package.

The new third-energy paper moves from additive quadruples to ordered additive
sextuples and then selected higher-energy families. Its local cyclic forcing,
exact finite characteristic reduction, third-energy sum of squares, and
higher-energy formulas are supplied in the manuscript. The Jensen approach is
credited to Report 288 and developed explicitly for the present quantities.
The reviewed sources did not reveal the same arbitrary-group m=3 theorem; no
exhaustive search of all hereditary-energy literature or publication-priority
determination has been completed.

5. CLAIMS, PROOFS, AND COMPUTATION

The main paper's theorems use no global Szemeredi or square-difference theorem
as a premise. Its numerical checks are diagnostics supporting proofs in the
text. The companion's universal m=3 theorem includes a finite computer-assisted
lemma with an exact integer checker and a proved reduction covering arbitrary
targets. The companion's higher-energy index-two formulas and cyclic-coset
bound have proofs independent of that enumeration.

Both papers distinguish proved statements from further questions. In
particular, this package does not establish a new integer density exponent,
the universal m>=4 rigidity threshold, or a complete third-energy endpoint
classification. It includes no Lean development and claims no external peer
review or certification. Source comparison and a second AI-assisted proof
review are useful checks, but do not establish those statuses.

See README.txt for build and verification commands and the papers themselves
for complete hypotheses, proofs, quantitative limitations, and research agendas.
