2. ENERGY AND COSET MATERIAL: PRINCIPAL LABELS

Place sections/energy.tex and sections/cosets.tex beside the Part II
energy/coset stability material, particularly sources 14 and 45.

  prop:mixed
    Exact nonnegative mixed-set energy remainder. Keep a>=b>0 and the
    counting-measure convention. The identity is stronger information
    than the inequality obtained by discarding its two remainder terms.

  thm:pointwise; thm:multilevel
    Universal coefficient-one overlap gap and the refinement from an
    attained difference-overlap spectrum. The truncation function uses
    SUM multiplicities; its nodes are attained DIFFERENCE multiplicities.

  thm:pointwise-equality
    Complete positive-gap equality classification. Punctured subgroup
    cosets include quotient index three. The separate two-coset family
    uses quotient difference order at least four or infinite order;
    allowing order three there duplicates the first family.

  prop:weighted
    Bounded-weight version using minimum overlap, not product overlap.
    For weights bounded by M the defect is 1-E(f)/(M*(sum f)^3).
    This proposition does not assert a weighted coset-distance theorem.

  thm:coset; cor:outlier; thm:coset-equality
    Canonical subgroup H={d:2*r_A(d)>|A|}, sharp quadratic distance
    envelope for epsilon<=1/4, the retained occupancy loss, and all
    canonical-envelope equality cases.

  cor:unique; prop:threshold-sharp
    Sharp threshold epsilon<2/9 for a unique closest subgroup coset,
    and sharpness of the existence/closure threshold 1/4, respectively.
    Uniqueness among cosets of the canonical H holds for epsilon<1/4;
    uniqueness when the subgroup may vary stops at 2/9.

3. PUNCTURE AND TRANSLATION MATERIAL

Place sections/punctures.tex immediately after the coset theorem and
alongside source 45's punctured-coset stability material.

  prop:puncture-remainder; lem:puncture-budget
    Exact missing-set identity and stronger outlier count. Source 45
    supplies the missing-set framework; the sharper triple count is
    already implicit in source 14. Retain those attributions.

  thm:puncture; thm:puncture-equality
    Repair with profile delta*tau(eta/delta^3), in the strict outer
    epsilon<1/4 and strict inner eta/delta^3<1/4 range, with all equality
    cases. The outer C is the canonical most occupied H-coset, which
    need not be a globally closest coset above defect 2/9.

  prop:translation-decomposition; thm:oriented-puncture
    The actual one-translation gap theta_d supplies the same repair
    profile and forces d outside the underlying subgroup of every
    minimizing hole. The minimization keeps the outer C fixed.

  prop:puncture-sharpness
    Exact nested cyclic examples and optimal uniform asymptotic leading
    constant one in the theta_d/delta^2 and eta/delta^2 scales.

  prop:puncture-endpoint; ex:puncture-inner-endpoint
    The inner normalized gap may equal 1/4. At that endpoint the missing
    set is a union of two stabilizer cosets in the equality case, with
    quotient difference order greater than two. Oriented equality also
    requires K intersect (K+d) to be empty. The outer epsilon<1/4
    hypothesis remains strict; the proposition gives a cyclic obstruction
    at the outer endpoint. These endpoint statements must travel with
    their additional equality classification.

Hole uniqueness holds below the inner defect 2/9. Outer closest-coset
uniqueness holds below the outer defect 2/9. They are separate tests.

4. RESTRICTION MATERIAL AND THE CHANGED CONSTANT

Place sections/restriction.tex and sections/filter.tex beside the
single-relation restriction material of Gowers Lemma 9.3, Corollary 9.4,
and sources 04, 17, and 58.

  rf:thm:expectation
    The repeated-filter expectation theorem is a PREDECESSOR RESULT
    from source 58, reproved here to make the constant substitution
    auditable. Its torsion and repeated-point errors decay with the
    number of layers. Do not claim this theorem, its weighted coefficient
    summation, or its signed first-moment selection as newly originated.

  rf:thm:selection; rf:lem:finite-quotient
    The exact selection certificate and finite-target reduction used
    in the quantitative consequence. The finite quotient preserves only
    the finitely many relevant discrepancies; it is not an embedding of
    an arbitrary abelian target.

  ff:prop:certificates; ff:thm:53; ff:cor:asymmetric
    New rational degree-twelve filter, exact integer certificates,
    common exponent 53, and stronger asymmetric powers. Preserve both
    the explicit size hypothesis and the retained-count inequality.
    Counts include all ordered tuples and all repetitions.

  prop:moment-interface
    Existing fourth-to-sixteenth moment lifting followed by the new
    exponent-53 consequence. The moment implication itself is already
    present in sources 04, 17, and 58.

The improvement is from source 58's all-cyclic common exponent 54 to 53.
The original Gowers exponent is 2^19. Sources 04 and 17 already have
better powers in their different prime-field settings; the all-cyclic
theorem here does not dominate those results. The general finite-domain
expectation theorem retains a torsion factor. A dimension-independent
uniform theorem on all bounded-exponent groups is not established.

5. PRINCIPAL FILTER COST

Place sections/uniform.tex with the positive-filter method analysis
following the source-58 fixed-order theorem.

  uf:thm:uniform
    Explicit bounds for every pair of integers m,a>=2. Keep the entire
    admissible class: finite real even trigonometric polynomials,
    0<=K<=1, K(0)=1, and nonnegative Fourier coefficients. Monotonicity
    of the coefficients is NOT assumed for the universal lower bound.

  uf:prop:concentration
    The convolution-concentration input, proved using the classical
    circle Riesz--Sobolev rearrangement inequality. Preserve the explicit
    attribution to Christ--Iliopoulou, Theorem 1.1 and equation (1.8).

  uf:cor:regimes
    Polynomial-order, faster-growing-order, slower-growing-order, and
    fixed-parameter consequences. Theta functions supply the limiting
    calculation, but finite normalized Fejer means keep the upper bound
    inside the stated finite-polynomial class.

The principal quantity is -log(P_m)/log(P_m/Q_{m,a}). It controls a
specified principal-spectrum certificate. Its lower bound also applies
to independent heterogeneous scalar layers by weighted averaging. It
does not lower-bound the performance on every particular input, every
use of nonprincipal terms, or every conceivable selection method. The
finite-degree error, collision cost, and torsion threshold are separate
optimization problems. No exact value of I_{16,2}, sharp second-order
asymptotic, or optimum common finite exponent is claimed.
