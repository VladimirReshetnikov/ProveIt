## Complete-file records versus partial excerpts

`SOURCE_MANIFEST.json` records 50 complete files: 49 Lean files and one paper transcription. Each record has the repository path, commit-specific URL, byte length, complete-file SHA-256 and Git blob SHA-1. The complete files are deliberately not included in this compact distribution.

Preparation rechecked all 50 available complete files against their byte lengths, SHA-256 and Git blob SHA-1, where the Git hash includes the standard `blob <length>\0` header. `source_excerpts.json` contains 56 exact Lean excerpts totaling 171 source lines. Their original UTF-8 bytes, including final LF, were matched to the recorded inclusive line ranges without internal omissions. Each excerpt has its own partial-text SHA-256 and an explicit link to its complete-file SHA-256. The two paper spans contain mathematical symbols only and use zero-based byte offsets on a specified source line; `end_byte_exclusive` is excluded from the span.

The offline checker captures every pinned data file once, verifies the pin on those immutable bytes and parses those same captured bytes. It verifies the bundled partial-text hashes, metadata links, fixed data pins and inventory without reopening data paths between pin checking and use. Its result concerns that validated snapshot, rather than a later filesystem state. It cannot reconstruct or authenticate the unbundled complete-source files from those excerpts, recheck their inclusion in full files, validate the repository commit, or authenticate the original paper. Hashes demonstrate byte identity relative to the supplied records, not authorship or a trusted signature. No full third-party paper prose, upstream runtime, complete proof tree or earlier report runtime is bundled.

`provenance/PROOF.md` reproduces the frozen analytic proof byte-for-byte, SHA-256 `967e1b57b5823d5eff3282e48420ec64a35bd3562ca5bdfa19a21081dcb2358c`. It remains historically pinned; the Report298 article can separately discuss later source developments.

## Original paper and exponent association

W. T. Gowers, “A new proof of Szemerédi’s theorem,” GAFA 11 (2001), 465–588. The convention and Theorem 18.2 appear on printed p.585, [PDF page 121](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/TOPICS/vdw/sz-thm-gowers-proof.pdf#page=121). The paper's arrow notation is right-associated ordinary exponentiation. At `k=5`, its threshold is `2^(2^(delta^(-2^16384)))`.

The [pinned transcription passage](https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/af74fb522c24a6331886b11db76942642a6a0e42/Combinatorics/Ramsey/Papers/sz-thm-gowers-proof/sz-thm-gowers-proof.tex#L3838-L3840) and pinned Lean `szemerediThreshold` independently specify the association. The small bundled spans retain only the relevant mathematical expressions; explanatory prose is paraphrased here. The source manifest records the original PDF reference and previously recorded repository PDF blob identity, but neither the PDF nor a new authentication of it is supplied by the offline checker.

## Computational checks and limits

The standard-library companion recomputes 17 monomial exponent pairs and 106 integer identities or sufficient inequalities. Its inert formula ledger retains every numerical branch used in the proof, including q=0 with totalized division, natural floors and ceilings, both square thresholds and the final iteration. No tower value or floating-point value is evaluated. The manuscript, rather than finite sampling, establishes the inequalities for all real densities.

The 27-test suite covers independent expansions, mutation of every numeric and string ledger leaf, corrupted formula code and data, normal/optimized parity, numeric and JSON bounds, symlinks, hard links, special files and read-only execution. A genuine file-replacement interleaving verifies that previously hashed bytes, rather than replacement metadata, are parsed. Reproduction logs belong outside the public source tree. See `companion/README.md` and `REPRODUCING.md` for commands and safety limits.
