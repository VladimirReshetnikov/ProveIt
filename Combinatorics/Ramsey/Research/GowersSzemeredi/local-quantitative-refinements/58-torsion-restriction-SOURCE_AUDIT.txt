3. QUANTITATIVE COMPARISON AT SIXTEEN VARIABLES

Notation: b = |B|. T_16 counts all ordered additive sixteen-tuples,
including repetitions. G_16 counts those also respected by the map.
The common input is G_16 >= alpha b^15, with 0 < alpha, eta <= 1.

Source       Domain/target setting             Retained T_16 divided by b^15
Gowers 9.3   Cyclic setting                    (alpha eta / 4)^(2^19)
Source 04    Same prime field                  alpha^16 eta^15 / (2*3200^8)
Source 17    F_p-vector spaces; weights        alpha^16 eta^15 / (2*18^16)
Present      Every cyclic domain;             (alpha eta / 4)^54
             arbitrary abelian target

The associated conditions and quality conclusions are essential:

Gowers, Lemma 9.3:
  The modulus is sufficiently large in terms of the input parameters.
  The respected proportion is at least 1-eta. The repository's current
  formal baseline has an all-moduli exact companion.

Source 04, exact polynomial restriction theorem at d=8:
  b >= 242*3200^8*(alpha eta)^(-16).
  The respected proportion is at least 1/(1+eta), hence at least 1-eta.
  Its convenient order-eight corollary uses the coarser constant 2^(-95)
  after substituting alpha=gamma^7.

Source 17:
  L = ceil(17/(alpha eta)), p > 2L-2,
  N >= 2*L^16*(2L-1)^15*beta^(-15),
  where N=|V| and beta=b/N. The respected proportion is at least 1-eta.
  In the prime-cyclic specialization the size condition implies the
  characteristic condition. Fractional input weights and vector-valued
  maps are allowed in this source's theorem.

Present theorem and finite-quotient corollary:
  b >= (4/(alpha eta))^54.
  The respected proportion is at least 1-eta. Every cyclic modulus is
  allowed, including arbitrarily small prime factors. The proof first
  treats finite abelian targets. A finite quotient of the subgroup
  generated by phi(B) preserves all relevant zero/nonzero discrepancies,
  giving the same conclusion for arbitrary abelian target groups.

Sources 04 and 17 have better prime-field retention powers than 54.
Source 04 has a better threshold degree than source 17; source 17 has
a larger retained constant at sixteen variables and additional weighted
generality. The present result should not be described as dominating
those prime-field theorems. Its quantitative improvement over the original
Lemma 9.3 preserves every cyclic modulus and supplies an explicit threshold.

4. PRIOR INGREDIENTS CREDITED IN THE ARTICLE

Gowers introduced repeated random cosine/Riesz restriction and the
expectation comparison favoring respected configurations. Conditional
independence of point selections is part of that architecture.

Source 04 already uses powered-cosine kernels, nonnegative Fourier
weights, weighted exceptional-relation summation, and a separate
repeated-point correction. In particular, summing weights instead of
counting coefficient vectors is an established ingredient here.

Source 17 already gives Fejer selection, an exact signed certificate,
weighted/vector-valued forms, finite-field rank gaps, and scalar-method
barriers. Source 29 further treats actual affine ranks of nongeneric
tuples. The present work does not claim to originate rank-sensitive
selection or a signed first-moment objective.

Source 40's audit records the exact small-torsion principal spectrum.
Thus the expression sum_{a|n} c_n^m itself is not claimed as new.

The moment implication G_16 >= gamma^7 b^15 from gamma b^3 respected
quadruples was already obtained in source 04 and used in source 17.
It is included as an interface consequence, not as a new discovery.

5. CONTRIBUTIONS ESTABLISHED IN THIS ARTICLE

A. A repeated-filter expectation bound with decaying errors:

   E G_m(B') >= P^k G_m(B),

   E U_m(B') <= Q^k U_m(B)
      + [tau_G(2D)*(R_m^k-P^k) + binom(m,2)*R_2^k] b^(m-2).

   Here tau_G(2D)=max_{1<=d<=2D}|G[d]|, P=sum c_n^m,
   Q=sum_{2|n}c_n^m, and R_s=int K^s. Arrays with some nonprincipal
   layer have total coefficient weight exactly R_m^k-P^k, so no factor
   k is lost. The repeated bad tuples are charged their decaying
   survival R_2^k instead of probability one.

B. Explicit domain and target torsion treatment. Nonprincipal coefficient
   vectors yield a nonzero minor of size at most 2D. Its multiplication
   kernel controls the number of completions. On every cyclic group,
   |G[d]|=gcd(N,d)<=2D in this range. The target discrepancy-order spectrum
   controls principal survival. The proof also supplies weighted torsion
   profiles, heterogeneous filters, and discrepancy-order refinements.

C. Certified retention exponent 54 at sixteen variables, using the fixed
   filter cos^50(pi t) and exact integer inequalities for binomial sums.
   The exact parameters give more precise nonintegral powers. The integer
   54 is a convenient certified choice, not a globally optimal constant.

D. Fixed-order asymptotic optimality of the principal-spectrum cost.
   For each fixed integer a>=2,

   I_{m,a} := inf_K [-log P_m(K) / log(P_m(K)/Q_{m,a}(K))]
       = m log m / (2 log a) + O_a(m log log m),

   with +infinity assigned to zero-gain filters. The class consists of
   finite real even trigonometric polynomials with 0<=K<=1, K(0)=1,
   and nonnegative Fourier coefficients. The lower bound does not assume
   decreasing coefficients; powered cosines attain the leading term.
   The same principal-spectrum cost bound covers any finite list of
   independent, heterogeneous scalar layers in this class, because their total
   cost-to-gain ratio is a weighted average of the individual ratios.
   Zero-gain layers add nonnegative cost and no gain. The quantity being
   optimized is exactly the ratio formed from the principal weights P_m
   and Q_{m,a}; the theorem does not bound every possible analysis of
   the resulting random subsets. Extra coefficient relations, particular
   input structure, and favorable outcomes may improve actual counts
   beyond a guarantee based only on those principal weights.

E. The arbitrary-abelian-target corollary uses only a finite quotient
   preserving the finitely many discrepancies relevant to B and m.
   The constants are unchanged. It does not assert a finite quotient
   embedding the whole target group.

6. RESOLVED PROJECT QUESTION AND REMAINING BOUNDARIES

Source 17's composite-modulus question is answered for a single balanced
additive relation and a common selected subset, with explicit torsion
and size dependence. This does not settle arbitrary linear systems or
the higher arrangements needed in the full Gowers induction.

The following distinctions must be retained during integration:

* No new global bound for progression-free sets, Ramsey numbers, or
  Szemeredi's theorem follows without further parameter propagation.
* The large explicit size hypothesis is part of the theorem, not an
  omitted technicality or an unconditional finite-size assertion.
* No optimality of exponent 54, the size threshold, or the best possible
  subset is asserted.
* The asymptotic method theorem has fixed a. It does not provide a
  growing-a uniform error estimate.
* The method lower bound concerns only the principal-spectrum cost
  I_{m,a}. It is not an impossibility theorem for obtaining stronger
  conclusions by a different analysis of the same selected sets.
  Adaptive restrictions, joint filters outside the independent scalar
  product class, signed Fourier filters, and structural extraction
  methods are also outside this principal-cost argument.
* The general finite-abelian-domain theorem retains a torsion factor.
  In families with large bounded-exponent torsion, its sufficient size
  hypothesis may be vacuous. A dimension-independent all-group theorem
  is not asserted.
* The finite calculations certify the stated constants and selected
  finite expectation examples. The infinite families are justified by
  the written proofs, not by exhaustive finite testing.
* The existing formal baseline is not a Lean proof of this strengthening.
  The accompanying article provides proposed integration interfaces;
  no new Lean theorem is claimed to have been compiled or kernel-checked.
* No first-in-the-literature priority claim or resolution of a major
  longstanding open problem is made. The resolved local project question
  and the method extremal problem are stated precisely.

This package supplies a self-contained mathematical contribution and
reproducible certificates. Independent referee review and any later
formalization should preserve the domains, tuple conventions, thresholds,
and method-class qualifications recorded above.
