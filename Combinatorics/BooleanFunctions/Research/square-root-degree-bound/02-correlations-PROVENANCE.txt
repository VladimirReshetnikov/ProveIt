PRIOR WORK REQUIRING EXPLICIT CREDIT

CHHL (2018 conference / 2019 Theory of Computing), Claim 3.3, already gives
the maximal-retention restriction. Blasiok et al. (2021, revised 2024),
Claim 33, gives its explicit derivative identity. The normalized/Jensen
3^(ell/2) comparison in this article follows immediately from that identity;
it is sharper than the 4^ell statement in Bansal--Sinha Theorem 3.4 but is
not claimed as a new restriction method or a new best-known bound.

Keller and O'Donnell--Tan provide related bias-reduction and composition
frameworks. Gilmer--Saks--Srinivasan provide prior composition-limit and
critical-exponent methodology for other Boolean complexity measures.

Fejer--Riesz, Bernstein, Riesz interpolation, Bernstein--Szego, and
Berry--Esseen are classical analytic inputs. The scalar biased singleton
bound is explicitly identified as a classical consequence, independently
of the observation formulation. Complete bibliography and primary links
are in article.tex and article.pdf.

PROPOSED EXTENSIONS

The manuscript supplies complete written proofs of the fixed-count moment
invariant, the exact binomial witness, the singleton-degree frontier and
its initial-slope characterization, explicit transfer refinements and
conjunction constant asymptotics, finite-observation ceilings and their
strictness/arithmetic gap, and the exact uniform degree-two extremum.
No claim of exhaustive historical priority is made. The source Gaussian
reporting mechanism is reconstructed with direct attribution.

The Python programs certify their stated finite computations. They do not
formalize the infinite analytic arguments or certify novelty. No repository
source code or third-party manuscript text is bundled here.
