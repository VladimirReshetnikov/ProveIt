## Corrections to new queue summaries

The [patch](canonical_j3_0ac86bb58_queue_guards.patch) affects only the newly written article/README wording. It was applied and verified in a temporary copy. The original source theorem bodies and all code remain unchanged.

1. **Initial resource and drift guards are essential.** At article line 10461 and the README source-20 summary near line 394, indefinite repetition is described as the conjugacy equation alone. The actual theorem at article lines 29414 onward requires `a>0`, `L>=R`, `b>=a` and `U^(b/d) q = q V^(a/d)`, where `d=gcd(a,b)`. The maximum-copy formula divides by `a`, so it also requires the readful case. A closed single action reading `a` and writing `a`, from the empty queue, satisfies the conjugacy equation but cannot perform its first read: `L=0<R=1`. Separately, the action reading `aa` and writing `a`, from `q=aa`, satisfies conjugacy and the initial resource guard but fails after one copy because `b<a`. The checker implements these two literal word actions independently. For `a=0`, every action is append-only; a closed macro repeats from every queue and is handled separately. The patch restores all these qualifications.

2. **Existential power elimination and single-fold preservation differ.** The new note at article line 29800 equates elimination “at the level of existence” with Part IX's exponentiation boundary. The cited proposition at lines 9017–9031 concerns a fixed-dimensional *single-fold* ordinary representation and its implication for single-fold MRDP. Ordinary existential elimination follows MRDP and makes no such fiber claim. The adjacent unchanged source text already states this distinction correctly. The patch makes the editorial citation say it too.

3. **The storage question is answered in part.** The question at article lines 10687–10694 asks for arbitrary powers satisfying the fixed-size quadratic unique-summary contract stated at lines 8778–8798. The queue construction provides concatenation and externally fixed powers; variable powers retain `Pow` predicates. Four new article summaries and two README summaries are therefore changed from “answered” to “answered in part.” Their detailed qualifications already acknowledge the remaining problem; this patch aligns the short summaries with them.

## Scope, counts and transfer of earlier reductions

Source 19 uses natural witnesses on finite loopless undirected multigraphs with threshold at least one; sink connectivity is not assumed except where it guarantees termination. Its lattice class is a stable periodic background plus finitely many positive additions. The copied signed false root and directed counterexample preserve these boundaries. The source's `47` witnesses in dimension three are finite-deviation **fields**, not 47 scalar integers. Their unique finite codes have unbounded support/length, and neither a paid universal loader nor uniqueness-preserving scalar compression is supplied. Imported Cairns/MRDP dependencies remain explicit.

The finite sandpile compiler has `11n+12E` natural coordinates and `12n+16E` quadratic residuals; their SOS has exact degree four. Source 16 instead supplies single cubic polynomials with `13n+12E` baseline or `10n+6E` compact coordinates. The transferred source-19 projection gives `8n+12E` coordinates and `9n+16E` residuals, or 44 fields and 57 local residuals in dimension three. Its new coordinate count is below the compact cubic's exactly when `E<n/3`. The quoted numerical examples are correct: at `(n,E)=(2,1)`, 34→28 against 38 and 26; at `(10,15)`, 290→260 against 310 and 190. These are different degree/form tradeoffs, not competing complete operation counts.

The sandpile projection uses `u=z+alpha`, `k=e+beta`, `r=z+e+beta`. Its off-zero correction is the prior review's restoration-graph identity, adding `sum[((1-z)alpha)^2+((1-e)beta)^2]`; the paragraph's substitution context matters. It is a bijection of natural zero sets, not an equality of the old and projected polynomials on the same unrestricted coordinates. No operation saving is inferred from deleting rows or fields.

Source 20 compiles a supplied acyclic, control-compatible whole trace grammar, not merely a fixed program and a freely quantified unbounded execution. Its finite counts are `6g+3c+1` coordinates and `6l+9c+3` quadratic residuals. For the doubling example `l=1,c=16,g=17`, these are 151 and 153. The earlier exact restoration projection gives `6c+1` coordinates and `6c+3` residuals, here 97 and 99. Its recursive resource substitution makes the complete SOS equal on the restoration graph for all integer assignments; natural nonnegativity of restored cancellation values is proved at natural zeros. Affine expansion can enlarge sparse arithmetic, so no fully paid gate saving follows from these counts.

For the supplied infinite-loop compiler, `6g+3c+2H+1` coordinates and `6l+9c+2H+3` residuals apply in the theorem's nondegenerate case `0<a<=b`. `H` counts two fixed binary concatenation chains. The source theorem explicitly returns constant zero in zero variables when `a=0`, and constant one when `a>0,b<a`. The imported theorem and proof retain these cases verbatim. Fixed grammar exponents and variable power predicates remain separate. An action-periodic infinite run need not revisit a queue configuration; periodic lassos do not exhaust universal nontermination. There is no ordinary fixed-arity universal equation or improvement of the 87-operation benchmark here.
