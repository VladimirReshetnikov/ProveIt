## 2. Hand challenge of Proposition 18.2

Write `mu=u(H_u−1)/2`, `rho=1/(4u)`, and `q_j=[x^j]Q_u(x)`. The inherited single-spine convolution is an exact nonnegative-coefficient lower bound. Replacing `rho^j` by `(theta*rho)^j` for `0<theta<=1` preserves its direction and gives `R >= Q_u(theta*rho) Pr(J_theta<=b)`. Each factor has negative-binomial shape one half and parameter `theta*m/u<1`. Its mean and variance increase with that parameter; hence the inherited variance bound remains at most `u²`. This is a coefficient/probability argument on paper, not an evaluation of a saved recurrence or table.

The harmonic threshold exists and the stated bound `kappa_K <= exp(2K+3)` is valid. One can avoid any precision assumption about Euler's constant: convexity of `1/x` gives `H_n >= log n + 1/2 + 1/(2n)`, and the same ceiling argument printed at article 1380 follows. The illustrative minima also check by hand: `H_10=7381/2520<3<H_11=83711/27720`. For the second example, convexity gives `H_82 <= H_10+log(55/7)` and `H_83 >= H_10+log(83/10)+(1/83−1/10)/2`. The elementary bounds `0.69313<log 2<0.69315`, obtained from four terms and a geometric tail of `2 sum_{j>=0} (1/3)^(2j+1)/(2j+1)`, together with `log(1−x)<−x` and `log(1+x)>=x−x²/2`, imply `H_82<5<H_83`. Thus the examples 11 and 83 do not require replaying a scientific checker.

For `u<4*kappa` the proposed right side is negative, and `R>=1` suffices. Otherwise `theta=1−kappa/u` lies in `[3/4,1)`. The logarithmic normalization loss is at most `(kappa/2)H_u`. The crucial mean shift has the exact summand `kappa*(u−k)/(k*(k+kappa*(u−k)/u))`; replacing its second denominator factor by the larger `k+kappa` gives a lower bound, because all numerators are nonnegative. Splitting that bound into `u*(1/k−1/(k+kappa))−kappa/(k+kappa)` yields the printed telescoping estimate. With `kappa/u<=1/4`, the two discarded terms total at most `1/4+(log 5)/4<1`. Thus `mu−E J_theta >= (u/2)(H_kappa−1)` uniformly, including `u=4*kappa`.

The hypothesis `b>=mu−Ku` and `H_kappa>=2K+3` now imply `b−E J_theta>=u`. Cantelli bounds the upper tail by one half, including the strict event `J_theta>b`. The inherited elementary factorial estimate and `H_u<=1+log u` give

`log R >= u/2−1/2−(log u)/4−(kappa/2)(1+log u)−log 2 >= u/2−2*kappa*(1+log u)`.

The final weakening is uniform for `kappa>=1` and `u>=1`; for instance its constant slack is at least `3/2−1/2−log 2>0`. Empty negative-binomial sums at `u=1` fall in the trivial branch.

Consequence (1) legitimately chooses a different `K=max(0,(H_u−1)/2−alpha)` at each pair, because the preceding result is uniform in K. Its loss divided by u is bounded by `2e³(1+log u)(1/u+exp(−2alpha))`, which tends to zero under `alpha−(log log u)/2 -> infinity`. The upper half follows from the inherited bound `E/u=3*2^(1/3)*(1+alpha)^(1/3)*exp(−alpha/3)` and the separately required condition `log(b+1)=o(u)`. The examples in the statement retain that sequence condition; this review does not discard it when summarizing the result.

For consequence (2), use Part I's saddle `u_*=N/t`, not Part II's different saddle. Throughout `u=u_*(1+epsilon)`, `|epsilon|<1/log N`, the printed estimates are uniform. The lower bound for `alpha−(H_u−1)/2` tends to `log 2−1>-1` for r=2 and to infinity for r=1. Fixed `K=1` therefore applies throughout the window for large N, yielding `−O(log u)` below. The same lower bound on alpha gives `exp(−alpha/3)=O(u^(−1/(3r)))`, while the upper bound `alpha=O(log u)` controls the remaining factor. Together with `b<N` and `log N=O(log u)`, this proves the stated upper error `O(u^(1−1/(3r))*(log u)^(1/3))`, with constants depending only on r. Admissibility and integer endpoints introduce no missing branch. This closes the previous window gap but does not replace the global localization theorem away from this window.
