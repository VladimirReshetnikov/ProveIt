## Foundations: what the representation actually costs

Archive 1 reconstructs the advertised surreal/ZFC interpretation in an ordered-field language enlarged by birthday, and its omnific version adds the binary primitive `h(a,b)=birthday(a/b)` with a zero-denominator convention. Its explicit fraction formulas are ordinary algebraic identities, but their entries range over omnific integers, not ordinary integers. The whole-support monomial shift used to clear denominators requires an ordinal bound for every exponent in a set-sized surreal support. It is not a bounded integer operation on a finite numerator/denominator encoding.

The selected prefix proof is coherent: a first disagreement supplies a shorter common prefix distinguishing the two numbers. But the formula quantifies over all earlier-birthday numbers. Likewise, the graph validity formula tests every coded subset for well-foundedness and root generation. Calling those formulas finite-dimensional first-order formulas does not replace their quantifiers by a fixed number of existential integer witnesses or paid ring gates.

Archive 2 offers a more native primitive language: ordered omnific arithmetic with strict birthday precedence. Its selected block argument stores arbitrary ordinal-indexed bits at `omega * beta`, using natural ordinal multiplication. It explicitly fills the gaps caused by failure of continuity at limits; disjoint blocks with limit endpoints suffice. Conditional on the cited omnific sign/initiality facts, the displayed prefix and block arguments check out. Those external sign facts were not independently checked here.

Even a finite bit set in that particular storage construction receives an ordinal-length word: for a positive finite length `n`, the prescribed birthday is `omega*(n+1)`, rather than a finite binary word. Thus interpreting its block operations as a handful of ordinary integer multiplications would change the domain. A usable computational bridge would need a finite integer representation of the restricted objects, a paid graph for birthday/sign reading on that representation, and a replacement for the universal subset tests with a proved positive-integer converse. None is supplied in the read spans. This identifies missing obligations; it is not an impossibility theorem for every possible finite restriction.

There is a concrete cross-manuscript lead worth preserving. Archive 1 expressly leaves reduction of quotient birthday to unary restricted birthday open. Archive 2 claims fraction-birthday comparison definable from native birthday precedence, via its graph interpretation and numerical round trip. Birthday and birthday precedence are interdefinable on the intended omnific carrier by the displayed greatest-element-of-a-birthday-fiber definition. If the full second construction is audited, it appears to answer the first question at the level of first-order definability. This intake has not read the complete interpretation and round-trip proofs, so it does not certify that closure. The second text itself stresses that this is a separate effective translation for each formula, not a universal truth evaluator, and leaves formula/proof-length costs open. No cheap evaluator follows from the claimed definability result.

Both packages separate set theories from class theories. Archive 1's read reflection argument keeps its essential premises: a uniform finite-dimensional first-order interpretation in ZFC, a finitely axiomatized target, and `Con(ZFC)` for the negative conclusion. Reflection yields a set model of the finite target; applying this to the stated NBG conventions would force ZFC to prove its own consistency. The argument is consistent with those explicit imported metatheoretic premises. The more general model-cardinality and two-sorted class-lift claims in archive 2 remain source claims here, not newly checked theorems. A larger universe, class sort, birthday predicate or large-cardinal axiom cannot be counted as a free ordinary-integer oracle.

## Transseries: finite truncation versus a compiler

The four new transseries guides retain their distinctions between formal expansion, selected analytic realization, summation direction, logarithm lift and inverse branch. None of the read interfaces asserts unrestricted inversion for every transseries. Their exact finite checks and high-precision numerical diagnostics are historical source claims; no such test was rerun and they are not interval proofs.

Archive 3 advertises a correction to the fixed-argument cyclotomic conjecture's unrestricted directional-equality clause, while retaining Gevrey/continuation/positive-ray statements and a separate uniform-parameter problem. Its three-eta/four-Euler-factor minimality claims count analytic product factors under specified balance conditions. They are not arithmetic gate counts for evaluating those infinite products. The archive's guide also explicitly preserves logarithmic winding information in complex inversion.

Archive 4 supplies a useful, directly checked finite-support boundary. For finitely many actions, separation of their convex hull from zero gives `|A(n)| >= c*|n|`. Conversely, flooring a nonnegative real zero combination produces infinitely many multiindices with bounded image. The displayed proof therefore distinguishes properness from merely finite fibers. Finite fibers alone do not give a finite action cutoff: actions `1` and `-sqrt(2)` illustrate the distinction. With countably many generators, even a common positive directional margin leaves infinitely many generators below one cutoff unless a separate local-finiteness condition is imposed.

As an elementary consequence, for a fixed finite alphabet of size `s` with an effective positive separation bound `c`, an action-modulus budget `R` restricts candidates to `|n| <= floor(R/c)`, hence at most `(floor(R/c)+1)^s` tuples. This is a finite enumeration bound, not a constant-cost algorithm or fixed-arity polynomial. For arbitrary complex action inputs, obtaining certified separation and deciding equalities additionally needs a specified effective representation. The source's continuation-cost example likewise bounds integer pairs by `|m|+|n|/alpha <= L` for `alpha>0` irrational, and correctly delays zero until a nonempty cancellation costs `2*min(1,1/alpha)`. It describes possible continued Borel locations, not a free singularity or halting oracle.

Archives 5 and 6 advertise exact Mellin–Borel completion and critical inverse charts. They distinguish an original function from lateral or median sums, preserve independent flat completion data, and exclude discriminant collisions from simple-root charts before supplying separate moving-fold constructions. The stated action conversion `A/r` or numerical action `24` is an analytic scale, not a paid-operation reduction. The claimed resolutions of specific Fantini–Rella conjectures, as well as archive 3's claimed counterexample to a repository conjecture, are preserved with their stated versions and scope. Their full proofs and the external papers were not read, so this intake neither accepts nor refutes those resolution claims.
