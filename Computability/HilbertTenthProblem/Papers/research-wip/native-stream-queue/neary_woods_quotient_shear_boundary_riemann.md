# The positive quotient shift blocks a proposed 241-operation U9 shear

The complete 242-operation U9 source does not acquire an established
241-operation successor by simply unshifting its recoder quotient.
The proposed affine change removes one addition algebraically, but it
cannot preserve the positive parent fiber at ordinary input x=1.
Its putative inverse also fails positivity on some raw positive tuples.
This is a boundary for one specific source edit, not an arithmetic lower
bound or a theorem that no other 241-operation construction exists.

## 1. The actual private cone and proposed saving

In each complete 242 source, write

    h=quotient_hat, g=fusion_output_slack, m=modulus=Q-1.

The exact source definitions are

    K=h+g,
    factored_quotient_minus_one=h-1,
    factored_unhat_product=m*(h-1),
    factored_unhat=m*(h-1)+z.                                 (1)

The complete direct consumer lists are:

| Name | All consumers |
| --- | --- |
| `quotient_hat` | `K`, `factored_quotient_minus_one` |
| `fusion_output_slack` | `K` |
| `factored_quotient_minus_one` | `factored_unhat_product` |
| `factored_unhat_product` | `factored_unhat` |
| `factored_unhat` | `joint_Z_difference` |
| `K` | `and__scaled_B`, `mask_product` |

The source also computes Q=m+1 literally. The frozen recoder output
called Ahat-1 in the earlier interface is the current `factored_unhat`.
No AND typing is required to establish these source identities.

The tentative 241 suggestion was to supply positive a,b in place of
h,g and set the old coordinates to

    h_old=a+1,       g_old=b-1.                              (2)

Then K_old=a+b and m*(h_old-1)+z=m*a+z. Thus one could retain the
K addition and output multiplication/sum while deleting the private
h-1 subtraction. Both exits in the table would agree under (2), so the
whole-output polynomial identity would hold over every commutative ring.
The prospective ledger would be 241=129M+112A, with 43 supplied ports
still designated positive witnesses. No such source is accepted or
emitted as a universal construction here.

This algebraic identity alone does not establish the required positive
domain or a complete zero-set map.

## 2. Every positive parent zero at x=1 has h=1

The accepted complete U9 theorem recovers its exact low recoder. The
frozen dyadic-duration recoder's Sections 4--5 give, writing D for its
fixed bit-dilation width and n for its decoded duration,

    Q=2^(D*n),
    x=sum_(j<n) x_j*2^j,
    A=sum_(j<n) x_j*2^(D*(n+1)*j),
    z=sum_(j<n) x_j*2^(D*j),
    A=(Q-1)*(h-1)+z.                                        (3)

This is the retained ordinary-input decoder, not an assumption that an
arbitrary supplied tuple already represents bits. The accepted U9 source
retains that contract, and (1) binds its actual quotient port to (3).

At x=1 only x_0 is one, so A=z=1 for every permitted duration. Since
Q>1, the last equation in (3) forces h=1. This applies to every full
positive parent zero at x=1 on a valid U9 program slice, independently
of the padded duration or the choice of native auxiliaries.

But (2) with a>0 always has h_old>=2. Therefore no positive new tuple
can map by (2) to any positive parent zero in the x=1 fiber. Equivalently,
restoring a parent zero with h=1 requires a=0, outside the proposed
strictly positive domain. Allowing arbitrarily long leading-zero
padding does not change this conclusion.

This is a quantified parent-fiber obstruction. No numerical program
slice accepting 1 or enormous native witness has been materialized here.
The inherited universal theorem guarantees accepting slices for sets
containing 1, but this note does not supply their concrete coefficients.
On any such slice the proposed positive inverse cannot establish the
claimed full parent-zero correspondence.

## 3. Retained failures and precise open scope

**Remark 1 (the advertised positive-zero 241 shear fails).** The proposal
that (2) gives a complete positive-coordinate successor after deleting
the subtraction is false. Equations (2)--(3) show that every positive
parent zero at x=1 is outside its image. This does not assert that the
unreviewed substituted polynomial has no positive zeros at x=1: zeros
whose restored parent coordinates leave the positive domain could be
spurious, and are not classified by the parent theorem.

**Remark 2 (the inverse is not positive on every positive tuple).** At
b=1, formula (2) gives g_old=0. Thus the simpler assertion that the
complete positive parent theorem applies automatically to every new
positive zero is unsupported without a separate zero-dependent guard.
The raw counterexample refutes unconditional positivity; it does not
claim an actual child compiler zero. Even if a new argument forced
b>=2 at every child zero, Remark 1 would still obstruct completeness
through (2) for a parent language accepting input 1.

**Open question 1.** Can a different coordinate map, a paid treatment of
the zero unshifted quotient, or a separate complete-source identity
reduce the 242-operation bound? The present search proposed (2) from
the two private quotient consumers, but supplies none of those missing
constructions. Its signed all-ring identity is not a substitute for
positive-zero soundness and completeness. No blanket obstruction to
witness reparametrization is claimed.

The source scan also found no identical operation records in either
242 parent, allowing operand exchange for addition and multiplication.
That literal structural fact is not an exhaustive identity search and
provides no minimum-operation certificate. The authoritative complete
bound remains 242=129M+113A, with 43 positive witnesses and degree at
most 936.
