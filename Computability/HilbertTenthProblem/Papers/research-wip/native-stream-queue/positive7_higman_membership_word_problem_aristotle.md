## 1. Exact finite construction and query words

Suppose K=<X|R> contains an embedded free group F=<a,b,c>, and a finite
list L=(ell_1,...,ell_l) satisfies

    F intersect H = <h_n:n in U>,  H=<L> in K,
    alpha=a^(b^23), beta=b^c, h_n=alpha^(beta^n).       (1)

Here U is the already fixed set of integers represented by the accepted
tuples (23,n,0,...). The actual U contains only positive integers. All
conjugation is right conjugation x^y=y^-1*x*y. Adjoin a fresh t and set

    Q=<X,t | R, t^-1*ell_i*t*ell_i^-1=1 (1<=i<=l)>.
                                                               (2)

This is the HNN extension of K by the identity isomorphism on H. Thus K
embeds in Q by the HNN normal-form theorem. It is a finite presentation
because L is finite; no presentation of H, membership algorithm for H,
or word problem for K is used in constructing (2).

For each ordinary integer n define the finite query word

    W_n=[h_n,t]=h_n^-1*t^-1*h_n*t.                    (3)

Then the exact equivalence is

    W_n=1 in Q  if and only if  n belongs to U.        (4)

## 2. Both implications, with the subgroup boundary explicit

First, for every h in K the defining HNN extension gives

    h^-1*t^-1*h*t=1 in Q  iff  h belongs to H.         (5)

The forward direction of H-membership implies commutation because t fixes
each generator of H and hence every element of H. Conversely, if h is
outside H, the word h^-1*t^-1*h*t contains no Britton pinch: the only
possible pinch uses the middle h, which is not in the associated subgroup.
It is therefore nontrivial in Q. This proves necessity, including arbitrary
relations inside H. It does not assume that t commutes only with H inside
all of Q; the statement is exactly about the embedded base K.

Next the assignment a->a^(b^23),b->b^c,c->c is a free-group automorphism,
as established in the separate transducer note. Therefore alpha,beta freely
generate a rank-two subgroup of F. The normal closure of alpha in that
free group has free basis {alpha^(beta^n):n in Z}. Thus the h_n are distinct
free-basis elements and

    h_n belongs to <h_j:j in U> iff n belongs to U.   (6)

This is free-group reduced-word membership in a subgroup generated by a
subset of a basis. It is not a claim that arbitrary membership in H is
decidable. Since h_n lies in F, premise (1) and (6) give h_n in H exactly
when n in U. Combining this with (5) proves (4). Negative n and n=0 cause
no exception to the argument; the actual U excludes them as appropriate.
