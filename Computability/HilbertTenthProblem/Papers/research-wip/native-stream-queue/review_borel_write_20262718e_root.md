## Corrections made

1. **The trivial exponent group was omitted from two summaries.** The guide's Section 270 summary and the Batch 96 article commentary after Question 237.1 called the support-code domain coanalytic-complete in every case other than a nonzero cyclic subgroup of R. At `Gamma={0}` every support is a subset of a singleton, so the code domain is the whole ambient coefficient space. Every continuous preimage of that space is again the whole domain, excluding the advertised completeness. Both summaries now state the three cases explicitly. The guide's unbound `L` is replaced by `Gamma`. Proposition 270.1 already had the correct zero case; the separate strong-family predicate is F-sigma-complete there because it requires eventual zero coefficients. Numbered Review remark 1 retains the original claim and counterexample in the article and guide.

2. **A dense-group citation lacked a hypothesis.** The commentary after Question 28.4 used the dyadic-root corollary to cover every countable dense exponent group. Density does not supply a nonzero element divisible by every power of two: in `Z+sqrt(2)Z`, uniqueness of rational coefficients would force both integer coefficients to be divisible by every power of two, hence zero. The conclusion remains supported by the group classification, now cited directly. Article Review remark 2 retains the original citation, its missing hypothesis and the counterexample. No classification theorem was changed.

3. **The earlier review coverage was overstated.** “All four archives read in full” is replaced by complete manuscript reads and authentication of archive members. All four intake reports explicitly excluded PDF reading; the fourth also recorded selected human-read spans of its saved JSON despite full parsing. Article and guide Review remark 3 retain the original wording and these concrete limits. The Section 270 attribution is made equally precise. The historical flow-intake assessment is qualified by the publication's already retained counterexample to its finite-sampling remark: in finite rational rank every strict derivation integrates, so the claim that finite sampling can never certify integration needs the infinite-rank hypothesis. The existing Proposition 285.4 supplies that repair. Its proof and the original numbered remark are preserved.

None of these findings is converted into an unsupported open question. Previously open effective recognition, certified arithmetic and higher-rank questions stay open at their stated scope.

The trivial-group counterexample and the distinction between single-series and strong-family validity are direct. The standard-Borel equivalence is correctly stated for inherited Borel structures: a Borel subset of a Polish space is standard Borel, and a standard relative Borel structure makes the inclusion a Borel injection, so Lusin–Souslin forces the subset to be Borel. The countable subgroup-of-R specialization separates zero, nonzero cyclic and noncyclic groups. This uses the cited descriptive-set-theoretic foundations, not a new proof of them.

The flow finding is likewise a genuine qualification of earlier coverage. A nonzero countable rational-rank-one group in the divisible setting has one basis value for a strict logarithmic derivation. A nonzero value has positive valuation, giving a common positive gain; the zero value gives the zero derivation. Thus the finite-rank theorem rules out the proposed nonintegrable counterexample under that promise. The independent flow review separately challenges the published infinite-rank repair, basis-time Borel interface and common-gain BCH argument. Its exact read spans and inherited lemmas remain explicit.

The effective barriers concern their particular representations. Countable support/incidence certificates are infinite sequences. A computable legal array can have a noncomputable sum, and arbitrary coefficient-program indices do not give a finite existential integer certificate for raw legality. Those observations do not forbid finite-support computations with explicit support bounds. Conversely, Borel classification, real coefficients, basis choices and finite-window existence do not furnish paid ordinary-integer operations.
