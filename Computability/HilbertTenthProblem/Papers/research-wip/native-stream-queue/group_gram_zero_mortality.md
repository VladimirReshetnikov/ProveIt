# Seven-dimensional scalar zero and mortality from the universal group route

There is a fixed seven-dimensional integer matrix alphabet and a positive
quartic input column for which one scalar-zero test represents every
recursively enumerable set, using only two fixed program numerals in the
ordinary input loader. The column costs **12=6M+6A**. A rank-one adapter
turns the same test into unrestricted matrix mortality in dimension seven;
its full input matrix costs **15=9M+6A**, or **13=5M+8A** after a fixed
change of basis making that input matrix nonnegative. Exactly one
mortality generator depends on the ordinary input.

This is an endpoint alternative, not a smaller complete universal
Diophantine equation. The selected matrix word remains unbounded. A fully
paid positive certificate for a **fixed** signed-shear word is given below,
but its size grows with that word. No uniform word, regular controller,
history packing, or geometry predicate is included in these counts.
The [checker](group_gram_zero_mortality.py) and
[receipt](group_gram_zero_mortality.json) use exact integer arithmetic.

## 1. What the primary mortality results actually supply

[Cassaigne, Halava, Harju and Nicolas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.0644)
define mortality and zero-corner problems with the finite matrix set as
the instance. Their dimension/alphabet bounds do not by themselves supply
a fixed alphabet with an ordinary numerical input loader. Their scalar
zero-to-corner reduction also conjugates the matrices using the supplied
boundary vectors, so its fixed-alphabet status must be checked separately.

[Bell's thesis, Theorem 4.13](https://techreports.csc.liv.ac.uk/tr2008/ulcs-08-005.pdf)
does construct a fixed semigroup with undecidable membership, removing
the distinguished input tile from an encoded universal machine. Its
remaining target is the inverse matrix encoding of the input word. This
does not supply the arithmetic cost of loading an ordinary integer.

[Honkala, Sections 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00688) proves a stronger
all-r.e.-sets contract using two fixed upper-triangular nonnegative integer
matrices. The acceptance relation is nonannihilating equality, and the
construction explicitly starts from a universal Diophantine polynomial.
It is not a two-generator mortality theorem or an independent arithmetic
improvement. Dropping nonannihilation would admit zero-output witnesses.

The construction below instead starts from the already established
[fixed universal group alphabet](group_commutator_universal_substrate.md),
whose ordinary input interface has been proved separately. It does not
infer universality merely from undecidability.

## 2. Exact seven-dimensional scalar-zero theorem

Let Γ be any torsion-free subgroup of SL(2,Z), and let K be a subgroup of
Γ×Γ generated by a fixed finite alphabet, including generator inverses.
Suppose a target L belongs to Γ. For

    A = [p q; s t]

define the integral three-dimensional representation

    S(A) = [ p²       −2pq       q²   ]
           [ −ps      pt+qs      −qt  ]
           [ s²       −2st       t²   ].

The coordinates (a,b,c) represent the symmetric matrix
G=[a,−b;−b,c]. Direct multiplication gives

    S(A)(a,b,c)^T  ↔  A G A^T,
    S(AB)=S(A)S(B).

Consequently S(A) is invertible over Z, with inverse S(A^−1). Set

    ρ(A,B)=diag(S(A),S(B),1),
    u=(1,0,1,1,0,1,−4).

If (a,b,c) represents G=L^−1 L^−T, put

    v_L=(a,b,c,a,b,c,1)^T.

Then, for every (A,B) in K,

    u ρ(A,B) v_L
      = ||A L^−1||_F² + ||B L^−1||_F² − 4 ≥ 0,

and equality holds **if and only if A=B=L**.

Proof: an integral determinant-one 2×2 matrix has squared Frobenius norm
at least two. Equality means exactly two entries are ±1 and the others
are zero, so the matrix is one of

    I, −I, J, −J,       J=[0,−1;1,0].

All three nonidentity possibilities have finite order. Both A L^−1 and
B L^−1 belong to Γ, which is torsion-free. Each norm is at least two;
their sum equals four exactly when both matrices are I. This proves
both directions without a bound on the chosen word or its entries.

The torsion hypothesis cannot be discarded. For any L, the pair (J L,L)
passes this scalar-zero test while its first block is different from L.
Thus unrestricted physical shear words require the original fixed
macro-language control. The test is not equality on all of SL(2,Z)².

## 3. Ordinary positive input, fixed program numerals, and a paid loader

The established universal group construction provides one fixed K_U
inside a fixed free subgroup Γ×Γ. For each r.e. set S there are positive
fixed integers α,β, divisible by twelve, such that for every x>0,

    x∈S  iff  (L_r,L_r)∈K_U,
    r=αx+β,
    L_r=[1+r,1; −r²,1−r].

For example, the program-index interface there has
α=12·2^(p+1), β=12·2^p. The factors depending only on p are fixed
numerals, not a run-time exponentiation. The condition L_r∈Γ is inherited
from that construction. For arbitrary positive α,β the arithmetic below
still holds, but this particular group-membership universality claim
requires the established compatible target family.

The inverse and its Gram matrix are

    L_r^−1=[1−r,−1; r²,1+r],
    a=(r−1)²+1,
    b=(r−1)r²+(r+1),
    c=r⁴+(r+1)².

For r≥1, all a,b,c are strictly positive and ac−b²=1. In particular all
seven coordinates of v(x)=(a,b,c,a,b,c,1)^T are positive, even though
the represented Gram matrix has a negative off-diagonal entry.

Here is the complete input schedule. Copying outputs costs nothing;
every displayed multiplication, including a fixed-numeral product, is paid.

| Computation | M | A |
|---|---:|---:|
| r=αx+β | 1 | 1 |
| h=r−1, t=r+1, z=r² | 1 | 2 |
| a=h²+1 | 1 | 1 |
| b=hz+t | 1 | 1 |
| c=z²+t² | 2 | 1 |
| Total | 6 | 6 |

The fixed seven-dimensional alphabet is the image under ρ of the finite
signed generator alphabet for K_U. It does not depend on S or x.
Combining the previous two sections proves the exact universal contract

    x∈S  iff  ∃ word w over that fixed alphabet: u ρ(w) v(x)=0.

The empty word is permitted. In the present positive target family it
does not accept, since L_r is not I or an integral rotation. The theorem
does not need to impose nonemptiness separately.

This is a scalar-zero interface with varying input column. It must not
be silently relabeled fixed-corner reachability with all boundary
matrices fixed. Converting both boundary vectors to canonical basis
vectors by an input-dependent change of basis can change every generator.

## 4. Rank-one mortality without a regular boundary restriction

More generally, let F be a fixed finite alphabet of invertible d×d
matrices, let u be a nonzero row and v a nonzero column, and put R=vu.
Then

    0 belongs to the semigroup generated by F∪{R}
      iff ∃ A∈F*: uAv=0.

The sentinel R may occur any number of times; no once-only or ordered
boundary convention is required. To prove the converse, a word with no
R is invertible. A word with k≥1 copies of R factors as

    A_0 R A_1 R ... R A_k
      = (A_0 v) [product_(i=1)^(k−1) (u A_i v)] (u A_k),

where every A_i is a possibly empty word over F. Its two outer vectors
are nonzero because A_0 and A_k are invertible. The product is zero
exactly when an internal scalar is zero. In the other direction uAv=0
gives RAR=0. This also covers A=I.

Apply the lemma with d=7 and the preceding u,v(x). Thus **one fixed
seven-dimensional alphabet, together with one input-dependent rank-one
generator R_x**, gives a universal mortality interface. The alphabet's
cardinality is the inherited signed group alphabet size plus one; no
small universal numerical alphabet is claimed or transcribed here.

The nonzero columns of R_x are four copies of v(x) and one copy of
−4v(x). Their only new nonconstant entries are −4a,−4b,−4c. Charging
these three products gives a full **15=9M+6A** matrix loader.
Signed fixed integer numerals are allowed, as elsewhere in this ledger.
Negative entries are computed matrix entries, not negative existential
witnesses. There is no extra choice, sign, or rank witness in this adapter.

There is a preferable form with only nonnegative entries in the input
matrix. Let P be the identity matrix with its first row replaced by u.
Since u_1=1, elementary row additions construct P and det(P)=1. Its
inverse has first row 2e_1^T−u and all other rows unchanged. In particular
uP^−1=e_1^T. Conjugate every fixed generator once, replacing F by PFP^−1.
The input sentinel becomes

    P R_x P^−1 = (Pv(x)) e_1^T,
    Pv(x)=(2a+2c−4,b,c,a,b,c,1)^T.

Only its first column is nonzero. Its first entry is
2(a+c)−4=2(r²+1)²>0, so the whole column is strictly positive. Sharing
that square with c gives this complete **13=5M+8A** loader:

| Computation | M | A |
|---|---:|---:|
| r=αx+β | 1 | 1 |
| h=r−1, z=r², u=z+1 | 1 | 2 |
| a=h²+1 | 1 | 1 |
| b=hu+2 | 1 | 1 |
| v=u², c=v−a+2 | 1 | 2 |
| first=v+v | 0 | 1 |
| Total | 5 | 8 |

Indeed hu+2=(r−1)r²+r+1 and v−a+2=r⁴+(r+1)², so these are exactly
the earlier b,c. The resulting column is (first,b,c,a,b,c,1)^T.
There is no negative fixed numeral in this loader. The original
twelve-operation scalar loader is retained for the fixed-word schedule
below; the thirteen-operation schedule computes the conjugated sentinel
directly and does not append a free coordinate to that earlier DAG.
Conjugation preserves all products and zero matrices, so the sentinel
factorization and both directions of the mortality equivalence are
unchanged. The conjugated fixed generators can still have negative entries.

Those negative fixed entries are essential to this particular interface.
For nonnegative matrices, the support of a product is its Boolean matrix
product: no cancellation is possible. Thus mortality depends only on the
generator zero patterns, and is decidable by closing a finite set of at
most 2^(d²) Boolean matrices under multiplication. The nonnegative
sentinel above has the same zero pattern for every positive x. If all
the other fixed generators were nonnegative too, mortality would be
independent of x. This is a structural obstruction to that simplification,
not an obstruction to signed matrix mortality.

## 5. Three-addition Gram steps and a fully positive fixed-word certificate

A signed upper unit shear E_ε=[1,ε;0,1], ε∈{−1,1}, acts by

    b'=b−εc,       a'=a−εb−εb',       c'=c.

A signed lower unit shear acts by

    b'=b−εa,       c'=c−εb−εb',       a'=a.

Each is **three additions/subtractions**, with no constant product.
The formula reuses b' to avoid multiplying b by two. It applies on all
integer triples; it does not rely on a hidden Gram typing condition.

For a fixed word of t such shears, each operating on one of the two
blocks, the following gives a positive-witness certificate. Supply one
common D>0 and represent the evolving signed b by bhat=b+D. Keep a,c
unshifted. Compute 2D=D+D once and initial bhat=b+D once. At a step let
z denote the unchanged diagonal and y the changing diagonal. Supply
the two positive next coordinates bhat',y', and compare

    bhat'=bhat−εz,
    y'=y−εbhat−εbhat'+2εD.

These right-hand sides cost respectively one and three additions.
Use direct subtraction for ε=1 and direct addition for ε=−1; ε is fixed
by the chosen word. Copy the other block and unchanged diagonal for free.
Finally compare

    a_final+c_final+A_final+C_final = 4.

This endpoint costs three additions. The positive coordinates introduced
are D and two updated coordinates per step: **2t+1** in total. There
are **2t+1** comparisons. The complete literal schedule is

    12 input gates + 2 shared shift gates + 4t step gates + 3 endpoint gates
      = 4t+17 = 6M+(4t+11)A.

Squaring every residual and summing gives a single polynomial with

    10t+19 = (2t+7)M+(8t+12)A.

The polynomial has exact total degree **8** for every fixed word. All
residuals are linear combinations of the degree-at-most-four loader
outputs and supplied degree-one witnesses. At t=0 the endpoint contains
2c, whose leading input term is 2α⁴x⁴. At t>0 the first shear's upper
b-residual or lower diagonal residual contains initial c with nonzero
coefficient. Its quartic term cannot cancel against initial a,b, whose
degrees are respectively two and three, or supplied witnesses. Hence
some residual has degree four, and the highest squares in the sum of
squares cannot cancel over the reals. This degree-eight family has an
unbounded number of witnesses as t grows; it is not one fixed universal
degree-eight polynomial.

All initial six Gram coordinates are computed by the loader; they are
not six additional witnesses. Repeated initial coordinates and unchanged
states are aliases. The source also covers t=0; no optimality is claimed.

Soundness is direct induction after decoding b=bhat−D. The equalities
force exactly the stated Gram action. There is no independent freedom
to choose a different determinant or history. For completeness, actual
Gram matrices A G A^T are positive definite, so both diagonal coordinates
are positive at every time. The finite set of signed off-diagonal values
admits D larger than all their absolute values. Hence every required
supplied coordinate is strictly positive. The endpoint is exactly the
scalar zero of Section 2.

This fixed-word certificate is exact even for words outside Γ², but then
its endpoint means equality modulo the four integral rotations, as
explained earlier. To represent K_U, words must concatenate the fixed
generator macros and their inverses. An arbitrary duration and arbitrary
choice of those words have **not** been encoded by this family of
fixed-word polynomials. In particular, this certificate does not replace
the completed [uniform matrix compiler](group_complete_matrix_compiler.md).

## 6. What this changes, and what remains open

The endpoint theorem has no height-dependent one-vector hypothesis. It
tests full block equality using a fixed scalar functional on six Gram
coordinates. The input is an explicit positive quartic column, and the
mortality adapter permits its rank-one input generator to repeat freely.
These are exact improvements in the interface, not an operation-count
improvement over the current complete universal equation.

Compared with four evolving vector coordinates, this route has six Gram
coordinates and three signed additions per physical shear. It may trade
the one-vector endpoint's height argument for more selected history
arithmetic. Any packed implementation must still pay for the common
word, regular macro controller, selected products, length/radix geometry,
and signed-history representation. The unrestricted rank-one mortality
lemma removes a sentinel-order condition only; it does not make the
intervening matrix word free.

The checker independently compares the symmetric-square formulas with
dense matrix multiplication, tests the positive quartic loader, verifies
the full rank-one factorization with multiple sentinels, and checks every
literal fixed-word residual on arbitrary positive assignments. It also
checks accepted words, changed inputs, witness mutations, and the explicit
torsion false positive. Both mortality loaders and the fixed conjugation
are checked; finite Boolean support closures test the nonnegative
obstruction. Exact finite differences additionally verify nonzero
degree-eight input slices of 256 fixed-word polynomials. These finite
tests do not enumerate universal
group generators or establish the unbounded theorem by experiment.

An independent full proof/source/default review passed without findings.
It checked the Gram representation, Frobenius minimum and torsion
restriction, rank-one factorization with arbitrary separators, the
13-operation nonnegative loader and its unimodular change of basis,
the support obstruction, and the exact degree-eight fixed-word proof.
The reviewer also verified the three primary-source boundaries directly.

A second independent full proof/source/default review passed without
findings, including a separate reading of all three primary sources.
It additionally checked 512 direct off-diagonal residual, sum-of-squares,
loader and dense-Gram cases. Both reviews confirmed that the universal
target compatibility and the unpaid variable-word obligations are explicit.
