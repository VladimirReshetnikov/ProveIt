The model uses **finite actual atom kernels** inside an ambient set-with-atoms universe with Choice. This is not the usual claim that a finite permutation support suffices. The named action is that of a pure, set-sized group; its graph need not be an internal set. The full Replacement criterion requires finite generated atom orbits and only finitely many atoms in finite orbit types having finite positive multiplicity. At a fixed positive kernel bound `k`, the second condition is finiteness of atoms whose **centralizer** orbits have size at most `k`. The proof explicitly adds the whole finitely repeated stratum touched by a parameter to its finite reservoir.

The distinctions relevant to computation remain explicit:

- All fixed finite bounded-output schemes may hold while full Replacement fails; an internally varying bound is stronger.
- Separately naming infinitely many operations does not provide a relation uniformly evaluating their names.
- Finite-word evaluation is first-order definable through an existing finite path; this does not give a constant-arity paid arithmetic representation of paths of unbounded length.
- Pure-valued Collection and the pure surreal universe can remain intact when atom-valued Replacement fails.

The finite base-image algorithm is stated for explicitly supplied finite transitive permutation actions, with `O(s n²)` transition checks and `O(n)` working memory per candidate beyond stored input/output. Its 203 reported checks, including 35 brute-force comparisons, check finite component facts. A finite truncation does not refute Replacement. No Lean/Rocq proof is claimed. The new text also credits the existing naming-elementary-embeddings report for the full criterion in the finitely-many-permutations case.

This is a useful warning against confusing definability and uniform evaluation, but the inspected material supplies no paid ordinary-input compiler.

## Source 04: a substantive residue-image interface, with nonstandard and oracle boundaries

The third commit genuinely inserts source 04 in Sections 23–26, a Section 1.7 summary, scope/provenance material, and the explicitly marked write-time Scott-set corollary. Static comparison gives **236 → 313** labels; all 236 previous labels remain and all 77 additions use `isg:ptg:`. Existing section/question numbers move as the README explains; labels are the stable references.

The most relevant inspected statement is the exact residue-image theorem for a **nonstandard model of full PA**:

- its local image is the integral part of the field of points with a fast name computable from some member of its standard system;
- its full profinite image consists of factorial-residue sequences computable from **one shared** member of the standard system;
- the canonical prime-coordinate idempotents recover that standard system;
- combined with the imported classical Scott theorem, the countable-model images are exactly the rings associated with countable Scott sets.

The full local and profinite proofs and the write-time corollary were read. The local reverse construction uses an internally finite, nonstandard-length digit sum. The simultaneous profinite reverse construction uses **overspill of finite coherence**, rather than assuming an arbitrary nonstandard decoded prefix remains coherent. These are model-theoretic realizations, not ordinary finite integer witnesses constructed by a charged circuit. The weaker `IΔ0` residue-image problem stays open in the text; the internal comprehension/induction steps are not silently downgraded to bounded induction or EFA.

A precise obstruction worth retaining is that identical ordinary finite quotients or completions do not determine the distinguished simultaneous image. Individual local coordinates can each be available while a common-oracle coherent sequence is unavailable. This does not obstruct an ordinary-integer compiler by itself; any proposed use would need a separate effective, finite representation and input theorem.

The perfect-gap application has an additional external boundary: properness of the Borel model's standard system imports Glazer's theorem from seminar slides. The publication explicitly says its write did not consult those slides or establish a published proof. This review has not checked them either. The exact PA image branch does not depend on the Borel properness input. The analytic-derivation/perfect-set proof was **not** read in full here, and the main perfect-gap theorem is not independently certified by this intake.

The effective limitations are unusually decisive for compiler routing. Root computation is nonuniform in finite isolating data; no global root selector is supplied. Every computable scalar already belongs to the relevant oracle field, so none of the independent perfect-family members can have a computable name. The paper explicitly rules out a computable Cantor-space parametrization without extra information. Countable external field parameters do not mean adding an arbitrary join oracle to the Turing ideal. The Hahn transfer uses set-sized groups/fields and coefficient-preserving arithmetic; its omnific image is Cantor-parameterized but discrete in the surreal order topology, and independence is over the restricted coefficient field, not all reals.

Its finite-check table reports digit, Laurent-tail, Newton, factorial-residue and CRT identities. These do not test a Borel model, overspill, a perfect set or independence over an uncountable field. The countable-Scott-set corollary is a real write-time addition, but no fixed paid polynomial or new Diophantine operation bound is provided.
