## Independent mathematical checks

Composition acts right to left. The primitive right shift and reflection give sigma^-1=rho sigma rho; consequently zeta_i frees index i and T_i swaps i,i+1. The two projection macros actually used free indices <0 and >1. Every fixed power and finite liberation list expands to allowed operations.

For the pair construction, T_1 tau S gives (m,0,m-1,0) and T_1 sigma^2 S gives (0,n,0,n+1). The two complementary finite liberations followed by intersection give L2 exactly, without assuming a new sum operation. Aligned four-blocks and the shift by -2 cover both parities of adjacent pair indices. For z_i=(g(2i),g(2i+1)), every transition either starts at zero or subtracts (1,-1). A nonzero pair must reach a first future zero because g has finite support; if that takes t steps, its value was (t,-t). Conversely the finite descending chain realizes each t>=0. This proves the needed relation directly.

The two liberations preserve the pair from a single original M witness: one preserves all nonnegative coordinates and the next preserves all coordinates <=1, hence both preserve 0,1. Conversely, patching arbitrary coordinates outside 0,1 requires only finite support and yields the required intermediate witness. Intersecting with support {0,1} gives the nonnegative opposite pairs, and union with their swap gives all integer opposite pairs.

The two singleton constants follow by intersecting tau S or S with zeta Z. For the final expression, each of the eight factors has support inside F={1,...,8}; liberations do not change coordinates outside F. The three opposite-pair factors are placed exactly at {1,3}, {3,5}, {5,7}. Writing n=f(3), they force f(1)=f(5)=-n and f(7)=n; the other factors impose n in U and the four signs at 2,4,6,8. The converse meets every factor. Empty U gives the empty set, and n=0 is permitted when 0 is in U. E_U occurs once in the expression.

With a at even coordinates and b at odd coordinates, the resulting word is b^-n a b^n a b^-n a^-1 b^n a^-1=[b^-n a b^n,a], under the stated commutator convention. This check uses the normalized convention explicitly; it does not silently identify the two different printed parity conventions in the earlier embedding source.

## Retained source and reviewer boundaries

1. **Projection index.** The printed pi_2 retains coordinate 2. An M witness with pair (2,-2) has coordinate 2 equal to 1, so the later support restriction rejects it. The literal restricted expression gives only (0,0),(1,-1), or only (1,-1) with the strict sign condition. The author's pi_after1 repairs this local error.
2. **Existence index.** The source asserts a witness with g(0)=n,g(2)=-n for each n>0. Every even coordinate of M is nonnegative, so this fails already for n=1. Coordinate 1 is the negative partner. The preceding conditional involving a negative even coordinate is vacuous; it is the subsequent existence assertion that is false.
3. **Left-liberation sign.** The printed sigma^-i pi_left sigma^i frees indices <-i. At i=1 it excludes the unit function at index 0 on input Z, while the stated intended liberation includes it. The correct conjugation for indices <i is sigma^i pi_left sigma^-i. Only the correctly derived macros enter this theorem.
4. **General-swap exponent.** The printed formula at k=0,l=2 simplifies to (tau sigma) tau (sigma^-1 tau)^-1=tau sigma^2. It takes the unit function at 2 to the unit function at 4, whereas the claimed transposition takes it to 0. The author's adjacent T_i needs no such formula. These four concrete formula corrections do not refute the opposite-pair theorem or certify other constructions in the paper.
5. **Reviewer wording.** My preliminary message called the eight inner slots “eight nonzero slots.” For n=0 the four variable slots vanish. The corrected statement is eight inner coordinates, of which positions 2,4,6,8 are fixed nonzero. The word identity and the author theorem are unchanged.
