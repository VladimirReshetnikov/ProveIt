## Findings and mathematical challenge

### 1. Four-signal periodic validity is a supplied-word theorem

Source 26 fixes the machine, complete periodic word, labels, speeds and outgoing section. Removing translation and at least one collision zero-gap leaves dimension at most two only for a collision-anchored section with at most four live signals. An arbitrary observation phase would add a coordinate. I read and challenged the complete chronological chamber, the all-iterate criterion, all order-two spectral cases and the sign compiler (article 10754–11043).

The proof retains strict next-contact comparisons, including ties belonging to the stated complete batch. In the nonreal case a nonzero scalar observation cannot stay nonnegative; for real spectrum, both parity subsequences are required. The distinct positive-root test correctly permits a zero dominant coefficient, whereas a zero smaller root requires a strictly positive tail value. The sign-separated radical removal does not reverse an inequality. The natural one-hot sign gadget also fixes its magnitude slack at a zero atom, so the witness tuple really is unique. Observable clock cancellation is essential: spectral radius below one is not necessary when a neutral or growing whole-matrix mode is invisible to the clock.

There is one wording qualification. The Part-X overview at line 10723 and the section heading at 10925 say “one natural witness”; the theorem and its explicit ledger at 10957–10970 say **one unique tuple with `4A+Gamma` natural coordinates**, and `6A+Gamma+1` residuals. It is not a one-coordinate certificate. The README uses the same shorthand, as identified in Pascal's guide review. This does not invalidate the explicit theorem; a summary should say “one natural witness tuple.” The arity is fixed per supplied macro, not uniformly over all macros, and the generic machine/word-to-formula frontend is still not implemented.

### 2. Timing, semialgebraicity and real computation do not imply ordinary-input universality

Source 24's four-particle timing relation is the decidable quadratic family `t=k^2+(2x+3)k`. Its monic equation forces a rational root to be integral; the real-witness interpretation instead admits noninteger roots. The stronger editorial obstruction (10712–10718) is sound: a finite real existential polynomial certificate projects to a semialgebraic set, whose one-dimensional natural trace is finite or cofinite. A fixed-x hit set is infinite with infinite complement. This argument concerns external time/input coordinates alone; it does not rule out other external-output interfaces or natural witnesses.

Source 25 explicitly assumes the source-17 scattering data and source-18 charts. I checked its conditional statement and its copied-input quartic adapter, not the general chart derivation or those imported dynamics. The natural selector sum chooses one chart; nonnegative private-coordinate sums kill all inactive coordinates; pooled denominator clearing preserves the output value. Copying external inputs avoids multiplying quadratic input expressions directly by selectors. The bound of two evolution parameters is not two total witnesses: the displayed whole ledger is `3mJ+sum(k_h)+I`, including selectors, private copies, gap quotients and inequality slacks.

For sources 27/29, the disk minus dense boundary orbits is an obstruction to finite polynomial-sign classification, not to existential integer arithmetic. I challenged source 29's all-real proof (12985–13094): on an admissible closed circle arc, deleting a countable dense set gives a non-`F_sigma` set by Baire; fixing finitely many integer witnesses in a hypothetical mixed real/integer certificate gives semialgebraic projections, hence a countable `F_sigma` union. The finite-path exact-real field-operation argument has the same upper bound and excludes floors, digit oracles and completed infinite computations. None of this proves Turing undecidability for encoded rational or positive-integer gaps. The denominator proof supplies a finite candidate exponent for each rational rotation contact. I did not re-audit the 138/114-event physical tables or the generic rotation constructor.

### 3. Small local matrix certificates represent a decidable property

I checked the full-section inverse and determinant proof of source 31 (14250–14423). The ambient landing projection has a kernel, but its restriction between transverse collision hyperplanes is invertible, with the displayed explicit inverse. The sorted-face determinants telescope to sign `(-1)^m` for a literal same-face return. This does not imply global reversibility of the rule table, nor does it forbid singular maps after hidden-coordinate projection, on a proper slice or in a different collision model.

The source-32 fixed-center factorization and pulled-back guards are coherent (14971–15085). Its 780-event coefficient-one shear and parameter-dependent compiler counts are physical word counts, not arithmetic gate counts. They do not imply polynomial word size in coefficient bit length.

For source 33 I checked the local normalization and certificate (15423–15473, 15617–15823). If an invertible rational `N` takes one interior positive-cone point to another, physical input/output translations reduce it to a fixed-center matrix; the concatenated exact guard list is strict at that chosen ray. This supplies some local chamber, not an invariant chamber or an infinitely valid orbit. The rational linear-feasibility decision is distinct from constructing a short physical word.

The eight-positive-witness certificate imposes `Kg=h>0`, `b in {1,2}` and `det K=(2b-3)d` with `d>0`. These exactly express cone compatibility and nonsingularity. The five residual squares have degree six. Its displayed signed-entry arithmetic ledger sums to 26 multiplications and 22 additions/subtractions, hence 48 operations; nine positive-input differences give 57. I checked that displayed ledger arithmetically, not the stored DAG arrays. The centered-coordinate conversion is excluded from those counts. This decidable local-realizability predicate cannot be entered as a universal operation improvement.

Source 28's 344/367-operation rotation certificates and source 30's per-contact 81-witness/44-equation construction remain source-reported complete arithmetic ledgers here; I read their interfaces and accounting but did not re-expand their DAGs. Source 30's strict kernel adds one common radius witness/equation, so it is `1+81J` and `1+44J`, not a uniform total independent of the contact count.

### 4. Five live signals support a real counter-program interface, with explicit input cost

Sources 34–35 compile each fixed finite deterministic two-counter program into a finite table that works for every encoded counter pair. This is stronger than a finite selected run, but it is not an instantiated universal counter table with an optimized arithmetic certificate. The source program still determines the control labels/rules. The encoding is

`x/D=1/20+2^(-a)/10`, `(D-y)/D=1/20+2^(-b)/10`.

I checked its all-counter margins, increment/decrement affine updates, per-program phase copying, and the finite trace proof. The complete forward/reverse nondestructive test chronologies were not re-audited. The zero-A band has `4x<y<8x`; the positive-A band has `y-8x>=D/20`, with reflected B bands. Those margins justify using the tested branch templates on all encoded counters rather than just selected samples.

The clock proof is substantive paid geometry: each padding speed change occurs at a contact, the entry loop supplies a real contact before changing speed, and the fixed loops/transfers plus target loops preserve the four markers and exactly cancel the six output-linear core times. The six positive final padding coefficients give `30D`; the actual worst branch costs 54 contacts, and the common speed set has 39 values. I checked those formulas and interface accounting in 16831–17024, not an executable collision trace. Strictly positive flights and a constant positive instruction duration exclude finite-time accumulation along an infinite encoded instruction run.

The polynomial in 17157–17254 has `T(E+2)+54` positive witnesses and `T(E+Z+4)+33` residual slots. Its Boolean shifted selectors, state-code linkage, updates and positivity correctly reject a decrement from zero. A virtual halt edge supplies arithmetic padding only. Initial halt at T=0 and first-halt-exactly-T are treated separately. The two POWER modules pay for encodedness and reject unencoded positive gaps, conditional on the cited constructive Pell theorem interface. Their actual all-exponent external theorem proof was not re-established here. Unique decoding/trace does not make the full Pell witness fiber finite.

The primitive initialization calculation is consistent: for large `m=max(a,b)` the numeric scale is proportional to `2^m`, and its bit length is proportional to `m`, not `log m`. Paying a POWER relation does not prove a small witness-height or polynomial-time physical initialization bound in the counter's binary length.

### 5. Later arity reductions preserve the finite-horizon boundary

For source 36, the clipping proof uses tests at transition entry. A representative started at T remains positive before every one of the first T transitions; final counter values may differ. Its finite acceptance table has `(T+1)^2` entries and can be produced by at most `(T+1)^2*T` transitions. The interpolation and six-square formula then force a unique class code and two positive tail slacks. I read the proof and cost bounds, not the later encoded-gap counting/asymptotic theorem.

For source 37, I read the 22-to-16-to-14-to-13-to-12 POWER reductions (18148–18557). Nonnegative congruence quotients follow from positive residues bounded by their moduli; removed expressions have their domains restored. Rational square recovery gives integral `alpha`, and, in the twelve-leaf form, integral `u`. Crucially, its direct quotient `q_alpha` is strictly positive. Since `|u|>=alpha`, the equation `beta=alpha+u*q_alpha` rules out a negative u. Completeness first moves along the Pell progression to a strict quotient. Thus the last map is a bijection only with the strict subset of the thirteen-leaf solution space, not all old tuples. The output is included among the twelve leaves; only eleven are auxiliary after fixing it. The displayed progression retains an increasing q_b, so accepted fibers remain infinite. These are witness/degree reductions, not a newly counted gate reduction. The external constructive Pell statements are imported as stated in the article; their source/formal proof was not independently checked.

For source 38, I checked the tensor and disjoint-cell arguments, strict-positive boundary conditions, degree calculations and the displayed native gate bounds (18913–19294). The tensor residuals force clipped coordinates despite those expressions having no assumed sign before equations. The one-witness product works because positive-integer cells are disjoint and its tail polynomial is zero exactly below the threshold; allowing witness zero would admit wrong tail cells. A product of nonnegative sums of squares is not generally one residual square. Squaring that product changes the degree and costs a multiplication.

The zero-versus-one auxiliary classification is valid in its stated class: one polynomial equality for a fixed finite clipping table, unrestricted degree, ordinary positive integer inputs. Vanishing on a full infinite tail grid forces vanishing on its entire coordinate flat, giving necessity; the product of coordinate-flat quadratic factors proves sufficiency. It is not a minimality theorem for arbitrary computably enumerable predicates.

I checked the native gate ceilings `4K^2+2K+14` (tensor) and `3T^2+10T+7` (one-witness product) from the displayed sharing, including the empty-table convention. These are horizon-dependent native formulas; the complete 28/27-witness gap compositions additionally pay two POWER modules and two gap equations. The complete fiber stays infinite although its decoded projection is unique. A single fixed witness count for each member of a horizon-indexed family is not a single fixed polynomial for the union over all horizons. The absent arithmetic module for the varying acceptance table/factorials/products is the precise remaining compiler bottleneck.
