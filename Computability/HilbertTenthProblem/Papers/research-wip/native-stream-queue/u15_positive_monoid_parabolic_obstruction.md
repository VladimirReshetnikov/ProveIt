# A positive-monoid obstruction to the U15 parabolic block shortcut

**Theorem.** Let an injective homomorphism encode the free positive monoid on letters a,b into GL2(Z). The image of

`W=(ab)^3 b²=abababbb`

is neither parabolic nor finite-order, and no positive power of it is parabolic. The same holds for a faithful positive-monoid encoding of the entire twenty-letter U15 alphabet, by restriction to its two tape letters. Thus weakening full free-group faithfulness to positive-word faithfulness does not make this particular block parabolic.

This closes the monoid-only loophole left by the [group-faithful obstruction](u15_faithful_parabolic_obstruction.md). It does not classify other words or encodings and does not lower a universal Diophantine operation bound. The determinant-positive case uses an explicitly stated published semigroup theorem. The remaining determinant-negative case is an elementary integral trace argument given in full below.

## 1. Why the weaker encoding condition was relevant

In the [directed193 construction](group_directed_semigroup193.md), the unchanged lower-marker group forces a target product into the form `A_s C B_rev(s)`. Rearranging its upper equality gives

`Phi(w # h(s)) = Phi(g(s) [J1] #)`.

Both sides are positive words. Injectivity of the upper encoding on the entire positive monoid therefore suffices to reflect this literal equation. Group faithfulness of the upper encoding is sufficient but stronger than necessary. The separate lower-marker construction remains unchanged. Faithfulness on just the two tape letters would not by itself validate the full twenty-letter directed construction.

No nonempty positive word can have finite-order image: two distinct positive powers would coincide. Moreover positive-word injectivity survives projectivization by the central signs. If two positive words have images differing by a sign, their squares have equal images, so their word squares, hence the words themselves, are equal. Consequently multiplying either letter matrix by -I cannot create a positive finite-order word without one already existing for the original matrices.

Write the two letter matrices as A,B. If their determinants differ, W has determinant -1 and

`tr(W²)=tr(W)²+2`.

Trace zero makes W²=I, which is impossible. Every other integral trace makes W² hyperbolic. This settles the mixed-determinant case, including the claim about positive powers.

## 2. Both determinants positive

Here A,B lie in SL2(Z). Their positive-word images in PSL2(Z) form a discrete inverse-free semigroup. It is nonelementary: a discrete elementary subgroup of PSL2(R) is virtually cyclic and cannot contain exponentially many distinct positive words of a bounded length in two generators.

The primary input is Jacques–Short, *Semigroups of Isometries of the Hyperbolic Plane*, **Theorem1.4**, including its final dichotomy: for a nonelementary two-generator semidiscrete inverse-free semigroup in PSL2(R), either both generators map a closed interval properly into itself, or the semigroup is contained in a rank-two Schottky group. The generators can be reordered to satisfy the theorem's trace-order convention. See [IMRN2022, issue9, pp.6403–6463, DOI10.1093/imrn/rnaa291](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnaa291), published online December9,2020, and the [accepted manuscript](https://oro.open.ac.uk/72496/1/__Penelope_mcsusers_Staff_is3649_master-folder_papers_submitted_semigroups_semigroups-IMRN-2.pdf), pp.3–4. The theorem statement, relevant definitions and proper-containment convention in Theorem7.1 were read directly; the paper's whole classification proof is an external theorem, not independently re-established here.

The words “strictly inside” in that paper mean **proper containment**, not necessarily containment in the interior. The following endpoint argument is therefore needed. An orientation-preserving proper selfmap of a closed interval either has image in its interior or fixes exactly one endpoint. If both generators fixed the same endpoint the semigroup would be elementary. Otherwise they either include an interior-image generator or fix opposite endpoints; in the latter case both mixed products map the interval into its interior. Every positive word using both letters consequently has interior image. Such a Möbius transformation has distinct fixed points in the interval and its complement, and is hyperbolic. This applies to W. Pure parabolic powers of one generator are not excluded by this argument.

In the Schottky alternative, the subgroup generated by A,B is a noncyclic two-generated subgroup of a free group. It has rank two, and the induced surjection from F2 is an isomorphism because F2 is Hopfian. The letter pair is therefore group-faithful. The already reviewed rank-two peripheral argument now applies: W cannot be a parabolic peripheral element. For clarity, in the basis `c=ab,b`, W is `c³b²`. It is not primitive, since killing it admits a nonabelian S3 quotient; it is not a proper power, since its abelianization has coprime entries3,2; and its nonzero abelianization excludes a commutator power. Rank-two orientable free Fuchsian cores have only primitive or commutator peripheral classes, up to powers and conjugacy.

This proves the SL2(Z) case without assuming that every free positive monoid extends to a faithful free-group encoding.

## 3. Both determinants negative: trace reduction

Now `det A=det B=-1`. Neither trace is zero, since that would make the corresponding letter an involution. Change central signs so that

`s=tr A>0, t=tr B>0, X=AB, x=tr X`.

All are integers. The absence of positive finite-order words is preserved by these sign changes. Cayley–Hamilton gives `B²=tB+I`. If `y_n=tr(X^n B)`, then

`y_0=t, y_1=xt+s, y_(n+1)=x y_n-y_(n-1)`.

It follows that

`tr W=(t²+1)x³+st x²-(2t²+3)x-st`.                     (1)

The commutator trace is

`K=tr(ABA^-1B^-1)=x²-stx-s²-t²-2`.                    (2)

For example, (2) follows directly from `A^-1=A-sI`, `B^-1=B-tI`, `tr(X²)=x²-2`, `tr(A²B)=sx+t`, and `tr(AB²)=tx+s`.

Values |x|<2 make the determinant-one positive word X finite-order. For x≥2, (1) equals

`t² x(x²-2)+x(x²-3)+st(x²-1)>2`.

At x=-2, (1) is `3st-4t²-2`. If it were +2, reduction modulo3 would give `t²=2 mod3`, impossible. If it is -2, then `3s=4t`, and `tr(X³B)=3s-4t=0`. Thus the positive determinant-negative word X³B is an involution. It remains only to rule out trace±2 with `x=-z`, z≥3.

## 4. An integral classification when x≤-3

Suppose `tr W=2ε`, where ε is ±1. Put

`D=z²-1, N=z³-3z+2ε=(z-ε)²(z+2ε)`.

Solving (1) gives

`s=[z(z²-2)t²+N]/(tD)`.

Substitution in (2) yields the exact identity

`K=2-m²,  m=(t²+εN)/(tD)`.                           (3)

Here m is rational, while K is integral. A rational number whose square is an integer is an integer, so **m∈Z**. Set `u=t/(z-ε)>0`. Equation(3) implies

`u²-m(z+ε)u+ε(z+2ε)=0`.                              (4)

This is a monic integer polynomial. Its rational root u is therefore a positive integer. These integrality steps apply to every pair of integer matrices; they are not numerical search assumptions.

### 4.1. Trace -2

For ε=-1, (4) says `u²-m(z-1)u-(z-2)=0`. The number

`v=(z-2)/u=u-m(z-1)`

is a positive integer. Since `z-1=uv+1`,

`m=(u-v)/(uv+1)`.

Its absolute value is strictly less than1, hence m=0 and u=v. Thus K=2.

This forces A,B to commute in the present integral setting. Indeed B has distinct irrational quadratic eigenvalues: `t²+4` cannot be a square when t is a nonzero integer. In an eigenbasis for B, the commutator-trace formula shows that K=2 forces at least one off-diagonal entry of A to vanish, so A preserves one B-eigenline. Rationality of both matrices makes the Galois-conjugate eigenline invariant too. Hence A is diagonal in that basis and commutes with B. This already contradicts positive-monoid injectivity (`ab` and `ba`). More specifically, W is diagonalizable with determinant1 and trace-2, so W=-I.

### 4.2. Trace +2

For ε=+1, m>0 by its defining formula. The other root of (4) is the positive integer

`v=(z+2)/u=m(z+1)-u`.

Therefore `uv=z+2≥5` and

`u+v=m(uv-1)`.

Assume u≤v. If u=1, then v≥5 and `m=1+2/(v-1)` is not integral. If u=2, then v≥3 and `m=(v+2)/(2v-1)≤1`, with equality only at v=3. If u≥3, then `0<u+v<uv-1`, contradicting positive integrality of m. Thus

`z=4, m=1, {u,v}={2,3}`,

and the only trace triples are

`(s,t,x)=(23,6,-4)` or `(34,9,-4)`.

Set the positive determinant-negative words

`U=XB=abb, V=X²B=ababb`.

Their traces are respectively `(-1,-2)` or `(-2,-1)`. Since `UV^-1=X^-1`, the determinant-negative trace identity gives

`tr(UV)=tr U tr V+tr X=-2`.

In the first case, `tr(U²V)=tr U tr(UV)+tr V=0`; in the second, `tr(UV²)=tr V tr(UV)+tr U=0`. Hence a positive word is an involution in either case, again contradicting faithfulness. This exhausts the determinant-negative case.

`A=[[5,7],[13,18]], B=[[4,-3],[-3,2]]`,
