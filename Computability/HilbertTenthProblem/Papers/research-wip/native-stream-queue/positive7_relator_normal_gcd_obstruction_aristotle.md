# A determinant-one obstruction to the permutation-only g=1 shortcut

A primitive common left normal of a relator/inverse difference need not have any coprime pair of coordinates. This remains true for an actual fixed integer determinant-one matrix, not merely an abstract primitive vector. The following counterexample rules out choosing g=1 uniformly by output permutation in the accepted relator-plane recipe. It does not rule out other integer factorizations, coefficient absorption, special relators or a different paid schedule.

## 1. An explicit small matrix

Take

    P=[[1651,1575],[630,601]].                              (1)

Its determinant is

    1651*601-1575*630=992251-992250=1.

For the inherited representation S(P), the common left-normal numerator is

    (s,p-t,-q)=(630,1050,-1575)=105*(6,10,-15).

The positive gcd of the three primitive entries6,10,15 is1, so n=(6,10,-15) is primitive. Its pairwise gcds are

    gcd(6,10)=2, gcd(6,15)=3, gcd(10,15)=5.                  (2)

Consequently every output-coordinate permutation has g=gcd(n1,n2)>1. Changing the global sign of n does not change these gcds.

Root suggested realizing this primitive vector through a Pell equation with discriminant460. A smaller integral realization uses

    B0=[[5,15],[6,-5]], B0^2=115I,
    1126^2-115*105^2=1267876-1267875=1,
    P=1126I+105B0.

This gives exactly(1). Root's integer-parameter construction with A0=2B0=[[10,30],[12,-10]] also works: squaring this unit gives x=2535751,y=118230 with x^2-460y^2=1 and P^2=xI+yA0. No Pell existence theorem, numerical search or scientific program is needed for this explicit witness.

## 2. The primitive normal cannot be replaced by another one

For a general P=[[p,q],[s,t]] in SL2(Z), the top-left two-by-two minor of S(P)-I is

    det[[p^2-1,-2pq],[-ps,pt+qs-1]]
       =2qs(p^2-1)-2p^2qs=-2qs,

because pt-qs=1 implies pt+qs-1=2qs. In(1), q and s are nonzero, so this minor is nonzero. The already proved left invariant n*(S(P)-I)=0 gives rank at most2; the minor gives rank at least2. Thus the rational left kernel has dimension1 and is generated by n.

Every primitive integer vector on this rational line is n or -n. Indeed primitivity of n gives an integer linear combination of its entries equal to1; if a rational multiple of n has integer entries, the same combination shows that multiple is an integer. A primitive multiple then has absolute multiplier1. The common fixed normals of S(P) and S(P^-1) are therefore exactly this same primitive pair, not a larger family from which a coprime coordinate pair could be chosen.

The relator-plane action uses

    C=[[1,0,0,-1],[1,1,0,-2],[2,0,1,-4]],
    W=[(S(P)-I)C | (S(P^-1)-I)C].

The first three columns of C form a determinant-one matrix, so C maps Z^4 onto Z^3. In particular (S(P)-I)C has the same rank2 as S(P)-I. The common normal annihilates both blocks of W, so W also has rank2 and the same rational left kernel. Choosing a normal from W directly cannot avoid(2). This statement concerns its rational column plane; it does not assert that its integer column lattice is the whole primitive plane lattice.

## 3. Exact boundary of the failed shortcut

**Review remark 1 (refuted primitive-normal shortcut).** The proposed shortcut was: "For every SL2(Z) relator, permute the primitive common left normal so gcd(n1,n2)=1, then replace the reconstruction product g*B by an alias of B." Matrix(1) refutes the permutation premise: g is2,3 or5 for every permutation, and Section2 excludes a different primitive normal. The uniform21M+19A append proof remains valid and unchanged; this counterexample invalidates only the proposed uniform alias justification.

At this same unrestricted local cut, deleting the multiplication while keeping all its coefficient forms unchanged would actually change an output polynomial. For any permutation, the third row of W' is nonzero: otherwise its rank2 plane would be a coordinate plane, contrary to its normal having three nonzero entries. Thus the compiled form B=sum_j (W'_{3j}/g)z_j is nonzero as a polynomial in the independent selected words. Since g>1, g*B differs from B. This does not exclude a different recompilation that absorbs factors or reconstructs the plane by other means.

**Open question 1 (other paid reconstructions).** A different fixed integer basis, integral output transformation, shared coefficient arithmetic or absorption strategy may improve reconstruction. Its complete runtime arithmetic and accumulator updates would have to be charged. Neither a20M+19A impossibility theorem nor an optimality claim follows from this example.

The counterexample belongs to the generic fixed-data domain P in SL2(Z). Its occurrence in the numerically unmaterialized relator list of the inherited universal presentation is not asserted. No ordinary input, native witness, complete compiler zero or new complete-source bound is constructed here.
