## Primary theorem and its interval convention

Theorem 1.4 and the preceding definitions were read directly in the
[Jacques–Short accepted manuscript](https://oro.open.ac.uk/72496/1/__Penelope_mcsusers_Staff_is3649_master-folder_papers_submitted_semigroups_semigroups-IMRN-2.pdf),
pages 3–4. The proper-containment convention was checked against Theorem 7.1
and its proof. The [published metadata](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnaa291)
identifies IMRN 2022, issue 9, pages 6403–6463, first published online on
December 9, 2020.

The relevant dichotomy is exactly the one used in Section 2: a nonelementary
two-generator semidiscrete inverse-free semigroup either preserves a closed
interval properly under both generators or lies in a rank-two Schottky
group. Reordering the generators meets the trace convention. The theorem's
interval condition does not itself assert interior containment. An early
working interpretation was corrected before the frozen packet: the final
proof explicitly supplies the necessary endpoint argument.

For the integral matrices here, projectivization remains injective on
positive words: a possible sign discrepancy disappears after squaring,
and equality of two word squares forces equality of the words. Discreteness
comes from PSL2(Z). No nonempty positive word is the identity or has finite
order. An elementary generated subgroup would be virtually cyclic, which
cannot accommodate the exponential number of distinct positive words in
bounded generator length. These facts supply the theorem's hypotheses.

An increasing proper interval selfmap either moves both endpoints inward
or fixes precisely one. A common fixed endpoint would be elementary.
Otherwise, opposite fixed endpoints make both mixed products interior;
the presence of an already interior generator has the same consequence
for any word using it. Thus every positive word using both letters, including
W, maps the interval into its interior and is hyperbolic. This argument
correctly leaves pure powers of parabolic generators available.

In the Schottky alternative the subgroup generated by the two letters is
a noncyclic two-generated free group, hence rank two. Hopficity makes the
two letters a free basis, so the pinned predecessor's peripheral-word
obstruction applies. The earlier surface classification remains an explicit
inherited dependency. No finite relation search is substituted for it.
