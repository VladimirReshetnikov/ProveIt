## 2. The reports' mathematical interface

Both reports count closed untyped lambda terms up to alpha-equivalence, with ordered application and unrestricted syntax. An abstraction and an application each have weight 1; a variable has weight `s`, where `s` is 0 or 1. For `u` abstractions and `b` applications there are `b+1` variable leaves, and the size is `u+b+s(b+1)`. A leaf at unary depth `d` has exactly `d` possible ancestor binders. Consequently a fixed unary/binary skeleton contributes the product of its leaf depths, equivalently the valid de Bruijn choices. These are binding and syntax-construction statements, not beta steps.

Report 109 states four logarithmic scales for the counts, an asymptotic inverse count threshold, and a joint large-deviation/concentration analysis of the abstraction count and maximum unary depth. Maximum unary depth measures abstractions along a root-to-leaf path; it is not execution time or the length of a reduction. Report 110 states uniform logarithmic approximations and every fixed inverse-logarithmic order, together with a controlled inverse count threshold. These are analytic claims, not merely finite experimental counts. This review identifies their scope without certifying the majorant, saddle, large-deviation or asymptotic-reversion proofs.

In both reports the inverse is explicitly `nu_s(y)=min{n : A_s(n)>=y}`: the smallest syntax size with at least `y` closed terms. It is not an inverse halting-time function or a bound on normalization. Report 109's context recurrence `L_m(z)=m*z^s+z*L_(m+1)(z)+z*L_m(z)^2` and Report 110's analogous two-variable recurrence decompose syntax into variable, abstraction and application cases. Their recursive shape is not an operational reduction relation. The reports explicitly separate unrestricted terms from typed, linear, affine, beta-normal and differently weighted de Bruijn models.

## 4. Retained boundaries and open bridges

**Remark 1 (counterexample to a prospective inference, not an author error).** Closed counted syntax, even at fixed `(u,b)` and fixed size, does not certify termination. The terms `Omega=(lambda x. x x)(lambda x. x x)` and `N=lambda x. lambda y. (((x x) x) x)` each have two abstractions, three applications and four variable leaves, hence size `5+4s`. `N` is beta-normal. `Omega` contracts to itself; in the local weak contextual relation its children are abstractions, so the only available contraction is this root self-loop. Thus it has no normal-form path. The same remains true under full beta because its lambda bodies contain only variable applications. The reports do not assert the invalid termination inference; this example records the boundary on using their counts as a computational certificate.

**Open question 2 (semantic and arithmetic bridge).** A usable computational substrate still needs a fixed syntax encoding, a capture-avoiding substitution/step representation with its exact reduction semantics, a sound and complete finite-history or halting interface, and an ordinary-input universality connection for that same semantics. A proposed Diophantine compiler must then charge all syntax, selector, history, range and positive-domain operations. None of these obligations is discharged by the reviewed count or binding-depth theorems. In particular the distinct contextual-beta and weak-call-by-value models cannot be silently identified.

**Open question 3 (analytic validation remains separate).** This is a scope-limited substrate triage, not a full proof audit of Reports 109–110. Their analytic claims and supplementary finite-check audit claims retain their original validation status. No new flaw in those analytic theorems is alleged, and no experimental or formal-verification claim is added by this review.

The appropriate research disposition is to retain these reports as syntax-enumeration background. Continue operational compiler work from a specified reduction/acceptance interface; do not credit these counting results with a halting representation or an arithmetic-complexity improvement.
