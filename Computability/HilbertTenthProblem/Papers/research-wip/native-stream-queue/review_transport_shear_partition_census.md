    q-F-v*r=1-F-(v-1)r<=0.

## Uniform exact degrees

All ordinary and witness variables have degree one; compiler numerals have degree zero. The exact unchanged factor and comparison polynomials are inherited by the literal ancestry check, with their nonzero homogeneous leaders proved in the pinned asymmetric and first-root notes. These include both normalized and ordinary auxiliary norms, coupled and uncoupled linear units, the computed auxiliary-gap ordinate, and the retained ordinary strong and linear residuals.

Only the transport leader changes. Writing Q=(B-1)J for its degree-one part, the new leader is

    T2=w*(Q-F-Z-alpha-ell*x)-t*Q.

Its `wF` coefficient is -1, so it is a nonzero polynomial uniformly on every valid fixed-program slice. Its exact degree is two. The first-root leader is `-(w*s²*k*Q⁷)²`; the old gap-root leader is `w*s²*k*Q⁷*(2g-k)`. Both are nonzero polynomial expressions for admissible B>1. A factor such as `2rho-delta` may vanish on a particular numeric line without being the zero polynomial; the uniform degree claim concerns the full independent variables.

Products of nonzero polynomials have additive degree. The highest homogeneous parts of real squares cannot cancel. Therefore, if group degrees are d_i and ordinary residual degrees are e_j, the exact whole degrees are

    SOS: 2*max({d_i},{e_j}),
    anchor a: d_a + 2*max({d_i:i!=a},{e_j}).

The maximum of an empty squared-residual set is zero, giving degree d_a for pure product-minus-one. This proof handles tied maximal groups without a unique-leader assumption and does not substitute equations valid only at zeros.
