# Scoped mathematical review of the new hat-query threshold report

The arrival at `afd7ffabb` contains a self-contained proof chain for the sharp deterministic expected-query threshold2. The principal chain was read and checked as a mathematical argument, with no finding in the inspected proof. Its cost is the expected number of observed hats. ## Main proof chain and its necessary hypotheses

Each player predicts its own independent fair bit from other hats. Scores are centered individually, but can be dependent. The proof correctly avoids treating the divergence event as a tail event: after conditioning on a finite initial cylinder it discards the corresponding owners, leaving the remaining scores conditionally centered.

A common depth bound d permits at most2^d−1 possible observed coordinates per player. The symmetrized visibility graph has finite induced average degree at most twice that number, hence admits a finite coloring by finite greedy colorings and a finitely branching tree. Flipping each color class independently annihilates the orbit sum of every remaining owner's score. Cylinder-preserving flips and the cylinder-density lemma establish nullity; the analogous finite Baire intersection establishes meagerness. The argument tolerates unbounded incoming visibility and does not assume independent correctness scores. The separate self-contained, weaker Boolean sign estimate was checked, but the sharper externally attributed constant was not verified against its cited book.

A feasible path of L queries costs at least `sum(j=0..L−1)2^(−j)=2−2^(1−L)` in expectation. Repeated observations only increase a prefix probability. Thus a common expected cap C<2 gives depth at most `floor(1−log2(2−C))`; an infinite feasible path forces mean at least2. Together with the coloring obstruction this supplies the lower half of the threshold without the sharper external analytic inequality.

The partner-first block algorithm supplies the upper half. For m pairs, the complete-block surplus is −m only on the all-black assignment, +1 only for a singleton white hat on the active partner class, and0 otherwise. These events have probabilities4^(−m), m4^(−m) and the remaining mass. The active player's query tail is truncated geometric, with mean `2−2^(−(2m−2))`; the other partner observes one hat. Nonbad blocks have no negative completed pair, and unfinished pairs contribute at most1/2 in absolute value.

With `L_m=ceil(4^m/m^(1+epsilon))`,0<epsilon<=1, bad-block probabilities sum finitely while positive-block probabilities have divergent sum. Disjoint blocks give independence for the positive events. The summability argument and the prefix bound prove almost-sure divergence at every physical prefix, while individual means approach2 from below. The supplied variance/subsequence proof for independent indicator counts supports the stated growth equivalents; the physical-index estimate follows from `N_M~(8/3)4^M/M^epsilon`. The epsilon=1 schedule is computable by integer arithmetic. This establishes the stated deterministic threshold as a supremum over players, not an attained per-player cost of2.

The extension to unbounded count budgets requires eventual positivity. Sparse eligible players can be grouped into ordered blocks; the positive-budget complement receives neutral pairs, with at most two unfinished pairs across the two subsequences. No bound on query distance follows from this count construction. The separately inspected local construction needs h(i) tending to infinity. With free independent private coins, activation of the costly rule gives sufficiency for every expected cap C>1; the report correctly does not claim the optimal randomized threshold.

## Arithmetic research consequence

The finite-tree identity `f=(1+x_j)g_+/2+(1−x_j)g_−/2` controls Boolean degree by worst-case depth. It does not control the number of arithmetic gates by average query cost. At the threshold2, the successful construction expressly requires trees of unbounded depth, despite geometric tails. Its computable schedule also depends on integer powers, and its infinite success event is a probabilistic limit rather than a finite accepting computation certificate.

A useful future compilation question is whether a fixed program can check a packed finite decision tree and its evaluated path while charging tree lookup, branching and termination with fixed witness arity. The inspected list-coding discussion proposes formalization interfaces only; it does not price these obligations. None of the threshold, degree or coloring constants can therefore be entered in the universal-equation gate table. The84-operation general source and85-operation degree155 alternative are unchanged by this review.
