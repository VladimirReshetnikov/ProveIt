## Literal identity and positive inverse

Every one of the 39 actual sources has the same checked definitions

\[
 q=(B-1)J+1,\quad r=q-1,\quad X=wq,\quad
 C=q-F-Z-\alpha-\ell x,\quad U=q-F,
\]

and the transport cone

\[
 N_t=(K+wq)C+U-zr.
\]

The old supplied `zplus` has exactly one consumer, `local_rhs=zplus*repunit`; the computed `kinner=Kconstant+wn2` is private to `innerC`. Replace `zplus` by positive `transport_quotient=t` and `kinner` by `Kconstant+w`. No source row is removed, reordered or free of charge. The `wn2=w*q` row remains live elsewhere. Then

\[
 N_t'=(K+w)C+U-tr,
 \qquad z=t+wC,\qquad t=z-wC.
\]

The helper independently expands the complete local identity at independent `r,w,K,C,t,U` cuts, checks the literal definitions connecting those cuts, and proves every other factor and the entire finalizer unchanged under the shear. Both maps are inverse on all integer coordinates because `C` is independent of the quotient port.

The mathematical bootstrap applies to these grouped sources. The probe recovers the **actual complete finalizer** as a polynomial in factor ports and the retained strong/linear residuals. These selected saved schedules use either a product of all factors minus one, or a sum of squares of retained comparisons and disjoint group-product-minus-one residuals. Each factor occurs exactly once. Therefore a complete integer zero forces the transport factor to be `+1` or `-1`. No decoded-history or individual norm-sign conclusion is needed. The general nonempty integer-anchor case is not needed for these particular 39 saved schedules.

For either positive quotient `v`,

\[
 U-vr=1-F-(v-1)r\le0.
\]

The coefficient of `C` is at least2. If `C<=-1`, the transport value is at most−2, contradicting its being a unit. Hence `C>=0` at every parent or child positive zero. A child zero restores `z=t+wC>0`. At a parent zero with transport sign `epsilon`,

\[
 rt=(K+w)C+U-\epsilon
   =(K+w+1)C+Z+\alpha+\ell x-\epsilon\ge2,
\]

so `t>0`. This establishes a full positive-zero bijection **within each selected parent source**, before using any inherited native or universal theorem. The computed auxiliary ordinate in gap bases may be negative off-zero, but it is not used in this bootstrap. No cross-base witness bijection or positive-orthant map is claimed.

## Uniform exact-degree argument

Hold compiler numerals fixed and give all supplied witnesses and ordinary input degree one. Let

\[
 Q=(B-1)J,\qquad C_1=Q-F-Z-\alpha-\ell x.
\]

Only the transport leading form changes:

\[
 wQ C_1\quad\longrightarrow\quad T_2=wC_1-tQ.
\]

The new degree is exactly2, uniformly on every admissible fixed compiler slice: the coefficient of `tJ` is `-(B-1)`, which is nonzero. Every other factor and retained residual remains the identical polynomial, so its authenticated exact degree and nonzero leading form transfer unchanged.

For a group with index set `G`, its exact degree is the sum of its factor degrees. Products of nonzero real polynomials are nonzero. The complete product-minus-one degree is the sum of all factor degrees; the SOS degree is twice the maximum group or retained-residual degree. Highest terms of real squares cannot cancel. This proves both upper and lower degrees uniformly, not merely on the two numeric slices checked for each source.

The source checker derives the appropriate factor bases from actual producer definitions: first-root versus gap-root; the input modulus `Delta` versus `a+1`; normalized versus ordinary strong treatment; auxiliary ordinate versus positive gap; and coupled versus uncoupled linear form. It verifies the old exact degree against the saved record and the identified literal grouping before replacing the transport weight3 by2. Two complete modular dense univariate expansions independently attain each new exact degree and all factor degrees. Raw syntactic gate-propagation bounds are saved separately and are not mislabeled exact.
