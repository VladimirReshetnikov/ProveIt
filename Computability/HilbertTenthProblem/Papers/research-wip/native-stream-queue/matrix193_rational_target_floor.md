# Rational integral-preserving bases retain the three-gate target floor

The [integral-basis result](matrix193_integral_target_floor.md) extends to **every rational basis change that keeps the entire inherited twenty-letter group integral**. There are only two invariant rational lattice shapes up to homothety. The second shape also admits a complete zero-diagonal classification, and every surviving frame loses the group-wide first-row injectivity required by the current membership projection.

Consequently three gates remain optimal for the generic two-output target in the same model: independent supplied chi and psi, fixed integer constants, binary addition/subtraction/multiplication, and equality as polynomials in those two independent coordinates. No Pell-index or norm relation is used to lower that count.

The scope is the inherited whole group H', not just the subset of products satisfying a particular lower-target condition. A collision in H' does not itself supply a false semigroup input. Basis changes that only preserve integrality or injectivity on a smaller product set, other projections, changed alphabets, additional supplied registers, or identities restricted to Pell points remain outside the result. The exact index and unbounded membership certificate are still unpaid. No complete universal Diophantine lower bound is claimed.

## 1. Normalize the inherited group

Use the actual Gamma1(5) recoding and the normal integral frame from the pinned predecessor. In that frame the group is H, it contains

    T=[[1,1],[0,1]], U=[[1,0],[5,1]],

and it is a subgroup of Gamma1(5). Its repeated block and shifted block are

    W1=[[-14,3],[65,-14]],
    D=W1+14I=[[0,3],[65,0]], D²=195I.

Changing between the original H' frame and this frame is integral unimodular, so arbitrary rational basis changes in one correspond exactly to arbitrary rational basis changes in the other. Since the additional conjugation by U uses an element of H, it does not change the group H.

Let S be any invertible rational matrix such that S inverse H S consists of integral matrices. All determinants are one. Its column lattice Lambda=S Z² is therefore H-invariant, and conversely every such invariant lattice gives an integral conjugate. Invariance means equality: the group contains inverses.

## 2. Classify the invariant rational lattices

Every full-rank rational lattice has a triangular basis

    (s,0), (r,t), with s,t>0 and 0<=r<s.

For completeness, scale the lattice to an integral one. Its intersection with the horizontal axis and its projection to the vertical axis are nonzero cyclic groups, generated by s and t. Choose a lattice vector over t and reduce its horizontal coordinate modulo s. This gives the stated basis; scaling back preserves rationality.

After homothety by s, write

    B=[[1,q],[0,n]], 0<=q<1, n>0, q,n rational.

The two necessary invariance conditions are

    B inverse T B = [[1,n],[0,1]],
    B inverse U B = [[1-j,-jq],[5/n,1+j]], j=5q/n.

Thus n, 5/n, j and jq are integers. In particular n is a positive integer dividing5, so n is1 or5.

If n=5, then j=q is integral and 0<=q<1, forcing q=0. If n=1, write j=5q. Then j is one of0,1,2,3,4, while jq=j²/5 is integral. Since5 is prime, j=0 and again q=0. Hence, up to homothety,

    Lambda=Z² or Lambda=diag(1,5) Z².                 (1)

These necessary possibilities are both sufficient for all of H, not only for T and U. Every H matrix has integral entries and its lower-left entry is divisible by5. Conjugation by A5=diag(1,5) multiplies the upper-right entry by5 and divides the lower-left entry by5, leaving an integral determinant-one matrix. The standard lattice is invariant as well.

Any rational S with the stated property can therefore be written

    S=c A_n M, with c nonzero rational,
    A_n=I or A5, M in GL2(Z).

The scalar cancels in conjugation. It remains to consider integral unimodular bases in precisely two frames. The first is the complete integral case already proved in the predecessor. The second has

    H5=A5 inverse H A5,
    T5=[[1,5],[0,1]], U5=[[1,0],[1,1]],
    D5=A5 inverse D A5=[[0,15],[13,0]].

## 3. Classify all zero-diagonal bases in the second frame

Suppose M is integral unimodular with determinant eta=±1 and

    M inverse D5 M=[[0,b],[c,0]].

Then bc=195. A right sign change of the second basis vector changes both b and c, so assume c>0 initially. If the first column of M is (p,r), the second is D5(p,r)/c. Taking determinants gives

    Q(p,r)=13p²−15r²=c eta,
    c in {1,3,5,13,15,39,65,195}.                     (2)

Complete modular images give the following unrestricted exclusions:

The [receipt](matrix193_rational_target_floor.json) saves every residue in each of the fourteen finite images. Absence from an image excludes all integer p,r, not just bounded representatives.

If Q=13, reduction modulo13 gives r=13h. Equation(2) becomes p²−195h²=1, and the forced second column gives

    M=[[p,15h],[13h,p]]=C.

If Q=−15, reduction modulo3 and modulo5 gives p=15h. Then r²−195h²=1 and

    M=[[15h,r],[r,13h]]=C J,
    C=[[r,15h],[13h,r]], J=[[0,1],[1,0]].

Thus, including the omitted sign of c, every zero-diagonal frame is

    M=C K, K in {I,E,J,JE}, E=diag(1,−1),
    C=zI+hD5, z²−195h²=1.                             (3)

Conversely these matrices are unimodular and give zero diagonal, so the classification is exact.

Every integral norm-one unit z+h sqrt(195) is a signed power of 14+sqrt(195). Indeed, a positive unit greater than one has h>=1 and z>=14; the smallest possible value is 14+sqrt(195), which is itself a unit. Multiplication by an appropriate power of its inverse reduces any positive unit to the interval [1,14+sqrt(195)), where that same bound leaves only1. Negation and inversion cover all remaining signs and sizes.

Writing W5=A5 inverse W1 A5, the fundamental matrix is

    14I+D5=−W5 inverse.

Therefore C lies in ±H5. Its conjugation preserves H5, even if the central sign itself is not a group element. From(3), every zero-diagonal basis yields one of the groups

    H5, E inverse H5 E, J inverse H5 J, (JE) inverse H5 (JE).

The first two contain a nonidentity lower unipotent coming from U5; the last two contain one coming from T5, since J exchanges the two directions. Identity and that lower unipotent have equal first rows. Every second-frame zero-diagonal basis therefore fails whole-group first-row injectivity.

Together with the predecessor's first-frame classification, this proves the failure for every rational integral-preserving zero-diagonal frame.

## 4. The same two-gate reduction applies

In any admitted rational frame the repeated matrix is integral, since it belongs to the integral conjugate of H. Hence its shift Dnew=Wnew+14I has integral entries:

    Dnew=[[u,v],[w,−u]], u²+vw=195.

In particular v is a nonzero integer. The first row of the inverse even-block power is

    (chi+28u psi, 28v psi).                            (4)

If u=0, Section3 and the inherited first-frame result exclude whole-group first-row injectivity. If u is nonzero, both outputs require gates, since 28u and 28v are nonzero integers of magnitude at least28. A single binary gate from the initial ports and fixed constants cannot produce the first output's two linear terms with that psi coefficient. In a circuit with at most two gates, the first gate must therefore be the second output, and the last gate must add or subtract it from chi. This forces u=±v. A nonlinear intermediate cannot evade this argument: with only two gates and two required nonwire outputs, that first intermediate must itself equal the linear second output.

The integral lower shear

    L=[[1,0],[−u/v,1]]

then makes both diagonal entries zero. Its first row and that of its inverse are (1,0). Thus conjugating any group by L multiplies each old first row on the right by the invertible matrix L; it preserves both injectivity and its failure. A two-gate injective frame would consequently give an injective zero-diagonal frame, contradicting the complete two-lattice classification.

The original H' frame attains three gates and already has the required injectivity. Thus the rational extension retains an attained three-gate floor in exactly the declared model. The requirement that the whole inherited group remain integral is essential to this reduction; integrality of a smaller set is not silently substituted for it.
