## The actual theorem and the challenged step

Fix the normalized source, its two-register recorder, primes 2/3/5/7/11,
and the existing strict binary-five compiler. Each of 233 incoming collision
pairs has two possible static history-bit assignments. On a supplied finite
enabled normalized trace with common initial data/history and clean work,
assigning bit zero to each pair's first encountered incoming edge minimizes
cumulative CA arrival time at every corresponding normalized boundary.
This compares separately compiled rules, not equal-time configurations.
Exactly 2^227 assignments minimize the common clean-startup family; the six
startup first encounters are fixed. The theorem does not compute first
encounters or halting behavior for an arbitrary unbounded input run.

For the literal physical counter update `c -> c+delta`, the compiler gives

\[
 \tau=\lambda+|(c+\delta)^2-c^2|,\quad
 \lambda=72D+115+4J=36684691.
\]

Enabled decrements require `c>=1`; zero-update rows cost one CA microedge.
Therefore the full path clock is
\(\lambda M+TV(A^2)+TV(B^2)+Z_0\), with `Z0` counting zero-update rows.
This is total variation along the path, not an endpoint potential. The path
`0 -> 1 -> 0` has variation 2 and zero endpoint square difference.

The important possible loophole is the recorder comparison when the larger
history takes bit zero and the smaller takes bit one. For histories `h+d`
and `h`, `d>=1`, match the first `h` transfer iterations, skip the extra `d`,
and host the smaller bit-one preparation in the first larger doubling
iteration after skipping its `W>0,W-`. The encoded input ratio at the matched
preparation is `11^(d-1)>=1`. After the extra doubling iterations both work
counters equal `h` and the history gap is `2d-1>=1`. The remaining operations
match at ratio at least 7. All paired operations have the same prime and
symbol and are enabled, and all unmatched larger operations cost positively.
The boundary case `d=1,h=0` also works; it requires no strict input ratio at
the preparation. The strict excess comes from the unmatched operations.

For the other bit pairs the same transfer/doubling injection is direct.
Whole prime-macro clocks are increasing on their enabled input domains,
including test floor transitions. This proves strict greater recorder cost
for a larger history and propagates the first differing bit's delay. The
argument does not require alignment of individual physical-counter rows
inside different prime macros.

Common `lambda` cannot follow just from a row count. The proof correctly also
tracks the multiset of destination-restorer primes: swapping bit entries
permutes their incoming operations and therefore their restorer requirements.
Private entries are disjoint, so this local invariance remains true after any
set of flips. The fixed size parameters are 122,622 controls, 141,561 literal
rows, 66,066 moving rows, 75,495 zero-update rows and `J=0`; the resulting
`D=509508` and `lambda` are orientation-independent. This intake checks the
argument and arithmetic, but does not independently rebuild those full tables.
