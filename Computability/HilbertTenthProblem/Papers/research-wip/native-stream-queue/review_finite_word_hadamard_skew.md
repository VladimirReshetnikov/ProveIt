The central-band proof is valid for every externally fixed integer `n>=2`. Equality of two exponents gives `n(i-i')=(n-1)(j-j')`; since `|j-j'|<n`, coprimality forces equality of both index pairs. Thus every binary coefficient of the product is zero or one, so the lower tail cannot carry into the extracted band. Writing the exponent as `L+n(i-j)+j` shows that an off-diagonal term is below `L` when `i<j` and at least `L+n` when `i>j`. The diagonal terms occupy exactly `L+i`. This proves the identity without a finite-test extrapolation or a large-radix assumption.

The positive integer extraction also passes. Bit hats give bits zero or one through `(t-1)(t-2)=0`. All four Horner loaders are actual paid rows: the ordinary binary inputs, the spacing-n first word, and the reversed spacing-(n-1) second word. The fixed-n decomposition offset `PQ+P+1` compensates the low, middle and high positive hats. With strictly positive integer hats and slacks, the two bounds force `0<=low<P` and `0<=h<Q`; `high>=0`. The product equation and Euclidean uniqueness recover the middle remainder. Canonical remainders produce all positive witnesses, including when either input, the low remainder or the high quotient is zero. Inputs outside the n-bit range have no accepted witness.

The domain of the external output port is essential. It is strictly positive `Hhat=1+h`, while `x,y` may be zero. I independently checked the excluded-domain tuple `n=2,x=y=3,Hhat=0,low_hat=2,high_hat=3,low_slack=1,output_slack=5`, with all bit hats 2: the full polynomial vanishes. It falsely gives `h=-1` and is excluded by the theorem's `Hhat>0` hypothesis. The author added the general all-ones version before freezing. This is a necessity example, not a counterexample to the claimed positive-domain relation. The exact relation is over integer coordinates; no real-witness extraction claim follows.
