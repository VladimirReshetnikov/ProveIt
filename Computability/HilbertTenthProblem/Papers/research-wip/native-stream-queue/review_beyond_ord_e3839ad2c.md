## Computational and mathematical assessment

**Finite syntax does not make arbitrary coefficients effective.** The first two articles use finite supports or finite hereditary trees whose labels are sets or ordinals. Their comparison constructions are finite structural operations relative to those labels and their supplied order. The third explicitly says that no natural-number program can contain an arbitrary ordinal coefficient (2037-2086). The fourth separates mathematical presentations, relative comparison, recursively presented coefficient languages, and proof-carrying constructors (1946-1992). This distinction prevents their “finite computation traces” from being misread as uniformly bounded integer witnesses, a Turing decision procedure for arbitrary ordinal data, or a paid Diophantine circuit.

**Uniform semantics is a hypothesis, not a free evaluator.** The selected diagonal arguments use an admitted uniform relation coding the fibers and an available well-order of their index class. An external enumeration of formulas does not provide their joint interpretation. The truth-jump argument read in Beyond_Ord_Research648-698 adds full truth for the specified external inaccessible-rank structure. The finite-level evaluation discussion in the first article is a schema for each fixed standard level. Neither licenses a single internal truth evaluator in the unchanged language. The papers also retain the distinction between set-valued recursion on finite codes and class-valued ETR, including when the index is only omega.

**A genuine targeted proof lead is the choice-free power argument.** I checked the complete standalone proof in beyond_ord426-504, together with its set-subset lemma215-257. The greedy construction chooses unique least next exponents and coefficients from already well-ordered admitted classes. An infinite run would produce, by set-valued recursion and Class Replacement, a forbidden descending sequence of selected exponents. It does not invoke arbitrary predecessor choice from a class with no minimum. The alternate set-subset proof correctly uses least-rank set slices and their unique order minima. This local challenge found no defect.
