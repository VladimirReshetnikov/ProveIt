# Exact input periods for the independent-gamma87 relaxation

The unresolved [independent-gamma87 source](complete75_independent_gamma87_alias.md)
has an exact input-extension criterion on each fixed genuine outer/main
history. It includes both parity branches of the input Pell index. On a
history whose marker is an ordinary odd power, only the odd branch can
occur, and the criterion becomes a single divisibility condition on the
difference of ordinary inputs.

There is also a uniform obstruction class determined by the ternary digits
of the genuine main index. This is stronger than the previous sufficient
CRT test at the out-of-range R=11 example. It does **not** construct a
rejected ordinary input accepted by the full87 source, establish its
universality, or improve the complete 75-operation certificate /
88-operation polynomial bounds.

The [checker](complete75_independent_gamma87_period.py) and
[receipt](complete75_independent_gamma87_period.json) are a successor audit;
the parent arithmetic source is unchanged.

## 1. Genuine parameters and the complete two-branch criterion

Fix all the outer and main coordinates of a genuine compiled history.
The unchanged proof gives

    q=2^t>=16, R>=3q+1, R=3 mod4, X=2^R,
    Y=s*q^3, a=Y(X+1), A=a+2, Delta=A^2-1, H=4a+3,
    u=2d*x+b>0, b odd, -q<W<q.

In particular a is even, because q divides Y, and 3 divides a, because
R is odd and 3 divides X+1. Thus Delta is odd, A is even, and 3 divides
the odd number H. These deductions use the genuine parameters; they do
not hold for every freely chosen small a.

The only remaining input equations in independent-gamma87 are

    kappa=u+delta*Delta,
    mu=W+a*kappa+rho*H,
    mu^2-Delta*kappa^2=1,                  delta,rho>0.       (1)

Positivity of mu follows before classifying this norm: Y>=q^3 and X>0
give a>q, while kappa>0 and W>-q give
mu>-q+a*kappa+rho*H>0. The parent main/sign proof needs only a positive
main quotient gamma, which the independent positive sigma supplies.
No gamma>rho comparison remains.
Consequently kappa=psi_A(v), mu=chi_A(v) for a positive integer v. As in
the parent proof, with E_A(v)=chi_A(v)-a*psi_A(v),

    E_A(v)=2^v mod H,
    psi_A(v)=v mod Delta       if v is odd,
    psi_A(v)=A*v mod Delta     if v is even.                (2)

Let

    O=ord_H(2), g=gcd(2Delta,O).

On this domain the exact criterion for positive delta,rho in (1) is:

* W modulo H belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by 2;
* for its discrete logarithm j modulo O,

      j=u mod g  OR  j=A*u mod g.                          (3)

The first branch gives odd v and the second gives even v. The criterion
is independent of the representative chosen for j.

To prove necessity, multiply the even congruence in (2) by A, using
A^2=1 mod Delta. Since Delta is odd, u is odd and A*u is even, including
parity gives exactly

    v=u mod 2Delta   OR   v=A*u mod 2Delta.                (4)

The power congruence is v=j mod O. CRT gives (3). Conversely each
compatible pair of congruences has arbitrarily large positive solutions
v. Choose v>max(u,|W|,2). The quantities

    delta=(psi_A(v)-u)/Delta,
    rho=(E_A(v)-W)/H                                     (5)

are positive integers and solve (1). Here psi_A(v)>=v and the positive
recurrence gives E_A(v)>2^v for v>=2. This proves sufficiency with the
required strict positivity, rather than only modular compatibility.

Since 3 divides H, O and g are even. Also 2^j=(-1)^j mod3. Thus the
two branches in (3) are disjoint: W=2 mod3 selects the odd branch,
W=1 mod3 selects the even branch, and W=0 mod3 is impossible. In
particular a small positive odd-power marker never uses the even branch.

This is a mathematical characterization, not an added low-cost circuit
for computing an order or a discrete logarithm.
For a freely chosen signed W outside the genuine width bounds, the
same equivalence is asserted only with the additional requirement
mu>0. Without that requirement a negative Pell root could occur. The
positive-extension direction (5) is valid for any fixed signed W.
For example a=12,u=3,delta=4,rho=1,W=-20381 gives kappa=783,
mu=-10934 and norm one, although W modulo51 is19, outside the subgroup
generated by2. The checker retains this out-of-domain scope counterexample.

## 2. Exact ordinary-input aliases on one compiled history

Start with a positive parent88 zero at input x0. Its genuine marker is

    u0=2d*x0+b, W=2^u0, 0<u0<R.

Move to another positive ordinary input x, and make only the following
outer-coordinate change:

    alpha_new=alpha_old+2d*(x0-x).                         (6)

Require alpha_new>0. This preserves

    C=q-F-Z-alpha-2d*x,

and therefore preserves W, the packed R, every other outer quantity,
all main/strong/auxiliary coordinates, and the seven factors other than
the input norm. Replace the parent sigma by gamma=rho_old+sigma_old,
as in the established positive forward map to87. The new input delta
and rho can then vary independently of every other factor.

The candidate has a positive input extension on exactly these fixed
outer/main data if and only if

    g divides 2d*(x-x0),
    equivalently  m_alias divides x-x0,
    m_alias=g/gcd(g,2d).                                  (7)

Indeed the discrete logarithm of W is u0 modulo O. Its odd parity
eliminates the second branch, so Section 1 gives exactly (7). Sufficiency
constructs the remaining positive coordinates by (5); it is a full
source extension of this conditional fixed history. The width condition
(6) also preserves the required upper bound on the new ordinary input.

One can always choose the new v>R. Then rho exceeds the fixed gamma:

    rho-gamma=[E_A(v)-E_A(R)+2^R-2^u0]/H>0.

Thus the old same-coordinate positive inverse is unavailable even when
x=x0; that fact alone does not imply an incorrect input language.

Equation (7), together with (6), is necessary and sufficient for this
particular history-preserving transfer. It does not classify all outer
histories for a given input. To refute the full candidate one would
still need an actual compiled parent history and a rejected x satisfying
both conditions. No such complete false-input instance is asserted here.

## 3. A uniform ternary-index obstruction

Write r0=(R-1)/2, so r0 is odd. The genuine half-binomial formula is

    Y=(sum_(j=0)^r0 binom(2r0,r0+j)*X^j)/2,
    X=2^R, a=Y(X+1).                                    (8)

It implies the exact classification

    a=6 mod9
      iff r0=0 mod3 and every ternary digit of r0 is 0 or 1;
    otherwise a=0 mod9.                                 (9)

For completeness, X=-1 mod3 and the alternating upper-half identity
give

    2*sum_(j=0)^r0 (-1)^j binom(2r0,r0+j)=binom(2r0,r0),
    Y=binom(2r0,r0)/4 mod3.

The alternating identity follows by pairing the two halves of the full
binomial expansion at -1. Over the field of three elements,
(1+z)^(3^i)=1+z^(3^i). Expanding by ternary digits therefore gives the
usual digitwise binomial coefficient rule. If r0 has a digit 2, at its
lowest such digit the addition r0+r0 has a carry, making that coefficient
zero modulo3. If all digits are 0 or1, no carry occurs and the coefficient
is (-1)^(number of1 digits)=2 mod3, because r0 is odd.

If the last digit of r0 is0, then R=1 mod6 and X+1=3 mod9. A nonzero
Y residue is2, giving a=6 mod9. If the last digit is1, then R=3 mod6
and X+1=0 mod9. If any digit is2, Y=0 mod3. This proves (9).

For every genuine history in the first class, 9 divides both H and
Delta. Since ord_9(2)=6, necessarily 6 divides g. Thus an odd-power
marker alias must satisfy

    u=u0 mod3, or d*(x-x0)=0 mod3.                       (10)

When 3 does not divide d, ordinary inputs in different classes modulo3
cannot share that fixed history through the87 input extension. This is
an unconditional exclusion **within the stated genuine-index class**;
it does not assert that every compiled history is in that class.

The class is compatible with the native numerical range: R=491515,
t=5, q=32 has R=3 mod4, 3q+1<=R<q^4, popcount(R)=17=3t+2, and its
r0 satisfies the ternary condition. This numerical fixture does not
supply masks, transport, or a compiled computation, and is not called
a complete history.

## 4. The complete local obstruction from powers of three

Let d3=v3(Delta), e3=v3(H). The component of the CRT condition obtained
by retaining only the modulus 3^e3 is

    3^h divides u-u0, h=min(d3,e3-1).                    (11)

The order formula ord_(3^e)(2)=2*3^(e-1) follows by induction from
v3(4^n-1)=1+v3(n), itself obtained by expanding (1+3)^n and then cubing.
Consequently (11) is necessary and sufficient for compatibility of
the two congruences at this local power-of-three modulus. Other prime
factors of H may impose further conditions in the full g.

Because H=4(a+3)-9, the genuine residue classes from (9) give:

| Genuine a modulo27 | h | Necessary ordinary-input condition |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 0,9,18 | 0 | No added power-of-three condition |
| 6 | 2 | 9 divides d*(x-x0) |
| 15,24 | 1 | 3 divides d*(x-x0) |

For a=6 mod27, d3=2 and e3>=3. For a=15 mod27, d3=e3=2.
For a=24 mod27, d3>=3 and e3=2. For 9|a, d3=e3=1.
These establish the table even when a higher valuation is unbounded.
In particular the table is not an assertion that v3(g)=h: orders at
other prime factors of H can contribute additional powers of three.

## 5. A conditional factorization class with a small sufficient period

A useful contrasting sufficient condition is H=3p with p prime. No
such factorization is proved here for an actual complete history.
Under this condition, (9) forces 9|a: the other native residue would
make 9 divide H and hence p=3, impossible for these parameters.
Write a=3z; z is even and divisible3. Then

    p=4z+1, Delta=3*(3z+1)*(z+1),
    gcd(2Delta,p-1)=6.                                  (12)

Fermat's theorem and the factor3 show that p-1 is a period of two
modulo H. Thus the true g is either2 or6, and (7) certainly holds
whenever

    3 divides d*(x-x0).                                 (13)

If 3|d it holds for every x allowed by the positive width. This gives
a precise factorization target for future attempts; it is not evidence
that such a target occurs in the enormous native numbers. No uniform
small bound on g for all genuine histories has been derived.

## 6. Executable evidence and scope

The source audit checks every direct consumer of x, alpha, delta and
rho. It then compares all seven unchanged factors and the full output
on 512 signed assignments under (6), allowing independent changes of
delta and rho. These are all-assignment identities, not claimed zeros.

An independent direct Pell recurrence runs over complete joint periods
for twelve small free parameters and compares every tested W residue
with the two-branch criterion. Separate materialized input components
check strict positivity for both branches and negative representatives
of W. The checker verifies the alternating-binomial and ternary tests
on 256 native indices, the local valuation table, and the conditional
3p period formula on small numerical examples. None of those small
components is promoted to a complete compiler tuple.

Normal execution compares the deterministic receipt; --write-receipt
regenerates it. The result sharpens the unresolved87 question to an
exact fixed-history period and width test, with a provable exclusion
class. It neither resolves full87 universality nor states a general
operation-count lower bound.

Independent root and native-controller proof/source reviews passed, as
did fresh receipt replays. They checked the genuine-domain positivity
before Pell classification, both CRT branches, exact alpha/seven-factor
transport, the odd-r0 ternary argument, and the distinction between local
and full order obstructions. A separate native-controller implementation
checked 127,722 second-order recurrence steps, 7,029 residue comparisons
and 33 exact integer native Horner/local-three cases. The negative-root
scope fixture was added during review and the final default replay passed.
No complete false-input witness or new universal arithmetic bound is
inferred from these audits.
