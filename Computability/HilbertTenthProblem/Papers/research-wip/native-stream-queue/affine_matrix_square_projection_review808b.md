## What the report establishes

For an affine integral curve `C+nD` that is unimodular identically, `C` is
unimodular and `N=C^-1 D` is nilpotent. If its nilpotency index is `k+1`, the
evaluation subgroup generated by `U(n)=I+nN` is free abelian of rank `k`, with
the exact integral basis `U(1),...,U(k)`. The exponent vector is

`lambda_i(n) = binomial(n,i) binomial(k-n,k-i)`.

Finite logarithms and interpolation at `0,...,k` prove this for every integer,
including negative inputs. The proof correctly keeps the integral lattice,
rather than merely its rational span. Any `k+1` distinct exponent vectors are
affinely independent. After an accepted point `n0` is chosen, subgroup
membership is precisely membership of `lambda(n)-lambda(n0)` in the
intersection lattice. If that lattice has rank `r<k`, there are at most `r+1`
accepted inputs. If it has full rank, the hit set is exactly periodic.

The sharp universal period for quotient exponent `e` is
`P_k(e)=product_{p|e} p^(v_p(e)+floor(log_p(k)))`. The Newton-basis change is
unimodular. The simultaneous binomial-divisibility argument handles negative
integers using inversion in the truncated ring; it does not use a floating
point logarithm. Arbitrary prescribed nonempty finite sets of size at most
`k` are realized by the translated curve and a rank `|F|-1` subgroup.
The supplied-lattice certificate has a unique integer witness, or a unique
natural signed-pair witness after disjoint-support constraints.

The commuting-polynomial extension is sound: simultaneous triangularization
and the constant determinant force all normalized coefficients into a common
nilpotent algebra. Logarithmic degree is at most `D(d-1)`. A separating linear
functional gives the finite bound in the deficient-rank case. Pointwise
unipotence alone is correctly excluded from the hypothesis.

The report carefully distinguishes a fixed set being recursive from computing
that set from arbitrary subgroup generators. Its nonconstant all-or-empty
family would solve an undecidable fixed subgroup-membership problem if the
intersection lattice could always be extracted.

The degree-two universal loader reconstructs prior repository work. The
graph-group retraction preserves precisely the selected commutators; the
Mihailova fibre product is finitely generated; and the shear-conjugate free
family permits arbitrary finite presentation rank with fixed images of the
two input letters. Effective Higman embedding remains an external theorem.
The report does not print a numerical universal subgroup or an unbounded
Diophantine matrix-word checker. Thus this proves a sharp *input degree* in
the specified interface, not a universal-polynomial operation improvement.

The 6-dimensional multiplication block and the `6g+2` circuit construction
have the claimed integer zero-set bijection. Original Diophantine witnesses
are still counted separately; four-square substitution does not preserve
their multiplicity. The one-witness decidability result applies only to the
printed cyclic-block model, where all conditions reduce to finitely many
univariate quadratics.

## A checked square-block reduction

If a circuit gate is `z=x^2`, its old blocks are

`T(2x,u)` and `T(0,u-2z+x)`.

The second is a power of `P=I+E12+E23` exactly when `u=2z-x`.
Delete that block and coordinate and retain only `T(2x,2z-x)`.
Its membership equation is `4(z-x^2)=0`. The pencil remains jointly affine
and unimodular on every integer assignment. On every old zero the erased
coordinate has this unique value, so projection and restoration give a
bijection of the complete integer zero sets.

The polynomial identity is stronger than zero equivalence: after restoring
`u=2z-x`, the old second residual vanishes identically, and the first residual
is exactly the retained one. Hence the full old sum of squares equals the
new sum of squares on the restoration graph, even off the zero set.

For `g` multiplication gates including `s` squares, the structural bounds
become dimension `6g-3s+2`, `2g-s` added coordinates and `2g-s+1` residuals,
still of degree at most four. The printed three-gate example goes from
dimension 20 to 17, six added coordinates to five, and seven residuals to
six. Syntactically different affine operands can be recognized as equal by
combining their coefficients. No total operation count is asserted: affine
form evaluation and general circuit sharing still need a charged compiler.

The companion independent checker verifies 392 canonical projection pairs,
1,960 signed full-polynomial identities, exact symbolic residual identities,
2,175 independently solved finite-hit cases, 2,736 period checks, 672 excluded
proper period divisors, eight affine-independence determinants and 56 large
integer interpolation cases. It also checks 25 malformed circuit calls.
Public circuit operations revalidate causality, so a mutable gate list cannot
bypass the dependency check. Low-level linear-algebra helpers are tested on
their stated exact, independent-column inputs; they are not general validated
serialization interfaces.

## Primary dependencies checked

The current [Mikaelian abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04347) states an
explicit embedding algorithm for effectively enumerated presentations. The
review checks this interface, not an execution or full formal verification of
that algorithm. [Bogopolski and Ventura](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.0690)
provide the cited Mihailova-subgroup context. The finite-generation step used
here also has a direct proof in the report. Integer interpolation, the exact
period formula, affine independence and the circuit identities were checked
directly from the supplied proofs; no external publication-priority claim
has been verified.
