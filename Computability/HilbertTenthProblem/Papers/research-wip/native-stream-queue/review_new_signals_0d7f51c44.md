## What is useful next, and what is not established

Reports 64–65 supply a precise interface worth retaining for a future arithmetic design: ordinary shifted counter inputs; a fixed physical table for all their values; a uniform ordered section; explicit guarded branching; and an optional exact clock. The missing step is an actual fixed-arity integer history representation with all initialization, control, chronology and branch checks charged. Five live signals, 19/39 speeds, 32/54 contacts, and 30D time do not by themselves bound the arithmetic work of that representation. The state/rule alphabet still depends on the fixed program. No numerical universal improvement should be entered from these counts.

The native-gap construction makes the initialization obligation particularly clear. Its primitive integer gap height is Θ(2^m), where m is the larger initial counter; the gap bit height is Θ(m), exponential in that counter's own binary length. It pays the two power relations in the bounded certificate and explicitly has infinitely many full Pell-adapter witnesses, although the decoded counters and finite trace are unique. This is neither an efficient witness-height guarantee nor a fixed-fold unbounded representation.

The real-input results are genuinely stronger than a bare failure of semialgebraicity, within their stated model: a non-F_sigma real validity set cannot be a countable union of semialgebraic projections obtained by fixing finitely many integer witnesses. That argument requires agreement on all positive real gaps. It does not transfer to a discrete rational encoding or to ordinary integer inputs, for which Reports 58–60 supply positive existential certificates. No oracle, post-Zeno interpretation, or arbitrary real primitive was included in this review's arithmetic routing.

No specific contradictory theorem/interface statement requiring an author correction was found in the selected passages. This does not certify omitted chronology, Pell proofs, source DAGs, simulation tables, archived tests, or inherited source17/18 dynamics. Published internal audit labels and fixture counts remain historical assertions. The strongest reviewed scope boundaries are useful independently of whether all those full proofs later pass: local versus invariant realization; full-section versus projected maps; periodic supplied words versus branching computation; fixed-horizon versus fixed-arity arithmetic; and real versus integer input domains.
