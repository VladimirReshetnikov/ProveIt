## 2. A radius making every sphere root positive

Let A range over the finitely many distinct **nonzero absolute values of doubled retained entries**. Assign each A a distinct prime p_A≡3 modulo4, with p_A≠3 and p_A∤2A. Such a finite assignment is effective: test successive integers7,11,15,… by exact trial division, skipping used primes and divisors of A. There are infinitely many primes3 modulo4. An elementary proof constructs4 times the product of any finite purported list minus1; some prime factor is3 modulo4 and is outside the list. Excluding the finitely many prime divisors of any fixed2A does not obstruct the assignment.

Compile T by the simultaneous fixed congruences

    T≡1 (mod2),  T≡0 (mod3),
    T≡A+p_A (mod p_A²)  for every nonzero A.

Their moduli are pairwise coprime. Choose the least simultaneous residue, then add a multiple of the full fixed CRT period until T>max A. Thus T is positive, odd, and divisible by3. All new T/L/CRT numerals depend only on the fixed matrix table, never the ordinary input x, the chosen duration h, or any supplied variable.

For each nonzero A, put n_A=T²−A². Then

    n_A≡2A p_A (mod p_A²),

so its p_A valuation is exactly1. Consequently n_A cannot be a sum of two integer squares: modulo a prime3 modulo4, u²+v²≡0 forces p_A to divide both u and v, hence their square sum would be divisible by p_A². The familiar assertion that−1 is not a square follows directly by raising a hypothetical square root to the power p_A−1.

On the other hand T is odd and every doubled entry A is even, so n_A is positive and congruent1 or5 modulo8. Legendre's three-square theorem gives n_A as a sum of three integer squares. None of the three coordinates can vanish, since that would be a two-square representation. Taking absolute values therefore gives **three strictly positive integer roots**.

For a zero entry, use the explicit positive decomposition

    T²=(2T/3)²+(2T/3)²+(T/3)².

No zero entry occurs in this numeric fixture, but this branch makes the recipe uniform over the inherited fixed-context family. The only external existence theorem used here is the [three-square criterion stated in the Archive of Formal Proofs entry by Danilkin and Chevalier](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Three_Squares.html). The entry states that a natural number is a sum of three squares precisely when it is not of the form4^a(8k+7).
