## Independent mathematical challenge

Write `s_m(x)=sin(2*pi*m*x)` for a **positive integer** m. For a continuous real period-one mask satisfying `a(x)+a(x+1/2)=2`, the literal doubling transfer operator gives

```
(T_a s_m)(2x)
 = [a(x)+(-1)^m*a(x+1/2)]*s_m(x)/2.
```

If m=2n, this is s_m(x), so `(T_a s_m)(y)=s_n(y)`. Distinct positive sine harmonics are linearly independent; consequently this cannot equal lambda*s_m for any real lambda, including zero. There is no exceptional even mode hidden in normalization.

If m is odd, the same formula becomes `(a(x)-1)*s_m(x)`. The eigenfunction equation is therefore

```
[a(x)-1-2*lambda*cos(2*pi*m*x)]*s_m(x)=0.
```

The nonzero set of s_m is dense, and the bracket is continuous. Thus it vanishes everywhere. Conversely `a=1+2*lambda*cos(2*pi*m*x)` satisfies both normalization and the required eigenfunction identity for odd m. The argument never divides at a sine zero; no finite Fourier support or evenness hypothesis is needed. Evenness is a conclusion for a mask with such an eigenfunction.

Cosine attains both endpoints of [-1,1], so the minimum of the classified mask is exactly `1-2*abs(lambda)`. This gives strict positivity precisely for `abs(lambda)<1/2`, nonnegativity precisely for `abs(lambda)<=1/2`, and zeros at equality. At lambda=0 the constant mask one annihilates every odd sine harmonic. Retaining this exception is essential: the statement excluding two harmonics is explicitly for nonzero eigenvalues.

For distinct positive odd m,n, nonzero eigenvalues lambda,mu would force `lambda*cos(2*pi*m*x)=mu*cos(2*pi*n*x)`. Integrating against the first cosine over one period gives lambda/2=0, a contradiction. Hence the author correctly states the stronger unequal-eigenvalue conclusion. In particular a scalar nonzero identity on the span of two or more individual sine harmonics is impossible. Reordering, rescaling or changing basis **within that same span** cannot change this fact, since a scalar identity is basis independent.

Among the inherited allowed integer dilations b>=2, the accepted b=3, q=5 identity/decrement construction therefore has minimum dilation for every encoding using the span of two distinct pure sine harmonics. This does not extend the q=5 denominator minimum: that older claim retains its finite-even, standard-frequency scope. It also does not constrain arbitrary two-dimensional subspaces generated by mixtures of several harmonics, arbitrary functions, state-dependent encodings, unnormalized operators or general eigenspace multiplicity. The author preserves each of these limits.
