# The U15 unary block cannot become parabolic in a faithful integral 2-by-2 representation

**Theorem.** Let `F(a,b)` be the free group on the two physical tape letters, and let

```
W=(ab)^3 b^2 = abababbb.
```

Every faithful representation `F(a,b) -> SL2(Z)` sends W to a hyperbolic matrix. In particular, neither W nor any positive power of W can become unipotent or parabolic by choosing a different faithful integral representation. This is a representation-independent obstruction for the fixed physical word, stronger than the current calculation `trace(Phi(W))=-8942`.

The result also holds for faithful `GL2(Z)` representations in the precise sense that no positive power of W has a nontrivial parabolic image. It does not prohibit a different source encoding, a higher-dimensional representation, or a separately proved positive-monoid encoding that is not faithful as a group representation. No Diophantine operation bound or matrix-membership certificate is supplied.

The frozen [U15 initialization note](u15_unary_block_interface.md) derives the universal family `U_S W^x V_S` on the actual finite tape interface. Its W is `01010111`; here a denotes physical 0 and b denotes physical 1. That source theorem and all contexts remain unchanged. The [193-generator construction](group_directed_semigroup193.md) uses a faithful upper encoding of the 20 active letters, including the two bits, fifteen states, two brackets and separator. Its lower marker encoding is separate.

The geometric input used here is the classical finite-type theorem and peripheral presentation, checked in Linda Keen, *Canonical polygons for finitely generated Fuchsian groups*, Acta Mathematica 115, pp. 1–16: Section 3, Theorem 2, and Section 4, equation (1) and the following standard-generator statement. The [primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02392200) gives finite-type quotient surfaces for finitely generated Fuchsian groups and generators corresponding to handles, punctures and boundary holes. Below we derive the rank-two consequence directly. We do not rely on a classification of all trace-equation solutions, a finite search over representations, or a primitive-word algorithm.

## 2. Three elementary facts about W

Change the free basis to `c=ab, b=b`. The inverse is `a=cb^-1`, so this is an automorphism of the free group. In that basis,

```
W=c^3 b^2.
```

First, W is not primitive. If it were a member of a free basis, the quotient by its normal closure would be infinite cyclic. But

```
F(c,b)/<<c^3 b^2>>
```

has a nonabelian quotient: send c to the 3-cycle `(123)` and b to the transposition `(12)`. The relation maps to the identity, while these permutations generate the nonabelian group S3. A cyclic group has no such quotient.

Second, W is not a proper power. Its abelianization in the original basis is `(3,5)`. If `W=v^k` with `|k|>1`, both entries would be divisible by k, contradicting `gcd(3,5)=1`. Conjugacy does not change this argument.

Third, W is not conjugate to a nonzero power of a commutator of any free basis. All such elements have zero abelianization, whereas `(3,5)` is nonzero. This assertion is invariant under every change of basis.

The same three arguments work for each individual published ordinary-symbol block

```
A_i=(ab)^(8i-5) b^2, i>=1.
```

Put `r=8i-5`, so r is odd and at least three. Killing `c^r b^2` admits the nonabelian quotient generated by an r-cycle and a transposition in the symmetric group on r letters. Its abelianization `(r,r+2)` is primitive and nonzero. Thus changing which single ordinary symbol represents unary data does not evade the result. No corresponding assertion about every arbitrary concatenation of different blocks is needed here.

## 3. Why a parabolic would contradict these facts

Let rho be faithful into `SL2(Z)`. Its projective image is still faithful: if a word maps to `-I`, its square maps to I, contradicting injectivity and torsion-freeness of a free group. The image in `PSL2(R)` is discrete because its matrices are integral. It is a torsion-free, non-elementary rank-two Fuchsian group.

Suppose rho(W) were parabolic. A maximal parabolic cyclic subgroup gives a cusp peripheral in the quotient surface. Truncate that cusp and every other end to obtain a compact orientable core. If the core has genus g and b boundary components, then `b>=1` and

```
rank pi_1 = 2g+b-1 = 2.
```

The two possibilities are `(g,b)=(0,3)` and `(1,1)`.

For a sphere with three holes, choose two boundary loops u,v as a free basis. Its peripheral loops are conjugate, up to inversion, to `u`, `v`, and `uv`. Each is primitive. Every parabolic element is a nonzero power of one of these maximal cusp generators.

For a torus with one hole, the sole peripheral loop is conjugate, up to inversion, to `[u,v]` for a free basis u,v. Every parabolic element is a nonzero power of it.

The second case contradicts W's nonzero abelianization. In the first case, the absence of proper powers reduces W to a primitive element, which Section 2 excluded. Hence rho(W) is not parabolic.

Faithfulness also excludes the identity and finite-order elements. In a discrete subgroup of `PSL2(R)`, an elliptic element has finite order. The remaining type is hyperbolic. Hyperbolic elements and all their positive powers have absolute trace greater than two. Over the integers this gives `|trace(rho(W))|>=3`, without claiming a sharp minimum for this particular word.

## 4. Allowing determinant minus one does not help

Let rho instead be faithful into `GL2(Z)`. Projectivization again remains injective. Matrices of negative determinant act on the hyperbolic plane by orientation-reversing isometries; the orientation-preserving subgroup has index at most two. It is finitely generated, so its quotient has finite type by the same theorem. Taking the finite quotient gives a finite-type surface for the full image, possibly nonorientable. Torsion-freeness excludes reflections with fixed points, so this quotient is a surface rather than an orbifold with mirror boundary.

If the quotient is orientable, Section 3 applies. Otherwise, assuming a parabolic exists, its compact core has k crosscaps and b boundary components, with `k>=1`, `b>=1`, and `k+b-1=2`. The possibilities are `(k,b)=(1,2)` and `(2,1)`.

In the first case the presentation is `r^2 t1 t2=1`. In the free basis r,t1, the two peripherals are `t1` and `(r^2 t1)^-1`, both primitive: replacing t1 by `r^2 t1` is an invertible Nielsen change. W cannot be a power of either.

In the second case the peripheral is `(r^2 s^2)^-1`. Its homology, and the homology of every power, belongs to `2 Z^2`. Every basis change preserves this sublattice, but W has homology `(3,5)`, outside it. This case is also impossible.

Finally, an orientation-reversing matrix M of infinite order cannot acquire a parabolic positive power. Since `det(M)=-1`, Cayley–Hamilton gives

```
trace(M^2)=trace(M)^2+2.
```

If `trace(M)=0`, then `M^2=I`, contrary to faithfulness. Otherwise its integer trace is nonzero, and M squared is hyperbolic. Its nonzero powers stay hyperbolic. Together with the orientation-preserving argument, this proves the stated `GL2(Z)` extension.

## 5. What this closes, and what may still change

Conjugating the current representation preserves trace. An automorphism of the free tape alphabet preserves the primitive, power and commutator distinctions above. More generally, any faithful representation of the entire active state/tape alphabet restricts to a faithful representation of its two tape generators. Thus assigning new integral 2-by-2 matrices to the other states, brackets and separator cannot avoid this obstruction for W.

In particular, no such recoding gives the existing eight-gate affine target component for this exact repeated block, even after replacing the input family by a fixed positive power of W. If a hyperbolic matrix M had all entries of `L M^x R` polynomial in x for fixed invertible context matrices L,R, multiplication by the inverse contexts would make all entries of `M^x` polynomial. The trace on even x instead grows exponentially, a contradiction. This is an obstruction to a witness-free polynomial straight-line loader, not to an arithmetic certificate with additional witnesses.

A representation change can still reduce the hyperbolic eigenvalue and coefficient sizes. That is distinct from making the matrix parabolic. A proposed two-letter representation must also extend injectively to all active letters before the directed-semigroup proof transfers; choosing two small matrices alone does not supply that extension. A separate recoding packet may establish such an extension and rebuild the full arrays.

A different physical input word would require its own U15 initialization theorem. Replacing the bit block by a new atomic symbol requires a revised source and compiler; it is not a change of constants in the current binary interface. Dropping group faithfulness also requires a replacement proof of the exact positive-word implication used by the semigroup reduction, plus the marker and state-alphabet obligations. No claim here rules out that broader route. Higher-dimensional integral representations and nondiscrete real or complex representations are outside this theorem.
