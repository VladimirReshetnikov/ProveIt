## Review observation1: computable legal arrays need not have computable certificates

This deduction was suggested by root and independently checked here; it is an interface clarification, not a source defect.

Use Gamma=Z with its computable height table. Arrange that every machine's halting time is at least1. Define the binary array X by `X[n,e]=1` for e>=0 exactly when machine e first halts at step n, and set all negative-exponent entries to0. Each entry is decidable by bounded simulation, uniformly in n,e. Its union support is contained in N and each exponent has at most one active row, so X is strongly summable. Its sum coefficient at e is exactly the halting characteristic function. A uniformly computable sequence of real sum coefficients would decide halting by approximation to error less than1/4, and therefore cannot exist.

Any uniformly computable integer incidence certificate m_e for this X would decide halting by simulating machine e through step m_e. A uniformly computable real certificate would do the same: a precision1 rational approximation to m_e supplies an effectively computed integer upper bound, for example the nonnegative maximum of0 and the ceiling of that approximation plus1. If the machine had not halted by that bound, the certificate excludes a later halt. Thus this computable legal array has no uniformly computable incidence-certificate sequence of either kind. Its support bounds can all be0, so the obstruction is entirely incidence.

With effective finite windows and the computable integer height table, the article's finite regularizations of X are computable and converge. They have no effective coordinatewise convergence modulus that would make their limiting incidence bounds uniformly computable. A convergent computable sequence of names is therefore weaker than an algorithm producing its limit.

## Review observation2: no uniform existential-integer legality compiler on arbitrary computable arrays

For each machine e, let `Y^(e)[n,0]=1` exactly when e has halted by step n, with every other exponent coefficient0. This is a uniformly total computable binary family. Its union support is always contained in the singleton{0}; it is strongly summable exactly when e never halts, because a halt produces infinitely many nonzero entries in that one column.

Suppose a uniform effective process turned arbitrary indices of computable arrays into finite existential Diophantine recognition problems that accepted exactly the strongly summable arrays. Apply it to these uniformly total indices. Enumerating integer witnesses and evaluating the resulting finite polynomial equations would enumerate nonhalting machines, a contradiction. The same argument applies to a fixed polynomial with effectively computed ordinary-integer parameters. No appeal to an effective version of the boldface completeness theorem is needed.

This excludes the unrestricted recognition interface just stated, not computations on already certified inputs, finite-support promises, infinite certificate sequences, or other restricted support languages. It establishes no numerical lower bound on any paid finite compiler for a restricted language.
