## Proof and interface challenge

The fixed setting is `Z <= Gamma <= Q`, real coefficients, and supports
finite below every real bound. The allowed group consists of
coefficient-Borel **order-preserving** field automorphisms. Unordered
automorphisms, higher rational rank, arbitrary Hahn supports and
non-Borel derivations are expressly outside the main theorem.

- **Field and automatic regularity, lines 184–366.** Fixed-support slices
  are Polish products, but the valuation topology of the full field is
  not treated as Polish. Borel additive coordinate functions are real-linear
  and depend on finitely many inputs on each slice. The Baire generic-support
  argument limits the number of nonzero output functionals below a cutoff.
  A sequence violating global valuation continuity can be placed in one
  left-finite slice, yielding the required contradiction. Continuity then
  preserves the explicitly defined summable families. Rational monomial
  differentiation and summation show that every Borel derivation is `aE`.
  None of these steps presupposes a common denominator for the whole support.

- **Coordinate group and scalar rigidity, lines 367–538.** Ordered
  automorphisms fix real constants and scale the rational value group by
  `r in U_Gamma={r>0:r Gamma=Gamma}`. Positive rational-root uniqueness
  gives the substitution description. The tangent substitution inverse
  has a proved uniform positive gain; its infinite inverse is not assumed
  admissible without support control. For a nonconstant scalar h, the
  real standard part is removed only when h is finite. For negative
  valuation its constant coefficient is not an invariant. The signed
  rational root has leading exponent `q/kappa(q) in U_Gamma`, so it is an
  allowed coordinate. Fixing the canonical nonconstant scalar forces
  fixing t and then the whole field. This proves uniqueness as well as
  orbit classification. The chosen function kappa on a countable exponent
  set is Borel; it is not thereby an effective algorithm.

- **Named pair and tuple classification, lines 539–647.** The map
  `(a,b) -> (h=b/a, j=aE(h))` is equivariant and invertible on the
  noncommuting locus. The scalar coordinate gives the exact coefficient
  `A=Theta_h^(-1)(j)/E(H)`. Its second formula agrees with the chain rule
  and the conjugation orientation. Canonical pairs normalize to themselves,
  and the ratio proves their stabilizers trivial. This establishes an
  actual Borel cross-section and the unique witness
  `Theta_(h2) Theta_(h1)^(-1)`. For countable ordered tuples the first
  noncommuting index pair is an invariant Borel choice. These arguments
  do not transfer uniqueness to unmarked subalgebras or unordered tuples.

- **Nonsmoothness contrast, lines 648–703.** On the positive Euler line,
  conjugacy is multiplication by U_Gamma. When its logarithms are dense,
  invariant sets with the Baire property are meagre or comeagre. A countable
  separating family for a purported complete invariant would make it
  constant on a comeagre set, contradicting the countable orbit fibres.
  The commuting-pair embedding preserves this obstruction. This is a
  descriptive-set-theoretic distinction, not a running-time lower bound.

- **Finite-dimensional Lie classification, lines 704–866.** A finite
  real span has exactly as many distinct coefficient valuations as its
  dimension. Brackets at different valuations have leading exponent p+q
  with nonzero coefficient proportional to q-p. Repetition excludes two
  distinct positive or two distinct negative exponents, giving the listed
  spectra and dimension at most three. The two-dimensional basis correction
  and ratio normalization yield the affine algebra; the three-dimensional
  coefficient comparison yields the split projective algebra. The exponent
  orbit and its sign are retained. No analytic local conjugacy theorem is
  silently substituted for the stated formal coordinate group.

- **Whole-field actions and BCH, lines 867–981.** The map from additive
  real time to the positive rational value-group multipliers is trivial
  by divisibility and ordinary prime valuations. With the explicitly
  assumed coefficient derivatives and Borel generator, negative gain is
  therefore impossible. The affine substitutions are globally admissible
  for p>0, and their group law is checked on the rigid scalar `t^(-p)`.
  Coefficientwise analytic dependence follows from finite cutoff sums.
  The BCH expression is a real closed formula, while its complex Taylor
  series has the separately stated radius; zero-gain flows are not treated
  as valuation-convergent operator exponentials.

## New review-side theorem: a smooth orbit problem can conceal nonhalting

This deduction was proposed by root and independently challenged by this
reviewer and Aristotle. It is **not** a result attributed to the manuscript,
and does not refute its Borel cross-section or effective-cutoff question.
The exact input representation is part of the theorem.

Fix `Gamma=Z`, so the field is the real Laurent-series field and
`U_Gamma={1}`. Use a standard effective enumeration of machines, with a
mandatory initial step so any first halting time is a positive integer.
For the machine with index e define

```
A_e = 1 + sum_(n>=1) [e first halts at step n] t^n,
P_e = (A_e E, t A_e E),
P_* = (E,tE).
```

Here e determines a **total coefficient program** uniformly: for exponent
0 return 1, for negative exponents return 0, and for positive n simulate
exactly n steps and test whether the first halt is at n. Every A_e is
promised to be a polynomial with at most two nonzero rational coefficients,
valuation 0, and leading coefficient 1. Its terminal degree is not supplied
and cannot uniformly be recovered. This is a finite code for a coefficient
oracle, not an explicit finite coefficient list.

Both derivations are Borel and their bracket coefficient is

`A_e E(t A_e)-t A_e E(A_e)=t A_e^2`,

which is nonzero. Thus the entire indexed family satisfies the promised
noncommuting hypothesis. Its ratio is t, its scalar normalizer is already
the identity, and its normal coefficient is A_e.

If an allowed automorphism conjugates P_e to P_*, ratio equivariance forces
it to fix t. For completeness, the needed rigidity in this special case
has a direct proof. A coefficient-Borel additive field map fixes R by the
measurable Cauchy equation and preservation of 1. It therefore fixes every
finite Laurent polynomial. For any Laurent series f, its truncation P_N
through exponent N has `|f-P_N|<t^N`. Order preservation and g(t)=t give
`|g(f)-P_N|<t^N`, hence `|g(f)-f|<2t^N` for all sufficiently large N.
A nonzero Laurent series has a least exponent and cannot satisfy all these
bounds. Thus g is the identity.

It follows that

```
P_e is conjugate to P_*  iff  A_e=1  iff  machine e never halts.
```

The reverse implication uses the identity automorphism. On this explicit
uniform family of integer program indices, conjugacy to the fixed pair is
therefore co-c.e.-complete; nonconjugacy is c.e.-complete. Failure can be
semidecided by finding the positive coefficient that records the halt.
This is not a complexity assertion about every unspecified presentation
of computable polynomials.

There can be no uniform effective procedure turning these program indices
into finite ordinary existential Diophantine conditions whose solvability
is equivalent to conjugacy. Such a procedure, followed by enumeration of
all integer or natural witness tuples, would semidecide nonhalting. In
particular no fixed ordinary existential Diophantine relation on these
indices represents this conjugacy slice.

This does not concern polynomials supplied as explicit finite lists, where
the equality A_e=1 is decidable. It does not preclude restricted presentations
with a supplied support endpoint, algorithms with stronger oracles, or the
manuscript's non-effective Borel classification. The normalizer is computably
the identity on the entire family, including all cutoff approximations.
The obstruction is **exact equality of the invariant represented by a
program**, not finding the normalizing coordinate. No paid compiler,
arithmetic saving or new universal finite-operation bound follows.
