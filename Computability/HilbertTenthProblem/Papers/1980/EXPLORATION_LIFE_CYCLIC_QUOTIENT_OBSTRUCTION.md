# A limit on cyclic and bounded-phase Life quotients

This note rules out a specific proposed simplification of the
[periodic Life preimage interface](EXPLORATION_LIFE_PERIODIC_PREIMAGE.md).
A single cyclic quotient cannot represent every positive instance. The
same is true of a fixed number of cyclic phase planes when their common
shift commutes with the physical translations. This is a statement about
these translation encodings, not a lower bound for arbitrary arithmetic
encodings or for complete certificates.

## 1. Positive targets that need two independent periods

For any integer p>=5, define the periodic configuration

    x_p(i,j)=1 iff i mod p and j mod p both belong to {0,1}.

This consists of separated 2-by-2 live blocks. It is a Life still life:
each live cell has three live neighbors, while each dead cell has at
most two. Thus x_p itself is a totally periodic preimage of x_p.

Its exact period lattice is p Z^2. Indeed, projecting the live set on
either coordinate gives {0,1} in Z/pZ. A translation preserving this
set has residue 0: its image of 0 forces residue 0 or 1, and residue 1
would take 1 to 2. Consequently a period (a,b) must have p|a and p|b.

If z is *any* preimage of x_p, every period of z is a period of x_p,
because Life commutes with translations. In particular, if z is given
on a finite quotient G=Z^2/Lambda, then

    Lambda is contained in p Z^2,
    G maps surjectively onto (Z/pZ)^2.                         (1)

This applies to all preimages of this positive target, not just the
displayed still-life preimage. Passing to a larger finite cover retains
(1).

## 2. A single cyclic quotient is impossible

A quotient of a cyclic group is cyclic, whereas (Z/pZ)^2 is not cyclic
for p>=2. Hence (1) rules out every cyclic G.

In particular, an affine position encoding

    (i,j) maps to a*i+b*j modulo T

has cyclic image and cannot represent x_p with the correct physical
translations. Choosing different packing weights does not fix this.
Nor does a unimodular change of lattice coordinates: it preserves
p Z^2 and the noncyclic target quotient.

For a skew torus with period generators (m,0),(s,n), compatibility with
x_p requires p|m, p|s and p|n. Its Smith invariant gcd(m,s,n) is therefore
at least p, so this compatible skew torus is still noncyclic. A shear
that makes the quotient cyclic necessarily loses this target. Allowing
arbitrary shear, larger periods, or a different preimage does not evade
the implication (1).

## 3. A fixed number of commuting cyclic planes is also insufficient

Suppose an encoding has the following properties:

* Its cell set is one transitive finite quotient G of the physical Z^2
  translations. Equivalently, it is one finite periodic lattice, with
  the usual translation action on its cells.
* It has a permutation sigma, representing the common within-plane
  shift, that commutes with both physical translations.
* Sigma has at most K cycles. Each cycle is one cyclic plane; physical
  translations may permute the planes and apply constant offsets.

Any permutation commuting with this regular translation action is
itself a translation. Write g=sigma(0). For every h in G, commutation
gives sigma(h)=h+g. Thus the number of cyclic planes is

    [G:<g>].

Under the surjection (1), <g> maps into the cyclic subgroup generated by
its image g_bar. This gives a surjection

    G/<g> -> (Z/pZ)^2/<g_bar>.

Every element of (Z/pZ)^2 has order at most p, including when p is
composite. Therefore

    [G:<g>] >= p^2 / order(g_bar) >= p.                       (2)

Taking p>max(K,4) gives a positive Life target that this encoding cannot
represent. The obstruction covers a fixed number of equal-size phase
planes with commuting shifts, including constant phase permutations
and offsets. It does not cover position-dependent routing that fails
the commuting-shift hypothesis, unboundedly many planes, nonlinear
encodings, or gadgets that change the simulated local problem.

The lower bound p is sharp for x_p: on G=(Z/pZ)^2 translation by (1,0)
has exactly p cycles. This is a bound on cyclic planes in
the specified representation, not a paid-operation lower bound.

## 4. A conditional escape by changing the acceptance problem

A different periodic tiling problem might permit arbitrary independent
padding of an accepting computation rectangle. The exact sufficient
property is the following: positive inputs have valid accepting
torus presentations of every size M>=M0, N>=N0, while negative inputs
have no accepting periodic presentation. These rectangles have matching
opposite edges and pass all local checks across both seams, so they
repeat to valid plane configurations; open-boundary rectangles would
not suffice. This property is **not established here** for Life or for
a particular universal tiling construction.

Under this hypothesis, choose an integer m large enough and dimensions
M=m+1, N=m. The quotient is cyclic, since gcd(M,N)=1. An explicit
isomorphism is

    (i mod (m+1), j mod m) maps to m*i+(m+1)*j modulo m(m+1).

It is well defined, and injectivity follows by reducing modulo m and
m+1 separately. It therefore preserves both physical translations and
every local neighborhood, including multiplicity. In radix B the two
translation multipliers are W=B^m and B*W, so they share one variable
stride. This avoids two unrelated variable powers at that particular
indexing step. The two cyclic word shifts are multiplication by W and
B*W modulo B^(m(m+1))-1.

For soundness of a translation-invariant local tiling relation, the
cyclic length need not equal m(m+1). Given any length T>=1 and a word c
whose local neighborhoods at every k use offsets

    k + m*dx + (m+1)*dy modulo T,

the pullback z(i,j)=c[m*i+(m+1)*j modulo T] is a valid periodic plane
configuration for the same local relation. It has periods (T,0),(0,T).
The map onto cyclic positions is surjective, since (-k,k) maps to k,
so a marked symbol or finite marked event expressed using these offsets
in c lifts to the plane.
The converse construction from an accepting rectangle only needs the
particular length T=m(m+1) supplied by the padding hypothesis. It is
therefore unnecessary to enforce that equality solely for soundness.

If an independent kernel has established q=512^T, a stride W=512^m,
with 1<=m<=T, can itself be enforced by positive d,v with just

    W*v=q;        W-1=511*d.

These use 2M+1A. The first equality makes W a power of two, the second
uses the order 9 of 2 modulo 511 to make its exponent a positive
multiple of 9. The other stride is 512*W; the cost of that product or
its absorption into a local convolution is separate. These three
geometry operations do not enforce a prescribed Life target.

There is still no complete certificate count: the required padding
and universality theorem, accepting marker, raw input interface,
scalar tile predicate, convolution, and packed bounds need exact
proofs and paid schedules. A generic periodic tiling theorem does not
imply this padding property; fixed macrocell scales can themselves
force a common factor into both periods. The lemma identifies a
possible change of machinery rather than repairing the all-Life-target
encoding rejected by Sections 1-3.

## 5. Reproducible bounded checks

[The checker](../verification/explore_life_cyclic_quotient_obstruction.py)
verifies the still-life and exact-period claims on a finite range,
checks every cyclic-generator candidate on bounded compatible skew
tori, and verifies the consecutive-dimension isomorphism and all eight
neighbor offsets. Its receipt is the adjacent JSON. The general proofs
above do not rely on extrapolating these finite checks. No complete
certificate or new operation-count improvement is claimed.

Author and independent complete scoped proof/source review pass. A fresh
read-only verifier run exactly matches the saved receipt. The checks cover
21 still-life targets, 5,495 period tests, 108 skew tori and 11,676 cyclic
generators; the constructive lemmas have 41,600 consecutive-torus neighbor
checks, 76,032 arbitrary cyclic lift checks and 2,724 stride tests.
