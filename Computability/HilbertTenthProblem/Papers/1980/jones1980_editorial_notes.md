# Editorial notes: Jones, *Undecidable Diophantine Equations* (1980)

James P. Jones, "Undecidable Diophantine Equations", *Bulletin (New Series)
of the American Mathematical Society* **3** (1980), no. 2 (September),
pp. 859–862. DOI 10.1090/S0273-0979-1980-14832-6. Received March 5, 1980.

This document compares three objects:

- **the scan**: `original/1980/jones1980.pdf`, four printed pages 859–862;
- **the naive OCR**: `original/1980/jones1980.tex`, the Mathpix OCR of the scan;
- **the reconstruction**: `Papers/1980/jones1980_corrected.tex` (and its PDF),
  the edition's reading of the intended mathematical content.

It accounts for every discrepancy between the naive OCR and the
reconstruction, and says why the reconstruction reads as it does. It is the
single editorial account of this article; a dated log of revisions and
checks is `Papers/EDITORIAL_NOTES.md`, whose register IDs (such as
`80-R4-01`) are cited below where a row of that log concerns an entry. Page
numbers below are those of the original printing unless stated otherwise.

The article is a four-page announcement: three universal systems
(Theorems 1–3), a table of (unknowns, degree) pairs (Theorem 4), and a
proof-theoretic corollary of the operation count $o=100$ (Theorem 5). It
gives no proofs. The full derivations appeared in Jones, *Universal
Diophantine Equation*, J. Symbolic Logic 47 (1982), 549–571 (edition in
`Papers/1982/`; cited below as "1982"), whose Theorems 1–3 are the same
systems. Several corrections below rest on the derivations there.

## 2. Systematic differences (described once)

These differences occur throughout. None changes the content, and they have
no entries in the register.

1. **Reflow and pagination (LAYOUT).** The article is newly typeset
   (11pt, letter paper, four pages). The line breaks, hyphenation and page
   breaks are new. Forced page breaks before Theorem 1 and before Theorem 3
   (with `\enlargethispage`) keep each of those systems on one page. They
   are not the original page breaks. The running heads read "JAMES P.
   JONES" / "UNDECIDABLE DIOPHANTINE EQUATIONS" (scan: "J. P. JONES" /
   "UNDECIDABLE DIOPHANTINE EQUATIONS"). The byline "BY JAMES P. JONES" is
   set as "JAMES P. JONES", and the address block is set in small caps
   rather than full capitals.
2. **Original pagination marks (EDN; log 80-R4-01).**
   `\origpage{n}` puts a bold `[n]` in the margin of the line on which
   page *n* of the Bulletin printing begins. The marks are accurate to the
   line, not the word. [859] is at the first body paragraph (title page).
   [860] is inside "The existence of such | a universal polynomial". [861]
   is inside "one exponential | function appear". [862] is at "A different
   measure of size". All four were checked against the scan.
3. **Edition apparatus (EDN).**
   The reconstruction adds the journal line (volume, number, date, pages,
   which the scan prints above the title and the OCR omits), a DOI link, and
   an edition notice. The notice says that the text is a corrected,
   continuously revised transcription, that the bounds are those of 1980 and
   not a survey of later work, and what the margin numbers mean. It names
   this document as the account of every discrepancy, and says that a dated
   log of revisions and checks is `Papers/EDITORIAL_NOTES.md`. The header
   comments of the source say the same ("% Continuously revised edition;
   last source revision 24 September 2026." and "% A dated log of revisions
   and checks is ../EDITORIAL_NOTES.md.").
4. **Front-matter footnote (EDN).** The scan's p. 859 footnote
   ("Received by the editors March 5, 1980. AMS (MOS) subject
   classifications (1970). Primary 02F25, 10N05; Secondary 10B15. © 1980
   American Mathematical Society 0002-9904/80/0000-0406/$02.00") is kept
   word for word. It is moved from the middle of the paragraph where
   Mathpix placed it (`\footnotetext`) to the end of the article. The code
   is labelled "Original publication code".
5. **Citations and macros (LAYOUT).** Numeric citations are generated by
   `\cite`. The bibliography order (Baker, Davis–Putnam–Robinson, Jones 1978,
   Matijasevič 1970, Matijasevič 1977, Matijasevič–Robinson, Siegel)
   reproduces the original numbers [1]–[7]. A double citation "[2], [4]" is
   rendered "[2, 4]". The name is encoded by the macro `\Matijasevic`
   (`Matijasevi\v{c}`), which keeps the source's transliteration. The OCR's
   preamble (`mhchem`, `stmaryrd`, footnote overrides), raw `$$` displays
   and hard `\\` breaks are gone.
6. **Mathematical typesetting (LAYOUT).** The variable $l$ is
   set as $\ell$ everywhere. No new variable is introduced. Implicit
   products are written without spaces (`el g^2` → `e\ell g^2`). $\Leftrightarrow$ is
   set as $\Longleftrightarrow$. The displayed systems are rebuilt with
   `gathered`/`aligned`, and long lines are split at algebraically valid
   points. Separating commas and a final period are placed consistently
   between and after display equations. The two formulas for $r$ (Theorems
   2 and 3) are kept identical. Theorem statements use a theorem
   environment, and the systems are displayed after the statement. The
   28 unknowns of Theorem 3 are listed in a display. The Theorem 4 list
   of "$\nu=\dots,\ \delta=\dots$" pairs becomes a two-column-pair table
   headed $\nu$, $\delta$, keeping the original pairing and order.
7. **Bibliography formatting (LAYOUT).** Titles are in italics,
   volumes in bold, page ranges use en dashes, and initials are
   non-breaking. Substantive bibliographic changes are register entries
   1980-23 to 1980-26.
8. **Punctuation (LAYOUT).** Commas are normalized without
   changing any mathematical condition:
   - Commas removed between a subject and its predicate, or before an
     appositive formula: "problem, were taken" (p. 859), "every recursively
     enumerable set, $W$ can", "the exponential relation, $y=2^x$ is",
     "the r.e. sets, $W_1, W_2, \ldots$, can be listed", "the binary
     relation, $x\in W_v$, is r.e.", "every r.e. set, by mere change",
     "a new equation, $v=\ldots$" (p. 860).
   - Commas added: "$z,u,y$, instead" (p. 860), "As stated above, our", "λ,
     and four parameters", "$b=2^w$, appears", "λ, and the four parameters",
     "$5^{60}$; however, the equation" (p. 861), "summing squares, one",
     "Alternatively, one", "$\delta$, is, however, very large", "Via Gödel
     numbering, the" (p. 862), "become, in effect, an r.e. set", "As with
     $\nu$ and $\delta$, it".
   - Lists "$z, u$ and $y$" and "$z, u, y$ and $x$" are set as
     "$z,u,y$" and "$z,u,y,x$".
9. **Emphasis (LAYOUT).** The scan
   italicizes the defined terms *universal* ("there must exist *universal*
   diophantine equations", p. 859) and *operations* ("indicated arithmetical
   *operations*, $o$", p. 862). The OCR loses both, and the reconstruction
   does not restore them. The operation symbol $o$ is set in math italics.

## 3. Discrepancy register

Entries follow the order of the original. The "OCR" line quotes

### Page 859

**1980-01 — Greek ν (number of unknowns) versus Latin v (index).** OCR.
- *Location:* p. 859, first paragraph, the displays of Matijasevič's
  theorem and of the universal polynomial; p. 861, the paragraph before
  Theorem 4 and the Theorem 4 table.
- *OCR:* Latin `v` throughout: `P\left(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{v}\right)` (twice
  in ¶1), `\exists x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{v} P\left(x, x_{1}, x_{2},
  \ldots, x_{v}\right)`, "the unknowns $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{v}$",
  `U\left(x, v, x_{1}, \ldots, x_{v}\right)` (text and display),
  "the total number of unknowns, v.", "v can be reduced", "$v=9$",
  "for $\delta$ and $v$", and all sixteen `v=` in the table.
- *Scan:* two distinct letters. Greek ν (pointed at the base) is the arity
  or number of unknowns. The rounded italic *v* is the index parameter.
- *Reconstruction:* $x_\nu$, $U(x,v,x_1,\ldots,x_\nu)$, "unknowns, $\nu$",
  "$\nu$ can be reduced", "$\nu=9$", "$\delta$ and $\nu$", and the table
  column head $\nu$. Latin $v$ is kept where the scan has it: $W_v$, the
  second argument of $U$, "parameter $v$", and $v=((zuy)^2+u)^2+y$ (p. 860).
  The OCR already had $\nu$ on p. 862 ("As with $\nu$ and $\delta$").
- *Justification:* the scan shows this directly. The two letters also play
  different mathematical roles. The arity of $U$ is fixed, whereas its
  index parameter varies; reading both as $v$ would make the number of
  unknowns grow with the index.

**1980-02 — "The first steps … were taken" becomes "An essential step … was
taken".** CLAR (not marked in the text).
- *Location:* p. 859, second paragraph.
- *OCR = scan:* "The first steps toward the eventual negative solution of
  the entire (unrestricted) form of Hilbert's tenth problem, were taken in
  1961 by Julia Robinson, Martin Davis and Hilary Putnam [2]."
- *Reconstruction:* "An essential step toward … problem was taken in 1961
  by Julia Robinson, Martin Davis and Hilary Putnam [2]."
- *Justification:* this keeps the cited 1961 result without the
  categorical claim that no earlier steps existed. The comma is dropped
  under the punctuation class (§2.8).

**1980-03 — $x_2$ dropped from two existential prefixes.** LAYOUT.
- *Location:* p. 859, the displays of the Davis–Putnam–Robinson and
  Matijasevič representations.
- *OCR = scan:*
  `\exists x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{\mu} P(x, x_{1}, \ldots, x_{\mu}, 2^{x_1}, \ldots)`
  and `\exists x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{v} P\left(x, x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{v}\right)`.
- *Reconstruction:* `\exists x_1,\ldots,x_\mu` and
  `\exists x_1,\ldots,x_\nu\; P(x,x_1,\ldots,x_\nu)`.
- *Assessment:* this abbreviates the author's notation and has the same
  meaning. It is not an error correction. The later display
  $\exists x_1,\ldots,x_\nu\,U(\ldots)$ in the scan already uses the short
  form. The explicit $x_2$ could be restored without any other change.

**1980-04 — Diacritic in "Matijasevič's Theorem".** OCR.
- *Location:* p. 859, "Since there exist r.e. nonrecursive sets, …".
- *OCR:* `Matijasevic's Theorem` (no caron). *Scan:* "Matijasevič's"
  with the caron, as everywhere else in the article.
- *Reconstruction:* `\Matijasevic's`, which renders as Matijasevič's.

**1980-05 — "Theorem" becomes "theorem" in named theorems.** LAYOUT.

**1980-06 — "particular undecidable diophantine equations" becomes
"particular undecidable families of diophantine equations".** CLAR (not
marked in the text).
- *Location:* p. 859, fifth paragraph.
- *Justification:* undecidability concerns deciding solvability
  uniformly as a parameter varies. A single fixed equation is either
  solvable or not, so it is not an undecidable problem. The universal
  polynomial display that follows is unchanged apart from 1980-01.

**1980-07 — Grammar and wording.** TYPO.
- p. 859: "Thus a single polynomial, **in** a fixed degree" becomes "**of**
  a fixed degree".
- pp. 860–861, the statements of Theorems 1, 2, 3: "sufficient that the

### Page 860

**1980-08 — Editorial index convention before Theorem 1.** CLAR (marked:
"*Editorial index convention.*").
- *OCR = scan:* nothing. Theorem 1 follows the paragraph on Kummer's theorem.
- *Reconstruction:* a paragraph saying that in Theorems 1–3 the triples
  $\langle z,u,y\rangle$ are *admissible polynomial codes*, meaning the
  triples constructed in (4.1) of the 1982 paper with the normalization of
  its §3. This is the class for which the equivalences are proved; an
  arbitrary positive triple is not automatically such a code. The
  paragraph says that the precise coefficient convention is "recorded in
  the editorial notes (entry 1980-08)", that is, here (log 80-R4-02).
- *The convention (from 1982 §3 and (4.1)):* take the normalized
  nonnegative representing polynomial $P$ of 1982 §3 with $\delta=4$,
  $\nu=58$. For a multi-index $\mathbf i=(i_0,\ldots,i_{58})$ put
  $|\mathbf i|=\sum_j i_j$ and
  $c_{\mathbf i}=4!/\bigl(i_0!\cdots i_{58}!\,(4-|\mathbf i|)!\bigr)$. The
  *scaled* coefficients $P_{\mathbf i}$ (not in general the ordinary
  coefficients of $P$) are defined by
  $4!\,P(X_0,\ldots,X_{58})=\sum^*_{\mathbf i}c_{\mathbf i}P_{\mathbf i}X_0^{i_0}\cdots X_{58}^{i_{58}}$,
  where $\sum^*$ runs over $|\mathbf i|\le4$. Choose a power of two
  $z>1+\max^*|P_{\mathbf i}|$. Then
  $u=\sum_{j=1}^{58}(2z)^{5^j}$ and
  $y=\sum^*_{\mathbf i}(z+P_{\mathbf i})(2z)^{5^{59}-\sum_{j=0}^{58}i_j5^j}$.
  The normalization includes $P(x,0,\ldots,0)>0$ for the relevant $x$;
  positivity, not mere nonvanishing, is needed there.
- *Justification:* the 1982 edition states this scope, and the
  announcement's systems are the same systems. The paragraph restricts
  the claimed equivalence to the codes actually constructed in the proof.
  It does not claim that the equivalence fails for every other triple, and
  it does not change universality, since every r.e. set has an admissible
  index.

**1980-09 — $q=b^{560}$ becomes $q=b^{5^{60}}$.** OCR.
- *Location:* the first line of Theorem 1 (p. 860), Theorem 2 (p. 860) and
  Theorem 3 (p. 861), and the degree paragraph after Theorem 3 (p. 861):
  "however the equation $q=b^{560}$ can be replaced".
- *OCR:* `q=b^{560}` (four times). *Scan:* a raised $60$ on the exponent
  $5$, that is $b^{(5^{60})}$.
- *Reconstruction:* $q=b^{5^{60}}$ in all four places. The OCR was already
  right in the p. 862 occurrence ("modified to do away with
  $q=b^{5^{60}}$") and in "The degree is $5^{60}$".
- *Justification:* the scan shows it. The stated degree of the system,
  $5^{60}$, requires it. 1982 Theorems 1–3 have $q=b^{5^{60}}$. This is
  (U2)–(U3) of 1982, $B=b^{\delta+1}$ and $q=B^{(\delta+1)^{\nu+1}}$, with
  $\delta=4$ and $\nu=58$: $q=b^{5\cdot5^{59}}$. A comparison of every
  number in the OCR against the scan found this to be the only numeral
  discrepancy in the 1980 OCR.

**1980-10 — The single-parameter sentence.** CLAR. The rewording is not
marked; the footnote is marked "Editorial clarification".
- *Location:* p. 860, the paragraph after Theorem 1.
- *OCR = scan:* "If the reader prefers the traditional indexing with a
  single parameter $v$, he need only add to our equations a new equation,
  $v=\left((z u y)^{2}+u\right)^{2}+y$ and regard $z, u$ and $y$ as
  unknowns instead of parameters."
- *Reconstruction:* "To use indexing with a single parameter $v$, one need
  only add to our equations the equation [display]
  $v=\bigl((zuy)^2+u\bigr)^2+y$ and regard $z,u,y$ as unknowns instead of
  parameters." A footnote says that this gives a suitable effective
  one-parameter indexing, which need not coincide numerically with a
  previously chosen indexing, and that the coding is injective on
  positive-integer triples.
- *Justification:* the word "traditional" suggested that the resulting
  labels are the usual ones, which they need not be. Injectivity is what
  makes $v$ a valid index. Put $m=zuy$ and $t=m^2+u$. Since
  $1\le u,y\le m$, we have $m^2\le t<(m+1)^2$ and
  $t^2\le v=t^2+y<(t+1)^2$. So $t=\lfloor\sqrt v\rfloor$, $y=v-t^2$,
  $m=\lfloor\sqrt t\rfloor$, $u=t-m^2$ and $z=m/(uy)$. The map is
  injective but not onto; unused indices can be given the empty set. The
  product $zuy$ is correct as printed and must not be replaced by a sum
  or a Cantor pairing (see §5). Lean: `Jones1980.indexCode_injective`
  (`Lean/Diophantine/Paper1980/IndexCode.lean`).

**1980-11 — The multiplier of the third packed block: $q^4$ becomes $q^7$.**
ORIG.
- *Location:* the equation for $r$ in Theorem 2 (p. 860) and Theorem 3
  (p. 861).
- *OCR = scan:*
  `\left(2(e-z\lambda)\cdot(1+xb^5+g)^4+\lambda b^5+\lambda b^5q^4\right) q^{4}`.
- *Reconstruction:*
  $\bigl(2(e-z\lambda)(1+xb^5+g)^4+\lambda b^5+\lambda b^5q^4\bigr)q^7$,
  in both theorems.
- *Justification:* the derivation in 1982 §4, (U12)–(U24), fixes the
  exponent. The three blocks are
  $S_1=g$, $T_1=q^3-1-(b-1)\ell$;
  $S_2=e+\ell q^2$, $T_2=\theta\lambda$;
  $S_3=2(e-z\lambda)(1+xb^5+g)^4+\lambda b^5(1+q^4)$, $T_3=(b^5-2)q$.
  These are exactly the upper and lower entries of the three binomial
  coefficients of Theorem 1. The block capacities are $N_1=q^3$, $N_2=q^4$,
  $N_3=q^9$, with $N=N_1N_2N_3=q^{16}=n$. The packing is
  $S=S_1+S_2N_1+S_3N_1N_2$ and $T=T_1+T_2N_1+T_3N_1N_2$, and
  $r=S(N^2-N)+(T+1)(N^2-1)$. So the third block starts at $N_1N_2=q^7$,
  not at $q^4$. The printed 1982 paper (p. 552) has the same error, so
  the two printed papers do not corroborate each other here; the
  derivation decides. The difference between the printed and corrected
  right-hand sides is the nonzero polynomial
  $S_3(q^4-q^7)(n^2-n)+(b^5-2)(q^5-q^8)(n^2-1)$.

**1980-12 — The last term of the second bracket: $(b^5-2)q^5$ becomes
$(b^5-2)q^8$.** ORIG.
- *Location:* the same two equations for $r$.
- *OCR = scan:* `+\left(b^{5}-2\right) q^{5}\right]`.
- *Reconstruction:* $+(b^5-2)q^8$.
- *Justification:* $T_3=(b^5-2)q$ sits in the third block, at

  $T_1$ cancels against the $+1$ of $T+1$, which is why the printed
  bracket begins $q^3-b\ell+\ell$. No $-1$ may be reinserted.

### Page 861

**1980-15 — The Pell scaling exponent: $p=2ws^2r^2n^2$ becomes
$p=2ws^2r^2n^6$.** ORIG.
- *Location:* Theorem 3, p. 861.
- *OCR = scan:* `p=2 w s^{2} r^{2} n^{2}`.
- *Reconstruction:* $p=2ws^2r^2n^6$.
- *Justification:* 1982 Lemma 2.25, equations (B1), (B5), (B9), (B10),
  gives $P=2M^2U$ with $M=RY$, $Y=N^2s$ and $U=N^2w$. Hence
  $P=2(RN^2s)^2(N^2w)=2wR^2s^2N^6$. The same substitutions give the

**1980-16 — $4\alpha\gamma$ becomes $4a\gamma$.** ORIG. The text marks it
with an editorial footnote to Theorem 3.
- *Location:* Theorem 3, p. 861, the equation for $d$.
- *OCR = scan:* `d=b w+c a-2 c+4 \alpha \gamma-5 \gamma`. The scan
  clearly shows α.
- *Reconstruction:* $d=bw+ca-2c+4a\gamma-5\gamma$, with Latin $a$ (the
  Pell parameter), and a footnote saying that this is an original
  typographical error, not an OCR error.
- *Justification:* let $X_j+Y_j\sqrt{a^2-1}=(a+\sqrt{a^2-1})^j$. Both
  sequences satisfy $Z_{j+2}=2aZ_{j+1}-Z_j$, so $D_j=X_j-(a-2)Y_j$ does
  too, with $D_0=1$, $D_1=2$. Since $2^{j+2}-2a\,2^{j+1}+2^j=-(4a-5)2^j$,
  induction gives $X_j-(a-2)Y_j\equiv2^j\pmod{4a-5}$. With $d=X_j$ and
  $c=Y_j$ (from $d^2=(a^2-1)c^2+1$), the corrected equation reads
  $d-(a-2)c=bw+(4a-5)\gamma$, which imposes exactly the required
  congruence $bw\equiv2^j\pmod{4a-5}$. With $\alpha$ in place of $a$ the
  congruence is lost. This is the case $V=2$ of the general congruence
  $\chi_A(n)\equiv V^n+(A-V)\psi_A(n)\pmod{2AV-V^2-1}$, obtained by
  sending $\sqrt{A^2-1}$ to $V-A$ in the quadratic ring (1982, remark
  after Lemma 2.28). Corroboration: the 1982 paper prints $4a\gamma$
  (the JSTOR-indexed text of 1982 was consulted). The $\alpha$
  in $e\ell g^2+\alpha=(b-xy)q^2$ is correct and unchanged.

**1980-18 — The nine-unknown entry: $1.6\times10^{45}$ becomes $D_9$, with
a note.** CLAR. The note under the table is part of the text but is not
labelled "editorial".
- *OCR = scan:* `v=9, & \delta=1.6 \times 10^{45}`.
- *Reconstruction:* the cell reads $D_9$. Under the table:
  "$D_9=47216\cdot5^{58}+9728\approx1.638134073\times10^{45}<1.7\times10^{45}$.
  The printed $1.6\times10^{45}$ rounds this expression downward." The
  note also refers to "the editorial notes (entry 1980-18)" for the
  distinction between evaluating this expression and deriving it; that
  distinction is stated below (log 80-R4-02).
- *Justification:* $D_9$ is the expression reported in the full 1982
  derivation (§3). Exactly,

### Page 862

**1980-19 — "effectively axiomatizable", and the missing verb.** CLAR (not
marked in the text). The 1978 and 1982 editions make the same change.
- *OCR = scan:* "Via Gödel numbering the theorems of an axiomatizable theory
  $T$ become in effect an r.e. set, and the search for proofs the search
  for solutions of a diophantine equation." Theorem 5: "For any
  axiomatizable theory $T$".
- *Reconstruction:* "… of an **effectively** axiomatizable theory $T$
  become, in effect, an r.e. set, and the search for proofs **becomes** the
  search for solutions …". Theorem 5 reads "For any **effectively**
  axiomatizable theory $T$".
- *Justification:* the argument needs the set of theorems to be r.e. That
  holds for an effective presentation, not for an arbitrary axiom set. In
  traditional usage "axiomatizable" often already means this, so the
  change makes the convention explicit. It does not assert that the author
  meant otherwise. No consistency or soundness hypothesis is needed.

**1980-20 — "These solutions faithfully reflect the logical
complexity …".** CLAR (not marked in the text). It was first registered as
a "logical-interpretation correction"; it is classified here as CLAR.
- *OCR = scan:* "These solutions faithfully reflect the logical complexity
  of the original proof, for the entire deduction is effectively
  recoverable from the solution."
- *Reconstruction:* "A formal deduction is effectively recoverable from
  such a solution, but this recoverability does not assert preservation of
  proof length or computational complexity."
- *Justification:* effective recoverability gives no quantitative bound. In
  an effectively axiomatized theory one can recover *some* proof from any
  certificate of provability by enumerating proofs until the certified
  proposition appears. So recoverability alone says nothing about proof
  size. The replacement keeps the true half of the sentence and drops the
  unsupported "faithfully reflect" claim.

**1980-21 — The editorial footnote to Theorem 5.** CLAR (marked "Editorial
clarification").
- *OCR = scan:* no footnote. Theorem 5: "… then $P$ has another proof
  consisting of 100 additions and multiplications of integers." The
  preceding paragraph defines $o$ as "the number of additions, subtractions
  and multiplications necessary to evaluate or determine the correctness
  of [a] proposed solution" of "the equations of Theorem 3, modified to do
  away with $q=b^{5^{60}}$", and asserts $o=100$.
- *Reconstruction:* a footnote with four statements.
  - "Another proof" means an arithmetic certificate under a fixed effective
    encoding of provability. It is not necessarily a 100-step derivation
    in the proof calculus of $T$.
  - The count is of indicated operations on whole integers. It does not
    bound the lengths of the integers, the number of bit operations, or
    the work of finding a certificate.
  - The preceding definition of $o$ includes subtraction, although the
    theorem mentions only additions and multiplications.
  - "The count 100 is reproduced by counting every indicated sign of
    Theorem 3 once (powers not counted)". The footnote refers to
    `jones1980_theorem5_operations.pdf` for this reading and for
    certificates under a calculator model.
- *History:* an earlier revision of this edition ended the footnote with
  "The exact count 100 is retained as the author's reported count, not
  independently reconstructed". That sentence was replaced by the finding
  explained below, and should not be restored.
- *What "$o=100$" means (the finding on which the last sentence of the
  footnote rests):* take the seventeen equations of the printed Theorem 3
  other than $q=b^{5^{60}}$. Count every indicated $+$, $-$ and
  multiplication (juxtaposition, $\cdot$, or a numeral coefficient) once,
  and do not count exponentiation. The result is exactly 100: 33
  additions, 15 subtractions and 52 multiplications, with 44
  exponentiations not counted. By equation, in printed order, the counts
  are 6, 3, 2, 2, 2, 0, 32, 4, 3, 5, 4, 5, 3, 10, 3, 4, 12. The count was
  checked again by hand when these notes were compiled. The same rule
  gives exactly 87 for the fourteen equations of Theorem 2.12 of the 1976
  paper (the authors' 87), and 241 for the 1978 system (1.3), where the
  author reported 243. No other counting rule that was tried gives both 87
  and 100. So "modified to do away with $q=b^{5^{60}}$" means that this one
  equation is set aside, not that a polynomial replacement is counted. The
  reading is presented as evidence, not as settled. The satellite work
  found that under a strict calculator model (powers by repeated
  multiplication, only $+$ and $\times$) the printed system with the
  exponential equation deleted needs 127 operations even with sharing. So
  100 is not a strict straight-line count of the printed Theorem 3.
- *Beyond the article:* the satellite article
  `Papers/1980/jones1980_theorem5_operations.tex` and the
  `*_PROOF.md`/`EXPLORATION_*.md` files study straight-line certificates
  for modified systems in detail, with Lean formalizations under
  `Lean/Diophantine/Paper1980/` and `Paper1982/OperationCount.lean`. That
  work is not an editorial note on the article, and the article text does
  not depend on it beyond the footnote's pointer.

## 5. Readings examined and retained

Each of these readings was examined and left as printed, or changed and
then kept without a net discrepancy. They are recorded so that the work is
not repeated.

- **$a+f^2(d^2-a)$** in the last equation of Theorem 3 is correct. It is
  not $d^2-a^2$, and it is not the $f^2-a$ of the 1982 low-degree
  construction. 1982 Lemma 2.28 gives $G=A+F^2(D^2-A)$ (P5), and the remark
  after it also allows $A+F^2(F^2-A)$. The two variants are not to be
  harmonized.
- **$o$ in $(d+of)^2$** is the letter $o$, an unknown (it is in the list
  of 28), not zero.
- **$\alpha$ in $e\ell g^2+\alpha=(b-xy)q^2$** is correct. Only the $\alpha$
  of $4\alpha\gamma$ was wrong (1980-16).
- **$zuy$** in $v=((zuy)^2+u)^2+y$ is a product, not a typo for a sum or a
  pairing function. The coding is injective (1980-10). 1982 (U0) uses a
  variant ending in $+z$. Both codings are valid, and neither is required
  to agree with any earlier enumeration.
- **$\eta n^2=\binom{2r}{r}$**, not $\eta2^n$ (1980-14).
- **Retained exactly apart from typography:** $p^2k^2-k^2+1=\tau^2$,
