\section{Verification and mathematical boundary}
The new unbounded reductions are proved in the preceding sections. Finite
checks authenticate the fixed numerals, exact polynomial identities,
cancellation-sensitive degrees, literal circuit transcription, and declared
variable and operation counts. Finite tests cannot prove the unbounded
native compiler or the refutation-calibration theorem.

The inherited results are retained with their source identifiers and
ordinary proof statements. An independently kernel-checked composite theorem
for these new literal equations, or an explicit encoded object-language ZFC
derivation, is not supplied. If an inherited ordinary calibration is treated
as an outstanding theorem to be re-established, the final consistency
conclusion is conditional on it; the new algebraic reductions remain
independently proved. No assumption of $\Con(\ZFC)$ is needed to establish
an equivalence with $\Con(\ZFC)$.

\subsection{Offline replay}
The release includes the literal polynomial packets, binary arithmetic DAGs,
fixed coefficient payloads and finite reconstruction recipes, ordinary proof
texts, source pins and notices, independent audits, and this editable TeX.
Python 3.10 or later and SymPy suffice for the complete arithmetic replay;
no network access, theorem-prover installation, numerical solver, or blank
machine simulation is required. From the extracted root run
\begin{center}
\verb|python3 verify.py --output /tmp/con-zfc-replay.json|.
\end{center}
The driver authenticates the exact file inventory, works in isolated copies,
and invokes both normal and optimized Python checks explicitly. It also
replays the selected original root-fusion and parity packages without
changing them. The parity package deliberately rejects its entire driver
under optimized Python; this expected policy result is recorded separately
from its seven successful optimized algebraic auditors. It is not represented
as an optimized whole-driver pass.

The assembled replay comprises $35$ top-level commands in ten isolated
groups. Seventeen literal artifacts are rebuilt byte-for-byte in each of
two modes, giving $34$ comparisons. Six archive-integrity corruption controls
are additional to the mathematical mutation controls in the independent
audits. These counts describe the packaged replay plan, not a count of
proved theorems. The final ZIP receipt records the fresh-extraction result.

\subsection{What changed and what remains}
The new eleven-integer construction is a substantial combination of distinct
ideas, not a claim that its individual ingredients originated here. The late
intersective polynomial and full auxiliary interpolation theorem are credited;
so are the earlier trace and parity methods. The centered budget removes the
nearest-square coordinate, even strong ordinates make four radicands odd,
and fixed parity weights replace all variable separation weights. The twelve-
integer equation is the less expensive alternative. The fifteen-integer
bounded-guard absorption and the positive one-sided residue bound are separate
improvements.

The power-fusion interfaces eliminate special-function calls but still lack
an ordinary-polynomial compilation competitive with these endpoints. The
repository's direct C222 quartics and its nine-positive and ten-integer
high-degree constructions remain relevant alternatives. No result here
proves a lower bound or establishes a globally simplest equation.
