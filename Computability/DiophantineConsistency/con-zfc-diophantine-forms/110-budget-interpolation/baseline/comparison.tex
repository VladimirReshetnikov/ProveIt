\subsection{Combining the reductions}
The parity-15 reduction uses radicands $A=4a+1$, $B=4b+1$ and a nonzero
integer $C$, with weights $1,2,4$.  In an irrational squareclass the
least weighted coefficient has parity preventing cancellation.  The
primitive eight-sign norm is divisible coefficientwise by 256.
Its application must prove $Q\ne1$ before recovering the deleted guard
and retain the exceptional $Q=0$ branch.  The modulus-four reduction
instead recovers an omitted odd strong multiplier through a rational
complementary root and a private-root sign choice.  These are different
mechanisms, not equivalent free-polynomial rewritings.

Root fusion first obtains realness of the guard root, then positivity
and noncancellation, and only then rationality and integrality.
Completeness can require fresh compatible canonical Pell witnesses.
Fixed-prime separation uses signed valuations before positivity;
one cannot silently import a positive-radicand hypothesis.

The small-degree integer families retain the whole source-34 tuple:

$2^{1113}$ and $2^{666104}$, with the remaining calibrated ports.
