\section{An unrestricted-integer quartic companion}
\label{sec:integer-companion}

The direct shared-frame construction also admits a literal equation over
unrestricted integers.  Starting from its $1\,153$ nonnegative coordinates,
we obtain a polynomial
\[
 P_{\mathbb Z}\in\mathbb Z[z_1,\ldots,z_{1690}],\qquad
 P_{\mathbb Z}=\sum_{j=1}^{842}r_j^2,
 \qquad \deg r_j\leq 2.
\]
Its exact degree is four.  The delivered expansion has $21\,381$ nonzero
monomials and maximum absolute coefficient $2\,649\,643\,805\,758$.
The construction preserves solvability of the natural shared-frame
equation, and therefore inherits its equivalence with
$\neg\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})$.  The following argument treats the
domain change before applying any coordinate eliminations.

\subsection{Guarding only the original nongates}

For integers $a,b,c$, put
\[
 H(a,b,c)=a^2+b^2+c(c+1).
\]
The image of $H$ is exactly $\mathbb N$, including zero.  Nonnegativity
follows because $c$ and $c+1$ are consecutive integers.  For the converse,
let $n\geq0$.  The integer $4n+1$ is not of the form $4^u(8v+7)$, so
Legendre's three-square theorem gives a representation by three integer
squares.  Modulo four, exactly one square is odd.  Writing its entry as
$2c+1$, and the other entries as $2a$ and $2b$, gives
\[
 4n+1=(2c+1)^2+(2a)^2+(2b)^2,
 \qquad n=H(a,b,c).
\]

The frozen shared-frame system has $1\,153$ coordinates and $1\,021$
quadratic residuals.  Of the coordinates, $795$ are arithmetic gate
outputs and $358$ are nongates.  Every gate $g$ has an actual retained
residual
\[
 g-f(\text{earlier coordinates})=0,
\]
where $f$ has nonnegative integer coefficients and all its coordinates
have smaller indices in the fixed variable order.  There are $710$
multiplication rows and $85$ addition rows of this form.
The literal certificate identifies and independently checks all $795$
rows, including the coefficient signs and strict index order.

For every original nongate $x$, append three private signed coordinates
$a_x,b_x,c_x$ and the residual
\[
 x-a_x^2-b_x^2-c_x^2-c_x=0.
\]
Keep all original coordinates and residuals.  The result has
\[
 1\,153+3\cdot358=2\,227
 \quad\hbox{integer coordinates},\qquad
 1\,021+358=1\,379
 \quad\hbox{quadratic residuals}.
\]
Every natural solution extends to a signed solution by the representation
above.  Conversely, in any signed solution all $358$ original nongates
are nonnegative.  Induction along the original coordinate order then makes
every arithmetic gate nonnegative, since its defining polynomial has
nonnegative coefficients.  The original residuals still vanish.  Thus
the signed checkpoint and the frozen natural parent are equisatisfiable.

\subsection{Exact signed projection}

If an integer polynomial system contains $v-f(\mathbf y)=0$, with $f$
independent of $v$, deleting that equation and substituting
$v=f(\mathbf y)$ in every other equation induces a bijection of integer
zero sets.  The inverse restores the uniquely forced value $f(\mathbf y)$.
The same holds if the defining residual is $f-v$.  Coefficient signs in
$f$ are unrestricted: integrality is automatic for any integer
polynomial on integer inputs.

The terminal optimizer records every such restoration, with degree at
most two, and retains a step only when all substituted residuals have
degree at most two.  Affine restorations preserve this bound.  For a
quadratic restoration, the optimizer requires the removed coordinate to
occur only linearly outside its defining row.  Independently of this
proposal rule, the checker performs every substitution and directly
checks the degree of every resulting row.  At each step the restoration
uses only still-live coordinates, so reverse chronological restoration
is well defined.

The delivered signed certificate contains $537$ eliminations: $308$
affine and $229$ quadratic.  It removes $446$ original gates and $91$
original witnesses; no free coordinates are discarded.  Exactly one
residual is removed at every step.  Thus the endpoint has
\[
 2\,227-537=1\,690\quad\hbox{coordinates},\qquad
 1\,379-537=842\quad\hbox{residuals}.
\]
Its sum of squared residuals is the stated $P_{\mathbb Z}$.

The original sign constraints are preserved by this signed projection.
In particular, the checker substitutes into all affected guard equations.
Restoring every deleted coordinate recovers a zero of the complete
$2\,227$-integer checkpoint, where the earlier induction proves that all
original coordinates are natural.  This establishes
\[
 \exists\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^{1690}\ P_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbf z)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\mathbf x\in\mathbb N^{1153}\ P_{\mathrm{shared\ frame}}(\mathbf x)=0.
\]
Combining this with the direct-calculus calibration proves within ZFC
\[
 \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall\mathbf z\in\mathbb Z^{1690}\ P_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbf z)\ne0.
\]
No consistency assumption enters the transformations.

\subsection{Finite certificates and the verification boundary}

The integer companion component contains the frozen natural parent,
the $2\,227$-integer checkpoint, both conversion certificates, and both
the residual and fully expanded forms of the final polynomial.
Canonical hashes bind each transformation to its actual input and output.
The independent verifier imports neither the builder nor the optimizer.
It checks complete coverage of all original gates and nongates, all $537$
monic substitutions, every coefficient in the final expansion, the degree,
the coordinate count, and the coefficient height.  Negative controls
remove a sign guard or corrupt a restoration and require rejection.

From that component directory, \texttt{python3 verify\_integer.py} and
\texttt{python3 -O verify\_integer.py} run these checks offline.
The additional option \texttt{--rebuild} reconstructs six primary files in
a temporary directory and compares them byte for byte.  These finite
checks certify the literal algebra and counts.  The universal
proof-calculus and Pell theorems are inherited from the parent argument;
this supplement supplies an ordinary proof and exact finite certificates,
not a new end-to-end formal kernel proof.
