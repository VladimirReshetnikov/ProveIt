\begin{abstract}
We combine direct quadratic-system encoding with selective, rather than
full Sidon, separation of monomials. Nonuniform overlapping target intervals
further reduce the exponent range. A sparse signed-borrow padding replaces
a dense geometric sum; a calibrated two-block binary construction then
replaces a generic three-block carry-to-binomial conversion. Independently,
we reduce the denominator used to absorb a Pell-index quotient and seed a
square-condition guard with an already required radicand. The deliverable
contains explicit division-free polynomial circuits, fixed coefficient
recipes and payloads, ordinary mathematical proofs, and reproducible
finite certificates. None of these equivalences proves consistency, and
no global optimum or new end-to-end kernel certificate is asserted.
\end{abstract}

The accompanying source maps retain immutable links; later repository

For every validated equation below, the claimed theorem is
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash
 \left(\neg\exists\mathbf x\in D^r\;F(\mathbf x)=0\right)
 \longleftrightarrow\Con(\mathrm{ZFC}),
\]
where the domain $D$ is stated explicitly. Natural numbers include zero;
positive integers do not; unrestricted integers are a third domain.
Registers in the formulas are polynomial abbreviations, not additional
unknowns. All powers in the final expressions have fixed nonnegative
integer exponents. Square roots and divisibility appear only in proofs,
not as extra operations allowed in the delivered polynomial.

The proof direction is always constructive solvability versus a finite
syntactic ZFC refutation. The literal K215 calibration and native-window/Pell
compiler remain identified inherited mathematical dependencies. The new
adapters are ordinary proofs formalizable in ZFC. Finite audits certify
source identities, exact arithmetic, degree witnesses and bindings, not
unbounded compiler correctness. We neither assume nor establish
$\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})$.

\section{What is simplified structurally}
A quadratic parent with 31 positive coordinates and 22 residuals can be
shifted to nonnegative coordinates without forming a quartic sum of
squares. Earlier direct encodings separated all 528 padded quadratic
monomials with a full $B_2$ set, although only 71 of those monomials occur
in the residuals. We separate each occurring monomial from every possible
contaminant, while allowing irrelevant monomials to collide with one
another. This is a certified finite condition, not an appeal to random
hashing. Target intervals may overlap provided no unwanted contribution
lands on a tested coefficient.

The same principle applies to the rest of the construction: impose only
the relation needed at the next interface. The binary threshold can be
calibrated to the known mask instead of a generic maximum; the absorbed
index congruence needs only $Y$-divisibility initially, rather than full
$XY$-divisibility. In each case the proof explicitly reconstructs what was
omitted before invoking the inherited interface.

\section{Proof status and newer repository developments}
The repository has made important formal progress independent of these
explicit reductions. At the post-baseline snapshot
\texttt{59e9d4d22b34abdcc27f378796b9bfa28c6f1cc2}, its status document
reports a closed executable nine-variable expression from the concrete
refutation enumerator, with checked external Lean equivalences
\texttt{ExplicitZFC.noSyntaxZero\_iff\_conZFC} and
\texttt{noNaturalZero\_iff\_conZFC}. Its numerical coefficient payload
is not materialized. The same document still marks the P67 compiler proof
in Lean and an object-language ZFC derivation as open. We inspected these
status statements, but did not rebuild that Lean development.

These are different constructions and proof layers. Our finite,
materialized circuits do not inherit a kernel certificate merely because
another executable expression has a Lean theorem. Conversely, it would
now be incorrect to describe all repository coefficient recipes as
noncomputable choices. The report preserves this distinction and includes
the reviewed status document as provenance.

\section{Limits and next mathematical targets}
The smaller selective code is a concrete certificate, not a minimum
among all labelings. The native coefficient tuple is kept coherent;
replacing one port in isolation is forbidden. A smaller published tuple
could be substituted only with its complete adapter and a newly verified
bound on every derived coefficient. We do not hide that cost by calling
all large integers unit-size data.

The attempt to absorb the remaining transport quotient is not promoted
to a ten-positive-variable theorem. Removing the transport equation
loses the early positivity and size bounds; a proposed rational-index
repair leaves a modular branch that does not force the required original
index congruence. The archive records the obstruction separately from
validated constructions. No search for an actual consistency refutation
is performed.

The most promising next proof-assistant target is the general selective
extraction and signed-borrow theorem: it has a small finite certificate
interface and is independent of the set-theoretic calibration. The
calibrated two-block carry lemma is similarly elementary. Formalizing
these reusable interfaces would be more valuable than treating finite
sample tests as if they certified the complete Diophantine reduction.
