# Signed modular binomial numerators at one common Pell root

Research note for ProveIt, 25 October 2026. This note describes the source
`stages/modular/raw_source.json`, generated by `build_modular.py`, and its precise
relationship to the fixed-base, linear-beta source in
`stages/fixed_base/raw_source.json`.

## 1. Result, attribution, and logical scope

The source has six retained exact-power requests and eleven modular Pell
evaluations. After inherited Horn factorization and certified integral monic
projection, its baseline has 233 unrestricted integer variables and 178
quadratic residuals. Their squared sum is a quartic with 4,818 nonzero
monomials and coefficient height 128,446. These are measured baseline
figures; separately documented affine improvements may change the final
delivered equation.

The common-root mechanism, freeing the Pell center, and replacing a
successor power by multiplication are inherited, not novel claims. In the
supplied closure they appear explicitly in
`parent/upstream/round17/architecture/proofs/INHERITED_MODULAR_FACTORIAL_PROOF.md`,
Sections 4--5, with attribution there to package 108's Pell-residue work.
The new source combines that mechanism with the fixed radius `R=16^t`,
binomial Horn weights, the signed semantic anchor, and the one-norm
remainder representation. Because the global CRT representatives need no
longer be positive, this combination requires the signed arguments proved
below. It also uses a sharper common modulus already present in the
normalized range equations.

The conclusion is an ordinary mathematical proof, formalizable in ZFC,
composed with the named inherited C222-to-ZFC calibration. The finite
JSON certificates verify source mutations and exact polynomial algebra;
they do not themselves establish the unbounded arithmetic or consistency
equivalence. No proof of Con(ZFC), global variable minimum, worldwide
priority claim, or new end-to-end proof-assistant term is asserted.

## 2. The literal changes

Write `a_0,...,a_8` for the nine actual CRT representatives, whose stored
coordinates in this source are `a_i-1`. Write `J` for the actual derived
label expression (stored wire `add.380` plus one). All these values are
unrestricted integers before decoding. Retain

    t=16+h^2,       R=16^t,       N=B^x,
    q=(BH)^x=6z+3,  y=RB(2v+1)^2,
    D_t=q^t,       D_y=q^(y+1),  M=q^x.

Replace the former radix majorant by

    H=2+2(R+t)+sum_(i=0)^8 a_i^2,

and explicitly impose `B=2b+1`. A new wire for H keeps its product with
B quadratic. The six displayed exponential equalities are certified by
the retained ordinary polynomial Pell interface. Exponentiation is notation
for the interpreted outputs, not an operation in the final polynomial.

The former request for `D_(t+1)=q^(t+1)` is replaced by the quadratic
identity `D_(t+1)=q D_t`. Its private addition and quotient wires become
irrelevant definitional coordinates, removed by the checked projection.

Let `X_e(A),Y_e(A)` be the canonical signed Pell polynomials. At each
of the eleven upper indices

    R+t; a_0,...,a_8; J

replace the exact numerator `(q+1)^e` by a coordinate

    mu_e=X_e(A)+(q+1-A)Y_e(A).                         (2.1)

The coordinate is retained when a normalized digit equation multiplies it
by a denominator ratio, so every literal residual remains quadratic.
The eleven `mu_e^2` terms are removed from the square-frame definition;
these evaluations grow with A and must not be required to be small.
All eleven nominal indices remain among the bounded frame atoms.

The existing shared range-product wire is exactly `NM`. Add the single
equation

    T_(q+1)=2A(q+1)-(q+1)^2-1=NM h_0.                 (2.2)

Its quotient is unrestricted. Finally move the old terminal Pell norm
from A to a new L, and impose

    A=L^2+u^2+v^2+w(w+1).                            (2.3)

The old norm now proves `|L|>W^W`; thus A is positive and greater than
`W^W`, while (2.3) permits every integer A at least `L^2`. There are
seven new raw coordinates: L,u,v,w,H,b,h_0. The old numerator quotients
and unnecessary successor wires disappear by exact algebraic projection.

The source mutation records all changed rows and new variables. No
nominal-index Pell norm, cap, or pooled orientation equation is removed.

## 3. Why the center can be chosen freely and arbitrarily large

For integers u,v,w, the form

    G(u,v,w)=u^2+v^2+w(w+1)

is nonnegative and represents every nonnegative integer. One proof applies
the three-square theorem to `4n+1`: this number is never of the excluded
form `4^a(8b+7)`. Its three-square representation has one odd coordinate
and two even coordinates, by reduction modulo four, and division by four
gives `n=u^2+v^2+w(w+1)`.

The retained frame rows are

    U=W(2z+1),       V=(W+1)U,       L^2+U^2-V^2=1.

They give `L^2-((W+1)^2-1)U^2=1`. Classification of the Pell solutions
and the congruence `Y_e(W+1)=e mod W` imply that the nonzero index has
absolute value at least W, because U is a nonzero multiple of W. Thus
`|L|>W^W`. Conversely the index `e=W` yields a solution with `U/W` odd.
For even W this follows from the standard congruence modulo `W(W+2)`;
for odd W it follows from the parity recurrence at the even center W+1.
This supplies the required integer z.

The signed semantic-anchor proof uses the size of the center only through
`|A|>W^W` and `|A-1|>4W^3`, plus its Pell equations at that center.
Equation (2.3) gives stronger positive-center bounds. Consequently the
old cap still bounds every classified principal index by W, and its
pooled errors still force

    (X_i,Y_i)=(X_(k_i)(A),Y_(k_i)(A))

for every signed nominal index k_i, including J. No positivity of these
nominal indices is presumed. The anchor's even-index completeness is
unchanged and is available at every sufficiently large positive A.

The exact-power lemma is valid also when its base is negative. For an
integer base n with `|n|>=2`, bounded index `|k|<W`, and bounded output
`|m|<W`, its modulus is `T_n=2An-n^2-1`. If k is nonnegative, the canonical
Pell congruence and the frame bounds force `m=n^k`. If `k=-j<0`, the
same congruence forces `m n^j=1` after bounded reduction, impossible
over the integers. Bases 1 and -1 have their direct unit interpretation.
This argument does not use any binomial digit or its remainder bound.

## 4. An acyclic sign and size bootstrap

The fixed-base request first gives `R=16^t>1`. The added oddness equation
gives `B!=0`. The beta formula makes y a nonzero multiple of R, regardless
of B's sign. Because `q=6z+3`, its absolute value is at least three.
The retained exact request at exponent y+1 therefore implies `y+1>=0`.
A negative y would be at most `-R<-1`, which is impossible. Hence

    y>0,       B>0,       y>=RB>B.

This supplies B's sign before interpreting its binomial digit. The squared
majorant satisfies `H>2`. Hence the exact request at base BH implies
`x>=0` and `q=(BH)^x>0`. The progression excludes q=1, so x>0.

The two retained endpoints read

    x=B+1+(y+1)u_0,
    x=1+[1+(t+1)y]u_t.

If `u_0<=-1`, then `x<=B-y<0`, a contradiction. Thus `x>=B+1>=2`.
The positive coefficient in the second identity forces `u_t>=1`, giving

    x>=2+(t+1)y.                                     (4.1)

All digit denominators are now positive, with

    t,t+1,y+1<=x,       D_t,D_(t+1),D_y dividing M.

The remaining exact requests give `N=B^x` and `M=q^x`. In particular
`N|q`, while B,q,N,M are all odd. The common modulus

    Omega=NM

is positive and odd. No signed global CRT representative has yet been
interpreted as nonnegative, and none needs to be.

## 5. One common root works for every signed index

Set `n=q+1` and `s=q+1-A`. Equation (2.2) implies

    s^2=A^2-1 modulo Omega.

Substituting this square root into the canonical Pell-unit identity gives

    mu_e = (q+1)^e modulo Omega                      (5.1)

for every integer e. Negative exponents in this congruence mean powers
of the inverse unit in the finite quotient ring. They are legitimate:
`N|q` implies `gcd(q+1,Omega)=1`. Equivalently the norm-one inverse
`X_e-Y_e s` evaluates to the inverse of `(q+1)^e`.

For completeness, the congruence

    2(q+1)A = (q+1)^2+1 modulo Omega                 (5.2)

has a unique residue class, because both two and q+1 are units modulo
the odd modulus Omega. Choose A in this class as large as necessary,
in particular above `L^2`, then represent `A-L^2` by G in (2.3).
All preexisting semantic coordinates, the frame W, and its L witness
were chosen before this step. There is therefore no circular dependence
between the frame and the eleven growing values mu_e.

## 6. Generalized binomial coefficients for signed upper arguments

For every integer n and nonnegative j, the generalized coefficient
`binom(n,j)` is an integer and

    |binom(n,j)| <= max(1,|n|)^j.                    (6.1)

For n>=0 this follows by bounding subsets by ordered selections, with
the usual zero convention for j>n. For `n=-a<0`, use

    binom(-a,j)=(-1)^j binom(a+j-1,j).

The last positive coefficient counts multisets of size j from a colors;
each multiset is the image of an ordered j-tuple, so it is at most `a^j`.
The j=0 case is one in both arguments.

For every integer n and k>=0, in the ring where 1+q is a unit,

    (1+q)^n = sum_(j=0)^k binom(n,j)q^j mod q^(k+1). (6.2)

For negative n this is the finite truncation of the formal power series
of `(1+T)^n`, or follows by multiplying by its positive inverse; no
analytic convergence or infinite expression occurs in a polynomial row.
For a range digit with D=q^k, the higher terms are multiples of ND
because `N|q`. The exact B digit instead uses the modulus qD. Both
moduli divide Omega: `k<=x` for all range digits, and `t+1<=x` for the
B digit.

Combining (5.1)--(6.2), put

    r_0(n,k)=sum_(j=0)^(k-1) binom(n,j) q^j.

Then

    mu_n = r_0(n,k)+D binom(n,k) mod ND,              (6.3)

and the same formula holds modulo qD for the B digit. Here r_0 may be
negative. Its absolute bound, rather than a positivity premise, is what
makes the centered representation exact.

## 7. A structural signed bound on the actual J expression

The supplied `J_majorant_certificate.json` is generated directly from the
raw scalar arithmetic graph by `audit_J_majorant.py`. It extracts all 91
ancestor gates of stored wire `add.380`. Its only free leaves are the
four stored wires `a-1,b-1,c-1,d-1`. They satisfy absolute value at most
H, because `H>=2+a_i^2>=|a_i-1|` for every signed integer a_i.

If a gate is `g=sum_m c_m product_(i in m) v_i`, and all its inputs
satisfy `|v_i|<=H^(e_i)` with H>=2, then

    |g|<=H^e,
    e=ceil(log_2(sum_m |c_m|))+max_m sum_(i in m)e_i. (7.1)

This is a triangle-inequality bound and applies irrespective of signs.
The finite ledger computes exponent 3,649,856 for the stored J-minus-one
wire. Adding one gives

    |J|<=H^3649857 <= H^ell,       ell=2^22.          (7.2)

Thus the inherited ell budget is valid for all signed representatives;
no extension from a positive-input polynomial estimate is assumed.

For each of the eleven digits, let C bound all absolute binomial
coefficients through its requested position. In fact one can take C with

    C^2<=H^x<=q.                                    (7.3)

For an ordinary upper representative a, (6.1), `max(1,|a|)^2<=H`,
and k<=x prove (7.3). For the B digit, n=R+t<=H and k=t, while
`2t<=x`. For J, use (7.2) and

    x>=2+(t+1)y >= (t+1)R >= 2ell(t+1),

since `R=16^t>=16^16>2ell`. These inequalities are available before
authenticating B. Also q>=2^16. The geometric sum now gives the uniform
signed remainder estimate

    |r_0(n,k)|/q^k < C/(q-1) <= sqrt(q)/(q-1)<1/32. (7.4)

This covers the constant coefficient when an upper argument is zero,
as well as every negative upper argument.

## 8. Centered normalized digits recover exact generalized coefficients

The retained common norm is

    M^2=1+4 sum_i rho_i^2+G(u_*,v_*,w_*).

It forces `|rho_i|<M/2`. The ratio equations give `v_i D_i=M` with
positive D_i and M, so each ratio v_i is a positive integer. A range
row is

    v_i mu_i = rho_i + MN z_i.

Consequently rho_i is divisible by v_i and, on putting
`r_i=rho_i/v_i`, one has

    mu_i=r_i+D_i N z_i,       |r_i|<D_i/2.

Equation (6.3) implies `D_i|(r_i-r_0)`. By (7.4), the absolute value
of their difference is less than D_i. Thus `r_i=r_0`, and division by
D_i in (6.3) gives exactly

    N divides binom(n_i,k_i).                        (8.1)

No nonnegativity of n_i or r_0 was required.

The exact B row reads

    v_t mu_(R+t)=rho_0+M(B+qz_0).

The same centered reconstruction gives
`B=binom(R+t,t) mod q`. The coefficient is positive and below q/2
by (7.3), and `0<B<q/2` because `q=(BH)^x` with H>2 and x>=2.
Hence the congruence is literal equality:

    B=binom(R+t,t).                                  (8.2)

For completeness, choose each rho_i to be `v_i r_0`. Formula (7.4)
gives `|rho_i|<M/32`. There are eleven remainders, so

    4 sum_i rho_i^2 < (44/1024)M^2<M^2.

The integer `M^2-1-4 sum_i rho_i^2` is nonnegative and has a G
representation. Thus the one-norm constraint remains constructively
complete for these modular evaluations.

## 9. The range lemma extends to every integer upper argument

The inherited lemma for nonnegative n is:

    p^e | binom(n,k)  implies  n mod p^e < k,

for a prime p and positive e,k. Its usual valuation proof is retained.
If `k>=p^e`, the conclusion is automatic, also for signed n. Suppose
`k<p^e` and `n=-a<0`. The identity in Section 6 and the nonnegative
lemma give

    r=(a+k-1) mod p^e < k.

Then `n mod p^e=k-1-r`, which lies in `[0,k-1]`. This proves the
same range lemma for every integer n. The false converse is neither
needed nor asserted.

After (8.2), the fixed-base source proves `R>=lcm(1,...,t)^2` and
`B>(t+1)^t`. Its generalized binomial CRT theorem uses (8.1) only to
recover small nonnegative residues modulo each allocated prime power.
The signed extension just proved supplies those residues with exactly
the same bounds. Polynomial evaluation of J and the local rule expression
commutes with these reductions. Their bounded local meanings are therefore
unchanged even if a global representative was negative.

The inherited strict local budget gives local rule magnitude below `2^x`;
the full fixed-base proof verifies its hypotheses using (4.1), `y>=RB`,
and the authenticated large B. The decoded finite rows, beta endpoints,
and large-evaluation Horn product identity therefore yield a finite C222
refutation, and the retained calibration yields a finite ZFC refutation.
This is semantic decoding, not pointwise restoration of every coordinate
of the older all-natural source.

## 10. Constructive completeness, including the new congruences

Start with a finite C222 refutation and follow the fixed-base source:
choose a cofinal horizon t, set R=16^t and B=binom(R+t,t), and choose
the pending-multiset accumulators with weights 1+jB. B is odd, since
`v_2(R)=4t>floor(log_2 t)` gives `v_2(R+i)=v_2(i)` for 1<=i<=t.
Thus the added odd-B constructor is complete.

Choose y=RBw^2 with w an arbitrarily large odd multiple of the odd part
of t!. The power of two in R supplies the missing 2-adic divisibility,
so y is an even multiple of t! and can exceed every accumulator. The
beta CRT then provides arbitrarily large suitable even x. Let N=B^x.
The inherited amplified CRT gives all actual representatives modulo N^2.

For the signed cap, choose a odd and b,c,d even; the inherited parity
identity makes J even. Choose the other five representatives even too.
Then H is odd. If 3 divides B, BH is already an odd multiple of three.
If 3 does not divide B, N^2 is a unit modulo six. Adjust two representatives
outside a,b,c,d by multiples of N^2, keeping them even. Their squares
can each be 0 or 1 modulo three, and the sum can be any residue modulo
three. Thus choose them to make H divisible by three. All such changes
preserve the amplified CRT requirements and leave J unchanged.

Choose a sufficiently large in its fixed class modulo `6N^2`. This
preserves H modulo six, preserves the CRT data, and makes the positive
even J dominate every other requested nominal index, as in the inherited
cofinal-anchor argument. Hence `q=(BH)^x` is a positive odd multiple
of three and supplies z in the new progression. The enlarged squared H
only strengthens the coefficient estimates in Section 7.

With these semantic values fixed, compute the six true exact powers,
the derived successor denominator, and the frame W. Choose its Pell L
witness. Solve (5.2) for an arbitrarily large positive A above L^2,
and fill (2.3) using G. Complete every canonical Pell pair and the even
semantic anchor at this A. Define the eleven mu coordinates by (2.1),
their quotients by (6.3), and their centered remainders by Section 8.
All are integers. Fill the remaining definitional gates and old unused
quotients arbitrarily with their corresponding gate values, then apply
the certified projection and coordinate changes.

Thus a finite refutation gives a source zero. Section 9 proves the
converse. With the inherited C222/ZF/ZFC calibration, ZFC proves that
the final ordinary integer polynomial has a zero if and only if there is
a finite ZFC refutation. Its no-zero sentence is therefore equivalent,
in ZFC, to Con(ZFC).

## 11. Reproduction and boundaries of the checks

`build_modular.py` records the exact source mutation, runs the inherited
Horn factorization and integral monic elimination, verifies the projection
certificate, applies the inherited integral translations, and expands the
entire squared sum. `audit_J_majorant.py` produces the signed structural
bound from the literal source graph. All polynomial rows have integer
coefficients and degree at most two; no semantic exponent, binomial,
inequality, or quantifier is hidden in a literal residual.

The proof relies on the explicitly named inherited Pell classification,
semantic-cap construction, generalized binomial CRT, local C222 compiler,
and ZFC calibration. The new arguments make their changed interface
hypotheses explicit. Computation checks finite algebra and certificates;
the ordinary proofs establish the unbounded implications.
