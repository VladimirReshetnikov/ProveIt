# Arithmetic proof for the thirteen-integer construction

## 1. Statement and dependency boundary

The primary new construction is the ordinary polynomial represented by
`five_root_integer13.json`. It has thirteen unrestricted integer unknowns
and exact total degree 1392. The accompanying fourteen-, fifteen-, and
sixteen-integer equations have exact degrees 696, 244, and 108.

The proof below is an ordinary mathematical proof formalizable in ZFC. Its
logical endpoint is the same fixed syntactic refutation predicate as the
source-34 native compiler and the K215 calibration in ProveIt. The native
compiler and that calibration remain inherited mathematical dependencies.
This is not a claim that a new complete Lean/Rocq theorem or an encoded
object-language ZFC derivation has been checked.

The fixed integers are the complete source-34
`mixed-parity-unrounded-1064921` tuple beta, K0, MC, MF, together with

    cone_A = 2^1113,  cone_B = 2^666104.

Here beta=2^1064921-1 and MF is already the shifted mask. The six exact
integer byte strings and their SHA-256 identities are in `coefficients/`.
No coefficient is selected by deciding consistency. In particular beta is
odd, beta=1 modulo 3, and beta>17.

The specific inherited mathematical inputs are:

1. Source 70's signed residue-orientation construction and its canonical
   completeness family (`integer/section.tex`, especially its final two
   subsections). It supplies canonical q=2^t with t odd, R=3 modulo 4,
   X=2^R, and the odd scale parameters used below.
2. Source 71's determinant guard and its completeness for the same whole
   source-34 tuple (`report.tex`, Sections 2--4). It supplies positivity of
   the calibrated determinant for canonical native witnesses.
3. The incoming Pell-congruences construction's seventeen-integer CRT
   parent `transport/degree88_17.json`, for the fifteen- and sixteen-variable
   companions. Its first root is a midpoint coordinate and its auxiliary
   root is centered at V; the inverse changes are justified in that source.
4. Source 61, Lemma “Positive auxiliary reconstruction,” reproduced in the
   incoming Pell-congruences package as `positive/upstream/package61_report.tex`.
   This supplies fresh auxiliary witnesses after the normalized strong Pell
   choice used for the fourteen- and thirteen-variable equations.

These inputs all precede the new root separation and norm constructions.
Their exact copies and source pins should accompany an integration of this
proof. There is no use of Jones's or Sun's variable-compression finalizer in
the new reduction.

## 2. A polynomial norm convention

For formal square roots r_i with r_i^2=A_i, let

    F_m(Z; A_1,...,A_m) = product over all epsilon_i in {+1,-1}
                         of (Z + sum epsilon_i r_i).

Changing a sign of any r_i permutes the factors. Consequently F_m is a
polynomial with integer coefficients in Z and the A_i. This is a finite
algebraic definition, not an additional quantifier. It has degree 2^m in Z,
and F_m=0 over the complex numbers exactly when one of the signed sums of
the roots equals Z, up to an immaterial simultaneous sign convention.

The executable construction uses quadratic arithmetic rather than storing
formal roots. If w^2=bw+c, multiplication of pairs (E,O) representing E+Ow
is

    (E1,O1)*(E2,O2) =
      (E1*E2+c*O1*O2, E1*O2+O1*E2+b*O1*O2).

The product over the two quadratic roots is

    Norm(E+Ow) = E^2+b*E*O-c*O^2.

These identities remain true when the quadratic is reducible or has a
repeated root. Thus no irreducibility premise is hidden in the builder.

## 3. Root separation using rational 3-adic orders

Only orders of rational numbers at 3 are needed; a theory of valuations on
algebraic extensions is unnecessary.

**Lemma.** Suppose nonzero algebraic numbers alpha_i have rational squares.
Suppose that, in every subset containing at least two of them, one square
has strictly smaller 3-adic order than the other squares, and that this
smallest order is even. Then no nonempty signed sum of the alpha_i vanishes.

**Proof.** Singletons do not vanish. In a larger subset, divide every root
by 3^e, where 2e is the unique smallest square order. The smallest square
is now a unit in Z_(3), while every other square belongs to 3 Z_(3).
Write beta_0 for the smallest root and beta_1,...,beta_s for the others.
The product

    product over epsilon_1,...,epsilon_s
       (beta_0 + sum epsilon_i beta_i)

is a polynomial with integer coefficients in all the squared roots: sign
invariance makes each exponent even. Modulo 3 it is

    (beta_0^2)^(2^(s-1)),

which is a nonzero unit. None of the factors can vanish. QED.

**Corollary.** Under the same hypothesis, a rational signed sum of the
alpha_i forces each alpha_i to be rational.

**Proof.** The field generated by finitely many square roots of rational
numbers is a finite Galois extension of Q. An automorphism changes some
root signs. Subtracting its image of the rational sum gives twice a signed
sum of precisely the changed nonzero roots. The lemma rules this out, so
every automorphism fixes every root. The fixed field is Q. QED.

For the applications, four square orders are exactly

    4, 2, 0, -2*v_3(H), with v_3(H)>=1.

A fifth root, when present, has square order at least 6, or is zero. Every
subset containing an old root therefore has a unique even minimum. A
subset consisting only of the new root is harmless: a nonzero singleton
cannot vanish, and a zero root is already rational. The same corollary
applies. Negative rational radicands are allowed in this algebraic
argument; their impossibility is deduced only after rationality is proved.

## 4. An integrality lemma for the auxiliary root

**Lemma (adjacent-coefficient Pell descent).** Let Q>1 and V be integers.
If y is rational and Q V^2-(Q-1)y^2=1, then y is an integer.

**Proof.** Write y=a/m in lowest terms, m>0. The equation implies
m^2 divides Q-1. Replacing signs by absolute values preserves the equation.
If |V|=1, then |y|=1. V=0 is impossible. For V>1 and y>0, set

    V'=(2Q-1)V-2(Q-1)y,
    y'=(2Q-1)y-2QV.

V' is an integer since m divides Q-1. Expansion preserves the equation.
Its positive branch has y>V and QV>(Q-1)y, the latter because

    Q^2 V^2-(Q-1)^2 y^2=Q V^2+Q-1>0.

These inequalities give -V<V'<V; the norm excludes V'=0. Moreover
2Q-1=1 modulo m, so y' has the same reduced denominator m. Induction on
|V| reaches |V|=1 without changing that denominator. Hence m=1. QED.

A rational number whose square is an integer is itself an integer, by
writing it in lowest terms. We use this separately for the other roots.

## 5. The common polynomial abbreviations

For the fourteen-variable equation the independent integer coordinates are

    J6,C,z,h,r,v_scale,w3,c,T,pair_root,pair_pos1,pair_pos2,pair_pos3,xi.

For the thirteen-variable equation replace c by the abbreviation

    c=2*c1+1

and delete pair_pos1. Its independent coordinates are therefore

    J6,C,z,h,r,v_scale,w3,c1,T,pair_root,pair_pos2,pair_pos3,xi.

In both cases define, by ordinary polynomial arithmetic,

    J=6J6+1,                 q=beta*J+1,
    W=4(3w3+1),              X=qW,
    Y=(2v_scale+1)q^3,       E=XY,
    a=(X+1)Y,               H=4a+3,
    Delta=a^2+H,            U=(2r+1)c^2,
    F2=1+Delta*U^2,         Q=Delta^2*U^2,
    delta=Q-1,
    F=(K0+W)C-z(q-1),
    R=(q(q-F)-C)(q^2-1)+(MC+q MF)J,
    k=R+1+hE,               eta=c-Yk,
    u=EYk,                  A=u(u+k)+1,
    V=F2(cT-R)-c,
    D0=X+ac,                Dm=1+Delta*c^2,
    f0=(a+2)U.

The calibrated determinant and joint guard are

    d=(cone_A F)(cone_B C)-(q-cone_A F-cone_B C)^2,
    Teta=eta(k-eta),
    Bguard=d*Teta-(pair_root^2-d-Teta+1)^2
                  -1-pair_pos2^2-pair_pos3(pair_pos3+1),
    G=Bguard-pair_pos1^2.

Thus G=0 is exactly the same joint positivity guard as in the inherited
integer equations. All these names are abbreviations, not extra unknowns.

For a positive integer n the ternary form

    Pos(x,y,z)=1+x^2+y^2+z(z+1)

represents n. One obtains this from the three-square theorem applied to
4n-3, which is 1 modulo 4 and has one odd and two even squares. Conversely
Pos is always positive over unrestricted integers.

The equation G=0 implies d>0 and Teta>0. Indeed d*Teta is greater than a
square. The two factors have the same nonzero sign. If d=-b,Teta=-e were
both negative, with b,e>0, then

    (pair_root^2+b+e+1)^2 > be,

contradicting G=0. Conversely, when d,Teta>0, put s=d+Teta-1>=1 and choose
an integer square nearest s. Its distance from s has square strictly less
than s, hence less than or equal to d*Teta, because s<=d*Teta. The remaining
strictly positive difference is a Pos value. This supplies the four joint
guard coordinates without restricting any arithmetic coordinate.

## 6. Premises before any guard is recovered

This order matters for the thirteen-variable equation.

J=6J6+1 is nonzero and odd. Consequently q is even and |q|>=beta-1>=16.
The integer W is a nonzero multiple of four, |W|>=4, while 2v_scale+1 is
odd and nonzero. Therefore

    |X|>=64, |Y|>=4096, |a|=|X+1|*|Y|>=63*4096.

In particular Delta=(a+1)(a+3)>0. Also a is even, so Delta and H are odd.

Modulo 3 one has J=1,q=2,W=1,X=2. Hence a=0, H=0, Delta=0 modulo 3.
The integer H is nonzero because H=4a+3. Thus v_3(H)>=1.

For thirteen variables c is syntactically odd, so U is nonzero and Q>1
without using G. In fact Q>=9. For fourteen variables G=0 supplies
Teta>0. This gives |k|>=2 and 0<|eta|<|k|; since c=Yk+eta,

    |c|>(|Y|-1)|k|>1.

Thus U is nonzero and Q>1 there as well.

Writing b=XY^2 gives A=b(b+1)k^2+1>=1 for every integer b,k. Modulo 3,
b is either 0 or 2, so A=1 modulo 3. The remaining facts are

    F2=1 modulo 3, Dm=1 modulo 3, Q=0 modulo 9,
    B=(Q V^2-1)/(Q-1)=1 modulo 3 in Z_(3).

The denominator Q-1 is a 3-adic unit and is nonzero. These statements do
not require first, main, strong, auxiliary, or guard root recovery.

## 7. The fourteen-variable norm and its single equation

Define the polynomial

    Z=H[xi+18f0+3(2u+k)+2V]+2D0,

    N14=delta^8 F_4(Z;
          324 H^2 F2, 36 H^2 A, 4 Dm, 4 H^2 (Q V^2-1)/delta).

The displayed division is only a readable presentation. F_4 has degree at
most eight in each radicand, so delta^8 clears every denominator exactly.
The executable file constructs this integer polynomial without division,
using successive quadratic norms. The actual single equation is

    P14=N14+G^2+N14*G^2=0.                         (14)

For integers, (14) is equivalent to N14=G=0, because
(1+N14)(1+G^2)=1 and the second factor is a positive integer.

At a zero, the norm factorization supplies algebraic roots f,tau,y,d0 with

    f^2=F2, tau^2=A, y^2=B, d0^2=Dm,

and a signed equality

    Z/(2H)=9f+3tau+y+d0/H.

Signs can be absorbed into the four roots. Their squared 3-adic orders are
4,2,0,-2*v_3(H), respectively. Section 3 makes all four roots rational.
The integer-square lemma makes f,tau,d0 integers; Section 4 makes y an
integer. In particular all radicands are now nonnegative.

Set gamma=(d0-D0)/H. The equality of roots shows that 2gamma is an
integer. H gamma is also an integer, and H is odd, so gamma is an integer.
This last step is essential: the midpoint carrier was not assumed to have
even numerator before root recovery.

## 8. The thirteen-variable norm: recover a guard root last

Use c=2c1+1 and all abbreviations of Section 5. Define

    N13 = product over the two roots w^2=Bguard
               of N14_raw(xi-54w),                       (13)

where N14_raw means the norm N14 before its G finalizer. This is a single
integer polynomial: the two-root product is exactly the quadratic norm
implemented in `build.py`. There is no separate guard in (13).

The norm factorization yields one rational sum of five algebraic roots:
the old four weighted roots 9f,3tau,y,d0/H and the new weighted root 27w.
The old squared orders are 4,2,0,-2*v_3(H); the new squared order is at
least 6, or the new root is zero. All premises from Section 6 are available
without G because c was made syntactically odd. Section 3 therefore makes
the four old roots rational and also w rational. Since w^2=Bguard is an
integer, w is an integer. Set pair_pos1=w; this restores G=0 exactly.

The same integrality arguments recover f,tau,y,d0 and gamma. The additional
term 27w in the halved carrier is an integer, so the argument
2gamma,Hgamma integral and H odd remains unchanged.

This proves soundness of the guard fold without assuming the guard whose
root was removed. Allowing Bguard to be negative during the formal norm
calculation introduces no false solutions: rational root recovery rules
that case out at every zero.

## 9. Restore the inherited signed arithmetic system

After either Section 7 or Section 8, all the old arithmetic witnesses can
be recovered as integers. The transport coordinate is
alpha=q-F-C. Restore zeta=k-eta and the old first-Pell register P=k+u.
The first norm tau^2=A says exactly tau^2-P^2+Pk-1=0.

The ordinary strong ordinate and its odd scale are

    I_old=Delta*U,
    i_old=Delta*(2r+1),
    r_old=(i_old-1)/2.

Delta is odd, so r_old is an integer; I_old=i_old*c^2 and
I_old^2=Delta(f^2-1)=Q.

The two deleted CRT quotients have explicit integer restorations:

    o=f(cT-R),
    j=F2*T-1-Delta*(2r+1)^2*c^3*R.

Indeed V+c=f o and V+R=c j. Here (F2-1)/c is the displayed polynomial
Delta*(2r+1)^2*c^3, so no unproved divisibility or rank argument is needed
for this normalized source.

Restore the elementary product registers by

    L=q^2-1, N=q^3, C2=c^2, F2_old=f^2,
    D=d0, Baux=Q, g=y^2-V^2.

Then the main norm is D^2-Delta*C2=1 and the auxiliary norm is
y^2-Baux*g=1. The retained packing, first-index, and transport equations
are literal identities after these substitutions. For the source-70
affine coordinate use J3_old=(J-1)/3=2J6; w3 is unchanged. The guard
G=0 supplies d>0 and eta*zeta>0, whose Pos representations restore the
old private guard triples.

Thus every new zero gives an integer zero of the residue-oriented,
determinant-guarded source system. Its named native/compiler/calibration
theorem yields a finite ZFC refutation. In particular the new proof does
not assume a positive radix before the inherited signed argument recovers
the appropriate arithmetic branch.

## 10. Canonical completeness of normalization

Start with a finite syntactic ZFC refutation. The inherited source-34 and
source-70 completeness proofs give canonical arithmetic witnesses with

    q=2^t, t odd, R>=3, R=3 modulo 4,
    X=2^R, Y an odd positive multiple of q^3,
    c=psi_{a+2}(R)>2.

They also give eta,zeta>0 and d>0. The notation chi_A,psi_A denotes

    chi_A(n)+psi_A(n)*sqrt(A^2-1)
       =(A+sqrt(A^2-1))^n.

The native J=(q-1)/beta is odd, since it is a finite repunit in the even
base 2^1064921. It is 1 modulo 3 by the existing residue orientation.
Consequently J=6J6+1 for an integer J6. The main Pell parameter a+2 is
even. The recurrence modulo 2 gives c odd because R is odd; hence c=2c1+1
for an integer c1 in the thirteen-variable equation.

Choose m=cR, and put

    f=chi_{a+2}(m), U=psi_{a+2}(m).

Then f^2-Delta*U^2=1. Composition gives

    U=c*psi_D(c), with D=chi_{a+2}(R).

Since D^2=1 modulo c, the Pell recurrence gives
psi_D(c)=c*D^(c-1)=0 modulo c. Thus c^2 divides U. The index m is odd
and a+2 is even, so U is odd; c is odd too. The quotient U/c^2 is
therefore an odd integer, exactly 2r+1 for an integer r. This proves
completeness of the normalized odd strong scale, rather than assuming it.

Source 61's positive auxiliary reconstruction now supplies positive T,y
with

    V=f^2(cT-R)-c,
    Q V^2-(Q-1)y^2=1,
    Q=(Delta U)^2.

For clarity, its construction is V*=chi_S(R)/S, y*=psi_S(R), where
S=Delta U. The odd-index identities give V*=-c modulo f^2 and
V*=-R modulo c. Since f^2=1 modulo c, the integer

    T=(V*+c+R f^2)/(c f^2)

has the required property. This chooses fresh auxiliary witnesses; it
does not claim that every old quotient had the extra factor f.

These fresh strong and auxiliary choices leave q,F,C,k,eta and hence d,
eta*zeta unchanged. Section 5 supplies the joint guard witnesses.
Finally set

    xi14=18f+6tau+2y+2gamma-18f0-3(2u+k)-2V,
    xi13=xi14+54*pair_pos1.

Both are integers and make the appropriate norm factor vanish. Hence a
refutation supplies a zero of each new equation.

Combining Sections 9 and 10 gives, for the literal supplied coefficients,

    ZFC proves (no integer zero of N13) iff Con(ZFC),
    ZFC proves (no integer zero of P14) iff Con(ZFC).

This does not prove either side of either equivalence individually.

## 11. The fifteen- and sixteen-integer companions

For these two equations use the inherited seventeen-variable CRT parent.
Keep f as an independent integer, put J=3J3+1, U=(2r+1)c^2, Q=U^2,
F2=f^2, and V=f*c*T-f^2*R-c. Keep the separate strong row

    Sstrong=Q-Delta*(f^2-1).

All other common abbreviations have the same meanings. Guard G=0 implies
|c|>1 and hence Q>1. Modulo 3 one has Delta=0; Sstrong=0 implies 3 divides
U and therefore 9 divides Q. Thus the radicands 9A and
B=(Q V^2-1)/(Q-1) are distinct modulo 3, and their weighted square orders
are 2 and 0. The fixed multiplier 3 replaces the larger variable
multiplier U used in the previous two-root merger.

Set t=2u+k, ell=k^2-4, and

    j(x)=delta*x^2+delta*(6t+4V)*x
                      +9delta*ell-4(V^2-1),
    h0=12delta*t*V,
    N16(x)=j(x)^2+2h0*j(x)
             +576delta*A*(1-V^2)+144delta^2*ell*V^2.

The exact identity is

    N16(x)=delta^2 F_2(x+3t+2V;36A,4B).

It replaces the two first/auxiliary root witnesses by one integer x;
root separation and the integrality lemmas prove soundness. Completeness
uses x=6tau+2y-3t-2V. The sixteen-variable polynomial is the sum of squares
of N16, the main row, Sstrong, and G.

For fifteen variables, also take the quadratic norm eliminating gamma:

    N15(x)=H^8 product over gamma satisfying
                   (H gamma+D0)^2=Dm of N16(x-2gamma).

This is exactly the denominator-free polynomial generated by the quadratic
arithmetic engine. The third weighted root has order -v_3(H), below the
other two. The same Galois and integrality argument recovers gamma as an
integer, including the odd-H midpoint step. Let S=Sstrong^2+G^2. The one
equation is N15+S+N15*S=0, equivalent to N15=Sstrong=G=0.

These two companions compose directly with the existing CRT parent. In
particular they retain that parent's proved preliminary rank lemma and
CRT divisibility proof; finite polynomial checks do not replace them.

## 12. Exact degree, including the centered strong-root cancellation

All degrees count only existential coordinates after fixing the six
coefficient ports. The coefficient construction gives the upper bounds
108 for the sixteen-variable polynomial and 244 for the fifteen-variable
polynomial by direct arithmetic propagation. Finite-field certificates
with the actual ports give matching nonzero leading coefficients.

For fourteen variables naive propagation gives 700, because it loses a
cancellation in the final centered strong-root norm. Here is a rigorous
upper bound of 696.

Immediately before the last quadratic norm, write the polynomial in its
carrier as sum a_i*x^i, 0<=i<=8. Exact DAG propagation bounds the coefficient
degrees by

    (292,295,294,277,260,237,214,191,168).

The final quadratic root is w=f-f0, where

    w^2+2f0*w+(U^2-1)=0,
    deg(f0)<=9, deg(U^2-1)<=6.

Scale all independent variables by a real parameter t along a fixed generic
direction, including x=xi. On the nonempty open set where the degree-nine
leading coefficient of f0 is nonzero, one quadratic root is O(t^9). The
other is O(t^-3), by the exact quotient formula using the product U^2-1
and the large root. For large t the two roots are real because f0^2 has
degree eighteen and U^2-1 has degree six. Thus the two arguments xi-18w
are respectively O(t^9) and O(t). The product of the two polynomial values
is therefore

    O(t^(M9+M1)),
    M9=max_i(deg(a_i)+9i)=312,
    M1=max_i(deg(a_i)+i)=296.

The norm has total degree at most 608. To justify passing from generic
directions to a polynomial degree statement, any homogeneous component of
degree greater than 608 would vanish on that nonempty open set of slopes;
a real polynomial vanishing on an open set is identically zero.

Guard G has exact degree 44: its leading part is minus the square of the
degree-22 polynomial eta(k-eta). Therefore P14=N14+G^2+N14*G^2 has degree
at most 608+88=696.

The supplied exact finite-field specialization modulo the prime 65521,
using the actual fixed coefficients, gives degree 608 and leading
coefficient 32234 for N14, and degree 696 and leading coefficient 50632
for P14. This proves the lower bounds and hence exact degrees.

For thirteen variables the complete quadratic-norm circuit propagates a
degree upper bound of 1392 without any unresolved cancellation. The same
exact specialization gives a nonzero coefficient 56423 at degree 1392.
Thus its total degree is exactly 1392.

More explicitly, before folding the guard root, the degree-sixteen norm
as a polynomial in xi has coefficient-degree upper bounds

    (612,607,606,597,588,571,554,537,520,
     497,474,451,428,405,382,359,336).

Bguard has degree at most 44. Giving its formal square root weight 22,
the degree of either substituted factor is at most

    max_i(deg(coefficient_i)+22i)=696.

The quadratic norm therefore has degree at most 1392. This argument is
also a direct weighted-degree calculation in the quadratic algebra
w^2=Bguard; it does not assume an integral or real value of w beforehand.

The checker uses arbitrary-precision integer convolution followed by
reduction modulo 65521. It records every variable's fixed slope and the
actual coefficient residues. A single nonzero specialized coefficient is
a rigorous degree lower certificate; no random identity test is being
used as a proof of the norm identities or of the unbounded consistency

## 13. What this proof establishes

The new mechanism is rational-root extraction through distinct local
orders. It permits main-Pell quotient recovery, normalized strong-Pell
recovery, and finally a guard-root recovery to share one integer carrier.
The guard fold is valid because its required nondegeneracy conditions are
established syntactically before that guard is restored.

The resulting counts and degrees are explicit properties of the delivered
polynomials. They are not global minimality statements. The earlier
nine-positive and ten-unrestricted-integer constructions remain different
points in the comparison. The ordinary mathematical proof retains its
named native semantic and ZFC-refutation calibration dependencies.
