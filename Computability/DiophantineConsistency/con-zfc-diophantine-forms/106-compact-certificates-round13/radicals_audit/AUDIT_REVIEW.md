## Domain bootstrap, without circular guard assumptions

For every unrestricted integer assignment, J=6j+1 is odd and 1 modulo 3. The actual beta is odd and 1 modulo 3, so q is even and 2 modulo 3. Thus q is nonzero. Also W=12w+4 is nonzero and 1 modulo 3, and X=qW is 2 modulo 3. Since Y=qv is even, a=(X+1)Y is a multiple of 6. Consequently

    Delta=(a+1)(a+3)>=3, Delta=3 modulo 4,
    H=4a+3 is odd and nonzero, H=0 modulo 3,
    H=3 modulo q, gcd(H,2q^2)=1.

These statements include v=0, when a=0, Delta=H=3. The new proof does not assume v nonzero at this stage.

Let b=XY^2. Then A_tau=1+b(b+1)k^2 is at least 1, and b is 0 or 2 modulo 3, so A_tau=1 modulo 3. A_main=1 modulo 3, and the normalized A_f is 1 modulo 3. In the normalized construction Q=(Delta I)^2 is a multiple of 9 and Q-1 cannot vanish. In every ordinary construction the unit-product finalizer first gives S=0. Hence Q=I^2=Delta(f^2-1) is a square divisible by 3, so is divisible by 9. Again Q-1 cannot vanish. We therefore have only Q=0 or Q>1, and

    A_y=(QV^2-1)/(Q-1)=1 modulo 3 in Z_(3).

When Q=0, A_y=1 exactly. No auxiliary positivity claim is needed before root extraction; negative rational radicands are permitted in the algebraic separation argument.

## Fixed-prime separation really applies to every changed subset

The relevant weighted root squares have distinct orders at 3:

- d0/H has square order -2*v_3(H), when the main root is folded;
- y has square order 0;
- 3*tau has square order 2;
- 9*f has square order 4 in the normalized packet;
- a folded guard 9*p1 or 27*p1 has square order at least 4 or 6, or is zero.

For ordinary15 and centered16, omit the main root when it is retained explicitly. For centered15 divide the factor equality by 2U; its roots are d0/H, 3*tau and y. Every subset of at least two nonzero roots has a unique smallest square order, and this smallest order is even. A subset containing only a guard root is a nonzero singleton and cannot sum to zero.

To prove the lemma, divide a putative signed sum by 3^e so that its unique smallest square is a unit in Z_(3), while all other squares lie in 3*Z_(3). The product over the signs of the other roots is an integer polynomial in the squared roots. Modulo 3 it is a positive power of the unit square, hence not zero. Thus none of those signed sums can vanish. This proof works for negative radicands and dependent squareclasses.

The field generated by the finitely many square roots is a finite Galois extension of Q. Subtracting the image of a rational factor equality under an automorphism gives twice a signed sum of its changed roots. The preceding lemma forces that changed set to be empty. Every root is therefore rational. Rational square roots of integer radicands are integers; in particular the recovered guard is nonnegative without having assumed it so.

For the rational auxiliary root, write y=A/m in lowest terms. The equation QV^2-(Q-1)y^2=1 implies m^2 divides Q-1. If Q=0 then y=+/-1. If Q>1, V=0 is impossible. After taking positive signs, when V>1 the transformation

    V'=(2Q-1)V-2(Q-1)y,
    y'=(2Q-1)y-2QV

preserves the equation, makes V' integral, and gives 0<|V'|<V. The inequalities follow from y>V and QV>(Q-1)y. The reduced denominator of y' remains m, because 2Q-1=1 modulo m. Descending reaches |V|=1, when y=+/-1. Hence m=1. This proves auxiliary integrality before using the carrier congruences.

In the 13- and 14-variable packets, the factor equality is

where L is the appropriate integral weighted sum of f,tau,y,p1. Reduction modulo M=2q^2 gives M dividing v-q^2 because H is invertible modulo M. Reduction modulo H gives H dividing d0-b0 because M is invertible modulo H. Therefore

    v=q^2(2v_old+1), Y=(2v_old+1)q^3,
    gamma=(d0-b0)/H is an integer.

For ordinary15 and centered16 the same reduction modulo M works without a main quotient because gamma is retained. For centered15, the main terms are 2Mb0 and 2Md0. Since H is odd, 2M remains invertible modulo H, so exactly the same main quotient is recovered. The possibly odd value of n need not be prescribed in advance.

Now v is nonzero. The recovered or explicit guard row is

       =1+p1^2+p2^2+p3(p3+1)>0.

## Unit-product finalizers

Every factor of an integer product equal to 1 is +1 or -1. If all residuals except N are never -2, their factors 1+R cannot equal -1, so they all equal 1, and the unrestricted factor 1+N must also equal 1. Therefore N and every other residual vanish.

Here S and D are never 2 modulo 4, and 4G is always 0 modulo 4. Explicitly, because Delta=3 modulo 4, S is I^2-1 for even f and I^2 for odd f. For even c, D=d0^2-1 is 0 or 3 modulo 4; for odd c, D=d0^2 is 0 or 1. This establishes the finalizers over the entire integer domain. N itself is unrestricted; asserting N!=2 modulo 4 is unnecessary.

### Normalized13

Pair the two signs of the auxiliary root first. Write Z=UV+s and let

    B=s +/-9U f +/-3U tau +/-M d0 +/-27U p1,
    s=Un+Mb0+H(v-q^2).

The weights of the four formal roots f,tau,d0,p1 are 7,11,5,14. Their squared radicands have at most twice these weights, so reductions in the root algebra never increase weighted degree. Thus B has weight at most 20. The paired factor is exactly

    delta*((UV+B)^2-U^2*Cy/delta)
      =delta*B^2+2delta*U*V*B-U^2*(V^2-1).

The equality uses Cy=(delta+1)V^2-1 and is checked as an integer polynomial identity. The three term bounds are respectively 62,67,50. Taking the product over the sixteen signs of the other four roots therefore gives degree at most 16*67=1072. That product is exactly the cleared five-root norm, including its delta^16 factor.

### Centered15

Use the exact centered two-root identity, whose integer expansion is separately checked. In that identity replace M by U, t0 by 2u+k, ell by k^2-4, and x by

    x=Un+2Mb0+H(v-q^2) +/-2M d0.

Its weight is at most 7. Define

    j=delta*(x^2+U(6t0+4V)x+9U^2 ell)-4U^2(V^2-1),
    h=12delta*t0*V*U^2.

Then j has weight at most 30 and h at most 36. Every term of

    j^2+2hj+576delta U^4 A_tau(1-V^2)
          +144delta^2 U^4 ell V^2

has weight at most 66. The exact identity uses t0^2=4A_tau+ell and holds without any Pell zero assumptions. Taking the two main-root conjugates gives the cleared three-root norm of degree at most 132. Adding the product-finalizer factors contributes at most 10+28, giving 170.
