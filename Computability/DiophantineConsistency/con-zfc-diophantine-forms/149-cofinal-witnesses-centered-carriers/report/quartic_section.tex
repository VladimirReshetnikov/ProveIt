\subsection{A degree-three obstruction, not a general lower bound}
For the 178 residuals in 246 variables, form the integer matrix of the
degree-two and degree-three coefficients of
\[
 R_i,\ x_0R_i,\ldots,x_{245}R_i\qquad(0\le i<178).
\]
There are $178(246+1)=43,966$ columns.  An independent sparse elimination
finds full column rank modulo the prime $1,000,003$.  Therefore an integer
minor is nonzero, and the matrix has full column rank over $\mathbb Q$.
It follows that
\[
 \deg\!\left(\sum_i L_iR_i\right)\le1,
 \quad L_i\text{ affine over }\mathbb Q
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad L_i=0\ \text{for all }i.
\]
This rules out \emph{all affine-polynomial multipliers}, including the
nonlinear combinations $x_jR_i$, not only constant row combinations as in
the prior round.  It does not rule out higher-degree multipliers, semantic
witness replacements, a different source circuit, or smaller quartics by
other methods.  A deliberately duplicated residual must lose rank.
