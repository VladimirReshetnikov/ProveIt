# Modular binomial certificates through a cofinal Pell frame

## Status and precise scope

This note proves the new arithmetic transformation used in `modular_binomial.py`.
It replaces the eleven **full numerator powers** `(q+1)^n` used by the supplied
eight-column direct C222 proof certificate with residues of already indexed Pell
coordinates. It does not change the C222 logical source, factorial identities,
CRT product identity, local nonnegative source polynomial, or their calibration.
The inherited low-digit estimates are retained. The final exported object is an
ordinary quartic over nonnegative integers, because every displayed multiplication
is materialized by quadratic gates and every residual is squared and summed.
No variable exponentiation remains in that final object.

The input package is `ConZFC_407_888_Quartics_2026-10-24.zip` from ProveIt commit
`ec80841c07e91be8956ce6ed4af9a9c0b782d3d9`. Its arithmetic source is source690.
The new builder currently creates source655 (12 exact positive-power calls, three
exact binomial calls, eight binomial-divisibility calls, 15 independent Pell
indices). The exported natural equation and its exact projection certificate are
separate finite artifacts; these finite checks do not replace this unbounded proof.

## 1. Notation and inherited range hypotheses

For `A>=2` write `D=A^2-1` and define the Pell coordinates by

    X_0=1, Y_0=0,
    X_(j+1)=A X_j+D Y_j,
    Y_(j+1)=X_j+A Y_j.

Thus `X_j+Y_j s=(A+s)^j` in the ring `Z[s]/(s^2-D)`.

The direct source supplies natural `H,t,x,y,M0`, with `H>=2` and
`H>=k` for every requested binomial lower index `k`. Concretely,
`R=2+t+x+y+M0`, `H=R^8388608`, and lower indices belong to
`{H,t,t+1,y+1,x+1}`. Every upper argument `n` is positive. The source has
one positive CRT modulus `B`. Its recycled radix is

    q=B(V_H+1),
    rho_H=(H+2)^(H+2),
    V_H=(rho_H+1)^H.

The current graph still represents `rho_H,V_H` and every denominator `q^k`
by exact positive-power certificates. The inherited aggregate representative
bound is `sum a_i <= 8B`. Soundness of the existing radix proof establishes,
**in its original dependency order**, that `q>=2`, `B|q`, and

    binom(n,j)<q for all 0<=j<=k

for each of the eleven requested pairs `(n,k)`. Only these lower coefficients
need bounds. In particular we do not need `(q+1)^n` itself to be bounded by q.

The new transformation removes only the graphs for the full powers `(q+1)^n`.
Their indices n remain requested Pell indices. Index correctness is supplied
by the unchanged shared index criterion and exact additive-index identities.

## 2. A cofinal parameter instead of a fixed parameter

Let S be the finite set of semantic atoms appearing as a base, exponent or
output of an **exact** positive-power request retained in the graph. Put

    W=2+sum_(a in S) a,
    Z=z+1,
    L^2-(((W+1)^2-1)(WZ)^2)=1,
    A=L+sigma,   sigma>=0.                         (1)

The old construction used A itself in the norm. We use L as the growth
lower bound and allow A to increase freely above it.

Classifying nonnegative solutions of the norm in (1) gives
`L=X_e(W+1)` and `WZ=Y_e(W+1)`. Since `Y_e(W+1)=e (mod W)`,
`e>0` and `W|e`, so `e>=W`. Consequently

    L >= (W+1)^e >= (W+1)^W > W^W,
    A >= L > W^W.                                 (2)

For every retained exact-power request `(n,k,m)`, all its atoms are at most W.
Its old modulus `T_n=2An-n^2-1` therefore satisfies
`T_n>m` and `T_n>n^k`. The old Pell congruence proves `m=n^k` exactly as
before. The shared index proofs require `A>=2`, positivity of their indices,
and their explicitly retained `Y>=index` rows; they do not require those
indices to be bounded by W. Thus the modular-only indices need not enter S.

Completeness is cofinal: after choosing all genuine semantic values, choose
W as above, set `L=X_W(W+1)`, `Z=Y_W(W+1)/W`, and then choose **any** integer
A>=L. It supplies the natural slack `sigma=A-L`. All original exact-power
and shared-index completeness arguments remain valid for this A.

## 3. One root congruence for every binomial

Let

    b=q+1,  M=q*q^H=q^(H+1).

The denominator `q^H` already occurs in the H-factorial digit graph, so M
needs only one multiplication, not another variable-exponent power graph.
Add a natural quotient h0 and impose

    2 A b = b^2+1+M h0.                            (3)

This means that b is a root of `T^2-2AT+1` modulo M.

**Cofinal solvability of (3).** Since `gcd(b,q)=1`,
`gcd(2b,M)=gcd(2,M)`, which is either one or two. If M is even then q is even
and b is odd, so `b^2+1` is even. Therefore the linear congruence
`2b A = b^2+1 (mod M)` always has a solution. Its solutions form an arithmetic
progression, hence include arbitrarily large A. Choose one above L and b;
then `h0=(2Ab-b^2-1)/M` is a nonnegative integer. This is compatible with
(1) and with all shared Pell-index witnesses. No circular choice is involved:
q,H and their genuine exact powers are chosen before A.

**Uniform residue identity.** Equation (3) makes the assignment `s -> b-A`
a ring homomorphism

    Z[s]/(s^2-(A^2-1)) -> Z/MZ,

because `(b-A)^2-(A^2-1)=b^2-2Ab+1=0 (mod M)`. Applying it to
`(A+s)^n=X_n+Y_n s` proves

    R_n := X_n+(b-A)Y_n = b^n (mod M).             (4)

This is a formal polynomial identity, valid for every n>=0. It does not
require the full value b^n to be stored or bounded.

**Positivity.** For n>=1 we have Y_n>0 and X_n>=0. The norm gives

    X_n^2-(A-1)^2 Y_n^2 = 1+2(A-1)Y_n^2 >0.

Hence `X_n>(A-1)Y_n` and

    R_n > (b-1)Y_n >=0.                            (5)

The signed expression R_n is therefore a positive integer.

## 4. Exact binomial digits

Fix a requested pair `(n,k)` with `1<=k<=H`; put `d=q^k`.
The quotient ring identity (4) holds modulo `qd=q^(k+1)`, because `qd|M`.
Let c be the proposed binomial value. Replace the old full-power digit graph
by natural witnesses h,r,s,o satisfying

    X_n+bY_n = A Y_n+r+d c+qd h,
    d=r+s+1,
    q=c+o+1.                                      (6)

Thus `0<=r<d`, `0<=c<q`, and `r+dc` is the least nonnegative residue of R_n
modulo qd. By (4), it is also the least residue of `(q+1)^n` modulo qd.
The carry-free low-digit hypothesis gives that residue as

    sum_(j<k) binom(n,j) q^j + binom(n,k) q^k,

whose first part lies in `[0,d)`. Uniqueness of Euclidean division by d
therefore gives `c=binom(n,k)` and identifies r. This proves soundness.
Conversely take the actual binomial digit and lower residue. Equation (4)
makes `(R_n-r-dc)/(qd)` an integer; (5) and `0<=r+dc<qd` make it
nonnegative. It supplies h, and the ordinary positive gaps supply s,o.

For k=0 the same argument works with d=1 and r=0, although the source only
requires positive lower indices.

## 5. Binomial divisibility without the digit

For a range request, the needed relation is only `B|binom(n,k)`. Since
`B|q` and `k<=H`, we have

    Bd=B q^k | q^(H+1)=M.

Use natural witnesses h,r,s satisfying

    X_n+bY_n = A Y_n+r+Bd h,
    d=r+s+1.                                      (7)

Equation (7) says the least residue of R_n modulo Bd lies in `[0,d)`.
The same is true of `(q+1)^n` by (4). Its binomial expansion has lower part
r0 in `[0,d)` and higher quotient

    binom(n,k) + q*integer.

Since `B|q`, reduction modulo Bd gives

    r0+d*(binom(n,k) mod B).

This lies in `[0,d)` exactly when `B|binom(n,k)`. Thus (7) is equivalent to
the desired divisibility, using the same low-digit hypothesis as the old
fused certificate. Completeness again follows from (5): the quotient
`(R_n-r)/(Bd)` is nonnegative.

## 6. Soundness order and preservation of the calibrated source

1. The lower growth norm and the unchanged indexed-Pell criteria prove
   correctness of all 12 retained exact powers. In particular the powers
   of q are exact. Their base equation rules out q=0, so `q=B(V_H+1)`
   gives B>=1 before correctness of the CRT product is used.
2. Equation (3) and the recovered Pell coordinates prove (4) simultaneously
   for every requested upper argument n. This statement does not depend on
   correctness of any binomial or factorial.
3. The old factorial low-digit bound validates the H-factorial binomial
   using (6), so its unchanged algebraic identity gives T=H!.
4. Validate the t-factorial similarly. The inherited raw remainder bound
   on B and the aggregate bound `sum a_i<=8B` now prove the remaining
   carry-free low-digit inequalities. This is exactly the old radix proof;
   no final CRT product identity has yet been presumed.
5. Apply (6) to the CRT-product binomial and (7) to the eight range tests.
   Then the old CRT-product, decoding, local C222 certificate and logical
   calibration proofs apply without modification.

Completeness reverses the source construction. Start from a finite calibrated
C222 refutation; choose the existing padded horizon, canonical CRT column
representatives, factorial values, recycled radix and exact denominator
powers. Form W and L from the retained exact-power requests. Choose A
cofinally in the residue class (3), then build the unchanged shared index
witnesses and all rows (6),(7). This constructs a solution of the new raw
quartic. The checked monic contraction preserves existence in both directions.

All arguments are finite elementary arithmetic, Pell recurrences, Euclidean
division, and finite CRT, formalizable in ZFC. They combine with the supplied
C222/refutation calibration to prove the same Con(ZFC) equivalence. They do
not prove that the polynomial has no solutions or that ZFC is consistent.

## 7. Optional signed quotient witnesses

The quotients h in (6),(7) need not initially be required nonnegative in an
integer-domain compilation. Once the other coordinates and bounded residues
are nonnegative, (4),(5) show that the displayed residue is the least one;
then `R_n=residue+positive_modulus*h` forces h>=0. The root quotient h0
also has a recoverable sign: exact q is among the atoms bounded by W, hence
A>W>=q and A>=q+1=b. Thus `2Ab-b^2-1>=b^2-1>0` and (3) forces h0>0.
Removing their independent sign guards is valid if the integer compiler
leaves their product wires signed until this semantic recovery and proves
all other needed signs without using the deleted guards. The separate
mixed-domain certificate must check that dependency condition.
