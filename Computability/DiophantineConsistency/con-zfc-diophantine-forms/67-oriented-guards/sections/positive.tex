\section{One free scale and an index absorber: fourteen positive variables}
\label{sec:positive30}

A useful new tradeoff is a polynomial of exact degree $30$ in fourteen
strictly positive integer unknowns, with $87$ binary arithmetic operations.
The comparison is the existing fourteen-positive degree-$52$, $79$-operation
construction: degree decreases, cost increases. It also saves one variable
relative to the incoming integral-root degree-$24/90$, $26/88$ and $30/79$
constructions. No global minimality or domination is asserted.

The idea is to free \emph{only} the ordinate scale $Y$ using a monic-root
carrier, while retaining $X=wq$. The recovered divisibility $q^3\mid Y$
then permits the adaptive $U=4qY$ index absorber to delete its quotient
witness. Attempting to merge both scale offsets into a single square root
does not imply their separate divisibilities, and is not used here.

\subsection{Literal polynomial}

$\beta=2^{590069}-1$, $K_0=K$, $M_C$, and $M_F=M_{F,\mathrm{source}}$,
with the latter already shifted. Its four actual integer payloads and binding

\[
 J,F,\alpha,z,f,i,T,w,Y,r,\eta,\zeta,u,\gamma.
\]
All remaining symbols are polynomial abbreviations:
\begin{align*}

\begin{align*}

There are no divisions, variable exponents or hidden existential variables
in this literal expression. The five residuals can alternatively be used
as a system of degrees $12,14,10,15,2$, with a shared $78$-operation circuit.

\subsection{Correctness of the new composition}
At a zero, the rational square root
$\tau=\ell+r+Y/q^3$ of the integer $A$ is integral. Hence $q^3\mid Y$;
write $Y=sq^3$ with $s>0$. This recovery precedes every application of an
old scale-dependent bound. The transport equation gives the inherited
whole-tuple bounds
\[
 q\ge16,\quad C>0,\quad 3q<R<q^4-2,\quad E\ge q^4>R+2.
\]
The first Pell equation supplies
$\tau=\chi_{P_0}(n)$ and $k=2\psi_{P_0}(n)$, where
$P_0=2XY^2+1$ and $n>0$.
The second residual makes $g=D_*/U$ integral. It is positive, because
\[
 D_*>U(X+ac)-Hk>ak(UY-5)>0.
\]
Thus $g=\chi_{a+2}(p)$ and $c=\psi_{a+2}(p)$ for $p>0$.
The absorber gives $U\mid H(k-R-1)$ without cancelling $H$.
The adaptive modulus satisfies $U\ge4q^4$, $U\mid4XY^2$ and
$\gcd(U,H)\le3$. For the last claim, $\gcd(Y,H)\mid3$; every prime
of $q$ divides $Y$; and if $3\mid q$ then $27\mid Y$, so $H\equiv3$
modulo $27$. These are precisely the properties needed by the retained
absorber proof. Moreover,
$k\equiv2n\pmod{4XY^2}$, with $U\mid4XY^2$. Consequently
\[
 \mu=\frac{U}{\gcd(U,H)}>R+1,\qquad
 2n\equiv R+1\pmod\mu,\qquad n\ge(R+1)/2\ge25.
\]
The standard first/main Pell comparison gives $p>n$, and therefore
$c>(a+2)\Delta^2$, $c>2p$ and $c>2R$.
These bounds justify the inherited \emph{ordinary} strong-rank lemma for
$(ic^2)^2=\Delta(f^2-1)$, yielding a main Pell index $m$ with
$p\mid m$, $c\mid m$, $c^2\mid f^2-1$ and $m\ge c>2p$.

Set $y=V+u$. The auxiliary identity is exactly
\[
 QV^2-(Q-1)y^2=1.
\]
Although $V$ need not be positive, $y$ is: the identity gives
$|y|\ge|V|$, while $u=y-V>0$ rules out $y<0$; zero is impossible.
With $T_{\rm old}=fT$, the signed expression
$V=c(T_{\rm old}f-1)-Rf^2$ is the inherited auxiliary expression.
Its index proof gives $p=R$; the absorber then gives $n<R$ and
$2n=R+1$. Hence
\[
 h=\frac{k-R-1}{E}>0,\qquad
 \gamma_{\rm old}=\gamma-\frac{k-R-1}{U}
\]
are integral and recover the original equations.
The positivity of the latter is not inferred from integrality:
$k\ge4P_0>X$, $c>k>X$ and $g>(a+1)c$ imply
$g-(X+ac)>c-X>0$, so $\gamma_{\rm old}>0$.
The inherited ordinary compiler therefore produces a finite ZFC refutation.

Conversely, a finite refutation supplies canonical compact-tuple witnesses.
Convert the normalized strong ordinate by $i=\Delta i_0$, and use the
absorber shift $\gamma=\gamma_{\rm old}+(k-R-1)/U>0$.
The Integral Root positive-gap lemma gives $\tau>\ell+s$, so
$r=\tau-\ell-s>0$. The retained squared-modulus completeness proof chooses
$T_{\rm old}$ divisible by $f$ and $y>V$, giving positive
$T=T_{\rm old}/f$ and $u=y-V$. This is an existential choice of canonical
witnesses, not a same-coordinate identity for arbitrary old witnesses.
All five residuals vanish. The imported compiler calibration and this
composition prove in ZFC that $P_{14}$ has a positive zero exactly when
there is a syntactic ZFC refutation; negation yields the desired equivalence
to $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})$.

\subsection{Degree and replay boundaries}
Writing $Z=UX+H(U\gamma-v)$ gives exact identities
\begin{align*}
 A_0&=q^6[r^2+\ell(2r-k)-1]+2q^3(\ell+r)Y+Y^2,\\
 A_1&=2UacZ+Z^2-U^2(Hc^2+1).
\end{align*}

For the second, $Z$ has leading term $H_0R_0$ of degree $7$, where
$R_0=(\beta J)^3(\beta J-F)$; its term $H_0U_0\gamma$ has only degree $6$.
The auxiliary residual has degree $15$, with leading form
$2E_0^2R_0f^4u$, where $E_0=w\beta JY$.
Hence the unique degree-$30$ homogeneous part of $P_{14}$ is
\[
 4w^4(\beta J)^{10}Y^4(\beta J-F)^2 f^8u^2,
\]
which is nonzero for the actual positive $\beta$.
The primary circuit uses $47$ multiplications, $24$ additions and
$16$ subtractions; the cancellation certificate is a separate circuit,
not the advertised evaluator.
