# Scope and unproved variable-reduction avenues

This packet proves a smaller-degree equation with thirteen unrestricted integers. It does not establish a twelve-integer Jones equation, optimality, or a globally simplest consistency equation.

## The zero spectator is a real branch of the abstract norm

Take a0=1, d1=d2=2, z1=1, z2=2. Then C1=9 and C2=2601=51^2. If a1=0, the final factor B3 is1 regardless of the sign of d3, and C3 remains a square. Thus it is invalid to infer the last strict sign condition from square recovery alone. The source-positivity bootstrap in section.tex is essential: after the first two guards, it forces A>=2 and C>0 and hence a1>0 before the final sign is recovered.

## The a0 factor cannot simply be dropped from the nonzero multiplier

There is an explicit false-zero family if a0 is omitted from Z while the other formulas remain unchanged. Set epsilon=w=i=z1=z2=z3=1 and g=0. Let the source determine R,N. Set s=0, h=R, phi=1-(2R+1)-2 and choose any j. Then b=2, P=A=0, K=C=1, a0=D=E=0, F=G=I=1 and a1=0. Also J=-5 and Q=0. All three stack prefixes vanish. With residue r=0 and normroot v=-23, one has Theta=-23 J^5, Sigma=J^5, and X=0, making the raw norm zero. Including a0 in Z instead makes Sigma=0 and the output Theta^4 nonzero, correctly excluding this branch.

## Unproved routes to twelve variables

- Replacing s by a square would remove its sign witness, but the Jones completeness theorem does not assert that its canonical s is a square. Cofinal freedom of the base alone is not a proof of this extra arithmetic restriction.
- Identifying the intersective residue r with j would require compatible Pell congruences modulo a multiplier that itself contains guard witnesses, including the a1-dependent final Pell witness. Existing cofinal Pell growth does not prove that compatibility.
- Dropping the phi guard would require a signed extension of the product-Pell index theorem and a proof that no negative or small C branches survive the interval relation. No such extension is proved here.

These are research questions, not steps used in the equivalence proof.
