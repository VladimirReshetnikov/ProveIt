                   A degree-separated equation in nine unknowns
                                   Explicit Diophantine consistency reduction

                                                                  Abstract
          A new degree-separated source code combines with sparse carry padding, an even-parameter
      parity calibration, and a positivity-first Pell finalizer to produce an explicit integer polynomial in nine
      positive unknowns of exact degree 2,676,548. Its supplied circuit uses 476 binary arithmetic operations.
      Against the previous degree 7,344,328 and 738-operation construction, these are reductions of 63.56%
      and 35.50%. All fixed coefficient payloads, finite certificates, and authored rebuilding and checking
      programs are supplied. Absence of zeros is provably equivalent in ZFC to Con(ZFC), using the
      identified inherited compiler and Jones ratio theorems. No proof of consistency or global minimality
      is claimed.


1     A degree-separated nine-variable equation
The primary equation has nine strictly positive unknowns, exact total degree 2 676 548, and 476 live binary
operations. Its separately translated nonnegative form has the same degree. The complete division-free
DAGs, coefficient payloads, exact-degree certificates, and a standard-library checker accompany this
section. Register names below abbreviate polynomials; they introduce no additional unknowns.

Scope. The ordinary mathematical theorem is that ZFC proves existence of a zero in the stated domain
equivalent to existence of a finite syntactic ZFC refutation. Its negation is the requested consistency
equivalence. The original package31 compiler and the numerical Jones ratio theorems are explicit
imported dependencies. This is not a proof of consistency, a new end-to-end kernel formalization, an
unrestricted-integer nine-variable claim, or a global minimum claim.
