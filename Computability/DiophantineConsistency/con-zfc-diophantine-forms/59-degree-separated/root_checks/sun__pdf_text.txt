1     Degree-separated coding: a ten-integer equation
There is a completely fixed integer polynomial

                                             F10 (f, g, h, k, l, w, x, y, z, m)

of exact total degree 8 469 528, evaluated by a division-free circuit of 533 binary arithmetic operations, for
which ordinary mathematics formalizable in ZFC proves

                      ¬∃(f, g, h, k, l, w, x, y, z, m) ∈ Z10 (F10 = 0)            ⇐⇒    Con(ZFC).

This is a new source-coding architecture, not a new unknown-count theorem. The inherited compiler
calibration and Sun arithmetic bridge remain stated mathematical dependencies. No consistency decision,
global minimality claim, or new end-to-end proof-assistant certificate is asserted.
    The structural change is to split the extraction convolution by homogeneous degree. A linear label may
now collide with a quadratic label: their kernels are separate summands. This relaxes the finite isolation
problem rather than merely finding a better placement for the previous problem. Elementary row operations,
one shared positivity shift, borrow-tolerant target padding, and exact mask-population calibration complete
the construction.

are exact polynomial identities, not a relaxation of the equation.
