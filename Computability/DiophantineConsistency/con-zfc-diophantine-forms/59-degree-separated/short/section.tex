\subsection{Why the combinations preserve existence}
The exact all-value pullback is
\[
 c((fT)f-1)-Rf^2=f^2(cT-R)-c.
\]
Thus a new positive quotient gives the old positive quotient $T_{\rm old}=fT$.
Completeness is not the assertion that every old quotient is divisible by $f$.
Instead, at a canonical compiler witness, write $A=a+2$ and
$c=\psi_A(R)$, where $R\equiv3\pmod4$. Ordinary strong rank supplies
\[
 I^2=\Delta(f^2-1),\qquad c\mid I,\qquad f^2\equiv1\pmod c.
\]
For $R=2j+1$ the integer polynomial $Q_j$ defined by
$\chi_t(R)=tQ_j(t^2)$ satisfies
\[
 Q_j(0)=(-1)^jR,\qquad Q_j(1-A^2)=(-1)^j\psi_A(R).
\]
The recurrence $Q_{j+1}=(4Z-2)Q_j-Q_{j-1}$, with $Q_0=1$ and
$Q_1=4Z-3$, proves both identities. Since $j$ is odd, set
\[
 V_*=\frac{\chi_I(R)}{I},\qquad y_* =\psi_I(R).
\]
The quotient is integral, $V_*\equiv-c\pmod{f^2}$ and
$V_*\equiv-R\pmod c$. Since $\gcd(c,f)=1$, the number
\[
 T_*=\frac{V_*+c+Rf^2}{cf^2}
\]
is a positive integer and satisfies the new quotient formula. The auxiliary
norm remains one. This argument works with the ordinary strong coordinate;
it never divides an arbitrary supplied $i$ by $\Delta$.

The root gaps use exact polynomial identities:
\begin{align*}
 (L+r)^2-L(L+k)&=r^2+L(2r-k),\\
 A_0\bigl(V^2-(V+u)^2\bigr)+(V+u)^2
