\section{Validation and reproducible archive}
The root verifier authenticates the complete release, copies it into a
temporary directory, and executes 25 finite replay stages without modifying
the sealed originals. The stages cover kernel reconstruction, source and
circuit identities, independent literal evaluators, exact degree witnesses,
coefficient bindings, guard counterexamples and a cross-family metadata
check. Generated JSON circuits are compared byte-for-byte with the delivered
versions. The machine-readable stage and output lists are authoritative.

The standard root run uses ordinary Python. The nine-variable primary
verifier deliberately rejects \texttt{-O} and \texttt{-OO}; that rejection
is documented and was tested, not misreported as a successful optimized
proof. Its separate independent checker uses explicit checks and passes in
both modes. Other component receipts distinguish normal and optimized
runs. NumPy is required for one direct-merger diagnostic; the other canonical
families and the direct merger's independent symbolic checker use the Python
standard library. The late thirteen-positive authored checker additionally
uses SymPy; its separate independent review is standard-library only.
Tested versions are Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5 and SymPy 1.14.0.

The strongest local checks are exact identities, not sample evaluations.
The split-source checker parses the original 22 residuals, applies and
inverts the two row operations, authenticates all 89 coefficient records,
and verifies all 22,544 interactions and 22 formal target identities.
The direct merger's independent checker expands its complete formal norm,
including zero, negative and coincident specializations. Degree certificates
combine valid upper bounds with nonzero leading homogeneous forms or
specializations modulo primes; merely evaluating random points would not
establish exact degree.

Finite examples additionally stress source bounds, signed borrows, zero
spectators, CRT parametrizations, and positive-coordinate reconstruction.
Ten deliberately corrupted source/code cases are rejected. Three separate
root corruption controls test release integrity only. These distinctions
are retained in the receipts: a file-hash rejection is not an independent
number-theoretic proof, and a finite test is not a consistency decision.

All primary source31 bindings agree on the same four actual payloads.
The nine-variable comparison tuple and the late thirteen-positive compact
tuple are explicitly separate. Active proof
and payload paths are checked after copying to an isolated directory;
historical metadata is identified as inert provenance. No network access
is needed for replay. The imported machine/compiler and representation
results retain immutable references and original notices; the replay does
not claim to rebuild their theorem-prover developments.

The PDF is compiled from the supplied TeX, inspected page by page, and
packaged with the explicit circuits, coefficient payloads, proofs and
checks. Rebuilding a PDF may change its metadata. The final archive is
re-extracted and replayed before delivery; its checksum is supplied outside
the archive to avoid a self-referential checksum.
