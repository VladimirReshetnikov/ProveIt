\section{Scope and the exact logical claim}
The objective is an explicit fixed polynomial $P$ with integer coefficients
such that existence of a zero is equivalent to existence of a finite syntactic
refutation of ZFC. For the domain $D$ stated for each construction, the
corresponding ordinary arithmetic theorem gives
\[
 \mathsf{ZFC}\vdash
 \bigl(\neg\exists\mathbf x\in D^n\;P(\mathbf x)=0\bigr)
 \longleftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC}).
\]
This is an equivalence, not a proof of either side. No consistency assumption
is used to establish the reductions. The new transformations are ordinary
unbounded proofs relative to explicitly retained source calibrations; the
packet does not supply a new end-to-end Lean, Rocq or encoded object-language
ZFC certificate. The distinction concerns substantive inherited calibration
theorems, not merely a missing formal proof file.

The report compares three distinct domains: nonnegative integers, strictly
positive integers and unrestricted integers. A count in one domain is not
silently transferred to another. A displayed polynomial abbreviation is not
an extra unknown. Intermediate arithmetic DAG registers denote substitutions
into the final polynomial, not existentially quantified values. Proof-only
Pell sequences, divisions and radicals are not operations in the supplied
division-free polynomial graphs.

Operation counts mean live binary additions, subtractions and multiplications.
Squaring and multiplication by any fixed integer each count as one
multiplication. Constant payload size and circuit cost are separate metrics;
the compact native coefficient equations use enormous fixed numerals, whereas
the direct calculus quartics have fully expanded, much smaller coefficients.
There is no single ordering that makes one construction uniformly simplest.

For Pell notation, for $b\ge2$ and $m\ge0$ write
\[
 \chi_b(m)+\psi_b(m)\sqrt{b^2-1}
   =(b+\sqrt{b^2-1})^m.
\]
Different source sections also call these coordinates $X_m(b),Y_m(b)$.
They are used only in the mathematical proofs.
