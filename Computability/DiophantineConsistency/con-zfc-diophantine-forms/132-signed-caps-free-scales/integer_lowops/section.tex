\subsection{The sign branch and noncircular square recovery}
Unconditionally, $D_m>0$, $D_\tau\ge1$, $P$ is odd and nonzero,
and $\delta,D_f,D_a$ are nonzero. The zero-free quintic makes
$\mathcal A\ne0$; at $S=0$ the output is $\delta\mathcal A^2\ne0$.
Thus $r,s\ne0$ at any zero.

The three integers $B_P,D_f,D_a$ are pairwise coprime, with no sign
assumption: $D_f=D_a=1$ modulo $B_P$, and modulo $D_f$ one has
$D_a=A^2D_m$. Since $A\mid U$ and $D_m\mid B_P$, $D_f$ is coprime
to both factors of this remainder. Equation~\eqref{eq:lowops-product}
gives
\[
 (\delta Z/S)^2=B_PD_fD_a.
\]
Their absolute values are therefore separately squares. But
$\Delta A^2=(A^2-1)A^2$ is divisible by4, so $D_f\equiv1\pmod4$.
A negative square is impossible, proving $D_f>0$.
If $c\ne0$, a negative $B_P$ would make
$D_f=1+\Delta U^2B_P<0$. If $c=0$, then $D_m=D_f=D_a=1$ and the
product itself makes $B_P=P$ positive. Thus in all cases $B_P>0$, $P>0$,
and $D_a>0$. Write
\[
 p_0^2=B_P,\qquad f^2=D_f,\qquad e^2=D_a.
\]
Now $D_g>0$ implies $\mathcal B\ge2$. The centered strip excludes
$c=0$, because the literal $Y$ is nonzero.

Set $I=Up_0$ and $T_{\rm old}=p_0T$. These restore the exact strong and
auxiliary formulas; adjacent-Pell descent makes $y=e/\delta$ integral.

\emph{before} assuming the first-Pell square.
The early absolute-folding theorem uses only
