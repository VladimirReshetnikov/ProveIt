\section{Literal polynomial conventions}
The JSON files are mathematical definitions, not merely summaries of an implementation. For each natural or signed direct endpoint, its \nolinkurl{equation.json} ordered list \texttt{variables} fixes the independent coordinates $x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}$. A term \texttt{[c,[i,j]]} means $c x_i x_j$, a repeated index gives a power, and an empty index list means the constant $c$. Every integer coefficient is stored in full. A residual row is the sum of its listed terms. The equation is exactly
\[
 P(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})=\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}R_r(x)^2=0.
\]
The separately supplied \nolinkurl{expanded_polynomial.json} is the collected sum, including its constant coefficient. In that expanded SOS file, \texttt{variables} stores the coordinate count; the ordered names remain in the adjacent equation file. Thus the displayed definition has no unbounded product, variable exponent, factorial, binomial, unspecified numeral, or quantifier as an algebraic primitive. Those operations occur only in the mathematical interpretation of zeros. The final rows have degree at most two, and the supplied nonzero quartic coefficients prove the exact final degree, rather than only an upper bound.

The height-one endpoints instead store the final collected polynomial directly. Their certificates list every vertex, edge, factor occurrence, equality class and unit path. The report proves the exact half-sum-of-squares identity of this polynomial, and the independent checker recollects that identity. It does not replace the published coefficient file by an informal graph specification. All nonzero coefficients are exactly $1$ or $-1$, the constant coefficient is exactly $1$, and every counted variable occurs.

For the eleven-integer equations, the complete acyclic arithmetic graph is the ordinary polynomial definition. A leaf is an integer constant, one of eleven independent integer variables, or one of six \emph{fixed} numerical ports. Each internal gate is binary addition, subtraction or multiplication. The output recursively denotes a unique element of $\Z[j,C,z,h,r,v,w,T,c,n,s]$. Named registers are abbreviations, not new unknowns. There is no division or radical gate. Cost counts live binary operations, including squares and multiplication by numerical constants; copying a register and reading a fixed leaf are free.

The large numerical values affect coefficient height but do not add existential variables. For the scalar graphs the exact degree and operation count are certified, with an explicitly attained highest-degree coefficient; their full collected support and overall coefficient height are \emph{not} claimed to have been materialized.

\subsection{Reading the proof text}
The new natural, signed and scalar proofs below are included unabridged. Literal ASCII formulas preserve exponent carets, inequalities and subscripts exactly, with presentation-only line wrapping. The independent character checker removes Markdown and TeX presentation separately and compares every remaining non-whitespace character. The height-one proof is native mathematical TeX; its exact source is separately retained. Complete inherited proof sources are included in the archive, with an active dependency map rather than an assertion that every historical file is part of the new result.
