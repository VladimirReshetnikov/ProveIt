\subsection{Height one and small-variable objectives}
The incoming shared-incidence height-one example uses 2215 unrestricted integer variables and 30468 collected monomials. The new incidence-local constructions preserve height one and exact quartic degree while offering smaller dimension, with support tradeoffs disclosed in the results table. This is a coefficient-height result, not a claim to have fewer variables than the direct signed quartics. A coefficient-height-one equation with hundreds of additional variables is not universally simpler. A matched-source control isolates the compiler improvement: the same 442-variable signed source that underlies the incoming 2215/30468 example gives 1254 variables and 27714 terms under the new compiler profile. The further gains at 793/51982,806/23719 and 994/11744 also use the improved 287- and 309-variable sources and selected exact affine refactorizations. The archive retains the matched source and reproduces its comparison polynomial and certificate by hash.

The inspected incoming eleven-integer linear-multiplier points include degree/cost 440/126 and 412/127; the free-scale point is 352/132. The new 352/125 point has no larger degree and fewer operations than each of these, under the reported live-binary-gate convention. The eleven-variable cofactor family is a separate high-degree objective. Its degree/cost pairs are 328/130, 340/129, 352/125 and 398/121. Smaller degree is not automatically smaller arithmetic cost. The preceding Round16 product and two-root points include 966/109, 882/110, 810/112 and 654/117, so these remain relevant low-cost comparisons. Positive-domain ten-variable equations are not mixed into the unrestricted-integer frontier. Expanded scalar support and total coefficient height were not computed and are not assigned artificial zero values in a comparison.
