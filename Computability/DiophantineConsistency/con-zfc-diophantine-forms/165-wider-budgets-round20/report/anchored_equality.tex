\subsection{Absorbing equality dummies into anchored products}
A different new operation removes an equality dummy without changing any
prescribed square label. Suppose two aliases $a,b$ of one non-unit logical
value are tied by a fresh dummy $d$ through
\[
 C(a,b,d)=a^2+b^2+d^2-ab-bd-da.
\]
Assume $d$ occurs nowhere else. Let $u$ be an existing unit alias already
anchored to one by $U$. Substitute $d=au$ and delete the coordinate $d$.
The new equality penalty is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchored-cycle}
 \widetilde C(a,b,u)
 =a^2+b^2+a^2u^2-ab-abu-a^2u.
\end{equation}
It has degree four and is the literal polynomial $C(a,b,au)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:anchored-equality}
Under the stated incidence restrictions, replacing $C(a,b,d)$ by
\eqref{eq:anchored-cycle} preserves the integer zero set by a polynomial
bijection after deleting one coordinate. It also preserves the universal
source lift identity \eqref{eq:networklift}. If the entire resulting
collected polynomial has height one, exact degree four and all declared
coordinates present, it is a certified height-one quartic with one fewer
independent integer variable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For arbitrary real $a,b,u$,
\[
 2\widetilde C=(a-b)^2+(b-au)^2+(au-a)^2\ge0.
\]
At a zero of the complete energy, its unchanged unit penalty forces
$u=1$. The new equality penalty then vanishes exactly when $a=b$.
Thus the old dummy has the unique value $d=a=b$, while every other
summand is unchanged. Extending with $d=a$ maps a new zero to an old
zero. Conversely, at an old zero $d=a=b$ and $u=1$, so deleting $d$
gives a new zero. These maps are inverse polynomial maps on the zero sets.

For a canonical source lift, all unit aliases equal one and all ordinary
aliases equal their logical values. Consequently $au=a=d$ identically
on that lift, including away from source zeros. No prescribed incidence
label or divergence has changed, so the universal lift identity is retained.

Polynomial substitution preserves nonnegativity but not automatically
coefficient height. The term $a^2u^2$ may collide with graph-energy terms,
and several absorbed dummies may share a unit alias. The acceptance test
therefore collects the whole energy after every proposed substitution,
including all other penalties. It explicitly checks the finite claims
about coefficients, degree and the live coordinate set. No extra unit
coordinate is introduced: $u$ was already present and anchored.
\end{proof}

Several such absorptions can be composed, using the same or different
existing unit aliases, provided the full polynomial test accepts the
frozen final choices. Unlike Round~19 polarization, this operation leaves
the prescribed square monomials in the incidence graph untouched. Unlike
a formal substitution $d=a$ alone, it does not insert a coefficient $-2$
into the equality row before the complete energy is considered. Its
quartic product with an already anchored unit provides additional distinct
monomials that can retain height one.
