\begin{abstract}
We test new mechanisms rather than only reoptimize positional weights. The
strongest completed polynomial is a sextic in \textbf{34 unrestricted integer
unknowns}, improving the reviewed 35-variable sextic. Its key step deliberately
allows a negative power coordinate, recovers its absolute value by signed Pell
orbits, and only then excludes the negative branch by a Mersenne congruence.
A cleared transport equation absorbs a quotient without losing integrality.
Two positive-domain compositions give degree 30 in 13 unknowns at 94 operations,
and degree 114 in 12 unknowns at 105 operations. These are modest numerical
improvements, not the large breakthrough sought.

Two other mechanisms are proved at the interface level: inert-prime CRT
batching for bounded universal certificates, and a fixed-input boundary
absorbed by an existing affine coordinate. Neither has yet been compiled
into a smaller complete ordinary polynomial. We give their precise remaining
obligations, a failed attempt to merge two Pell roots, and dominated
mixed-degree examples so that unsuccessful ideas are not mistaken for
records. All consistency claims use explicit inherited arithmetic/compiler
results; this is not a new end-to-end proof-assistant certificate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Objective and what this iteration actually establishes}
Let $\N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$ and $\N_{>0}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$. Our target is an
explicit polynomial $P$ with fixed integer coefficients and a declared domain
$D$ such that
\[
\mathrm{ZFC}\vdash
\bigl(\neg\exists \boldsymbol{x}\in D^n\ P(\boldsymbol{x})=0\bigr)
\longleftrightarrow\Con(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
This is an equivalence, not a proof of either consistency or absence of zeros.
The coefficient integers are not chosen depending on the truth of consistency.
No unknown is hidden in a coefficient, function call, or register abbreviation.
For the three completed constructions, the JSON files specify literal
polynomials or arithmetic DAGs, and coefficient bindings identify the full
inherited tuple. In contrast, special-function certificates below are
explicitly not counted as ordinary polynomials.

There is no single scalar notion of ``simplest.'' We compare the domain,
number of unknowns, total degree, live arithmetic-gate count, coefficient
size, and proof dependencies. The signed sextic wins one variable, not degree;
the positive constructions lower degree at a one-operation cost. No global
minimality or first-in-literature claim is made. ``New'' refers to the
mechanism developed in this reviewed iteration, not an exhaustive priority
search.

\subsection{Three distinct levels of assurance}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Exact finite checks: symbolic identities, degrees, live-gate counts,
coefficient hashes, and deliberately corrupted controls.
\item Unbounded mathematical proofs: signed-power recovery, witness
reconstruction, CRT soundness and completeness, and conditional boundary
transport. These establish more than the finite tests alone can establish.
\item Imported endpoint calibration: the inherited native compiler and
Pell/source equivalences connect the arithmetic source to finite ZFC
refutations. We identify these dependencies rather than replacing them by
an unproved assertion that a universal machine ``exists.''
\end{enumerate}
None of these statements claims that the new composite endpoint has been
accepted by Lean or Rocq. In particular finite tests cannot certify the
nonexistence of a counterexample to an unbounded arithmetic lemma.

\section{A compact complete specification of the new sextic}
This section gives the entire 19-residual equation, not only a recipe for
finding it. The four fixed coefficient ports are
$\beta,K_0,M_C,M_F$, bound as a complete inherited source34 tuple in the
JSON artifact and its local upstream provenance. In particular
$\beta=2^{1064921}-1$; the other ports are supplied by their authenticated
coefficient payloads and exact recipes, not arbitrary parameters.

The 34 unrestricted unknowns are
\[
\begin{gathered}
J,F,\alpha,f,h,r,v,\tau,\eta,\zeta,y,\gamma,o,j,E,c,a,D,L,V,I,B,\Delta,u,Z,\\
s_1,s_2,s_3,t_1,t_2,t_3,w_1,w_2,w_3.
\end{gathered}
\]
Here $v$ is \texttt{v\_scale}, $B$ is \texttt{Baux}, and the three triples
are respectively the JSON \texttt{FA\_pos}, \texttt{FC\_pos}, and
\texttt{eta\_zeta\_pos} coordinates. Define polynomial abbreviations
\[
\begin{gathered}
q=\beta J+1,\quad k=\eta+\zeta,\quad H=4a+3,\quad C=q-F-\alpha,\\
X_*=D-ac-H\gamma,\qquad
R_*=[(q-1)(q-F)+\alpha]L+J(M_C+qM_F),\\
\Pi(x_1,x_2,x_3)=x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2+x_3+1.
\end{gathered}
\]
The positive-value representation $\Pi$ is the inherited three-coordinate
guard: its range on $\Z^3$ is exactly the positive integers. Its use here
requires that precise theorem, not merely that every displayed value is
positive.
Let the residuals be
\begin{align*}
 e_1&=L-q^2+1,\\
 e_2&=a-E-(2v+1)q(L+1),\\
 e_3&=E(X_*+1)-aX_*,\\
 e_4&=c-k(a-E)-\eta,\\
 e_5&=\tau^2+(2\tau-k)E(c-\eta)-1,\\
 e_6&=\Delta-a^2-4a-3,\\
 e_7&=D^2-\Delta c^2-1,\\
 e_8&=k-1-Eh-R_*,\\
 e_9&=(K_0q+X_*)C-qF-Z(q-1),\\
 e_{10}&=V+c-fo,\\
 e_{11}&=V-cj+k-1-Eh,\\
 e_{12}&=I-(2r+1)c^2,\\
 e_{13}&=B-I^2,\\
 e_{14}&=B-\Delta f^2+\Delta,\\
 e_{15}&=BV^2-By^2+y^2-1,\\
 e_{16}&=X_*-4qu^2,\\
 e_{17}&=F\alpha-\Pi(s_1,s_2,s_3),\\
 e_{18}&=FC-\Pi(t_1,t_2,t_3),\\
 e_{19}&=\eta\zeta-\Pi(w_1,w_2,w_3).
\end{align*}
The requested single equation is
\[
\boxed{\quad P_{34}=\sum_{i=1}^{19}e_i^2=0,\qquad\boldsymbol{x}\in\Z^{34}.\quad}
\]
The abbreviations are to be expanded, not existentially quantified.
Every residual has degree at most 3, including $e_9$: $X_*$ has degree2,
while $C$ is affine. Hence $P_{34}$ has degree at most6. Its
$r^2c^4$ coefficient is 4, so the degree is exactly 6. Over the integers,
$P_{34}=0$ is equivalent to simultaneous vanishing of all 19 residuals.
The prior section's two-way witness construction therefore yields the stated
ZFC-provable equivalence, with the inherited endpoint theorem.

The script \texttt{check\_displayed\_system.py} independently compares these
compact expressions with all 19 literal JSON rows by symbolic expansion.
This prevents an attractive printed abbreviation from silently changing
the actual delivered polynomial.

\section{What failed, what remains, and the recommended next attack}
The signed-power technique is the completed advance: weakening a positivity
constraint can lower the number of witnesses if a later arithmetic invariant
recovers the sign. Its proof must establish rank bounds \emph{before} assuming
that sign. Skipping that ordering would make the argument circular.

The inert-prime certificate changes the bounded-quantifier mechanism itself.
The next decisive experiment is not another small exponent search: instantiate
its factorial, binomial, power and remainder graphs with shared wires, apply
it directly to the small unflattened local refutation relation, and count the
entire resulting polynomial. Both soundness and completeness must survive
the chosen natural/integer-domain conversions. Until those graphs and all
witnesses are explicit, there is no final variable saving to report.

The fixed-input boundary could replace a large specialized transition
alphabet by a small universal one. The missing objects are finite but
substantial: a pinned machine table with the correct blank-background model,
a constructive refuter-to-input compiler, the exact compiled input word,
and all regenerated coefficient ports and rank-controller certificates.
A theorem of universality by itself is not the required compiler. Input
length enters the fixed coefficients and must be charged. No such endpoint
is claimed in this packet.

Mixed-degree projection is useful algebra but not a current record: the
26-positive sextic and 21-positive octic are dominated by already archived
25-positive sextic and20-positive octic constructions. Their value here is
as checked source alternatives for future positional compression. Higher
source degree can worsen the necessary radix exponent enough to erase the
benefit of fewer coordinates.

Finally, the attempted 11-variable outer-root merger fails to preserve two
independent residue channels simultaneously. The explicit counterexample in
\texttt{low\_degree/FAILED\_ROOT\_MERGER.md} blocks the proposed cancellation,
not every conceivable 11-variable construction. Recording this obstruction
is preferable to advertising a root deletion whose converse is unproved.

\section{Formalization boundary and reproducibility}
The latest reviewed Lean work proves important pieces of signed-index
step-down and split-source existence. The signed-index interface admits
signed auxiliary congruence coordinates and does not require an odd target
index at entry. However our negative-$c$ branch still needs an explicit
adapter to that interface. The new polynomial, its literal coefficient
binding, native calibration, and the internal ZFC consistency statement
have not been newly joined into one checked theorem here.
The recent source-polynomial equivalence and counted arithmetic-DAG identity
are also different obligations. Repository status and review prose differed
on one late build claim, so this report does not silently upgrade it to a
verified endpoint. See the pinned formal documents and late snapshot.

The release includes the TeX and PDF, explicit JSON equations and DAGs,
authenticated upstream coefficient dependencies, original incoming packets,
mathematical proofs, independent audits, and replay scripts.
Run \texttt{python3 verify.py} from the extracted release. The root checker
validates the sealed file manifest and executes the documented component
checks in an isolated copy. The recorded validation results distinguish
algebraic reconstruction, finite diagnostics, and imported theorems.
Run \texttt{python3 negative\_controls.py} for the packaged integrity controls.
No check searches for a ZFC inconsistency or decides consistency.
