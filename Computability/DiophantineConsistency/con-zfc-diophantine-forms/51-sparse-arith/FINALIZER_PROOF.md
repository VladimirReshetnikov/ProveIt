# A coprimality and remainder separator for three square roots

Research note, 23 October 2026. The notation follows the Sun finalizer in
ProveIt package 40, pinned at commit
`124c07c0b77a9e20e84676514d8b1ff78536ea3f`.

## 1. The replacement

Keep the source polynomials and every Sun/Lucas expression unchanged. In the
final three-root norm replace the weights

    (1, r0, r0*(Pn^2 + K^2 + 3))

by

    (1, b, b*K).

This is not an unconditional relation-combining identity for arbitrary
radicands. Its correctness uses the actual arithmetic structure of this
finalizer. The argument below handles signed input variables, including the
exceptional input f=0, before using positivity.

## 2. Exact norm identity

For variables t,a0,a1,a2 define

    V = t^2 + a2 - a0 - a1,
    W = V^2 + 4*t^2*a2 - 4*a0*a1,
    N(t,a0,a1,a2) = W^2 - 16*t^2*a2*V^2.

Over the complex numbers, for any choices of square roots,

    N(t,a0,a1,a2)
      = product_{e0,e1,e2 in {-1,1}}
          (t + e0*sqrt(a0) + e1*sqrt(a1) + e2*sqrt(a2)).

In particular this is a monic polynomial of degree eight in t, with integer
coefficients when the three radicands are integers. Repeated squareclasses
or zero radicands do not invalidate the identity.

Set

    t = Sigma*m - Theta,
    a0 = Sigma^2*r0,
    a1 = Sigma^2*b^2*r1,
    a2 = Sigma^2*b^2*K^2*r2.

The proposed polynomial is N(t,a0,a1,a2). When it vanishes and Sigma is
nonzero, the number m-Theta/Sigma is a rational zero of the monic integral
polynomial N(t,r0,b^2*r1,b^2*K^2*r2). The rational-root theorem makes it an
integer. Thus Sigma divides Theta. It also follows from the product identity
that some signed sum

    e0*sqrt(r0) + e1*b*sqrt(r1) + e2*b*K*sqrt(r2)

is rational. Signs of Sigma,b,K can be absorbed into the independent signs.

## 3. The general coprimality lemma

**Lemma.** Let b,J,A,B,K be integers, b nonzero, gcd(b,J)=1, and |b|>1.
If some signed sum

    sqrt(b*J) +/- b*sqrt(A) +/- b*K*sqrt(B)

is rational, then b*J is a nonnegative integer square.

**Proof.** Suppose bJ is not a nonnegative square. Write bJ=d*a^2 where
a is a positive integer and d is a signed squarefree integer other than 1.
Distinct signed squarefree radical classes are linearly independent over
the rationals; this follows by taking the character basis in the finite
multiquadratic extension generated by all the roots under consideration.
The coefficient of sqrt(d) in the displayed rational sum must vanish.
Each of its last two terms, if present in this class, has coefficient an
integer multiple of b. Therefore b divides a. Hence b^2 divides a^2 and
therefore divides bJ, which implies b divides J. This contradicts
gcd(b,J)=1 and |b|>1. The argument works equally for negative d. QED.

**Two-root lemma.** Let K be a nonzero integer and A,B be integers such that
A is not K^2*B. If sqrt(A)+K*sqrt(B) is rational, then A and B are
nonnegative integer squares.

**Proof.** Denote that rational number by q. If q=0, squaring gives
A=K^2*B, excluded. If q is nonzero, squaring q-sqrt(A)=K*sqrt(B) gives
sqrt(A)=(q^2+A-K^2*B)/(2q), a rational number. A rational square root of
an integer is an integer. Now sqrt(B)=(q-sqrt(A))/K is rational and hence
an integer. The same argument covers either sign of either root. QED.

These lemmas identify the relevant mechanism: divisibility excludes
cancellation between radical classes. No upper bound on their magnitudes
is required.

## 4. Structure of the Sun finalizer

All variables here are arbitrary integers until positivity is proved.
Use the inherited definitions

    b = 1 + 3*f,
    L = l*Y,
    U = 2*L*X,
    V0 = 4*g*w*Y,
    A = U*(V0+1),
    B0 = 2*X+1,
    C = B0+(A-2)*h,
    Pn = U^2*V0,
    K = X+1+k*(Pn-2),
    S = 2*A-5,
    T = 3*b*w*C-2*((b*w)^2-1),
    Delta = 8*cap^3*g*K^2
              -g^2*(32*(C-K*L)^2*cap^3+g^2*K^2),
    O = f^2*l^2*Delta,
    xi = z*x,
    D = (A^2-4)*C^2+4,
    E = C^2*D*xi,
    F = 4*(A^2-4)*E^2+1,
    G = 2*(A+2)*(A-2)^2*E^2-1,
    H = C+(B0-C)*F+16*E*F*y,
    I = (G^2-1)*H^2+1,
    r0 = b*(1+(3*O-4)*b^2*z^2),
    r1 = (Pn^2-4)*K^2+4,
    r2 = D*F*I.

In the final support-aware source package, `g` remains one of the ten
integer unknowns. The positive parent witnesses are recovered through the
raw-source square `(1+g+ell)^2`; no additional unknown is introduced.
Every statement below applies before that source decoding takes place.

The inherited quintic merger defines Sigma=xi*S^5 and Theta as the
homogenization at (T+S*y,S) of

    pi(v) = (v^3-v-1)*(v^2+23).

Since S=2A-5 is odd, it is nonzero. The quintic has no rational zero,
so Theta is nonzero for every integer assignment. At Sigma=0 the norm is
Theta^8, so a zero of the proposed equation necessarily has Sigma nonzero.
It follows that xi,z,x are nonzero. Monicity gives Sigma|Theta, and the
unchanged quintic merger proves S|T.

## 5. Recovering the first square, including b=1

Let J=1+(3O-4)*b^2*z^2. Since J is 1 modulo b, gcd(b,J)=1.
The integer b=1+3f is nonzero. If |b|>1, the coprimality lemma applied to
the rational signed root sum proves r0 is a nonnegative square.

The only remaining possibility is b=1: b=-1 is not 1 modulo 3. Then f=0,
O=0, and r0=1-4*z^2 is odd. We show that it cannot be a nonsquare radical
in a rational signed sum. Both r1 and r2 are divisible by 4: Pn is even
and A is even, so r1=(Pn^2-4)*K^2+4 and D=(A^2-4)*C^2+4 are divisible
by 4. Suppose r0=d*a^2 is its signed squarefree decomposition with d not
1. Since r0 is odd, d and a are odd. Whenever r1=d*c^2 or r2=d*e^2
lies in the same squareclass, its integer coefficient c or e is even.
Thus the coefficient of sqrt(d) in the signed root sum is an odd integer
plus even integers, which cannot vanish. This contradicts rationality.

Consequently every root of the proposed equation has r0 a nonnegative
integer square. Since z is nonzero, the actual b=1 case has r0<0 and
therefore supplies no root at all. This proof does not use positivity of
r1 or r2, a Lucas theorem, or any source size bound.

For completeness r0 cannot be zero: J=0 would give
(3O-4)*b^2*z^2=-1, hence |b|=|z|=1 and O=1. The congruence b=1 mod 3
then gives b=1,f=0,O=0, a contradiction. Thus r0 is a positive square.

## 6. Positivity and nonvanishing of K

From r0=bJ square and gcd(b,J)=1, unique factorization makes b and J
signed squares with the same sign. A negative nonzero square cannot be 1
modulo 3. Therefore b is a positive square and J is a positive square.
If O<=0 then J<=1-4*(bz)^2<0, contradiction. Hence O>0. In particular
f,l are nonzero and Delta>0. Also cap=b*top>0, since the inherited source
base is positive and top is its fixed positive power.

If K=0, the formula for Delta gives

    Delta = -32*g^2*C^2*cap^3 <= 0,

contradicting Delta>0. Therefore K is nonzero. No lower bound on |K|,
no positivity assertion about g, and no source inequality is needed here.

## 7. A Pell-remainder square lemma

**Lemma.** Let P,K,R be integers, with K nonzero and R divisible by 4. Put A=(P^2-4)*K^2+4. If a signed sum sqrt(A) +/- K*sqrt(R) is
rational, then A and R are nonnegative integer squares.

**Proof.** If the rational sum q is nonzero, the two-root lemma's proof
makes both roots rational, hence integers. If q=0, squaring gives
A=K^2*R. The defining expression for A then implies K^2 divides 4, so
|K| is 1 or 2. If |K|=1, the equality gives A=R=P^2, already squares.
If |K|=2, it gives R=P^2-3. An integer square is 0 or 1 modulo 4,
so this value is 1 or 2 modulo 4, contradicting the assumed divisibility
of R by 4. These exhaust the cases.
QED.

This handles the potential zero-sum degeneracy directly; it avoids
unnecessary size estimates for K.

## 8. Recovering the remaining two squares and completing the bridge

After r0 is known square, divide the rational signed root sum minus its
integer first root by the nonzero integer b. We obtain a rational signed
sum of sqrt(r1) and K*sqrt(r2). The hypotheses of the preceding lemma hold:
Pn is even, K is nonzero, and r2 is divisible by 4. Hence r1 and r2 are
nonnegative integer squares.

Thus all three square predicates and Sigma|Theta have been recovered,
with no circular use of positivity. The rest of the inherited proof can
now recover g>0 and 0<g<2*cap from Delta>0, obtain the source size
inequalities, and apply Sun's reverse bridge exactly as before.
Conversely, true source witnesses provide the same three square
predicates and the same divisibility as before. Their signed weighted
root sum with weights (1,b,bK) is an integer, giving a suitable integer m
and a zero of the new polynomial. Hence the new finalizer has precisely
the same existential solvability predicate as the inherited one.

The separator proof has used only integer factorization, elementary
properties of multiquadratic fields, rational-root integrality, and
congruences modulo 3 and 4. None of the new local steps invokes the
native-window compiler or an additional analytic/Pell size estimate.

## 9. Concrete metric audit

The pinned inherited package-40 construction has ten unrestricted integer
unknowns, 782 operations, and exact total degree 237,520,456. The final
support-aware source has maximum target 7,243, span 8,841, and uses the raw
positive parent with the `(1+g+ell)^2` source square. Combined with the new
arithmetic separator and common-subexpression optimizations, it has:

| Unknowns / domain | Operations | Exact total degree |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10 unrestricted integers | 709 | 54,768,104 |

The literal evaluator contains 189 additions, 25 subtractions, and 495
multiplications. Its exact-degree certificate specializes all ten input
variables to T and finds the coefficient at the propagated upper bound
T^54,768,104 to be 323,596,601 modulo 1,000,000,007. This nonzero
coefficient proves that the upper bound is the exact total degree. The
independent evaluator also obtains nonzero leading coefficients modulo
two other primes. Fixed coefficients are the unchanged finite native tuple
and inherited source recipes. The shared products Sigma^2*b^2 and K^2 are
used directly to construct a1 and a2; the conceptual weight b*K need not
be computed as a separate live gate.

These are metrics of the full combined construction, not an attribution
of every improvement to the separator alone. The target schedule, raw-parent
source shift, and circuit common-subexpression changes are separate local
improvements composed with this theorem.

## 10. Attribution and limitations

Sun's general ten-integer-variable representation is from
Zhi-Wei Sun, "Ten unknowns for Hilbert's tenth problem over the integers,"
arXiv:2609.01594v2 (2 September 2026), especially Sections 3--7:
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01594v2 . The repository already replaces Sun's
original intersective sextic by the quintic used here; that degree-five
merger is not a new result in this note. The source packing and the native
compiler equivalence remain separate dependencies.

The new contribution in this note is the arithmetic separator (1,b,bK)
and its complete bootstrap proof for this finalizer. It is a local theorem
about replacing the norm weights, not a new reduction below ten integer
unknowns and not an independently formalized native compiler theorem.

The classical alternative quintic (v^3-19)*(v^2+3) is also intersective
and has a slightly cheaper homogeneous circuit, but it has deliberately
not been substituted into the audited candidate: keeping the inherited
merger leaves the comparison and proof dependency smaller.
