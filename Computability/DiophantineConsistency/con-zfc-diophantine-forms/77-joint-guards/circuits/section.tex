\section{Shared arithmetic circuits for the integer family}
\label{sec:joint-circuits}

The joint-sign equations admit short shared evaluators without changing
their polynomials.  In the following table, every binary addition,
subtraction and multiplication is charged, including scalar multiplication
and squaring.  Fixed numeral leaves and reuse of registers are free.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
Integer variables & Exact degree & Full polynomial gates & Residual gates\\\hline
19&88&109&96\\
20&32&112&97\\
21&20&115&98\\
22&16&118&99\\
23&12&121&100\\
25&8&127&102
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are delivered circuit upper bounds, not assertions of minimum
arithmetic complexity.  Each circuit is literally equal to the source
sum of squared residuals, not merely equivalent in integer solvability.
The source tuple, variable list, signs and auxiliary-row choice are unchanged.

The schedule shares $q-1$, $q^2$, $q^3$, $W$, $X$, $Y$, $C$, $k$,
$H$, $\Delta$ and the determinant terms.  It evaluates
$W=12w_3+4$, and, where the actual packet has eliminated the coordinates,
uses the polynomial identities
\[
 E=XY,\quad a=E+Y,\quad a-E=Y,\quad
 \eta=c-kY,\quad c-\eta=kY.
\]
For a coordinate that remains free these identities are \emph{not}
assumed: the appropriate subtraction and restoring residual remain.
In particular the twenty-five-variable equation retains both its free
$\zeta$ and its restoring row.

The independent checker does not expand the high-degree final sum of
squares.  It first binds the factored source residuals by exact full-inlining
syntax-tree comparison, or the already-expanded lower-degree rows by exact
sparse algebra.  It then validates $X,Y,W$ against the original inputs,
checks every residual in the polynomial ring with these three independent
cut registers, and verifies the finalizer over independent residual symbols.
The largest cut residual has $1{,}931$ monomials.  Substitution of the
validated cuts proves exact equality with the original polynomial.

The compact displayed nineteen-variable first norm is licensed by the
small exact identity
\[
 E(kY)\bigl(k+E(kY)\bigr)=XY^2(XY^2+1)k^2\qquad(E=XY).
\]
Both normal and optimized Python checks validate all six circuits, including
live-operation accounting and the unchanged sum-of-squares finalizer.
No numerical sampling, approximate arithmetic or reduction modulo another
residual is used.  These algebraic and finite-data checks do not add a new
unbounded compiler theorem or a new formal consistency calibration.
