# A three-root parity merger: fifteen integers, degree 204

## Scope

The literal `parity15_unit_product.json` is an ordinary integer polynomial
in fifteen unrestricted integer unknowns, of exact total degree 204.
Its three residuals have degrees 14, 14, 176. The companion
`parity15_sos.json` is their sum of squares, of exact degree 352.
Both have an integer zero if and only if the copied 17-integer parent
has an integer zero. Composition with the parent's identified
native/Pell/refutation calibration gives the requested ZFC-provable
nonsolvability equivalence. No global minimality or new proof-assistant
certificate is claimed.

The sixteen-integer construction in `PROOF.md` establishes the private
root translations and the crucial fact delta nonzero independently of
the positivity residual. Here we remove its third private root as well.

## 1. Three squares with a rational linear sum

**Lemma.** Let A and B be integers congruent to 1 modulo 4, and C any
nonzero integer. If, for some signs, the complex square roots satisfy

    x = epsilon1*sqrt(A)+2epsilon2*sqrt(B)+4epsilon3*sqrt(C)

with rational x, then A, B, and C are integer squares. (The signs of the
chosen square-root branches may be absorbed into the epsilons.)

**Proof.** Write each nonzero integer as a square times a signed squarefree
integer: A=a^2*d_A, B=b^2*d_B, C=c^2*d_C, with a,b,c positive integers.
Since A,B are odd, a and b are odd and d_A,d_B are odd.

Distinct signed squarefree radicals are linearly independent over Q,
including the rational squareclass 1. This follows, for example, from
the sign-change automorphisms of the multiquadratic field generated by
the relevant square roots. This elementary statement covers negative
squareclasses by including sqrt(-1).

Group the three terms by squareclass. If an irrational squareclass
contains the A term, its coefficient is an odd integer plus zero or more
even integers, and cannot vanish. If it contains B but not A, its
coefficient is 2 times an odd integer plus a multiple of 4, and cannot
vanish. If it contains only C, its nonzero coefficient is +/-4c.
Thus no irrational squareclass can occur. All three classes must be 1,
which means that all three radicands are positive integer squares.
This proof uses only integer parity after the standard squareclass
decomposition; it does not require a size majorant or separation bound.

## 2. A primitive polynomial for the three-root norm

Put A=4a+1, B=4b+1, x=2t+1, and

    U = t(t+1)-a-B+4C,
    M = U^2+4x^2*C-A*B,
    N = M^2-16x^2*U^2*C.                         (1)

This is an integer polynomial. Its exact norm identity is

    256N = product_{epsilon1,epsilon2,epsilon3 in {-1,1}}
             (x+epsilon1*sqrt(A)
                 +2epsilon2*sqrt(B)+4epsilon3*sqrt(C)).      (2)

One derivation first groups the signs of sqrt(A),sqrt(B). Their product,
at an odd argument 2s+1, is

    16{[s(s+1)-a-B]^2-A*B}.

Now substitute s=t+/-2sqrt(C) and multiply the two expressions. The
even part is M and the odd part is +/-4xU sqrt(C), giving (1)-(2).
The supplied independent auditor expands the eight-factor product in a
formal multiquadratic algebra and verifies (2) coefficient by coefficient.

Consequently, if N=0, one factor in (2) vanishes and the lemma proves
that A,B,C are integer squares. Conversely, if A and B are odd integer
squares and C is an integer square, choose square roots u,d,e and put

    t=(u+2d+4e-1)/2.

This is an integer and N=0. The unrestricted sign of t is permitted.

An equivalent primitive expression, useful for circuit comparison, is

    n=t(t+1)-a-B-4C,
    N=[n^2-A*B-4A*C-16B*C]^2-16A*B*C*x^2.

The two expressions are the same polynomial, not merely equivalent
zero sets.

## 4. Recovering the rational auxiliary root integrally

The norm supplies an integer square root e of Caux. Therefore

    y=e/delta

is rational and satisfies QV^2-(Q-1)y^2=1. It remains to prove that y is
integral. This is a universal Pell denominator lemma; the proof is
included to make the new implication complete.

**Lemma.** For an integer Q>1 and an integer V, every rational y satisfying
QV^2-(Q-1)y^2=1 is an integer.

**Proof.** Replace V,y by their absolute values. V=0 is impossible. If
V=1, then y=1. Assume V>1 and write y=m/n in lowest terms, n>0. Clearing
denominators shows n^2 divides Q-1, since m,n are coprime. In particular
(Q-1)y is an integer. Define

    V'=(2Q-1)V-2(Q-1)y,
    y'=2QV-(2Q-1)y.

Then V' is an integer, y' is rational, and direct expansion preserves
the same equation. Since y>V, V'<V. Also (Q-1)y<QV, proved by squaring
and using the equation, implies V'>-V. The equation excludes V'=0,
so 1<=|V'|<V. Finally y' has exactly the same reduced denominator n:
its numerator modulo n is -m, because 2Q-1 is 1 modulo n. Induction on
the positive integer V therefore reduces to V=1 and proves n=1.

In our application Q=I^2 is nonnegative and not 1. If Q>1, this lemma
applies. If Q=0, delta=-1, r_aux=-1, Caux=1 and y=e/delta is directly
an integer equal to 1 or -1. These cases cover every possibility.

## 5. Exact existence equivalence

are now fifteen unrestricted integer unknowns. Retain only the main
and strong Pell residuals and the new norm N in (1), with

    a=A4, B=B_guard, C=Caux, t=t_parity.

For soundness, a zero makes delta and Caux nonzero independently of

Restore

    tau=2u-2P+k,

The last quotient is integral because d is odd. The private-root
identities from `PROOF.md` restore the first and auxiliary equations;

For completeness, any parent zero gives integer roots u and y through
the two source translations. Put d=2pair_pos3+1 and e=delta*y. Then
u^2=A, d^2=B_guard and e^2=Caux. The integer

    t_parity=(u+2d+4e-1)/2

makes the norm vanish. The two retained rows were already zero.
This proves an existence equivalence using every parent zero; it does
not need a new canonical-witness restriction or a new coefficient tuple.

## 6. Degree and finalization

The main and strong residuals have degrees 14 and 14. The new primitive
norm has degree 176. The packet's safe degree computation and nonzero
leading specialization at the authentic fixed coefficients prove each
degree exactly. Therefore the sum of squares has degree 352.

For the smaller-degree single equation use the integer-unit finalizer

    (1+4r_main)(1+4r_strong)(1+4N)-1=0.

Every factor is 1 modulo 4. An integer product equal to 1 has factors
in {1,-1}; the residue excludes -1. This is therefore equivalent to
the three residuals vanishing. Its exact degree is 14+14+176=204;
the supplied nonzero top-coefficient certificate also checks the
specialized literal polynomial.

The main simplification is the squareclass parity condition. It removes
three private root coordinates in favor of one unrestricted carrier,
without a large polynomial majorant. The integer-unit finalizer is a
separate familiar elementary step. None of this reduces the much
lower variable-count high-degree universal frontier.

## 7. Centered sixteen-variable companion

The sixteen-variable merger admits a polynomial centering that preserves
oddness. Put z0=XY^2/4=q(3w3+1)Y^2, so P0=4z0k is a multiple of 4.
Use x=P0+2t+1. The primitive norm becomes

    H16=t(t+1)+4z0kt+2z0k-z0k^2,
    N16=(delta*H16-r_aux)^2-A*delta*r_aux.

This follows by replacing t in the uncentered formula by t+P0/2 and
cancelling P0^2/4. P0/2=2z0k is an integer polynomial, so the new
carrier still ranges over exactly the odd integers. The same elementary
sixteen-variable proof applies. Its norm degree is 60, the other three
residual degrees are 14,14,44, and its SOS finalizer has exact degree
120. The alternate unit-product finalizer has degree 132 and is retained
only as a reproducible comparison.
