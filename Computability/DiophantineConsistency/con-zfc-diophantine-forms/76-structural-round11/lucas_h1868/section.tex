\section{Norm saturation without an extra variable}

The ten-integer equation from the preceding packet used a Lucas certificate
whose internal square carried a redundant copy of a number that the same
certificate proves to be square. Removing that copy lowers the exact degree
from $1\,576\,864$ to $1\,245\,324$, with the same ten unrestricted
integer unknowns and $480$ binary operations. This combines the certified
height1868 packing with the norm-saturated certificate. All twelve actual
fixed integer leaves are unchanged; an exact shared power plan saves one
operation overall.

\subsection{The modified polynomial}

Retain the source registers $X,Y,\mathscr C$, and write
\[
\begin{gathered}
 b=6t+4,\quad g=bg_0,\quad k=b(3k_0+2),\quad C=6c_0-1,\quad \xi=zx,\\
 L=lY,\quad U=2LX,\quad V=4gwY,\quad W=bw,\\
 A=U(V+1),\quad B_1=2X+1,\quad P_{1/2}=(LX)UV,\\
 K=X+1+k(2P_{1/2}-2),\quad Q=(P_{1/2}^2-1)K^2+1,\\
 S=2A-5,\qquad T=3WC-2(W^2-1),\\
 \Delta=8\mathscr C^3gK^2-g^2\{32(C-KL)^2\mathscr C^3+g^2K^2\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Define the new certificate registers
\[
\begin{gathered}
 D=(A^2-4)C^2+4,\quad E_0=C^2\xi,\quad E=DE_0,\quad E_2=E_0E,\\
 F=4(A^2-4)E_2+1,\quad G=(A/2-1)(F-1)-1,\quad \Omega=16E,\\
 H=C+F(B_1-C+\Omega y),\qquad I=(G^2-1)H^2+1.
\end{gathered}
\]
Here $A/2=(LX)(V+1)$ is an integer polynomial, not a division operation.
The sole structural change is
\[
 E^2=C^4D^2\xi^2\quad\longmapsto\quad E_2=C^4D\xi^2
\]
in $F$ and $G$; the old expression for $\Omega$ is retained.
Sharing $E_0,E,E_2$ uses the same three multiplications as the former
$E$ and $E^2$.

Put
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \rho=T+Sy,\quad \Sigma=\xi S^5,\quad
 \Theta=(\rho^3-\rho S^2-S^3)(\rho^2+23S^2),\\
 r=bQ,\quad a_0=r+(9\Delta-3)(rz)^2,\quad a_1=DFI,
 \quad Z=\Sigma m-\Theta.
\end{gathered}
\]
The completed ordinary equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm-saturated-ten}
 \boxed{\bigl(Z^2-\Sigma^2(a_0+a_1)\bigr)^2
          -4\Sigma^4a_0a_1=0.}
\end{equation}
The ten variables are $t,g_0,c_0,k_0,l,w,x,y,z,m\in\mathbb Z$.
The complete source prefix, sparse kernel data, actual fixed coefficients,
and division-free circuit are supplied in the accompanying \texttt{lucas\_h1868}
component. No consistency-dependent integer is selected.

\subsection{Reliability with the weaker internal square}

Suppose $A$ is even, $|A|>2$, $B$ is odd with
$1<B<|A|/2-1$, $C$ is odd with $|C|>1$, and $\xi\ne0$.
Use the displayed certificate with $B$ in place of $B_1$.
If $DFI$ is square, then
\[
 C=\pm u_B(A),\qquad u_0(A)=0,\quad u_1(A)=1,\quad
 u_{n+2}(A)=Au_{n+1}(A)-u_n(A).
\]
If additionally $3\mid A$, $B\equiv3\pmod4$, and
$C\equiv-1\pmod3$, the sign is positive.

Indeed $D,F,I$ are positive. Since $D\mid E_2$,
\[
 F\equiv I\equiv1\pmod D,
 \qquad 2G=(A-2)F-A,
 \qquad4I\equiv D\pmod F.
\]
As $F$ is odd, these are pairwise coprime. Each is therefore a square.
Let $d=\sqrt D>0$, a proof-only integer. Pell classification supplies
indices $s,\mu,t$ such that
\[
 C=u_s(A),\qquad4C^2d\xi=u_\mu(A),\qquad H=u_t(2G).
\]
The coordinate $C$ is odd and not $\pm1$, so $s$ is odd and $|s|\ge3$.
Alternatively, the bound needed for the next index can be seen directly:
$d\ge|A|$, hence $|u_\mu(A)|\ge4|A|>|u_2(A)|$;
$\mu$ is even and nonzero, so $n=|\mu|\ge4$.
Then $F=(v_n(A)/2)^2$. The standard index-separation and rank-divisibility
lemmas give
\[
 s\equiv\pm t\pmod{2n},\qquad C\mid n.
\]
The two crucial new polynomial identities are
\[
 \frac{F-1}{\Omega\xi}=\frac{(A^2-4)C^2}{4},\qquad
 \frac{2G+2}{\Omega\xi}
       =\frac{(A+2)(A-2)^2C^2}{4}.
\]
Both right-hand sides are integers. Thus $H\equiv B\pmod\Omega$,
while $H=u_t(2G)\equiv u_t(-2)=t\pmod\Omega$ because $t$ is odd.
Since $2C\mid\Omega$, we obtain $s\equiv\pm B\pmod{2C}$.
Lucas growth gives $|s|\le|C|$ and $B<|A|<|C|$, so the only
possible representatives are $s=B$ and $s=-B$.
Finally
\[
 u_{2j+1}(A)\equiv(-1)^j\pmod{A^2}
\]
shows that $u_B(A)\equiv-1\pmod3$ on the indicated slice, and selects
the positive sign.

\subsection{Full residue interpolation}

The modified certificate must also retain the full residue freedom used
by the divisibility merger. Let $A>2$ be even, $B>0$ odd,
$C=u_B(A)$ and $d=v_B(A)>0$, so $D=d^2$.
For every $N>0$ there is a positive $\xi$ divisible by $N$ such that
every residue class modulo $\xi$ contains a $y$ for which $DFI$ is square.

Choose a positive matrix period $n$ modulo $4NC^2d$ for
\[
 M_A=\begin{pmatrix}A&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \xi=\frac{u_n(A)}{4C^2d},\qquad r_0=\frac{v_n(A)}2.
\]
Then $N\mid\xi$, $n$ is even, $r_0$ is odd, $F=r_0^2$, and
\[
 \Omega=16C^2d^2\xi=4d\,u_n(A).
\]
The Cayley--Hamilton period $\lambda=4nr_0$ works modulo $F$.
For $q=\lambda/\gcd(\lambda,\Omega)$ we have
$\gcd(q,\xi)=1$: if $p\mid\xi$, then $p\nmid r_0$ and the Lucas
valuation bound gives
\[
 \nu_p(\lambda)=\nu_p(4n)
 \le\nu_p(4u_n(A))\le\nu_p(\Omega).
\]
Thus the extra factor $d$ in $\Omega$ strengthens, rather than weakens,
the required absorption of prime powers.

For a prescribed class $c\pmod\xi$, choose a positive $L$ with
$q\mid L$ and $L\equiv c\pmod\xi$. Set
$j=B+\Omega L$ and $H_0=u_j(2G)$.
The period gives $H_0\equiv C\pmod F$, while the displayed
identities give $H_0\equiv B\pmod\Omega$.
As $F\equiv1\pmod\Omega$, the number
\[
 y=\frac{H_0-C-(B-C)F}{\Omega F}
\]
is integral. Working modulo $\Omega\xi$ instead yields
$Fy\equiv L\pmod\xi$, and therefore $y\equiv c\pmod\xi$.
The Pell identity makes $I$ square. This is a direct proof for the new
scale: simply multiplying the old $\xi$ by $d$ would not establish
residue freedom modulo the new $\xi$.

\subsection{Signed soundness and completeness}

The unchanged normalizations give $a_0\equiv1\pmod3$ and
$a_1\equiv0\pmod3$ on every signed assignment. Hence $a_0\ne a_1$.
The root-free homogeneous quintic makes $\Theta\ne0$ because $S$ is
odd and nonzero. At a zero of \eqref{eq:norm-saturated-ten}, therefore,
$\Sigma\ne0$. The two-square norm argument gives integral square roots
of $a_0,a_1$, together with $\Sigma\mid\Theta$; prime valuations then
give $S\mid T$.

The coprime factorization of $a_0$ and its residue class force $b,Q$ to
be positive squares and $\Delta>0$. Thus $K\ne0$, $0<g<2\mathscr C$,
and $l\ne0$; the last assertion follows from $Q=1-K^2\le0$ if $l=0$.
The source bounds give $X>3b$ and $Y>256$, so $|L|\ge Y$ and
$|A|>2B_1+2$.

The indispensable new observation is that the guard already excludes
the exceptional Lucas coordinates:
\[
 4(C-KL)^2<K^2,\qquad
 |C|>|K|(|L|-\tfrac12)\ge Y-\tfrac12>1.
\]
Thus the weakened internal square does not need to reproduce the old
standalone exclusion of $C=\pm1$. Reliability gives $C=u_{B_1}(A)$.
The proof-only integer $(C-B_1)/(A-2)$ restores the former arithmetic
interface, after which the unchanged reverse bridge and fixed source
calibration supply a finite refutation.

Conversely a refutation supplies the same canonical source witnesses.
With $r=bQ$ a positive square and $\Delta>0$, the coefficient
$(9\Delta-3)r$ is positive nonsquare because its $3$-adic valuation is
odd. Pell's theorem supplies $z>0$ making $a_0$ square. Use the new
interpolation theorem with $N=z$, and put $x=\xi/z$.
Intersectivity of $(v^3-v-1)(v^2+23)$ prescribes a class of $y$ modulo
$\xi$ giving $\Sigma\mid\Theta$ while $a_1$ remains square.
Then
\[
 m=\Theta/\Sigma+\sqrt{a_0}+\sqrt{a_1}
\]
solves \eqref{eq:norm-saturated-ten}. This proves the existential
consistency equivalence with no sign assumptions on arbitrary inputs.
