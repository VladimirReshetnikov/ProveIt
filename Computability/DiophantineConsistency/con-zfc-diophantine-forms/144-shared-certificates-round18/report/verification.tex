\section{Reproduction and evidence boundaries}
\label{sec:verification}
\subsection{Portable entry points and normative artifacts}
From the extracted release root, the integrated computational entry point is
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
python3 -B verify\_release.py
\end{quote}
It checks the release inventory and invokes the explicit component plan,
including normal and optimized interpreter runs and deterministic rebuilds.
The mathematical checks require Python and its standard library, not a
network connection, repository checkout, external computer-algebra system
or theorem-prover installation. Rebuilds and any receipt-writing inherited
checks run in disposable copies so they cannot alter the sealed reference
files. The final integrated replay receipt records the tests actually run;
a command listed here is not by itself evidence that it passed.

The direct equations are the complete residual arrays and adjacent expanded
coefficient arrays. The selected signed paths are the dimension, grouped
and sparse \file{centered_merged_odd} variants and the sparse
\file{centered_odd} four-pool variant. The natural catalog identifies
\file{natural221}, \file{natural222}, \file{natural223} and
\file{natural224_sparse}. The height-one catalog identifies
\file{compact571}, \file{balanced593}, \file{sparse646} and
\file{sparser742}. The scalar circuits are
\file{free_whole_center11_c316.json},
\file{free_whole_center11_c308.json},
\file{free_whole_center11_c304.json} and
\file{free_whole_center11_c300.json}.
Each component's catalog and binding file give exact paths and hashes;
these are not unspecified polynomial families.

The editable report has a separate build command, from the release root,
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
sh report/build\_report.sh
\end{quote}
A normal TeX installation is needed only for that step. The builder uses a
fresh temporary directory, creates a local format when the installed
pdfLaTeX format is absent, runs three passes, and rejects overfull boxes and
unresolved references. Mathematical verification does not require TeX.

\subsection{Recorded component checks}
The following results were obtained before report finalization.
\begin{itemize}
\item The signed independent checker reconstructed all twelve emitted
variants in named coordinates, checked every retained and changed row,
and collected every coefficient. Normal and optimized runs passed;
30 deliberate coefficient corruptions were rejected. A separate audit rejected 60 raw-transformation mutations and
rechecked the unbounded spherical, merged and odd-square arguments.
\item The natural checker verified all four endpoint expansions and
nonnegative restoration maps, including the inherited 618-gate,
397-restoration affine export to the 334-coordinate raw source.
It checked 37581 collected endpoint coefficients, rejected 21 negative
controls, and reproduced 20 mathematical files byte for byte. Normal and
optimized isolated component runs passed. A separate raw-transformation
audit rejected 15 changed-row controls.
\item The height-one checker verified every occurrence boundary, source
pullback, graph and tree invariant, equality class, literal coefficient,
and symbolic lift at every vertex. All four profiles passed normal and
optimized replay, 13 corruption classes per profile were rejected, and
selected source transformations and outputs regenerated byte for byte.
The same-source comparison from 806 variables and 23719 terms to
603 variables and 12289 terms also passed its separate replay.
\item The scalar checker authenticated all six coefficient payloads and
reconstructed their complete finite recipe. It checked the universal norm,
center identities, exact polynomial remainders, leading-degree cuts,
attained actual coefficients, circuit counts, liveness and source pins.
Normal and optimized runs passed, rejecting 1130 gate mutations in
each mode. All four circuit files and the metric catalog reproduced exactly.
\item A separate incoming-artifact implementation recollected all
coefficients of the nine direct comparison equations identified in
Section~\ref{sec:comparison}; each agreed with its supplied expansion.
No incoming producer was needed for this comparison check.
\end{itemize}
These are finite algebra, authentication and regression checks. The
unbounded mathematical audits are ordinary arguments within this
AI-assisted workflow, not outside human review or a proof-assistant build.

\subsection{Inherited source closure}
The archive retains the complete authenticated direct and architecture
trees under \file{prior_direct_closure}. They include the numerical
calculus, Pell and CRT arithmetic, indexed-Pell criterion, factorial
fixed point, constructor bounds, orientation-first argument and strict
quotient proof, together with the named logical interfaces. Their
historical READMEs describe their original package, not a new execution
claim in the present release. Active bindings identify the exact inputs
used by the new components.

The scalar component separately retains the full source34 numerical
recipe and native/compiler interface, signed-quadratic and rank
arguments, carrier and saturation proofs, supported-modulus interpolation,
and the Round~17 linear-cofactor theorem. The mathematical handoff is
explained in Section~\ref{sec:scalar}: a new zero is converted to an actual
old zero before any inherited refutation theorem is invoked.
The height-one theorem is an unconditional transformation of a certified
integral quadratic system; its consistency meaning comes only through
its supplied signed source.

\subsection{What successful replay cannot establish}
Authentication establishes file identity, not mathematical truth.
Coefficient replay establishes exact algebra and literal artifact binding,
not an unbounded classification theorem. The ordinary proofs establish
witness implications, conditional on their identified inherited inputs;
no finite search for zeros is used. The logical calibration concerns
syntactic refutation, not a claim that ZFC is consistent or inconsistent.
Retaining Lean interface files is not a substitute for rebuilding a
transitive kernel-checked development. The manifest and visual rendering
receipts similarly certify the delivered files and their presentation,
not a new logical endpoint.
