\section{Fifteen positive variables at degree 44}
\label{sec:offset44}

The low-degree supplement is a polynomial in fifteen strictly positive
integer variables of exact degree 44 evaluated by 76 binary operations:
41 multiplications, 19 additions, and 16 subtractions. This improves the
earlier degree-44 evaluator's 78 operations by two. The root-gap
substitutions themselves are inherited: historical package 12 used the
auxiliary gap, and package 15 used both gaps. Package 10's $Q_{56}$ used
four grouped residuals, 74 operations, and degree 56. The change here is
their combination with a different pairing of the norm, index, and
transport factors. It is neither the first degree-44 construction nor an
operation-count improvement over $P_{67}$ \cite{historical,active}.

All four coefficient ports use package 31's complete tuple \cite{package31},
$B=2^{1064921}$. Write them as $\beta,K_0,M_C,M_F$, where $\beta=B-1$
and $M_F=M_F^{\rm native}+B-1$ is the already shifted high mask.
The tuple is specified in Appendix~\ref{sec:coefficients}. The original package 36
binding in the copied active evaluator is not retained; no identity with
its fixed Lean polynomial is asserted.

The names in this section are local. This is a scalar presentation of the
same ordinary strong-norm compiler interface as the parent in
Section~\ref{sec:sparse}; every abbreviation is defined again below.
The multiplier $i$ is an ordinary multiplier, not a normalized multiplier.
The variables are
\[
 J,F,\alpha,z,f,h,i,T,s,w,r,\eta,\zeta,u,\gamma
 \in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
The following quantities are polynomial abbreviations:
\begin{align*}
q&=\beta J+1,& C&=q-F-\alpha,\\
X&=wq,&Y&=sq^3,&E&=XY,\\
k&=\eta+\zeta,&c&=kY+\eta,&a&=E+Y,\\
H&=4a+3,&\Delta&=a^2+H,&D&=X+ac+\gamma H,\\
L&=E(kY),&G&=(q-1)(q-F)+\alpha,\\
R&=G(q^2-1)+(M_C+qM_F)J,&&\\
V&=c(Tf-1)-Rf^2,&A_0&=\Delta(f^2-1),&I&=ic^2.
\end{align*}
Define
\begin{align*}
N_0&=r^2+L(2r-k),&N_1&=D^2-\Delta c^2,\\
N_a&=(V+u)^2-A_0u(2V+u),&&\\
N_k&=k-hE-R,&N_t&=(K_0+w)C+(q-F)-z(q-1).
\end{align*}
The proposed single equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offset44}
\boxed{(N_0N_k-1)^2+(N_1N_t-1)^2+(N_a-1)^2+(I^2-A_0)^2=0.}
\end{equation}
Its literal division-free evaluation is in \file{short/offset44.json}.

\paragraph{Soundness.}
A zero of~\eqref{eq:offset44} gives
$N_0N_k=N_1N_t=N_a=1$ and $I^2=A_0$. Restore
$\tau=L+r>0$. The exact identity
\[
(L+r)^2-L(L+k)=r^2+L(2r-k)
\]
expresses $N_0$ as $\tau^2-n(n+1)k^2$ for $n=XY^2\ge2$.
The negative Pell equation with this consecutive-product discriminant
has no solution with $k>0$: multiplication by
$2n+1-2\sqrt{n(n+1)}$ decreases the positive second coordinate of any
putative norm-minus-one solution. Thus the unit $N_0$ is positive and
$N_0=N_k=1$. Similarly $\Delta=(a+2)^2-1$ is 0 or 3 modulo 4, so
$N_1$ cannot be $-1$; consequently $N_1=N_t=1$.

Put $y_0=V+u$. The second exact identity is
\[
A_0\bigl(V^2-(V+u)^2\bigr)+(V+u)^2
=(V+u)^2-A_0u(2V+u).
\]
If $y_0=0$, the equation $N_a=1$ would imply $I^2V^2=1$.
But $I=ic^2>1$, so this is impossible. Hence $y=|y_0|>0$.
The restored positive coordinates solve the inherited ordinary strong
system
\[
\begin{gathered}
\tau^2-L(L+k)=1,\qquad N_1=1,\qquad A_0(V^2-y^2)+y^2=1,\\
N_k=N_t=1,\qquad I^2=A_0.
\end{gathered}
\]
The authenticated ordinary strong-rank and compiler theorems therefore
give blank K215 halting \cite{package31}.
This argument does not assume that the computed expression $V+u$ is
positive away from zeros, and it does not divide $i$ by $\Delta$.

\paragraph{Completeness.}
Start from the canonical positive normalized witness supplied by a blank
halting computation and use the inherited transfer
$i_{\rm ordinary}=\Delta i_{\rm normalized}$. It yields the ordinary
system above with $V>1$, $y>0$, and $A_0>1$. The strict inequality $V>1$
is essential: it excludes the degenerate auxiliary root $V=y=1$ whose
gap would vanish. It follows from the inherited auxiliary positivity
argument and the congruence $V\equiv-c\pmod f$ with $f>2c>2$.
Set $r=\tau-L$ and $u=y-V$. The norm equations give
\[
\tau^2-L^2=Lk+1>0,\qquad
(A_0-1)(y^2-V^2)=V^2-1>0.
\]
Therefore both new coordinates are strictly positive, and the two
identities prove~\eqref{eq:offset44}. Composing with the ordinary compiler
and K215 calibration establishes, by ordinary mathematics formalizable
in ZFC, that the absence of positive zeros is equivalent to
$\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})$. No new proof-assistant certificate or
checked object-language ZFC derivation is claimed.

\paragraph{Exact degree.}
The five factor degrees are respectively $12,18,22,7,2$; $I^2-A_0$ has
degree 22. For the main factor, setting $Z=X+\gamma H$ gives the identity
\[
(ac+Z)^2-(a^2+H)c^2=Z^2+2acZ-Hc^2,
\]
which lowers its apparent degree 22 to 18. The four residual degrees in
\eqref{eq:offset44} are consequently $19,20,22,22$. With
$Q=\beta J$ and $k_0=\eta+\zeta$, the degree-44 homogeneous part is
\[
i^4k_0^8s^8Q^{24}
 +4k_0^2w^4s^6T^2f^6u^2Q^{22}.
\]
The monomial $i^4\eta^8s^8J^{24}$ has nonzero coefficient $\beta^{24}$.
Thus 44 is the exact degree. The standard-library audit independently
checks the literal 76-gate schedule, all ports' liveness, the three
polynomial identities, and this complete homogeneous part. Normal and
optimized Python runs give the same ledger.
