TERMINAL NONLINEAR WIRE ELIMINATION: INDEPENDENT ORDINARY PROOF

Let D be either N={0,1,2,...} or Z. Let V be a finite variable set,
g in V, and r_0,...,r_s in Z[V] have total degree at most two. Suppose,
after exact coefficient collection, r_0=epsilon*(g-Q), epsilon in {1,-1},
g does not occur in Q, and deg Q<=2. Suppose every other r_i has the form
r_i=a_i*g+P_i, with a_i an integer constant and g absent from P_i. For
D=N assume additionally that every coefficient of Q is nonnegative.
(It suffices more generally to prove Q(D^(V\{g})) is contained in D.)

Define r'_i=a_i*Q+P_i for i=1,...,s. Every r'_i is an integer polynomial
of degree at most two. Projection forgetting g gives a bijection from
the D-valued zero set of the original residual family onto the D-valued
zero set of the reduced family. The inverse is the explicit polynomial
extension g=Q. Indeed, r_0=0 forces exactly this value of g. After that
substitution the remaining original equalities are exactly r'_i=0.
The nonnegative-coefficient assumption proves that the extension belongs
to N when its arguments do. Over Z this assumption is unnecessary.

Consequently F=sum_(i=0)^s r_i^2 and F'=sum_(i=1)^s (r'_i)^2 are ordinary
polynomials of degree at most four, and their solvability over D is
equivalent. This uses the elementary fact that a sum of squares of
integers is zero precisely when each square is zero. There is no bound
on witnesses, no appeal to experiments, and no unbounded conjunction in
this reduction: V and the residual family are fixed finite objects.

Iteration. Apply the same rule to a succession of current residual
systems, saving the chosen variable g_j and its then-current recipe Q_j.
At step j all earlier eliminated variables have already been substituted
through every remaining row. Thus Q_j contains no g_i with i<=j. Given
a final witness, reconstruct g_j in reverse chronological order. Every
argument needed by Q_j is either retained or was eliminated later and
has therefore already been reconstructed. This proves the lifting map is
well-defined without assuming that the initial equations came from an
acyclic circuit. Successive unique extensions compose to a bijection.

Free variables. If a variable occurs in no surviving residual, removing
it also preserves existential solvability, since it can be assigned zero
in both N and Z. This additional projection is generally many-to-one.
It must not be described as the bijection above. If eliminated recipes
refer to such a free variable, initialize its value (e.g. to zero) before
performing reverse reconstruction. Fixed parameters are not existential
variables and must not be pruned under this rule.

Exact degree. If at least one final residual has a nonzero homogeneous
quadratic part q_i, the degree-four homogeneous part of F' is sum q_i^2.
This is a nonzero polynomial: if it vanished identically, evaluating at
every real tuple would force every q_i to vanish identically, contrary to
the hypothesis. Hence the final degree is exactly four. One may also
give an exact rational/integer test tuple on which sum q_i^2 is nonzero.

Sharpness. The natural-number condition cannot simply be dropped:
g=1-x and x=2 have no natural solution, whereas after eliminating g the
remaining equation x=2 does. The linear-use condition prevents a degree
increase: substituting g=x*y into g*z-1 produces a cubic residual. The
unit coefficient is also material: 2*g-x=0 expresses a parity condition
and cannot be solved for g by an integer polynomial in x.

Implementation obligations. Normalize polynomial coefficients before
testing any hypothesis; zero coefficients do not count as occurrences.
Monomials must retain multiplicity, so (g,g) is nonlinear, as is (g,x).
After each elimination perform exact coefficient collection. A trace
should identify the chosen current row, its sign, the eliminated variable,
and Q; an independent verifier can derive Q again and check the entire
substitution directly. Modular tests supplement but cannot replace this
exact symbolic check. Reconstructing the final sum-of-squares coefficients
by an independent convolution supplies a separate export check.

Choice of order. The theorem proves every accepted step, not optimality
of a greedy strategy. In an acyclic monotone gate-definition subsystem,
two currently eligible variables cannot occur quadratically in one
another's definitions, so eliminating one preserves linearity of the
other. This makes an independent-set formulation unnecessary for that
special case. Exact cancellations, alternative defining rows, and sparse
fill-in can nevertheless make order consequential in a general system.
A deterministic sparse-fill priority is reasonable; measure final
variables, rows, monomials and coefficient height separately. A reduction
in variable count alone need not improve all measures of simplicity.

FORMALIZATION STATUS

This is an elementary finite algebraic proof formalizable in ZFC (indeed,
far weaker arithmetic suffices after coding finite polynomials). It is
not itself a generated Lean/Rocq proof or an encoded object-language ZFC
derivation. When composed with an already established ordinary proof that
the parent equation has a D-valued zero iff there is a ZFC refutation, it
establishes the same equivalence for the new fixed polynomial. It cannot
supply a missing parent consistency-calibration theorem.
