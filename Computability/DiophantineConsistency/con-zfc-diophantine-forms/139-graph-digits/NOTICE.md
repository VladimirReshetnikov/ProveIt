# Attribution, licensing and proof boundaries

This is AI-assisted mathematical research prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov
and the ProveIt project. New material comprises the incidence-support graph
compiler and its proofs, selected literal height-one equations, pooled natural
digit bounds and fused index cap, two compact Pell centers, verification
programs, and the integrated exposition.

ProveIt supplies the inherited fixed source equations, finite numerical
calculus and native-machine consistency calibrations, modular/Pell/binomial
lemmas, source coefficient tuple, and selected construction/checking helpers.
The report and each component identify which results are reused. Retained
upstream files are byte-pinned and preserve their licenses and notices.

The main new graph theorem is independent of any consistency assumption. The
consistency interpretation uses the identified source theorems, including the
C222-to-ZF and ZF-to-ZFC refutation interfaces for the direct and graph routes,
and package 133's native/compiler/refutation chain for the compact route.
The new ordinary proofs are formalizable in ZFC. This release does not claim
to supply a newly compiled end-to-end Lean/Rocq development or object-language
ZFC proof term.

“Independent” reviews and checkers were assigned separately within the same
AI-assisted workflow; they are not external human endorsements. Coefficient
checks and mutation controls establish finite artifact identities, not the
universal arithmetic conclusions that require the written proofs.

New material is released under the repository's MIT No Attribution license,
reproduced in LICENSE. No worldwide novelty or global optimality is claimed.
