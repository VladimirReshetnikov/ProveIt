\section{Reproduction and evidence boundaries}
\label{sec:verification}
The complete release is distributed as two ordinary, disjoint ZIP parts,
each no larger than 4 MB. Both use the same top-level directory. Extract
both archives into the same parent directory before verifying; neither
part alone is a complete release. From the combined extracted root run
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
python3 -B verify\_release.py
\end{quote}
The integrated verifier checks the sealed inventory and executes its
explicit verification plan in a disposable temporary copy. The option
\texttt{--output ../receipt.json} stores a fresh receipt outside the
release; \texttt{--integrity-only} checks byte inventory rather than
mathematical reproduction. Optimized checks set
\texttt{PYTHONOPTIMIZE=1}, so child Python processes inherit that mode.
Python 3.10 or later and its standard library suffice for the finite
mathematical checks; no repository checkout, network connection,
external computer algebra system or proof assistant is required.

\subsection{Complete definitions and deterministic rebuilds}
The three selected direct equations are under
\file{signed/principal217}, \file{signed/low217} and
\file{signed/dimension216}. Each contains its entire residual array
and collected quartic. Frozen certificates specify the semantic source
edits, two common-base groups, every shear and row operation, monic
restoration, Hessian direction and affine consequence. Reproduction
replays those finite choices; it does not rerun an optimizer.
The retained strict arithmetic source and calibrated factorial-free
proof closure are included alongside the pooled and quadratic modules.

The height-one catalog contains each full coefficient array, graph,
source binding and frozen equality-cycle replacement. Its matched
Round~20 inputs are authenticated before their support differences
are measured. The positive scalar graphs are
\file{scalar/positive10/positive10_degree240_ops82.json},
\file{positive10_degree232_ops85.json} and
\file{positive10_degree214_ops87.json} in that same directory.
The integer graphs are
\file{scalar/integer11/integer11_degree298_ops146.json} and
\file{integer11_degree300_ops144.json} in that directory.
Their respective four-port and six-port numerical tuples have complete
compressed payloads, finite reconstruction recipes and independent
byte/bit-length and digest bindings. Hashes are not substituted for
missing numerical values.

The native mathematical report is rebuilt with
\begin{quote}\ttfamily
sh report/build\_report.sh
\end{quote}
This requires pdfLaTeX. It compiles three passes in a fresh temporary
directory and rejects overfull boxes or unresolved references.
\file{report/render_residuals.py} generates the principal appendix
directly from the literal JSON. Its receipt binds both source and TeX
hashes; an independently implemented parser checks every printed
coefficient and monomial. PDF font and metadata bytes may differ across
TeX installations; the literal arrays and coefficient payloads are the
normative mathematical objects.

\subsection{What the finite checks establish}
\begin{itemize}
\item Independent intake checking authenticates all seven root inventories,
recollected 99 incoming direct expansions, and binds the relevant Git
blobs and extracted member bytes. These checks establish identity and
finite algebra, not every unbounded theorem of an incoming packet.
\item The signed checker reconstructs the exact source mutations and all
34 affine square atoms, authenticates group ports and canonical-pair
wires, and replays every algebraic operation. Both restoration directions,
unit conditions, permitted quadratic substitutions and every final
quartic coefficient are checked. The independent full-chain receipt
binds the actual 217- and 216-variable outputs.
\item The height-one checker reconstructs all divergence and equality
squares independently, checks integrality of their half-sum and every
collected coefficient, and verifies source occurrence coverage,
constructor pullbacks and the universal lift including the hub term.
The changed cycle ledger uses only the two declared aliases and existing
anchored units. Its exact full-polynomial difference is checked;
coefficientwise equality of old and new polynomials is not falsely
asserted.
\item The positive scalar checker imports no producer. It verifies
free-ring identities at every live gate, complete leading forms,
the cubic inverse identity, the trace substitution and all four
numeral reconstructions. Both alternative operator labels at all
254 arithmetic gates are rejected: 508 mutations.
\item The integer scalar checks prove the exact identical-center
rewrite and integral center shear, collect both complete cancellation
remainders, and establish attained actual degrees. A separate checker
binds 23 full highest forms, including the rebased $h$ terms.
Both alternative operator labels at all 290 arithmetic gates are
rejected: 580 mutations. Normal and optimized checks remain active
with Python assertions disabled.
\end{itemize}
The release receipts state the exact commands executed and their
terminal outcomes. Ordinary mathematical reviews are AI-assisted reviews,
not represented as external human refereeing. Source hashes, a successful
circuit replay and a finite corruption suite have different evidentiary
roles and do not replace each other.

\subsection{The unbounded and logical boundary}
The new pooling exactness, signed bootstrap, positive transport and
witness constructions are unbounded ordinary arguments, not extrapolated
from finite tests. Their retained inputs include canonical Pell
classification and rank/folding, prime-power CRT and uniform
half-binomial results, and the identified arithmetic/native-to-refutation
calibrations. The complete selected source texts, pins and notices remain
in the release. A relocated historical path is resolved in its component
closure map rather than silently changing its theorem or coefficient tuple.

The C222/ZF and ZF/ZFC interfaces remain those stated in
Section~\ref{sec:foundation}. No entire transitive Lean or Rocq
calibration build is claimed here. Inclusion of interface names is not
a new formal proof object; finite verification does not prove the absence
of zeros, consistency, or a global lower bound. These distinctions apply
equally to the direct, height-one and scalar equations.
