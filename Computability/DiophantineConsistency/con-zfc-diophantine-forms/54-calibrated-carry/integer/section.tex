\section{Ten integers with equal residual weights}
\label{sec:equal-integer}

The recommended circuit defines a fixed polynomial
\[
 F_{10}(f,g,h,k,l,w,x,y,z,m)\in\mathbb Z[f,g,h,k,l,w,x,y,z,m]
\]
with ten unrestricted integer unknowns, \textbf{554} live binary arithmetic
operations, and exact total degree \textbf{14,699,152}. Ordinary mathematics
formalizable in ZFC, using the identified native compiler and Sun
representation theorems, proves
\[
 \neg\exists\mathbf u\in\mathbb Z^{10}\;(F_{10}(\mathbf u)=0)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
This neither proves consistency nor claims a universal improvement on Sun's
ten-variable theorem. It is not an end-to-end proof-assistant certificate.
The prior balanced circuit used 637 operations and degree 34,378,296.

\subsection{Fixed source and a calibrated carry bridge}

The selective source has 31 natural coordinates and 22 quadratic residuals,
with 109 nonzero coefficient records. Only twelve old positive coordinates
are shifted by one; positivity of the remaining nineteen follows from the
source equations. All four native ports are bound together to the verified
215,492-byte tuple. The already shifted mask port is not shifted a second time.

For the recommended positional certificate, $W=170$, padding $p=169$,
$D_{\max}=4086$, and $K_c=4426$. Write $w_i$ for its 31 coordinate positions
and $H_j$ for its 22 targets. With signed source residuals
$r_j(\mathbf n)=\sum_\alpha p_{j,\alpha}\mathbf n^\alpha$, define
\begin{align*}
 c_{j,\alpha}&=(2-|\alpha|)!\prod_i\alpha_i!\;p_{j,\alpha},\\
 e(\alpha)&=(2-|\alpha|)p+\sum_i\alpha_iw_i,\\
 D_c(B)&=\sum_{j,\alpha}c_{j,\alpha}B^{H_j-e(\alpha)},&
 Z_c(B)&=\sum_jB^{H_j}.
\end{align*}
The independently checked support-isolation certificate proves
\[
 [B^{H_j}]\bigl(B^p+\textstyle\sum_i n_iB^{w_i}\bigr)^2D_c(B)
   =2r_j(\mathbf n).
\]
It also proves that $D_c$ has positive leading coefficient and
$K_c\ge\max H_j+2$. No full Sidon property is needed.

Let $C_0=1+\sum|c_{j,\alpha}|$. The actual fixed coefficients satisfy
$\beta_S=2^{864026}>8192C_0$. Define
\begin{align*}
 b&=1+3f,& B&=\beta_Sb^2,& \mathcal C&=bB^W,\\
 \ell&=\sum_iB^{w_i},&N_0&=B^{W+1},&N_1&=B^{K_c+1},&N&=N_0N_1,\\
 M_0&=N_0-1-(b-1)\ell,\\
 T_c&=M_0+N_0(B-2)Z_c(B),\\
 J_c&=2(B^p+g)^2D_c(B)+Z_c(B),&S_c&=g+N_0J_c,\\
 X&=N^2+(S_c+T_c)N+(N-1-S_c),\\
 Y&=2^{13824395}B^{4775}b.
\end{align*}
All exponents are fixed nonnegative integers. The final expression for $Y$
is the literal polynomial form of the exact mask-popcount threshold.

Suppose $f\ne0$, $b$ is a square, and $0\le g<2\mathcal C$.
Then $b\ge4$, $B>2b$, $D_c(B)>0$, and
\[
 2(B^p+g)^2D_c(B)<18C_0b^2B^{K_c}<N_1/256.
\]
The distinct targets and their two-position headroom give
$Z_c(B)<2B^{K_c-2}$ and $(B-2)Z_c(B)<B^{K_c-1}$.
Consequently $S_c,T_c\ge0$ and $S_c+T_c<N$. Thus
$X\ge N^2>3b$ and $Y>\max(b,256)$, before any power-of-two recovery.
Also $b,B,N_0,N\equiv1\pmod3$, so
$X\equiv1+T_c\equiv1-22\equiv0\pmod3$.

If $b$ is also a power of two, the low mask decodes
$g=\sum_i n_iB^{w_i}$ with $0\le n_i<b$. Write $h_t$ for the coefficients
of $(B^p+\sum_i n_iB^{w_i})^2D_c(B)$. Then $|h_t|<B/8$.
The nominal digit of $J_c$ at a target is $2h_t+1$. Every incoming borrow
is $-1$ or $0$; after normalization this digit is $0$ or $1$ exactly when
$h_t=0$. These are precisely the digits disjoint from the mask $B-2$.
Hence $S_c\mathbin{\&}T_c=0$ detects all 22 residuals.

Writing $N=2^u$, the separated binary blocks give
\[
 \operatorname{popcount}(X)
 =1+u+\operatorname{popcount}(T_c)-c(S_c,T_c),
\]
where $c$ is the nonnegative propagated carry count. For $b=2^k$ and
$B=2^v$, the mask has
$\operatorname{popcount}(T_c)=(W+23)v-31k-22$.
Legendre's formula
$v_2\binom{2X}{X}=\operatorname{popcount}(X)$ now proves
\[
 Y\mid\binom{2X}{X}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S_c\mathbin{\&}T_c=0.
\]
Conversely, a source zero supplies arbitrarily large $b=4^q$ and
$b\le g<\mathcal C$: the unshifted coordinates $D,\Delta$ are both positive,
and at most one has position zero. This supplies exactly the inherited
Sun source interface. The sparse-padding and popcount mechanisms are
adapted from the eighth incoming archive; the complete interior-padding
proof is in \texttt{SPARSE\_SOURCE\_PROOF.md}.

\subsection{Why the smaller weights are valid}

The norm identity is
\[
 R^2-16Z^2a_2Q^2
 =\prod_{\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\in\{\pm1\}}
  (Z+\epsilon_0\sqrt{a_0}+\epsilon_1\sqrt{a_1}+\epsilon_2\sqrt{a_2}).
\]
It holds over the complex numbers, including dependent squareclasses and
zero radicands. Since $S$ is odd, it is nonzero. The quintic
$(v^3-v-1)(v^2+23)$ has no rational root, so $\Theta\ne0$.
At $\Sigma=0$ the norm is $\Theta^8$, excluding that branch. Monicity of
the normalized norm and the rational-root theorem imply
$\Sigma\mid\Theta$, and the product identity gives a rational signed sum
\[
 \pm\sqrt{r_0}\ \pm b\sqrt{r_1}\ \pm b\sqrt{r_2}.
\]
No positivity has yet been assumed.

Put $J=1+(3O-4)b^2z^2$; then $\gcd(b,J)=1$. If $|b|>1$ and
$r_0=bJ$ were in a nontrivial signed squarefree class $d$, write
$r_0=da^2$. Linear independence of distinct squarefree radical classes
would force $b\mid a$: every other contribution in this class is a
multiple of $b$. This would imply $b\mid J$, a contradiction.
The exceptional case $|b|=1$ is $b=1$, hence $f=0$ and
$r_0=1-4z^2$ is odd. Both other radicands are divisible by four, so
an odd squareclass coefficient cannot be canceled by their even
coefficients. Thus $r_0$ is a nonnegative square in every case.
It cannot vanish: $J=0$ forces $b=1$ and $O=1$, inconsistent with $f=0$.
The actual $b=1$ case is then impossible because $z\ne0$.

Unique factorization and $b\equiv1\pmod3$ make $b$ a positive square.
Since $J>0$ and $z\ne0$, one has $O>0$, hence $f,l\ne0$ and $\Delta>0$.
Now $\mathcal C>0$. The formula for $\Delta$ first excludes $K=0$ and
$g\le0$, and then yields $g<2\mathcal C$. The source size bounds apply,
so $|L|\ge2$, $|U|\ge4$ and, as $V\in4\mathbb Z$, $|A|\ge4$.
Furthermore
\[
 K^2>4g(C-KL)^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |C|>|K|(|L|-\tfrac12)>|K|.
\]
For $V\in4\mathbb Z$, $|V|\le(V+1)^2$, whence $|P_n|\le A^2$ and
\[
 r_1<A^4C^2+4.
\]
In contrast, $C$ is odd, $D\ge A^2\ge16$, and
$E^2=C^4D^2\xi^2\ge C^2$. Thus $F\ge A^2C^2+1$.
Since $G$ is odd, $I\ge1$, so
\[
 r_2=DFI\ge A^4C^2+A^2>A^4C^2+4>r_1.
\]
This strict separation is established before assuming either remaining
square predicate. Subtracting the integral first root and dividing by $b$
leaves a rational sum $q=\pm\sqrt{r_1}\pm\sqrt{r_2}$. It cannot be zero
because $r_1\ne r_2$. Squaring gives
$\pm\sqrt{r_1}=(q^2+r_1-r_2)/(2q)$; both roots are therefore rational,
hence integral. Negative candidate radicands and dependent classes have
been resolved, not assumed away.

We recover the same three square predicates and divisibility as the
inherited finalizer. Conversely those predicates provide an integral signed
weighted root sum and hence a suitable integer $m$. This proves the exact
existential equivalence of the replacement.

\subsection{Circuit and exact-degree certificates}

The circuit shares a homogeneous evaluation of the positive parent,
with only the declared twelve coordinate shifts and diagonal corrections
for factorial weighting. Negative row signs reverse a subtraction.
A hybrid alternative uses sparse Horner evaluation only for rows whose
homogeneous offsets would be negative. Exact expansion matches all
115 resulting monomials over four independent native-port indeterminates.

Further exact gate savings use, with $a=A/2$ constructed without division,
\begin{gather*}
 G+1=(a-1)(F-1),\qquad G^2-1=(G+1)(G-1),\\
 T=2+bw(3C-2bw),\qquad
 H=C+F\{16Ey-(A-2)h\},\\
 R=Q^2+4(Z^2a_2-a_0a_1).
\end{gather*}
The live gate count is 347 multiplications, 149 additions and 58
subtractions. The propagated upper degree is 14,699,152. Substituting
all ten variables by $T$ gives a nonzero coefficient
$982070748$ modulo $1000000007$ at that degree, proving exactness.
Two additional primes independently give nonzero top coefficients.

A 513-operation alternative has exact degree 25,838,224; a 558-operation
hybrid has degree 14,696,016. These are search results, not minimality
claims. The independent verifier also replays every source and tail,
checks fixed-byte hashes, and checks exact ring identities. Finite
regressions include 38,016 strict-separation cases and negative controls
showing that $(1,b,b)$ is not an unconditional square detector.
The mathematical proofs, not the finite samples, establish universality.
