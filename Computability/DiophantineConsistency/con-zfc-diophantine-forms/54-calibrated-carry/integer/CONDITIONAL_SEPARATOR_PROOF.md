# Equal residual weights for the Sun ten-integer finalizer

## Result and scope

Replace the inherited three-radical weights

    (1, r0, r0*(Pn^2+K^2+3))

and the arithmetic-separator weights `(1,b,b*K)` by

    (1,b,b).

This is a source-conditional replacement, not an arbitrary-radicand identity.
Its new step is a strict inequality `r2>r1` obtained after recovering only
r0 as a square. In particular, the inequality does not assume the other two
square predicates, the Pell equations, or positivity of the original ten
input variables. Negative radicands, repeated squareclasses, b=1, K=0, and
Sigma=0 are handled before their respective exclusions are used.

The theorem applies to any source polynomials X,Y,cap with the following
interface. For b=1+3f, cap is positive whenever b is positive. Whenever
f is nonzero, b is a positive square, and 0<g<2cap, the source gives
X>=1 and Y>=2. The stronger source bounds X>3b and Y>max(b,256) used by
Sun imply this interface. Thus every source variant in the accompanying
construction, including selective coordinate shifts, can use the theorem.

The native compiler and Sun's ten-integer representation remain inherited
dependencies. This note is a new local separator theorem and does not claim
an end-to-end formal proof or a reduction below ten integer unknowns.

## 1. Full tail notation

All variables f,g,h,k,l,w,x,y,z,m are unrestricted integers. Named expressions
are polynomial abbreviations, never new unknowns. Use

    b=1+3f; L=lY; U=2LX; V=4gwY;
    A=U(V+1); B1=2X+1; C=B1+(A-2)h;
    Pn=U^2 V; K=X+1+k(Pn-2); S=2A-5;
    T=3bwC-2((bw)^2-1);
    Delta=8cap^3 g K^2-g^2[32(C-KL)^2cap^3+g^2K^2];
    O=f^2l^2 Delta; xi=zx;
    D=(A^2-4)C^2+4; E=C^2D xi;
    F=4(A^2-4)E^2+1;
    G=2(A+2)(A-2)^2E^2-1;
    H=C+(B1-C)F+16EFy;
    I=(G^2-1)H^2+1;
    r0=b[1+(3O-4)b^2z^2];
    r1=(Pn^2-4)K^2+4; r2=DFI;
    Sigma=xi S^5; t=T+Sy;
    Theta=(t^3-tS^2-S^3)(t^2+23S^2).

The new scaled radicands and center are

    a0=Sigma^2 r0;
    a1=Sigma^2 b^2 r1;
    a2=Sigma^2 b^2 r2;
    Z=Sigma m-Theta.

For any integers Z,a0,a1,a2 put

    Q=Z^2+a2-a0-a1;
    R=Q^2+4Z^2a2-4a0a1;
    Norm(Z,a0,a1,a2)=R^2-16Z^2a2Q^2.

The displayed equation is `Norm(Z,a0,a1,a2)=0`.

## 2. Exact norm and zero-scale exclusion

For arbitrary complex square roots the exact identity is

    Norm(Z,a0,a1,a2)
      = product_(e0,e1,e2 in {-1,1})
          (Z+e0 sqrt(a0)+e1 sqrt(a1)+e2 sqrt(a2)).

This identity is valid with zero radicands and dependent squareclasses.
It is checked independently by exact polynomial expansion. The normalized
Norm is monic of degree eight in Z.

A is even, so S=2A-5 is odd and nonzero. The polynomial
`(v^3-v-1)(v^2+23)` has no rational root: the cubic's only possible rational
roots are +/-1, neither is a root, and the quadratic has no real root.
Therefore Theta is nonzero for every integer assignment. If Sigma=0 the
new equation is Theta^8=0, impossible. Hence every root has Sigma!=0,
xi!=0, z!=0, and x!=0.

Divide the norm equation by Sigma^8. Its rational zero
`m-Theta/Sigma` belongs to a monic integer polynomial, so the rational-root
theorem makes it an integer. Thus Sigma divides Theta. The product identity
also yields a rational signed sum

    +/-sqrt(r0) +/-b sqrt(r1) +/-b sqrt(r2).

Signs of Sigma or b can be absorbed into the independent signs. This
reasoning does not assume any radicand is positive.

## 3. Recover r0 without sign assumptions

Let `J=1+(3O-4)b^2z^2`. Then gcd(b,J)=1 and b!=0 because b=1 modulo 3.

First suppose |b|>1. If r0=bJ is not a nonnegative square, write
`r0=d a^2` with a positive integer and d a signed squarefree integer other
than 1. Distinct signed squarefree radical classes are linearly independent
over Q. One justification is the character basis of the finite
multiquadratic extension generated by these roots. Each of the last two
roots, if it belongs to this same class, contributes an integer multiple
of b to the coefficient of sqrt(d). Rationality would force b|a, hence
b^2|r0=bJ, hence b|J, contradicting gcd(b,J)=1 and |b|>1.

The only case with |b|=1 is b=1, since -1 is not 1 modulo 3. Then f=0,
O=0, and r0=1-4z^2 is odd. Meanwhile r1 and r2 are divisible by 4:
Pn is even and A is even, so both r1 and D are divisible by 4. If r0 lies
in a nontrivial signed squarefree class, its class coefficient is odd;
any root of r1 or r2 in the same class has an even integer coefficient.
An odd integer plus even integers cannot vanish. Thus r0 is again a
nonnegative square. Since z!=0, the actual b=1 case has r0<0 and cannot
occur at a root.

Also r0 cannot be zero. Otherwise J=0 would give
`(3O-4)b^2z^2=-1`; hence |b|=|z|=1 and O=1. But b=1 forces f=0 and
O=0. Therefore r0 is a positive square.

Coprimality now makes b and J signed squares of the same sign. A negative
nonzero square is never 1 modulo 3, so b is a positive square and J>0.
If O<=0, then `J<=1-4b^2z^2<0`, a contradiction. Consequently O>0,
f!=0, l!=0, and Delta>0. At this point cap>0 by the source interface.

## 4. Bootstrap the size inequality from Delta only

If K=0 then `Delta=-32g^2 C^2 cap^3<=0`, impossible. Thus K!=0.
If g<=0, the first term of Delta is nonpositive and its subtracted term
is nonnegative. Hence g>0. Discarding the nonnegative square term from
Delta>0 gives `8cap^3gK^2>g^4K^2`, whence `g<2cap`.

The source interface now applies: X>=1 and Y>=2. Since l!=0,
`|L|>=2`, and therefore `|U|=2|L|X>=4`. V belongs to 4Z, so V+1!=0
and `|A|>=4`. Also C is odd, hence nonzero.

Again from Delta>0, discarding only its final nonnegative term and dividing
by the positive `8cap^3g`, we obtain

    K^2 > 4g(C-KL)^2.

As g>=1 this implies `|C-KL|<|K|/2`. The reverse triangle inequality gives

    |C| >= |KL|-|C-KL| > |K|(|L|-1/2) > |K|.

For V in 4Z we have `|V|<=(V+1)^2`: it is immediate for V>=0; for
V<=-4, put n=-V>=4 and use `(n-1)^2>=n`. Hence
`|Pn|=U^2|V|<=A^2`. It follows that

    r1=(Pn^2-4)K^2+4 < A^4 C^2+4.

No claim that r1 is nonnegative has been used.

On the other hand, D>0 and `D>=A^2>=16` because C^2>=1. Since xi!=0,
`E^2=C^4D^2xi^2>=C^2`. Also `4(A^2-4)>=A^2`, so

    F=4(A^2-4)E^2+1 >= A^2C^2+1.

G is odd, so G^2>=1 and I>=1 for every integer H. Consequently

    r2=DFI >= DF >= A^4 C^2+A^2 > A^4 C^2+4 > r1.

This proves the required strict separation without either remaining
square predicate or any Lucas/Pell theorem.

## 5. Recover the remaining squares

Subtract the integer signed root of r0 and divide by b!=0 in the rational
signed sum. We obtain `q=+/-sqrt(r1)+/-sqrt(r2)` in Q. If q=0, squaring
would give r1=r2, contradicting the strict inequality just proved.
For q!=0, squaring after isolating a root shows

    +/-sqrt(r1)=(q^2+r1-r2)/(2q),

which is rational. A rational square root of an integer is an integer.
The other root is rational as well. Thus r1 and r2 are nonnegative integer
squares. This proof includes negative candidate radicands and dependent
classes: those possibilities have either been excluded by the earlier
bootstrap or resolved algebraically, not assumed away.

We have recovered exactly the three square predicates and Sigma|Theta
required by the inherited finalizer. Conversely those predicates make any
signed weighted root sum an integer; with Sigma|Theta, choose m to make
one linear norm factor zero. Therefore the new finalizer has precisely
the same existential solvability predicate as the inherited one.

## 6. Exact factoring and degree methodology

The circuit retains the previously certified exact rewrites

    G=(A/2-1)(F-1)-1,
    H=C+F(B1-C+16Ey),
    t^3-tS^2-S^3=t(t^2-S^2)-S^3,
    f^2l^2=(fl)^2, b^2z^2=(bz)^2.

Here A/2 is constructed as `(lYX)(V+1)`, without division. The two residual
scaled radicands share `Sigma^2 b^2`; the third no longer needs a factor
K^2. All exponents are fixed; all gates are binary +,-,*.

The operation count uses only nodes reachable from the output. Degree is
proved by a propagated total-degree upper bound together with a nonzero
coefficient at that bound after substituting every input variable by T,
computed modulo a prime. The separate verifier also replays literal source
and tail formulas, checks fixed coefficient bytes, and expands source
polynomials over four independent native coefficient indeterminates.
Finite tests are not substitutes for the universal mathematical argument.

## 7. Final exact gate savings

The final builder additionally uses four identities, without changing the
polynomial of any given source/separator variant. Put Cshift=(A-2)h,
W=bw, Gplus=(A/2-1)(F-1), and xa=Z^2a2. Then

    C=B1+Cshift;
    T=2+W(3C-2W);
    H=C+F(16Ey-Cshift);
    G^2-1=Gplus(Gplus-2);
    R=Q^2+4(xa-a0a1);
    Norm=R^2-16Q^2xa.

Reusing Cshift avoids one subtraction/addition pair; the factored T,
G-square difference, and shared norm multiplier each save one live gate
relative to the preceding factored implementation. A negative source-row
sign is implemented by reversing its residual subtraction, not by an extra
multiplication by -1. These are exact ring identities, independently
checked before the literal circuit replay.
