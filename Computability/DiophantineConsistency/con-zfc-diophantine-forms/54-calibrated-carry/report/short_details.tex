\subsection{All short family domains and literal variants}
Every short family polynomial has fifteen strictly positive inputs. The
$70/80$, $68/101$ and inherited $66/157$ graphs use $\tau,y$ instead of
$r,u$; the $71/78$ graph uses $r,y$; the $72/64$ and $73/62$ graphs use
$r,u$. Other input names are exactly those listed above. The $73/62$
version retains the old quotient register
$V=c(Tf-1)-Rf^2$, whereas the squared-modulus versions use
$V=f^2(cT-R)-c$. They are existence-equivalent through constructive witness
rebuilding, not equal polynomials on arbitrary input tuples.

The 66-operation formula is inherited and rebound to the whole native tuple,

For the 68-operation product version define $N_s=1+I^2-A_0$ and use
$N_0N_1N_aN_kN_tN_s-1$. The factor $N_s$ is a unit at a zero and cannot
be $-1$ modulo four, so $N_s=1$ and the preceding proof applies. Its degree
is $22+18+30+7+2+22=101$, attained because all factor leaders are nonzero.
For the inherited 66-operation version set
$Q_0=(\Delta ic^2)^2$ and use
\[
 N_0N_1\{Q_0(V^2-y^2)+y^2\}N_kN_t(\Delta f^2-Q_0)-\Delta.
\]
Its direct ordinary bridge takes $i_{\rm ordinary}=\Delta i$ and
$T_{\rm old}=fT$ after unit extraction. Thus no missing historical coefficient
payload is needed to interpret the active instance.

The squared quotient does \emph{not} directly remove a radical from the
previous eleven-/twelve-positive equations: it removes the occurrence that
coupled the strong square to the auxiliary Pell norm. The following explicit
Pell-fiber test isolates that failure. Take $a_0=3$, $\Delta=8$, $R=3$, $c=35$, $i=1$,
$S=9800$, and
\[
 D_{\rm strong}=12005001,\quad T=1,\quad V=384159997,\quad y=384159999.
\]
The main Pell root is 99. Then $D_{\rm strong}$ is nonsquare, but
$V=D_{\rm strong}(cT-R)-c$ and
$S^2V^2-(S^2-1)y^2=1$. This is not asserted to be a compiler zero; it
shows exactly why that naive radical deletion is invalid. The new short
frontier therefore makes no new eleven-/twelve-variable claim.
