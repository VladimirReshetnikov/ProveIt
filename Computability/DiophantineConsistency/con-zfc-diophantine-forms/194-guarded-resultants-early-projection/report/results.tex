both matched predecessors. They trade degree against cost with one another.

The natural quartic reduces the previous $202$-variable, $154$-row,
$9{,}353$-term equation to $201$ variables and $6{,}002$ terms, with a small
height increase from $1{,}854{,}167$ to $1{,}860{,}103$. The older
$202$-variable height-$416{,}488$ equation remains a lower-height alternative.
The degree-six and degree-eight results exchange degree for fewer variables;
none is represented as a quartic.

The $209$-integer quartic has height $2^{44}$ and largest residual
coefficient $2^{22}$. Against the later-inspected $210$-variable,
$150$-row equation with $5{,}282$ terms and height $2^{40}$, it saves one
variable and one row but adds $204$ terms and multiplies height by $16$.
This is a dimension tradeoff, not uniform dominance. The earlier
$215$-variable low-height equations remain separate alternatives.

The preliminary $212$-variable construction is preserved as a method
control in the archive, with its distinct scaled-Pell proof. It is
numerically dominated by the incoming 210 and is not a final frontier claim.
The tempting odd-affine horizon was rejected because of a signed
$t=-1,x=0,q=1$ bootstrap escape; the accepted horizon $t=4u+2$ explicitly
excludes both $0$ and $-1$ before sign recovery.

The pinned low-padding2963 coefficient tuple uses $69{,}685$ raw bytes.
Its encoding is credited to the incoming source. The present work verifies
its complete binding to each new positive front rather than inventing that
encoding or silently changing a previously calibrated polynomial.
