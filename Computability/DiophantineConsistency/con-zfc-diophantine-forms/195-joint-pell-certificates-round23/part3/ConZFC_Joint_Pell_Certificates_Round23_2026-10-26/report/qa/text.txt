                   Round 23 research report and reproducible artifacts

             Prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov and the ProveIt repository

                                             Abstract
       This round improves several explicit polynomial presentations of syntactic ZFC consistency,
   while keeping their domains and size objectives separate. The principal unrestricted-integer
   quartic has 210 variables, 150 quadratic residuals, 5276 collected monomials and coefficient
   height 224 . A 209-variable quartic trades a larger height for one fewer coordinate.
       A new unweighted joint Pell certificate authenticates signed indices and power requests
   with two successive integrality arguments. Its selected joint equation reaches 113 variables at
   degree 16. A separate unfused monic-elimination control reaches 138 variables at degree eight;
   the smaller count has a substantially larger expanded support. Constructor fusion and inlining
   give a height-one quartic in 484 unrestricted integers, and reduce support at 493 variables
   from 24828 to 17058. Two complete ten-positive-integer circuits have exact degree/gate pairs
   202/99 and 190/120, using the unchanged coherent Start-only coefficient tuple. Their lower
   degrees cost additional gates.
       The report gives the unbounded witness arguments, precise inherited calibration boundary,
   complete native equations or finite substitution specifications, actual coefficient artifacts,
   and reproducible finite checks. It compares all nine incoming packages and selected current
   repository sources at a pinned snapshot. Ordinary ZFC proves that each selected equation’s
   unsolvability on its stated domain is equivalent to Con(ZFC) through the identified source
   calibration. No truth of consistency, globally simplest equation, external refereeing or new
   end-to-end proof-assistant build is claimed.

1     Selected results and what changed
The useful results are four distinct improvements, not a single ordering of “simplicity.” Every
coefficient, coordinate and arithmetic gate is fixed in the supplied artifacts. All statements
of equivalence use ordinary unbounded proofs composed with the identified inherited logical
calibration.

1.1   Unrestricted integer quartics
All four selected direct equations are sums of squares of quadratic polynomials on Z and have
exact degree four.

                          Endpoint       Variables       Rows    Terms     Height
                          Principal           210         150     5276       224
                          Dimension           209         149     5278       246
                          Balanced            211         151     5401     65537
                          Lower height        212         152    18381      5200

The principal has the same dimension and row count as incoming Pell Growth 210, but six fewer
collected terms and a height reduction from 240 to 224 . The 209-variable point is a dimension
tradeoff, not a universal improvement: its height is larger. The balanced 211-variable point
improves the incoming balanced 214-variable control’s dimension, rows, support and height. The
212-variable point does not beat the retained Round22 heights 4096 and 2304; those remain
genuine alternatives.
    The new arithmetic work replaces a positive quadratic horizon by a signed affine horizon
whose sign is recovered from an existing power request. Threshold four needs new local-budget
and odd-selector completeness arguments. A separate, explicitly certified affine-atom frame and
difference orientation lower coefficients. Monic growth splitting then makes a finite variable/height
tradeoff. Appendix A prints every residual of the principal equation.

2     Logical meaning and exact polynomial definitions
Throughout, N = {0, 1, 2, . . .}, Z>0 = {1, 2, 3, . . .} and Z is unrestricted. The domain belongs to
an equation’s definition. An equation over positive integers is not an unrestricted-integer equation;
a natural-domain chart needs a separate sign-restoration proof.
    Let Ref ZFC (r) be the fixed arithmetized predicate saying that r codes a finite syntactic ZFC
refutation. For each selected polynomial P on its stated domain D, the ordinary claim is

                          ∃x ∈ Dn P (x) = 0     ⇐⇒      ∃r ∈ N Ref ZFC (r).                        (1)

The unbounded arithmetic transformations below compose with the explicitly identified inherited
calibration. Formalizing this proof chain in ZFC yields

                            ZFC ⊢ ∀x ∈ Dn P (x) ̸= 0 ←→ Con(ZFC).
                                                       
                                                                                                   (2)

This is an equivalence, not a proof of either side. It does not assert consistency, decide solvability,
give a globally smallest polynomial, or provide a new end-to-end proof-assistant kernel check.

2.1   The retained calibration boundary
For the direct arithmetic families, the named logical interfaces are
      KEndpoint.c222_iff_zf_refutation
      ConstructibleChoice.zf_refutation_iff_zfc_refutation.

The first relates the fixed numerical calculus to a finite ZF refutation; the second transports between
ZF and ZFC refutations. Their exact sources, immutable historical pins and ordinary arithmetic
bridges are retained in the combined source closure. The coefficient-height-one families have
separately identified original quadratic sources and transfer their inherited zero-set calibrations
through the explicit graph maps below. They are not silently rebased to the newest direct system.
    The positive scalar branch uses its own machine, native-encoding and positive-P66 calibration
at one complete tuple of fixed coefficients. Its source theorem is not inferred merely from the
sizes of those numerals. The whole tuple, exact finite language, coefficient recipes and unbounded
native proof are supplied; replacing a single port in isolation is not licensed. The source package’s
old pin and this round’s current comparison pin are different records and are never substituted for
each other.
    There are four different kinds of evidence here. The logical calibration relates finite syntax to
the original arithmetic source. Unbounded proofs then establish Pell classification, cofinal witness
construction, CRT compatibility and sign recovery. Exact algebra transfers the source zero set to
finite new arrays or circuits. Finally independent finite checkers reconstruct those actual arrays,
not merely an abstract theorem instance. A hash proves byte identity, not a theorem. Coefficient
reconstruction proves an identity, not an unbounded Pell assertion. Successful finite replay is not
an end-to-end formal derivation of (1).

The sum of squares is zero exactly when every row is zero, on each of the stated integer domains.
The degree of a merged reciprocal row need not be two; the advertised total degree is separately
checked after collection. The quartic endpoints have degree-at-most-two rows and a nonzero

complete output, including equality and unit terms.
    A scalar circuit is a finite directed acyclic graph whose leaves are the stated independent
variables or supplied fixed integer constants. Every internal gate is binary addition, subtraction
or multiplication. It therefore denotes a unique ordinary polynomial over Z. Registers are
abbreviations, not new quantified unknowns. Fixed powers in the printed formula stand for the
specified multiplication gates; no quotient or square-root gate is used. All numerical ports are
supplied in full, not replaced by a hash or a promise that a suitable large integer exists.

For a nonzero collected polynomial P =
                                             P           α,
                                                α cα x

              deg P = max |α|,         | supp P | = #{α : cα ̸= 0},       ht(P ) = max |cα |.
                        cα ̸=0                                                        α

Support includes the constant if nonzero. Residual count is not support. Every independent
coordinate must be live after collection. Scalar cost counts live binary arithmetic gates, including

squaring and multiplication by fixed constants; reading a leaf and copying a register are free. Dead
gates do not count. Circuit gate count is not bit complexity. When no full scalar expansion is
supplied, its support and full coefficient height are unmeasured, not zero and not the maximum
literal leaf.
    Pareto comparisons hold only among the listed, certified presentations on compatible domains,
with degree fixed where stated. A point dominates another if it is no worse in every compared
measure and strictly better in at least one. Smaller dimension, lower degree, sparser support,
coefficient height one and fewer circuit gates are distinct objectives. No worldwide priority or
global minimality conclusion follows from this finite catalog.


3    The retained proof-calculus architecture
The numerical calculus has truth code 0, falsity code 1, modus ponens, generalization, eight logical
axiom schemata, and a guarded seed schema. Its literal square-shell constructors are

              I(a, b) = 2((a + b)2 + a),                            M (a, b) = I(a, b) + 1,
             A(a, b) = 2((a + b)2 + 3a + 4b + 3),                   E(a, b) = A(a, b) + 1.

They encode implication, membership, universal quantification, and equality. Put N (a) = I(a, 1)
and X(a, b) = N (A(a, N (b))). The pairing maps Q0 = (a + b)2 + a and Q1 = (a + b)2 + 3a + 4b + 3
enumerate complementary square shells; parity supplies the four constructors. The inherited
decoding theorem transports finite refutations between these codes and the original Cantor-coded
C222 calculus, fixing 0 and 1 [7].
   For clarity the retained logical codes are

                          r1 = X(a, E(a, b)),
                          r2 = I(A(a, I(b, c)), I(A(a, b), A(a, c))),
                          r3 = I(E(a, b), I(M (c, a), M (c, b))),
                          r4 = I(A(a, A(b, c)), A(b, A(a, c))),
                          r5 = I(E(c, a), I(M (c, b), M (a, b))),
                          r6 = I(X(a, A(a, b)), b),
                          rG = I(E(c, a), I(E(c, b), A(M (a, b), E(a, b)))),
                          r8 = I(I(I(c, d), a), I(I(a, c), I(b, c))).

Define

                    Req(b, k, m) = A(b, I(m, N (A(b, I(m, N (M (b, k))))))),
                       S(a, b, k) = A(k, I(M (k, a), M (k, b))),
                    T (a, b, k, m) = N (A(a, I(S(a, b, k), Req(b, k, m)))),
                 Clos(a, b, k, m) = A(b, I(M (b, k), N (A(a, I(m, T (a, b, k, m)))))),
                     nks(a, b, c) = A(c, I(Clos(a, b, c, c), N (Req(b, c, c)))),
                      RV(a, b, c) = I(M (a, b), A(c, M (a, b))),
                              r9 = N (I(RV(a, b, c), nks(a, b, c))).

24     Further questions with concrete proof obligations
  1. Can the affine horizon use a smaller progression than the selected threshold-four family?
     Every proposed change must reprove the initial signed bootstrap, exclude exceptional zero or

                                                54
      unit bases, retain the actual q t request, and construct an odd binomial selector cofinally. A
      formal coefficient saving without those conditions is not an equation for the same arithmetic
      relation.
  2. Can a different integral basis for the affine frame lower coefficients without weakening the
     error budget too far? The relevant tests are the full actual-atom ledger, raw classified-index
     bounds, |A| > W W , the orientation residue sum, and the appropriately strengthened power
     or joint theorem. Bounding only the nominal requests is not enough before orientation.
  3. Can the unweighted joint certificate be composed with a lower-height source or another
     block partition before monic elimination? The matched unfused control isolates the current
     orientation saving; further apparent dimension improvements must report degree, collected
     support and height as well. At zero or mixed-sign bases, the second integrality step cannot
     be replaced by a direct nonnegative-sum argument.
  4. Can exact elimination retain the joint theorem’s small degree while avoiding its large
     expansion? A compact arithmetic DAG and a sparse expanded polynomial measure different
     resources. Any further deletion needs its unit pivot, acyclic restoration and attained final
     degree checked on the actual emitted source, not only on a symbolic model.
  5. Can a joint search over constructor fusion, additive inlining, scale expansion and graph
     aliases improve the height-one catalog? The search cost is not final support, and individual
     changes can increase support. Every candidate still needs the complete triangular pullbacks,
     occurrence coverage, unit anchoring, integral energy and universal lift including the hub
     term.
  6. Can the newer direct arithmetic fronts improve those height-one networks? Their smaller
     original dimensions do not imply fewer aliases or occurrences after normalization. Such a
     transfer needs a new measured graph, not a relabelled inherited source theorem.
  7. Can higher quotient centers reduce the positive scalar degree at a reasonable gate cost? The
     key obligations are an exact division-free integer polynomial evaluator, an all-sign pre-rank
     center bound, a transport valid at the auxiliary index-three branch, and a strictly positive
     completeness lift. Bare b2 is not licensed by the proved U Eb2 inequality.
  8. Can these ordinary compositions be given a complete formal-kernel replay? This would
     require the actual transitive logical and arithmetic source closure and explicit port bindings.
     Finite coefficient reconstruction, mutation rejection and byte replay are useful prerequisites,
     not substitutes for such a formalization.
These are bounded research directions with named proof obligations. They are not claims of
achievable savings, exhaustive negative results, global lower bounds or mathematical priority.
