\subsection{Product constructors for constants}
The new normalization retains the old logical coordinate list, constant
values, scale values and source rows. If a needed constant $K_c$ has
available earlier constants $K_a,K_b$ with $1<a\le b<c$ and $ab=c$,
replace its additive definition by
\[
 K_c-K_aK_b=0.
\]
The chosen pair is the lexicographically least $(b,a)$ among the eligible
pairs. This is fixed certificate data, not an unknown of the equation.

\begin{proposition}
These replacements preserve the normalized integer zero set literally,
and preserve the final network zero set by the canonical source lift and
projection. They introduce no new normalization coordinate or sign branch.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Constants are ordered increasingly. Both factors precede $K_c$, even when
$a=b$. Induction through the unchanged additive and new multiplicative
monic definitions forces $K_c=c$ uniquely. Conversely the intended values
satisfy every definition. Every source row and scale has exactly the same
pullback. Thus the normalized zero sets agree in the same coordinates.
Each final network separately has the already proved inverse projection
and unique lift through the common source, which supply the desired
bijection even though the final aliases and flows may differ. The universal
identity~\eqref{eq:networklift}, including the hub term, remains valid.
No division is used. Positivity of a forced constant is not a restriction
on a quantified final coordinate.
\end{proof}
The monic row now has two signed monomial occurrences rather than three.
This changes the graph search and can remove more than one eventual alias
or flow; final savings are measured after complete coefficient collection.

\subsection{Chained scale constructors}
The smallest selected endpoint also replaces a scale definition
$S(c,x)-K_cx=0$ when an earlier scale $S(b,x)$ and a constant $K_d$
exist with $d>1$, $b<c$ and $db=c$. The new definition is
\[
 S(c,x)-K_dS(b,x)=0.
\]
Constants precede all scales and scales are ordered by
(coefficient, source coordinate). Induction therefore forces
$S(c,x)=d(bx)=cx$ for every integer $x$, including negative and zero
values. Conversely those values satisfy the new rows. The monic pivot
and predecessor on the \emph{same} original coordinate are essential;
merely factoring the numerical coefficient without checking the base
coordinate would not establish the identity. This again preserves both
normalized zero sets and the final source-lift bijection without division.
The deterministic choice is the lexicographically least available $(d,b)$.

The independent checker removes row orientations and verifies the
identical coordinate list and metadata, every changed and unchanged row,
all earlier-factor conditions, every exact source pullback, and the
complete collected final polynomial. It tests source/constant corruption
classes and a separate chained-scale corruption. No heuristic claim of
optimality is used to prove correctness or numerical metrics.
