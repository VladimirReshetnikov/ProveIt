\begin{abstract}
This investigation produces two concrete improvements in the direct proof-certificate branch of the ProveIt consistency project. A polynomial radius in nine Euclidean quotients makes the associated terminal power outputs admissible before their signs are known. Their power equations then recover the omitted signs. After exact integer elimination this gives a quartic in \textbf{577 unrestricted integer variables}, with \textbf{7,326 monomials} and coefficient height \(2,445,790,974,704\). The matched predecessor has 604 variables and 9,676 monomials at the same degree and height. A lower-height companion has 582 variables, 6,069 monomials and height 964,213.

A separate construction replaces repeated coefficients and linear occurrences by private variables forced to equal one. Applied to the squared-output unprojected system, it yields a quartic in \textbf{3,564 unrestricted integer variables} whose nonzero coefficients are exactly drawn from \(\{-2,1,2\}\). Its 14,518 coefficients are supplied explicitly and checked in full.

Both constructions preserve the existence of a finite syntactic ZFC refutation, using the inherited numerical C222 calibration. Consequently their unsolvability is provably equivalent in ZFC to \(\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})\), by ordinary mathematical arguments formalizable in ZFC. Neither construction uses K215 or a Jones universal polynomial. The new arguments are not presented as completed Lean or object-language ZFC formalizations. No global minimum, historical novelty theorem, consistency proof, or improvement over the repository's fewest-variable branch is claimed.
\end{abstract}
