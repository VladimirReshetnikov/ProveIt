# Fixed logical calibration and scope

The frozen polynomial is not tied to an unspecified recursively enumerable
set. It encodes derivability of falsity code1 in the explicit finite-schema
C222 calculus, including free truth0, eight unconditional axiom schemas,
the guarded bundle, modus ponens and generalization.

The ordinary source chain is:

    polynomial zero <=> finite C222 derivation of1
                    <=> syntactic ZF refutation
                    <=> syntactic ZFC refutation.

The named calibration results are KEndpoint.c222_iff_zf_refutation and
ConstructibleChoice.zf_refutation_iff_zfc_refutation at ProveIt commit
08200eb10fde2db408c69dffd840d6920a569b1f. The second is the standard
constructible-universe equiconsistency of ZF and ZFC. The actual files,
exact hashes and primary-source URLs are preserved in:

inherited_proofs/intake/ConZFC_Shell_Reciprocal_660_2026-10-24/ConZFC_Shell_Reciprocal_660/upstream/ConZFC_407_888_Quartics/upstream/provenance/upstream/calibration/CALIBRATION_PINS.json

The numerical calculus is displayed in calculus.tex and implemented in
direct_calculus.py in the parent of that calibration directory. The exact
compositional arithmetic comparison is audit_calculus.py. The square-shell
constructor recoding is described by shell_encoding_proof.md in the
preserved reciprocal package; all its code and finite data remain included.
The new shared-factorial label separation and Horn-flow proof are in
inherited_proofs/FACTORIAL_CARRIER_PROOF.md.

The present work proves new arithmetic reductions over the frozen source.
It does not rerun or recheck the complete Lean dependency closure for the
calibration, and it does not supply a new BProv object-language derivation.
Its claim is the inherited ordinary mathematical equivalence, with the new
arguments formalizable in ZFC, not a newly machine-checked end-to-end theorem.
No theorem relies on assuming consistency or observing that bounded tests
find no refutation. The reproducer is self-contained and does not need the
remote repository.
