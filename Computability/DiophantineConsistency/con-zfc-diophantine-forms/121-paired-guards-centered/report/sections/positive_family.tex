\subsection{Root recovery before transport}
On every positive input, $D>1$, $E\ge c^2$, $F_s>c+2$ and $\delta>0$. The integer $b$ is either zero, giving $V=-c$, or satisfies $|V|\ge F_s-c>1$. Thus $a=DF_s$ and $d_y=D_y/\delta$ are positive. They cannot be equal: $\delta a=D_y$ is impossible modulo $D$, since $D_y\equiv-1\pmod D$.

The four-factor identity is
\[
 (z^2-a-d_y)^2-4ad_y
 =\prod_{\epsilon,\nu\in\{-1,1\}}
       (z-\epsilon\sqrt a-\nu\sqrt{d_y}).
\]
Applied with $z=Z/M$, it gives a rational signed sum at a zero. This sum is nonzero because the radicands differ. Squaring and solving linearly shows that both roots are rational. Since $a$ is an integer and $\gcd(D,F_s)=1$, the factors are squares:
\[
 D=\tau^2,\qquad F_s=f^2,\qquad I=\tau E,
 \qquad f^2-\Delta I^2=1,\quad c^2\mid I,\quad Q=(\Delta I)^2.
\]
The adjacent-Pell descent makes $y=\sqrt{d_y}$ integral. Indeed, write $y=a_0/m$ in lowest terms with $m>0$. Then $m^2\mid Q-1$, and the norm-preserving transformation
\[
 v'=(2Q-1)v-2(Q-1)y,\qquad y'=(2Q-1)y-2Qv
\]
keeps $v'$ integral and the denominator $m$, while $0<|v'|<v$ for $v>1$. Descent from $v=|V|$ reaches $v=1,y=1$. Therefore
\[
 Z/M=\epsilon\tau f+\nu y. \tag{P1}
\]

The retained all-index folding lemma now gives $c=\psi_A(p)$ with $p$ odd. The first Pell equation gives $k=2\psi_{2v_0+1}(n_0)$. If $n_0=1$, then $\eta=\zeta=1$ and $c=2Y+1$ lies strictly between $\psi_A(1)$ and $\psi_A(2)$, a contradiction. Hence
\[
 k\ge8v_0+4,\quad c>kY,\quad c\ge\psi_A(3)=4\Delta+3.
\]
The unchanged pre-transport mask estimate is $R<c\alpha/4$. It gives
\[
 b>c(T+3\alpha/4)>c,\qquad V>1,\qquad K_+>0. \tag{P2}
\]
These steps do not assume transport nonnegativity, the main-index equality, evenness of $q$, or divisibility of $e$.

\subsection{Why the auxiliary term alone suffices}
Set $S_0=\Delta I$, so $Q=S_0^2$. Pell classification of the auxiliary norm gives $S_0V=\chi_{S_0}(\nu_0)$. Reduction modulo $S_0$ shows that $\nu_0$ is odd. By (P2),
\[
 V=F_sb-c>(F_s-1)c=\Delta I^2c>4Q.
\]
The indices $1$ and $3$ give $V=1$ and $V=4Q-3$, respectively. Thus $\nu_0\ge5$, and
\[
 V\ge16Q^2-20Q+5>8Q^2,\qquad
 b=(V+c)/F_s>8Q. \tag{P3}
\]
Here $F_s<Q$. Also $Q-1>2F_s$, so
\[
 0<y-V=\frac{V^2-1}{(Q-1)(y+V)}<\frac b4,
 \qquad \frac e{\mu^2}<\frac b4. \tag{P4}
\]
The second inequality follows from $b-e=c(T+\alpha)-k+1>0$ and $\mu^2>4$; it is an upper bound even when $e<0$. Finally
\[
 \tau f<ADE<Q,\qquad X<D<Q,
 \qquad \tau f+X/q^4<2Q<b/4. \tag{P5}
\]

On the direct chart the exact center is
\[
 \frac ZM=n+V+S+\frac{K_+}H+\frac Y{q^3}
 -\frac{WB}{q(q-1)}-\frac X{q^4}-\frac e{\mu^2},
 \qquad W=1+q(q-1)b.
\]
If $B\le-1$, then $WB/[q(q-1)]<-b$. Solve this relation and (P1) for $n$. Since $S\ge0$, (P4)--(P5) give
\[
 n<b/4+b/4+b/4-b=-b/4<0,
\]
a contradiction. Thus $B\ge0$. The ordinary chart has $W=1+(q-1)b$ and omits $X/q^4$; the same proof gives $B_t\ge0$.

\subsection{Soundness and positive completeness}
For the direct chart, the complete old fused polynomial is recovered by the universal substitution
\[
 n_{\rm old}=n+S+HDE\,B. \tag{P6}
\]
The old transport majorant was $HDE+b$, so (P6) makes the entire carrier and norm identical at every integer assignment. At a new positive zero, $B\ge0$, hence $n_{\rm old}>0$. The retained old soundness theorem therefore gives a finite syntactic refutation.

For the ordinary chart, the now-licensed inherited proof gives successively $C>0$, $3q<R<q^4-2$, the main index $p=R$, even $q$, $\mu^2\mid e$, and the first index $(R+1)/2$. The exact ratio estimate and whole-product projection give
\[
 H\mid q^3(q-1)(X-2^R)(2^RX+1),
\]
with the complete product smaller than $H$ in absolute value; hence $X=2^R$. No potentially nonunit factor is canceled modulo $H$. The residual carrier fractions give $q^3\mid Y$ and $q-1\mid B_t$, while fixed-minus parity gives $R\equiv3\pmod4$. Fresh unscaled source reconstruction uses $A_{\rm source}=Y(X+1)+2$. The same coherent native calibration gives a finite refutation.

Conversely, start with the retained canonical source witnesses from a finite refutation. They have $X=2^R$, $R\equiv3\pmod4$, $q^3\mid Y$, $q^4\mid X$, $D=\tau^2$, and positive first/main coordinates. Choose a positive Pell matrix period modulo $\tau c^2$, and set
\[

 i=I/(\tau c^2)>0.
\]
The finite order of an invertible matrix over this finite residue ring guarantees such a period. The new literal kernels are then exactly $F_s=f^2$ and $Q=(\Delta I)^2$.

Use the explicit auxiliary-completeness witness
\[
 V_*=\frac{\chi_{\Delta I}(R)}{\Delta I},\qquad
 y=\psi_{\Delta I}(R),\qquad
 T_*=\frac{V_*+c+Rf^2}{cf^2},\qquad T=T_*-\alpha.
\]
The retained lemma proves integrality. Its elementary growth estimate gives $V_*\ge9Q^2$ and $T_*>9\Delta I>\alpha$. Hence $T>0$, and
\[
 h_y:=y-V_*>\frac{V_*}{4Q}\ge\frac{9Q}{4}. \tag{P7}

Write $\kappa=K_+/H$, $s_Y=Y/q^3$ and $\rho=e/\mu^2$; these are canonical integers, with $0<\kappa<c<I$, $0<s_Y<c<I$ and $\rho\ge0$. The canonical transport quotient $t$ is nonnegative. All three choices of center obey $S-\tau f<2DE<2I^2$. Define
\[
 n=Wt+\rho+\tau f-S+h_y-\kappa-s_Y
       +\begin{cases}X/q^4,&\text{direct chart},\\0,&\text{ordinary chart}.\end{cases}
\]
The negative loss is less than $2I^2+2I<4I^2$, while (P7) exceeds $4I^2$. Thus $n>0$, even when $t=0$, and the center equals $\tau f+y$. This proves the converse with genuinely positive coordinates, not a presumed positive inverse of (P6).

The equivalence to the absence of finite ZFC refutations inherits the exact retained source calibration. This ordinary mathematical argument does not assert that a new Lean endpoint, an internal encoded ZFC certificate, or a proof of consistency has been produced.

\subsection{Exact degree, not a syntactic bound}

For an exact global certificate, scale every unknown by a formal $t$ and work in the Laurent-series field at $t=\infty$. Choose roots $g^2=DF_s$ near $ADE$ and $y^2=D_y/\delta$ near $V$. The identities
\[

\[

\]
This proves the global upper bounds and attainment; exact univariate replay is an additional transcription check, not the degree proof itself.
