# Attribution, scope, and provenance

The new prime-separation construction, article, compact evaluator, degree
certificates, wrapper verifier, and internal mathematical reviews are
AI-assisted research prepared in this collaboration for Vladimir Reshetnikov.
New material is offered under the MIT No Attribution license in `LICENSE`.
No historical priority, external peer review, or global minimality is claimed.

This work builds on Vladimir Reshetnikov's ProveIt repository and its
contributed Diophantine consistency manuscripts. Inherited files retain
their own notices and licenses, preserved under `licenses/`, `inherited/`,
and `ten_integer/upstream/`. See their individual provenance ledgers.

The shared arithmetic-DAG helper `new_integer_compression/circuit.py` is
copied from the incoming radical-residue package. The full source-34 native
coefficient tuple and the determinant scales are copied without numerical
changes. The retained coefficient generator, literal K215 transition table,
and 943-tile list come from the incoming Pell-congruences package's source-34
component. Earlier source-34 attribution includes Wade's native compiler;
its license and notices are supplied in `licenses/`.

The new polynomial transformation has an ordinary mathematical proof
formalizable in ZFC, composed with explicitly identified inherited compiler,
Pell, canonical-completeness, and syntactic-refutation calibration theorems.
The finite executable checks certify their stated algebra and data scope.
This package does not include a newly checked complete Lean/Rocq theorem
or an encoded derivation inside the repository's object-language ZFC calculus.

The ancillary ten-integer graphs preserve the parent polynomials by three
exact identities. Their parent data and proof dependencies are independent
of the new root merger and remain attributed in that directory.
