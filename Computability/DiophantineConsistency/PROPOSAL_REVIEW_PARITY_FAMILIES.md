## First family: preserve parity while removing private roots

Its elementary two-root merger is exact for all integers a,delta,r with delta nonzero:

```
exists integer u,y: u²=4a+1 and delta*y²=r
iff exists integer t:
  [delta(t(t+1)-a)-r]²-(4a+1)delta*r=0.
```

Put x=2t+1. The exact identity

```
16G=[delta(x²+4a+1)-4r]²-4(4a+1)x²delta²
```

makes `u=[delta(x²+4a+1)-4r]/(2x delta)` rational with integer square. Thus u is an odd integer, and `(x-u)/2` is integral. The affine odd x excludes the false zero-carrier branch of an unconstrained norm. Conversely the sum u+2y is odd and gives t. This proof needs no Pell classification or positivity.

For the degree204 endpoint, the three base radicands are `A=4A4+1`, `B=4b_guard+1`, and `Caux=(Q-1)(QV²-1)`. Weights1,2,4 prevent signed cancellation inside any nontrivial rational squareclass: the least weighted term has odd square coefficient, while every later term contributes an even coefficient. The arbitrary last radicand can be negative; the lemma then excludes it at a rational root sum rather than assuming it positive.

The retained strong residual first proves `Q=I²` is nonnegative and Q is not1, using the unconditional large-parameter bound Delta>1. This does not invoke the deleted positivity row. Therefore delta is nonzero, and `Caux` is nonzero. If Q>1, the rational auxiliary root is integral by adjacent-coefficient descent. The exceptional Q=0 is handled directly: delta=-1,Caux=1 gives y=+/-1. The new odd square root of B reconstructs the deleted private guard coordinate `(d-1)/2`; this restores the original guard only after its square has been proved. No denominator is canceled using a guard that has not yet been recovered.

The primitive three-root polynomial is the exact eight-sign norm divided by the fixed constant256. This fixed division yields integer coefficients, not a new divisibility condition. It removes the first root, auxiliary gap and one private guard coordinate in favor of one unrestricted carrier. The two retained main/strong residuals each have degree14, and the primitive norm degree176. The product of their three1-mod4 unit factors therefore has degree204. Completeness reconstructs every parent zero through its actual private roots; a new canonical Pell reselection is unnecessary for this fifteen-variable result.

The thirteen-variable chart is more restrictive. Its c and normalized strong multiplier are odd by construction, establishing the four odd base radicands before the guard is known. Its completeness uses the same explicit Rc strong-index and squared-modulus auxiliary construction reviewed for root fusion. The main-root congruence also has the needed parity: canonical main root and b0 are even, while H is odd, so the quotient gamma is even and the new odd carrier exists. This is an existence reconstruction from the calibrated canonical family, not an inverse for every old zero.

## Second family: modulus four recovers the omitted odd strong multiplier

This family eliminates the strong coordinate as well. Write

```
d=c⁴, Q=Delta(f²-1),
m=XY², B0=mk, A=1+m(m+1)k², S=k(2m+1).
```

The retained guard alone gives d>0,Delta>1,A>0,|k|>=2,|m|>1 and `S²-4A=k²-4>=0`. It also excludes Q=1. None of these facts uses an omitted first or strong equation. Because X and m are multiples of4, B0 is0 modulo4 and A is1 modulo4.

For sixteen variables take `n=4n0+2`, `L=n+B0`, `U=d(L²-A)+Q`, `N2=U²-4dL²Q`. At a zero L is nonzero. Then `u=U/(2dL)` is rational with `d u²=Q`, while the exact companion identity makes `L-u` a rational square root of integer A. It is an odd integer. Since L is even, u is also an odd integer and restores `r=(u-1)/2` without any rational-square assumption on Q/d. The three other residuals restore the remaining rows.

Completeness changes only the sign of the private first root tau_old to make it agree with u modulo4. This sign occurs nowhere else in the parent. Then `(u+tau_old-B0-2)/4` is an integer and yields the desired carrier. Its exact norm factorization is checked over a free integer polynomial ring. The degree56 norm squared gives degree112, with uniform coefficient `2^40*3^72*beta^60` at `c^16 J^60 w^12 v^16 h^8`.

The fifteen-variable variant uses a three-root norm with signed radicands

```
A, Q/d, 16S²(QV²-1)/(Q-1),
L=4n0+2+B0+4SV=2 mod4.
```

It handles all exceptional branches explicitly. Q<0 leaves exactly one imaginary root; Q=0 would leave an odd root of A plus a multiple of4; Q=1 is impossible; Q>1,V=0 again leaves exactly one imaginary root. In the remaining positive case the final radicand is at least `16S²>=64A`, so the first and final roots cannot cancel. A nonzero rational sum of three positive roots with some irrational root must consist of an exactly canceling irrational pair and one rational root. The other two possible pairs would leave either an odd integer or a multiple of4, contradicting `L=2 mod4`. Thus all roots are rational, adjacent-Pell descent supplies an integral y, and u is the missing odd integer as above. Negative S and all root signs are allowed.

The finalizer uses Gsum=A2²+A5²>=0 for every input. Therefore `(1+Gsum)(1+N3)=1` forces Gsum=N3=0 without a sign promise on N3. Centering n0 by hYR is an integral coordinate shear and preserves its residue. These arguments are valid but give fifteen-variable points dominated by the first family.
