## Strong-root deletion is the concrete next lead

The [complete90 proof](../HilbertTenthProblem/Papers/research-wip/native-stream-queue/complete90_signed_root_elimination.md)

operation. Their universal results are90/406/17-positive and92/325/17-positive.
They restore a unique **signed** integral root first; its positivity requires
the actual valid-compiler signed-quotient theorem. Mere rational-square
integrality does not establish that last step.

The same local algebra applies to the fixed P67 data in
`36-small-tuple/baseline/product67.json`. Write

```text
Delta=A, c=R10a, R=r_lhs, T=auxiliary_quotient,
S=Delta*i*c², Q=S²,
P=norm_first*norm_main*norm_index*norm_transport,
D=1+Delta*i²*c⁴, b=c+R*D, u=c*T,
a=Q*(D*u²+b²)-(Q-1)*y²-1, B=2*Q*u*b.
```

Two explicit candidate tails are

```text

F75 = P*(a²-D*B²+1)-1.
```

Their efficient schedules reuse`4*(P*Q*D*u²)*(P*Q*b²)` for the first
squared cross term, and`4*(Q*D*u²)*(Q*b²)` for the second. The actual P67
has50 computed rows independent of`f` and17 depending on it. Keeping the

On the retained positive domain,`D>c>0` and`b=c+R*D` cannot vanish for any
integer`R`, so`B!=0`. The norm argument restores a unique nonzero integral

The **initial remaining obligation** was to specialize signed-`T`
positivity to this fixed blank source. The symmetry
`(f,T)->(-f,-T)` would then force the restored root positive. Until that
hypothesis was proved, these were candidates rather than calibrated
