## 2. Producer polynomial route

A polynomial dictionary maps sorted finite variable-index tuples to
nonzero integers. The empty tuple denotes the constant monomial.
Producer multiplication concatenates and sorts each pair of monomials,
adds their integer products, then removes zero coefficients. Addition
is coefficientwise. norm collects duplicate terms, and terms serializes
all remaining coefficients in sorted tuple order.

For each complete source monomial c*x_i1*...*x_ik the producer starts
with c and multiplies one factor at a time. Factor x21 is replaced by
z21-2z10-2z11; every other factor is its corresponding variable. This
is the ordinary homomorphism induced by the displayed substitution.
Finite addition over every source monomial therefore computes r_j(Sz)
for every row, including constants, mixed terms and repeated factors.
The complete SOS is then collected from every row square. All original
row/header/variable metadata are copied exactly; only actual term lists
and the stated variant suffix change.

## 3. Independent checker route and complete identities

The checker does not import build.py or share its polynomial functions.
It parses every coefficient and index using exact Python int type, so
booleans and floats are rejected. Indices must lie in the actual input
namespace. Collection sorts indices and adds duplicate monomials,
allowing semantically equivalent term presentations in controls.

For a monomial with k occurrences of index21 remove those occurrences
to obtain a fixed remaining monomial M. For fixed d in{-2,2}, the
checker emits, for every r,s>=0 with r+s<=k, the term

    c*binom(k,r)*binom(k-r,s)*d^(r+s)
       * M * z10^r*z11^s*z21^(k-r-s).

The trinomial theorem proves that their sum is
c*M*(z21+d*z10+d*z11)^k. Unlike the producer, this route performs no
factor-by-factor polynomial multiplication for substitution. d=-2 is
the fixed forward shear; d=2 is its inverse. No other recipe is searched
or materialized.

For a collected row sum_i c_i*M_i, its square is the sum of the diagonal
c_i^2*M_i^2 and, for each unordered distinct pair,2c_i*c_j*M_i*M_j.
The checker uses exactly that upper-triangle pair accumulation. It
collects every original source square and verifies the full existing
expanded polynomial. It then substitutes in every actual source row,
collects all transformed squares and compares their entire dictionary
with direct d=-2 substitution of the entire old quartic. This proves
the complete forward identity, not just a leading form or a sample.

For each actual new saved expanded polynomial, the checker reconstructs
every actual row and its full SOS, compares every coefficient with the
independently reconstructed forward polynomial, then applies d=2 to
the entire actual quartic and compares every coefficient with the whole
old polynomial. The inverse identity is thus also checked on actual
saved data. Both substitutions leave z10,z11 fixed, so the maps compose
to the identity on all generators, hence on all integer polynomials.
