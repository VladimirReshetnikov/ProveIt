# Direct-ordinate 86-operation candidate

This graph has a checked all-model no-zero equivalence with the 87-operation literal; see the proof scope and provenance below. Starting from the pinned 87-operation graph, the auxiliary signed ordinate is `V = f²*(T*c − R) − c` and the old positive offset witness appears only in `y_old = V + u_old`. The candidate deletes that addition and instead uses a new positive witness `y` directly in the auxiliary factor:

```text
old: y_old = V + u_old
     Fa = y_old² − Q*(y_old² − V²)
new: Fa = y² − Q*(y² − V²)
```

The new graph has 86 live binary operations (47 multiplication, 21 addition, 18 subtraction), with all 13 witnesses and four symbolic ports live. This counts the unshifted output DAG and is not a degree certificate or minimality claim. [`audit.py`](audit.py) pins the 87-row parent, checks topology and output liveness, confirms the old offset has no other use, and verifies the exact integer-polynomial identity for the auxiliary factor under the **signed** substitution `u_old = y − V`; a sign mutation is rejected. The generated [candidate](candidate.json) SHA-256 is `e9889a81a491b85491dde749e82158109ea98ff94ed41fc0f05dc810ec58b91f`. Normal Python and `python -O` produce identical [audit output](AUDIT.json).

The signed substitution alone does not prove equivalence over positive witnesses: `y − V` must admit a positive old offset, and the old signed ordinate can be negative. The Lean proof now derives the missing facts from the actual 86 literal: its factor units, auxiliary norm, signed rank residue and bounds give `|V| < y`, so a positive old offset exists. Conversely, an actual positive 87 zero supplies its auxiliary norm and a positive ordinate, yielding a new witness.
