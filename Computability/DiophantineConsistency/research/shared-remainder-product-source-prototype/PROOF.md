## 1. The existing integer guard graph

Write G(u;a,b,c)=u-a²-b²-c²-c. Over integer auxiliaries,

    exists a,b,c: G(u;a,b,c)=0 iff u>=0.

The forward implication uses a²,b²>=0 and c(c+1)>=0 for every integer c.
For completeness, apply the existing Legendre three-square theorem to

identity by4 gives u=a²+b²+c²+c. This includes u=0 with a=b=c=0.
This is precisely the guard encoding already used in the accepted parent.

    rs-a²-b²-c²-c=0.

## 2. The integer pair lemma uses only an anchored sum

For integers r,s,a,b suppose

    a>=0, b>=0, r+s=a+b, rs>=0.

Then r,s>=0. If r<0, the product condition forces s<=0, hence r+s<0,
contradicting a+b>=0. Similarly s<0 is impossible. The boundary sum0 is
included and forces r=s=0. Conversely r,s>=0 immediately implies rs>=0.
The lemma needs no power meaning or strict positivity of a denominator.

Thus, wherever the body implies N=1+a+b=1+r+s and the direct anchor
guards a,b>=0 are retained, replacing the two guards r>=0,s>=0 by the
single guard rs>=0 is equivalent on ALL integer zeros of that body.
N>=1 is a consequence of the anchors, not an additional premise or a
Pell-derived fact used to justify the replacement.
