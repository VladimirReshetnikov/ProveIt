\subsection{What the measurements mean}

The three machine replacements retain their public theorem headers byte for byte. Together they change 33 to 17 nonblank declaration lines, or 22 to 6 proof-body lines: \textbf{16 lines removed with no helper or import overhead}. R1 changes 31 to 23 declaration lines, or 26 to 18 body lines, also without an external helper. These are measurements against pinned source text, not a built virtual checkout. The ledger argues that the same obligations survive prior outcomes; it does not generate or compile a combined earlier-patch tree.

E1 changes 18 to 7 declaration lines, or 17 to 6 proof-body lines. Its local identity proof is included. One explicit Mathlib import is charged separately: 11 fewer theorem lines become 10 fewer module lines.

R2 is deliberately reported differently. Its replacement span has \textbf{28 to 29 nonblank code lines}, including the pulse definition, paired proof, projections, and horizontal client. It decreases the span's bytes from 1,912 to 1,553, but that is not evidence of less kernel work. The benefit is one shared case proof and an explicit spatial invariant; old west/east statement shapes are normalized, so this is not a pure proof-body edit.

\subsection{Claims not made}

There is no measured compile-time improvement, proof-term compression, or new axiom audit. Physical source lines are a readability proxy, not proof complexity. No combined percentage is offered: it would mix proof-only edits, an added import, and an API-changing alternative. Larger abstractions without a demonstrated remaining consumer are deferred rather than added to the recommendation count.
