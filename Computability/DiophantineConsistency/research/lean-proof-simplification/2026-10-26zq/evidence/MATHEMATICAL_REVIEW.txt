or commutative-monoid hypothesis is needed.

orderOf(1)=1, not 3. An orbit indexed by three exponents is not injective.

3. Whole-controller residue transport
------------------------------------
The generic pow_power_fixed induction is correct in any monoid. A fixed
point of x->x^p remains fixed under exponents p^n. It does not require
p prime, an invertible element, or a finite field.

The coprimality transfer is also correct without primality or coprimality
assumptions on d and p: congruence modulo d*p preserves gcd with d*p;
coprimality with both d and p implies coprimality with d*p^n. The one-way
form handles n=0. The converse fails:3 is coprime to 2*3^0=2 but is not
coprime to 2*3=6. Therefore no unconditional iff across n=0 is justified.

The Gamma identity genuinely treats rw and n independently. Each exponent
is separately reduced by the fixed-point law. The natural subtraction

that guard is false: in ZMod 5, the cast of natural 0^2-1 is 0, whereas
(0:ZMod 5)^2-1=4. This does not claim the entire Gamma theorem necessarily
fails at B=0; it explains why the specific cast rewrite needs its guard.

4. Arbitrary-seed translation
----------------------------
Translation in an additive group is injective, so finite cardinality is
preserved. The sum formula is card(S)*t+sum(S). Surjectivity of the
cardinality action is sufficient for a prescribed sum; a full automorphism
is stronger than necessary. The ZMod specialization correctly constructs
the inverse from coprimality. It even makes sense at T=0, where coprimality
forces a singleton; the production finite cyclic setting normally has T>0.

A constraint P is preserved only under the displayed translation-stability
hypothesis. Physical safe-position predicates are not automatically stable.
For example, a fixed distinguished origin may be forbidden; translating
a safe singleton can move it to that origin.

Counterexample without surjectivity: in ZMod 4, translating{0,1} produces
only sums 1 and 3 since every sum is 2t+1. This does not contradict the next
theorem: arbitrary two-element subsets, unlike translates of that one seed,
do realize every sum in ZMod 4.

5. First-lift seed
------------------
The first_lift_data proof correctly extracts g>1 from the residue bound,
then p|(g-1) and not p^2|(g-1). The separate bound c<p is unnecessary:
the literal equality g%p^2=1+p*c already enforces it when p>1.

Oddness and primality in order_from_first_lift are essential to the
underlying exact-order result. Controls:
- p=2,g=3,c=1 satisfies the first-lift equation, but g has order 2 modulo 8,
  not 4; this rejects dropping oddness at k=2.
- p=9,g=28,c=3 satisfies the first-lift equation and p is odd, but g has
  order 3 modulo 81, not 9; this rejects replacing prime by merely odd.
- c=0,g=1 gives no nontrivial lift and exact order remains 1.

A proposed all-exponent annihilator law
  p^(k+1)|(g^m-1) iff p^k|m
is a stronger intermediate than the existing special case m=p^k, but is
equivalent to the already-maintained exact-order endpoint. Its possible
novelty is the proof organization LTE->all exponents->order, not a stronger
final mathematical claim. Both m=0 and k=0 must be included explicitly.

6. Arbitrary fixed-cardinality subset sums: primary new proposal
--------------------------------------------------------------
The constructive staircase proof is correct. Write an offset delta as
a*q+r,0<=r<a. For i=0,...,a-1 use
  i+q+1_{i>=a-r}.
These positions are strictly increasing; their sum is
sum_{i<a}i+a*q+r. From delta<=a*(T-a), q<=T-a. If q=T-a, then r=0;
otherwise the largest position is at mostT-1. Thus every integer sum in
the exact interval
  [sum_{i<a}i, sum_{i<a}i+a*(T-a)]
is realized by an a-subset of[0,T).

For1<=a<T the interval width covers T-1 because
  a*(T-a)-(T-1)=(a-1)*(T-a-1)>=0.
Taking delta to be the representative of u-sum_{i<a}i gives every residue.
The Nat theorem correctly allows a=T,delta=0; the modular surjectivity
theorem correctly keeps the stricter a<T. No coprimality, field, prime,
prime-power, or finite-group additive-combinatorics theorem is required.

Controls for the strict cardinality hypotheses:
- At a=0 modulo 2, only sum 0 occurs, so target 1 is impossible.
- At a=T=3, only the full subset occurs and its sum is 0, so target 1 fails.

The affine consequence for weights 1+C0*z modulo C0*T is valid under
0<C0<T. Choose a as the least positive representative of the projection:
1<=a<=C0. A projected zero requires a=C0. Then use the additive fiber of
the projection and the arbitrary-cardinality subset-sum theorem.

This is NOT an unchanged-signature replacement for the existing Boolean
theorem, which promises 0<a<C0. Without a correction, projected-zero
targets cannot have a positive cardinality strictly below C0. The new
bound must permit a=C0. Existing exists_coordinate requires a<C0; it can
be reused on z-a with representative 0 and then translated back.

The C0<T hypothesis has real content. At C0=T=2, the affine weights are
{1,3} modulo 4. Their subset sums are{0,1,3}, so target 2 is impossible.

The actual power-orbit decomposition uses C0=d*auxiliary, not d alone.
Removal of the optional Boolean term is an arithmetic-interface result.
It does not prove enough safe physical positions, preserve a particular
shape class of subsets, or complete the final controller realization.
