{\Large\bfseries\color{ink}Three Further Lean Proof Simplifications}\\[5pt]
{\large For ProveIt's DiophantineConsistency development}\\[10pt]
{\normalsize A sharp rank bound, a two-equation zero test, and an elementary unit proof}\\[8pt]
Prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov\quad\textbar\quad 26 October 2026
\end{center}

\begin{abstract}
This review proposes three residual simplifications after treating all ideas in
43 earlier proposal packets as available. The principal arithmetic result is
the sharp upper bound $R\le q^4-q^3-q-1$ for the reduced native-rank constraints,
proved by an explicit sum of nonnegative terms. It replaces a sequence of
coarser estimates in \code{source_window_bound}. A second result proves the
zero-test selector from annihilation and balance alone, removing the Boolean
idempotence argument from two existing soundness proofs while keeping the
literal certificate unchanged. A smaller third change proves that the actual
controller base is a unit from oddness of the modulus, without an element-order
calculation. The accompanying proof-only patches remove \TotalSaved{} physical
lines from four current modules, with no new production helpers or imports.
The mathematical kernels and source-shaped verification files were checked with
Lean 4.32.0 and the repository's pinned mathlib revision. A complete rebuild of
the production import graph was not performed; that remaining integration check
is explicitly separated from the checks completed here.
\end{abstract}

\section{A sharp upper bound for the native rank}

\subsection{The existing proof obligation}

The current \code{source_window_bound} proves
\[
  \operatorname{nativeRank}(x)+1 < x_{17}
\]
from positive source coordinates, $\beta>0$, $K\ge0$, and the two upper mask
bounds. Its existing proof estimates a quadratic expression $G$, estimates the
two mask contributions separately, combines those estimates, proves a further
comparison of powers, and finally compares the result with the source scale.
Those steps are visible in the pinned
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/60fcb2a69b7f1fdd4c3592ea5f164223cad50abe/Computability/DiophantineConsistency/Lean/DiophantineConsistency/NativePellBootstrap.lean#L514-L563}{source declaration}.

Write the relevant source coordinates as
\[
 J=x_0,\qquad F=x_1,\qquad \alpha=x_2,\qquad
 \beta=\mathrm{ports}_0,\qquad q=1+J\beta,
\]
and let $M_C=\mathrm{ports}_2$, $M_F=\mathrm{ports}_3$,
$R=\operatorname{nativeRank}(x)$, and $E=(x_{17}:\Z)$. The already established
source identities give
\begin{align}
 R&=\bigl((q-1)(q-F)+\alpha\bigr)(q^2-1)+(M_C+qM_F)J,\label{eq:rank}\\
 E&=q^4ws,\qquad w=x_{10}\ge1,\quad s=x_9\ge1.\label{eq:scale}
\end{align}
The existing positive-transport theorem gives $q-F-\alpha>0$. Thus, over
integers, the arithmetic assumptions relevant to the upper bound are
\begin{equation}
 F,\alpha,J\ge1,\quad C:=q-F-\alpha\ge1,\quad
 M_C\le\beta-1,\quad M_F\le2\beta-1.\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
In particular $q=F+\alpha+C\ge3$. No lower bound on either mask port is needed
for this upper estimate. This agrees with the actual theorem: it does not
assume the full \code{PortBox} record.

\subsection{The exact deficit}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp reduced upper bound]
If \eqref{eq:rank}, \eqref{eq:box}, and $q=1+J\beta$ hold in $\Z$, then
\[
 \boxed{R\le q^4-q^3-q-1.}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The following polynomial identity is the entire substantive estimate:
\begin{align}
 q^4-q^3-q-1-R
 ={}&q(q^2-1)(F-1)\notag\\
 &+(q^2-1)(q-F-\alpha-1)\notag\\
 &+(q+1)(J-1)\label{eq:deficit}\\
 &+J\bigl((\beta-M_C-1)+q(2\beta-M_F-1)\bigr).\notag
\end{align}
After substituting $q=1+J\beta$, Lean's \code{ring} tactic checks the identity.
Every summand on the right is nonnegative: $q\ge3$, $q^2-1\ge0$, and each
remaining factor is one of the nonnegative slacks in \eqref{eq:box}. Therefore
the deficit is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

The important change in proof organization is that the mask terms are not
estimated independently and then recombined with a loose bound on $G$.
Equation \eqref{eq:deficit} keeps all the integrality margins until the end.
Each hypothesis has a visible role in a particular nonnegative factor. This
also makes future changes to a mask threshold easier to assess: they change
specific terms of the certificate.

\subsection{Why the bound is sharp, and what that does not imply}

For any integer $q\ge3$, set
\[
 J=1,\quad \beta=q-1,\quad F=1,\quad \alpha=q-2,\quad
 M_C=q-2,\quad M_F=2q-3.
\]
These values satisfy the reduced constraints, and every term in
\eqref{eq:deficit} is zero. Hence equality holds. Both the equality and the
admissibility of this family are proved in the delivered Lean file.

This establishes sharpness for the reduced arithmetic box only. It does not
assert that these values extend to a positive solution of every source row,
the Pell equations, or the final Diophantine equation. The source has additional
constraints that were deliberately not used here. A smaller bound could still
follow from those extra constraints.

The strict integer hypotheses matter. The conversion of, for example,
$M_C<\beta$ into $\beta-M_C-1\ge0$ uses integer discreteness. The proposition is
not stated for arbitrary real variables under the same strict inequalities.
It also retains the positive $F,\alpha,J$ and transport-gap hypotheses; these
are not convenient assumptions silently introduced by the proof.

\subsection{Recovering the existing window theorem}

Equation \eqref{eq:scale} gives $q^4\le E$. The new bound then gives
\[
 R+1\le q^4-q^3-q<q^4\le E,
\]
because $q\ge3$. Thus the existing strict window statement follows with room
to spare. The original theorem's type and downstream uses stay identical.

The supplied production patch inserts the deficit argument directly into
\code{source_window_bound}. It adds no helper declaration and no import. The
separate general theorem is included as a reusable mathematical artifact, not
as an uncharged production dependency. The patch explicitly unfolds
\code{nativeTarget} when checking the identity; it does not rely on accidental
unfolding by \code{ring}.

The current declaration changes from \WindowOld{} to \WindowNew{} physical
lines. More significantly, the separate $G$, mask, recombination, and
$q^2<q^3$ estimates are replaced by one equality and nonnegativity of its
right-hand side. There is no claimed elaboration-time improvement: the new
\code{ring} obligation is larger, and timing would require a controlled full
module comparison.

\subsection{Relation to earlier proposals}

Packet \code{2026-10-26u} already proposes a \emph{lower} native-rank envelope,
including $(2q-1)(q^2-1)$. Packet \code{2026-10-26zb} compares ranks using the
scalar $C+qF$ at fixed surrounding parameters. Both are treated as available.
Neither supplies the upper deficit \eqref{eq:deficit}, its sharp reduced-box
bound, or this replacement of \code{source_window_bound}. The new statement
therefore complements those results rather than reimplementing them.

\section{Prove the zero test without the Boolean equation}

\subsection{The certificate already contains enough information}

The current internal certificate has three fields:
\begin{equation}
 b^2=b,\qquad bn=0,\qquad n+b(s+1)=s+1.\label{eq:zerocert}
\end{equation}
Its soundness proof first applies the internal Boolean-root theorem to the
first field, then handles $b=0$ and $b=1$. The external natural-coordinate
certificate uses the integer rows
\[
 b(b-1)=0,\qquad bn=0,\qquad n-(1-b)(s+1)=0
\]
and follows the same strategy. The relevant pinned declarations are
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/60fcb2a69b7f1fdd4c3592ea5f164223cad50abe/Computability/DiophantineConsistency/Lean/DiophantineConsistency/ObjectZeroTest.lean#L23-L36}{\code{ObjectZeroTest.certificate_sound}}
and
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/60fcb2a69b7f1fdd4c3592ea5f164223cad50abe/Computability/DiophantineConsistency/Lean/DiophantineConsistency/QuadraticBoolean.lean#L10-L27}{\code{QuadraticBoolean.zero_certificate_sound}}.

The first field is not needed to determine the selector.

\begin{proposition}[Annihilation and balance determine the selector]
Let $A$ be a commutative semiring with cancellation by nonzero factors, let
$u\ne0$, and suppose
\[
 bn=0,\qquad n+bu=u.
\]
Then $b=1$ if $n=0$, and $b=0$ if $n\ne0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $n=0$, the balance equation becomes $bu=u=1u$. Cancel the nonzero right
factor $u$ to obtain $b=1$. If $n\ne0$, the annihilation equation and absence
of zero divisors give $b=0$.
\end{proof}

This proof does not first establish that $b$ is Boolean. Booleanity is a
consequence of the selector equation. For the internal certificate take
$u=s+1$ and use the existing theorem that this internal successor is nonzero.
For the external certificate, rearrange the third integer row into the
balance equation and use that a natural slack plus one is positive.

\subsection{The actual proof edits}

The production changes retain the definitions of both certificates and all
three literal polynomial rows. They change only the two soundness proof
bodies. In each proof, the first field is explicitly cleared, and the case
split is on $n=0$. The remaining equations then simplify directly.

Explicit clearing is a useful implementation detail here. An underscore in a
destructuring pattern may leave an automatically named hypothesis available to
automation. Since this proposal is specifically about not using the first
field, the delivered proof uses the clearing pattern and is checked after that
change. The independent general theorem has no idempotence hypothesis at all,
which makes the logical reduction unambiguous.

The external body changes from \ZeroExternalOldBody{} to
\ZeroExternalNewBody{} lines, and the internal body from
\ZeroInternalOldBody{} to \ZeroInternalNewBody{} lines. Together these direct
edits remove \ZeroSaved{} physical lines without adding a shared helper or an
import. A general shared theorem is available in the standalone artifact if
future clients justify it, but its code is not hidden from the production
accounting.

\subsection{Why this works for nonstandard internal naturals}

The argument is algebraic. It does not replace the internal carrier with
$\N$, enumerate its values, or perform external induction on its elements.
The existing internal semiring, linear order, canonical additive order, and
strict ordered-ring structure provide cancellation by a nonzero factor.
The canonical additive order supplies the \code{ExistsAddOfLE} premise needed
by mathlib's ordered-domain instance. The existing
\code{ObjectNaturalDiscrete.add_one_ne_zero} supplies the nonzero successor
fact for arbitrary internal slack values. The delivered abstract-carrier
fixture checks the argument under these algebraic contracts.

There are genuine hypotheses here. If $u=0$ and $n=0$, both equations hold for
every $b$, so the nonzero condition cannot be dropped. Likewise, arbitrary
semirings with zero divisors do not support the conclusion. For example, in
$\N\times\N$, choose $n=(1,0)$, $b=(0,1)$, and $u=(1,1)$. Annihilation and
balance hold, but $b$ is neither the zero nor the unit of the product semiring.
The report therefore does not advertise the result as a theorem of all
commutative semirings.

\subsection{Relation to earlier proposals and to equation size}

The nearest earlier work includes packet \code{2026-10-26m}'s use of the
zero-test correctness theorem in conditional clients, packet
\code{2026-10-26x}'s simplification of idempotent cancellation, and packet
\code{2026-10-26zg}'s split-zero interpretation of signed expressions. These
are useful but different: they still consume Booleanity, simplify how it is
proved, or normalize the interpretation of rows. The present argument shows
that the other two constraints already force the selector.

Removing the literal Boolean row could be a separate encoding experiment.
It is not part of this patch: doing so would alter the generated system and
require new row, degree, operation-count, and representation checks. The
delivered proposal is only a proof simplification and a precise redundancy
theorem. All public statements, witness counts, and executable expressions
remain unchanged in the proposed files.

\section{Use oddness to prove the controller base is a unit}

\subsection{The existing endpoint needs less than an order theorem}

The current \code{block_base_isUnit} obtains positive order of
$B^{a-1}$ from \code{block_order}, infers that this power is a unit, and then
deduces that $B$ is a unit. For this particular conclusion, the established
numeric shape of the base and modulus is enough:
\[
 B=2^{94517},\qquad d=47\cdot2011,\qquad a=120199,\qquad
 m=(da)a^k.
\]
The existing controller-data module already provides \code{B_eq},
\code{d_odd}, and \code{auxiliary_odd}.

Since $m$ is odd, $\gcd(2,m)=1$. Hence the residue class of $2$ is a unit in
$\Z/m\Z$, and so is its $94517$th power. The proof works also at $k=0$;
there is no positive-exponent side case for $k$.

In Lean, the useful chain is
\begin{lstlisting}
Nat.coprime_two_left
ZMod.isUnit_iff_coprime
IsUnit.pow
\end{lstlisting}
with \code{Nat.cast_pow} moving the exponentiation into \code{ZMod}. This
keeps $2^{94517}$ symbolic. An earlier test form applied coprimality to the
large natural power and triggered an elaborator warning about reducing the
large exponent; the supplied final proof avoids that form.

The declaration changes from \UnitOld{} to \UnitNew{} physical lines. The
claim is strictly local: the proof term no longer uses \code{block_order}.
The surrounding module still needs its power-coordinate imports for other
results. There is no claim that the whole module's import graph, total build
time, or all controller order calculations disappear.

\subsection{Novelty boundary}

Previous packets contain general coprime-thickening, fixed-reference Gamma
certificates, exact-order alternatives, and weaker controller annihilation
facts. Those mechanisms are excluded. This proposal is only the remaining
endpoint replacement for the actual base $B$. In particular,
\code{2026-10-26zq/evidence/late_head_scope_addendum.md} explicitly leaves the
base-unit proof in use after its own subset construction. The present small
change can therefore coexist with that proposal.

\section{Further research questions}

These are follow-up questions suggested by the three results, not additional
claims of novelty.

\paragraph{Can the exact source improve the sharp reduced bound?}
The reduced-box extremizer need not satisfy the Pell and transport equations.
Does an additional source row exclude it, and if so, can that exclusion be
expressed as another positive term in \eqref{eq:deficit}? A useful result would
be a new source-specific margin with a short certificate, not a larger
collection of numerical estimates.

\paragraph{Should the upper envelope become public?}
The current patch keeps the argument local to avoid helper overhead. If a
second independent client needs a bound on the native rank, the separately
checked \code{upper_envelope} is a candidate public interface. Its precise
strength and mask-sign assumptions are already documented. The decision
should be based on real consumers and total code cost.

\paragraph{Where else is an algebraic certificate premise redundant?}
The zero-test example suggests inspecting certificates for constraints implied
by their remaining equations and nonzero guards. This is a research direction,
not a claim that arbitrary rows can be removed. Each candidate needs a proof
over the actual carrier, including nonstandard internal values, and its
literal encoding should remain fixed during proof-only refactoring.
