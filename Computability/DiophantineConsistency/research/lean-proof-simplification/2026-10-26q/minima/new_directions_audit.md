## 1. Share the actual negative-norm obstruction across four polynomial presentations

### Mathematical mechanism

The one arithmetic fact is

\[
X^2-(A^2-1)Y^2\ne -1\qquad(A,X,Y\in\mathbb Z).
\]

Modulo four, a square is zero or one. If A is odd, A²−1 is zero modulo four and the displayed norm has residue zero or one. If A is even, A²−1 is −1 modulo four and the norm is the sum of two squares, with residue zero, one, or two. Neither case permits −1 modulo four. This proof requires no positivity, nonzero coordinate, Pell-index, or consistency assumption.

Four existing declarations currently recheck this same fact:

| Target | Substitution into the norm |
| --- | --- |
| `ShortEquation.strong_ne_neg_one` (19–26) | A=a+2, X=f, Y=t |
| `ShortSystem.square_aux_ne_neg_one` (27–33) | A=t, X=t*h, Y=y |
| `MinimumNorm.main_norm_ne_neg_one` (56–62) | A=a+2, X=x, Y=y |
| `MinimumNorm.square_auxiliary_ne_neg_one` (125–132) | A=I, X=I*V, Y=V+e |

The identities are polynomial identities. In particular,

\[
t^2(h^2-y^2)+y^2=(th)^2-(t^2-1)y^2,
\]

and `a²+4a+3=(a+2)²−1`.

### Smallest implementation

No new general theorem is required. `ShortEquation.strong_ne_neg_one` already quantifies an arbitrary integer a, so its shifted parameter covers every A by setting a=A−2. Preserve that theorem and its existing finite certificate. Replace the other three proofs by specialization and `ring`:

```lean
convert ShortEquation.strong_ne_neg_one (t - 2) (t * h) y using 1 <;> ring
```

The `MinimumNorm` auxiliary case additionally unfolds its existing `auxiliary` definition. `MinimumNorm` gains one low-level import of `ShortEquation`. This introduces no dependency cycle: `ShortEquation` imports only mathlib modules. All existing public statements and all polynomial definitions are preserved.

A cleaner unshifted public alias can be added later if it improves many callers, but it is unnecessary for the supplied patch and should not trigger a fourth modular verification.

### Why it survives the exclusion baseline

The previous reports already know finite checking and already contain the bespoke ZMod-four proofs as unchanged source. The new proposal identifies their common discriminant normal form and removes three separate finite proof obligations by instantiating an existing theorem. The baseline auditor searched all proposal/report/note/prototype text, including all five incoming ZIPs, and found no recommendation to unify these four declarations. It is distinct from the excluded product-of-units decomposition, signed Pell congruence API, and Pell parity reuse.

## 2. Turn equality at several heterogeneous minima into one saturation argument

### The remaining proof burden

`MinimumNorm.minima76_zero_iff` (220–238) receives three arithmetic certificates: n0² and n1² have minimum one with specified equality cases; the coupled block na²+W² has minimum one with equality exactly at na=1 and W=0. Two other squares have minimum zero. The old proof invokes `nlinarith` five times to recover each equality separately from the final sum.

This is a finite-order equality argument. Let

\[
E=(n_0^2,n_1^2,n_a^2+W^2,(n_k-1)^2,r_t^2),
\qquad m=(1,1,1,0,0).
\]

The hypotheses prove mᵢ≤Eᵢ. The finalizer equation is ΣEᵢ=Σmᵢ=3. Therefore every inequality is equality. The existing equality-case lemmas then recover the six desired semantic conditions.

### Library basis and checked implementation

The pinned mathlib theorem is `Finset.sum_eq_sum_iff_of_le`. Its source is generated by `@[to_additive]` from `prod_eq_prod_iff_of_le` in `Mathlib/Algebra/Order/BigOperators/Group/Finset.lean`, around lines 516–528, at mathlib revision `81a5d257c8e410db227a6665ed08f64fea08e997`.

The small helper in the patch reverses equality orientation and removes the univ-membership binder:

```lean
private theorem sum_at_lower_bounds {ι : Type*} [Fintype ι]
    (value lower : ι → ℤ) (h : ∀ i, lower i ≤ value i) :
    (∑ i, value i) = (∑ i, lower i) ↔ ∀ i, value i = lower i := by
  simpa only [Finset.mem_univ, forall_true_left, eq_comm] using
    (Finset.sum_eq_sum_iff_of_le (s := Finset.univ) (fun i _ => h i))
```

The replacement forms the five-element vector above, supplies its lower bounds, rewrites the original finalizer equation with `sub_eq_zero`, and simplifies the five equality cases. No polynomial definition or theorem assumption changes.

For future finalizers the optional semantic form in `MinimaSaturation.lean` accepts predicates Pᵢ and equivalences Eᵢ=mᵢ iff Pᵢ. Then the whole correctness statement becomes a conjunction over Pᵢ. This accommodates blocks with distinct minima and blocks whose internal structure is not a square.

### Why this is more than the old sum-of-squares proposal

The excluded residual-sum idea proves that a sum of nonnegative squares is zero exactly when every residual vanishes. Here the relevant endpoint is equality at a nonzero minimum and the coupled block must remain intact. It is not valid to treat na² and W² as two independent minimum-one terms, nor does the old zero-squares certificate provide the supplied norm equality cases. The useful new abstraction is equality saturation over arbitrary separately certified blocks, with their heterogeneous lower bounds and semantic attainment predicates.

The baseline audit found no `MinimumNorm`, minimum-saturation, heterogeneous-minimum, or `Finset.sum_eq_sum_iff_of_le` proposal in the earlier corpus.

### Honest payoff

The local proof body shrinks from 13 nonblank/trailing-blank-trimmed physical lines to 9, and its five `nlinarith` invocations become zero. At this single current caller, charging the helper and explicit imports yields **seven extra source lines**. This should therefore be recommended for conceptual clarity and reuse across additional finalizers, not advertised as a net source-size win on its own. No build-speed improvement was measured.

Combining both supplied proposals changes `MinimumNorm.lean` from 308 to 307 physical lines and `ShortSystem.lean` from 558 to 554. The combined total is 866 to 861. These small numbers include all new imports and the shared saturation helper.
