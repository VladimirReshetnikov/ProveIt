1. INTERNAL ZERO-SUM AND ZERO-PRODUCT PROOFS

Files reviewed:

The proposed addRel_zero_iff retains arbitrary x,y in the model's V. It does not add hypotheses that x or y is an external numeral. Their required membership in internal omega is extracted from AddRel itself, exactly as in the original proof. The zero case follows by addition functionality and the zero law. In the successor case, totality supplies the predecessor sum, the successor graph law identifies the alleged zero result with a successor, and vsucc_self plus the empty-set membership specification gives a contradiction. None of those steps requires a predecessor induction hypothesis.

For mulRel_zero_iff, the original explicit hx and hy hypotheses remain. The y=0 branch is immediate. In the y=succ n branch, multiplication totality gives b=x*n and the successor law gives x*y=b+x. If this is zero, the already established zero-sum characterization gives x=0. This is not a hidden appeal to no-zero-divisors: the addition result is proved independently, and multiplication totality/uniqueness predate the no-zero-divisors instance.

nat_zero_or_succ quantifies over every internal natural, including nonstandard ones. Its source proof uses an actual definable formula and omega_ind, with no dependence on the arithmetic lemmas being replaced. Thus the new imports do not create mathematical circularity. The correct explanation is that two specialized redundant induction scripts, two private formulas and two satisfaction lemmas are removed. It would be inaccurate to say that induction disappears from the foundational dependency chain: the shared case eliminator is itself already proved by internal induction.

The private zeroSumStage is not the public cancellation formula. additionCancellationForm still uses cancellationStage, which remains unchanged. Removing the two unused private stage formulas therefore does not silently change the exported object-language arithmetic statement.

The standalone multiplication prototype explicitly calls NoVacuousInduction.addRel_zero_iff. Although its imports transitively include the old upstream addition module, it does not use the old zero-sum theorem to prove its replacement. The full replacement multiplication file appropriately calls the replacement namespace's ObjectAdditionCancellation.addRel_zero_iff. A combined integration build should rebuild the addition module before that dependent file.

Minor patch hygiene identified: the original explanatory docstring immediately before addRel_zero_iff was removed together with zeroSumStage. The structural author was asked to restore it. This does not affect proof correctness.

3. DIVISIBILITY COMPOSITION IN SIGN BRANCHES

File: arithmetic_work/DiophantineConsistency/NativePellStepDown.lean, local hJp_of_sign.

The delivered code uses ordinary divisibility operations rather than a newly defined signed-congruence relation. This distinction matters for accurately describing the result.

After applying Int.modEq_iff_dvd to J congruent to t*s, the proof has divisibility of t*s-J. For t=1, subtracting this from s-p or s+p yields J-p or J+p. For t=-1, adding it to s+p or s-p yields p-J or -p-J, and negating yields J-p or J+p. The use of hsp'.symm in the negative branch is necessary and correct: it reverses the alternatives before mapping them back into the original conclusion order.

This preserves both sign alternatives without assuming c>0 or c nonzero beyond whatever the outer theorem already states. It removes four explicit quotient witnesses and their manual linear-combination calculations. The supporting natAbs-square simplification also preserves signed input semantics: (V.natAbs : Int)^2=V^2 uses the square of the absolute value, and does not assert equality of V and its absolute value.

Novelty: no such branch-level divisibility composition is proposed in the audited packets. Do not count the incidental sq_abs cleanup as a separate major new idea.

4. INTEGER-ONLY NONSQUARE NORM NONVANISHING

File: arithmetic_work/DiophantineConsistency/MinimumNorm.lean, norm_ne_zero_of_not_isSquare.

Assuming zero norm gives x^2=y^2*d. Thus y^2 divides x^2, and Int.pow_dvd_pow_iff at nonzero exponent2 gives y divides x. Write x=y*k. Since y is nonzero, y^2 is nonzero; cancellation yields d=k*k, contradicting nonsquareness.

All sign cases are preserved. In particular, y need not be positive, and d need not have any positivity premise. The existing y!=0 premise is used exactly for cancellation. If y=0 were dropped, x=0 would be a genuine counterexample; the candidate does not drop it. The proof does not replace integer squareness with rational squareness and thereby change the theorem's domain.

The narrower imports remove direct Rat and FieldSimp dependencies. This is conceptually sound, but consumers which relied on those modules only through a transitive import may need an explicit import. Only a relevant downstream build can establish that integration consequence. No performance improvement follows from the import edit alone.

Novelty: no MinimumNorm, norm_ne_zero, equal-power divisibility or rational-coercion elimination proposal appears in the audited prior packets.

5. MONOTONICITY IN THE PELL BASE PARAMETER

File: arithmetic_work/PellBaseMonotonicity.lean.

coordinates_mono_base proves the two coordinates together using the first-order positive recurrences. For A<=B, the factors A, A^2-1 and both preceding coordinates are weakly ordered in the required direction. Nat.sub_le_sub_right correctly handles the subtraction by1; no unjustified cancellation of truncated subtraction occurs. Both sequences are defined under their existing A>1 and B>1 hypotheses.

For index_lt_of_coordinate_lt, the key chain is
  yn_B(n) < yn_A(p) <= yn_B(p).
Strict monotonicity in the index for the fixed base B then gives n<p. The proof does not assume monotonicity of the very index comparison being proved; it uses the newly proved base monotonicity and the existing fixed-base strictMono_y separately.

The stronger helper main_index_gt_first_of_base_le needs A<=P, c=yn_A(p), k=2*yn_P(n), and k<c. The doubling equation supplies yn_P(n)<=k, so the required strict coordinate comparison follows. Separate positive-index assumptions and c>1 are unnecessary for this helper. The compatibility theorem intentionally keeps the old public hc, hp, hn and strict base-separation hypothesis. Its hAP implies A<=P; the unused-variable warnings on the compatibility assumptions are expected, not evidence of an omitted premise in the old contract.

The strengthened helper works uniformly at boundary indices too: impossible cases are ruled out by the strict coordinate inequality rather than being assumed away. The companion header check confirms that the compatibility wrapper matches the original theorem's assumptions and conclusion, modulo its temporary name.

Novelty: no base-parameter monotonicity proposal was found in six audited packets. This should be presented as an additional source-specific proof simplification, not as a claim that Pell monotonicity itself is new mathematics.

6. FINITE-PERMUTATION INVERSE INVARIANCE

Files:
- root_work/FiniteInvariant.lean
- root_work/replacements/NativeFieldCycles2011.lean
- root_work/finite_invariant.patch
- original NativeFieldCycles2011Checks.lean.

The initial exception_eq_511 argument is unchanged. Under an assumed value510, three predecessors force the exceptional branch's fixed source value to be511. With that fact, an even state cannot use the exceptional branch, and the existing successor_mod_four shows that its successor is divisible by4, hence even.

The proof then uses the finite-permutation theorem: a forward-invariant predicate on a finite type under a bijection is invariant under the inverse. Applying it to the even-state predicate supplies an even predecessor of the assumed value510. successor_mod_four at that predecessor makes510 divisible by4, the contradiction.

Finiteness and bijectivity are indispensable for the inverse-invariance step, and both were already present in the original public theorem. The proof does not accidentally generalize to an arbitrary injective map on an infinite type. Its new imported theorem supplies the same finite recurrence reasoning in a standard reusable form instead of constructing a positive period and subtracting1 from its witness.

The actual511 self-loop is preserved. Take A=Fin1, sigma=id, S constantly511, epsilon constantly1 and sstar=511. Then (2*511+1)%512=511, all original hypotheses hold, and the conclusion merely excludes510. The original regression module explicitly records this self-loop and the all-zero loop. Both remain legitimate examples under the unchanged theorem header. offset20_excludes_510 is unchanged, retaining its ability to apply to arbitrary maps on infinite types.

Novelty: earlier reports praise or warn about the existing offset19 proof but do not propose finite inverse-invariance. Their warnings about preserving511 are fully respected here.
