1.1

What makes the proposals useful

The Pell changes consume facts in the form already available: an additive natural square identity on
entry to classification, and an integer norm invariant for integer-valued goals. The residue change
gives the decoder a named arithmetic contract with a concrete witness. These eliminate identifiable
proof obligations, rather than just making tactic scripts more opaque.
The report supplies exact replacements, mathematical justifications, the closest earlier proposals, counterexamples to an overgeneralized statement, measured source costs, and explicit verification limits.
Read Section 3 for the two low-cost edits, Section 4 for the canonical decoder, and Section 6 before
applying any patch. The repository was inspected and candidate copies were checked; no upstream
files or branches were changed.

2.1

What was counted as already available

The exclusion rule is deliberately stronger than “not yet merged.” A previous optional interface, incomplete prototype, rejected-but-already-described direction, or a mechanism proposed for a nearby
client is prior work for this task. Renaming a helper, adding a parameter, moving a tactic proof into a
wrapper, or applying the same abstraction to another file does not establish a fresh idea.
Three parallel audits reviewed the primary manuscripts and substantive notes for packets a–m, n–z,
and za–zq plus incoming. The archive retains those ledgers. Searches were used to locate close comparisons, followed by review of the relevant proposal and source context; an absent identifier alone was
not treated as proof of novelty. The historical incoming README also discusses unrelated equation
and K215 research. The audit covers its relevant Lean simplification content and the tracked incoming
archive, not every manuscript transitively linked from that historical queue.
Several initially attractive directions were discarded on this basis: generic finite-sum rigidity; additional carry uniqueness or reset locality; scanner fusion; source-register decoding abstractions; alignedword equivalences; shared Pell growth/error estimates; finite-controller synthesis; native coefficient
envelopes; stronger arbitrary-subset coverage; and standard import/profiling advice. They would repeat earlier work.

Two small Pell proof reductions

These are the recommended first changes. Both preserve theorem statements and use already imported
infrastructure. Neither adds a helper, an import, a stronger hypothesis, or new executable data.

3.1

Feed the square identity directly to Pell classification

The target is SignedPellResidue.signed_residue_cases, especially the block beginning at baseline line
62. The preceding argument already proves
(A |V |)2 = (A2 − 1)|y|2 + 1
as an equality of natural numbers. The current proof then creates another equality with explicit multiplication, calls nlinarith, uses omega to turn addition into truncated subtraction, and finally passes
that fact to Pell.eq_pell.
The additive equality is exactly the premise of Nat.sub_eq_of_eq_add'. It already contains the information that makes the natural subtraction exact. Normalize squares and multiplication association at the
point of use:
obtain ⟨n, hx, hy⟩ := Pell.eq_pell hA
(x := A * V.natAbs) (y := y.natAbs) (by
change (A * V.natAbs) * (A * V.natAbs) (A * A - 1) * y.natAbs * y.natAbs = 1
simpa only [pow_two, mul_assoc] using
Nat.sub_eq_of_eq_add' hn)

The apostrophe is significant: it selects the orientation of the addition equation used here. The explicit
change exposes the discriminant hidden behind the imported Pell.d definition; restricted simplification alone does not unfold that interface. Both details are present in the successfully checked patch.
The change removes the local hp bridge and three physical source lines: the complete module goes from
102 to 99 lines. It leaves the absolute-value conversion that produces hn, the index-parity argument, and
all subsequent sign/congruence cases intact. Those later stages are adjacent to earlier proposals and
receive no new credit here.

3.2

Use the integer Pell norm theorem where the goal is integer-valued

The target is ThirteenFreshSeed.auxiliary_exists. Two local goals assert an integer norm identity for
Pell sequence values. The baseline starts from χ_sq, an identity over natural numbers, casts it with
congrArg, proves or supplies a guard for natural subtraction, and normalizes the resulting integer expression.
Pinned Mathlib already provides the integer invariant Pell.pell_eqz. Its discriminant conversion is
Pell.dz_val. These are available through the module’s existing imports. For the first norm identity,
the entire proof becomes:
have hp : f ^ 2 - delta * (yn hA t : ℤ) ^ 2 = 1 := by
simpa only [f, delta, xz, yz, dz_val, az,
pow_two, mul_assoc] using pell_eqz hA t

For the second, use the same integer invariant, substitute the existing coordinate equation hx, and retain
the short polynomial normalization needed for the chosen sign of y:
have hnorm : (S : ℤ) ^ 2 * V ^ 2 ((S : ℤ) ^ 2 - 1) * y ^ 2 = 1 := by
have h := pell_eqz hS R
simp only [xz, yz, dz_val, az, hx] at h
dsimp [y]
nlinarith only [h]

This removes four lines at the first site and three at the second. The complete module goes from 217
to 210 physical lines. The local hSS subtraction guard disappears because the direct integer theorem
already packages the correct discriminant. Other guards and casts used elsewhere in the construction
remain necessary and are not removed.
The useful principle here is specific: choose the invariant in the scalar domain of the target equation. It
is not a new general casting framework. Earlier integer-norm proposals address a different obligation,
such as avoiding rational square arguments or minimizing a fundamental unit. This change substitutes
an existing theorem for two constructive sequence-norm bridges. The earlier fresh-seed replacement
itself retains those bridges, which provides a particularly close counterfactual comparison.

3.3

Expected effect and limits

Taken together, these two patches remove ten physical lines with no helper overhead. They reduce
intermediate facts and two uses of natural-subtraction cast machinery. No compile-time, memory, or
object-size improvement has been established. The validation section distinguishes complete modifiedmodule checks from a project rebuild, and records the dependencies used. Their small scope makes
these the best initial adoption candidates even though their absolute line savings are modest.

Decode bounded witnesses by canonical residues

The target is the forward direction of BoundedForallCertificate.boundedForallCertificate, baseline
lines 32–63. Its arithmetic argument is also present in the lower-level MRDP.boundedForall_dioph, in
the sibling HilbertTenthProblem source tree. That second site matters for the placement and cost of a
shared helper; it is not an additional independent proposal.

4.1

The unnecessary choice

The certificate contains, for every witness coordinate i, a divisibility condition
P |

ui
∏

(ai − t),

t=0

where subtraction and the product are over natural numbers. After choosing a prime p dividing the
row modulus, the proof knows that p divides this descending product. It currently selects an arbitrary
factor divisible by p, obtains an index t, and uses min(ai , t) to repair the effect of truncated subtraction.
A choose then assembles these choices into a witness function.
The downstream proof needs just two properties of this function: its coordinates are bounded by u,
and each coordinate is congruent to the corresponding ai modulo p. There is a distinguished value
with the congruence property: the least nonnegative residue ai mod p. The product condition says
exactly that this distinguished value lies inside the required interval.

4.2

The precise arithmetic contract

For a prime p and arbitrary natural numbers a, u, prove
p|

u
∏

(a − t)

⇐⇒

a mod p ≤ u.

(1)

t=0

There is no assumption u < p, no positivity condition on a, and no conversion of natural subtraction
to integer subtraction in the theorem statement. The supplied kernel-checked theorem is:
theorem prime_dvd_descendingProduct_iff {p a u : ℕ}
(hp : p.Prime) :
p ∣ ∏ t ∈ Finset.range (u + 1), (a - t) ↔
a % p ≤ u

For the forward direction, primality gives some t ≤ u with p | a − t. Set b = min(a, t). Natural
subtraction gives a − t = a − b, and b ≤ a permits the congruence step. Therefore
a mod p = b mod p ≤ b ≤ t ≤ u.
The minimum is used only inside this small arithmetic lemma. It disappears from the certificate decoder and its chosen witness.
For the reverse direction, let r = a mod p ≤ u. The factor at index r occurs in the product, and the
standard remainder identity gives p | a − r. Divisibility of the product follows immediately. This also
explains why the canonical residue is the right witness: it identifies a specific factor when the interval
test holds.

The client becomes deterministic after choosing the row prime

The replacement block is:
let j := fun i => a i % p
have hWitness_bound i : j i ≤ u i :=
(prime_dvd_descendingProduct_iff hp).mp
(hPrime_product.trans (hWitness_product i))
have hWitness_congruence i : a i ≡ j i [MOD p] :=
(mod_modEq _ _).symm

The rest of the argument keeps its structure. Monotonicity of the nonnegative polynomial evaluations
supplies the row bound. Coprimality with the factorial scale puts that bound below p. Cancelling
the scale recovers the row-index congruence, polynomial congruence transports evaluations, and the
below-p bounds turn congruence into equality. The supplied focused decoder check retains these
downstream steps so that the replacement is tested beyond the standalone number-theoretic fact.
The phrase “deterministic decoder” has a limited meaning here: once the row prime p has been selected, j is a concrete residue function. The proof still chooses a prime divisor of the row modulus.
It still uses the existing CRT construction in the reverse direction. This is not a claim that the whole
certificate development becomes constructive, choice-free, or computationally cheaper.
The polynomial certificate, quantifier arity, number of witnesses, products, and stored syntax stay
unchanged. In particular, the proposal does not replace the falling-factorial condition with a modulo expression in the Diophantine formula. The modulo operation appears only in the mathematical
decoding proof. This distinction preserves the representation and depth/cost obligations that other
parts of the development depend on.

4.4

Boundary cases and why primality is essential

For u = 0, the product has one factor a, and the equivalence reduces to p | a if and only if a mod p = 0.
If a ≤ u, the product contains a zero factor, and the residue bound holds because a mod p ≤ a. If
u ≥ p − 1, every least residue lies in the interval, exactly as the statement predicts. No large-prime side
condition is being smuggled into the helper.
Primality cannot be replaced by mere nonzeroness. For p = 6, a = 3, and u = 1, the product is 3 · 2 = 6,
so p divides it, but a mod p = 3 > 1. This small counterexample catches the tempting but invalid
composite-modulus version. The prime-selection step in the original decoder is therefore retained for
a mathematical reason.
The finite coordinate type can be empty: a residue function on Fin 0 is well-defined and its coordinate
obligations are vacuous. If the outer row range is empty, the original bounded-universal statement
already handles that case. The change adds no special-case branch.

4.5

Placement and cost

A shared helper belongs at the lower arithmetic layer already imported by the certificate theorem, or
in a small dependency imported by that layer. Placing it only above BoundedForallCertificate would
prevent the lower MRDP.boundedForall_dioph copy from using it without reversing the dependency direction. The candidate therefore includes the lower-layer edit and the requested target edit together.

7.1

What to measure after integration

The reported line counts are physical lines in the exact baseline and candidate files, with helpers
charged in full. They are useful for checking the narrow compression claim. They do not measure
proof-term size, elaboration work, conceptual simplicity, or the downstream cost of an import. No
runtime improvement is promised.
For these small edits, normal module compilation and the repository’s existing checks are appropriate.
A separate benchmark campaign is unnecessary unless elaboration actually regresses. For the canonical helper, review the semantic interface and the two real clients before adding more generic variants.
The supplied composite-modulus counterexample is a more useful guard against an incorrect generalization than a collection of tests mirroring the proof text.
The arithmetic source statements, literal large constants, generated programs, certificate dimensions,
and theorem hypotheses are not changed by the proposed patches. Preserving them is a concrete part
of the design, because changes to executable data or representation cost would invalidate unrelated
downstream equalities even if the high-level mathematics stayed true.
