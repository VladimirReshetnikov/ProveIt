\section{Results and recommendations}
\label{sec:results}
\textbf{Three recommendations survive the final novelty screen.} Two are small, immediately useful Pell proof reductions. Together they remove ten physical lines, without introducing helpers or imports. The third replaces arbitrary bounded-witness selection with a canonical residue function; it simplifies the proof contract but adds eleven lines across its shared helper and two clients.

The largest initially promising candidate was withdrawn after a closing review found a newly added incoming proposal that already supplies its architectural payoff. It is retained only as screened-out evidence. This is intentional: the requested assumption that all earlier proposals are already applied takes priority over producing a longer list.

All three retain the existing public theorem statements and certificate data. No source-size saving is claimed by adding the negative and positive counts across unrelated optional changes. The eleven-line cost of the canonical interface remains a cost even though the two Pell changes save ten lines elsewhere.

\subsection{What makes the proposals useful}
The Pell changes consume facts in the form already available: an additive natural square identity on entry to classification, and an integer norm invariant for integer-valued goals. The residue change gives the decoder a named arithmetic contract with a concrete witness. These eliminate identifiable proof obligations, rather than just making tactic scripts more opaque.

The report supplies exact replacements, mathematical justifications, the closest earlier proposals, counterexamples to an overgeneralized statement, measured source costs, and explicit verification limits. Read Section~\ref{sec:pell} for the two low-cost edits, Section~\ref{sec:residue} for the canonical decoder, and Section~\ref{sec:validation} before applying any patch. The repository was inspected and candidate copies were checked; no upstream files or branches were changed.
