\textbf{Use the fixed coefficient already present in the statement.} In
\src{CurrentControllerPositions2011}{13}{17}, write
\[
 p=120199,\qquad A=p^k,\qquad P_k(j)=2+(p-1)j.\operatorname{val},
 \qquad j:\operatorname{ZMod}(p^k).
\]
The ordinary cell modulus is $p^{k+1}=pA$. These are the actual controller positions, not an arbitrary affine family. Unfolding only \code{position}, \code{auxiliary} and one \code{pow_succ} turns the local obligations into Presburger arithmetic with coefficient $120198$. The term $p^k$ can remain an opaque natural atom. Only the small actual auxiliary $120199$ is exposed, not the huge block base or literal kernel $K$. No symbolic power is evaluated as an enormous numeral, and no generic theorem is added.

\textbf{Sharp budget.} The canonical representative satisfies $j.\operatorname{val}+1\le A$. Therefore
\[
 P_k(j)+A+p
 = 2+(p-1)j.\operatorname{val}+A+p
 \le 2+(p-1)(A-1)+A+p=pA+3.
\]
The candidate replaces a multiplied inequality, a separate power equality and nonlinear arithmetic by the following entire body:
\begin{lstlisting}
  have hv : j.val+1 ≤ auxiliary^k := ZMod.val_lt j
  simp only [position, pow_succ, auxiliary] at hv ⊢
  omega
\end{lstlisting}
This preserves the subtraction-free public budget, hence its existing natural-subtraction endpoint and safe-next-cell clients. The source is \src{CurrentControllerPositions2011}{24}{43}.

\textbf{Injectivity needs only cancellation by a fixed positive integer.} If $P_k(i)=P_k(j)$, then
\[
 2+120198i.\operatorname{val}=2+120198j.\operatorname{val}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad i.\operatorname{val}=j.\operatorname{val}.
\]
The candidate applies \code{ZMod.val_injective}, unfolds the two fixed definitions in the equality, and lets \code{omega} cancel the coefficient. This replaces a separate positivity fact and \code{nlinarith}; no primality argument or new finite-order principle is needed. See \src{CurrentControllerPositions2011}{79}{84}.
