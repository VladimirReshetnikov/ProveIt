A pair (p,n) represents the formal difference p-n. Its equivalence relation
is p+n'=n+p'; multiplication is (ac+bd,ad+bc). Neither component is truncated.
Its magnitude is the natural absolute difference (p-n)+(n-p), where each
subtraction is the existing internal natural subtraction.

The first substantive law is multiplicativity of magnitude. For p>=n and
q>=m, put u=p-n and v=q-m. Justified additive identities p=n+u and q=m+v
give pq+nm = pm+nq+uv. This proves the magnitude law for that orientation;
swapping either pair gives the remaining three orientations. The proof
uses only natural addition, multiplication, order and justified subtraction.

Cross-sum equivalence is preserved by multiplication. A pair equivalent to
one has magnitude one. In a natural arithmetic model, a product equal to
one has both factors one: positivity gives the lower bound one, and
multiplication bounds each factor above by the product. Iterating this
argument over five actual factors gives magnitude one for each.
