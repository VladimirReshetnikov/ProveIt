The literal adaptive strong factor yields `I²+Δ=Δf²` in every selected
`IΣ₁` model, where `I=c²i` and `Δ=(a+1)(a+3)`. The theorem derives this
from the actual positive unit of the strong factor and its explicit signed
pair representation. Its current natural client needs only a zero of the
literal current expression; it assumes neither a factor unit nor a norm.

This is the normalized strong hypothesis consumed by the generic internal
half-index theorem. It is not the fourteen-variable literal client's stronger
syntactic form, and it does not by itself identify the main Pell index. The
auxiliary factor, its signed magnitude and the sign congruences remain to be
bound to that theorem.
