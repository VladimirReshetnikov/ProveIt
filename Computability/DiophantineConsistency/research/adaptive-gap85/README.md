# Conditional 85-operation gap-coordinate candidate

This is a candidate, not yet a proved Con(ZFC) equation. The pinned 86-operation parent's positive coordinate `alpha` occurs only in `C = q - F - alpha` and in the rank seed `G = alpha + (q-F)*(q-1)`. Replace `alpha` with a new positive coordinate `C` and rewrite the rank seed as `G = (q-F)*q - C`. This removes one graph operation, producing a live 85-operation circuit with the same 13 positive coordinates and four fixed ports. The 85-row `candidate.json` and `AUDIT.json` are generated by `audit.py`.

`audit.py` checks the exact sparse integer-polynomial identities under the signed substitution `alpha = q-F-C` and verifies the other graph rows remain structurally identical. It checks all 85 operations, all 13 witnesses and all four ports are live. The graph count is 47 multiplications, 20 additions and 18 subtractions. Reproduce from the repository root with `python3 Computability/DiophantineConsistency/research/adaptive-gap85/audit.py`. These checks are not a degree or minimality certificate. The independently replayed [conditional literal binding](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-literal-independent.md) identifies the exact 86 and generated 85 zero tests under explicit signed-gap and signed-rank equivalence assumptions. The independently replayed [coordinate arithmetic](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-arithmetic-independent.md) proves the two conditional positive-coordinate transformations and rank identity in arbitrary IΣ₁ models. The independently replayed [signed-hole bridge](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-holes-independent.md) derives the literal equivalence from `F+alpha+C=q`, and the [witness lift](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-lift-independent.md) constructs a positive 85 witness from an actual positive 86 zero and a positive 86 witness from an 85 zero under the explicit reverse bound. The independently replayed [machine client](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-machine-independent.md) further proves that the fixed-port 85 no-zero statement implies represented-machine nonhalting in every IΣ₁ model; the converse remains conditional on the reverse bound. These results retain historical provider boundaries.

Positive witnesses are the unresolved issue. An actual positive 86 zero has `F+alpha<q` by `AdaptiveDirectOrdinate86Transport.expressionAt_transport_positive86`, so its new gap `C=q-(F+alpha)` is positive. Conversely, an 85 zero would recover positive `alpha=q-(F+C)` only if `F+C<q`; that inequality has **not** been established from the new equation. The independently replayed [one-sided HA endpoint](../../reviews/ha85-implication-snapshot-independent.md) proves existence of an HA derivation from the exact fixed-port 85 shifted no-zero sentence to Foundation’s ordinary ZFC consistency sentence. Its coherent 86 HA parent and HA/ZF/Foundation/Mathlib/package transitive source ancestry remain historical, not a complete current-source closure. The reverse direction has been checked only under that bound. Until the bound itself is proved for actual 85 zeros, there is no unqualified 85-equation zero-set, machine, HA, or Con(ZFC) claim. No counterexample to the reverse bound is claimed either.

These replayed gap clients inherit the v13 model snapshot. Subsequent merges changed its `AdaptiveDirectOrdinate86FixedForward` and inherited `ArithmeticAdaptiveRankWindow2011` sources; the checked gap results remain snapshot results, not a complete replay of the later workspace source graph.

The independently replayed [factor-unit theorem](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-units-independent.md) proves that an actual 85 zero forces each of its four signed factors to equal +1 in every IΣ₁ model. It combines a magnitude-one argument with finite mod-4 sign exclusions and assumes no factor units. Its proof uses the historical model provider snapshot and inherited project, Foundation and package boundaries; it does not establish the missing reverse bound or a full source-current closure.

The reverse bound cannot be obtained from the first and auxiliary factor-unit equations alone. The independently replayed [actual-factor family](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-family-general-independent.md) proves that for arbitrary integer `beta≥1` and `K≥1` and arbitrary other ports there are positive witnesses with both of those actual factors equal to one but `F+C≥q`. These witnesses are not zeros of the full 85 equation: the same theorem proves the full polynomial nonzero. The universal statement applies mathematically to positive fixed beta and K, but a direct Lean specialization to the enormous fixed constants has not yet been checked. Thus a reverse-bound proof needs at least one additional consequence beyond the first and auxiliary factor-unit conditions, such as a main or strong factor condition. The family does not show that the bound fails for a full zero. A second independently replayed [first-plus-strong family](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-first-strong-family-independent.md) constructs positive integer witnesses for every positive natural beta and K, and arbitrary other integer ports, with the actual first and strong factors equal to one while the bound fails. Its main and auxiliary factors and full-zero status are not asserted. Direct Lean specialization of either family to the enormous fixed constants remains separate.

The independently replayed generic signed half-index theorem `AdaptiveGap85GenericSignedHalfIndex.signed_half_index_of_facts` shows that a Pell index equals the **magnitude** of a rank represented by an arbitrary signed pair, without choosing its sign. It assumes the displayed main, strong and auxiliary Pell norms, a positive ordinate and rank magnitude, the Pell-growth inequality, and `2*|rank|<c`; it derives the required square residue from those assumptions. The cross-parent replay also binds this theorem to an actual positive 85 zero, under explicit `m≥6`, positive rank magnitude and `2*|rank|<c` hypotheses. Those growth and half-size hypotheses have not been proved for all such zeros. The replay checks its two new sources over an explicitly historical model/project/Foundation/package boundary; it does not establish the missing `F+C<q` bound or 85-equation equivalence.

A [checked small-index example](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-small-index-author.md) shows that the first/main Pell-index equations, positive `eta` and `zeta`, and `q³∣Y` alone do **not** force the main index to be at least six: `q=2`, `Y=8`, `X=14`, `n=2`, `m=3`, `eta=2159`, and `zeta=5013` satisfy those conditions in every IΣ₁ model. This example does not check any factor-unit equations and is not a full 85 zero, a fixed-port counterexample, or a counterexample to `F+C<q`.

A generic [signed-rank orientation lemma](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-rank-orientation-author.md) proves that if `n>0`, `(rp+1) mod M=(rn+2n) mod M`, and `|rp−rn|+2n<M`, then `rp>rn` and `|rp−rn|+1=2n`, without assuming either the sign or nonzeroness of the rank. This is useful for interpreting a signed rank once a size bound is known. The strict size assumption has not been established for every actual 85 zero, so this lemma does not prove the reverse witness bound. Its replay retains a historical model/project/Foundation/package boundary.

The [artificial-port oriented-rank example](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-small-oriented-independent.md) shows that even the actual first and auxiliary factors, positive rank, the exact first-index identity `R+1=2n`, and `R+2n<q⁴` do not alone imply `F+C<q`. At its positive witness the main and strong factors are not units and the full polynomial is nonzero; it is not a full zero or a counterexample for the fixed ports.

A [cross-parent replayed fixed-port adapter](../../reviews/adaptive-gap85-merged-adapters-cross-parent.md) shows that an actual shifted 85 zero and a positive first Pell index `n` satisfy `qF+C<q²+2`, `F≤q`, and `F=q → C=1` **if** the signed rank satisfies `|rank|+2n<q⁴`. The fixed port bounds and coordinate positivity are discharged, but that size condition remains unproved. These inequalities are weaker than the missing `F+C<q` bound.
