## 2. What the local fold really proves

Let A>=3,c>=2,D>0 be integers with D²-(A²-1)c²=1. Let S=(A²-1)c,
let T>0,R be integers, and suppose the literal V above satisfies

    S²*(V²-y²)+y²=1.

Write chi_A,psi_A for the Pell sequences, with initial values(1,0),(A,1).
Main classification gives D=chi_A(m), c=psi_A(m), m>=2. Auxiliary
classification, retaining the sign e of V, gives an odd n=2j+1 and

    V=e*Q_j(S²),
    Q_j(0)=(-1)^j*(2j+1),
    Q_j(1-A²)=(-1)^j*psi_A(2j+1).

Here Q_0=1,Q_1=4Z-3,Q_(j+1)=(4Z-2)Q_j-Q_(j-1). Since
S²=(A²-1)*(D²-1), reduction modulo D gives psi_A(n)=±c modD.
The full signed index fold has a representative0<=r<=m with
psi_A(r)=±c modD. Because psi_A(r)<=c and D>2c, only psi_A(r)=c
is possible. Thus r=m and

    n=(2t+1)m for some integer t>=0; m is odd.

This is the endpoint r=m, which the old c²-ordinate proof deliberately
excluded using psi_A(m)>c. Reduction of the LITERAL V modulo c gives

    -R=e*(-1)^j*n modc.

It does NOT turn n into ±m modulo c. The strong-ordinate argument formerly
gave c|m, whereas D²-Delta*c²=1 alone gives no such statement.

## 3. Even the squared-modulus congruence leaves all odd multiples

For odd k, Pell composition gives

    psi_A(km)=c*psi_D(k),
    psi_D(k)=(-1)^((k-1)/2) modD².

The second congruence follows directly from the odd-subsequence recurrence:
psi_D(k) is an even polynomial in D with the displayed constant term.
Equivalently it follows by induction from psi_D(1)=1,psi_D(3)=4D²-1.
Consequently the odd Chebyshev quotient at n=km satisfies

    Q_((km-1)/2)(S²)
      =(-1)^((km-1)/2+(k-1)/2)*c modD².

If m=3 mod4 the sign is ALWAYS negative, for EVERY positive odd k.
The stronger congruence V=-c modD² therefore does not isolate k=1.
It cannot replace the missing c|m step.

## 5. A different tempting shortcut: fixing old i=1 is impossible locally

Keeping the OLD c² strong ordinate while simply fixing i=1 would instead
require f²=1+Delta*c⁴. The main norm gives

    1+Delta*c⁴=(cD)²-c²+1.

For c>1 this is below(cD)². Its difference from(cD-1)² is
c*(2D-c)>0, because D>2c. It lies strictly between consecutive squares.
Thus no integral f exists. This scoped argument rejects that exact
constant-multiplier substitution; it is not a lower bound for arbitrary
strong norms, additional congruences or different coordinate charts.

information by a cheaper genuine norm or another already-proved outer
