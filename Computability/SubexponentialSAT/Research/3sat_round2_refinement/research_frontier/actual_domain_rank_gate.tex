\section{What the actual Cauchy rows can and cannot improve}
The density-refinement intake already proves the Smith profile below
(its Section 9.4, equation labelled \texttt{eq:smith}). We do not claim
that theorem as new. The following connects it explicitly to actual
packet slots and states the resulting exact approximation obstruction.
All rank statements concern integral separated approximations to this
local kernel, not the complexity of SAT.

\paragraph{Actual packet subblock.}
At a retained node $t=2^i$, choose the allowed interpolation value
$\zeta=1$, so $T=2^i+2^{a+1}$. In a packet whose two labels differ
in coordinate $i$, take slots
$x=2^{i+1}k$ and $y=2^{i+1}j$, with $0\le k,j<J=2^{a-i-1}$.
Both slots have bit $i$ equal to zero, so these are crossing ports.
Set
\[
 \delta=1+2^{a+1-i},\qquad
 A=2^{-i}\mathcal C(T)/\mathcal C'(0).
\]
Then their crossing block is exactly
\[
 W_{2^{i+1}k,2^{i+1}j}(T)
 =A K_{kj},\qquad K_{kj}=\frac1{\delta+2(k-j)}.
\]
All denominators of $K$ are units. Moreover $v_2(A)=a+1-i$:
in the product for $\mathcal C(t+2^{a+1})$, the factor belonging to
$t$ has valuation $a+1$, every other factor has its unshifted
valuation, and the product of these other valuations equals that of
$\mathcal C'(0)$ by the complete residue grid.
The statement uses a filled, permissible packet, not a promise that
all inputs contain one. Such a block is enough to test claims that
hold uniformly for all permitted local kernels.

\paragraph{Exact invariant factors.}
For the $J\times J$ matrix $K$, the Smith valuations are
\[
 e_b=2b+2v_2(b!)=4b-2s_2(b),\qquad 0\le b<J.
\]
Indeed, an $q$-minor on distinct integer row and column indices has
valuation
\[
 q(q-1)+v_2\!\prod_{k<l}(x_l-x_k)
       +v_2\!\prod_{k<l}(y_l-y_k).
\]
The Vandermonde on any $q$ integers is divisible by
$\prod_{b<q}b!$, as follows by factoring $b!$ from column $b$ of
the binomial-polynomial evaluation determinant. Consecutive indices
attain equality. Thus the $q$th determinantal ideal has valuation
$q(q-1)+2\sum_{b<q}v_2(b!)$; successive differences give $e_b$.
The same proof applies over the unramified extension used by the
contraction algorithm.

\paragraph{Exact modular approximation rank.}
For any integer $h>0$, the least inner dimension $r$ in an integral
factorization $K\equiv UV\pmod {2^h}$ is
\[
 r_h=\#\{b<J:e_b<h\}.
\]
To prove the lower bound, apply the integral invertible Smith row
and column operations. If a rank-at-most-$r$ matrix approximated the
diagonal Smith matrix to precision $h>e_r$, consider its first
$(r+1)\times(r+1)$ principal block. Its determinant is zero. But the
unperturbed determinant has valuation $\sum_{b\le r}e_b$; every
term involving $k\ge1$ error entries has valuation at least
\[
 kh+\sum_{b=0}^{r-k}e_b
 >\sum_{b=0}^{r}e_b.
\]
Such higher-valuation terms cannot cancel the unperturbed determinant.
This is a contradiction. Conversely, retain exactly the Smith diagonal
entries below precision $h$ and discard the others, then undo the
integral changes of basis.
For the actual unnormalized block, replace $h$ by $P-(a+1-i)$
when approximation precision is $P>a+1-i$.

Consequently, a uniform integral separated expansion whose mode
coefficients have nondecreasing guaranteed valuations $w_b$ cannot
have $w_r>e_r$ on the normalized block: retaining its first $r$ terms
would contradict the rank bound. In particular, superlinear growth
$w_b/b\to\infty$ is impossible by a mere integral separated-basis
change on this local family. This leaves open nonseparated summation,
correlated cancellation, new deformations, or a different recursive
representation.

\paragraph{Why slope three does not transfer automatically.}
The factorial-Cauchy intake proves weights asymptotic to $3b$ using
an enlarged auxiliary row domain. It is incorrect to assert that the
actual reciprocal rows contain every even residue. For example,
$t=1$, $i=0$, and tile $y_0=0$ give crossing rows $x=2k$ and
$z_x=2/(1+2k+2^{a+1})\equiv2\pmod4$. For $i=1$, $t=2$, $y_0=0$,
and $a\ge3$, the possible rows are either $z_x\equiv2\pmod4$ or
$z_x\equiv12\pmod{16}$; residue $4\pmod{16}$ is absent.
These are congruence exclusions even at arbitrarily large packet size.
Thus the exact $4b+O(\log b)$ subblock obstruction is valid, whereas
an optimal $3b+O(\log b)$ obstruction on all actual rows needs a
separate proof. Splitting into additional row residue classes can also
increase mode-family multiplicity, so a locally larger slope alone
is not an end-to-end speedup.
