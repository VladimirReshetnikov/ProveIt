\begin{abstract}
We review the twenty-nine filed refinement packets and five newer incoming
packets at a fixed ProveIt commit, together with the user-specified Anthropic
formalization. Two quantitative improvements are distinguished. First, the
known square-root-degree 3-SAT grouping permits a new strict recursion
schedule with eventual time coefficient $13.690$ and simultaneous space
coefficient $12.798$, on the normalized wire scale
$s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}$. This is a degree-sensitive composition of
existing primitives, with the full child-entry cost retained. Its time gain
does not require the new consistency inequality.

Second, under the generic root-degree promise $s^{9/10}$, the smallest
comparable incoming cached time coefficient is
$29.81160894538\ldots$ on the scale $s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}$, where
$s$ is the normalized Boolean wire count. We improve the certificate for the
same finite family of contraction plans by requiring the support chosen for
one plan to remain consistent across every pair involving that plan.
Conservative pairwise maxima are replaced by exact zero-sum potentials.
The resulting time coefficient is $\NewTime\ldots$, with simultaneous
space coefficient $\NewSpace\ldots$, certified by integer arithmetic
and an independently reconstructed checker. The complete recursion charges
every child-table entry and preserves the inherited degree and precision
contracts. These are improvements of exponential upper-bound coefficients.
They do not establish a smaller logarithmic order for general 3-SAT, and
the algorithms use subexponential space.

We also prove a full-rank obstruction to a universal linear summary of
variable unions, and sharpen the counting of degree arrays in an already
known grouping reduction. These results identify specific limits of two
routes to a larger improvement without asserting a lower bound on 3-SAT
algorithms. The archive contains complete LaTeX sources, exact certificate
data, independent checks, and a reproducible finite contraction prototype.
\end{abstract}
