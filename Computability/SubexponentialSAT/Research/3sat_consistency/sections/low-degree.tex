\section{A degree-aware specialization for the 3-SAT reduction}
\label{sec:low-degree}

The generic degree allowance $s^{9/10}$ is deliberately broad. A reduction
with a smaller maximum degree should use a different retained-range schedule.
Here we prove a separate bound under maximum initial degree
$\lceil s^{51/100}\rceil$. Section~\ref{sec:3sat} records the wire and degree
premises needed to apply such a contraction theorem to 3-SAT. The new
schedule is useful in its own right; it is not obtained by simply rescaling
the generic displayed coefficient.

\subsection{A strict rational schedule}

Retain the same group sequence $10,13,7,7,\ldots$ and local coefficients
as in Section~\ref{sec:recursion}. Put
\begin{align*}
 \eta_0&=\frac{59}{800},&
 \eta_1&=\frac{181}{4000},&
 \eta_d&=\frac{89}{5000}\left(\frac49\right)^{d-2}\quad(d\ge2).
\end{align*}
For $\lambda=\lceil\log_2s\rceil$, use
\[
 u_d=\lfloor\eta_d\lambda\rfloor,\qquad
 B=\lceil6\log_2\lambda\rceil,\qquad
 a_d=\sum_{j\le d}(u_j+B),
\]
relative padding slack $\lambda^{-3}$, and direct evaluation below
$H=\lceil s/\lambda^{3/4}\rceil$. The two relevant cumulative fractions are
\[
 A_1=\eta_0+\eta_1=\frac{119}{1000},\qquad
 A_\infty=\eta_0+\eta_1+\frac95\eta_2=\frac{472}{3125}.
\]
The fixed logarithm bounds
\[
 \log_2 10<\frac{1661}{500},\qquad
 \log_2 13<\frac{3701}{1000},\qquad
 \log_2 7<\frac{281}{100}
\]
are certified by integer powering in the supplied checker. The following
positive gaps are exact rational values. The degree column subtracts
$51/100+2\eta_0\log_2 10$ at the root and
$51/100+A_d\log_2 r_d$ afterwards from one. The capacity column subtracts
$(1+2\log_2r_d)A_d$ from one.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Stage & Degree gap & Capacity gap\\\midrule
Root, $r=10$ & $1/200000$ & $87251/200000$\\
Depth one, $r=13$ & $49581/1000000$ & $81/500000$\\
Tail, $r=7$ & $20493/312500$ & $9/78125$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are bounds using the displayed rational logarithm upper bounds.
The fresh-side gate has exponent
$2A_d\log_2r_d+A_{d-1}\le(1+2\log_2r_d)A_d$, so the capacity column also
pays that gate. Moreover $A_\infty<51/100$, ensuring that all newly created
packet degrees remain within the retained degree budget. This proof uses
stage-specific capacity bounds; replacing every tail logarithm by
$\log_2 13$ would unnecessarily discard this schedule.

For completeness, the depth and precision arguments still close uniformly.
Provisionally consider at most $\frac34\log_3\lambda+O(1)$ active tail
levels. The smallest retained range in that window is a positive power of
$\lambda$, since $(9/4)^3<3^4$. All fixed exponent gaps in the table absorb
the $O((\log\lambda)^2)$ cumulative precision buffers and
$s/M\le\lambda^{3/4}$. Nearly lossless packing then gives actual tail child
ratio $2/7+o(1)<1/3$, proving the provisional depth bound.
The product of all relative padding losses is $1+o(1)$, and floors are
uniformly negligible. The retained deformation scales satisfy
$D\ge2^{a_{d-1}+B+1}$. Consequently the imported creation precision
$O(s\lambda^2 2^{a_{d-1}})$ divided by $MD/u_d$ is
$O(\lambda^{15/4}2^{-B})=o(1)$. This justifies the same sharp precision
and entropy bounds used in the generic theorem.

All numerical representations have polynomial bit length, because
$a_d\le(472/3125)\lambda+O((\log\lambda)^2)$.
The unchanged entry-access costs and the direct leaves are lower order on
$s/\sqrt\lambda$: the generic permitted original-entry cost
$2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ already suffices, while new packet descriptions cost
$\exp(s^{472/3125+o(1)})$ and leaves cost
$2^{O(s/\lambda^{3/4})}$. No stronger entry-access assumption is silently
introduced by the smaller degree premise.

\subsection{Charge the recurrence before comparing coefficients}

Let $\beta^2$ be the new exact ten-group coefficient from
Section~\ref{sec:consistency}. The retained thirteen- and seven-group
coefficients remain $\beta_{13}^2=1572/4225$ and
$\beta_7^2=632/1225$, with
$\alpha_r=\sqrt{2(r-1)}/r$. The geometric tail ratio is again $3/7$.
Unrolling the paid recurrence gives
\begin{align*}
 C_0&=\frac\pi{\ln2}\sqrt{\frac{1600\beta^2}{59}}
      =13.43744330254181\ldots,\\
 C_1&=\frac\pi{\ln2}
      \left(\sqrt{\frac{288}{59}}+
            \sqrt{\frac{100608}{152945}}\right)
      =13.68968074357885\ldots,\\
 C_\infty&=\frac\pi{\ln2}
      \left(\sqrt{\frac{288}{59}}+
            \sqrt{\frac{7680}{30589}}+
            \sqrt{\frac{1200}{15041}}\right)
      =13.56492576337809\ldots.
\end{align*}
Every tail local branch lies below the terminal branch because
$\alpha_7^2/(1-3/7)^2=3/4>\beta_7^2$. Exact rational enclosures prove
$C_1>\max(C_0,C_\infty)$.

The joint root selector gives local root space coefficient
$\frac{20}{21}C_0=12.79756505003982\ldots$.
The depth-one and initial tail coefficients are respectively
$3.67597904980247\ldots$ and $1.06178489349021\ldots$; later tail
coefficients decrease geometrically. Ancestor and descendant tables add
in size, so their exponent coefficients are maximized rather than summed.

\begin{theorem}[Degree-$51/100$ specialization]\label{thm:low-degree}
Under the normalized input and entry-access premises of
Section~\ref{sec:interface}, strengthened to maximum initial degree at most
$\lceil s^{51/100}\rceil$, there is a deterministic exact contraction algorithm
with
\begin{align*}
 T&\le\poly(s+\mathcal L)\,
       2^{(\widehat C_T+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda},\\
 S&\le\poly(s+\mathcal L)\,
       2^{(\widehat C_S+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda},
\end{align*}
where
\[
 \widehat C_T=C_1=13.68968074357885\ldots,
 \qquad
 \widehat C_S=\frac{20}{21}C_0=12.79756505003982\ldots.
\]
Thus $13.690$ and $12.798$ are safe eventual coefficients for time and space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The strict gate table, depth bootstrap, and precision argument establish all
premises of the imported local contraction and the new joint certificate
at every active call. The fully charged recurrence and the strict branch
comparisons give the time bound. The local space comparisons give the
space bound. All remaining entry and arithmetic costs were included above.
Exactness follows from the unchanged contraction identities and plan
regrouping, independently of the parameter estimates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What causes this specialization's improvement}

A same-schedule comparison is essential. Replacing the new root coefficient
by the older pairwise certificate gives root coefficient
$13.46705560666417\ldots$. That is still below $C_1$, so the old and new
root certificates give \emph{the same overall time coefficient} on this
schedule. The low-degree time improvement is therefore a degree-aware
composition of the available ingredients; it must not be attributed to
the new root potentials. The new joint certificate separately supplies the
smaller root space guarantee.

The old time-support argument on this schedule bounds local root space by
$13.46705560666417\ldots$; the new joint argument bounds it by
$12.79756505003982\ldots$. Dividing the old root bound by $21/20$ would
require its own augmented-support certificate and is not justified merely
by the new selector's existence. These comparisons concern valid upper
bounds, not matching lower bounds for actual memory use.

The standalone \texttt{check\_low\_degree.py} verifies the exact gate gaps,
recurrence radicals, interval comparisons, and the unchanged dominant time
branch for both root certificates. It also rejects three deliberately
false claims, including reuse of this schedule at degree exponent $0.52$.
The specialized coefficients remain per normalized wire; converting them
into a numerical coefficient per 3-SAT variable requires the explicit wire
ledger of the chosen reduction.

\subsection{Earlier usable bounds and a stronger inherited comparator}

The printed $25.163765\ldots$ polynomial-space bound at degree $s^{0.51}$
is not the only fair comparator \cite{exactkernels}. The eight-group cached
proof in \cite{cacheeight} uses, in its smaller-degree branch,
$\eta_d=1/(15\cdot2^d)$. Its root and retained-degree exponents are
$\delta+2/5$, while its fresh-side and capacity exponent is at most $14/15$.
It therefore works for every fixed $\delta<3/5$, including $51/100$,
although the stated corollary uses the stronger $s^{1/12}$ premise.
This already gives time $19.08387285\ldots$ and space $16.99635053\ldots$.
Replacing its matrix allowance by the available $12/5$ bound leaves its
literal block-table construction as the bottleneck, with time coefficient
$(\pi/\ln2)\sqrt{135/8}=18.61856916\ldots$.
Omitting the table-construction cost would give an invalid comparison.

A closer comparator simply multiplies every taper in the incoming generic
$10\to13\to7$ schedule by $17/4$, keeping its old pairwise certificate.
Its cumulative tapers are $5117/80000$, $9163/80000$, and $34/225$.
The degree and capacity gates hold for $\delta=51/100$; the tail check uses
the stricter certified bound $\log_2 7<14037/5000$.
The dominant root coefficient is then
\[
 \frac{29.81160894538347\ldots}{\sqrt{17/4}}
 =14.46075441781\ldots.
\]
The exact checker includes these gates and all paid branches. This is an
inherited composition evaluated here, not a theorem printed in the source.
It is a substantially stronger baseline than comparing only with the old
generic headline or the polynomial-space number.

The last improvement is about $5.33\%$ relative to the immediately
preceding time coefficient. The new root potentials are not its cause,
as the same-schedule calculation above makes explicit. None of these
comparisons proves optimality among all retunings of older primitives.
For the stronger degree-$s^{1/12}$ promise, earlier cached coefficients
as small as $13.37965\ldots$ remain available; the present specialization
does not supersede that separate regime \cite{flags}.
