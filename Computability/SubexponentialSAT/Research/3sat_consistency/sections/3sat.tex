\section{Consequences for 3-SAT and what remains unresolved}
\label{sec:3sat}

\subsection{A strict generic coefficient gain with the same asymptotic scale}

Under the generic $s^{9/10}$ degree promise, the fastest comparable incoming time coefficient was
$C_{\rm old}=29.81160894538347\ldots$. Theorem~\ref{thm:global} decreases
it by $0.06555185158\ldots$, approximately $0.21989\%$. Relative to that
packet's accompanying space upper bound, the new space coefficient drops
by approximately $4.97\%$. These percentages describe coefficients in
exponential upper bounds, rather than measured speedups or memory ratios.

If $E(s)=s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}$, the leading expressions compare as
\[
 \frac{2^{C_{\rm old}E(s)}}{2^{C_TE(s)}}
 =2^{(C_{\rm old}-C_T)E(s)}.
\]
Since the coefficient gap is fixed and positive, it is eventually larger
than the difference of the respective $o(1)$ terms. This is why the change
is a strict asymptotic improvement in the quantitative certificate despite
preserving the exponent's logarithmic order. No lower bound on the older
implementation follows from this ratio.

The useful resource alternatives are retained. The affine packet gives
$(30.2570356\ldots,25.9890677\ldots)$, with more time and less space.
The generic polynomial-space construction has time coefficient
$46.1848456\ldots$. Our cached result is not a replacement for either
resource requirement \cite{affine,flags}.

\subsection{Transfer to the actual 3-SAT reductions}

Counting assignments also decides satisfiability by testing whether the
count is nonzero. We restrict all applications here to clauses of width
at most three. The stronger counting interface is useful because the
inherited reductions reconstruct the exact count.

For fixed clause density, the native variable--clause network has
$O_D(n)$ wires. High-degree variables can be conditioned or represented
by the exact bounded-degree equality constructions already used in the
corpus. Invoking the improved contractor preserves the established order
\[
 (n+m)^{O(1)}2^{O_D(n/\sqrt{\log n})}.
\]
At arbitrary density, the source's sparse parity tests and incidence
encoding produce $O(n\log\log n)$ wires, so the order remains
\[
 (n+m)^{O(1)}2^{O(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}.
\]
In both cases the appropriate reduction must supply the degree, precision,
entry-access, and number-of-branches promises. The normalized coefficient
$C_T$ cannot be read as a coefficient per SAT variable without explicitly
multiplying by the reduction's wire constant and charging its other work.

There is a more precise ledger for the arbitrary-density construction.
Write $\lambda_n=\lceil\log_2n\rceil$ and fix a constant $c\ge1$ for the
source's sparse-query guarantee: at most $a\le6n$ equations, each of
degree at most three with at most $\lambda_n^c$ monomials. Then
\[
 K=3(a+2n)\le24n,\qquad N\le3a\lambda_n^c\le18n\lambda_n^c.
\]
The grouping in Section~\ref{sec:structure} has $B=o(n)$ nonempty group
pairs and degree $\sqrt n\,\operatorname{polylog}(n)$. Its wire bound gives
\begin{align*}
 M&\le B+\log_2\binom{N+K}{K}\\
  &\le (24c+o(1))\,n\log_2\lambda_n.
\end{align*}
The corresponding integer upper budget covering these wires and $n+1$
recovery precision is computable from the displayed binomial bound and
satisfies $s\le(24c+o(1))n\log_2\lambda_n$ and the degree-$s^{51/100}$
promise eventually. It yields the parametric leading contraction
coefficient $24c\,\widehat C_T$ on
$n\log_2\lambda_n/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$.
This is an explicit ledger in the fixed query parameter $c$; we have not
audited a numerical value of $c$ through the whole parity construction.

The outer work does not introduce another leading coefficient at this
scale. Sparsification has $2^{O(n/\sqrt{\lambda_n})}$ nodes,
high-incidence branching has at most $2^{n/\sqrt{\lambda_n}}$ branches,
the improved array count is
$2^{O_c(n/(\sqrt{\lambda_n}\log\lambda_n))}$, and parity construction
and candidate enumeration cost $2^{\lambda_n^{O_c(1)}}$ beyond the
contractions. Their logarithms are all
$o(n\log\lambda_n/\sqrt{\lambda_n})$ \cite{openai}.
Thus the smaller normalized coefficient improves the contraction
contribution using the same fixed 3-SAT reduction. It does not make
$13.690$ a numerical coefficient per original SAT variable.

Where the stronger degree-$s^{1/12}$ promise is available, the earlier
$13.37965\ldots$ cached option remains preferable to this specialization.
The present result is not a domination statement for every density regime.

The density-aware packets additionally retain the bound
\[
 (n+m)^{O(1)}
 2^{O\left(\frac n{\sqrt{\log n}}
       \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}
\]
in their stated regime. For example, at $m=\Theta(n\log\log n)$ they
replace a $\log\log n$ numerator by $\log\log\log n$. This genuine
regime-dependent order improvement is prior work and is not attributed to
the present certificate \cite{density,density2}.

\subsection{Why the present mechanism cannot supply the requested order gain}

The finite norm certificate affects only a constant in the inherited local
scale $M/\sqrt u$. The gate conditions retain $u=\Theta(\log s)$.
Changing support weights or finite menus therefore preserves
$M/\sqrt{\log s}$ unless another part of that interface changes.
The root precision cannot simply be reduced to a small constant: the
Hermite recovery increment still has size $\Theta(MD/u)$.

Removing the general-case $\log\log n$ penalty requires an encoding with
$o(n\log\log n)$ wires and paid local entries, or another method for
summing the incidence information. Replacing $\sqrt{\log n}$ by
$\log n$ requires a different contraction or mode-summation mechanism.
The earlier precision packet explicitly labels its structured-mode
acceleration as unproved \cite{precision}. Its conditional bound must
not be presented as an available algorithm, or ranked numerically against
the proved coefficient here.

The next section tests two concrete approaches to that larger goal. The
result is a representation barrier and a tighter enumeration bound, rather
than an asserted solution of the missing algorithmic step. The small
remaining freedom in the fixed nine-plan average, quantified in
Section~\ref{sec:weight-limit}, also argues against spending the next
iteration on reweighting those same plans alone.
