\section{A certificate that preserves support consistency}
\label{sec:consistency}

\subsection{A general exact inequality}

Let $\mathcal W_1,\ldots,\mathcal W_q$ be nonempty finite indexed families
of vectors in $\R^d$, and let $p_i\ge0$, $\sum_i p_i=1$, $q\ge2$.
Identical vectors may have distinct indices and distinct potentials.
For $i<j$ define
\[
 c_{ij}(v,z)=\frac{p_i^2\norm v^2+p_j^2\norm z^2}{q-1}
                     +2p_ip_j\langle v,z\rangle.
\]
Choose a function $\phi_{ij,i}$ on $\mathcal W_i$ and
$\phi_{ij,j}$ on $\mathcal W_j$ for each unordered pair. Require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conservation}
 \sum_{j\ne i}\phi_{\{i,j\},i}(v)=0
 \qquad(i\in[q],\ v\in\mathcal W_i).
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-certificate}
 A_{ij}=\max_{v\in\mathcal W_i,z\in\mathcal W_j}
 \{c_{ij}(v,z)+\phi_{ij,i}(v)+\phi_{ij,j}(z)\},
 \qquad U=\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Consistency certificate]\label{thm:consistency}
For every simultaneous choice $v_i\in\mathcal W_i$,
\[
 \norm{\sum_i p_i v_i}^{\!2}\le U.
\]
If every vector is nonnegative and the entropy estimate
\eqref{eq:entropy-interface} applies, the plan minimizing
$\max_{v\in\mathcal W_i}v\cdot g$ has local coefficient at most
$\beta=\sqrt{2U}/r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each squared term $p_i^2\norm{v_i}^2$ occurs in $q-1$ pair costs, with
coefficient $1/(q-1)$. Thus
\[
 \norm{\sum_i p_i v_i}^{\!2}
 =\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}(v_i,v_j)
 =\sum_{i<j}\{c_{ij}(v_i,v_j)+\phi_{ij,i}(v_i)+\phi_{ij,j}(v_j)\}.
\]
The second equality is exactly \eqref{eq:conservation}; it uses the same
$v_i$ at every pair containing $i$. Bound each summand by its maximum to
obtain the first conclusion.

For a given $g$, choose a support $v_i$ maximizing each plan's score.
The minimum of those scores is no larger than their convex average,
which is $(\sum_i p_i v_i)\cdot g$. The averaged support is nonnegative
and belongs to a fixed finite family. Apply
\eqref{eq:entropy-interface} and the first conclusion. No interchange of
a minimum and maximum, or simultaneous realizability of arbitrary pair
maximizers, is asserted.
\end{proof}

Zero potentials recover the incoming pair certificate. Nonzero potentials
can decrease the sum of maxima while leaving every true simultaneous
quadratic expression unchanged. Negative potential values are permitted;
only the support vectors used in the entropy estimate need to be
nonnegative.

\subsection{Joint storage control with the same certificate}

For every stored-block boundary $b_A$, the original time family contains
an input/output support $1400007\,b_A$. Append the support
$1470000\,b_A$, and give it the same potentials as that input/output
support. Conservation remains exact. The new normalized support is
$(21/20)b_A$, so the maximum over this augmented family is precisely
the score in \eqref{eq:joint-selector}.

\begin{lemma}[Augmented certificate]\label{lem:joint}
For the fixed data above, adding the $17$ boundary supports per plan does
not increase any exact pair maximum. Therefore the selector
\eqref{eq:joint-selector} simultaneously guarantees
\[
 F_j(g)\le(\beta_{10}+o(1))K(M,u),\qquad
 G_j(g)\le\left(\frac{20}{21}\beta_{10}+o(1)\right)K(M,u).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are $89$ augmented supports per plan. The time--time choices have
already been checked. The remaining number of choices is
\[
 \binom92(89^2-72^2)=98532.
\]
For each such choice the integer expression
\eqref{eq:integer-pair} is at most the corresponding old maximum.
The strict minimum margin is positive and is reproduced by both exact
checkers; no numerical comparison is used. This finite inequality is the
certificate premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:consistency}. There are $801$
conservation equations in the enlarged family. Apply that theorem to the
augmented score, then use the two terms in its defining maximum.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Discovery and its separation from verification}

The discovery method is a finite potential redistribution. With current
corrected pair matrix $M_{ij}(v,z)$, fix plan $i$ and define
\[
 a_{ij}(v)=\max_z M_{ij}(v,z),\qquad
 \overline a_i(v)=\frac1{q-1}\sum_{j\ne i}a_{ij}(v).
\]
Update $\phi_{ij,i}(v)$ by adding
$\overline a_i(v)-a_{ij}(v)$. The increments sum to zero for every $v$.
Each incident pair's conditional maximum then becomes $\overline a_i(v)$,
and the sum of its unconditional maxima cannot increase, because
\[
 (q-1)\max_v\overline a_i(v)
 \le\sum_{j\ne i}\max_v a_{ij}(v).
\]
This supplies a simple descent procedure. Numerical sweeps and a short
search over proof weights suggested the displayed data.

For each plan, the potentials on all but its last incident pair were
rounded to denominator $10^{12}$. The final potential was set to the
negative exact sum of the preceding ones. All pair maxima were then
recomputed as integers. The accepted certificate needs no floating-point
tolerance, optimization software, convergence assertion, or optimality
claim about the discovery procedure.

\subsection{A nearly sharp limit for this particular proof method}
\label{sec:weight-limit}

Let $t_i$ be the raw support with index $67,67,69,69,67,69,67,69,69$,
respectively, in the nine plans. Put $G_{ij}=\langle t_i,t_j\rangle$.
For any mixture $p$ with $\sum_i p_i=1$, this one allowed simultaneous
tuple already has squared normalized norm $p^TGp/L_*^2$.
The exact checker verifies that $G$ is positive definite and
$G^{-1}\mathbf1$ has positive coordinates. Completing the square gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture-lower}
 \frac{p^TGp}{L_*^2}
 \ge U_*:=\frac1{L_*^2\mathbf1^TG^{-1}\mathbf1}
 =16.206397979569626\ldots .
\end{equation}
The corresponding coefficient lower bound at the fixed root taper lies
within $4.3\cdot10^{-10}$ of the coefficient proved here. The comparison
is certified with rational bounds, including the exact Gram matrix and
its positive-definiteness certificate.

Thus another search over the mixture weights of these same nine plans
cannot yield a material improvement \emph{through this worst-case
average-norm argument}. This does not establish optimality of the actual
adaptive plan selector. Different menus, a proof of the minimum of plan
costs that avoids a fixed average, stronger information on generated
mode lists, or a different contraction representation remain possible.
