\section{Exact contraction interface and local cost supports}
\label{sec:interface}

\subsection{Input promises and imported dependencies}

The normalized input is a bipartite Boolean tensor network with at most
$s$ wires, polynomially many vertices, and maximum degree at most
$\lceil s^{9/10}\rceil$. Its value is
\[
 Z=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{E}}\prod_v T_v(x|_{\delta(v)}).
\]
The entries belong to $(\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell)$ with
$1\le P_0,\ell\le s$. A uniform entry procedure uses polynomial space
and $(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ bit operations, with fixed
constants. All subsequent estimates use these same promises.

The inherited computation extends scalars to
\[
 R_p=(\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[U,V]/(f(U),V^\ell),
\]
where $f$ is monic of degree $O(\log s)$ and irreducible modulo two.
Ring rank and coordinate precision remain polynomial in $s$. A tensor
description retains its creation precision and is evaluated using the same
canonical coordinate representatives at later precisions. Canonical
lifting is not used as a homomorphism.

We import the exact Cauchy deformation, local integrality, Hermite recovery,
and original SAT reductions from the source, and the compatible refinements
of packing, full-orientation modes, and sharp local precision from the
reviewed packets \cite{openai,asymmetric,joint,flags,pairwise}. The proof
below starts after those constructions have produced finite mode lists.
It only changes how the ensuing finite sum is evaluated and bounded.

\subsection{The mode sum and its entropy inequality}

At one active call, let $M$ be the wire count, $r$ a fixed group count,
$u$ the retained valuation range, $a$ the packet exponent, and
$D=2^{a-i}$ for $0\le i<u$. The imported identities give
\[
 p_t\le (6+o(1))\frac{MD}{u},\qquad
 M_{\rm child}\le\left(\frac2r+o(1)\right)M.
\]
For each directed group pair $(g,h)$, $g\ne h$, enumerate its exact
valuation budget and let $\mathcal I_{gh}$ be the finite list of mode
exponent tuples realizing that budget. Empty lists contribute zero and are
skipped. With $r$ fixed, the budget arrays are polynomially numerous in $s$.
Their exact list cardinalities, ranking, and unranking are supplied by the
ordinary finite knapsack recurrence for the known integral mode weights.

For one budget array the contribution is precisely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-sum}
 \sum_{\xi_{gh}\in\mathcal I_{gh}}
       \prod_{g=1}^r Z_g((\xi_e)_{e\ni g}).
\end{equation}
An entry of $Z_g$ is an actual recursive contraction, with its cost charged
later. Write
\[
 h_{gh}=\log_2|\mathcal I_{gh}|,\quad
 g_{\{g,h\}}=h_{gh}+h_{hg},\quad
 K(M,u)=\kappa\frac M{\sqrt u},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}.
\]
The inherited common-tilt estimate states that for each fixed nonnegative
unordered support $v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-interface}
 v\cdot g\le\left(\frac{\sqrt2\norm v}{r}+o(1)\right)K(M,u).
\end{equation}
The error is uniform on a fixed finite support family. The factor
$\sqrt2$ converts an unordered edge into its two directed copies and must
be retained.

For orientation, the mode weights have the form
$a+1-i+4j-2\operatorname{popcount}(j)$, and at most
$(2/r^2+o(1))M/D$ modes of each index occur on a directed pair.
The generating product has logarithm
$\pi^2/(24\theta)+O(\log^2(2/\theta))$ as $\theta\downarrow0$.
Combining this with the common precision budget and minimizing in $\theta$
gives \eqref{eq:entropy-interface}. The buffers in Section~\ref{sec:recursion}
make its error uniform on all active calls. We use this already proved
interface, rather than asserting that a finite checker proves its analytic
asymptotics.

\subsection{Contraction plans and the arithmetic backend}

A plan is an ordered partition of $[r]$ into three nonempty blocks. Each
block is built recursively by splitting its ordered list at its midpoint.
Merging disjoint blocks $A,B$ with outside block $O$ is a matrix product
whose three index classes correspond to $AB,AO,BO$. After the three main
blocks have been built, the remaining triangle is contracted.
Every step follows from finite distributivity over $R_p$.

The retained rectangular backend uses fixed field bounds
$\omega<19/8$ and dual exponent $\alpha>3/10$. These weaker rational
allowances follow from the published bounds in \cite{matrix}.
For sorted logarithmic dimensions $x\ge y\ge z\ge0$, set
$\varepsilon=10^{-5}$. The reviewed implementation gives the exponent
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rect-cost}
 F(x,y,z)=\max\left\{
 (1+\varepsilon/2)(x+y),\quad
 x+(47/56+\varepsilon)y+(15/28)z
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The input/output term is included. As a function of the unsorted triple,
this is the maximum of the distinct permutations of
$(1+\varepsilon/2,1+\varepsilon/2,0)$ and
$(1,47/56+\varepsilon,15/28)$, by the rearrangement inequality.

The exact ring bridge lifts coordinates to bounded integers, uses finitely
many nonexceptional odd primes for the fixed rational bilinear algorithms,
reconstructs integer products by CRT, and reduces modulo $2^p$ after
combining the ring structure constants. The number of coordinate products,
primes, and bits per coordinate is polynomial in $s$. Thus the field
algorithms yield the same displayed exponential coefficient over $R_p$
without inverting a nonunit. A fixed bilinear recursion processes one
recursive product at a time; its working storage is a polynomial factor
times the largest relevant input/output table \cite{rectangular}.

Put $L_*=1{,}400{,}000$. The integral triples are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triples}
 (1400007,1400007,0),\qquad(1400000,1175014,750000).
\end{equation}
An operation produces nine vectors by assigning these permuted triples to
its three crossing edge classes and zero to edges internal to a class.
There are $r-2$ operations per plan. At $r=10$ this gives $72$ supports
in dimension $\binom{10}2=45$. Denote their multiset by $\mathcal V_j$.
The time score for plan $j$ is
\[
 F_j(g)=\frac1{L_*}\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}v\cdot g.
\]
Repeated supports are harmless; their deterministic order is fixed by
postorder traversal and lexicographic order of the nine triples.

For each nonempty stored block $A$, let $b_A$ be the indicator of its
edge boundary. There are $17$ distinct such vectors per ten-group plan,
including all singleton tables. Define the storage score
\begin{equation}\label{eq:space-score}
 G_j(g)=\max_A b_A\cdot g.
\end{equation}
Every input and output of an internal matrix product is one of these
boundary tables. The final three matrices are also included. Constantly
many simultaneous tables change their size only by a constant factor.

\subsection{Exact plan selection}

The final algorithm chooses a plan minimizing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-selector}
 J_j(g)=\max\{F_j(g),(21/20)G_j(g)\}.
\end{equation}
This one choice will achieve both the time and space bounds. It is not
legitimate to choose one plan for time and another plan for space and
combine their guarantees.

There is an exact integer implementation. Set
$d_{gh}=|\mathcal I_{gh}|\,|\mathcal I_{hg}|$ for unordered pairs.
Augment each time support by the integer vectors $1{,}470{,}000\,b_A$.
For every augmented support $v$, form $\prod_e d_e^{v_e}$, take the maximum
within a plan, and choose the plan of smallest maximum, breaking ties by
index. Taking a base-two logarithm shows equivalence to
\eqref{eq:joint-selector}. The exponents, group count, and menu size are
fixed constants; the integers have polynomial bit length in $s$.
No floating-point comparison or optimization oracle is needed at runtime.
