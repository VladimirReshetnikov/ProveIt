\section{Outcome and the comparison actually being made}
\label{sec:review}

There are two results. The one relevant to the known arbitrary-density
3-SAT regrouping uses its stronger root-degree promise
$\Delta\le s^{51/100}$. A new strict degree-aware schedule gives the
eventual simultaneous bounds
\[
 T\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{13.690s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}},\qquad
 S\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{12.798s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}}.
\]
Section~\ref{sec:low-degree} proves this composition and distinguishes its
time improvement from the new support-consistency argument. The latter
also improves the wider generic interface: under the same root-degree
promise $\Delta\le\lceil s^{9/10}\rceil$ as the strongest incoming cached
packet, it gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-bound}
 \begin{aligned}
 T(s,\mathcal L)&\le (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(\NewTime\ldots+o(1))s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}},\\
 S(s,\mathcal L)&\le (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(\NewSpace\ldots+o(1))s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal L$ is the description length, and the two bounds are a
simultaneously valid pair. The theorem is relative to explicit imported
deformation, recovery, packing, and matrix multiplication results. The new
finite inequality and its exact certificate are proved and checked here.

The request for a breakthrough in the unrestricted logarithmic order is
\emph{not resolved}. In particular, this work does not replace
$n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ by $n/\sqrt{\log n}$ or by
$n\log\log n/\log n$. The improvement is nevertheless asymptotic at the
level of the displayed quantitative upper bounds within the specified
degree and resource classes. The generic coefficient alone would not
improve the strongest contractor usable after the existing 3-SAT regrouping;
the lower-degree specialization is essential. Ratios of these upper bounds are not lower bounds on
the running times of implementations.

\subsection{The two source manuscripts and their formal scope}

For 3-SAT, the OpenAI October 25 manuscript gives the general order
\[
 (n+m)^{O(1)}2^{O(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}
\]
in polynomial space, and the fixed-density order
$2^{O_D(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ when $m\le Dn$, with polynomial input factors
understood. Its pinned Lean scope page explicitly describes the weaker
formalized exponent $n(\log\log n)^2/\sqrt{\log n}$, still with polynomial
space. The manuscript's sharper appendix and the formalized scope are
different guarantees \cite{openai,openai-scope}.

At the exact Anthropic commit supplied by the user, \anthpin,
\texttt{MainResults.MainTheorem} states the existence of one deterministic
finite-stack Mathlib machine and a constant $C$ that count all satisfying
assignments within
\[
 2^{C\min\{3m/\sqrt{\log_2m},\,
                  9n\log_2\log_2n/\sqrt{\log_2n}\}}
\]
on the stated 3-CNF inputs. Those inputs have $n\ge4$, $m\ge2$, no duplicate
clause lists, and every declared variable occurring. Every stack alphabet
is explicitly required to be finite. The proof entry point supplies a
term for this proposition. The headline proposition contains no
polynomial-space conclusion \cite{anthropic-main,anthropic-solution}.

We inspected these statements and proof entry points; we did not rebuild
or independently replay either large Lean development. This report neither
denies the formal statements nor claims a fresh kernel verification.
Unknown constants in the separate Anthropic construction do not permit a
numerical speed comparison with the normalized-wire coefficient in
\eqref{eq:main-bound}. Our claims concern explicit normalized contraction
bounds in their stated degree classes, not a proof of being faster than
every possible implementation of both manuscripts.

\subsection{What the new idea changes}

The strongest incoming certificate bounds the norm of an average of nine
support vectors by summing thirty-six separately maximized pair terms.
The support chosen for one plan can consequently be different in each
pairwise maximum. A real simultaneous selection has only one support for
that plan. We exploit this consistency by redistributing zero-sum functions
of each selected support among its incident pairs.

This preserves the contraction plans, arithmetic backend, and recursion.
The simultaneous storage bound adds a boundary-size objective to the exact
plan selector. The primary improvement is in the proof of the
worst-case running time. Pairwise consistency and such potential
reparametrizations are classical finite optimization tools
\cite{wainwright}; the contribution here is their exact certificate for
this specific contraction family and the fully charged consequence.
