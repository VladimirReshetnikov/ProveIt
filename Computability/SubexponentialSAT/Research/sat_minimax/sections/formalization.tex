\subsection{The exact public endpoint}

The file \path{eth/MainResults.lean} imports Mathlib alone and defines
the public statements. Its main theorem asserts the existence of one
deterministic finite-alphabet Mathlib multi-stack Turing machine and one
constant $C$, both chosen before the clause width $k$, such that the machine
counts satisfying assignments in at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anth-main}
 2^{C\min\{km/\sqrt{\log_2m},\ k^2n\log_2\log_2n/\sqrt{\log_2n}\}}
\end{equation}
steps, with the stated integer rounding. Its premises are $k\ge1$,
$n\ge4$, $m\ge2$, at most $k$ literals per clause, no repeated clause,
and occurrence of every variable. Its output is the exact count in $n+1$
binary digits. The theorem about ETH is stated separately and follows
through explicit front ends.

The proof in \path{eth/Solution.lean} invokes
\path{CountedRoute.mainTheoremHolds}. The separate ETH endpoint is
constructed through \path{NamesInOrder} and \path{Wrap}. The two \path{sorry} declarations
in \path{eth/Challenge.lean} are intentionally unproved comparison
statements. The solution does not import that challenge file.

The pinned checkout contains $1{,}052$ Lean source files. A static scan
found the two intentional challenge placeholders and no other literal
\path{sorry}/\path{admit} declaration. That scan is not a kernel proof
check. The README reports the build, axiom listing with
\path{propext}, \path{Classical.choice}, and \path{Quot.sound}, and
the comparator/nanoda workflow. We inspected the source and entry points;
we did not independently rebuild the development or reproduce those
reported kernel checks in this session.

The supplied 49-page PDF is not byte-identical to the earlier 49-page
\path{eth.pdf} in ProveIt. Both hashes are retained in the source ledger.
The algorithmic order in \eqref{eq:anth-main} agrees with the previous
manuscript's stated order. Publication of the development is an important
change in what can be inspected, rather than a new smaller asymptotic
order to beat.

\subsection{Why its constants cannot simply replace wire constants}

The solution comments explicitly distinguish intermediate counted
operations from final Turing-machine steps. The simulation can multiply
the intermediate exponent by an unspecified constant, absorbed in the
big-$O$ theorem. Consequently an internal clique coefficient or rank
ratio is not a numerical competitor to the Cauchy contractor's
$C\,s/\sqrt{\log_2s}$ bit-time coefficient.

The two algorithms nevertheless suggest a useful research comparison.
The OpenAI construction organizes a weighted Cauchy mode sum, whereas the
Anthropic construction rewrites crossings with operators on a graded
polynomial space and then selects a contraction direction. Both currently
lead to a square-root logarithm. Section~\ref{sec:order-research} and its accompanying research
notes test whether their structures can be combined without hiding entry
costs or paying the gain back in a different parameter.
