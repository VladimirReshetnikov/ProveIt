\section{Attempts to change the logarithmic order}
\label{sec:order-research}

The numerical improvement is useful, but the requested larger goal is a
smaller order than $n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$. We investigated the
following routes and retain their precise status. None of the arguments
below proves a lower bound for SAT.

\subsection{What is already ruled out by the reviewed certificates}

Changing the Hermite taper alone is not a new route. The earlier precision
packet solves the real taper minimax problem, and the newest rectangular
packet strengthens this to arbitrary asymmetric node multiplicities under
independent jet-error recovery. Its leading precision coefficient remains
six. Similarly, full-block and actual-domain Cauchy/Smith separation is
already in the corpus. Merely rediscovering the slope-four mode list is
not an improvement.

The conserved degree-flow identity in the newest adaptive-frontier packet
is useful: incoming mode powers remove the degree allowance that outgoing
mode powers add elsewhere. But a whole-group coefficient extraction can
still be expensive. The same packet explicitly distinguishes formal
degree conservation from an admissible recursive entry algorithm. A short
binary rank for a mode tuple similarly does not make a high-degree group
tensor cheap to evaluate.

\subsection{Why straightforward composition of the two engines cancels}

Here is a quantitative obstruction for the displayed certificates, not
for all possible algorithms. Suppose an OpenAI packing schedule obeys
\[
 u\log_2 r\le B\log_2s,
\]
and its certified outer mode-bit size is $A M/\sqrt u$. Suppose the
Anthropic clique certificate applied to $r$ groups has coefficient at
least $a/\sqrt{\log_2r}$, as does the current square-root family up to
its fixed constants. Their composed \emph{certificate expression} is
at least
\[
 \frac{aAM}{\sqrt{u\log_2r}}
 \ge\frac{aA}{\sqrt B}\frac{M}{\sqrt{\log_2s}}.
\]
Thus allowing $r$ to grow, while retaining these two separate estimates,
does not yield a smaller order. It loses $\sqrt{\log r}$ in packing and
gains the same factor in clique evaluation. This argument is about those
upper-bound expressions; it does not lower-bound their algorithms' true
running time or exclude a joint analysis that avoids one of the charges.

There is a related exact calculation for rectangular grids in the
Anthropic construction. Its two intermediate partition-space envelopes
are proportional to $\sqrt{2h+t}$ and $\sqrt{2h-t}$. If the two recursive
directions have rates $e_1,e_2$, then
\[
 \max_{-2h\le t\le2h}
 \min\{e_1\sqrt{2h+t},e_2\sqrt{2h-t}\}
 =\frac{2\sqrt h}{\sqrt{e_1^{-2}+e_2^{-2}}}.
\]
The maximum occurs where the two terms agree. After normalization, the
combined rate is $(e_1^{-2}+e_2^{-2})^{-1/2}$. If
$e(K)=C/\sqrt{\log K}$ and the grid combines sizes as
$K=K_1K_2$, this is exactly the same rate $C/\sqrt{\log K}$. Unequal
grid sides alone therefore do not change the power within this envelope.

\subsection{A more concrete new route: compile the actual piece ranks}

The Anthropic proof bounds the ranks of a completed crossing piece by the
dimensions of intermediate Fock spaces. The completed matrix could in
principle have smaller rank after cancellations. Rather than changing the
Heisenberg algebra or its grading, one can ask directly for a uniform
rank theorem for those matrices. The accompanying
\path{research/MODULAR_RANK_CRITERION.md} supplies the full statement and
proof obligations; we give the central lemma here.

Let $N=2^\ell$ and suppose polynomially many integral arrays
$A_i(x,y,u,v)$ satisfy
\[
 \sum_i A_i(x,y,u,v)=d_\ell[x=v][y=u],
\]
with polynomial coefficient bit lengths and $2^{O(\ell)}$ entry cost.
For a prime $p\nmid d_\ell$, flatten a piece in the row direction
$(x,y)\mid(u,v)$ and the column direction $(x,u)\mid(y,v)$ over $\F_p$.
Denote their ranks by $r_i(p),c_i(p)$.

\begin{lemma}[Modular rank compilation]\label{lem:modrank}
Both exact factorizations of a piece can be constructed and verified in
$2^{O(\ell)}\poly(\log p)$ bit operations and space, using respectively
\[
 \lceil\log_2\max(1,r_i(p))\rceil,
 \qquad \lceil\log_2\max(1,c_i(p))\rceil
\]
shared Boolean bits. Every factor coefficient has at most
$\lceil\log_2p\rceil$ bits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each flattening is an $N^2$ by $N^2$ matrix. Gaussian elimination over
$\F_p$ takes $O(N^6)$ field operations. Its pivot columns and the
corresponding reduced-row coefficients give an exact rank factorization
$UV$. Pad the inner dimension to the next power of two and encode it by
Boolean bits. Zero rank uses one zero term and no index bits. Multiplying
$U,V$ verifies the factorization within the same time bound. Field
arithmetic has polynomial bit cost in $\log p$. Reduction preserves the
crossing identity and $p\nmid d_\ell$ permits its normalization.
\end{proof}

This modular formulation avoids a real height gap. A generic rational
Gaussian elimination proof only gives minor determinants with
$2^{O(\ell)}$ bits; it does not automatically meet the source's
polynomial coefficient-bit requirement. Modular factor coefficients
remain small. The lemma is not a polynomial-space claim in $\ell$.

The substantive \emph{open} target is, for some fixed
$1/2\le\rho<1/\sqrt2$, the uniform inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-target}
 \lceil\log_2\max(1,r_i(p))\rceil+
 \lceil\log_2\max(1,c_i(p))\rceil
 \le4\rho\ell+O(\log(2\ell)).
\end{equation}
It must hold for every needed piece and for a polynomially constructible
family of good primes with the signed-reconstruction size specified below. For the original
normalizer $d_\ell=h!$, taking primes above every reached $h$ avoids
division problems. The number of pieces must remain polynomial in
$\ell$, and the constants must be uniform; a few favorable pieces or a
single exceptional prime are not sufficient.

A rational-rank upper bound is especially useful: every larger minor is
an identically zero integer, hence vanishes modulo every prime. Thus one
may prove the rank bound over $\Q$ and construct the small-coefficient
factors over finite fields. A concrete intermediate target is
\[
 \max(1,\operatorname{rank}_{\Q}R_i)
 \max(1,\operatorname{rank}_{\Q}C_i)
 \le 2^{(8/3)\ell}\ell^{O(1)}
\]
uniformly for all pieces. This would give $\rho=2/3$ and denominator
power $\gamma=\log_2(3/2)=0.5849625\ldots>1/2$.

If \eqref{eq:rank-target} and the stated uniform interfaces are
established, the source recurrence gives
$\gamma=\log_2(1/\rho)>1/2$ and the corresponding fixed-width SAT
exponent $O_k(n\log\log n/(\log n)^\gamma)$.
There is an essential arithmetic detail: Appendix D uses truncation
congruences modulo powers of two. It cannot simply be rerun in odd
characteristic and followed by a CRT of the final count.

Instead supply it with an exact integer clique oracle. For a
$(K,\ell)$-clique with local weights satisfying $|W_v|<2^B$, its value
$Z$ obeys
\[
 |Z|\le 2^E,\qquad E=\binom K2\ell+KB.
\]
Compute the requested clique value modulo good primes whose product is
strictly greater than $2^{E+1}$, then reconstruct its signed integer value.
The original two-adic truncation and rational comparisons now run
unchanged. It is enough to reconstruct each clique value requested by the
outer reduction; internal recursive modular values need no such recovery.
The source's parameter bounds make $K,\ell,B$, and hence $E$, polynomial
in the original input size. Polynomially many polynomial-size primes
suffice and do not change the exponential order. The direct clause-count
branch, which counts directly modulo each prime, needs only the final
nonnegative count bound $2^n$.

The rank theorem is still missing. This is a \emph{conditional route}, not
a newly proved SAT bound. The accompanying note states the piece-count,
normalization, construction-time and coefficient-height obligations in
full so that a rank conjecture cannot silently replace an algorithm.

\subsection{Finite probes and their limitations}

Exact modular probes of the actual Boson pieces did not reveal a uniform
collapse. For example, central-piece row/column ranks over $\F_{1009}$
were $(25,25)$ for the full weight-$4$ sector, $(49,49)$ for weight $5$,
and $(225,225)$ for weight $7$. Their dimensions are respectively
$5^2$, $7^2$, and $15^2$, so these are full-rank finite witnesses.
Full modular rank also proves full rational rank for those particular
integer matrices. At these sizes the source's partition-dimension bound
has not entered its asymptotically useful range, so the observations
neither prove nor refute \eqref{eq:rank-target}.

The standalone rank compiler was separately checked on exact factorizations,
crossing identities, exceptional-prime controls, and CRT reconstruction.
An explicit negative control uses matrix-unit crossing pieces with ideal
individual ranks but exponentially many pieces; it fails the source
interface and cannot be promoted to a faster SAT theorem.

Borchardt's Cauchy-permanent identity is another possible algebraic tool,
but applying it only to one packet would speed a subdominant local routine.
A useful smaller-order result would still require a closed group-level
representation with paid entry evaluation. We did not establish that
representation or use this idea to claim an improved exponent.
