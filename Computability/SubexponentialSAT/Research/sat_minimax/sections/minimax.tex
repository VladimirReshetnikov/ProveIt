\section{An exact minimax theorem for a finite menu}
\label{sec:minimax}

The central new tool is a characterization of the exact worst-case cost of
a fixed finite menu of contraction plans. Earlier support certificates
average the plans with equal weights. That is a valid upper bound, but
need not be the optimum certificate for the same menu.

\begin{theorem}[Exact support-hull identity]\label{thm:hull}
Let $\mathcal V_1,\ldots,\mathcal V_q$ be nonempty finite subsets of
$\R_{\ge0}^{E}$, where $E$ is finite. Define
\[
 F(g)=\min_{1\le j\le q}\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}v\cdot g,
 \qquad
 \Lambda=\max_{v_j\in\mathcal V_j}
        \dist\bigl(0,\conv\{v_1,\ldots,v_q\}\bigr).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hull-exact}
 \max_{g\ge0,\,\|g\|_2\le1}F(g)=\Lambda.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
 \Lambda^2=\max_{v_j\in\mathcal V_j}
       \min_{p\ge0,\,\sum_jp_j=1}p^TGp,
 \qquad G_{ij}=v_i\cdot v_j.
\]
For rational supports, $\Lambda^2$ is rational and admits a finite exact
certificate consisting of rational convex weights and a projection witness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $g\ge0$ with $\|g\|_2\le1$. Select independently for each plan a
support $v_j$ attaining its maximum. If $w$ is the point of the convex
hull of these supports closest to zero, then
\[
 F(g)=\min_jv_j\cdot g\le w\cdot g\le\|w\|_2\le\Lambda.
\]
Conversely, choose a support tuple attaining $\Lambda$ and its closest
point $w$. The projection condition gives
$(v_j-w)\cdot w\ge0$ for every $j$. If $w=0$, the reverse inequality
is automatic. Otherwise take $g=w/\|w\|_2$. Nonnegativity of the
supports makes $g$ feasible, and
\[
 \max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}v\cdot g
 \ge v_j\cdot g\ge\|w\|_2=\Lambda
 \quad\hbox{for every }j.
\]
This proves equality. The Gram expression is the definition of the
distance to a convex hull.

The closest point lies in the relative interior of a simplex spanned by an
affinely independent subset of the rational vertices. Its affine projection solves a rational
linear system, so its coordinates and squared norm are rational. One can
enumerate faces to find it. The conditions below give an exact verification
that does not depend on numerical optimization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A small exact certificate}

For a proposed nonempty active set $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}$, solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hull-kkt}
 \begin{pmatrix}G_{AA}&-\mathbf1\\\mathbf1^T&0\end{pmatrix}
 \binom{p_A}{t}=\binom{0}{1}.
\end{equation}
Use $p_j=0$ off $A$. A solution certifies a closest point if
\[
 p\ge0,\quad \mathbf1^Tp=1,\quad Gp\ge t\mathbf1,
 \quad (Gp)_j=t\ \ (p_j>0).
\]
Indeed $p^TGp=t$, and for any other simplex vector $z$,
\[
 z^TGz=p^TGp+2(z-p)^TGp+(z-p)^TG(z-p)\ge t.
\]
The last term is nonnegative because $G$ is a Gram matrix. Singular active sets
can be replaced by an affinely independent subset representing the same
closest point. All checks are rational equalities or inequalities.

For an upper certificate it is even simpler: \emph{any} rational simplex
vector $p$ with $p^TGp\le B$ proves that tuple's distance squared is at
most $B$. A projection witness attaining $B$ proves sharpness. Our
checker first uses $p_j=1/q$. Only tuples for which the equal-average
bound exceeds the candidate threshold require the more expensive exact
face calculation. This makes exhaustive certification practical.

\subsection{Application to contraction plans}

Combine opposite directed entropies as
$g_{\{a,b\}}=h_{ab}+h_{ba}$. Suppose every support of plan $j$ is an
integer vector $v$ and its operation exponent is
$d^{-1}\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}v\cdot g$. If $R=\Lambda^2$ is the
certificate from Theorem~\ref{thm:hull}, its local normalized coefficient is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-hull}
 \beta_r=\frac{\sqrt{2R}}{dr}.
\end{equation}
To see this without assuming uniformity over infinitely many test vectors,
fix one support tuple and its rational closest combination $w$. Give both
orientations of each pair weight $w_e/d$. The squared directed norm is
$2\|w\|_2^2/d^2$. Apply the inherited weighted entropy estimate to this
vector. There are finitely many tuples, so its lower-order error is uniform
over every vector used in this argument. Select the plan minimizing its
actual operation exponent.

The former equal-average certificate is recovered by always taking
$p_j=1/q$. Thus the new theorem is never weaker for a fixed menu. It is
strictly stronger when the worst applicable tuple has a closer nonuniform
convex combination. It also supplies a matching worst-case entropy vector,
so one can distinguish a poor proof of a menu from a menu that must actually
be enlarged or changed.

\paragraph{Scope of sharpness.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:hull-exact} is exact for the nonnegative Euclidean
entropy relaxation. It does not assert that every maximizing vector $g$
comes from the exact weight lists of an actual Cauchy contraction. It is
therefore a sharp finite certificate for the stated model, not a lower bound
for SAT, for all contraction algorithms, or for the true runtime of a plan.

\paragraph{Exact online selection.}
Let $D_{ab}=|\mathcal I_{ab}|\,|\mathcal I_{ba}|$. For each plan compute
\[
 P_j=\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}\prod_{\{a,b\}}D_{ab}^{v_{ab}}.
\]
The plan minimizing $P_j$ also minimizes the real operation exponent.
All dimensions are positive integers; empty-list allocations contribute
zero and are skipped. The fixed supports have constant integer entries,
and the dimension counts have polynomial bit length, so this selection
has polynomial bit cost and requires no floating-point comparisons.
