\section{The complete recursive time and space bound}\label{sec:schedule}

A local operation bound alone does not prove a faster contractor. Every
singleton cache entry is itself a recursive contraction. With
\(\alpha_r=\sqrt{2(r-1)}/r\), the inherited entry-count estimate and a
local plan coefficient \(\beta_r\) give the paid recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paid}
 T_d\le\poly(s)
 \left[
    2^{(\alpha_{r_d}+o(1))K_d}T_{d+1}
    +2^{(\beta_{r_d}+o(1))K_d}
 \right],
 \qquad K_d=\kappa M_d/\sqrt{u_d}.
\end{equation}
We retain the already certified rational schedule from the newest
pairwise packet and improve its root and depth-one local operation bounds.

\subsection{Integer parameters and packing}

Let
\[
 \lambda=\lceil\log_2s\rceil,\qquad
 b=\lceil6\log_2\lambda\rceil,\qquad
 u_d=\lfloor\eta_d\lambda\rfloor,\qquad
 a_d=\sum_{j=0}^d(u_j+b).
\]
Choose
\begin{align}
 r_0&=10,&\eta_0&=\frac{301}{20000},\notag\\
 r_1&=13,&\eta_1&=\frac{119}{10000},\notag\\
 r_d&=7\quad(d\ge2),&
 \eta_d&=\frac{1549}{324000}
                 \left(\frac49\right)^{d-2}.\label{eq:schedule}
\end{align}
The cumulative fractions satisfy
\[
 \eta_0+\eta_1=\frac{539}{20000},\qquad
 \sum_{d\ge0}\eta_d=\frac8{225}.
\]
Stop at \(H=\lceil s/\lambda^{3/4}\rceil\) wires and evaluate directly.
Use relative packing slack \(\lambda^{-3}\).
Bounded exceptional sizes are handled by exhaustive contraction after
checking the parameter inequalities.

At depth one use the preceding three thirteen-group plans with our
anchored backend; in the tail retain the preceding certificate
\(\beta_7^2=632/1225\) \cite{trees,pairwise}.
The literal depth-one partitions are
\[
\begin{array}{l}
(0,1,2,3,4)\mid(5,6,7,8)\mid(9,10,11,12),\\
(12,10,1,6,4)\mid(9,0,3,8)\mid(5,2,7,11),\\
(0,8,11,12,1)\mid(7,10,4,3)\mid(5,2,6,9),
\end{array}
\]
and the tail partition is
\((0,1,2)\mid(3,4)\mid(5,6)\).
Each depth-one plan has ten internal merges and one final triangle,
giving \(11\cdot12=132\) supports at the same integer scale
\(\mathsf L\). Exhaustively evaluating all \(132^3=2299968\) triples
gives the exact maximum squared norm
\[
 B_{13}=688874649739825981703779802627980038.
\]
The maximizing operation is the final triangle in each plan, with
coefficient triples \((1,c,c),(c,1,c),(c,1,c)\).
Its squared norm before integer scaling is
\(90+108c+198c^2\). Lemma~\ref{lem:norm}, now with \(q=3,r=13\),
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta13}
 \beta_{13}^2=\frac{2B_{13}}{\mathsf L^2\,9\,169}
  =\frac{20+24c+44c^2}{169}
  =\frac{57151369949899}{169000000000000}.
\end{equation}
Two independently implemented generators and enumerations reproduce
this maximum. Their support indices differ because their traversals
differ; the underlying three vectors agree. The tail's older matrix
backend remains sufficient. Different fixed matrix algorithms can be
used at different recursion levels.

For clarity, here are the strict degree gates that make the schedule
valid. The exact integer inequalities
\[
 10^{1000}<2^{3322},\qquad
 13^{1000}<2^{3701},\qquad
 7^{100}<2^{281}
\]
bound the logarithms rationally. The dominant root and retained-side
exponents have positive gaps:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Stage & Exponent upper bound & Gap below \(1\)\\
\midrule
Root & \(9/10+2\eta_0(3322/1000)\) & \(39/5000000\)\\
Depth one & \(9/10+(539/20000)(3701/1000)\) & \(5161/20000000\)\\
Tail & \(9/10+(8/225)(281/100)\) & \(1/11250\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The fresh-side and packet-rounding exponents are also bounded away from
one, for example by \((8/225)(1+2(3701/1000))<1\).
These fixed gaps absorb the buffers, polylogarithmic losses, and
\(s/M\le\lambda^{3/4}\).

To avoid assuming the recursion depth in proving the gates, provisionally
check them through \(3\log_3\lambda/4+O(1)\) levels.
In this range the smallest taper is at least
\[
 \lambda^{\delta+o(1)},\qquad
 \delta=1-\frac{3\ln(9/4)}{4\ln3}>0.
\]
Nearly lossless packing then makes each tail child smaller than \(M/3\)
eventually. An active path cannot exceed the provisional bound.
Thus the argument closes inductively. Floors are negligible uniformly;
padding errors multiply to \(1+o(1)\) over the logarithmic depth.
More precisely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:masses}
 M_0\le s,\qquad
 M_1\le(1+o(1))s/5,\qquad
 M_d\le(1+o(1))\frac{2s}{65}
             \left(\frac27\right)^{d-2}\quad(d\ge2).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Precision, entry evaluation, and auxiliary work}

At a retained valuation, \(D_i\ge2^{a_{d-1}+b+1}\). The inherited
creation-time precision is \(O(s\lambda^2 2^{a_{d-1}})\).
Relative to \(MD_i/u_d\), it is
\[
 O(\lambda^{15/4}2^{-b})=o(1).
\]
Hence inherited precision is absorbed in the sharp local precision
estimate at every active depth.
Since \(a_d\le(8/225)\lambda+O((\log\lambda)^2)\), all precisions
have polynomial bit length.

Original entry routines cost \(2^{O(s^{9/10})}\) times a polynomial.
New packet routines cost \(\exp(s^{8/225+o(1)})\).
Direct leaf work costs \(2^{O(s/\lambda^{3/4})}\).
Each is \(2^{o(s/\sqrt\lambda)}\).
Immutable descriptions prevent recursive expansion of entry routines.
Polynomial overhead at \(O(\log\lambda)\) levels contributes only
\(O(\lambda\log\lambda)\) to the logarithm of the running time.
The new support menu and its selector are fixed finite data; their
construction does not add a size-dependent exponential term.

\subsection{Unrolling every branch}

On the scale \(s/\sqrt\lambda\), set
\[
 K_0^*=\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{\eta_0}},\qquad
 K_1^*=\frac{\kappa}{5\sqrt{\eta_1}},\qquad
 K_2^*=\frac{2\kappa}{65\sqrt{1549/324000}}.
\]
In the tail the ratio is
\(\gamma=(2/7)/\sqrt{4/9}=3/7\).
Moreover
\(\alpha_7/(1-\gamma)=\sqrt3/2>\beta_7\), so the complete tail
branch dominates any tail local termination.
Unrolling \eqref{eq:paid} therefore gives the maximum of
\begin{align}
 C_0&=\sqrt{B_*}\,K_0^*=C_*=\NewC\ldots,\label{eq:c0}\\
 C_1&=\alpha_{10}K_0^*+\beta_{13}K_1^*
   =\frac\pi{\ln2}\left(
        \sqrt{\frac{7200}{301}}+
        \sqrt{\frac{800\beta_{13}^2}{119}}\right)
   =29.0008821742914\ldots,\label{eq:c1}\\
 C_\infty&=\alpha_{10}K_0^*+\alpha_{13}K_1^*
       +\frac{\alpha_7K_2^*}{1-\gamma}\notag\\
   &=\frac\pi{\ln2}\left(
        \sqrt{\frac{7200}{301}}+
        \sqrt{\frac{19200}{20111}}+
        \sqrt{\frac{77760}{261781}}\right)
   =29.0657485246412\ldots.\label{eq:cinf}
\end{align}
The exact rational interval checker proves \(C_0>C_1,C_\infty\) and
\[
 29.1489213688721<C_0<29.1489213688722<29.149.
\]
It bounds \(\pi\) by alternating arctangent series, \(\ln2\) by its
positive series at \(1/3\), and square roots by integer squares.
No floating-point comparison is used in acceptance.

Local cache sizes are bounded by
\(\sqrt{B_*}K_0^*,\beta_{13}K_1^*,\beta_7K_2^*\).
The root is largest. Tables on an active ancestor path coexist, but their
sizes add; their leading exponent is the maximum, not the sum.
The fixed matrix recursion uses polynomial overhead relative to its
largest arrays. This proves the common time and space coefficient in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\subsection{Consequences for 3-SAT}

The direct bounded-density clause network and the sparse polynomial-query
reduction both retain their exact counting semantics.
For the latter, the original retained incidence degree is \(O(n)\),
but its compressed Boolean-wire count is \(O(n\log\log n)\).
Substituting the smaller contractor coefficient therefore improves the
same normalized contraction guarantee inside that reduction.
It does not turn \(s\) into \(n\) with constant one.
An explicit per-variable numerical coefficient would require all encoding
constants and outer costs to be made explicit under the same normalization.

The new cached branch can coexist with the former polynomial-space and
lower-space branches. A user who imposes a memory limit should select
the applicable resource guarantee; the lowest time coefficient alone
does not determine the best implementation.
