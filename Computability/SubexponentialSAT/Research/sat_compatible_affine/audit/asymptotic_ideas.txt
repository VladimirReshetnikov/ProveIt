# Attempts to change the asymptotic order for 3-SAT

Snapshot: `VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt@8e76d515063c4b9fbd8743a846b457e3a01bc039`.
This is an internal research note for the current iteration. No improved
unrestricted 3-SAT bound is claimed here. The positive coefficient improvement in the final report is separate
from the unsuccessful order-changing attempts documented in this note.

## 1. The baseline and attempts already covered in the corpus

The imported Boolean tensor theorem is
`lib/openai-math/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-reduction.tex`,
Theorem `thm:tensor-contraction`. On a network of size `s` with maximum
degree at most `ceil(s^(9/10))`, suitable polynomial descriptions, and
the stated ring and entry-evaluation conditions, it gives
`poly(s+L) 2^{O(s/sqrt(log s))}` time and polynomial space.

The arbitrary-density 3-SAT reduction in the same file uses
`s=Theta(n loglog n)`, yielding
`poly(n,m) 2^{O(n loglog n/sqrt(log n))}`. The strongest existing
density-dependent exponent is
`O(n min(log(2+m/n),loglog n)/sqrt(log n))`; see
`Research/sat_density_improvement/density_aware_sat_improvement.tex`,
Theorem `thm:main`. Its uniform sparse-query lemma keeps the actual
monomial sparsity rather than discarding it.

The existing files already address several tempting routes, so the
following cannot honestly be presented as newly proved order improvements:

* `Research/3sat_asymptotic_analysis/research_runtime/METHOD_BARRIERS.md`,
  section 3, explains why a direct second contraction of the encoded mode
  network violates the degree/oracle interface. Letting its group number
  `r` grow trades first-stage taper depth `O(log M/log r)` for second-stage
  packing depth `O(log r)`; in that model the two square-root factors cancel.
* Sections 1 and 2 of that file give restricted mode-enumeration and
  Hermite-taper barriers. These do not lower-bound all possible algorithms.
* Section 4 already optimizes an optimistic sparsification/density cost
  model `n[sigma+c log(1/sigma)/sqrt(log n)]`; its best scale still contains
  `loglog n`.
* `Research/3sat_asymptotic_analysis/research_decision/findings.md`,
  sections 7 and 8, already discuss a decision-only obstruction to
  selecting a small subformula and randomized extension-field norm
  compression. The latter supplies a valid one-equation encoding, but
  not the necessary low-cost coefficient oracle or low-wire recursive
  decomposition.
* `Research/3sat_asymptotic_analysis/research_runtime/CONDITIONAL_DEGREE_KERNEL.tex`
  states the missing degree-preserving compressed-kernel interface
  explicitly. It also explains why low incidence degree does not transfer
  to the Boolean degree of a binary subset index.

I explored changing the residue characteristic and a shared original-variable
deformation. The following two local lemmas make the obstructions to the
most direct versions precise. Neither is a barrier to all p-adic or
original-variable algorithms.

## 2. A prime-cycle localization obstruction

### Lemma (two good shifts force constancy)

Let `p` be prime, let `A` be any set of group labels, and let
`R,C : Z/pZ -> A` label row and column positions in one cyclic digit.
Call a shift `d` good if

`R(t)=C(t+d)` for every `t in Z/pZ`.

If two distinct shifts `d,e` are good, both `R` and `C` are constant,
with the same value. Consequently, for a nonconstant pair `(R,C)`,
at most one nonzero shift is good.

### Complete proof

Suppose `d != e` are good. For every `t`,

`C(t+d)=R(t)=C(t+e)`.

Put `s=t+d` and `delta=e-d`. Then `C(s)=C(s+delta)` for every `s`.
Because `p` is prime and `delta != 0`, the elements
`s,s+delta,...,s+(p-1)delta` are all of `Z/pZ`.
Iterating the equality therefore shows that `C` has the same value
everywhere. The equality `R(t)=C(t+d)` makes `R` the same constant.
This proves the lemma.

### Why binary swapping is exceptional for this construction

For `p=2`, there is only one nonzero shift. Thus a nonconstant pair can
make every nonzero shift good: choose `C(0)=a,C(1)=b`, with `a != b`,
and `R(t)=C(t+1)`. For an odd prime, retaining every nonzero shift would
retain at least two, forcing constancy by the lemma. A nonconstant
cyclic-coordinate construction can retain at most a `1/(p-1)` fraction
of the nonzero residues as good shifts.

The source's `quantitative-contraction.tex`, subsection
`subsec:tensor-modes`, uses the fact that adding an integer of valuation
`i` flips binary bit `i`. All odd unit residues have that same effect on
the binary digit, so the unordered-pair packet labeling makes `W(t)`
local for every retained node at that valuation. The lemma shows why
replacing the binary digit by one cyclic digit modulo an odd prime does
not preserve that feature with nontrivial row/column groups.

This is relevant to the tempting observation that a larger counting
prime reduces the number of p-adic precision digits. The observation
does not by itself provide the required locality or mode valuation
estimate. The lemma identifies a missing geometry; it does not show
that all odd-characteristic approaches are impossible.

### Sharpness and limitations

One good shift is always attainable with arbitrary nonconstant `C`:
fix `d0` and define `R(t)=C(t+d0)`. The prime assumption is necessary.
For a composite cycle, invariance under `e-d` only makes `C` constant
on cosets of the subgroup generated by that difference.

More generally, for a finite abelian group `G`, if a nonempty set `D`
of shifts is good, then `C` is constant on cosets of
`H=<d-e : d,e in D>`. Conversely, if `C` is constant on those cosets
and `R(t)=C(t+d0)` for one `d0 in D`, all shifts in `D` are good.
Proof: the two-shift equality gives invariance under each generator
and hence under their generated subgroup; conversely every
`d-d0` lies in `H`. This permits constructions on vector spaces and
other packet geometries; the cyclic prime lemma does not exclude them.

An independent exhaustive finite check over two group labels found:

| p | Pairs making all nonzero shifts good | Nonconstant pairs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2 | 4 | 2 |
| 3 | 2 | 0 |
| 5 | 2 | 0 |
| 7 | 2 | 0 |

These finite counts illustrate the proof; they are not its justification.

## 3. Original-variable coherence obstruction

### Lemma (locality labels propagate along incidence components)

Let `V` be a finite set of variables, let `F` be a finite collection
of factors with nonempty scopes `I_f subset V`, and let `G_inc` be
their bipartite incidence graph. Suppose there are maps

`g : V -> [r]`, `sigma : F -> [r]`

such that `g(v)=sigma(f)` for every incidence `v in I_f`.
Then all variable and factor vertices in each connected component
of `G_inc` have the same label.

### Complete proof

For every edge `(v,f)`, the two endpoint labels are equal by
hypothesis. Along any path, consecutive equalities imply equality
of the labels at its two endpoints. In a connected component every
two vertices are joined by a path, hence all their labels agree.

### Linear-form corollary

Let a vector space of target coordinates be decomposed as a direct sum
`E=E_1 direct-sum ... direct-sum E_r`. Assign each original variable
`v` a nonzero linear form/vector `ell_v in E`, shared by all its
occurrences. Suppose each factor is assigned a group `sigma(f)` and
locality is enforced by the structural condition

`ell_v in E_{sigma(f)} for every v in I_f`.

Then all factors in each connected incidence component are assigned
to one group. If, additionally, the `ell_v` belonging to that component
are linearly independent, that target group has dimension at least the
number of variables in the component.

### Complete proof of the corollary

If `v` occurs in factors assigned to distinct groups `a,b`, then
`ell_v` belongs to `E_a intersect E_b={0}`, contradicting nonzeroness.
Thus all factors incident to a variable have the same group, which
defines `g(v)` for every occurring variable. The lemma propagates
the label through each incidence component. All its `ell_v` therefore
belong to a single subspace `E_g`. Linear independence of `k` such
vectors implies `dim E_g >= k`, proving the dimension assertion.
Unused variables are isolated and may be handled separately.

### Scope of the obstruction

The corollary obstructs a particularly simple attempt to avoid
incidence expansion: substitute the same nonzero linear form for
every occurrence of an original variable and force each whole factor
to be local without additional measurements. For a connected formula,
this cannot distribute factors among several independent groups.
If the substitution also preserves independent variable directions,
it cannot achieve dimensional shrinkage in that one group.

Its assumptions deliberately exclude possible escapes:

* occurrence-dependent substitutions;
* rank-deficient substitutions or variables whose images vanish at a node;
* cancellations internal to a specific factor that make its actual
  evaluated support smaller than the structural scope bound;
* fixing shared measurements, boundary variables, or correction modes;
* a different representation whose factor-incidence graph changes.

The source's original-variable machinery in
`main_full_version.tex`, Definition/subsection around
`subsec:measurement-factorization`, explicitly uses measurements to
avoid this obstruction. Its locality condition says the factor is
constant on kernels of measurement maps outside its assigned group;
it does not assert that every substituted variable form is supported
entirely in that group. Appendix D instead uses occurrence wires and
crossing modes. The lemma does not refute either construction.

## 4. What remains a plausible order-changing target

A useful new kernel would have to retain a small number of independent
original-variable directions while exposing shared measurements whose
description, precision and recursive cost remain below the incidence
entropy `Theta(n loglog n)`. The existing conditional degree interface
would be sufficient, but it is not supplied by a binary encoding of
incidence subsets, nor by the two direct modifications above.

For an odd-prime route, one would need an explicitly different
localization design, enough interpolation conditions after the good-node
restriction, a correct p-adic jet-recovery loss, an integral separated
crossing kernel, and recursive closure. A reduced initial precision
alone does not establish a faster algorithm.

For a decision-only route, randomized isolation or extension-field
hashing may change output recovery, but the currently missing
coefficient oracle and recursive representation remain necessary.
No such lemma was proved in this workstream, and no smaller
worst-case asymptotic order is asserted.
