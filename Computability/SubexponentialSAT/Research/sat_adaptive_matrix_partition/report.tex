\begin{abstract}
We review the fifteen filed research packets and five newer incoming archives
in ProveIt at commit \texttt{fd0edd17e6a2}. The newest archives, which are
essential to the comparison, lower the best explicit general-degree
normalized-wire time coefficient to $35.3545880584$ with subexponential
space, and to $55.7094783189$ with polynomial space. We obtain two further
improvements by choosing group partitions from the actual exact-weight mode
list sizes. The principal cached construction has time coefficient
$34.3252827973$ and space coefficient $34.0241780075$. An adaptive streamed
separator construction has polynomial space and time coefficient
$50.2104348364$. Each coefficient multiplies $s/\sqrt{\log_2s}$ in a
base-two exponent, with $s$ the normalized Boolean wire budget.

The new finite combinatorial inequalities are proved by exact first and
second moments of partitions, and then composed with the existing algebraic
contraction interfaces. The full cost analysis pays for recursive group
entries, table construction, arithmetic over the actual coefficient ring,
and inherited precision. The resulting explicit bound functions are
asymptotically smaller than the comparable prior certificates. The
logarithmic order of the SAT exponent remains unchanged: this report does
not establish an $n/\log n$-type algorithm or a smaller unrestricted
$n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ order. The archive contains mathematical
proofs, exact finite verifiers, a nine-factor contraction prototype,
source inventories, and an independent review; it contains no complete
SAT solver or new proof-assistant formalization.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The common normalized interface}

Let $s\ge2$, $\lambda=\ceil{\log_2s}$, and let $\mathcal L$ be the total
graph and tensor-description length. The input is a bipartite Boolean
tensor network with at most $s$ wires, polynomially many vertices, and
maximum degree at most $\ceil{s^{9/10}}$. Its entries belong to
\[
 R_0=(\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell),\qquad
 1\le P_0,\ell\le s.
\]
For fixed constants, each initial entry is computable in
$\poly(s+\mathcal L)2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ time and polynomial space.
The constants implicit in these premises are fixed uniformly over the
input family. This is the normalization used by the comparison packets
\cite{contraction,tree}.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive cached contraction]
\label{thm:main}
Under this interface and the inherited exact identities specified in
Section~\ref{sec:interfaces}, the contraction can be computed deterministically
in time and space respectively
\begin{align}
 T(s,\mathcal L)&\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(\CT+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda},\label{eq:main-time}\\
 S(s,\mathcal L)&\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(\CS+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda},\label{eq:main-space}
\end{align}
where
\begin{align*}
 \kappa&=\frac{\pi\sqrt{40\log_2 9}}{\ln2},\\
 \CT&=\kappa\frac{\sqrt{916}}{45}
      =34.32528279728494\ldots,\\
 \CS&=\frac23\kappa=34.02417800749051\ldots.
\end{align*}
In particular, $34.33$ and $34.03$ are valid eventual coefficients.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive streamed contraction]
\label{thm:polyspace}
Under the same interface, an alternative deterministic construction uses
polynomial space and time
\[
 (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{(\CPS+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda},\qquad
 \CPS=\frac{\pi}{\ln2}\sqrt{\frac{3625}{42}}
       \left(1+\frac1{3\sqrt3}\right)
       =50.2104348363\ldots.
\]
Thus $50.22$ is an eventual polynomial-space coefficient.
\end{theorem}

Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:polyspace} improve the corresponding
strongest explicit general-degree certificates found in the twenty packets.
They are not claims that every input takes that much time, nor comparisons
between measured implementations.
For example, writing $f(s)=s/\sqrt{\log_2s}$, the ratio of the new and old
fastest \emph{displayed exponential certificate functions} is
\[
 2^{-(1.0293052611\ldots+o(1))f(s)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Polynomial factors contribute $O(\log s)=o(f(s))$ for polynomial-size
descriptions. Thus a smaller coefficient here is a strict asymptotic
improvement in an explicit upper bound, even though its logarithmic scale
remains $f(s)$.

\subsection{What remains unresolved}

For SAT variables $n$ and clauses $m$, the strongest established order
claimed by the reviewed framework is still
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sat-envelope}
 \poly_k(n,m)\,
 2^{O_k\!\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
       \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}.
\end{equation}
The density-aware packets supply this envelope \cite{density}. The new
contraction routines can replace the old engine under the same encodings;
they do not change the wire budget that produces the $\log\log n$ factor.
The conditional target $2^{O(n\log\log n/\log n)}$ in the precision packet
requires a structured summation algorithm that is still missing
\cite{precision}. We have not established that target or a faster order.
No numerical coefficient per SAT variable follows from a coefficient per
normalized wire without the encoder's constants and outer-loop accounting.

\section{Inherited exact interfaces and the entropy norm}
\label{sec:interfaces}

We retain the source contraction algorithm's Cauchy deformation, integral
local coordinates, Hermite reconstruction, canonical creation-time
representatives, and exact child-network identity
\cite{contraction}. The sharper packing and precision statements are taken
from the asymmetric, joint-Cauchy, adaptive, and cached-tree packets
\cite{asymmetric,joint,adaptive,tree}. The new work changes how a finite
mode sum is evaluated; it does not alter those identities.

At an active recursive call let $M$ be the number of wires, $u$ the number
of retained valuation levels, $a$ the packet exponent, and $D=2^{a-i}$ at
valuation level $i<u$. There are $r$ groups. Near-lossless packing gives
child size at most $(2/r+o(1))M$. The sharp recovery precision satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision}
 p_t\le(6+o(1))MD/u.
\end{equation}
Full-block Cauchy modes have guaranteed weights
\[
 w_q=a+1-i+4q-2s_2(q),\qquad q\ge0,
\]
where $s_2(q)$ is the binary digit sum. For each directed source-target pair
there are at most $(2+o(1))M/(r^2D)$ families of each index $q$.
These facts include the source/target locality needed for factorization.

\subsection{Exact budgets and paid group entries}

Enumerate the arrays of exact directed-pair weights $(b_{gh})$ with
$\sum b_{gh}<p_t$. The number of entries in an array is the fixed constant
$r(r-1)$, so the number of arrays is polynomial in $s$. For one array let
$\mathcal I_{gh}$ be the list of nonnegative mode-exponent tuples of exact
weight $b_{gh}$. An empty list makes that array contribute zero. Otherwise
the array's contribution is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-graph}
 \sum_{(\xi_{gh})\in\prod_{g\ne h}\mathcal I_{gh}}
     \prod_{g=1}^r Z_g((\xi_e)_{e\ni g}).
\end{equation}
Every entry of $Z_g$ is a real child contraction. Counting, ranking, and
unranking a list use the standard integer recurrence
\[
 D(j,b)=D(j-1,b)+D(j,b-w_j),\quad D(0,0)=1,
\]
with impossible budgets assigned zero. Its dimensions and integer bit
lengths are polynomial in $s$. Exact list sizes can consequently be used
in partition selection before the expensive entries are computed.

Creation-time precision matters here. A packet tensor retains its immutable
description and the canonical representatives chosen when it was created.
Later higher-precision calls reuse those representatives. A group entry
depends only on its incident pair indices; it does not secretly depend on
the other groups' indices. This is why caching and conditional separation
are valid, and why no free-oracle assumption is being introduced.

\subsection{A Euclidean bound for the list logarithms}

Write $h_e=\log_2|\mathcal I_e|$ and define
\[
 K(M,u)=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}\frac M{\sqrt u}.
\]
For a nonnegative vector $z$ on the directed pairs, the inherited
weighted-partition estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-entropy}
 \sum_e z_e h_e\le
 \left(\frac{\|z\|_2}{r}+o(1)\right)K(M,u).
\end{equation}
For clarity, its constant follows from
\[
 \sum_{q\ge0}-\ln(1-e^{-\theta(1+4q-2s_2(q))})
 =\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}+O(\log^2(2+1/\theta)).
\]
Use parameter $\theta/z_e$ for pair $e$, multiply its logarithmic bound
by $z_e$, and sum. The leading terms in natural logarithms are
\[
 \theta p_t+
 \frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}\frac{(2+o(1))M}{r^2D}
              \sum_e z_e^2.
\]
Optimization and \eqref{eq:precision} give
\eqref{eq:weighted-entropy}. The retained-level precision buffer makes
the remainder negligible uniformly over active depths. The dimension
$r(r-1)$ is fixed. Uniformity over the positive unit sphere follows either
from the displayed remainder or from a finite epsilon-net followed by a
limit. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 \|(h_e)\|_2\le(1+o(1))K(M,u)/r.
\end{equation}
This norm statement allows the partition to depend on the actual list sizes.
Treating all lists as equally large would lose precisely that flexibility.

\section{Arithmetic and schedule details for the cached theorem}
\label{sec:schedule}

\subsection{Matrix multiplication over the actual ring}

The coefficient ring during recursion is
\[
 R=(\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[U,V]/(f(U),V^\ell),
\]
where $f$ is monic of degree $O(\log s)$ and irreducible modulo two.
This ring can contain zero divisors and nilpotents; treating it as a field
would be invalid. We use the cached-tree packet's integer-coordinate bridge
\cite{tree}. We spell out its costs because they affect the theorem.

An established rational matrix-multiplication exponent strictly below
$12/5$ supplies a fixed finite rational bilinear algorithm
\cite{matrix}. Clear its finitely many constant denominators. The same
identities hold over every prime field outside a fixed finite exceptional
set. Recursive use of this fixed algorithm gives square cost at most
$O(N^{12/5})$ up to fixed constants and quadratic field-element storage.

The ring $R$ is a free module of polynomial rank over
$\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z$, with a polynomial-size integer multiplication
table. Lift coordinate inputs to nonnegative integers. A coordinate entry
of a product of $d_1\times d_2$ and $d_2\times d_3$ matrices is bounded by
$d_2(2^p-1)^2$ and has at most $2p+\ceil{\log_2d_2}+1$ bits. This is
polynomial in $s$. Polynomially many small nonexceptional odd primes suffice
for exact CRT reconstruction. Multiply every required coordinate pair,
recover its integer matrix product, apply the structure constants, and
reduce modulo $2^p$. This adds polynomial factors only.

Blocking rectangular products at side $\min(d_1,d_2,d_3)$ gives bit work
\[
 \poly(s+\mathcal L)d_1d_2d_3\min(d_1,d_2,d_3)^{-3/5}.
\]
Input, output, and temporary storage have at most a polynomial factor times
their table-cell counts. Thus the logarithmic work objective really is
$H_1+H_2+H_3-(3/5)\min H_i$, as used in the adaptive lemma. No nonunit
is inverted in $R$. The prototype in this archive uses ordinary scalar
multiplication to test the finite identity; it does not implement the
theoretical fast backend.

\subsection{Uniform packing and precision}

For the fixed-nine schedule in the preceding proof, put
$b=\ceil{6\log_2\lambda}$ and $a_d=\sum_{j=0}^d u_j+(d+1)b$.
The exact integer definition of $U$ gives
\[
 U=\frac{\lambda}{20\log_2 9}-\sqrt\lambda+O(1),
 \qquad \sum_{d\ge0}u_d\le2U.
\]
At the root, with $Q=9^{a_0}$ and $\Delta=\ceil{s^{9/10}}$,
the symmetric high-degree packing term has logarithm at most
\[
 \log_2(Q^2\Delta)
 \le\lambda-2\log_2 9\sqrt\lambda+O(\log\lambda).
\]
Below the root orient the retained side as the high-degree side. Its
degree remains at most $\Delta$, and the fresh side has degree at most
$2^{a_{d-1}+1}$. The asymmetric high-degree term $Q\Delta$ has the same
negative square-root slack, now with an $O((\log\lambda)^2)$ remainder.
The fresh-side and rounding terms $Q^2 2^{a_{d-1}+1}$ and $2^{a_d}Q^2$
have exponents bounded by
\[
 \frac{2\log_2 9+1}{10\log_2 9}\lambda+o(\lambda)
 <0.232\lambda+o(\lambda).
\]
These gaps dominate all required fixed polynomial logarithmic factors and
$s/M<\lambda$ while the call is active.

For a noncircular depth argument, first check hypothetical depths
$d\le\ceil{\log_4\lambda}+O(1)$. In that range
$u_d\ge\lambda^{1/2+o(1)}$, and the buffer has total size
$O((\log\lambda)^2)$. Packing gives child ratio $2/9+o(1)<1/4$;
this closes the proposed depth bound. The sharper ratio and the taper
decay yield the geometric rate $\gamma=2\sqrt2/9$.

At a retained level, $D\ge2^{a_{d-1}+b+1}$. Inherited precision is
$O(s\lambda^2 2^{a_{d-1}})$. Its ratio to $MD/u$ is at most
$O(\lambda^4 2^{-b})=o(1)$. The jet-evaluation loss is also lower order.
Thus the precision coefficient six is uniform on active paths. All ring
parameters, indices, descriptions, and interpolation lengths remain
polynomial in $s+\mathcal L$.

\subsection{Summing the complete recurrence}

Let $\alpha=4/9$ and $\beta=\sqrt{916}/45$. The local recurrence pays for
every child entry before the group tables are combined:
\[
 T_d\le\poly(s)\left(2^{(\alpha+o(1))K_d}T_{d+1}
                    +2^{(\beta+o(1))K_d}\right).
\]
A path ending at nonrecursive work at depth $d$ has normalized coefficient
\[
 \kappa\left(\alpha\sum_{j<d}\gamma^j+\beta\gamma^d\right)
 =\kappa\left(\frac\alpha{1-\gamma}
       +\left(\beta-\frac\alpha{1-\gamma}\right)\gamma^d\right)
 \le\kappa\beta.
\]
The strict margin $\alpha/(1-\gamma)<\beta$ makes this stable under the
uniform lower-order perturbations. More formally, for any sufficiently
small fixed $\eta>0$, the eventual bound uses $\kappa+\eta$ and
$\gamma+\eta<1$; after summing the geometric majorant, let $\eta$ tend
to zero. This avoids assuming that arbitrary $o(1)$ errors can be multiplied
through an increasing number of levels without control.

Polynomial branching factors across $O(\log\lambda)$ depths add
$O(\lambda\log\lambda)=o(s/\sqrt\lambda)$ to the exponent. Direct leaves
cost $2^{O(s/\lambda)}$; initial entry evaluation costs $2^{O(s^{9/10})}$;
packet entry work is $\exp(O(2^{a_d}\log s))$ with
$a_d\le(1/(10\log_2 9)+o(1))\lambda$. All are lower order, including
after the paid ancestor branching. One fixed polynomial in the original
description length pays for entry access rather than becoming an
additional recursive branching factor.

For space, the largest boundary table has 36 directed pairs, so its
logarithmic cell count is at most $(2/3+o(1))K_d$. Ancestor tables add
their storage sizes; their exponents do not add. The largest is at the
root, giving $\CS=(2/3)\kappa$. Exact ring arithmetic and index records
contribute polynomial factors per cell. This completes the resource
accounting in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{SAT consequences and the limits of the improvement}
\label{sec:sat}

For any fixed encoder and normalized network budget, both new engines give
strictly smaller explicit bound functions than their compared predecessors.
The cached engine offers the fastest reviewed general-degree coefficient;
the streamed engine improves the polynomial-space branch. Existing lower
degree or lower memory branches can still be selected when their premises
or resource limits apply. A finite portfolio of exact algorithms preserves
the minimum applicable time guarantee up to a constant scheduling factor,
provided its allowed memory regime is respected.

For bounded clause density, the source uses bounded-degree clause and
equality networks with $s=O_k(n+m)=O_{k,D}(n)$. The new engines therefore
retain the $2^{O_{k,D}(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ order. Their general-degree
coefficients must not be advertised as numerically beating the much smaller
coefficients already proved under special low-degree assumptions.

For arbitrary clause density, the source sparsifies and queries sparse
polynomial systems. Its retained monomial encoding has
$s=O_k(n\log\log n)$; the density-aware refinement yields the envelope
\eqref{eq:sat-envelope}. Replacing only the contraction engine preserves
these wire counts, and therefore the order. An additional wire-compression
or structured-summation theorem is needed for a smaller global exponent.

\subsection{Why the simplest order-improvement attempts do not close}

The prior reports already explain three relevant limitations
\cite{precision,barriers,density}. First, a mode inventory with
$\Theta(M/D)$ choices of each small weight and budget $\Theta(MD/u)$
can contain $2^{\Omega(M/\sqrt u)}$ binary exponent vectors even after a
total-degree cap is imposed. This is a counting obstruction to those
numerical constraints alone, not a SAT lower bound. Second, changing only
the bounded Hermite taper still incurs an $\Omega(MD/u)$ multiplier loss
at some active level in the stated recovery model. Third, the density-aware
sparsification cost model
\[
 n\left(b\sigma+a\frac{\log(1/\sigma)}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)
\]
is minimized at $\sigma=\Theta(1/\sqrt{\log n})$, leaving the
$n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ order. We do not present these existing
barriers as new results.

\subsection{An explicit incidence-support certificate}

The supplementary note supplies a deterministic illustration of the
existing raw-incidence encoding obstruction. For $1\le q<n/2$, on cyclic
indices define quadratic equations over $\mathbb F_2$ by
\[
 P_i(x)=x_i\sum_{j=1}^q x_{i+j},\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
Their $nq$ quadratic monomials are pairwise distinct across the rows: a
collision between two different rows would require two offsets summing to
$n$, impossible when both are at most $q<n/2$. Therefore a nonempty linear
combination of $r$ rows has exactly $rq$ monomials; Gaussian row operations
alone cannot make the nonzero rows sparser.

In the source incidence lift, the constant coefficient of $V$ is
\[
 [V^0]F(z,V)=\prod_i\left(1-z_{ii}\sum_{j=1}^q z_{i,i+j}\right).
\]
Different rows use disjoint incidence indeterminates. This coefficient has
exactly $(q+1)^n$ distinct monomials, all with coefficient $1$ or $-1$;
the $q^n$ choices taking one nonconstant term from every row have degree
$2n$. They survive modulo $2^{n+1}$. Any representation that assigns a
distinct pair consisting of a network index and a Boolean wire assignment
to every retained incidence monomial must therefore satisfy
\[
 R2^{s_{\max}}\ge(q+1)^n,
 \qquad s_{\max}\ge n\log_2(q+1)-\log_2R.
\]
For $q=\ceil{\log_2n}^{c}$ and the source's
$\log_2R=O(\sqrt n\log n)$ degree-array enumeration, this is
$(c+o(1))n\log_2\log_2n$.

The family satisfies the input premises of the source's sparse-query lemma,
including the occurrence threshold without high-incidence branching.
We do not claim that the specific family is generated by the source's
expander/greedy SAT reduction. The prior analysis already gives a
probabilistic 3-CNF-derived obstruction; this explicit example is a
supplementary certificate, not a new lower-bound order. Aggregating many
incidence monomials, identifying variable copies, or changing the
representation can evade it. Those are substantive possible research
directions, but no sufficiently cheap general algorithm is supplied here.

\section{Verification and reproducibility}
\label{sec:checks}

The archive separates mathematical dependencies from executed evidence.
The asymptotic statements are proved in this report. Finite tests validate
the exact combinatorial identities and local implementations used in those
proofs; they do not experimentally establish a theorem about all input
sizes.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Nine-group partition certificate.} The checker enumerates all
280 partitions into triples and verifies all 1,296 entries of the
36-by-36 covariance matrix. It also checks an exact rational positive
semidefinite certificate, with no numerical eigenvalue approximation.
\item \textbf{Full paid schedule.} Rational interval arithmetic certifies
\begin{align*}
34.325282797284&<\CT<34.325282797285,\\
34.024178007490&<\CS<34.024178007491.
\end{align*}
Integer inequalities certify strict
improvement over the predecessor and closure of the recursion. Forty-one
hypothetical depth records at three sample logarithmic sizes check the
packing gates; these samples supplement the uniform proof.
\item \textbf{Exact aggregation prototype.} Thirty-two nine-factor
contractions over $(\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^2)$, for $p=1,\ldots,7$,
agree with independent exhaustive edge-index enumeration. Tests cover
zero divisors, nilpotent coefficients, singleton dimensions, and unequal
list sizes. Another 128 profiles check the exact partition selector
against the proved analytic inequality as supplementary numerical tests.
\item \textbf{Streamed-tree certificate.} A separate exact checker verifies
the finite moments and rational matrix inequalities used in the
polynomial-space theorem. Its complete counts and constant intervals
are recorded in the accompanying result file. An independent checker
reconstructs the tree by dyadic least-common-ancestor intervals and verifies
all 14,400 ordered covariance entries. The minimax subset selector is
checked against all 315 balanced eight-group trees on twelve weight
profiles, and on three sixteen-group profiles. Each sixteen-group run
uses exactly 14,827 states and 462,465 splits.
\item \textbf{Incidence example.} Five exact small cases check row
independence, retained support sizes, and coefficient aggregation after
variable copies are identified.
\item \textbf{Independent mathematical review.} Separate derivations
checked the adaptive partition inequality, paid recurrences, schedule
gates, coefficient-ring bridge, and the scope of the SAT consequences.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reproduction}

The Python checkers use only the standard library. From the extracted
directory run:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 code/verify_all.py
make pdf
\end{verbatim}
The first command regenerates the certificate results and returns a failing
exit status on any violated check. The second requires a conventional
LaTeX installation with the packages listed in the source. The included
PDF was compiled from the included TeX files with shell escape disabled
and was rendered for visual review. The bundle contains checksums for its
files and the exact pinned source inventory.

The prototype deliberately implements ordinary matrix-shaped summation,
so its small examples test correctness of the reorganization. The fast
matrix backend is an imported theoretical algorithm. There is no measured
end-to-end SAT speedup claim, no Lean or Rocq build, and no claim that the
preprint's full proof has been independently certified. Those qualifications
also apply to the numerical comparisons in the opening table.

\section{Research outcome}

The new contribution is the use of exact mode-list size information to
choose a partition whose \emph{whole aggregation cost} admits a second-moment
bound. In the cached algorithm this reduces the final triangle cost below
table construction, exposing a smaller dominant coefficient. In the
polynomial-space algorithm it reduces the largest path cost of a streamed
separator tree. Both improvements retain the exact finite identities and
pay for the original child computations.

This gives genuine strict improvements of explicit exponential certificates
in both resource regimes. The next order-level advance must do more:
compress the incidence representation, exploit correlations beyond these
finite group sums, or provide a different recovery/recursion interface.
The present result does not resolve those missing steps.
