\subsection{Three different meanings of strongest}
For fixed clause width $k$, the strongest stated density envelope supported
by the reviewed ordinary mathematical derivations is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-envelope}
 \poly_k(n,m)\,
 2^{O_k\left(
 \frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}\,
 \min\{\log(2+m/n),\,\log\log n\}\right)}.
\end{equation}
It specializes to $2^{O_{k,D}(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ for fixed density
$m\le Dn$, and has unrestricted exponent
$O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})$~\cite{density,density2}.
All twenty packets retain these worst-case orders wherever they state
a proved global algorithm.

For \emph{explicit coefficients on normalized tensor wires}, the strongest
stated generic polynomial-space coefficient is
$55.70947831891\ldots$~\cite{adaptive}. Combining that new streamed
separator with the earlier multistage schedule gives the stronger derived
comparator $55.40466590661\ldots$, which we also exceed. Allowing subexponential memory, the strongest
stated generic time coefficient is $35.354588058401\ldots$, with space
coefficient $34.274803005023\ldots$~\cite{frontier,tree}.
Another cached algorithm uses time $37.93010908288\ldots$ and smaller
space $33.62304092166\ldots$. Thus there is a time-space portfolio,
not one method that dominates every memory allowance.

For \emph{conditional statements}, the strongest reviewed target is
\[
 \poly_k(n,m)2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\log n)},\qquad
 \poly_{k,D}(n,m)2^{O_{k,D}(n/\log n)}
\]
at unrestricted and fixed density, respectively. It requires an
unconstructed structured-mode summation interface~\cite{precision}.
Other conditional proposals require compact factorization,
degree-preserving recursion, or linear-wire compression~\cite{scalar,analysis}.
These implications are not available algorithms. Our coefficient
improvement does not imply bounds smaller than these conditional targets.

\subsection{Limits of numerical domination}
The separate manuscript credited to Claude has the same relevant
$n$-only order but supplies no matching explicit coefficient in the
normalized wire model. Consequently a numerical comparison with its
unspecified constant is unavailable~\cite{claude}. Similarly, an input-class-specific
coefficient such as $15.3919$ for degree at most $s^{1/12}$ must not be
compared as if it applied to the generic degree-$s^{9/10}$ class.
We retain those algorithms as options under their own premises.

A coefficient improvement also does not prove a lower bound on the
older algorithm's runtime. What can be compared are the displayed
certifying functions. For the new $C_T$ and the previous explicit
$C_{\mathrm{old}}=35.354588058401\ldots$,
\[
 \frac{2^{(C_T+o(1))\Escale(s)}}
      {2^{(C_{\mathrm{old}}+o(1))\Escale(s)}}
 =2^{-(C_{\mathrm{old}}-C_T+o(1))\Escale(s)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Polynomial prefactors do not change this conclusion. This is the
precise sense in which the completed positive result is an asymptotically
smaller upper-bound certificate.
