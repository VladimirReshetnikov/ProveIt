\section{A smaller certified coefficient from rectangular multiplication}
\label{sec:rectangular-new}

The latest incoming contraction-tree packet~\cite{tree} uses a square matrix multiplication
backend of exponent $12/5$ and proves time coefficient $35.3545880584\ldots$
on $s/\sqrt{\log_2s}$. Its polynomial-coordinate and Chinese remainder bridge
already permits field algorithms over the actual coefficient ring. We replace
its square-block rectangular bound by a genuinely rectangular algorithm,
including its input/output cost, and change the retained-range schedule.
No faster summation oracle is assumed. This improves the leading coefficient
of the displayed exponential upper bound; the logarithmic order is unchanged.

\subsection{The imported field facts and the exact ring interface}

Only two established field inequalities are needed:
\[
 \alpha>\frac3{10},\qquad \omega<\frac{19}{8}.
\]
Here $\alpha$ is the dual matrix multiplication exponent. For example,
\emph{New Bounds for Matrix Multiplication: from Alpha to Omega},
arXiv:2307.07970~\cite{mm}, gives stronger inequalities. No current-record assertion is
needed. The paper formulates tensor rank over an arbitrary field in
Sections 3.1--3.4; specialize the field to $\mathbb Q$. Fix $\varepsilon=1/100000$. The definition and constructive proof of
the dual exponent supply a fixed finite rational bilinear algorithm for
multiplying dimensions $N\times N^{3/10}\times N$ with exponent at most
$2+\varepsilon$. Choose a fixed square algorithm with exponent at most
$19/8$. Their dimensions and rational coefficients are constants independent
of the SAT input. Padding to fixed powers changes dimensions only by constant
factors; the bounds below allow those factors.

\begin{lemma}[Rectangular cost with the output term retained]
\label{lem:rectangular-new}
Let the logarithms to base two of the three matrix dimensions, in decreasing
order, be $x\ge y\ge z\ge0$. Matrix multiplication is possible in a polynomial
factor times $2^{F(x,y,z)}$ bit operations over the coefficient rings of the
normalized contraction interface, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangular-new-cost}
 F(x,y,z)=\max\left\{
 (1+\varepsilon/2)(x+y),\quad
 x+\left(\frac{47}{56}+\varepsilon\right)y+\frac{15}{28}z
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The workspace is a polynomial factor times the largest input or output
array. The polynomial depends only on the fixed algorithms and on the
normalized interface.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $\alpha_0=3/10$ and $\omega_0=19/8$. First suppose the two larger
logarithms are equal to $y$. If $z\le\alpha_0y$, pad the smaller dimension
and use the rectangular algorithm, with exponent at most $(2+\varepsilon)y$.
If $z\ge\alpha_0y$, take the tensor product of the rectangular algorithm at
logarithmic size
\[
 t_0=\frac{y-z}{1-\alpha_0}
\]
and the square algorithm at logarithmic size
\[
 t_1=\frac{z-\alpha_0y}{1-\alpha_0}.
\]
Then $t_0+t_1=y$ and $\alpha_0t_0+t_1=z$. Round the two recursion
depths upward independently in their respective fixed base dimensions.
Both padding ratios and the rank overhead are then bounded by constants
uniformly in $x,y,z$, including a depth that is zero. The rank exponent is at most
$(2+\varepsilon)t_0+\omega_0t_1$. Blocking the largest dimension contributes
$x-y$, so both cases are bounded by
\[
 \max\left\{x+y,\quad
 x+\frac{47}{56}y+\frac{15}{28}z\right\}+\varepsilon y.
\]
Since $y\le(x+y)/2$, this is at most \eqref{eq:rectangular-new-cost}.
The first branch is essential: a field exponent $2+\varepsilon$ does not
justify a free quadratic input/output branch. Permuting the three dimensions
uses the symmetries of the matrix multiplication tensor; transposition or
cyclic transposition of the fixed bilinear identities implements the
corresponding product. Concretely, permute the three linear-form factors in
the finite tensor-rank decomposition, regard the new third factor as output
coefficients, and execute that bilinear decomposition afresh. We do not
transpose a cached program and assume its memory schedule survives. This is
an arithmetic algorithm, not a promise that only a scalar trace is required.

For completeness, the coefficient ring is a free module of polynomial rank
over $\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z$, with a polynomial-size integer multiplication
table, and $p$ is polynomial in $s$. In particular this includes
\[
 R=(\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[U,V]/(f(U),V^\ell)
\]
with the monic polynomials and degrees in the source interface. Lift each
module coordinate to $[0,2^p)$. For each ordered pair of coordinates, compute
an ordinary integer matrix product. An unreduced scalar entry is less than
$d_2 2^{2p}$, hence has polynomial bit length. Clear the finitely many
constant denominators in the fixed field algorithms, omit their finitely many
exceptional primes, and perform the product over enough remaining odd prime
fields that their product exceeds this integer reconstruction bound. The
number and bit lengths of these primes, and CRT reconstruction, are
polynomial in $s$. Apply the specified integer structure constants and reduce
modulo $2^p$. Polynomially many coordinate pairs suffice. Thus no even
integer or nilpotent is inverted in $R$.

A fixed bilinear algorithm can process one recursive product at a time and
accumulate the output. At each level its input, output and auxiliary arrays
are bounded by a constant times the largest input/output size, and recursive
sizes decrease. The largest array size is the maximum of the three pairwise dimension
products, which is invariant under those permutations. Tensor products
and blocking preserve this bound. Coordinate
pairs and CRT primes can also be processed sequentially. Storing residue
information adds only polynomial bits per cell. Consequently the workspace
is as stated, including the output array. Sequential record reblocking and
sorting give the same leading bit-cost exponents without unit-cost
random-access assumptions on exponentially large indices.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The local entropy calculation}

Import the exact pair-index factorization, asymmetric packing, thin precision
gaps, and slope-four Cauchy modes from the reviewed packets. At a call with
$M$ wires and retained range $u$, write
\[
 K(M,u)=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}\frac M{\sqrt u}=\kappa\frac M{\sqrt u}.
\]
For an exact pair-budget array, let $h_e$ be the logarithm of the number of
mode tuples on the directed group pair $e$. The inherited common-tilt bound
is, for every fixed nonnegative vector $w$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangular-new-entropy}
 \sum_e w_eh_e\le
 \left(\frac{\sqrt{\sum_e w_e^2}}r+o(1)\right)K(M,u).
\end{equation}
There are only polynomially many exact budget arrays because all arities
below are fixed. Counts and rank/unrank operations use the inherited
polynomial dynamic program; an entry of a group function remains a real
recursive contraction.

Put
\[
 d=1+\varepsilon/2,\qquad
 v=\frac{47}{56}+\varepsilon,\qquad w=\frac{15}{28}.
\]
If a matrix operation has three disjoint classes of directed pairs with
cardinalities $q_1\ge q_2\ge q_3$, then Lemma~\ref{lem:rectangular-new} and
\eqref{eq:rectangular-new-entropy} give squared local coefficient, before
dividing by $r^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangular-new-cardinality}
 \Phi(q_1,q_2,q_3)=
 \max\{d^2(q_1+q_2),\ q_1+v^2q_2+w^2q_3\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed sorting the actual logarithmic dimensions selects one of six
permutations. For each permutation apply the weighted entropy estimate;
the rearrangement inequality pairs the largest squared weights with the
largest cardinalities for a uniform upper bound. This argument includes
empty and small pair lists and makes no separate asymptotic assumption on
individual pair budgets.

Build each block table by balanced binary merges and finish by contracting
three blocks as a triangle. A merge of group sets of sizes $a,b$, with
$k=a+b$, has class cardinalities
\[
 2a(r-k),\quad 2ab,\quad 2b(r-k).
\]
For final block sizes $k_1,k_2,k_3$, the three classes have cardinalities
$2k_1k_2,2k_1k_3,2k_2k_3$. The following complete maxima include every merge,
the final multiplication, and all input/output tables:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 r&\text{final block sizes}&\text{cache cardinality}&
 \text{largest boundary cardinality}&\max\Phi\\\hline
 8&(3,3,2)&14&30&30d^2\\
 10&(4,3,3)&18&48&48d^2\\
 6&(2,2,2)&10&16&16d^2\\
 4&(2,1,1)&6&8&8d^2
\end{array}
\]
These are rational inequalities, checked exactly by the accompanying
script. For instance the root triangle has classes $(18,12,12)$ and
\[
 18+12v^2+12w^2<30<30d^2.
\]
A size-three root merge has classes $(20,10,4)$ and also costs at most
$30d^2$. The input/output branch therefore dominates the root certificate.
Dropping it would falsely suggest a coefficient below the table-size cost.

Define
\[
 a_r=\frac{\sqrt{2(r-1)}}r,\qquad
 (j_8,j_{10},j_6,j_4)=
 \left(\frac{\sqrt{30}}8,\frac{\sqrt{48}}{10},
       \frac23,\frac1{\sqrt2}\right),\qquad b_r=dj_r.
\]
The time recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangular-new-recurrence}
 T_i\le 2^{(a_{r_i}+o(1))K_i}T_{i+1}
       +2^{(b_{r_i}+o(1))K_i}
\end{equation}
up to controlled polynomial factors. Thus every cached child entry is paid
for. The local table-space exponent is $(j_{r_i}+o(1))K_i$.

\subsection{A strict rational schedule}

Let $\lambda=\lceil\log_2s\rceil$, stop recursion at
$H=\lceil s/\lambda\rceil$, and use the arities and retained-range fractions
\begin{align*}
 &(r_0,r_1,r_2)=(8,10,6),\qquad r_{3+j}=4\quad(j\ge0),\\
 &f_0=\frac{49997}{3000000},\qquad
 f_1=\frac{67}{5000},\qquad f_2=\frac{17}{2000},\\
 &f_{3+j}=\frac{34003}{8000000}\left(\frac58\right)^j.
\end{align*}
Put $u_i=\lfloor f_i\lambda\rfloor$, $b=\lceil6\log_2\lambda\rceil$, and
$a_i=\sum_{t=0}^i(u_t+b)$. The leading cumulative fractions are
\[
 A_0=\frac{49997}{3000000},\quad
 A_1=\frac{90197}{3000000},\quad
 A_2=\frac{115697}{3000000},\quad
 A_\infty=\frac{499}{10000}.
\]
The root condition is $2A_0\log_2r_0+9/10<1$, while subsequent old-side
conditions are $A_i\log_2r_i+9/10<1$. The exact integer comparisons
\[
 10^{500}<2^{1661},\qquad 6^{200}<2^{517}
\]
give the following rational upper exponents, all strictly below one:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{regime}&\text{old-side exponent bound}\\\hline
0&499997/500000\\
1&1499817217/1500000000\\
2&599815349/600000000\\
 i\ge3&4999/5000
\end{array}
\]
At later calls the fresh-side degree is at most $2^{a_{i-1}+1}$, so the
remaining packing exponents are bounded by
$2A_i\log_2r_i+A_{i-1}$ and $(2\log_2r_i+1)A_i$; both are below one
with ample fixed margin. New packet degrees are $s^{0.0499+o(1)}$, below
the maintained $s^{9/10}$ bound. Choose padding tolerance $O(\lambda^{-2})$;
the strict power gaps absorb this tolerance, all polylogarithmic factors,
and the additive buffers.

For a noncircular depth check, provisionally verify these estimates through
$3+\lceil\log_{20/11}\lambda\rceil+O(1)$ active levels. Throughout this
range the smallest taper satisfies
\[
 u_i\ge\lambda^{\eta+o(1)},\qquad
 \eta=1-\frac{\ln(8/5)}{\ln(20/11)}>0,
\]
because $5/8>11/20$. Floors are uniformly negligible. Nearly lossless
packing gives children with at most $11M/20$ wires eventually, since every
arity is at least four; hence no active path exceeds the provisional depth.
This closes the bootstrap. Accumulated padding errors tend to zero, giving
\[
 M_0\le s,\quad M_1\le(1+o(1))s/4,\quad
 M_2\le(1+o(1))s/20,\quad
 M_{3+j}\le(1+o(1))\frac{s}{60\,2^j}.
\]
The buffer gives $D\ge2^{a_{i-1}+b+1}$. The inherited precision
$O(s\lambda^2 2^{a_{i-1}})$ is $O(\lambda^4 2^{-b})=o(1)$ times $MD/u_i$,
so the sharp coefficient-six Hermite precision and entropy calculation
remain valid at all active calls.

\subsection{The resulting time and space bounds}

For $i=0,1,2,3$, put
\[
 (m_0,m_1,m_2,m_3)=(1,1/4,1/20,1/60),\qquad
 K_i^*=\frac{\kappa m_i}{\sqrt{f_i}}.
\]
Subsequent coefficients decline with ratio
$\gamma=(1/2)/\sqrt{5/8}=\sqrt{2/5}$. Moreover
$a_4/(1-\gamma)>b_4$. Unrolling the complete cache recurrence, including all
child calls, gives the maximum of
\begin{align*}
 &b_8K_0^*,\\
 &a_8K_0^*+b_{10}K_1^*,\\
 &a_8K_0^*+a_{10}K_1^*+b_6K_2^*,\\
 &a_8K_0^*+a_{10}K_1^*+a_6K_2^*
       +\frac{a_4K_3^*}{1-\gamma}.
\end{align*}
Exact rational interval evaluation encloses these respectively by
\[
 33.993891,\qquad32.812822,\qquad31.412630,\qquad33.657386.
\]
The root is strictly largest. Ancestor and descendant tables occupy a sum
of geometrically decreasing array sizes, so their space exponents take a
maximum. The corresponding coefficients are
\[
 33.993721,\qquad9.590642,\qquad2.317442,\qquad1.158670,
\]
and again the root is largest.

\begin{theorem}[A coefficient below 34]
\label{thm:rectangular-new}
Under the normalized tensor interface and imported identities stated above,
there is a deterministic exact contraction algorithm with
\[
 T(s,\mathcal L)\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
       2^{(C_T+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},\qquad
 S(s,\mathcal L)\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
       2^{(C_S+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},
\]
where
\begin{align*}
 C_S&=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}
       \frac{\sqrt{30}}{8\sqrt{49997/3000000}}
       =33.9937208906\ldots,\\
 C_T&=\left(1+\frac1{200000}\right)C_S
       =33.9938908592\ldots<34.
\end{align*}
Consequently 34 is a valid eventual time coefficient and a valid eventual
space coefficient. The space use is subexponential, not polynomial.
\end{theorem}

Polynomial interpolation and budget factors across $O(\log\lambda)$ levels
contribute $O(\lambda\log\lambda)$ exponent bits. The numbers of
recursive branches and all multiplicity polynomials depend on $s$ alone;
description-length processing is charged once per visited call. Thus the
factor in $\mathcal L$ remains a fixed polynomial, rather than being
raised to the recursion depth. Direct leaves cost
$2^{O(s/\lambda)}$, the permitted original entry routines cost
$2^{O(s^{9/10})}$, and new packet entry routines cost
$\exp(s^{0.0499+o(1)})$. All are lower order after the displayed recurrence
charges. Immutable creation-time representatives retain the source oracle
accounting. No input, intermediate or output numerical precision grows beyond
a polynomial in $s$.

The new time coefficient is about $3.85\%$ below the newest delivered
$35.3545880584\ldots$. A stronger comparator is the old square-block method
retuned to its root-eight capacity boundary, namely
$35.0635988462\ldots$; the improvement over that comparison is about $3.05\%$.
Neither comparison asserts global optimality over every old schedule. For a
fixed common normalization, a strict coefficient decrease $\delta>0$ divides
the displayed upper bound by $2^{(\delta+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}}$. It does not
identify the actual runtime ratio. These are coefficients per normalized
wire, and cannot be substituted for hidden coefficients per SAT variable.
