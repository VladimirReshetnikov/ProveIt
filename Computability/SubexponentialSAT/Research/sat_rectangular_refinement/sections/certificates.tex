\section{Coupled support certificates}
\label{sec:certificates}

\subsection{Complete operation supports and exact selection}

A plan partitions the $r$ groups into three ordered nonempty blocks.
To build a block, split its ordered list at its midpoint, recursively
build both halves, and multiply their boundary tables. Finally contract
the three block tables. At an internal merge of disjoint sets $A,B$ with
outside set $O$, the three index classes are $AB,AO,BO$; the final triangle
has the three pair classes between its blocks. There are $r-3$ internal
merges and one final triangle, so every plan has $r-2$ operations.

If the three class entropy sums are $H_1,H_2,H_3$, the backend
\eqref{eq:rectangle} is the maximum of nine linear forms on the
\emph{unsorted} triple:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newsupports}
 \operatorname{Perm}(1+\eps/2,1+\eps/2,0),\qquad
 \operatorname{Perm}(1,3/4+\eps,1/2).
\end{equation}
This follows from the rearrangement inequality; the first set contains
three distinct permutations and the second six. They describe one sorted
cost bound, not nine algorithms executed in succession.

For $\eps=10^{-5}$ a common integer scale is $L_*=200000$, giving
\[
 \operatorname{Perm}(200001,200001,0),\qquad
 \operatorname{Perm}(200000,150002,100000).
\]
Give each unordered group edge its class coefficient and zero if it lies
inside one of the parts. For plan $j$ let $\mathcal V_j$ be the resulting
$9(r-2)$ integer support vectors, and define
\[
 F_j(g)=L_*^{-1}\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}v\cdot g.
\]
Then $2^{F_j(g)}$, up to polynomial factors, pays for every operation of
this plan after singleton tables have been formed. The input/output
branch of the backend pays for all local boundary arrays, so the same
coefficient also bounds local storage.

The algorithm selects the plan of least $F_j(g)$. This comparison can be
performed exactly. Put $c_{ab}=|\mathcal I_{ab}||\mathcal I_{ba}|$ and
compare the positive integers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exactselect}
 P_j=\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}\prod_{a<b}c_{ab}^{v_{ab}}.
\end{equation}
Their bit lengths are polynomial in the root parameter, since the menu,
dimension, support scale and exponents are fixed. No floating-point
logarithm comparison is necessary. The large fixed scale makes this a
complexity specification, not an assertion of practical efficiency.

\subsection{An exact hierarchy of quadratic certificates}

\begin{theorem}[Subset-coupled norm certificate]\label{thm:subset}
Let $q\ge2$ plans have finite nonnegative support families
$\mathcal V_1,\ldots,\mathcal V_q$. Give them nonnegative integer weights
$w_i$ with $W=\sum_iw_i>0$. Fix an integer $2\le k\le q$. For each
$k$-element subset $S\subseteq[q]$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:As}
 A_S=\max_{v_i\in\mathcal V_i,\ i\in S}
 \left(\sum_{i\in S}w_i^2\norm{v_i}^2
       +\frac{2(q-1)}{k-1}
          \sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in S}}w_iw_j\,v_i\cdot v_j\right).
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
 U_k=\frac{\sum_{|S|=k}A_S}{\binom{q-1}{k-1}W^2},
 \qquad \beta_k^2=\frac{2U_k}{L_*^2r^2}.
\end{equation}
Selecting the least-cost plan in the menu has local time and local space
coefficient at most $\beta_k$ on the scale $\K(M,u)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For arbitrary choices $v_i\in\mathcal V_i$, every diagonal term
$w_i^2\norm{v_i}^2$ occurs in $\binom{q-1}{k-1}$ subsets, and every
cross term $w_iw_jv_i\cdot v_j$ in $\binom{q-2}{k-2}$ subsets. Since
\[
 \frac{q-1}{k-1}\binom{q-2}{k-2}
 =\binom{q-1}{k-1},
\]
we have the exact identity
\begin{align*}
 \left\|\sum_i\frac{w_i}{W}v_i\right\|_2^2
 =\frac{1}{\binom{q-1}{k-1}W^2}
 \sum_{|S|=k}\left(
  \sum_{i\in S}w_i^2\norm{v_i}^2
  +\frac{2(q-1)}{k-1}\sum_{\substack{i<j\\i,j\in S}}
       w_iw_jv_i\cdot v_j\right).
\end{align*}
Replacing each bracket by its independent maximum gives the bound $U_k$.
For the actual entropy vector $g$, choose a maximizing support $v_i$ for
each $F_i(g)$. Then
\[
 \min_i F_i(g)
 \le\sum_i\frac{w_i}{W}F_i(g)
 =\frac1{L_*}\left(\sum_i\frac{w_i}{W}v_i\right)\cdot g
 \le(\beta_k+o(1))\K
\]
by~\eqref{eq:unordered}. The menu is finite, so the inherited error is
uniform. All local arrays are paid by the input/output support branch,
which proves the storage statement as well.
\end{proof}

There is no claim that the separate maximizing tuples for the different
subsets are simultaneously realizable. Taking their maxima only relaxes
an upper bound. At $k=q$ the certificate retains the complete support
tuple; at $k=2$ it is exactly the incoming pair certificate. The gain from
$k=3$ is proved by the actual integer computations below, not by an
assumed strict monotonicity for every arbitrary instance.

\subsection{A strict gain with the old backend}

Use the incoming nine ten-group plans, weights
\[
 (137,124,135,120,108,119,106,87,64),\qquad W=1000,
\]
and its old integer supports
\[
 \operatorname{Perm}(1400007,1400007,0),\qquad
 \operatorname{Perm}(1400000,1175014,750000),
 \quad L_*=1400000.
\]
The complete plans are stored as literal data in the archive, with
source provenance. There are 72 supports per plan. For $q=9,k=3$ the
cross coefficient in~\eqref{eq:As} is 8, the normalization is $28W^2$,
and the full check visits
\[
 \binom93\,72^3=31,352,832
\]
support triples. It gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oldtriple}
 \beta_{10,\mathrm{old},3}^2
 =\frac{13925744235456152241}{42875000000000000000}.
\end{equation}
The incoming pair value was
$15952347164548384057/49000000000000000000$.
Both use exactly the same operation supports and paid recursion.

Keeping its root taper $\eta_0=301/20000$, the new root coefficient is
\[
 C_{\mathrm{same}}
 =\kappa\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{10,\mathrm{old},3}^2}{301/20000}}
 =\sameC.
\]
The two other recurrence branches remain
$29.3352039993294\ldots$ and $29.06574852464125\ldots$, so the root
is still the maximum. This proves the same-backend assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. It isolates a genuine certificate improvement
from any improvement due to an imported matrix theorem.

\subsection{Updated root certificate and a cyclic thirteen-group menu}

With the new supports~\eqref{eq:newsupports}, the same weighted triple
computation at the root gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newtriple}
 \beta_{10}^2
 =\frac{16437522499887100933}{56000000000000000000}.
\end{equation}
For thirteen groups, start from the three ordered blocks
\[
 (0,1,2,4,7)\mid(3,6,8,12)\mid(5,9,10,11)
\]
and add $j$ modulo 13 to every label for $j=0,\ldots,12$. The resulting
13 plans have equal weights. Their exact pair certificate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic13}
 \beta_{13}^2=\frac{3132736483411}{10985000000000}.
\end{equation}
Each plan is fully charged, including the construction of its blocks.
The later menus are finite affine or punctured-affine menus and a final
four-group plan, likewise checked with all internal merges included.
Their exact data and certificates are included in the standalone verifier.

The seed and schedule were found by exploratory searches. The final proof
uses only the displayed rational data, complete finite enumeration, and
the analytic bounds above. A search objective or an optimizer's success
message is not used as a certificate of correctness or global optimality.
