\section{Outcome and exact scope}
\label{sec:outcome}

The objective was an improvement for \emph{3-SAT}, with a smaller
logarithmic order preferred to another constant improvement. The strongest
result established in this investigation is a strict improvement of the
explicit generic contraction certificate. The stronger objective remains
unresolved.

Write
\[
 \lambda=\ceil{\log_2s},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}.
\]
Here $s$ is a normalized Boolean wire budget, \emph{not} the number of SAT
variables. The common interface and all its entry costs are stated in
Section~\ref{sec:interface}.

\begin{theorem}[Certified improvement]\label{thm:main}
For the generic input interface of Section~\ref{sec:interface}, there is
a deterministic exact contractor with
\[
 T(s,\mathcal L)\le (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
       2^{(C_T+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda},\qquad
 S(s,\mathcal L)\le (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
       2^{(C_S+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda},
\]
where $C_T=\NewC<\SafeC$ and $C_S=\NewS<\SafeS$ are defined exactly
in Section~\ref{sec:schedule}. Thus $\SafeC$ and $\SafeS$ are safe
eventual coefficients for time and space, respectively.
The theorem imports the explicitly listed Cauchy deformation, mode,
Hermite-recovery, packing and entry-representation results, and the
characteristic-zero rectangular matrix-multiplication theorem of
Uzna\'nski~\cite{uznanski}. The new finite certificates and complete
schedule are proved and checked here. The displayed space bound is
subexponential.

Independently of the newer matrix theorem, the weighted triple certificate
with the preceding packet's $19/8$ and $3/10$ backend and its unchanged
schedule gives time and space coefficient $\sameC$.
\end{theorem}

The comparison in this theorem is between \emph{proved upper-bound
functions under the same normalized interface}. It is not a measurement
of SAT running times. In particular, replacing $C_{\rm old}$ by
$C_{\rm new}<C_{\rm old}$ changes the certified leading envelope by
\[
 2^{-(C_{\rm old}-C_{\rm new}+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda}.
\]
This is a strict asymptotic improvement in that envelope. It does not
establish a ratio between the actual running times of two implementations.

\subsection{What transfers to 3-SAT}

The source's direct clause/equality encoding at bounded clause density
has $s=O(n)$, while its general reduction has $s=O(n\log\log n)$.
Substitution therefore retains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:satorders}
 \poly(n+m)\,2^{O(n/\sqrt{\log n})}
 \quad(m\le Dn,\ D\text{ fixed}),\qquad
 \poly(n+m)\,2^{O(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}
 \quad\text{in general}.
\end{equation}
The inherited identities count satisfying assignments, so testing whether
the resulting count is positive decides 3-SAT. The mathematical contractor
is more general because that is the interface used by the 3-SAT reduction;
no other SAT width or unrelated counting problem is pursued here.

The reviewed papers do not give a common explicit conversion from every
generic coefficient per normalized wire to a coefficient per variable for
the dense reduction. Also, the Anthropic algorithm has a different
architecture and unspecified constants in its $O$ notation. Hence a claim
that $\NewC$ is a universal best constant for 3-SAT, or that this algorithm
asymptotically beats the Anthropic algorithm on all inputs, would be
unsupported.

\subsection{What is new, inherited, and still missing}

The new work consists of the complete weighted-triple certificate, its
strict improvement with the old backend, the finite and exact use of the
updated backend, the new paid schedule, the rectangular-grid minimax
calculation, and executable rank probes of the actual crossing operators.
The incoming pair-certificate report explicitly suggested higher-order
support certificates as future work; our triple result completes one
concrete instance of that suggestion. We make no priority claim for
quadratic identities, averaging, matrix interpolation, field descent, or
the general idea of a certificate hierarchy.

The logarithmic-order improvement would require a new structural theorem.
Section~\ref{sec:barriers} records what the examined approaches can prove,
why several plausible substitutions fail, and a precise crossing-factor
target that would suffice. Failed approaches and finite experiments are
kept separate from the contractor theorem.
