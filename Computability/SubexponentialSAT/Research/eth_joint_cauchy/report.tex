\begin{abstract}
We review the seven ETH refinement packets placed in ProveIt on October 26,
2026, together with the OpenAI preprint on which they depend and the separately
filed Claude manuscript. We give a constructive improvement to the contraction
engine. Instead of decomposing one column tile at a time, we eliminate a full
crossing orientation block. At valuation level $i$, its integral separated
modes have weights
\[
 a+1-i+4q-2s_2(q),
\]
with the same multiplicity bound as the existing modes of weights
$1+3q-2s_2(q)$. A unit triangular factorization proves the exact Smith profile
of the full padded block. The new step is the all-level, group-preserving
realization of that profile; the reviewed packets already contain the
corresponding local odd-unit Smith calculation.

Under the preprint's normalized Boolean tensor-contraction interface, we
derive polynomial-space time
\[
 (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(319.685758+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}}.
\]
The strongest explicit combination of the earlier mode basis and schedule
family approaches coefficient $368.654018$, so the improvement is beyond
simply combining those packets. A second construction caches group tensors,
forms three block tables, and applies division-free Strassen multiplication.
It gives time coefficient $225.597058$ and space coefficient $196.199446$ on
the same scale. Convenient eventual bounds are $320$ with polynomial space,
or $226$ time and $197$ space in the exponential coefficients.

These are smaller explicit exponential upper bounds. We do not establish a
smaller logarithmic order for unrestricted 3-SAT. The full counting consequence
uses the unchanged preprint reductions. Exact kernel and mode-summation
prototypes are supplied; an end-to-end fast SAT solver and a new Lean
formalization are not supplied.
\end{abstract}

\section{Outcome and the comparison being made}
\label{sec:outcome}

The useful result is a new integral decomposition that fits the existing
recursive algorithm, followed by an optional way to sum its modes with more
memory. Neither result assumes the unproved compression hypotheses in the
earlier research packets.

Let $n$ denote declared Boolean variables, $m$ input clauses, and $k$ a fixed
clause width. The strongest bound in the OpenAI manuscript, rather than its
older formalized endpoint, is
\begin{align}
 T_{\rm general}(n,m)&=(n+m)^{O_k(1)}
  2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})},\label{eq:general}\\
 T_{\rm sparse}(n,m)&=(n+m)^{O_{k,D}(1)}
  2^{O_{k,D}(n/\sqrt{\log n})}\quad(m\le Dn).
 \label{eq:sparse}
\end{align}
Both use polynomial space. The manuscript's quantitative appendix is the
baseline throughout this note \cite{openai,reduction,contraction}.

Our numerical coefficients apply to the normalized contraction size $s$,
not directly to $n$. The reduction from a formula to a tensor network has
additional width-dependent constants. In particular, writing
$2^{320n/\sqrt{\log n}}$ as the resulting universal 3-SAT bound would be
unjustified.

\subsection{Two precise guarantees}
\label{subsec:interface}
Use the preprint's following interface, with its size constant normalized to
one. A bipartite Boolean tensor network has at most $s$ edges, polynomially
many vertices, maximum degree at most $\ceil{s^{9/10}}$, and description
length $\mathcal L$. Its entries lie in
\[
 (\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell),
 \qquad 1\le P_0,\ell\le s.
\]
Each entry is evaluable in time $(s+\mathcal L)^b2^{A s^{9/10}}$ and
space $(s+\mathcal L)^b$, for fixed constants $A,b$.
All size and arithmetic bounds below are uniform for fixed interface
constants. Bounded exceptional sizes can be treated by exhaustive contraction.

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial-space improvement]\label{thm:main}
Using the unchanged deformation, interpolation, packing, and reduction
identities of the cited preprint, the normalized network can be contracted
deterministically in polynomial space and time
\[
 (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(C_{\rm ps}+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},
 \qquad
 C_{\rm ps}=\frac{\pi\sqrt{4200}}
 {\ln2\,(1-3\sqrt{12}/128)}
 =319.6857570777\ldots .
\]
Consequently the coefficient $320$ is valid for all sufficiently large $s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A faster option using more memory]\label{thm:fast}
Under the same interface and imported identities, there is a deterministic
contraction algorithm with bounds
\begin{align*}
 T&\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
   2^{(C_{\rm fast}+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},\\
 S&\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
   2^{(C_{\rm mem}+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},
\end{align*}
where, for $\omega=\log_2 7$ and $\kappa=\pi\sqrt{4200}/\ln2$,
\begin{align*}
 C_{\rm fast}
 &=\kappa\sqrt{\frac{43690-[1-(\omega-2)^2]14450}{65536}}
   =225.5970572839\ldots,\\
 C_{\rm mem}
 &=\kappa\sqrt{\frac{29240}{65536}}
   =196.1994455176\ldots .
\end{align*}
Thus coefficients $226$ for time and $197$ for space are valid eventually.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} preserves polynomial space.
Theorem~\ref{thm:fast} deliberately spends subexponential space to improve
the time coefficient. The latter is a different resource tradeoff, not a
polynomial-space speedup.

\subsection{How much improves?}

For the earlier explicit schedule $r=256$ and
$u_d=\floor{\lambda/(720\cdot12^d)}$, the geometric, existing factorial,
and new full-block bases have the following coefficients:
\[
 648.4410666487\ldots,\qquad
 374.3776243833\ldots,\qquad
 324.2205333244\ldots .
\]
The new basis alone multiplies the leading mode-enumeration coefficient by
$\sqrt3/2$ relative to the earlier factorial basis \cite{factorial}. The slightly larger
admissible taper scale $1/700$ gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

The earlier schedule paper also optimizes its parameter family
\cite{schedule}. Combining its boundary infimum with the old factorial
basis gives approximately $368.6540181$, rather than $374.3776244$.
Our polynomial-space coefficient is below both. This boundary value is an
infimum of a specified certificate family, not a lower bound for all possible
implementations of the earlier ideas. We compare explicit upper-bound
certificates; we have not measured a SAT runtime ratio.

\section{The crossing block and its arithmetic}
\label{sec:block}

Let $\OO$ be the preprint's unramified extension of $\mathbb Z_{(2)}$,
with fraction field $\KK$, and normalize $\vTwo(2)=1$. An element of
$\OO$ is a unit exactly when its reduction modulo two is nonzero.
The scalar factors below lie in $\OO$; tensor entries can lie in
$\Aring=\OO[V]/(V^\ell)$. Scalar integrality therefore also suffices when
the tensor ring has nilpotents. All logarithms used for sizes are base two;
$\ln$ denotes the natural logarithm.

At a recursive call, put $L=2^a$ and
\[
 \CC(T)=\prod_{v=-L}^{L-1}(T-v),\qquad
 \alpha=2^{a+1},\qquad
 \Gamma(T)=\frac{\CC(T)}{\CC'(0)}.
\]
For row slot $x$ and column slot $y$ in the same packet, the source kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
 W_{xy}(T)=\frac{\Gamma(T)}{T+x-y}.
\end{equation}
The cancelled expression is a polynomial. At a retained interpolation node
$t\ne0$, set
\[
 i=\vTwo(t)<a,\quad R=2^i,\quad D=L/R,\quad N=L/2,
 \quad T=t+2^{a+1}\zeta,\quad\zeta\in\OO.
\]
The preprint's integrality argument gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
 \vTwo(T)=i,\qquad \Gamma(T)\in2^{a+1}\OO.
\end{equation}
The second assertion follows directly by taking the factor $T-t$ out of
$\CC(T)$: every other factor has the valuation of its ordinary nonzero
grid difference, and those valuations add to $\vTwo(\CC'(0))$.
If $\zeta$ is a unit, the valuation is exactly $a+1$; when $\Gamma=0$,
all crossing entries vanish.

Within a packet with unequal symbols in coordinate $i$, crossing row and
column slots have the same bit in that coordinate. Fix that bit
$\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slots}
 \sigma(j)=(j\bmod R)+2R\floor{j/R},\qquad 0\le j<N.
\end{equation}
The slots in this orientation are $\varepsilon R+\sigma(j)$.
The offset cancels in $x-y$, so both orientations have the same scalar
difference kernel. All columns in one orientation belong to a single source
group, and its crossing rows belong to the other group.

\begin{lemma}[Denominators have known valuation]\label{lem:denom}
For any $j,k<N$, the element $T+\sigma(j)-\sigma(k)$ is nonzero and
has valuation at most $i$. If $j\not\equiv k\pmod R$, its valuation is
$\vTwo(j-k)<i$. If $j\equiv k\pmod R$, its valuation is $i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In the first case, insertion of the zero bit at position $i$ preserves the
lowest differing bit; adding $T$, of valuation $i$, cannot change it. In the
second case, the difference of the two slots is divisible by $2R$, while
$T/2^i$ is odd modulo two. In either case the normalized denominator is a
unit. This reasoning works for any $\zeta\in\OO$, including elements
outside the prime subring.
\end{proof}

\section{An exact integral decomposition of the whole block}
\label{sec:decomposition}

Write $s_h=\sigma(h)$. For $0\le b\le N$ define
\[
 P_b(x)=\prod_{h<b}\frac{x-s_h}{T+x-s_h},\qquad
 Q_b(y)=\prod_{h<b}\frac{s_h-y}{T+s_h-y}.
\]
Empty products are one. For $b<N$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fg}
 f_b(x)=\frac{T P_b(x)}{T+x-s_b},\qquad
 g_b(y)=\frac{T Q_b(y)}{T+s_b-y}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Finite Cauchy separation]\label{lem:separation}
For $x,y\in\{s_0,\ldots,s_{N-1}\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sep}
 \frac{1}{T+x-y}=\frac1T\sum_{b=0}^{N-1}f_b(x)g_b(y).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $H_b=P_b(x)Q_b(y)/(T+x-y)$. Cross multiplication gives
\[
 (T+x-s_b)(T+s_b-y)-(x-s_b)(s_b-y)=T(T+x-y).
\]
Consequently
\[
 H_b-H_{b+1}
 =\frac{T P_b(x)Q_b(y)}{(T+x-s_b)(T+s_b-y)}
 =\frac1T f_b(x)g_b(y).
\]
The term $H_0$ is the desired kernel, and $H_N=0$ because one numerator
factor is $x-x$. Telescoping proves the identity without an infinite series
or an approximation.
\end{proof}

For $b=qR+r$, $0\le r<R$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E}
 E_b=q+\vTwo(q!)=2q-s_2(q).
\end{equation}
The second equality is the elementary binary factorial-valuation identity.

\begin{lemma}[Two factorial gains]\label{lem:valuation}
For every $b,j<N$,
\[
 f_b(s_j),g_b(s_j)\in2^{E_b}\OO.
\]
Both vanish when $j<b$. At $j=b$, their valuation is exactly $E_b$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $j<b$, the factor with $h=j$ is zero. Suppose $j\ge b$, and write
$j=kR+v$. For a predecessor $h$ with residue different from $v$ modulo
$R$, Lemma~\ref{lem:denom} shows that the numerator and denominator in
the relevant factor of $P_b$ or $Q_b$ have the same valuation. Such a factor
contributes zero net valuation.

The predecessors with residue $v$ are $h=\ell R+v$ for
$\ell=0,\ldots,c-1$, where $c=q$ if $v\ge r$ and $c=q+1$ if $v<r$.
Their slot differences are $2R(k-\ell)$ and the denominators have valuation
$i$. Their combined contribution is
\[
 c+\vTwo\bigl(k(k-1)\cdots(k-c+1)\bigr)
 \ge c+\vTwo(c!)\ge q+\vTwo(q!).
\]
Here $k\ge c$ follows from $j\ge b$, and the first inequality follows
from the integrality of the ordinary integer $\binom{k}{c}$.
The remaining factors $T/(T+s_j-s_b)$ and $T/(T+s_b-s_j)$ are integral
by Lemma~\ref{lem:denom}. At $j=b$ they equal one; the same-residue
differences are exactly $2R,\ldots,2Rq$, giving equality in the valuation.
Only ordinary integer factorials have been used. No assertion that arbitrary
unramified extension elements have integral binomial polynomials is needed.
\end{proof}

Define the integral functions
\[
 F_b(x)=2^{-E_b}f_b(x),\qquad G_b(y)=2^{-E_b}g_b(y),
\]
and the target coefficient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 A_b(x)=\frac{\Gamma(T)}T\,2^{2E_b}F_b(x).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Group-preserving slope-four modes]\label{thm:modes}
For every crossing orientation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newW}
 W_{xy}(T)=\sum_{b=0}^{N-1}A_b(x)G_b(y),
 \qquad G_b(y)\in\OO,
\end{equation}
and, writing $b=qR+r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
 A_b(x)\in2^{w_q}\OO,\qquad
 w_q=a+1-i+2q+2\vTwo(q!)
     =a+1-i+4q-2s_2(q).
\end{equation}
There are $R$ such modes per $q$ in each orientation, and two orientations
per unequal-symbol packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the integral normalizations into Lemma~\ref{lem:separation}
and multiply by $\Gamma(T)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:gamma} supplies valuation
at least $a+1-i$ for $\Gamma(T)/T$. The remaining contribution is
$2E_b$. The index interval $0\le b<N=R(D/2)$ contains exactly $R$ indices
for each $0\le q<D/2$.
\end{proof}

For a mode $b$, the source linear form is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourceform}
 Z_b(X)=\sum_{\substack{y\text{ a real column}\\
                   y\text{ in this orientation}}}G_b(y)X_y.
\end{equation}
It reads the entire half-packet, rather than one tile. That half-packet still
lies in one source group. Its target coefficients are used only by rows in
the other group. Missing real slots are simply omitted from the sum. The
auxiliary pivots $s_h$ need not themselves be real ports.

\section{Exact Smith profile and what its optimality means}
\label{sec:smith}

Let $K_{jk}=1/(T+s_j-s_k)$ for the full padded orientation. Form matrices
$\mathsf L_{jb}=F_b(s_j)$ and $\mathsf U_{bk}=G_b(s_k)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:valuation} says that $\mathsf L$ is lower triangular,
$\mathsf U$ upper triangular, and both have unit diagonal. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ldu}
 TK=\mathsf L\,\operatorname{diag}(2^{2E_b})\,\mathsf U
\end{equation}
is an equivalence by invertible integral matrices. Since $E_b$ is
nondecreasing, it is a Smith factorization up to unit diagonal factors.

\begin{corollary}[The full-block invariant factors]\label{cor:smith}
The Smith valuations of $TK$ are
\[
 2E_b=4q-2s_2(q),\qquad b=qR+r,
\]
so each value indexed by $q$ occurs $R$ times. If $\zeta$ is a unit, the
Smith valuations of the actual crossing block $W$ are exactly
$a+1-i+4q-2s_2(q)$, with the same multiplicities.
\end{corollary}

For $i=0$, this specializes to the odd-unit profile already proved in the
density refinement packet \cite{density}. The added fact needed for the
algorithm is the full constructive factorization at arbitrary $i$, with
integral source functions supported in one group and no extra mode-family
factor. The invariant factors cannot be improved by invertible integral
changes of basis. This is a local statement about the full padded kernel.
It is not a lower bound for SAT, for general tensor contraction, or for sparse
real-port restrictions of this block.

\section{Compatibility with the recursive contraction}
\label{sec:compatibility}

The source packing produces at most $3M/L$ packets at an $M$-edge call.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:modes}, the number of modes of a fixed index $q$ is at
most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:colors}
 \frac{3M}{L}\,2R=\frac{6M}{D}.
\end{equation}
This is exactly the old multiplicity envelope. In particular, it has not
been multiplied by the number of denominator-valuation classes.

Each original row argument is still its local part plus a linear combination
of formal modes. The product of its affine Boolean row factors has the
property that the coefficient of a mode monomial $Z^\beta$ is divisible by
$2^{\sum w_q\beta_b}$. At working precision $p_t$, retain only
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 \sum_b w_{q(b)}\beta_b<p_t.
\end{equation}
All omitted terms vanish. Extract the targeted coefficients in each
packet-group tensor and substitute the source forms from
\eqref{eq:sourceform}. Each mode still has one source and one target, so the
same exact factorization holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recursion}
 F(t+\alpha\zeta)=\sum_{\beta\text{ satisfying }\eqref{eq:budget}}
                         \prod_{g=1}^{r}Z_{g,\beta}\pmod{2^{p_t}}.
\end{equation}
Here $Z_{g,\beta}$ is the contraction of the preprint's child network for
group $g$, with the new packet coefficients. Its wires and vertex groups
have not changed.

\subsection{Exact arithmetic costs}

Every quotient used in a feature can be evaluated by extracting the known
powers of two from each numerator and denominator, then inverting only
units. The net power remaining after division by $2^{E_b}$ is nonnegative
by Lemma~\ref{lem:valuation}. Thus one need not lift a previously computed
low-precision residue and divide it by a nonunit. The exact kernel prototype
implements this factorwise normalization.

Alternatively, exact rational coordinates have polynomial bit length and
can be reduced after cancellation. There are polynomially many factors,
each initially of polynomial bit length. The scalar denominators do not
depend on $V$, so nilpotent tensor coordinates introduce no new inversion
problem. Full feature tables have polynomial size in $s$; they can also be
streamed.

An entry of a packet tensor is a coefficient of a product of at most $L$
affine factors, multiplied by prescribed powers of source forms. The original
$\exp(O(L\log s))$ enumeration bound still applies, and the earlier
streamed finite-difference improvement remains available \cite{schedule}.
Neither is the leading cost under the schedule below. Descriptions retain
the preprint's creation-precision tags and canonical entry representatives.
No recursive composition of entry evaluators is introduced.

\section{Mode entropy and the polynomial-space bound}
\label{sec:entropy}

We may conservatively lower the weights to
\[
 \widehat w_q=1+4q-2s_2(q).
\]
The actual base $a+1-i$ is at least one. We may also extend the finite range
$q<D/2$ to all $q\ge0$ for an upper bound.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted partition estimate]\label{lem:entropy}
As $\theta\downarrow0$,
\[
 \sum_{q\ge0}-\ln(1-e^{-\theta\widehat w_q})
   =\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}+O(\log^2(2/\theta)).
\]
Consequently, if at most $c$ modes have each weight $\widehat w_q$,
the number $N(p,c)$ of nonnegative exponent tuples of total weight below $p$
satisfies
\[
 \ln N(p,c)\le
 \theta p+\frac{c\pi^2}{24\theta}
       +O(c\log^2(2/\theta)).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The sequence $\widehat w_q$ is increasing and
$\widehat w_q=4q+O(\log(q+2))$. Its counting function is therefore
$x/4+O(\log(x+2))$. Integrating
$-\ln(1-e^{-\theta x})$ against that counting function gives the main
term
\[
 \frac14\int_0^\infty-\ln(1-e^{-\theta x})\,dx
  =\frac1{4\theta}\sum_{j\ge1}\frac1{j^2}
  =\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}.
\]
Integration by parts bounds the discrepancy, away from the bounded initial
interval, by a constant times
$\int_1^\infty\theta\log(x+2)/(e^{\theta x}-1)\,dx$.
Splitting at $1/\theta$ gives $O(\log^2(2/\theta))$; the initial
interval is smaller. The generating product
$\prod_q(1-z^{\widehat w_q})^{-c}$ at $z=e^{-\theta}$ proves the
second assertion. If $c$ is not an integer, use the actual integer
multiplicities, bounded by $c$, in the same inequality.
\end{proof}

For $c=6M/D$, optimize the two leading terms with
$\theta=(\pi/2)\sqrt{M/(Dp_t)}$. This gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:percall}
 \ln N_\beta\le(\pi+o(1))\sqrt{Mp_t/D}.
\end{equation}
The error is uniform in the calls below: $p_t=(6+o(1))MD/u$, while
$D$ grows faster than any fixed power of $\lambda$. In particular,
$(M/D)\log^2(D/\sqrt u)=o(M/\sqrt u)$.
The analogous leading constants for slopes one and three are respectively
$2\pi$ and $2\pi/\sqrt3$, proving the matched gain.

\subsection{The schedule and every required inequality}

Set $\lambda=\ceil{\log_2s}$ and use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:schedule}
 r=256,\quad\rho=\frac6r=\frac3{128},\quad
 H=\ceil{s/\lambda},\quad
 u_d=\floor{\frac{\lambda}{700\cdot12^d}},\quad
 a_d=2\sum_{j=0}^d u_j.
\end{equation}
Calls with at most $H$ edges are leaves. At depth $d$, set
$u=u_d,a=a_d,L=2^a,D_{\rm arr}=r^{2a}$.
The geometric sum gives $a_d\le6\lambda/1925$, so
\begin{align*}
 L&=O(s^{6/1925}),&D_{\rm arr}&=O(s^{96/1925}),\\
 D_{\rm arr}^2\ceil{s^{9/10}}&=O(s^{3849/3850}),&
 LD_{\rm arr}&=O(s^{102/1925}).
\end{align*}
The last two bounds are $o(H)$; also $2L\le\ceil{s^{9/10}}$
eventually. Thus the source's three packing inequalities hold at every
active call. The child bound is $\rho M$, and new packet degrees are at
most $2L$, closing the graph invariants.

Since $s/\lambda<M\le s\rho^d$ at an active call,
\[
 u_d=\Omega(\lambda^\eta),\qquad
 \eta=1-\frac{\ln12}{\ln(128/3)}=0.3379616482\ldots>0.
\]
Thus all floors are uniformly negligible, $u_d\to\infty$, and
$u_d/M\to0$ throughout the active recursion.

Reuse the earlier exact taper count and valid precision allowance
\cite{precision,schedule}:
\begin{align}
 Q(a,u)&=L(2u-2+2^{1-u}),\nonumber\\
 h&=\ceil{\frac{M(2L-1)+1}{Q(a,u)}},\qquad
 q_t=h\max(0,u-\vTwo(t)),\label{eq:taper}\\
 p_t&=p+6hD_t+hu(a+1),\qquad D_t=L/2^{\vTwo(t)}.
 \label{eq:precision}
\end{align}
There are enough Hermite conditions because their exact total is $hQ(a,u)$.
The known Hermite multiplier loses at most $6hD_t$ powers of two; evaluating
the quotient jet loses fewer than $hu(a+1)$ more. These are exact safe
integer allowances, not floating-point estimates.

The inherited precision obeys
$p\le s\lambda^{O(1)}2^{a_{d-1}}$. A retained node has $i<u_d$, so
$D_t\ge2^{a_{d-1}+u_d+1}$. The inherited term and the final term of
\eqref{eq:precision} are therefore $o(MD_t/u_d)$ uniformly.
Also $h=(1+o(1))M/u_d$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p}
 p_t=(6+o(1))MD_t/u_d.
\end{equation}
All precisions remain polynomial in $s$. The preprint's residue field of
size $2^{4\lambda}$ still has more interpolation points than needed, since
the deformation degree is $O(s^{1+6/1925})$.

\subsection{Completing Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}

Equations~\eqref{eq:percall} and \eqref{eq:p} give at depth $d$
\[
 \log_2N_\beta\le(\kappa+o(1))
       \frac{s}{\sqrt\lambda}\gamma^d,
 \quad \kappa=\frac{\pi\sqrt{4200}}{\ln2},
 \quad\gamma=\rho\sqrt{12}=\frac{3\sqrt{12}}{128}<1.
\]
Polynomially many interpolation choices and groups per call contribute
only $O(\lambda\log\lambda)$ to the overall logarithm. Summing the
geometric exponents gives $\kappa/(1-\gamma)=C_{\rm ps}$.
Leaf work costs $2^{O(s/\lambda)}$, and entry evaluation costs
$2^{O(s^{9/10})}+\exp(O(L\log s))$ per appropriate call, both lower
order than $2^{s/\sqrt\lambda}$. The fixed polynomial in the description
length remains outside the exponential bound.

Depth-first traversal stores one tuple, one running group product and sum,
the polynomially sized interpolation data, and the creation descriptions
at each of $O(\log\lambda)$ levels. This proves polynomial space and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{A fully costed mode-graph evaluation}
\label{sec:modegraph}

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:fast}. It uses the same child networks
as before. It does not apply the contraction theorem to the high-degree
group tensors that obstruct the earlier conditional proposals.

\subsection{The number of modes for each directed group pair}

The packing construction pads every ordered full-label class to the same
multiple $H_{\rm pad}$ of $L$. At any fixed coordinate, for distinct group
symbols $g,h$, the number of packets with unordered pair $\{g,h\}$ is
\[
 \frac{2H_{\rm pad}D_{\rm arr}}{r^2L}.
\]
Indeed the two ordered symbol pairs account for exactly
$2D_{\rm arr}/r^2$ full-label classes, and these packets have $L$ slots.
One orientation has source $g$ and target $h$, the other the reverse.
There are $R$ modes of each index $q$ per orientation. Since
$H_{\rm pad}D_{\rm arr}\le3M$, each directed pair has at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paircolors}
 c_{gh}\le\frac{6M}{r^2D}
\end{equation}
modes of each $q$. Real-port omissions can only reduce the required set;
retaining dummy zero modes is harmless for an upper bound.

Partition each tuple $\beta$ by its \emph{exact} total weight $b_{gh}$ on
each directed pair. Enumerate nonnegative arrays with
$\sum_{g\ne h}b_{gh}<p_t$. Since $r=256$ is fixed and $p_t$ is polynomial
in $s$, this is a polynomial number of arrays, though with a very large
fixed exponent. Every mode tuple belongs to exactly one array.
For each pair, enumerate or index its finite list of tuples of that exact
weight. Empty lists make the array contribute zero. This indexing does not
require a counting oracle. With mode weights $w_1,\ldots,w_v$, the integer
table
\[
 D(j,b)=D(j-1,b)+D(j,b-w_j),\qquad
 D(0,0)=1,\quad D(0,b)=0\ (b>0),
\]
with negative-budget entries zero, counts unbounded nonnegative exponent
vectors of exact weight $b$ on the first $j$ modes. There are polynomially
many modes and $p_t$ is polynomial in $s$. These counts also have polynomial
bit length, since they are bounded by $(p_t+1)^v$. Thus the table, exact
rank/unrank operations, and emptiness checks take polynomial resources.
Backtracking only into branches with positive completion count enumerates
an exact-weight list with polynomial delay, including when most candidate
prefixes would be infeasible.

For one array, let $h_{gh}$ be the base-two logarithm of the size of its
pair list; ceiling it to use binary ranks adds only a constant per pair.
The group factor $Z_g$ depends only on pair indices incident with $g$.
Its value is obtained by recursively contracting the same $\rho M$-edge
child as in \eqref{eq:recursion}. No inexpensive group oracle is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Entropy for a selected set of directed pairs]
\label{lem:subsetentropy}
Write
\[
 K(M,u)=\frac{\pi\sqrt6}{\ln2}\frac{M}{\sqrt u}.
\]
For any fixed set $I$ of directed group pairs,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsetentropy}
 \sum_{e\in I}h_e\le
   \left(\sqrt{|I|/r^2}+o(1)\right)K(M,u).
\end{equation}
More generally, if disjoint pair classes have cardinalities $v_j$ and
total index lengths $H_j$, then for fixed nonnegative $w_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weightedentropy}
 \sum_jw_jH_j\le
 \left(\sqrt{\frac{\sum_j w_j^2v_j}{r^2}}+o(1)\right)K(M,u).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy} with the common per-pair color upper bound
$c=6M/(r^2D)$. For a pair with allocated weight $b_e$, the generating
product bounds its exact-weight count by $\exp(\theta_e b_e+cF(\theta_e))$,
where $F$ is the sum in Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy}. Assign weight $w_e$ to that pair, and
omit pairs with $w_e=0$. In its generating bound use parameter
$\theta_e=\theta/w_e$. Multiplying that bound by $w_e$ and adding gives
\[
 \ln2\sum_e w_eh_e
 \le\theta\sum_e b_e
  +\frac{c\pi^2}{24\theta}\sum_e w_e^2
  +O\bigl(c\log^2(2/\theta)\bigr).
\]
The number of pairs and their positive weights are fixed constants.
This bound holds also for small or zero allocated budgets; it does not
apply a large-budget saddle estimate to each pair separately.
Since $\sum_e b_e<p_t$, optimize the two displayed leading terms and
use $p_t=(6+o(1))MD/u$. The result is
\[
 \ln2\sum_e w_eh_e
 \le(\pi+o(1))\sqrt{Mp_t/D}\,
                         \sqrt{\sum_e w_e^2/r^2}.
\]
The error is uniform for the same reason as in Section~\ref{sec:entropy}.
This proves the two stated forms after division by $\ln2$; fixed index
rounding errors are lower order.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cache each group before making three block tables}

Partition the $256$ groups into blocks of sizes $85,85,86$.
For each individual group, evaluate and store its function $Z_g$ on all
incident pair-index tuples. This pays for the recursive children explicitly.
There are $2(r-1)=510$ incident directed pairs, so the number of child calls
per group is at most
\[
 2^{(a_0+o(1))K(M,u)},\qquad a_0=\sqrt{510/65536}.
\]

For a block $B$, form a table on all directed pairs joining it to its
complement. Each entry sums over its internal pair indices the product of
the cached $Z_g$ for $g\in B$. Straight enumeration of all incident indices
builds this table. For a block of size $k$ the number of incident directed
pairs and the number of boundary directed pairs are, respectively,
\[
 2rk-k^2-k,\qquad 2k(r-k).
\]
Their maxima for $k=85,86$ are $36550$ and $29240$.
Thus table construction has exponent coefficient
\[
 a_B=\sqrt{36550/65536}=0.7467989958\ldots
\]
on the scale $K(M,u)$, and storing the tables has coefficient
\[
 j_B=\sqrt{29240/65536}=0.6679573281\ldots.
\]
The individual group caches are smaller. Work inside a block uses cached
values; it does not multiply the block-table enumeration count by a new
recursive child cost.

This access cost can be realized by sequential records rather than an
assumed constant-time lookup into an exponential table. Enumerate a block's
boundary axes first and its internal axes last, and order each group's cache
by the restriction of that axis order. Projecting the full lexicographic loop
onto a group's axes either repeats a record, advances, or rewinds a suffix
when counters reset. A rewind crosses no more cache records than the full
suffix assignments just traversed. Charging rewinds to those preceding loops
bounds total tape movement by the enumeration count times the fixed number
of axes and the polynomial record length. This preserves the stated bit-cost
bound. The small prototype uses ordinary Python tables to check the algebra;
it is not an implementation of this tape-access schedule.

Combining the two directed collections between each unordered pair of blocks
into one index gives three tables on a triangle. Let their shared index
dimensions be $d_1,d_2,d_3$ with logarithms $H_1,H_2,H_3$. The contraction
is of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
 \sum_{x=1}^{d_1}\sum_{y=1}^{d_2}\sum_{z=1}^{d_3}
       A_{xz}B_{xy}C_{yz}.
\end{equation}
Enumerating all crossing indices already gives coefficient
$\sqrt{43690/65536}$ and time constant $239.828126$, with the same space
bound. The next step improves it further.

\subsection{Division-free rectangular multiplication}

Strassen's seven-product identity uses only addition, subtraction, and
multiplication \cite{strassen}. It therefore works over the current
commutative ring, including modulo powers of two and with nilpotents.
For rectangular dimensions $d_1,d_2,d_3$, split into square blocks of side
comparable to $\min d_j$, pad by at most a constant factor, and apply the
identity recursively to each square product. This gives arithmetic time
\[
 O\left(d_1d_2d_3(\min d_j)^{\omega-3}
              \poly(\log(2+\max d_j))\right),\qquad\omega=\log_2 7.
\]
The remaining inner product in \eqref{eq:triangle} is no larger in order.
The exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectcost}
 H_1+H_2+H_3-(3-\omega)\min(H_1,H_2,H_3).
\end{equation}
The three unordered block-pair classes have $14450,14620,14620$
directed pairs. Expression~\eqref{eq:rectcost} is the maximum of three
weighted sums, with weights $1,1,\omega-2$. Applying
\eqref{eq:weightedentropy}, the worst bound discounts the smallest class,
and is
\[
 (b_S+o(1))K(M,u),\qquad
 b_S=\sqrt{\frac{43690-[1-(\omega-2)^2]14450}{65536}}
 =0.7680409453\ldots .
\]
Notice $a_B<b_S$. Rectangular products can be processed one square block
at a time. Recursive Strassen storage for one block is quadratic in its
side; the three input tables and an output table fit within the already
bounded boundary-table sizes. Thus the space coefficient remains $j_B$.
Reblocking or transposing a stored matrix can be done by sorting its records
on their new indices. The largest such record set is a boundary table;
its logarithmic sorting overhead and record lengths are polynomial in $s$.
Sequential square-block arithmetic and this reordering therefore also
realize the claimed bit-operation bound without changing the space exponent.

\subsection{The entire recursive time and space cost}

Suppress polynomial factors per call. A correct upper recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fastrec}
 T_d\le 2^{(a_0+o(1))K_d}\,rT_{d+1}
          +2^{(a_B+o(1))K_d}+2^{(b_S+o(1))K_d},
\end{equation}
where $K_d\le(\kappa+o(1))s\gamma^d/\sqrt\lambda$.
The first term pays for all cached group functions; the other two build and
contract the tables. Exact weight arrays and interpolation choices multiply
the recurrence by polynomial factors.

At depth $d$, the coefficient from the preceding cached-child enumerations
and the current matrix work is at most
\[
 a_0\frac{1-\gamma^d}{1-\gamma}+b_S\gamma^d.
\]
It is bounded by
$\max\{a_0/(1-\gamma),b_S\}=b_S$, because
$a_0/(1-\gamma)=0.0960106375\ldots<b_S$.
The $a_B$ term and leaves are smaller. Hence the full time coefficient is
$\kappa b_S=C_{\rm fast}$, proving the time assertion.

The largest stored tables at depth $d$ have exponent at most $j_BK_d$.
Ancestor caches and tables have polynomially many levels and geometrically
decreasing exponent bounds, so their total has leading coefficient
$\kappa j_B=C_{\rm mem}$. Polynomial-bit ring entries and their arithmetic
costs do not change either coefficient. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:fast}.

\paragraph{Why this does not prove the old conditional targets.}
The pair-index representation was already suggested in the earlier packets.
Here the group tensors are evaluated by explicit tables and ordinary matrix
multiplication. Their work remains exponential in $M/\sqrt u$ and their
storage is subexponential in the original network size. We have not supplied
a polynomial-space, subexponential-in-the-mode-count oracle or a low-degree
factorization of the high-degree group tensors. The conditional
$s/\log s$ targets do not follow.

\section{Consequences for SAT and the limits of the result}
\label{sec:sat}

At fixed clause density, the source builds a bounded-degree Boolean network
with $O_{k,D}(n)$ wires. At unrestricted density, its sparse parity-query
reduction uses $O_k(n\log\log n)$ wires. Substitute either new engine
without changing those encodings. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives the same
orders \eqref{eq:general}--\eqref{eq:sparse}, with an improved explicit
contraction certificate. Theorem~\ref{thm:fast} gives a further time
coefficient reduction while using space of the same subexponential order
as its time.

The density-sensitive reduction of the reviewed packets can likewise be
retained. With the polynomial-space engine, its established envelope remains
\[
 (n+m)^{O_k(1)}
 2^{O_k\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
       \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}.
\]
The scalar parity lift, exact recovery budgets, and streamed coefficient
extraction are compatible with the new modes. Their results are inherited
improvements, not new contributions of this note.

\subsection{What was not obtained}

This investigation does not prove a smaller asymptotic order for the
worst-case exponent in $n$. Both new engines still have
$\log T=\Theta_{\rm bound}(s/\sqrt{\log s})$ as an upper-bound scale;
the notation here describes the scale of the certificate, not an algorithmic
lower bound. Neither $2^{O(n/\log n)}$ nor a removal of the dense-case
$\log\log n$ factor has been established.

The exact Smith profile explains why a further integral basis change in
this same full block cannot turn its linear valuation growth into a
quadratic profile. It does not rule out cancellation across modes, a more
economical reduction, a different kernel, or a different algorithm.
The fast option demonstrates that some useful sharing is possible without
the earlier structural oracle, but only yields a constant improvement on
the present entropy scale.

\section{A short arithmetic specification for the seven-product step}
\label{app:strassen}

For square block matrices $A=(A_{ij})$ and $B=(B_{ij})$, define
\begin{align*}
 M_1&=(A_{11}+A_{22})(B_{11}+B_{22}),&
 M_2&=(A_{21}+A_{22})B_{11},\\
 M_3&=A_{11}(B_{12}-B_{22}),&
 M_4&=A_{22}(B_{21}-B_{11}),\\
 M_5&=(A_{11}+A_{12})B_{22},&
 M_6&=(A_{21}-A_{11})(B_{11}+B_{12}),\\
 M_7&=(A_{12}-A_{22})(B_{21}+B_{22}).
\end{align*}
Then
\[
 AB=\begin{pmatrix}
 M_1+M_4-M_5+M_7 & M_3+M_5\\
 M_2+M_4 & M_1-M_2+M_3+M_6
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
These are polynomial identities with integer coefficients. They remain valid
modulo $2^p$ without an inverse of two and without a field assumption.
Recursing gives exponent $\log_2 7$. A depth-first schedule retains
$O(d^2)$ ring elements for a square block of side $d$; summing its geometric
storage across recursion levels preserves that order. This supplies the
specific matrix multiplication guarantee used in Theorem~\ref{thm:fast}.
