\section{Results and comparison rules}
\label{sec:results}
Three different kinds of progress survive a comparison against the newly combined source corpus: smaller explicit contraction coefficients, a lower arithmetic order for exact matching-potential updates, and a wider practical implementation of an already known coefficient identity. The independent public formalization is a fourth development, concerning proof status rather than the novelty or implementation of our refinements. Keeping these claims separate prevents a real improvement in one category from being mistaken for a result in another.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale}
 \lambda=\ceil{\log_2s},\qquad E(s)=s/\sqrt\lambda,\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}.
\end{equation}
Here $s$ is the normalized number of Boolean wires, and $\LL$ is the input-description length. A time coefficient $C$ denotes
\[
 T(s,\LL)\le\poly(s+\LL)\,2^{(C+o(1))E(s)}.
\]
A displayed space coefficient has the analogous meaning; ``polynomial'' means no such subexponential cache is required. A decimal rounded upward is a safe \emph{eventual} coefficient, not a finite-size runtime prediction. Every coefficient in this report is resource- and premise-qualified.

\subsection{What does not follow}
The inherited density-sensitive envelope remains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sat}
 \poly_k(n,m)\,
 2^{O_k\!\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
     \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}.
\end{equation}
The schedule changes an explicit normalized constant, not the power of $\log n$. A wire-size constant cannot be inserted unchanged into a bound in SAT variables $n$; the encoding's wire count, degree and description overhead must first be paid. Nor is there a common published leading constant allowing a numerical comparison with the independent crossing-decomposition algorithm.

A component arithmetic bound is not a bit-operation bound unless operand lengths and setup are charged. A component wall-time ratio is not a SAT speedup. A source scan is not kernel checking. Published kernel acceptance of specified statements is not automatic verification of every prose claim, cost-model explanation or new refinement. These distinctions will be maintained throughout.

\section{Normalized interfaces and proof dependencies}
\label{sec:interface}
Our theorems refine the contraction architecture in the supplied ProveIt packets. The normalized interface allows at most $s$ Boolean wires, maximum root degree $\ceil{s^{.9}}$ for the generic branch, polynomially many vertices and polynomial tensor-description length. Root precision and truncation lengths are at most $O(s)$. Original admissible entries can be evaluated in
\[
 \poly(s+\LL)\,2^{O(s^{.9})}
\]
time and polynomial space. Entries are represented at immutable creation precision; reducing a displayed residue does not authorize redefining an entry later at a more convenient precision.

Arithmetic occurs in a free finite quotient of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ring}
 (\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[U,V]/(f(U),V^\ell),
\end{equation}
with polynomial module rank, $\deg f=O(\log s)$ in the asymptotic interface, and polynomially bounded coordinate bit lengths. This ring can have zero divisors and nilpotents. A field algorithm cannot simply be invoked as if all nonzero elements were invertible.

The following interfaces remain inherited:
\begin{enumerate}
\item exact deformation and coefficient recovery;
\item full slope-four source/target-local modes and their integrality;
\item exact list indexing and weighted entropy estimates;
\item nearly lossless asymmetric packing, including degree and precision gates;
\item recursive child-factor construction and affordable entry access;
\item immutable creation precision and valid Hermite reconstruction;
\item the SAT-to-normalized-contraction reduction, including density and size accounting.
\end{enumerate}
The new finite lemmas and resource sums are proved relative to these interfaces. Static review of all new packets did not replace a full independent proof of the original contraction development. Published Lean/nanoda checking of the independent Anthropic source does not discharge these imports.

\subsection{Weighted entropy and paid recurrences}
At a call with mass $M$ and retained taper $u$, define
\[
 K=\kappa M/\sqrt u.
\]
For fixed directed edge coefficients $z$ on an $r$-group partition, the imported weighted-entropy statement gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
 \sum_e z_eh_e\le\left(\frac{\|z\|_2}{r}+o(1)\right)K,
\end{equation}
uniformly on the active schedule. The $h_e$ are exact mode-list logarithms. When a finite portfolio of support vectors is used, the maximum of finitely many error terms remains $o(1)$; no unbounded or data-dependent family is being silently inserted.

For a cached evaluator, constructing singleton group tables requires actual recursive child contractions. The common incident-pair coefficient is
\[
 \alpha_r=\frac{\sqrt{2(r-1)}}r.
\]
If local aggregation has coefficient $b_r$, the paid recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paid}
 T_d\le\poly(s)\left[
 2^{(\alpha_{r_d}+o(1))K_d}T_{d+1}
 +2^{(b_{r_d}+o(1))K_d}\right].
\end{equation}
Its exponent contains child-call prefixes. Using only the root aggregation cost can understate time. In contrast, cached tables at different depths coexist additively in memory; their exponents do not add. Local storage needs its own input/output/workspace envelope.

A streamed evaluator revisits entries and pays a sum of path contributions, but can execute depth first in polynomial space. Its local coefficient cannot be substituted into a cached maximum, or vice versa. Polynomial factors per recursion level contribute lower-order exponent terms only after depth and description growth have been controlled.

\section{Rectangular support portfolios}
\label{sec:rect}
The useful composition is to replace \emph{every} operation in each complete tree plan by the genuine rectangular cost envelope, while preserving the recursive entry costs. It is not enough to accelerate only the final triangle. This section first gives the three-plan certificate, which isolates the effect of combining existing ingredients. Any larger certified finite menu uses the same lemma with its number of plans substituted.

\subsection{The imported rectangular bridge}
For sorted nonnegative logarithmic dimensions $x\ge y\ge z$, the incoming rectangular packet proves the bit-cost envelope
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectcost}
 F(x,y,z)=\max\{d(x+y),\,x+vy+wz\},
\end{equation}
up to a polynomial factor in the normalized input parameters, where
\[
 \varepsilon=10^{-5},\qquad d=1+\varepsilon/2,
 \qquad v=\frac{47}{56}+\varepsilon,\qquad w=\frac{15}{28}.
\]
The first branch cannot be dropped. Arithmetic exponent $2+\varepsilon$ does not imply exactly quadratic work; the input/output term pays tables and is essential to the original certificate.

The external matrix facts needed are only dual exponent $\alpha>.3$ and square exponent $\omega<19/8$. The primary paper \emph{New Bounds for Matrix Multiplication: from Alpha to Omega}, arXiv:2307.07970, supplies the stronger inequalities $\alpha\ge.321334$ and $\omega\le2.371552$. No current-record claim is needed. \cite{matrix} Fixed rational bilinear algorithms, tensor products at fixed base and constant-factor padding yield the required intermediate aspect ratios. Blocking the longest dimension handles unequal large axes. Dimension permutations are performed on the finite tensor decomposition, not by assuming a transposed cached program has free workspace.

To apply the backend to \eqref{eq:ring}, the incoming bridge performs coordinate products over suitable odd primes, omits finitely many denominator primes, reconstructs the bounded integer coordinate products and then reduces in the actual ring. \subsection{Finite support lemma}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:support}
For a fixed menu of $k$ complete plans on $r$ groups, let
\[
 Q_r=\max_{v_j\in V_j\ (1\le j\le k)}
       \left\|\sum_{j=1}^kv_j\right\|_2^2.
\]
Under the weighted-entropy interface, minimum-plan work has local coefficient
\[
 b_r^2=\frac{2Q_r}{k^2r^2L_0^2}.
\]
The same coefficient bounds local stored arrays and backend workspace up to polynomial factors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every nonnegative $g$,
\begin{align*}
 \min_jF_j(g)&\le\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^kF_j(g)\\
 &=\frac1{kL_0}\max_{v_j\in V_j}
          \left(\sum_{j=1}^kv_j\right)\cdot g.
\end{align*}
For a fixed support tuple, put its coefficient on both directed orientations of each edge. Its directed squared norm is at most $2Q_r/(k^2L_0^2)$. Apply \eqref{eq:entropy} and take the maximum over the fixed finite set of tuples. This yields $(b_r+o(1))K$ uniformly. It neither interchanges min and max nor invokes unrealizable permutations of edge weights.

Every intermediate table is an input or output of one of the counted operations. In \eqref{eq:rectcost}, $d(x+y)$ dominates every pairwise dimension sum because $x,y$ are the two largest logarithms and $d>1$. Thus the selected maximum also bounds each stored array. The imported workspace envelope is bounded by the same largest input/output scale.
\end{proof}

For matching $j$, let $U_j$ count edges with neither endpoint assigned, $L_{ja}$ count edges whose left endpoint has label $a$ and whose right endpoint is free, and $C_{jab}$ count completed edges with label pair $(a,b)$. With the incoming threshold exponent $h_j$, the conditional-product table factors exactly as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
 P_{jab}=s_jx_{ja}B_{jab},\quad
 s_j=t^{-h_j}d^{U_j},\quad x_{ja}=c^{L_{ja}},\quad B_{jab}=t^{C_{jab}}.
\end{equation}
Maintain
\[
 R_{ja}=\sum_bB_{jab},\qquad
 S_j=\sum_ax_{ja}R_{ja},\qquad
 \Phi=\sum_js_jS_j.
\]
Initially $B$ is all ones, $R_{ja}=Q$, $x_{ja}=1$ and $S_j=Q^2$. Store only nondefault $B$ cells in a dictionary. At most one new cell arises per completed edge, so potential-state storage is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matchspace}
 O\!\left(KQ+\min\{M,KQ^2\}\right)
\end{equation}
rational values. This excludes the graph, the requested trace and the returned dense $Q\times Q$ load matrix. Producing the unchanged dense output still requires $\Theta(Q^2)$ writes and cells. Initialization includes $O(KQ)$ state writes plus the separately charged exact powers.

\subsection{Left-vertex candidates}
For an incident edge in matching $j$, the right endpoint remains unassigned. Assigning the current left vertex to label $a$ replaces $s_j$ by $s_j/d$ and $x_{ja}$ by $cx_{ja}$. Its common baseline change is
\[
 (s_j/d-s_j)S_j,
\]
and candidate $a$ receives the additional correction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leftdelta}
 (s_j/d)(c-1)x_{ja}R_{ja}.
\end{equation}
The $Q$ candidate corrections require $O(Q)$ operations per incident matching. Once a label is chosen, update $s_j,x_{ja}$ and $S_j$ in constant state work for that incident edge.

\subsection{Right-vertex candidates}
The left neighbor already has some label $a$. First replace $x_{ja}$ by $x_{ja}/c$, producing the common change
\[
 s_j(x_{ja}/c-x_{ja})R_{ja}.
\]
Candidate right label $b$ receives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rightdelta}
 s_j(x_{ja}/c)(t-1)B_{jab}.
\end{equation}
After selection, multiply $B_{jab}$ by $t$ and update $R_{ja}$ and $S_j$ in constant state work. An absent dictionary cell means $B_{jab}=1$, not zero.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:matching}
For the fixed left-then-right order and exact arithmetic, the factorized routine preserves every candidate potential, the least-label tie rule, the selected potential trace and the final label-pair loads of the maintained dense routine. Candidate-update work is $O(MQ+VQ)$ rational operations, compared with $O(MQ^2+VQ)$ for the maintained dense table.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:factor} is the product of the unassigned-edge, one-assigned-endpoint and completed-edge conditional factors, grouped by their state. Equations~\eqref{eq:leftdelta} and \eqref{eq:rightdelta} are exact algebraic expansions of the affected products. A vertex has at most one incident edge in any one matching, so no update for that matching is double-counted. Summing the common part and candidate corrections therefore reconstructs every dense potential exactly. Equal candidate values give the same minimum and, with the same smallest-label rule, the same choice. Induction over processed vertices proves all later states, traces and loads agree.

Each edge has two endpoint updates and contributes $Q$ candidate corrections at each. Scanning the label candidates at each vertex costs $O(VQ)$. Chosen-state updates are constant per incident edge. This gives the stated count.
\end{proof}

The proposition concerns update arithmetic. First-fit coloring can cost $O(MK)$, input sorting costs $O(V\log V)$, and initialization includes exact exponentiation. Fraction numerator and denominator growth remains charged; no constant-time-big-integer assumption is made. The factors have the same scale of bit growth as the original conditional products. Thus the algebraic improvement is meaningful without claiming that every rational operation has equal wall time.

\subsection{Positive-support scoring in both assignment phases}
The new factorization also composes with the \emph{inherited} positive-support delta technique. This further refinement is an exact application to the matching potential, not a claim to invent sparse conditional expectations. Store both $x$ and $B$ with implicit default one; no full row-sum arrays are needed.

During the left phase, no right vertex has been assigned, so $B$ is identically one. For incident matching $j$, put $s'_j=s_j/d$. After subtracting the label-independent unit baseline, candidate $a$ has nonnegative delta
\[
 \delta_a=\sum_{j\text{ incident}}s'_j(c-1)Q(x_{ja}-1).
\]
Only nondefault $x$ entries contribute. In the right phase, an incident edge has known left label $a$; first put $x'_{ja}=x_{ja}/c$, deleting the entry if it becomes one. Candidate $b$ has delta
\[
 \delta_b=\sum_{(j,a)\text{ incident}}
                 s_jx'_{ja}(t-1)(B_{jab}-1).
\]
Only stored cells in the relevant $B$ rows contribute. All deltas are nonnegative. Conditional averaging recovers each full candidate potential from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparsedelta}
 \Phi_{\rm candidate}=\Phi_{\rm current}+\delta_{\rm candidate}
                         -\frac1Q\sum_{\rm labels}\delta_{\rm label}.
\end{equation}
If fewer than $Q$ labels have positive delta, choose the least absent label; otherwise minimize $(\delta,\mathrm{label})$ lexicographically. This preserves every label, tie and selected potential by induction. At completion, all $x$ dictionaries are empty, and an independent exact final-potential check is
\[
 \sum_js_j\left[Q^2+\sum_{(a,b)\text{ stored}}(B_{jab}-1)\right].
\]
Let $X_j(v)$ count nondefault row factors at a left step, and $C_{ja}(v)$ count nondefault cells in a relevant row at a right step. Scoring work is
\[
 O\!\left(M+V+
 \sum_{v\text{ left}}\sum_{j\text{ incident}}X_j(v)+
 \sum_{v\text{ right}}\sum_{(j,a)\text{ incident}}C_{ja}(v)\right).
\]
Finding the least absent label uses at most the touched-support size plus one probes, not an unconditional scan of $Q$ labels. Each support is at most $Q$, so the worst case remains $O(MQ+VQ)$; the improvement is support-sensitive. At most $M$ nondefault cells of each kind exist, giving $O(K+M)$ rational potential-state storage. Input, assignments, trace and the unchanged dense $\Theta(Q^2)$ load output remain separately charged, as do coloring, sorting, initial powers and rational bit costs.

If a diagnostic requests every candidate value, constructing that output still costs $O(VQ)$ and is paid. Ordinary benchmarks request identical assignments, matching data, thresholds, selected trace and dense loads from all three methods; sparse averaging deliberately avoids materializing an unrequested candidate vector. Full candidate equality is tested separately. The array-factorized routine remains the stronger comparator for this additional sparse step.

\section{A new mixed-root implementation of an inherited identity}
\label{sec:mixedroots}
The projective coefficient identity is \emph{already present} in the
inherited kernel-improvements report, including individual root orders
$\beta_j+1$. Its implementation handles exponents one and two. The newer
round-three implementation uses one common odd order. Our new contribution
is an independently authored mixed-order $3/5$ implementation, extending
practical coverage to mixed exponents two and four in one fixed quotient
ring. This is not a new permanent formula or a new SAT exponent bound.

\paragraph{Exact identity and selection.}
Let $H(Z)=\prod_{r=1}^R\sum_j a_{rj}Z_j$ be homogeneous with
$\sum_j\beta_j=R$. Set zero-target variables to zero and omit them, handling
the empty product separately. Fix a minimum-target coordinate $k$ to one.
For every $j\ne k$, choose an available odd order
$q_j>\max(\beta_j,\beta_k)$ and an orthogonal root $\omega_j$. Then
\[
 [Z^\beta]H=
 \left(\prod_{j\ne k}q_j\right)^{-1}
 \sum_{0\le g_j<q_j}
 \left(\prod_{j\ne k}\omega_j^{-\beta_jg_j}\right)
 H\bigl(Z_k=1,(Z_j=\omega_j^{g_j})_{j\ne k}\bigr).
\]
Orthogonality retains a monomial only if
$\alpha_j=\beta_j+q_jm_j$, $m_j\ge0$, on every free coordinate.
Homogeneity gives $\alpha_k=\beta_k-\sum q_jm_j$, forcing all $m_j=0$.
Every divisor is odd, so this formula needs no guard bits.
For an available-order function $m(b)$ equal to the smallest available odd
order above $b$, a minimum-target pivot minimizes
$\prod_{j\ne k}m(\max(\beta_j,\beta_k))$: swapping a larger pivot for a
smaller one leaves their exchanged factor equal and can only decrease
all other factors.

For $\beta_j\in\{2,4\}$, orders $3$ and $5$ give
$P=\prod_j(\beta_j+1)/(1+\min_j\beta_j)$ evaluations. For example,
$\beta=(2,2,4,4)$ takes $75$ mixed-root evaluations, versus $125$ with
common order five and $112$ with the strongest inherited antipodal grid.
This is an implementation of the existing individual-order theorem.

\paragraph{Ring and precision.}
All timed algorithms use the same ring
\[
 (\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[U,V]/(U^4+U+1,V^\ell),
\]
with rank $4\ell$. The residue $U$ is checked to have order fifteen.
Classical precision-doubling Newton steps
$x\leftarrow x-x(x^{15}-1)/15$ lift it from $k$ to $\min(2k,p)$
bits. Writing $e=x^{15}-1$ gives
$(1+e)(1-e/15)^{15}=1+O(e^2)$; only odd $15$ is inverted.
The old bits and root identity are checked at each step. The preserved
bitwise routine is a regression oracle. There are $\lceil\log_2p\rceil$
lifting rounds, not $p-1$. The lifted root's fifth and third powers supply the order-three and order-five
roots. The program checks their full orthogonality sums, not merely
multiplicative order. The original source degree
$e=4\lceil\log_2s\rceil$ already contains the required residue subfield,
but representing and lifting its roots must still be paid. Our prototype
does not silently enlarge the source ring or implement arbitrary source
embeddings. Coefficients are canonicalized at immutable creation precision
$p$, and the result is returned at $p$.

\paragraph{Streaming and matched comparisons.}
A common primitive fifteenth root permits a single integer phase index
modulo fifteen. Mixed-radix Gray traversal updates the $R$ row sums after
one coordinate changes. With precomputed neighboring-root increments,
work is $O(RP+R\sum_jq_j)$ ring operations and storage is
$O(R\sum_jq_j+R+t)$ ring elements. No grid-indexed table is stored.
The common-root comparator shares the same arithmetic, validation and
Gray kernel. The antipodal comparator is \emph{anisotropic}: step one
for even targets and two for odd targets. Its guard loss is
$v_2(\prod\beta_j!)+\sum_{\beta_j\text{ odd}}\beta_j-1$, not the
larger uniform step-two loss. Existing sign/cube kernels remain in the
portfolio; the demonstration dispatcher does not claim to supersede them.

\paragraph{Exact checks.}
Normal and optimized Python both pass $1{,}880$ exact coefficient
equalities, $96$ affine-adapter equalities, $83{,}696$ independent monomial alias checks, $125$ Gray-grid
checks, $12$ doubling-versus-bitwise root fixtures and seven rejection controls. Coverage
includes noncanonical and negative coordinates, precision one, nilpotents,
zero and singleton targets, mixed odd targets, and exhaustive small binary
matrices. These are component regression tests, not a formal proof or a
complete SAT run. The imported programs were not executed.

\section{Barriers and unresolved interfaces}
\label{sec:barriers}
The strongest new results are constant improvements and component implementation changes. Several tempting routes to a smaller logarithmic order remain blocked by specific mathematical obligations. These are useful negative results, not claims that no other algorithm can do better.

\subsection{Independent-jet Hermite recovery}
The rectangular packet extends its Hermite optimization to arbitrary asymmetric node multiplicities. Inactive-node extension makes the independent-jet precision objective convex; averaging residue-tree automorphisms fixing zero gives a valuation-symmetric optimum. Backward rational recurrences compute the finite real optimum, and componentwise ceilings increase the normalized objective by less than two. Within this fixed independent-error model, the leading six survives arbitrary multiplicities.

That is a model-specific barrier. It does not rule out exploiting correlations among jets actually generated by the contraction, nor establish that every abstract admissible error pattern is attainable. A proposed improvement must exhibit and prove such additional structure rather than merely redistribute independent precision budgets.

\subsection{Explicit tables and balanced trees}
The table-width arguments apply to full boundary tables on complete pair graphs or the relaxed Euclidean entropy ball. A fixed tree has a balanced node, leading to a certificate width at least $2K/3$. Adaptive trees still have a uniform-positive-vector witness, with lower bound
\[
 \frac{4K}{9\sqrt{1-1/r}}.
\]
The related cardinality statement $2\ceil{r/3}(r-\ceil{r/3})$ concerns explicit full tables. These observations constrain the representation being optimized, not SAT itself, implicit contractions, cancellation-based methods or every feasible tensor family. An abstract equal-inventory witness is not proof that each SAT tensor realizes its extremal nonzero entries.

The four-plan success does not contradict these barriers: it improves the upper certificate within a finite menu and leaves the logarithmic scale unchanged. It also does not prove that four plans are best. Larger portfolios, different unbalanced trees or new backends might improve constants, but the finite costs of every table and recursive entry must remain included.

\subsection{Precision peeling and degree flow}
Discarding only a polylogarithmically thin budget tail retains the same leading entropy and inherited precision exponent. No free reset of cumulative precision follows from exposing a small residue or from a mode that currently evaluates to zero. In the Cauchy source recurrence, a zero displayed residue can later recover after normalization; it is unsound to terminate based only on that observation.

The source degree-flow identity is a valid algebraic invariant, but it does not supply the missing compiler. To obtain a genuinely smaller logarithmic order by recursion, one needs a proved interface simultaneously preserving low degree, compressed mode multiplicity, bounded local graph degree, short immutable descriptions and affordable recursive entry evaluation. None of the new coefficient schedules or public release automatically provides that package. This is the exact structural open loop rather than a vaguely missing optimization.

\subsection{Practical integration boundary}
The fixed rank-four mixed-root prototype does not find embeddings in every upstream source ring. The fast rectangular backend is not implemented end to end. The matching factorization is not the asymmetric labeler of the sharpest contraction theorem. Consequently, a complete contraction harness must still choose compatible branches, track creation precision, materialize only justified features, and account for all conversions and output requirements.

The next useful experiment is not a larger isolated speedup against an obsolete routine. It is an exact component-to-contraction integration with instrumented entry counts, bit lengths, cache sizes and preparation costs. Its output should reveal how much total time is spent in the optimized component and whether the hypotheses used in the asymptotic schedule are maintained. That would be new evidence; it is not inferred from the present synthetic tables.

\section{Conclusions}
The strongest final generic certificate in this review is the explicit four-plan rectangular composition: safe eventual time $30.492$ and space $28.854$ on the normalized $E(s)$ scale. Polynomial-space generic time is safely $46.072$. Exact outputs, carefully strengthened comparators and adverse cases support these component claims. They do not support a universal practical crossover or an end-to-end SAT speedup.

The independent Anthropic release now has public source and exact-tree published Lean/nanoda acceptance. That is a substantial proof-status change and deserves a direct correction of the older description. It does not alter the scope of the new normalized refinements, erase semantic auditing duties or supply a numerical performance comparison between the two independent architectures.

A smaller logarithmic order remains a separate research problem. It requires new structural control of recovery, degree, representation or summation, not another unqualified decimal constant. The present bundle makes the positive results reproducible while stating that boundary explicitly.
