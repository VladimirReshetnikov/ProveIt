\section{Why the SAT exponent order is still unchanged}
\label{sec:limits}

The new algorithms accelerate a finite graphical sum that was already
present in the contraction proof. Their local costs remain
$2^{\Theta(M/\sqrt u)}$ at the level of the proved upper-bound
expressions. The admissible taper range remains proportional to
$\log s$, because packet label counts and the high-degree packing gates
must remain polynomially bounded. The complete recurrence therefore
still yields a certificate of the form $2^{(C+o(1))\E(s)}$.
This observation describes the present analysis, not a lower bound on
the true running time of these algorithms or on every possible algorithm.

The two distinct routes to a better order should be kept separate.
One is to evaluate the mode graph faster than its present entropy scale,
using structure beyond arbitrary pair-index factors. The other is to
decrease the effective size or deformation degree before the graph is
formed, while preserving a usable recursive representation. The
degree-flow identity in Section~\ref{sec:degree-flow-new} contributes
to the second route. It supplies an exact conserved quantity but not
the required compiler.

Taking an infimum of constants, changing the base of a logarithm,
using a stronger matrix multiplication exponent, or omitting a
lower-order entry routine does not by itself establish
$o(s/\sqrt{\log s})$ in the exponent. Similarly, an unproved summation
oracle cannot be inserted into a recurrence and then counted as a
completed improvement. We retain the explicit resource accounting
throughout the new proofs.

\section{Reproducibility and verification}
\label{sec:verification}

The archive contains the complete LaTeX source, compiled PDF, original
Python prototypes, exact numerical certificates, source fingerprints,
an independent review, and rebuild instructions. The scripts use
Python's standard library. The exploratory numerical schedule searches
are not dependencies of any theorem or certificate.

\subsection{What the executable checks establish}

The quartet checker supplies a generic exact finite pair-graph evaluator,
an exhaustive reference evaluator, and an instrumented child-call ledger.
Its checks include $15{,}625$ four-group inequalities, $2{,}000$ weighted
tree profiles, and $80$ exact contraction comparisons over the nonfield
ring $(\mathbb Z/64\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^2)$. Every one of the $14{,}056$
leaf calls in those comparisons agrees with the predicted product of
index-domain sizes. Three additional fixtures reduce calls
$64\to16$, $128\to32$, and $256\to64$, with equal nonzero outputs.
These fixtures validate exact work counts; they are not wall-clock
measurements or SAT benchmarks.

The joint-objective checker verifies all endpoint-color covariance sums
for nine and twelve groups, $28$ integer-weight objective examples, and
$27$ exact finite mode-graph contractions modulo $2048$. Its rational
interval computation certifies every competing recurrence path and each
packing gate. Independently written code enumerates all $1{,}680$ labelled
partitions for nine groups and all $34{,}650$ for twelve groups, confirming
the same covariance constants and testing additional integer-weight examples.
A separate cached-block prototype checks $24$ contractions over
$(\mathbb Z/64\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^2)$, including all $945$ group-oracle
calls and $1{,}052$ block-construction visits in its fixtures.
The prototypes use literal final summation to check equality; they do not
implement or benchmark the theoretical fast-matrix backend.

The degree-flow checker verifies $80$ exact polynomial examples,
$2{,}598$ mode sectors, $640$ Boolean evaluations, $48$ permanent
coefficient identities, and $40$ low-degree deformation identities.
A negative control explicitly detects the incorrect rule that omits
outgoing-mode degree. This tests the algebraic invariant without
pretending to supply the missing recursive entry evaluator.

The numerical certificates use rational upper and lower bounds for
$\pi$ and logarithms and integer-square-root brackets. Ordinary floating
point is not needed to establish the coefficient inequalities. All
checks use explicit exceptions rather than removable Python assertions.

\subsection{Proof and implementation status}

The new separator and covariance inequalities are proved for all
nonnegative weights. The finite tests support the corresponding
implementations and arithmetic ledgers; they do not prove the
asymptotic theorems by sampling. The global contraction consequences
retain the imported identities and hypotheses in Section~\ref{sec:imports}.
No new Lean or Rocq formalization was built, and no complete
subexponential SAT solver was implemented or benchmarked.

The source manifest fingerprints $347$ files in the pinned requested
folders and the vendored preprint, plus all $230$ members of the five
incoming ZIPs. Fingerprinting establishes source identity; it is not
proof verification. The comparison ledger identifies all twenty
packets and their stronger-assumption or conditional statements.

\section{Further research questions}
\label{sec:further}

\paragraph{1. The optimal adaptive separator constant.}
For a fixed group count $r$, define
\[
 \Gamma_r=\sup_{h\ge0,\,\|h\|_2=1}
             \min_T\max_{v}\sum_{e\in E_v(T)}h_e,
\]
where $T$ ranges over binary separator trees and $E_v(T)$ is the union
of its cut-pair sets along the path to $v$. The quartet inequality is
sharp for four singleton groups within its three balanced trees, but
the repeated-quartet bound need not be sharp for larger $r$. A proof
of a smaller $\Gamma_r$, with a constructive selector and all expensive
leaf calls paid, would improve the polynomial-space coefficient again.
Such a result would still need a separate argument to change the
logarithmic order.

\paragraph{2. Choose table construction and final contraction together.}
The six-cost theorem shows why forming block tables and contracting them
should be optimized together. A larger finite family of full computation
trees may yield a better covariance or minimax bound. It could include
binary block construction, direct construction, rectangular-matrix
choices, and memory constraints under one objective. Exact pair cardinalities
make the finite search computable; a new analysis must control all
stages under the same selected partition.

\paragraph{3. Compile the conserved degree budget.}
The degree-flow identity permits group degree to move along a mode
without increasing its global sum. Can a representation preserve that
budget, compressed mode multiplicity, bounded graph degree, and
subdominant entry evaluation simultaneously? A useful positive result
must construct the entry evaluator. A useful negative result must be
restricted to a precisely specified representation or oracle model.
The permanent identity alone is not such a lower bound.

\paragraph{4. Exploit actual sparse crossing blocks.}
The incoming adaptive Smith-mode packet reduces the mode inventory for
some actual real-port matrices. It cannot increase the full padded
kernel's Smith slope uniformly. It remains possible that the specific
blocks generated by a SAT reduction have additional sparsity or rank
structure that combines with the present adaptive graph evaluation.
The question should be studied on recursively generated blocks, with
preprocessing and valuation-normalization costs included.

\paragraph{5. Close the verification gap at the right interface.}
A compact formal development can begin with the mode-graph identity,
exact list indexing, quartet selection, and finite covariance formulas.
These are smaller and more isolated obligations than an end-to-end
rebuild of the upstream SAT claim. A subsequent implementation should
measure actual mode-graph workloads and keep those results distinct
from the theoretical fast-matrix backend and from SAT-level timings.

The completed deliverable is a stronger pair of explicit contraction
certificates and an exact algebraic invariant for the next order-improvement
attempt. The stronger global asymptotic target remains open in this work.
