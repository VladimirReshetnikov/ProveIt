\begin{abstract}
We review the fifteen integrated refinement packets and all five incoming
SAT archives at ProveIt commit \texttt{fd0edd17e6a2}. We obtain two
smaller explicit upper bounds within their shared exact tensor-contraction
framework. Adaptive four-block separator conditioning gives
\[
 T\le (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(50.30468921410+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}}
\]
in polynomial space. A covariance bound controlling all three block constructions and the
final matrix contraction simultaneously gives time and space coefficient
$31.25239731266$ on the same scale. The strongest previously
displayed coefficients are $55.70947831891$ in polynomial space and
$35.35458805840$ with subexponential space. We also compare against a
stronger $55.40466590661$ polynomial-space composition of incoming results.

These are reductions of the leading coefficient in an exponential upper
bound. We do not obtain a smaller order than
$n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ for general-density SAT, or
$n/\sqrt{\log n}$ at fixed density. The stronger conditional proposals in
the corpus remain conditional. A separate exact degree-flow lemma identifies
a conserved algebraic resource, together with the representation and entry
evaluation obligations still preventing its use for an order improvement.
The report includes proofs, reproducible exact finite checks, immutable
source provenance, and integration notes. The new results import the
upstream deformation and recovery identities; this is not an independent
certification of the claimed refutation of ETH.
\end{abstract}

\section{Outcome and the meaning of the improvement}
\label{sec:outcome}

The requested goal was to improve on the combined frontier of the recent
proposals. The successful part of this investigation concerns the cost of
evaluating their mode graph. Choosing its contraction structure after its
exact index cardinalities are known gives a smaller certified exponential
bound. The investigation did not establish a smaller logarithmic order.
Both statements are essential to interpreting the result.

Put $\lambda=\ceil{\log_2s}$ and
$\E(s)=s/\sqrt\lambda$. The size $s$ counts normalized Boolean wires,
not SAT variables. Let $\mathcal L$ denote the total input description
length. The common interface consists of a bipartite Boolean tensor network
with at most $s$ wires, maximum degree at most $\ceil{s^{9/10}}$, and
entries in
\[
 (\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell),\qquad 1\le P_0,\ell\le s.
\]
The original entry routines have time
$(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ and polynomial space.
The fixed constants in these requirements are part of the interface.
The usual unramified extension of the scalar ring is used internally.
Tensor descriptions retain their creation precisions and their original
canonical representatives, as required by the source construction
\cite{upstream,asymmetric}.

\begin{theorem}[New relative contraction bounds]\label{thm:main}
Under this interface and the imported exact identities listed in
Section~\ref{sec:imports}, there are deterministic exact algorithms with
the following bounds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Polynomial space and time
$(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{(C_{\rm Q}+o(1))\E(s)}$, where
\[
 50.30468921409<C_{\rm Q}<50.30468921410.
\]
\item Time $(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{(C_{\rm A}+o(1))\E(s)}$ and
space $(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{(C_{\rm S}+o(1))\E(s)}$, where
\[
 C_{\rm A}=C_{\rm S}=\frac{\pi}{\ln2}
 \sqrt{40\log_2 9\cdot\frac{2489356}{6638625}}
 =31.2523973126509\ldots .
\]
\end{enumerate}
The first algorithm is proved in Section~\ref{sec:quartet}; the second in
Section~\ref{sec:joint-objective}. The $o(1)$ terms tend to zero for fixed
interface constants. For example, $50.305$ and $31.253$ are safe eventual
time coefficients, and $31.253$ is also a safe eventual space coefficient for
the second algorithm.
\end{theorem}

Here a space coefficient means a bound
$(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{(C_S+o(1))\E(s)}$. The two new algorithms
represent different memory budgets; the cached one does not provide
polynomial space. Lower-degree specializations in the corpus impose
stronger input assumptions and are not comparable to the general-degree
rows of this table.

The polynomial-space coefficient decreases by about $9.20\%$ against the
stronger composed baseline. The cached coefficient decreases by about
$11.60\%$ against the strongest displayed cached bound. More precisely,
the ratios of the corresponding exponential certificate functions are
\[
 2^{-(5.0999766925+o(1))\E(s)},\qquad
 2^{-(4.1021907458+o(1))\E(s)}.
\]
Both tend to zero. This comparison is between upper-bound functions.
It proves neither a ratio of actual algorithm runtimes nor a practical
speedup for SAT instances. The thresholds implicit in these constructions
can be enormous.

\subsection{Preserving the full existing envelope}
Different packets also offer density-sensitive bounds, low-degree bounds,
and other specialized interfaces. A finite algorithm portfolio can keep
those methods and add the two new ones. On inputs satisfying several
interfaces, interleaving the applicable exact algorithms incurs only a
fixed scheduling overhead relative to the first to finish. A memory-restricted
portfolio includes only algorithms respecting that restriction. Thus the
new results can strengthen the common general-degree certificate while
retaining every existing specialized option.

No such claim is made for an unimplemented conditional proposal. In
particular, a hypothetical $n/\log n$ counter would asymptotically beat
the present $n/\sqrt{\log n}$ scale. We have not constructed that counter.

\subsection{SAT-level bounds and conditional targets}
The two primary manuscripts~\cite{upstream,claude} state the general-density scale
\[
 (n+m)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}.
\]
The OpenAI Appendix D also states the fixed-density scale
$2^{O_{k,D}(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$. The density packets~\cite{densityprior} refine the common
envelope to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sat-envelope}
 (n+m)^{O_k(1)}
 2^{O_k\left(\frac n{\sqrt{\log n}}
 \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}.
\end{equation}
For example, density $m/n=\Theta(\log\log n)$ replaces a
$\log\log n$ numerator by $\log\log\log n$ in the corresponding
density bound. Our methods can be used within this framework, but the
displayed order in~\eqref{eq:sat-envelope} is unchanged.

The stronger proposal $O(n\log\log n/\log n)$, with fixed-density
$O(n/\log n)$, is conditional on an unproved summation interface~\cite{precisionprior}.
Other proposals need an unconstructed degree-preserving or linear-wire
compiler. Their hypothetical conclusions should neither be advertised
as implemented algorithms nor claimed to be surpassed here.
The separate Claude manuscript does not provide a matched explicit
wire coefficient. We make no numerical dominance claim over an unknown
constant in that different architecture.

\section{The exact mode-graph interface}
\label{sec:imports}

\subsection{Imported algebra and precision}
We import the upstream Cauchy deformation, integrality and Hermite recovery,
the child-network factorization, and the immutable creation-precision
convention \cite{upstream}. We import the refined Hermite leading loss,
the asymmetric near-uniform packing conditions, and the whole-block
slope-four integral modes \cite{asymmetric,joint,incomingadaptive}.
These imported claims are stated as hypotheses of the relative refinement.
The new graph arguments do not change their exact algebraic identities.

At an active call let $M$ be its wire count, $u$ its retained taper range,
$L=2^a$ its packet size, and $D=2^{a-i}$ at retained valuation level
$i<u$. For $r$ groups, the imported estimates are
\begin{align}
 p_t&\le(6+o(1))MD/u,\label{eq:import-p}\\
 c_e&\le\left(\frac2{r^2}+o(1)\right)M/D
 \quad\text{for each directed group pair }e,\label{eq:import-c}\\
 w_q&=a+1-i+4q-2\pc(q).\label{eq:import-w}
\end{align}
The number $c_e$ bounds the number of modes of index $q$ on pair $e$;
$\pc(q)$ is the binary popcount. Every mode has one source and one target.
Child networks have $(2/r+o(1))M$ wires. All $o(1)$ errors used below are
uniform over active depths under the stated schedules.

Fix an array of exact pair weights $b_e\ge0$ with $\sum_e b_e<p_t$.
For fixed $r$ there are polynomially many arrays. Let $\mathcal I_e$ be
the list of nonnegative mode-exponent vectors of exact weight $b_e$ on
pair $e$. Skip any array with an empty list. The exact contribution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mode-graph}
 Z=\sum_{(\xi_e)\in\prod_e\mathcal I_e}
       \prod_{g=1}^r Z_g((\xi_e)_{e\ni g}).
\end{equation}
An evaluation of $Z_g$ is a genuine recursive tensor contraction. It is
charged in all recurrences below, rather than treated as a constant-time
oracle. Exact list cardinalities, enumeration and rank/unrank are
available from the integer dynamic program
\[
 A(j,b)=A(j-1,b)+A(j,b-w_j),\qquad A(0,0)=1,
\]
with other zero or negative boundary cases assigned zero. Mode counts,
budgets and count bit lengths are polynomial in $s$.

\subsection{A uniform Euclidean entropy bound}
Set $h_e=\log_2|\mathcal I_e|$, and
\[
 \K(M,u)=\kap M/\sqrt u,\qquad \kap=\pi\sqrt2/\ln2.
\]
The adaptive algorithms require an estimate uniform in the actual weight
array, not just a bound for a single predetermined set of edges.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform entropy norm]\label{lem:norm}
For each fixed group count $r$ in our schedules,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 \|h\|_2\le(1/r+o(1))\K(M,u).
\end{equation}
Consequently any fixed finite family of possibly data-selected
nonnegative vectors $z$ satisfies
$\sum_ez_eh_e\le(\|z\|_2/r+o(1))\K(M,u)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lower the weights to $1+4q-2\pc(q)$ and extend their range to all
$q\ge0$. Their counting function is $x/4+O(\log(x+2))$, and therefore
\[
 \sum_{q\ge0}-\ln(1-e^{-\theta(1+4q-2\pc(q))})
 =\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}+O(\log^2(2+1/\theta)),\quad 0<\theta\le1.
\]
This follows by integrating against the counting function and the
identity $\int_0^\infty-\ln(1-e^{-x})\,dx=\pi^2/6$.
If $c$ is the common upper envelope in~\eqref{eq:import-c}, the
generating product gives
\[
 \ln|\mathcal I_e|\le\theta b_e+c\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}
       +O(c\log^2(2+1/\theta)).
\]
For $b_e>0$ take
$\theta=\min\{1,\sqrt{c\pi^2/(24b_e)}\}$. When the minimum equals
one, the excess cost is $O(c)$. The case $b_e=0$ has a singleton list.
As all budgets are polynomial, this proves the uniform estimate
\[
 h_e\le\frac\pi{\sqrt6\ln2}\sqrt{c b_e}+O(c\lambda^2).
\]
Taking the Euclidean norm and using $\sum b_e<p_t$ yields
\[
 \|h\|_2\le\frac\pi{\sqrt6\ln2}\sqrt{c p_t}+O_r(c\lambda^2)
 \le(1/r+o(1))\K(M,u).
\]
Indeed every schedule uses the additive buffer
$b=\ceil{6\log_2\lambda}$ and
$a_d=\sum_{j\le d}u_j+(d+1)b$, with $a_{-1}=0$, so
$D\ge2^{a_{d-1}+b+1}\ge\lambda^6$. The last error divided by
$M/\sqrt u$ is $O(\lambda^{5/2}/D)=o(1)$. This also handles zero
and arbitrarily small pair budgets. Cauchy--Schwarz proves the final
assertion without choosing an unjustified asymptotic saddle for each
individual list.
\end{proof}
