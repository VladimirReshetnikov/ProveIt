\begin{abstract}
We review all fifteen refinement packets filed under
\path{Computability/SubexponentialSAT}, the five subsequent SAT archives in
\path{docs/incoming}, and their common source manuscript at a pinned ProveIt
snapshot. We obtain three improvements to the best explicit contraction
certificates in that corpus. On the normalized scale
$s/\sqrt{\log_2s}$, where $s$ counts Boolean wires, the polynomial-space time
coefficient decreases from $55.7094783$ to $46.1848456$. The fastest general
cached time coefficient decreases from $35.3545881$ to $34.3252828$, with
space coefficient $34.0241780$. For roots of degree at most $s^{1/12}$,
the cached time coefficient decreases from $15.3919332$ to $13.3796491$.

The first improvement selects a separator tree from complete binary linear
flags. A coset-intersection inequality makes an adaptive minimax bound
rigorous. The second combines a centered entropy estimate for an adaptive
three-block partition with recursive matrix-based table construction.
Every recursive child evaluation is charged, and all schedules include
precision, packing, degree, and stopping checks.

These are strict improvements of leading coefficients in asymptotic
upper-bound functions. They do \emph{not} replace the
$n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ worst-case SAT exponent by a smaller
logarithmic order, and they do not establish the corpus's conditional
$n\log\log n/\log n$ target. The results are relative algorithmic
refinements of the imported deformation and recovery identities, with
new natural-language proofs and reproducible finite checks.
\end{abstract}
