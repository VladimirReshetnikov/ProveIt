\section{The common interface and its entropy geometry}
\label{sec:interface}

\subsection{Normalized tensors, arithmetic, and provenance}

The input is a bipartite Boolean tensor network with at most $s$ wires,
at most polynomially many vertices, and total graph/description length
$\mathcal L$. Its contraction is
\[
 Z=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{E}}\prod_v T_v(x|_{\delta(v)}).
\]
The general root degree bound is $\Delta=\ceil{s^{9/10}}$.
Initial entries lie in
$ (\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell)$ with
$1\le P_0,\ell\le s$.
Uniform entry evaluation uses
$\poly(s+\mathcal L)2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ time and polynomial space.
All constants in these input premises are fixed.

The imported algorithm works in
\[
 R_p=(\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[U,V]/(f(U),V^\ell),
\]
where $f$ is monic of degree $O(\log s)$ with irreducible reduction
modulo two. Its rank and precision are polynomial in $s$. Tensor
descriptions retain their creation precision: later calls evaluate an
entry at that precision and reuse its canonical coordinate representatives.
Canonical lifting is not assumed to preserve ring operations.

We import the following exact facts from the source and the reviewed
refinements \cite{source,asymmetric,joint,adaptive,tree}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Cauchy deformation, local integral evaluations, and tapered Hermite
recovery compute the original contraction exactly modulo the requested
power of two.
\item Nearly lossless asymmetric padding preserves the source/target
locality and gives child wire count
$(2/r+o(1))M$ at an active call with $M$ wires and fixed alphabet $r$.
\item Full-orientation joint Cauchy modes have certified weights
$a+1-i+4q-2\pc(q)$, precisely one source and one target group,
and the exact recursive group-factor identity described below.
\end{enumerate}

The new work changes how that finite group-factor identity is summed.
It does not alter its deformation, recovery, or ring semantics.

\subsection{Packing and precision conditions}

Let $\lambda=\ceil{\log_2s}$, $b=\ceil{6\log_2\lambda}$, and
\[
 a_d=\sum_{j=0}^d u_j+(d+1)b,\qquad
 L_d=2^{a_d},\qquad Q_d=r_d^{a_d}.
\]
Here $u_d$ is the retained taper range. At a node of valuation $i<u_d$,
write $D=2^{a_d-i}$. The reviewed sharp Hermite estimate is uniformly
\begin{equation}
 p_t\le(6+o(1))\frac{MD}{u_d}.
 \label{eq:precision-interface}
\end{equation}
For completeness, the exact linear-taper slope may be taken as
\[
 \rho=\ceil{\frac{M(2L_d-1)+1}
 {L_d(2u_d-2+2^{1-u_d})}},
\]
and a sufficient evaluation precision is
$p+6\rho D+\rho u_d(a_d+1)$.
The additive buffer makes the inherited $p$ and final additive term
lower order on the schedules below.

If $R,C$ bound the two sides' degrees, the sufficient padding gates are
\begin{equation}
 \frac{Q_dR}{M}=o(1),\qquad
 \frac{Q_d^2C(\log s)^2}{M}=o(1),\qquad
 \frac{L_dQ_d^2}{M}=o(1).
 \label{eq:padding-interface}
\end{equation}
At the root $R=C=\Delta$. Below it, retained tensors remain on one side,
and the fresh side has degree at most $2L_{d-1}$; thus one may use
$R\le\Delta$, $C\le2L_{d-1}$ in the general setting.
The separate lower-degree result tracks
$\max\{\Delta,2L_{d-1}\}$ instead. Every schedule is checked before using
the nearly lossless padding conclusion, closing the depth induction.

The source estimate for inherited precision is
$p=O(s\lambda^2 2^{a_{d-1}})$.
Since $D\ge2^{a_{d-1}+b+1}$, $u_d\le\lambda$, and all our leaf
thresholds have $s/M\le\lambda$, the ratio
$p/(MD/u_d)$ is $O(\lambda^4 2^{-b})=o(1)$.
This explains why the full precision history is compatible with
\eqref{eq:precision-interface}.

\subsection{Exact pair budgets and paid child factors}

For a fixed alphabet $r$, enumerate exact weight arrays
$(b_{gh})_{g\ne h}$ with nonnegative entries and sum below $p_t$.
There are polynomially many arrays because $r$ is fixed and $p_t$ is
polynomial in $s$. For one array, let $\mathcal I_{gh}$ be the finite
list of mode exponent tuples of that exact pair weight. Discard an
array if any list is empty. Define
\[
 h_{gh}=\log_2|\mathcal I_{gh}|,\qquad
 K(M,u)=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}\frac M{\sqrt u}.
\]
The contribution of this array is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{(\xi_{gh})\in\prod_{g\ne h}\mathcal I_{gh}}
       \prod_{g=1}^r Z_g((\xi_e)_{e\ni g}).
 \label{eq:pair-identity}
\end{equation}
Each $Z_g$ entry is a genuine recursive child contraction. It is
explicitly charged in every time recurrence in this report.

For any prescribed mode weights $w_1,\ldots,w_v$, the recurrence
\[
 D(j,z)=D(j-1,z)+D(j,z-w_j),\qquad D(0,0)=1
\]
with the usual zero/negative boundary conventions counts exact-weight
tuples. Budgets, mode counts, and integer bit lengths are polynomial
in $s$. This table supplies exact list sizes, rank/unrank, and
polynomial-delay iteration. No counting oracle is required.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted pair entropy]\label{lem:weighted}
For every fixed nonnegative vector $z=(z_e)$ on the directed group pairs,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_e z_e h_e
 \le \left(\frac{\|z\|_2}{r}+o(1)\right)K(M,u).
 \label{eq:weighted}
\end{equation}
The error is uniform over active calls and over any fixed finite family
of such vectors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We include the leading-constant calculation to make the interface explicit.
There are at most
$c=(2/r^2+o(1))M/D$ modes of each index on a given directed pair.
Lower their weights to
$\widehat w_q=1+4q-2\pc(q)$ and extend $q$ to all nonnegative integers.
Both changes only enlarge the list-generating function.
The weight-counting function is $x/4+O(\log(x+2))$, so
\[
 \sum_{q\ge0}-\ln(1-e^{-\theta\widehat w_q})
 =\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}+O(\log^2(2/\theta))
 \quad(\theta\downarrow0).
\]
This follows by partial summation and
$\int_0^\infty-\ln(1-e^{-x})\,dx=\pi^2/6$.
For pair $e$ with $z_e>0$, apply the generating-product bound at
$\theta/z_e$, multiply its logarithm by $z_e$, and sum over pairs.
Using the total exact budget gives
\[
 \ln2\sum_e z_eh_e
 \le \theta p_t+
 \frac{c\pi^2}{24\theta}\sum_e z_e^2+
 O_z(c\log^2(2/\theta)).
\]
Optimize the first two terms and use
\eqref{eq:precision-interface}. The optimum has scale
$\theta\asymp\sqrt u/D$. Since the additive buffer gives
$D\ge\lambda^6$ up to a fixed factor, the error is
$o(M/\sqrt u)$ uniformly. Zero weights are omitted.
For a finite family, take the largest error constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Euclidean entropy envelope]\label{cor:l2}
Uniformly over active calls and exact budget arrays,
\[
 \|h\|_2\le(1+o(1))K(M,u)/r.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed $\varepsilon>0$, take a finite $\varepsilon$-net of the
nonnegative unit sphere in the fixed dimension $r(r-1)$.
For nonzero $h$, one of its vectors has inner product with
$h/\|h\|_2$ at least $1-\varepsilon$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted} uniformly on that finite net.
This yields $\|h\|_2\le(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}(1+o(1))K/r$.
First take the uniform size limit, then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
This is precisely the asserted uniform $1+o(1)$ statement.
\end{proof}

This finite-net argument matters: an estimate stated only for each
fixed weight vector should not silently be used with a newly
data-dependent vector without a uniformity argument. The flag proof
uses a different valid route: its possible vectors already form a
fixed finite family.

\subsection{Why the matrix backend applies to this ring}
\label{sec:matrix-bridge}

The cached construction uses the already reviewed rational-field
matrix bound $\omega_{\mathbb Q}<12/5$; the primary result of
Alman et al.\ supplies a strictly stronger bound \cite{matrix}.
Here is the relevant ring bridge, restated from the contraction-tree
packet \cite{tree}.

Fix a finite rational bilinear square-matrix algorithm with exponent
strictly below $12/5$. Its base size, coefficients, and denominator
primes are constants. Away from that fixed finite set of primes,
the same bilinear identities hold over the prime field. Recursing
one product at a time gives quadratic working storage and a bound
$O(N^{12/5})$ on square matrix operations, with slack absorbing
fixed overhead.

Write each entry of $R_p$ in its specified polynomial-rank basis
over $\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z$, and lift coordinates to integers in
$[0,2^p)$. For matrices of dimensions $d_1,d_2,d_3$, an entry of an
ordinary coordinate-pair product is bounded by
$d_2(2^p-1)^2$. Its bit length is
$O(p+\log d_2)$, polynomial in $s$.
Compute each such product modulo enough nonexceptional odd primes
to exceed this reconstruction bound, recover the exact nonnegative
integer entries by CRT, combine them using the quotient ring's
integer multiplication table, and finally reduce modulo $2^p$.
The number and numerical magnitudes of the needed primes can be
bounded polynomially in the reconstruction bit length. There are
only polynomially many coordinate pairs.

For rectangular matrices, split into square blocks with side
comparable to $\min(d_1,d_2,d_3)$, padding by constant factors.
The resulting bit-operation bound is
\[
 \poly(s+\mathcal L)\,
 d_1d_2d_3\,\min(d_1,d_2,d_3)^{-3/5}.
\]
Storage is a polynomial factor times the input/output arrays.
Reshaping and external sorting of polynomial-bit records add a
polynomial factor. No inverse of two or of a nonunit is taken in
$R_p$. Thus for index-length logarithms $H_1,H_2,H_3$ the exponent
is
\[
 H_1+H_2+H_3-\tfrac35\min(H_1,H_2,H_3).
\]
The finite checkers accompanying this report check the new combinatorial
use of this backend; they do not implement the large theoretical
matrix algorithm.
