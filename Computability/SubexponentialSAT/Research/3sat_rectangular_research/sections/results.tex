\section{Results and their precise scope}\label{sec:results}

Let $n$ denote the number of declared variables, $m$ the number of input
clauses, and $L$ the number of literal occurrences after elementary
normalization. Clauses have at most three literals. Repeated literals,
tautologies, duplicate clauses and unused variables are handled explicitly.
In particular $L\le3m$; no assumption of bounded occurrence is imposed on
the original formula. Logarithms in displayed complexity coefficients are
base two. Polynomial factors include the time to read the original input.

The following two normalized statements concern the same Boolean tensor
interface used by the previous packets, stated in Section~\ref{sec:interface}.
They are mathematical refinements using the cited exact contraction and
matrix theorems as dependencies. They do not rest on an unproved new
structured-summation hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}[Two certified cached bounds]\label{thm:contractors}
For a normalized network with at most $s$ wires and root maximum degree at
most $\ceil{s^{9/10}}$, there is a deterministic exact contraction algorithm
whose time and space are at most
\[
 (s+\LL)^{O(1)}
 2^{(C_{\rm gen}+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},
 \qquad C_{\rm gen}=28.6327662765359\ldots.
\]
If the root maximum degree is at most $\ceil{s^{1/12}}$, the time and space
coefficients respectively become
\[
 C_{\rm low}=10.3367239855467\ldots,\qquad
 C_{\rm low,space}=10.3366545686347\ldots.
\]
The same original entry-evaluation premise is permitted in both statements.
Safe eventual coefficients are $28.633$ in the first case and $10.337$ in
the second. All coefficients are specified exactly by rational data, square
roots, and $\kappa=\pi\sqrt2/\ln2$ in Section~\ref{sec:schedules}; decimal
strings are descriptive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Occurrence-based 3-SAT bound]\label{thm:sat}
Every 3-CNF can be decided, and its satisfying assignments counted exactly,
in time
\[
 \poly(n+m)\,
 2^{(C_{\rm low}+o(1))L/\sqrt{\log_2L}},
\]
with space coefficient $C_{\rm low,space}$ on the same scale, as $L\to\infty$.
Bounded $L$ and formulas without nonempty clauses are handled directly.
In particular, for $m\to\infty$ the time is at most
\[
 \poly(n+m)\,
 2^{(3C_{\rm low}+o(1))m/\sqrt{\log_2m}},
 \qquad 3C_{\rm low}=31.0101719566402\ldots.
\]
For fixed $D>0$, $m\le Dn$ gives the conservative time bound
$\poly(n+m)2^{(3DC_{\rm low}+o(1))n/\sqrt{\log_2n}}$.
\end{theorem}

The count statement is used because the imported contraction identities
compute a count. Its only decision output is whether that integer is
positive. No additional SAT width or problem class is part of the application.

\subsection{A fair comparison against the previous proposals}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{.38\textwidth}rr@{}}
\toprule Same normalized premise & Previous time & New time\\\midrule
Root degree at most $s^{9/10}$, cached & $29.8116089454$ & $28.6327662766$\\
Root degree at most $s^{1/12}$, cached & $13.3796490663$ & $10.3367239856$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The preceding bounds are from \cite{pairwise,flags}.
The decreases in these certified leading coefficients are about $3.95\%$
and $22.74\%$. They are comparisons of proved upper-bound formulas under
matched assumptions, not measurements of SAT runtime. The new cached
algorithms use subexponential space. The polynomial-space coefficient
$46.1848455867\ldots$ under the general premise is not improved by
Theorem~\ref{thm:contractors} under a polynomial-space guarantee.

The preceding low-degree polynomial-space contractor has coefficient
$16.8122338018833\ldots$ \cite{pairwise}. Our normalization also gives its
occurrence-based consequence with that coefficient per literal. This is a
tighter SAT translation, not a new polynomial-space contractor.

To avoid overstating the gain, one should also grant the old contractors
the best available elementary normalization. High-occurrence branching
already appears in \cite{balanced}; changing its cutoff to $\log^2 L$
also exposes the low-degree interface with only a lower-order branching
cost. Thus a fair composed previous cached benchmark is
$13.3796490663\ldots$ per literal, even though the latest explicit
translation used trivalent equality trees and up to $3L$ wires.
Our stronger $10.3367239856\ldots$ coefficient improves that composed
benchmark as well. The equality-tree saving is not counted twice.

\subsection{What remains unresolved}

Combining the new contractor with the existing dense-formula reduction
retains the worst-case order
\[
 \poly(n+m)\,2^{O(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}.
\]
The earlier density-sensitive envelope
\[
 \poly(n+m)\,2^{O\left(\frac n{\sqrt{\log n}}
       \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}
\]
can still be used \cite{density,density2}. A fixed portfolio of the direct
and compressed algorithms preserves the better applicable bound. The
numeric constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:sat} do not replace the unspecified
constants in that dense reduction.

No smaller logarithmic-order bound, such as
$2^{O(n\log\log n/\log n)}$, has been proved here. No dominance is claimed
over the unspecified numerical constant in the independent Anthropic
algorithm. The improvement is substantial within the quantified contractor
frontier, but it is not the requested change of worst-case exponent order.
