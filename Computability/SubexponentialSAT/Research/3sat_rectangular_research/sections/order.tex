\section{What an improvement in exponent order would require}\label{sec:order}

The requested goal calls for more than optimizing constants. The source
methods have two distinct losses: partition-mode entropy gives a
square-root logarithm in the denominator, while the dense-formula
compression contributes the worst-case $\log\log n$ factor. The
results above improve the fully paid coefficient but leave both
mechanisms in place. This section records the most relevant structural
directions and one new obstruction to an attractive shortcut.

\subsection{Why faster multiplication changes the coefficient here}

The common-tilt bound is proportional to $M/\sqrt u$, and packing permits
$u=O(\log s)$. A better exact matrix exponent changes the support
coefficients and therefore the multiplier of $M/\sqrt u$. A different
fixed finite plan changes the same multiplier. Neither change alters
the singularity of the mode generating function. Even an idealized
quadratic square-matrix algorithm would therefore leave this particular
entropy calculation on the $s/\sqrt{\log s}$ scale.

This is an accounting statement about the present proof architecture.
It is not a lower bound for 3-SAT or for all possible contractors.
The global schedules also show why optimizing a local matrix cost in
isolation can fail: the terminal route pays for recursive child entries,
and it already determines the new low-degree time coefficient.

\subsection{A precise potential route beyond square-root entropy}

The independent Anthropic proof describes a graded-operator route whose
current partition basis has
$\log\dim V_E=\Theta(E^{1/2})$ \cite{anthropicpaper}. Its recurrence
balances that growth against a two-direction decomposition. At the level
of that accounting, replacing the growth by
$\Theta(E^\theta)$ with $\theta<1/2$ would replace the contraction
ratio by $\rho=2^{\theta-1}$ and the logarithmic exponent by
$-\log_2\rho=1-\theta>1/2$.

That substitution is not a construction. To use it one must supply, at
the same time, an exact scalar identity, effective factorizations in
both directions, polynomially bounded decomposition metadata, and
uniformly efficient entry access at the necessary precision. A small
graded space by itself does not provide these properties. Any future
proposal should state these requirements as a single complete interface
and pay for converting its outputs into the next recursive inputs.

\subsection{Sparse mode deletion does not preserve the required locality}

One tempting attempt is to retain only square-indexed, or otherwise
sparse, Heisenberg modes. This reduces the count of partitions formed
from the surviving weights. It also changes the current commutator,
and the original single-point locality argument then fails.

\begin{lemma}[No proper mode deletion with finite single-point locality]
\label{lem:locality}
Let $S\subseteq\mathbb Z_{>0}$, put $a_0=0$ and
$a_n=n\,\mathbf1_S(|n|)$ for $n\ne0$, and consider the formal
distribution
\[
 C_S(z,w)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}a_nz^{-n-1}w^{n-1}.
\]
If $(z-w)^H C_S(z,w)=0$ for some integer $H\ge0$, then $S$ is empty
or all of $\mathbb Z_{>0}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $H=0$, all coefficients vanish and $S$ is empty. For $H>0$, the
coefficient of $z^{H-m-1}w^{m-1}$ in the product is
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^H(-1)^j\binom Hj a_{m-j}.
\]
Its vanishing for every integer $m$ says that the $H$th finite difference
of the doubly infinite sequence $a_m$ is zero. Newton interpolation, or
induction on $H$, gives a polynomial $P(m)$ of degree below $H$ agreeing
with $a_m$ on all integers. If the positive complement of $S$ is infinite,
then $P$ has infinitely many zeros and is identically zero, so $S$ is
empty. If that complement is finite, then $P(m)=m$ at infinitely many
positive integers, hence $P(X)=X$ identically. No positive integer can
then be omitted, and $S=\mathbb Z_{>0}$.
\end{proof}

The sparse current commutator is exactly $C_S$. The cited locality
proof clears the commutator with a finite power of $z-w$; the lemma
shows that this proof cannot retain any nonempty proper set of modes
unchanged. Keeping multiples of a fixed integer may instead admit
locality at several roots, with a factor such as $(z^d-w^d)^H$.
That only rescales the usual partition weights:
$\log p(E/d)=\Theta(\sqrt{E/d})$, retaining the square-root growth
and the same doubling ratio. A different algebra or a different exact
decomposition may still work. The lemma excludes only the stated
shortcut; it does not exclude sparse bases by unrelated constructions.

\subsection{Other tempting shortcuts and the remaining problem}

The earlier packets already explain several accounting hazards. Reducing
the original target to parity does not automatically remove the Hermite
recovery precision or the full mode-allocation cost. Reapplying the
contractor to a compact second network also requires a factorization
with acceptable degrees and entry costs; merely declaring a small
number of large tensors does not supply it \cite{precision,barriers}.
The latest pairwise packet contains a scoped generic-oracle obstruction
to summing arbitrary independently supplied tables more cheaply
\cite{pairwise}. Such statements must not be promoted to complexity
lower bounds for 3-SAT's structured tables.

There are therefore two concrete, still unproved ways to surpass the
present worst-case order: construct a compatible exact decomposition
with entropy exponent below $1/2$, or compress dense 3-CNF instances to
an interface of $O(n)$ effective size while preserving the required
entry access and precision. The equality construction proves this
small-size statement for direct formulas with $L=O(n)$; it does not
solve the dense case. No result in this report claims that either
remaining construction has been achieved.
