   210	array there are
   211	\[
   212	 C_{gh}=\binom{N_{gh}}{d_{gh}}
   213	\]
   214	subsets of the specified size for this pair. Draw
   215	$\lceil\log_2 C_{gh}\rceil$ Boolean wires between the two group
   216	vertices to index them in lexicographic order. For $C_{gh}=1$
   217	both endpoints use the unique subset (possibly the entire incidence
   218	set) with no wires. Any unused bit string gives tensor entry zero.
   219	To decode a valid index, take the incidences in order and count the
   220	possible completions with or without each incidence using binomial
   221	coefficients. These have polynomial bit length, so decoding uses
   222	polynomial time and space.
   223	
   224	The union of the subsets over all pairs specifies a squarefree
   225	monomial of degree at most $K$ in $N$ indeterminates. Including
   226	all monomials of this degree bound gives the upper bound
   227	$\binom{N+K}{K}$ by stars and bars. Thus the number
   228	of wires $M$ in the network for this array satisfies
   229	\begin{align}
   230	 M &\le J^2+\log_2\prod_{g,h} C_{gh}
   231	      \le J^2+\log_2\binom{N+K}{K} \notag\\
   232	   &\le J^2+K\log_2\!\left(\frac{\mathrm e(N+K)}{K}\right)
   233	      = O_{k,c}(n\nu).
   234	 \label{eq:sharp-wire-budget}
   235	\end{align}
   236	Here $K=\Theta_{k,c}(n)$ and $N\le n\lambda^{O_{k,c}(1)}$.
   237	Also $\lceil\log_2 C_{gh}\rceil\le N_{gh}$, so the degree of each
   238	vertex is at most its number of incidences,
   239	$n^{3/4}\lambda^{O_{k,c}(1)}$.

   244	at a test group $g$ is the coefficient of its indicated monomial
   245	in $\prod_{i\in\mathcal T_g}\overline B_i(z_i,V)$. Since the $z_i$
   305	\[
   306	 2^{\,n/\sqrt{\lambda}+O_{k,c}(\sqrt n\log n)}.
   307	\]
   308	Both terms in this exponent are $O_{k,c}(n\nu/\sqrt{\lambda})$.
   309	Process the branches and arrays sequentially. On each branch,
   310	sum the contractions in $\mathcal B$ and apply $\mathcal S_\ell$.
   311	Adding the branch residues modulo $2^{n+1}$ gives the original
   312	count, since it lies between $0$ and $2^n$. This proves the

   314	sizes.
   315	\end{proof}

   317	\begin{proof}[Proof of the arbitrary-density bound in
   318	Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
   319	Normalize the formula on its original $n$ declared variables as
   320	in Section~\ref{subsec:general-reduction}. Empty clauses and
   321	formulas with no clauses have immediate answers. For $n\ge20$
   322	apply Lemma~\ref{lem:sparsify} with
   323	$\sigma=1/\lceil\sqrt{\lambda}\rceil$, where
   324	$\lambda=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$. The tree has
   325	$n^{O_k(1)}2^{O(n/\sqrt{\lambda})}$ nodes and polynomial depth.
   326	Every leaf has occurrence bound
   327	$B=O_k((\sigma^{-1}\log(2/\sigma))^{k-1})=\lambda^{O_k(1)}$
   328	and at most $Bn$ nonempty clauses.
   329	
   330	For a nontrivial leaf with $m_G$ clauses, use the initial parity
   331	checks of Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-checks} and the greedy procedure
   332	of Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy-hashes}. Each initial row has support size
   333	at most $D_0$, and each additional row has list length $P$.
   334	Both bounds are $\lambda^{O_k(1)}$. By \eqref{eq:hash-query}, every row
   335	gives a degree-at-most-$k$ equation with at most
   350	per leaf. Since every fixed power of $\lambda$ is
   351	$o(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})$, these costs and the number of leaves
   352	are absorbed in the claimed time bound
   353	\[
   354	 (n+m)^{O_k(1)}2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}.
   355	\]
   356	Here $m$ counts the input clauses. Traverse the tree
   357	depth first and process one query at a time. The leaf solution sets
   358	are disjoint, so their exact counts add with an $(n+1)$-bit
   359	accumulator. Together with polynomial space per query and per
   360	tree path, this gives the asserted polynomial-space bound.
   361	\end{proof}

   363	A single algorithm attains both bounds. With
   364	$\lambda=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ and $\nu=\lceil\log_2\lambda\rceil$,
   365	use the clause network when $m\le n\nu$ and the parity queries
   366	otherwise. In the first branch $n\le s=n+m=O(n\log\log n)$,
   367	so its exponent is $O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})$ even at growing
   368	density. For each fixed $D$ and all sufficiently large $n$, every
