\begin{abstract}
We review the seven ETH-related proposal archives and the separate 49-page
manuscript present in a pinned snapshot of \emph{ProveIt}, and improve the
strongest comparable explicit contraction bound. The new ingredient is a
deterministic two-stage labeling of a bipartite multigraph: balance row
degrees first, then choose column labels by exact rational conditional
expectation. This permits nearly equal loads in the pair-label classes under
$M\gg \Darr\Delta\,\polylog s$, instead of the earlier sufficient condition
$M\gg\Darr^2\Delta$. It also removes the leading factor three in packet
padding. An additive logarithmic precision guard replaces the source's
full extra taper block. Finally, an integer capacity rule uses the available
packing budget without leaving fixed asymptotic slack.

Combined with the already proposed exact Hermite taper and factorial Cauchy
modes, these changes give the normalized tensor-contraction bound
\[
 (s+\desc)^{O(1)}
 2^{(\Cstar+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},
 \qquad
 \Cstar=\frac{9\pi}{4\ln2}\sqrt{15\log_2 54}
       =94.7482022595\ldots,
\]
in polynomial space. The strongest numerically instantiated prior coefficient is
$648.4410666487\ldots$; its combination with the earlier Cauchy modes gives
$374.3776243833\ldots$. The prior tunable schedule family approaches
approximately $638.52749$, or $368.65402$ after those Cauchy modes;
the new result improves on this stronger comparison as well.
These are comparisons of proved upper-bound certificates, not runtime
measurements or lower bounds for previous algorithms. The general
fixed-width CNF order remains
$\poly(n,m)2^{O(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}$.
The independent 49-page manuscript has no explicit comparable coefficient.

The report proves the new statements, identifies all reused ingredients,
and supplies exact-arithmetic packing and schedule prototypes with finite
checks. It does not supply a complete SAT solver or a Lean formalization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and comparison with the reviewed work}
\label{sec:result}

The requested improvement is possible at the level of an explicit leading
exponential coefficient. This report does not establish a smaller
logarithmic order for unrestricted 3SAT. That distinction is central:
$\Cstar$ is a coefficient for a specified normalized tensor-network problem,
and is not automatically a coefficient multiplying the number of SAT
variables.

The October 25 OpenAI manuscript \cite{openai} states, for every fixed
clause width $k$,
\begin{align}
 T_{\rm general}(n,m)&=(n+m)^{O_k(1)}
       2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})},\label{eq:general}\\
 T_{\rm fixed}(n,m)&=(n+m)^{O_{k,B}(1)}
       2^{O_{k,B}(n/\sqrt{\log n})},
       \qquad m\le Bn,\quad B\text{ fixed}.\label{eq:fixed}
\end{align}
Its scope document \cite{scope} distinguishes these manuscript statements
from the released Lean endpoint, whose general exponent contains
$(\log\log n)^2$. This report compares against the sharper manuscript
construction. Removing a factor already removed in its appendix would
not be a new result.

\subsection{The new and reused ingredients}

There are three new algorithmic changes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A two-stage, deterministically derandomized labeling that improves the
power of the pair-class count in the sufficient packing condition.
\item An additive guard of $O(\log\log s)$ bits per recursion level, which
preserves precision without duplicating the entire taper budget.
\item An integer capacity rule which pays the guard explicitly and attains
the limiting leading coefficient with a single algorithm.
\end{enumerate}
The nearly lossless packet count follows from the new labeling and exact
padding arithmetic. The exact Hermite taper, factorial Cauchy basis,
deformation identities, and recursive tensor factorization are reused
and attributed; they are not claimed as new here.

\section{Normalized interface and inherited identities}
\label{sec:interface}

Let $s\ge2$ be an integer global size bound and $\desc$ the input description
length. The input is a bipartite Boolean tensor network with at most $s$
edges, maximum degree at most
\[
 \Delta=\ceil{s^{9/10}},
\]
and at most polynomially many described vertices. Its entries lie in
\[
 \mathcal B_{P_0,\ell}=(\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell),
 \qquad 1\le P_0,\ell\le s.
\]
For fixed constants, an entry is computable in
$(s+\desc)^{O(1)}2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ time and polynomial space.
These are the normalized hypotheses used by the prior explicit certificate.
A source theorem allowing $Cs$ initial edges requires the corresponding
change of normalization; the number $\Cstar$ below assumes at most $s$.

The original construction \cite{openai} works over an unramified
coefficient extension $\Ocal$ with residue field of size $2^{4\lambda}$,
where
\[
 \lambda=\ceil{\log_2s}.
\]
Arithmetic, coordinate precision, and truncated $V$-coordinates are retained.
Tensor descriptions also retain their \emph{creation precision}: each entry
is canonically lifted from its original creation-time residue. Lifting is
not assumed to commute with multiplication or addition.

At an active recursive call there are $M$ real edges. Packets have
$L=2^a$ slots. For the grid
\[
 S=\{-L,\ldots,L-1\},\qquad
 \mathcal C(T)=\prod_{q\in S}(T-q),\qquad \alpha=2^{a+1},
\]
the original kernel is
\[
 W_{xy}(T)=\frac{\mathcal C(T)}
                  {\mathcal C'(0)(T-(y-x))}.
\]
The denominator cancels a numerator factor; $W(0)=I$ on real ports.
The source deformation $F(T)$ therefore has $F(0)$ equal to the desired
contraction and degree at most
\begin{equation}
 \deg F\le M(2L-1).\label{eq:degree}
\end{equation}
The source proves coefficientwise integrality of $F(t+\alpha z)$,
the Hermite recovery formula, and the source/target factorization after
crossing modes are fixed.

The proofs below modify the packing and parameter interfaces to those
identities. They do not presuppose a new fast algorithm for summing a
general compressed tensor representation.

\section{A new deterministic near-balanced packing lemma}
\label{sec:packing}

The graph in this section is a bipartite multigraph. Parallel edges are
counted with their multiplicity. Write
\[
 Q=r^a,\qquad \Darr=Q^2,\qquad \mu=M/\Darr,
 \qquad L=2^a,
\]
where $r\ge2$ is an integer. The symbols used as vertex labels are strings
in $[r]^a$, equivalently $Q$ labels on each side.

\begin{theorem}[Two-stage labeling]\label{thm:labeling}
Suppose $M\le s$, $\lambda=\ceil{\log_2s}\ge4$, all degrees are at most
the positive integer $\Delta$, and
\begin{equation}
 M\ge4\Darr\Delta\lambda^4.\label{eq:new-packing}
\end{equation}
There is a polynomial-time, polynomial-space deterministic labeling
for which every pair-label class has load strictly below
\[
 B=\ceil{(1+2/\lambda)\mu}.
\]
All decisions can be made using exact rational arithmetic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{First stage: whole-row balancing.}
Assign each row vertex, with weight its degree, to a currently least-loaded
row label. Before an assignment the smallest load is at most the total
assigned weight divided by $Q$, hence at most $M/Q$. The new item has
weight at most $\Delta$. Consequently every final row-label load $R_j$
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 R_j\le M/Q+\Delta.\label{eq:row-balance}
\end{equation}
Vertices are not split and no edge is changed.

\textbf{Second stage: independent column labels.}
Temporarily assign each whole column vertex an independent uniform label
in $[Q]$. Let $w_{vj}$ be the number of edges from column vertex $v$
to rows carrying label $j$. Then
\[
 0\le w_{vj}\le\Delta,\qquad \sum_vw_{vj}=R_j.
\]
For a fixed cell $(j,k)$ its load is
\[
 X_{jk}=\sum_v w_{vj}\mathbf1_{\{\operatorname{label}(v)=k\}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb EX_{jk}=R_j/Q\le\mu+\Delta/Q.
\]
Independence is asserted for column vertices, not for individual parallel
edges.

Set
\[
 \eta=\lambda^{-2},\qquad z=1+\eta/\Delta.
\]
For $0\le w\le\Delta$,
\[
 z^w-1\le (w\eta/\Delta)e^\eta
 \le (w\eta/\Delta)(1+2\eta).
\]
The last inequality follows, for $0\le\eta\le1/2$, from
$e^\eta\le(1-\eta)^{-1}\le1+2\eta$.
The product formula for the moment generating function gives
\begin{align}
 \ln\mathbb E z^{X_{jk}}
 &=\sum_v\ln\left(1+\frac{z^{w_{vj}}-1}{Q}\right)\\
 &\le\frac{\eta}{\Delta}(1+2\eta)(\mu+\Delta/Q).\label{eq:mgf}
\end{align}
Since $\mu\ge4\Delta\lambda^4$ and $Q\ge1$,
\[
 \mu+\Delta/Q\le\mu(1+1/(4\lambda^4)).
\]
Also $\ln z\ge(\eta/\Delta)(1-\eta/2)$. Therefore
\begin{align}
 B\ln z-\ln\mathbb Ez^{X_{jk}}
 &\ge\frac{\mu\eta}{\Delta}
 \left[(1+2/\lambda)(1-\eta/2)
 -(1+1/(4\lambda^4))(1+2\eta)\right]\\
 &\ge\frac{\mu}{\Delta\lambda^3}\ge4\lambda.
 \label{eq:gap}
\end{align}
For completeness, the second inequality holds for every real $\lambda\ge4$:
after subtracting $1/\lambda$ from the bracket and multiplying by
$4\lambda^6$, the result is
\[
 P(\lambda)=4\lambda^5-10\lambda^4-4\lambda^3-\lambda^2-2.
\]
Writing $\lambda=x+4$ gives the exact positive-coefficient certificate
\[
 P(x+4)=1262+2360x+1551x^2+476x^3+70x^4+4x^5.
\]

Now consider
\[
 \Phi=\sum_{j,k}z^{X_{jk}-B}.
\]
Condition \eqref{eq:new-packing} implies $\Darr\le M\le s\le2^\lambda$.
Thus
\[
 \mathbb E\Phi\le\Darr e^{-4\lambda}
 \le\exp((\ln2-4)\lambda)<1.
\]

\textbf{Exact derandomization.}
Suppose some column labels have already been chosen. Let $A_{jk}$ be
their contribution to cell $(j,k)$. Apart from the common factor $z^{-B}$,
the conditional potential is the rational number
\begin{equation}
 \Psi=
 \sum_{j,k}z^{A_{jk}}
 \prod_{v\ {\rm unassigned}}
       \left(1-\frac1Q+\frac{z^{w_{vj}}}{Q}\right).
 \label{eq:conditional}
\end{equation}
Try every possible label of the next column vertex and choose one minimizing
this expression. The average of the $Q$ candidate values is its previous
value, so the potential does not increase. At termination $\Phi<1$.
If some $X_{jk}\ge B$, its summand would be at least one. Hence every
$X_{jk}<B$.

There are polynomially many cells and label choices because
$\Darr\le s$. All exponents are at most $M\le s$; the rational base has
polynomial bit length. Products over the explicitly described vertices
therefore have polynomial bit length, and rational comparison is exact.
Zero-degree vertices can be handled separately. This proves the claimed
construction and resource bounds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Padding and the original orientation construction}

Round the maximum pair-label load upward to a multiple $\Hpad$ of $L$.
By integrality, $X_{jk}\le B-1<(1+2/\lambda)\mu$, so
\[
 \Hpad\le (1+2/\lambda)\mu+L.
\]
There is no missing additive ceiling term in this inequality.

\begin{corollary}[Packet and group counts]\label{cor:packets}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:labeling}, also suppose $2L\le\Delta$. Put
\[
 \kappa=\frac{\Hpad\Darr}{M}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \kappa\le1+2/\lambda+\frac{L\Darr}{M}
 \le1+\frac3\lambda.\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
The source's orientation packet construction gives at most
$\kappa M/L$ packets and at most $\kappa M/r$ real ports on either side
in each coordinate-symbol group. At a retained valuation node
$i<u$, with $D=L/2^i$, there are at most $2\kappa M/D$ mode families,
and a child has at most $2\kappa M/r$ real edges.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Condition \eqref{eq:new-packing} and $L\le\Delta/2$ give
$L\Darr/M\le1/(8\lambda^4)$, proving \eqref{eq:kappa}.
Pad each of the $\Darr$ ordered label-pair classes to $\Hpad$ slots.

Fix the unordered pair of symbols in each coordinate. If $a'$ coordinates
have distinct symbols, there are $2^{a'}$ possible orientations. Each
packet takes $2^{a-a'}$ slots from each corresponding ordered class.
The $a$ binary slot bits record the orientations in unequal coordinates
and the copies in equal coordinates. Divisibility of $\Hpad$ by $2^a$
makes this assembly exact. The total number of packets is
$\Hpad\Darr/L=\kappa M/L$.

For any coordinate, a particular symbol appears in exactly a $1/r$
fraction of the padded ordered label classes on each side. Hence each
real-port group has size at most $\Hpad\Darr/r=\kappa M/r$.
The source creates a child from the corresponding row and column groups,
giving at most $2\kappa M/r$ edges.

At valuation $i$, each packet has $2^{i+1}=2L/D$ column tiles.
There is one mode family per relevant tile, so the total is bounded
by $(\kappa M/L)(2L/D)=2\kappa M/D$.
\end{proof}

The old sufficient condition was $M\ge2\Darr^2\Delta$, obtained by
bounding the sum of squared cell deviations. The new theorem improves
its dependence on $\Darr$, in addition to removing slack from the
padding count.

\begin{proposition}[Why the new power of $\Darr$ is natural]
For arbitrary bipartite multigraphs, a uniform conclusion
$\max X_{jk}\le(1+o(1))M/\Darr$ requires
$M/\Darr\ge(1-o(1))\Delta$ in general.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take a component consisting of two vertices joined by $\Delta$ parallel
edges, and add disjoint edges as needed. That entire bundle belongs to
one ordered pair-label cell under any whole-vertex labeling.
Its load is at least $\Delta$.
\end{proof}
This is a limitation of near-balanced whole-vertex packing. It is not
a SAT lower bound, nor a claim that the polylogarithmic factor in
\eqref{eq:new-packing} is optimal.

\section{Precision: the reused exact taper and the new guard}
\label{sec:precision}

Use the positive linear taper
\[
 q_i=h(u-i)\quad(0\le i<u),\qquad q_i=0\quad(i\ge u).
\]
There are $L/2^i$ grid points of valuation $i$. Consequently the exact
number of Hermite conditions is
\[
 hW(a,u),\qquad
 W(a,u)=\sum_{i=0}^{u-1}\frac L{2^i}(u-i)
       =L(2u-2+2^{1-u}).
\]
The smallest sufficient integer slope is the earlier refinement
\begin{equation}
 h=\ceil{\frac{M(2L-1)+1}{W(a,u)}}.\label{eq:h}
\end{equation}
It satisfies $h\le3M/u$ when $M\ge u$, and
$h=(1+o(1))M/u$ uniformly when $u\to\infty$, $u/M\to0$, and $L\to\infty$.

For a node $t$ with $v_2(t)=i<u$, the source's exact Hermite loss,
as used in \cite{precisionpacket}, is
\[
 B_i=D\sum_{j=1}^{a-i}\frac j{2^j}(q_i-q_{i+j}),
 \qquad D=2^{a-i}.
\]
For a linear taper,
\[
 B_i=hD\sum_{j=1}^{a-i}\frac{j\min(j,u-i)}{2^j}
 \le hD\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{j^2}{2^j}=6hD.
\]
The multiplier of the $z^j$ coefficient in recovery has valuation
$-B_i-j(a+1-i)$. Thus the exact sufficient precision is
$p+B_i+(q_i-1)(a+1-i)$; a convenient larger integral prescription is
\begin{equation}
 P_t=p+6hD+hu(a+1).\label{eq:precision}
\end{equation}
This preserves the earlier leading constant six and strictly increases
precision along recursive paths.

The source and earlier schedules used $a_d=2\sum_{j\le d}u_j$. Define instead
\begin{equation}
 g=\ceil{8\log_2(\lambda+1)},\qquad
 a_d=\sum_{j=0}^d u_j+(d+1)g,\qquad a_{-1}=0.
 \label{eq:additive-guard}
\end{equation}
For every retained $i<u_d$,
\begin{equation}
 D=2^{a_d-i}\ge2^{a_{d-1}+g+1}.\label{eq:guard-gap}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Inherited precision is negligible]\label{lem:precision-guard}
Suppose active calls have $M>s/\lambda$, depth $O(\log\lambda)$,
$1\le u\le\lambda$, $a\le\lambda$, and uniformly $u\to\infty$.
With \eqref{eq:h}, \eqref{eq:precision}, and \eqref{eq:additive-guard},
\[
 P_t\le(6+o(1))MD/u
\]
uniformly over active calls and retained nodes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $h\le3M/u$, one precision increment is at most
$18s2^a+3s(a+1)$. Summing preceding increments, using $P_0\le s$
and nondecreasing $a$, gives the convenient coarse bound
\[
 p\le22s\lambda^2\,2^{a_{d-1}}
\]
for sufficiently large $\lambda$. By \eqref{eq:guard-gap},
\[
 \frac{p}{MD/u}
 \le11\lambda^4\,2^{-g}=O(\lambda^{-4}),
\]
and
\[
 \frac{hu(a+1)}{MD/u}
 \le O(\lambda^2 2^{-g})=O(\lambda^{-6}).
\]
Finally $6hD=(6+o(1))MD/u$. All estimates are uniform under the
stated hypotheses.
\end{proof}
Only a logarithmic guard is needed. This is a distinct change from
counting the Hermite conditions more accurately.

\section{An integer capacity schedule}
\label{sec:schedule}

Fix
\[
 r=54,\qquad b=9,\qquad H=\ceil{s/\lambda},
 \qquad \Delta=\ceil{s^{9/10}}.
\]
Calls with $M\le H$ are leaves. Define
\begin{align}
 d_{\max}&=\ceil{\log_2\lambda},&
 G&=(d_{\max}+1)g,\label{eq:reserve}\\
 A&=\max\{A\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:
          4r^{2A}\Delta\lambda^6\le s\},&
 K&=A-G.\label{eq:capacity}
\end{align}
If the set in \eqref{eq:capacity} is empty, $\lambda<4$, or $K\le0$,
use direct contraction. Otherwise set
\begin{equation}
 u_d=\floor{\frac{8K}{9^{d+1}}},
 \qquad
 a_d=\sum_{j=0}^d u_j+(d+1)g.
 \label{eq:adaptive}
\end{equation}
If an actual active call has $u_d=0$, use direct contraction at that
call. This is a finite-size guard, not an additional global requirement
on $u_{d_{\max}}$.

\begin{lemma}[Packing and depth without circular assumptions]
\label{lem:capacity}
Every non-fallback active call has $a_d\le A$, satisfies the packing
hypotheses, and has children with at most $(1+3/\lambda)M/27$ edges.
No active call requires a depth larger than $d_{\max}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The geometric sum gives $\sum_{j\ge0}u_j\le K$.
At any depth $d\le d_{\max}$,
\[
 a_d\le K+(d_{\max}+1)g=A.
\]
The cap then gives
\[
 4r^{2a_d}\Delta\lambda^4\le s/\lambda^2<M.
\]
Also $r^{2a_d}\ge2^{2a_d}=L^2$, so
$4L^2\Delta\lambda^6\le s$. Since
$\Delta^3\lambda^6\ge s$, it follows that $2L\le\Delta$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:labeling} and Corollary~\ref{cor:packets} apply.

For $\lambda\ge4$, $(1+3/\lambda)/27<1/2$. Inductively a depth-$d$
node has at most $s2^{-d}$ edges, or an ancestor has already terminated.
A child beyond $d_{\max}$ therefore has fewer than $s/\lambda$ edges
and is a leaf. This closes the induction using only depths where
$a_d\le A$ was established.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform active taper growth]\label{lem:taper-growth}
For sufficiently large $s$, no active call takes a parameter fallback,
and uniformly over active depths
\begin{equation}
 u_d=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_*\lambda\,9^{-d},
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_*=\frac2{45\log_2 54}.
 \label{eq:u-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
In particular the smallest active $u_d$ tends to infinity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Maximality of $A$, together with
$\log_2\Delta=(9/10)\log_2s+o(1)$, gives
\[
 A=\frac{\lambda}{20\log_2r}
   -\frac3{\log_2r}\log_2\lambda+O(1).
\]
Also $G=O((\log\lambda)^2)$, so
\[
 K=\frac{\lambda}{20\log_2r}-O((\log\lambda)^2).
\]
For $\lambda\ge78$, the child ratio is at most $1/26$. Any active
depth consequently satisfies $d<\log_{26}\lambda$. Its unfloored taper
is at least a positive constant times
\[
 \lambda\,9^{-d}
 \ge \lambda^{\,1-\ln9/\ln26},
 \qquad 1-\frac{\ln9}{\ln26}=0.3256109658\ldots>0.
\]
Thus the absolute floor error below one is a uniform relative $o(1)$.
Substituting the leading term of $K$ proves \eqref{eq:u-asymptotic}.
The cap exists and $K>0$ eventually, and the actual active tapers are
then positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[An important finite-size distinction]
It would be incorrect to require $u_{d_{\max}}\ge1$ before accepting
the schedule: $d_{\max}$ is deliberately loose, and that artificial
taper can vanish even as $s$ tends to infinity. Only actual active
depths, which are bounded by $\log_{26}\lambda$, need positive taper.
\end{remark}

\subsection{All parameter decisions are exact}

For integers $s\ge2$, compute
\[
 \lambda=(s-1).\operatorname{bit\_length},\qquad
 d_{\max}=(\lambda-1).\operatorname{bit\_length},
\]
and
\[
 g=((\lambda+1)^8-1).\operatorname{bit\_length}.
\]
The integer $\Delta$ is the least integer with $\Delta^{10}\ge s^9$.
Integer comparisons determine $A$ from \eqref{eq:capacity}.
No floating-point logarithm is used to choose a feasible schedule.

For readers preferring fixed parameters, the choices
\[
 (r,b,\varepsilon)=(64,10,1/135)
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad(54,9,2/259)
\]
with $u_d=\floor{\varepsilon\lambda/b^d}$ and the same additive guard

For the second choice, strict packing slack follows from the exact

\section{Reusing the factorial Cauchy modes}
\label{sec:cauchy}

We restate the needed content of \cite{cauchypacket} to make compatibility
checkable. For a retained node, write $T=t+2^{a+1}\zeta$, $v_2(t)=i$.
In one column tile,
\[
 y=y_0+2^{i+1}j,\qquad 0\le j<J,\qquad J=D/2.
\]
For a crossing row, put
\[
 d_0=t-y_0+x,\qquad
 z_x=\frac{2^{i+1}}{d_0+2^{a+1}\zeta},\qquad
 c_x=\frac{\mathcal C(T)}
           {\mathcal C'(0)(d_0+2^{a+1}\zeta)}.
\]
The source provides $d_0\ne0$, $v_2(d_0)\le i$, and $z_x,c_x\in2\Ocal$.
The entry is $c_x/(1-z_xj)$.

Define
\[
 A_b(z)=\prod_{h<b}\frac{z-2h}{1-hz},
 \qquad
 B_b(j)=\prod_{h<b}\frac{j-h}{1-2hj}.
\]
The elementary identity
\[
 (1-bz)(1-2bj)-(z-2b)(j-b)
       =(1-2b^2)(1-zj)
\]
telescopes to
\[
 \frac1{1-zj}
 =\sum_{b=0}^{J-1}
 \frac{(1-2b^2)A_b(z)B_b(j)}
      {(1-bz)(1-2bj)}
 \qquad(0\le j<J).
\]
The remainder is zero because $B_J(j)=0$. Move the integer factorial
from the column falling product to the row coefficient:
\begin{align}
 \ell_b(j)&=
 \frac{(1-2b^2)\binom jb}{\prod_{h=0}^b(1-2hj)},\\
 r_b(x)&=c_x\,b!\,\frac{A_b(z_x)}{1-bz_x}.
\end{align}
Then
\[
 W_{xy}(T)=\sum_{b=0}^{J-1}r_b(x)\ell_b(j).
\]
The source feature is integral and all displayed denominators are units;
no factorial is inverted in the residue ring.

To justify the second factorial valuation over the extension, set
$w=z_x/2$ and $w_0=2^i/d_0\in\mathbb Z_{(2)}$. Direct subtraction gives
\[
 w-w_0\in2^{a+1-i}\Ocal.
\]
For $b<2^{a+1-i}$, the product
$\prod_{h<b}(w-h)$ has valuation at least $v_2(b!)$:
at each binary precision, count the integers $h$ in the appropriate
residue class of $w_0$. This is valid for every relevant $b<J$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 v_2(r_b(x))\ge
 w_b:=1+b+2v_2(b!)=3b+1-2s_2(b).
 \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
The near-prime-subring congruence is essential; the assertion is not
made for arbitrary extension elements $w$.

The new packing retains the same tile, slot, and source/target identities.
The adaptive schedule changes neither $J=D/2$ nor the congruence exponent
$a+1-i$. Thus \eqref{eq:weights} remains available with no extra families.
For a mode tuple $\beta$, terms with
$\sum_{f,b}w_b\beta_{f,b}\ge P_t$ vanish modulo $2^{P_t}$.
The remaining tuples factor into the original child contractions.

\section{Entropy and the complete time bound}
\label{sec:complexity}

Let $N(P,c)$ count nonnegative mode-exponent tuples of total weight below
$P$ with at most $c$ families. Extending finite tiles to all $b\ge0$
only increases this count. For every $\theta>0$,
\[
 \ln N(P,c)
 \le\theta P+c\sum_{b\ge0}-\ln(1-e^{-\theta w_b}).
\]
Because $w_b=3b+O(\log(b+2))$,
\[
 \sum_{b\ge0}-\ln(1-e^{-\theta w_b})
 =\left(\frac{\pi^2}{18}+o(1)\right)\frac1\theta
 \quad(\theta\downarrow0).
\]
This follows by a Riemann-sum comparison and
$\int_0^\infty-\ln(1-e^{-x})\,dx=\pi^2/6$; the finitely many smallest
weights and their logarithmic perturbations are lower order.
Optimizing $\theta$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \ln N(P,c)\le
 \left(\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{3}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{cP}.
 \label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}

At our calls,
\[
 c\le(2+o(1))M/D,\qquad P_t\le(6+o(1))MD/u.
\]
Ceilings in the family envelope are negligible because $M/D\to\infty$
uniformly. Optimizing the stated upper envelopes for $c$ and $P_t$, the entropy
saddle satisfies $\theta=O(\sqrt u/D)=O(\lambda^{-15/2})$, using $D\ge2^{g+1}$
and $u\le\lambda$. Hence the $o(1)$ in \eqref{eq:entropy} is uniform.
We obtain
\begin{equation}
 \ln N_{\rm call}\le
 (2\pi\sqrt{2/3}+o(1))\,M/\sqrt u.
 \label{eq:local-entropy}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Improved normalized contraction certificate]
\label{thm:main}
Under the normalized interface of Section~\ref{sec:interface}, the two-stage
packing, additive guard, and adaptive capacity schedule, combined with the
inherited contraction identities and factorial modes, compute the exact
contraction deterministically in polynomial space and time
\begin{equation}
 (s+\desc)^{O(1)}
 2^{(\Cstar+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},
 \qquad
 \boxed{\Cstar=\frac{9\pi}{4\ln2}\sqrt{15\log_2 54}}.
 \label{eq:main-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At depth $d$, the product of the small padding overheads satisfies
\[
 (1+3/\lambda)^d=1+o(1)
\]
uniformly for $d=O(\log\lambda)$. Thus
\[
 M_d\le(1+o(1))s\,27^{-d}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:taper-growth} gives
$u_d=(1+o(1))\varepsilon_*\lambda\,9^{-d}$.
Summing the logarithms of the branching bounds along a path using
\eqref{eq:local-entropy} yields
\[
 \left(\frac{2\pi\sqrt{2/3}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_*}}
       \sum_{d\ge0}(3/27)^d+o(1)\right)
       \frac{s}{\sqrt\lambda}.
\]
The geometric sum is $9/8$. Dividing by $\ln2$ and substituting
$\varepsilon_*=2/(45\log_2 54)$ gives precisely \eqref{eq:main-bound}.

The remaining operations do not change this coefficient. The number of
interpolation points, nodes, and child choices is polynomial in $s$.
Polynomial overhead across $O(\log\lambda)$ levels contributes
$O(\lambda\log\lambda)$ to the logarithm. Leaves have at most
$H=\ceil{s/\lambda}$ edges, giving a lower-order $O(s/\lambda)$ exponent.

For the arithmetic and entry routines, $a\le A=O(\lambda)$ and the
precision bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:precision-guard} is polynomial in $s$.
In fact $L\le s^{1/(20\log_2 54)+o(1)}$, so
$M(2L-1)<s^4$ eventually. The original residue-field extension has
enough distinct interpolation points. Existing vertices retain their
degrees and new packet vertices have at most $2L\le\Delta$ ports.
The original packet-entry cost $\exp(O(L\log s))$ and original entry
allowance $\exp(O(s^{9/10}))$ both have lower-order logarithms.
The earlier streamed finite-difference routine can also be used, but is
not necessary for this coefficient.

Each new packet entry is computed directly from its own creation-time
packet data, mode tuple, interpolation value, precision, and local kernel.
Previously existing tensors remain separate vertices and are evaluated
from their own original or creation-time descriptions. Thus entry routines
do not compose recursively. New entry evaluation has the stated
$\exp(O(L\log s))$ bound, while retained descriptions are immutable and
shared by reference. The number of recursive alternatives depends
polynomially on $s$ and not on $\desc$; the description-dependent work
per call retains a fixed polynomial factor in $s+\desc$.

The depth-first tuple enumeration, exact rational packing calculations,
coordinate arithmetic, and recursive child accumulation use polynomial
space. Each tensor keeps its creation-time canonical representatives.
The small-size and zero-taper fallbacks occur only for a finite range of
$s$, by the uniform growth proof, and do not affect the asymptotic bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A certified numerical enclosure}

The supplied checker uses rational alternating-series bounds for Machin's
identity and positive-series bounds for logarithms. It certifies
\[
 94.748202259534<\Cstar<94.748202259535.
\]
Floating-point arithmetic is used only for convenient table displays.

The adaptive schedule attains this leading coefficient with one algorithm.
It is stronger than merely showing that a family of algorithms approaches
the fixed-parameter infimum as a parameter tends to a boundary.
No global optimality assertion is made.

\section{Algorithm specification and integration}
\label{sec:algorithm}

The specification below identifies every replacement while preserving
the original exact-algebra interfaces.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Normalize the root to the declared $s,\desc,P_0,\ell,\Delta$ interface.
Construct the original unramified coefficient extension and retain the
creation-precision convention.
\item Compute $\lambda,g,d_{\max},G,A,K$ using integer arithmetic.
Use direct contraction when the root fallback applies.
\item At a call with $M$ edges, return a direct contraction if $M\le H$.
Otherwise compute $u_d,a_d$ and use the finite fallback if $u_d=0$.
\item Greedily balance whole row vertices among $r^{a_d}$ labels.
Assign whole column vertices by minimizing the exact conditional potential
\eqref{eq:conditional}.
\item Pad the pair-label loads to a common multiple of $L$, then use the
original orientation-and-copy assembly. Tag new tensor descriptions with
their creation precision.
\item Use \eqref{eq:h} for the Hermite taper. At each retained node use the
sufficient precision \eqref{eq:precision}.
\item Substitute the factorial Cauchy features and enumerate tuples of
total certified weight below $P_t$. For a fixed tuple, form the unchanged
source/target child contractions and evaluate them recursively.
\item Recover the Taylor data and then $F(0)$ by the original exact
interpolation and Hermite formulas. Reduce to the precision of the call.
\end{enumerate}

The natural integration point is the collision-packing subsection of
\texttt{quantitative-contraction.tex}, followed by its parameter,
precision, and running-time proofs. The source deformation, coefficient
extension, and tensor-creation semantics remain part of the proof
dependencies. The accompanying code implements the new labeling, packet
assembly on finite examples, integer schedules, and local checks; it does
not implement all eight steps as a production contractor.

\section{Consequences for SAT and limits of the improvement}
\label{sec:sat}

Applying the new contractor to the existing reductions preserves exact
counting, determinism, polynomial space, and the general and fixed-density
orders \eqref{eq:general}--\eqref{eq:fixed}. The density-aware checks from
\cite{densitypacket,densitypacket2} can be retained, giving the same
density-sensitive exponent envelope
\[
 (n+m)^{O_k(1)}
 2^{O_k\left(
 \frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
 \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}
\]
with the improved contraction component. The scalar lift of
\cite{scalarpacket} is compatible with the scalar special case $\ell=1$.

A numerical coefficient in terms of $n$ requires tracking the reduction's
wire budget, heavy-variable branches, array enumeration, and all other
leading terms. This report does not replace an unknown $O_k$-constant
in a SAT statement by $\Cstar$. Nor does it compare $\Cstar$ numerically
with the different method in \cite{alternate}, whose coefficient is not
specified.

For a concrete occurrence-based application, one may take the original
bipartite variable--clause incidence graph. Handle $N=0$ directly. If there
are $N\ge1$ literal occurrences, branch on variables occurring more than $\ceil{(N+1)^{9/10}}$
times. There are at most $N/(N+1)^{9/10}$ such variables. Within each branch retain every unfixed original incidence, including
incidences in clauses whose restricted tensor is constant; this preserves
the counting of all unfixed occurring variables. The remaining
variable equality tensors and clause tensors satisfy the normalized degree
cap with $s=N+1$ and can be evaluated in polynomial time. The branching
cost is lower order, and the count precision is at most $N+1$ after unused
variables have been removed. Thus for fixed width one gets the explicit
occurrence-based certificate
\[
 \poly(n,m)\,
 2^{(\Cstar+o(1))(N+1)/\sqrt{\log_2(N+1)}}.
\]
Unused declared variables contribute their known power of two afterward.
This application illustrates the normalization; the new mathematical
contribution is the contractor, not the elementary incidence encoding.

\subsection{Why the logarithmic order has not changed}

The changes above increase how much taper can be used and reduce the
number of mode families. Their leading scales are still
\[
 c=\Theta(M/D),\qquad P=\Theta(MD/u),\qquad u=\Theta(\log s)
\]
near the root. Therefore $\sqrt{cP}=\Theta(M/\sqrt u)$ persists.

There is also a direct obstruction to improving this order solely by
enumerating the same unrestricted mode tuples more selectively by degree.
Suppose $c=\Theta(M/D)$ colors have modes up to
$t=\floor{\xi D/\sqrt u}$, with a sufficiently small fixed $\xi>0$.
Since $w_b\le3b+1$, every zero-one choice among these $ct$ coordinates
has total weight at most $O(ct^2)\le P$ and total degree $ct\le M$.
There are
\[
 2^{ct}=\exp(\Omega(M/\sqrt u))
\]
such tuples. This is a count in the abstract enumeration model; it does
not assert that all their coefficients are nonzero, or lower-bound SAT.
It explains why merely adding a total-degree cap does not supply the
missing logarithmic improvement.

\section{Further research questions}
\label{sec:future}

\paragraph{Sum surviving modes without visiting them individually.}
A smaller logarithmic order requires more than the improvements proved
here. Can the actual structured mode sum be evaluated in polynomial space
with logarithm $o(M/\sqrt u)$? A useful theorem must account for the
large inherited precision and for high degrees in any compressed
mode network. An exact representation alone does not establish that
such a network can be contracted efficiently.

\paragraph{Use the degree structure of SAT reductions.}
The normalized certificate allows degrees as large as $s^{9/10}$.
Many reduction networks have considerably smaller effective degrees.
A degree-sensitive packing and entry-cost analysis may improve explicit
coefficients further. Its benefit should be stated with the actual input
and child degree bounds, rather than silently substituting a smaller
exponent into the generic theorem.

\paragraph{Reduce the reduction's incidence-encoding entropy.}
For arbitrary clause density, the existing subset encodings introduce
$O(n\log\log n)$ wires. Can a different exact representation reduce this
to $O(n)$ while preserving local entry evaluation, bounded degrees, and
the creation-precision semantics? Density-sensitive checks already help
in particular regimes, but do not by themselves remove the unrestricted
$\log\log n$ factor.

\paragraph{Sharpen the whole-vertex packing theorem.}
The parallel-edge example shows why a factor $\Darr\Delta$ is natural.
Can the $\lambda^4$ sufficient margin be reduced while preserving simple
exact derandomization and controlled description sizes? This is unlikely
to change the leading coefficient of the adaptive schedule, which already
pays only lower-order budget for polylogarithmic margins, but could reduce
its exceptionally large finite threshold.

\paragraph{Formalize the actual replacement interfaces.}
A formal verification should connect whole-vertex conditional expectations,
orientation packing, the new schedule's depth induction, and the factorial
mode features to the original machine-level definitions. The scope should
include immutable creation-time tensor lifts. Verifying only a numerical
inequality for $\Cstar$ would leave the algorithmic content unverified.
