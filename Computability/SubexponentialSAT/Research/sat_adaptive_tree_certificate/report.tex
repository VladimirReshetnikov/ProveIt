\begin{abstract}
We review the two primary manuscripts, fifteen placed refinement packets, and
five newer incoming archives in ProveIt at \pin. Under their common normalized
Boolean tensor-contraction interface, we obtain time
\[
 (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(30.891018920366\ldots+o(1))s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}}
\]
and space with coefficient $30.753912832055\ldots$ on the same exponential
scale. The improvement comes from selecting among three contraction trees for
each exact mode-weight allocation. An elementary support-averaging lemma
reduces its uniform analysis to finite integer certificates: $9{,}261$ mask
triples at nine groups and $35{,}937$ at thirteen groups. A strict rational
$9\to13\to7$ schedule pays for every recursively evaluated child entry.

The strongest submitted comparable coefficient was $35.354588058401\ldots$;
combining its ingredients with an existing boundary-retreat technique already
gives $35.063598846170\ldots$. The new bound improves both. It is a
coefficient improvement in an exponential upper bound, with subexponential
space. It does not improve the logarithmic exponent order for unrestricted
SAT, establish the conditional $n/\log n$-type targets, or independently
certify the upstream ETH claim. We also prove the exact full-table boundary
width of a complete directed mode graph, isolating why adaptive trees alone
do not remove the square-root logarithm in this representation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Outcome and interpretation}
\label{sec:outcome}

The principal result is a new, rigorously checkable coefficient certificate
for the same normalized contraction problem used by the latest packets.
Put $\lambda=\lceil\log_2s\rceil$. Our exact constants are
\begin{align}
 C_T&=\frac{\pi}{\ln2}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac{128000}{5103}}+
       \sqrt{\frac{134144}{41067}}\right)
   =30.891018920366092\ldots,\label{eq:CT}\\
 C_S&=\frac{\pi}{\ln2}\sqrt{\frac{302080}{6561}}
   =30.753912832054975\ldots.\label{eq:CS}
\end{align}
Thus $30.892$ and $30.754$ are convenient eventual time and space coefficients.
The variable $s$ counts normalized Boolean wires; it is not the number of SAT
variables. The conversion of a wire coefficient into a SAT-variable
coefficient requires the reduction's constants, which are not supplied by
the reviewed numerical comparisons.

\paragraph{Scope of the request.}
The new generic normalized certificate is smaller than all displayed
comparable generic certificates in the reviewed packets. Several low-degree
specializations have smaller numerical constants under stronger hypotheses;
they remain applicable. Conditional $n/\log n$-type proposals are not proved
algorithms to which this result can claim numerical dominance. The separate
Claude manuscript has an unspecified big-$O$ constant. No claim is made to
strictly dominate that unknown constant, every possible composition of the
corpus, or a globally optimal schedule. The useful envelope retains all
established applicable branches and adds the new generic branch.

\subsection{Established order envelope and conditional frontier}
For fixed clause width $k$ and $n$ active variables, the strongest
established density-sensitive order in the corpus is
\begin{equation}
 (n+m)^{O_k(1)}
 2^{O_k\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
       \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}.
 \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
The density constants are uniform in the stated range \cite{density}.
Unused variables can be removed and their count restored by a power of two.
The direct clause-network branch with exponent
$O_k(m/\sqrt{\log(m+2)})$ is retained when useful \cite{upstream,claude}.
Small sizes are handled separately, so no expression involving a small or
negative iterated logarithm is used literally.

At $m=\Theta(n)$ the exponent scale is $n/\sqrt{\log n}$; at
$m=\Theta(n\log\log n)$ it is $n\log\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$; the
unrestricted worst-case scale is $n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$.
Neither our method nor any of the five current incoming packets changes these
orders. The strongest conditional proposal remains
\[
 2^{O(n\log\log n/\log n)}\quad\hbox{in general},\qquad
 2^{O(n/\log n)}\quad\hbox{at fixed density},
\]
subject to the unproved structured-mode summation algorithm of
\cite{precision}. Its existence is not implied by compact rank encodings:
the encoded group tensors can violate the degree conditions of the
contraction theorem.

\section{The exact normalized interface and inherited ingredients}
\label{sec:interface}

The root is a bipartite Boolean tensor network with at most $s$ wires and
maximum degree at most $\lceil s^{9/10}\rceil$. Its entries lie in
\[
 (\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell),\qquad P_0,\ell\le s.
\]
The input-description length is $\mathcal L$. Entry routines permitted by
the reviewed interface take $(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ time
and polynomial space, with fixed constants. The source's extension ring,
immutable creation-precision tags, entrywise canonical lifts, Cauchy
deformation, and Hermite recovery are retained \cite{upstream,asymmetric,tree}.
This report supplies new aggregation and resource analysis, rather than an
independent proof of all those ingredients.

At an active call, let $M$ be its wire count, $r$ its fixed group count,
$u$ its taper length, $a$ its cumulative packet exponent, $i$ a retained
valuation, and $D=2^{a-i}$. The combined inherited interface provides:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Near-unit common pair padding, with padded mass $(1+o(1))M$ and
child size at most $(2/r+o(1))M$.
\item For every directed pair of distinct groups, at most
$(2+o(1))M/(r^2D)$ mode families of each index $q$, with weights
$a+1-i+4q-2\pc(q)$.
\item Sharp recovery precision $p_t\le(6+o(1))MD/u$, using an additive
logarithmic guard to absorb inherited precision.
\item Exact group factors $Z_g$ whose entries are recursively evaluated
child contractions. These entries are not unit-cost oracles.
\end{enumerate}

Allocate exact nonnegative weights $b_{gh}$ with $\sum_{g\ne h}b_{gh}<p_t$.
For each directed pair, let $\mathcal I_{gh}$ be the set of its mode-exponent
tuples of exact weight $b_{gh}$. Skip an allocation if one list is empty.
Write
\[
 h_{gh}=\log_2|\mathcal I_{gh}|,\qquad
 \K(M,u)=\kappa\frac{M}{\sqrt u},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}.
\]
The corresponding finite sum is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\xi_{gh}\in\mathcal I_{gh}}
     \prod_{g=1}^r Z_g\bigl((\xi_e)_{e\ni g}\bigr).
 \label{eq:graphsum}
\end{equation}
There are polynomially many exact-weight arrays because $r$ is a member of
the fixed set $\{7,9,13\}$ and all budgets are polynomial in $s$.

\begin{lemma}[Inherited weighted entropy estimate]\label{lem:entropy}
For every fixed collection of nonnegative directed weights $z_{gh}$,
uniformly over the exact-weight arrays and active depths used below,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{g\ne h}z_{gh}h_{gh}
 \le\left(\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{g\ne h}z_{gh}^2}}r+o(1)\right)\K(M,u).
 \label{eq:entropy}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the shared full-Cauchy counting argument of \cite{adaptive,tree}.
For completeness, lowering weights to $1+4q-2\pc(q)$ gives
\[
 \sum_{q\ge0}-\ln(1-e^{-\theta(1+4q-2\pc(q))})
 =\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}+O(\log^2(2/\theta)).
\]
For pair $e$, use parameter $\theta/z_e$ and multiply the logarithm of its
generating-function bound by $z_e$. Summing gives leading terms
\[
 \theta p_t+\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}
       \frac{(2+o(1))M}{r^2D}\sum_ez_e^2.
\]
Optimization in $\theta$ gives \eqref{eq:entropy}. The common saddle scale is
$\sqrt u/D$; the guard makes the error lower order uniformly. Zero weights
are omitted. The joint bound does not assume a separate large-budget
approximation for each individual exact list.
\end{proof}

Exact list sizes are computable by the standard unbounded-knapsack recurrence
\[
 N(j,b)=N(j-1,b)+N(j,b-w_j),\qquad N(0,0)=1,
\]
with zero values for negative budgets and $N(0,b)=0$ for $b>0$. Counts have
polynomial bit length. The same table gives enumeration and rank/unrank
operations with polynomial overhead. Thus later selections use explicit
integers, not a counting oracle or floating-point decisions.

\section{A uniform certificate for adaptive contraction trees}
\label{sec:portfolio}

\subsection{Operation supports}
For disjoint vertex sets $A,B$, put $O=[r]\setminus(A\cup B)$. The table
for $A$ retains all pair indices between $A$ and its complement, and sums
indices internal to $A$. Merging the tables for $A$ and $B$ is a rectangular
matrix multiplication. Its three independent index classes join
$A$ to $O$, $A$ to $B$, and $B$ to $O$.

Let their logarithmic dimensions be $H_1,H_2,H_3$. Using the existing exact
coordinate/CRT bridge and the established strict matrix exponent
$\omega_{\mathbb Q}<12/5$ \cite{mm,tree}, the operation has time exponent
\begin{equation}
 H_1+H_2+H_3-\frac35\min(H_1,H_2,H_3)
 =\max_{j=1,2,3}\left(\frac25 H_j+\sum_{k\ne j}H_k\right).
 \label{eq:matrixcost}
\end{equation}
The final three-block contraction uses the same formula for the triangle of
block-pair indices. Its concluding trace or inner product is no larger.

For clarity about arithmetic, the bridge expands a ring entry in polynomially
many integer coordinates, lifts coordinates to $[0,2^p)$, and computes
coordinate matrix products modulo sufficiently many nonexceptional odd
primes. CRT reconstructs their nonnegative integer values, then ring
structure constants are applied and the result reduced modulo $2^p$.
Each reconstruction bound has polynomial bit length. A fixed rational
bilinear matrix algorithm with exponent below $12/5$ supplies constant-size
identities; clearing its fixed denominators excludes only finitely many
primes. Square blocking gives \eqref{eq:matrixcost}. This neither divides
by a nonunit in the source ring nor assumes a field structure for it.
Sequential recursion and reconstruction use a polynomial factor times
input/output table storage. The finite checker tests exact regrouping; it
does not implement this theoretical fast backend.

For an undirected pair $e=\{g,h\}$, set $g_e=h_{gh}+h_{hg}$. Multiplying
each linear form in \eqref{eq:matrixcost} by five gives a vector
$v\in\{0,2,5\}^{\binom r2}$. Its entries are zero on edges internal to a
part of the operation tripartition, two on its discounted class, and five
on the other two classes.

A tree plan first partitions the ordered groups into three nonempty blocks.
Within each block, split its ordered list at $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, recur on
the two pieces, and merge them. Finally contract the three block tables.
There are $r-3$ binary merges and one final triangle, hence $3(r-2)$ support
vectors. Let $\mathcal V_j$ be the support set of plan $j$ and define
\begin{equation}
 F_j(g)=\frac15\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}v\cdot g.
 \label{eq:Fj}
\end{equation}
Up to polynomial factors, $2^{F_j(g)}$ pays for all its local operations
after the group-function entries have been cached.

\begin{theorem}[Finite support-averaging certificate]\label{thm:portfolio}
Fix $q$ tree plans on $r$ groups. Define the exact integer
\begin{equation}
 Q=\max_{v_j\in\mathcal V_j}
       \left\|v_1+\cdots+v_q\right\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
Selecting the plan with least $F_j(g)$ gives local nonrecursive time and
local storage at most a polynomial factor times
\[
 2^{(b+o(1))\K(M,u)},\qquad
 b=\frac{\sqrt{2Q}}{5qr}.
\]
Selection is deterministic and polynomial-time in the original size
parameter. The estimate is uniform over every feasible exact-weight array.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pointwise for every nonnegative $g$,
\begin{align*}
 \min_jF_j(g)
 &\le\frac1q\sum_jF_j(g)\\
 &=\frac1{5q}\max_{v_j\in\mathcal V_j}
                  (v_1+\cdots+v_q)\cdot g.
\end{align*}
For each tuple of support vectors, assign both orientations of an edge the
weight $(v_1+\cdots+v_q)_e/(5q)$. Its squared directed norm is
$2\|v_1+\cdots+v_q\|_2^2/(25q^2)$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy}.
The menu and all supports are fixed and finite, so the $o(1)$ is uniform
over all tuples. There is no exchange of a minimum and a maximum and no
assumption that vertex permutations act as arbitrary edge permutations.

For selection, compute each integer
\[
 P_j=\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}
       \prod_{\{g,h\}}\bigl(|\mathcal I_{gh}|\,|\mathcal I_{hg}|\bigr)^{v_{\{g,h\}}}.
\]
Comparing $P_j$ exactly is equivalent to comparing $F_j(g)$.
Their bit lengths are polynomial, and $q,r$ are fixed.

Finally, every local table is an input or output of one of the chosen
plan's matrix operations. Each such table has logarithmic size equal to a
sum of two $H_i$'s. For nonnegative $H_i$, expression
\eqref{eq:matrixcost} is at least every such sum. The imported bridge's
storage bound therefore puts every local table below $2^{F_j(g)}$ up to a
polynomial factor. Singleton caches are inputs to a first merge or the final
triangle, so their storage is included. Their expensive recursive evaluation
time is accounted for separately in Section~\ref{sec:recurrence}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What is new here.}
The prior adaptive packet minimizes total crossing entropy and then bounds
its flat block construction separately \cite{adaptive}. The tree packet
uses fixed trees and explicitly leaves uniform analysis of input-dependent
trees open \cite{tree}. Theorem~\ref{thm:portfolio} controls the complete
operation sets, including block construction, under a single adaptive choice.
Its proof uses elementary convexity and the inherited entropy inequality.
The contribution is the certified composition and resulting bound within
the inspected corpus; no literature-wide priority claim is made for
averaging, tree portfolios, or fast matrix multiplication.

\section{A fully paid \texorpdfstring{$9\to13\to7$}{9 to 13 to 7} recursion}
\label{sec:recurrence}

\subsection{Local recurrence}
Each group has $2(r-1)$ incident directed pairs. Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy}
therefore bounds the total number of entries to cache, up to fixed and
polynomial factors, by $2^{(\alpha_r+o(1))\K}$, where
\[
 \alpha_r=\frac{\sqrt{2(r-1)}}r.
\]
Every entry calls the actual recursive child contraction. Combining this
with Theorem~\ref{thm:portfolio} gives
\begin{equation}
 T_d\le\poly(s)\left[
       2^{(\alpha_{r_d}+o(1))\K_d}T_{d+1}
       +2^{(b_{r_d}+o(1))\K_d}\right].
 \label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
The three-plan coefficients apply at $r=9,13$. At $r=7$, use one fixed
plan with blocks $(0,1,2)\mid(3,4)\mid(5,6)$. Its largest squared
directed support gives
\[
 b_7^2=\frac{632}{1225},\qquad b_7=0.718275\ldots.
\]
The common polynomial exponent in \eqref{eq:recurrence} is fixed because
there are only three possible group counts. Exponential tables are sorted
and scanned in their relevant index order; index lengths and record lengths
are polynomial. Reshaping and sorting add polynomial factors in those
lengths, not an unaccounted exponential random-access assumption.

\subsection{Strict rational schedule}
Define
\begin{align}
 r_0&=9,& \eta_0&=\frac{63}{4000},\notag\\
 r_1&=13,& \eta_1&=\frac9{800},\notag\\
 r_d&=7\quad(d\ge2),&
 \eta_d&=\frac{77}{16200}\left(\frac49\right)^{d-2},\label{eq:schedule}\\
 u_d&=\lfloor\eta_d\lambda\rfloor,&
 B&=\lceil6\log_2\lambda\rceil,\notag\\
 a_d&=\sum_{j=0}^d(u_j+B),&
 H&=\left\lceil\frac{s}{\lambda^{3/4}}\right\rceil.\notag
\end{align}
Use the existing padding construction with relative slack $\lambda^{-3}$.
The strict polynomial packing margins below permit this choice and give
uniform relative padding error $O(\lambda^{-3})+s^{-c+o(1)}$ for a fixed
$c>0$. Its product over $O(\log\lambda)$ levels is $1+o(1)$.
Calls with $M\le H$ are leaves. For sufficiently large $s$, all active
$u_d$ are positive; a finite exceptional range is evaluated directly.
The exact capacity identities are
\begin{equation}
 \eta_0+\eta_1=\frac{27}{1000},\qquad
 \sum_{d\ge0}\eta_d=\frac8{225}.
 \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Packing and a noncircular depth argument}
The packing conditions imported from asymmetric balancing have, up to
polylogarithmic factors, the terms $Q^2\Delta$ at the root and
$QR$, $Q^2C$, $2^aQ^2$ later, where $Q=r^a$. Initially both sides have
degree at most $s^{9/10+o(1)}$. At later calls the retained side has degree
at most that bound and the fresh opposite side has degree at most
$2^{a_{d-1}+1}$ \cite{asymmetric,adaptive,tree}.

Exact integer inequalities certify
\[
 9^{100}<2^{317},\qquad 13^{1000}<2^{3701},\qquad 7^{100}<2^{281}.
\]
They imply the following strict leading exponent gaps:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule Stage & Dominant exponent upper bound & Gap below one\\\midrule
Root & $\frac9{10}+2\frac{63}{4000}\frac{317}{100}$ & $29/200000$\\
Depth one & $\frac9{10}+\frac{27}{1000}\frac{3701}{1000}$ & $73/1000000$\\
Tail & $\frac9{10}+\frac8{225}\frac{281}{100}$ & $1/11250$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The other fresh-side and packet-capacity terms are bounded by the exponent
\[
 \frac8{225}\left(2\frac{3701}{1000}+1\right)<1.
\]
All new packet degrees have exponent at most $8/225+o(1)<9/10$.

To avoid assuming the depth conclusion while proving it, consider
hypothetical active depths up to $3\log_3\lambda/4+O(1)$. In the tail,
\[
 u_d\ge\lambda^{\varepsilon+o(1)},\qquad
 \varepsilon=1-\frac{3\ln(9/4)}{4\ln3}>0.
\]
The cumulative guard contributes $O((\log\lambda)^2)$ to $a_d$.
The fixed positive exponent gaps above absorb these guards,
$s/M<\lambda^{3/4}$, and all packing polylogarithms uniformly.
Consequently padding is nearly lossless throughout the hypothetical range,
and every tail child has size $(2/7+o(1))M<M/3$ eventually. Two fixed
prefix levels do not change the depth bound. An active tail cannot reach
the hypothetical limit, closing the bootstrap. Floors are uniformly
negligible and padding errors accumulate to $1+o(1)$.
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 M_0\le s,\quad M_1\le(1+o(1))\frac{2s}9,\quad
 M_d\le(1+o(1))\frac{4s}{117}
                    \left(\frac27\right)^{d-2}\quad(d\ge2).
 \label{eq:Mdepth}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Precision, entries, and leaves}
Every retained valuation has $i<u_d$, so
$D=2^{a_d-i}\ge2^{a_{d-1}+B+1}$. The inherited creation-time precision is
at most $O(s\lambda^2 2^{a_{d-1}})$. Relative to $MD/u_d$, it is bounded by
\[
 O\left(\lambda^{15/4}2^{-B}\right)=o(1).
\]
The Hermite evaluation loss is lower order as well. Thus the sharp precision
and entropy estimates hold uniformly. All arithmetic bit lengths stay
polynomial in $s$, since $a_d\le(8/225)\lambda+O((\log\lambda)^2)$.

Direct leaf contraction takes $2^{O(s/\lambda^{3/4})}$ local work.
Original entry routines take $2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ times a polynomial. Fresh
packet routines take $\exp(O(2^{a_d}\log s))=\exp(s^{8/225+o(1)})$.
All are lower order on $s/\sqrt\lambda$. Immutable creation-time tensor
descriptions are retained, so later entry evaluation does not recursively
expand a previous contraction algorithm. The ancestor child-enumeration
costs multiplying leaf work are included in the next calculation.

\subsection{Exact sum of the recursion}
On the scale $s/\sqrt\lambda$, put
\[
 K_0^*=\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{\eta_0}},\qquad
 K_1^*=\frac{2\kappa}{9\sqrt{\eta_1}},\qquad
 K_2^*=\frac{4\kappa}{117\sqrt{77/16200}}.
\]
For $d\ge2$, the successive $\K_d$ coefficients decrease by
\[
 \gamma=\frac{2/7}{\sqrt{4/9}}=\frac37.
\]
Unrolling \eqref{eq:recurrence}, including the infinitely extended tail as
an upper bound, gives the maximum of
\begin{align}
 C_0&=b_9K_0^*,\notag\\
 C_1&=\alpha_9K_0^*+b_{13}K_1^*,\notag\\
 C_\infty&=\alpha_9K_0^*+\alpha_{13}K_1^*
        +K_2^*\max\left\{b_7,\frac{\alpha_7}{1-\gamma}\right\}.
 \label{eq:threecosts}
\end{align}
Here
\[
 \frac{\alpha_7}{1-3/7}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}>b_7,
\]
so all tail aggregation terms and the terminal leaf route are covered.
Exact rational interval calculations give
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule Branch & Coefficient\\\midrule
$C_0$ & $30.753912832054975\ldots$\\
$C_1$ & $30.891018920366092\ldots$\\
$C_\infty$ & $30.512927432780513\ldots$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus $C_T=C_1$, giving \eqref{eq:CT}. Products of the fixed polynomial
overheads across $O(\log\lambda)$ depths add only
$O(\lambda\log\lambda)$ to the exponent. Input descriptions are read with
a single fixed polynomial dependence on $s+\mathcal L$.

For space, local tables are bounded by the selected operation cost, as proved
in Theorem~\ref{thm:portfolio}. Ancestor tables on one active recursion path
add in size; their exponents do not add. Consequently the space coefficient is
\[
 \max\{b_9K_0^*,b_{13}K_1^*,b_7K_2^*\}=b_9K_0^*=C_S.
\]
This sharper memory argument is necessary. Using the old root boundary
cardinality coefficient $2/3$ alone would give about $34.0493$, and would
miss the benefit of selecting the tree from the actual entropy vector.

\begin{theorem}[Main normalized contraction certificate]\label{thm:main}
Under the explicit normalized interface and imported exact identities of
Section~\ref{sec:interface}, the deterministic construction above contracts
the root network in
\[
 (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{(C_T+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda}
 \quad\text{time},\qquad
 (s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}2^{(C_S+o(1))s/\sqrt\lambda}
 \quad\text{space},
\]
where $C_T,C_S$ are \eqref{eq:CT}--\eqref{eq:CS}.
The respective eventual coefficients $30.892$ and $30.754$ are valid.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Exactness follows from the inherited contraction identity
\eqref{eq:graphsum}: cached tables and the selected plan only reassociate
finite sums and products. Every weight allocation is included once.
The strict schedule validates all imported packing, degree, precision and
entry-access premises. The paid recurrence and local storage analysis above
give the asserted resources, including leaves and polynomial overhead.
\end{proof}

\section{Why this does not remove the square-root logarithm}
\label{sec:barrier}

\subsection{Exact boundary width for complete pair graphs}
The following theorem concerns a specified representation: constructing
complete subset-boundary tables. It is not a lower bound for SAT or all
tensor-contraction algorithms.

\begin{theorem}[Complete directed full-table width]\label{thm:width}
Give every ordered pair of distinct vertices in $[r]$, $r\ge3$, an
independent index set of size $Q\ge2$. A table for a subset $B$ retains
every crossing pair index, hence has $Q^{2|B|(r-|B|)}$ entries. Among binary
full-table contraction trees, the minimum possible maximum boundary-index
count is exactly
\begin{equation}
 W_r=2\left\lceil\frac r3\right\rceil
                \left(r-\left\lceil\frac r3\right\rceil\right)
       \ge\frac{4r^2}{9}.
 \label{eq:width}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Starting from the root, while a visited subset has more than $2r/3$
vertices, descend to its larger child. At the first subset of size at most
$2r/3$, its size $k$ is strictly greater than $r/3$. Therefore
\[
 \lfloor r/3\rfloor+1\le k\le\lfloor2r/3\rfloor.
\]
The minimum of $2k(r-k)$ on this integer interval is $W_r$, by concavity
and checking $r$ modulo three. This proves the lower bound. For the upper
bound, partition into three blocks of sizes differing by at most one,
contract within each, merge two blocks, and merge the third. Every internal
block subset has size at most $\lceil r/3\rceil$; the merged two-block
boundary equals the boundary of the remaining block. All are bounded by
$W_r$.
\end{proof}
An implementation materializing these tables must store at least
$Q^{W_r}$ cells, or perform at least that many output operations if it emits
every entry without retaining the full table. Adaptive permutations do not
alter the symmetric example. Even a hypothetical matrix exponent of two
cannot avoid this output-size cost. Sparse, compressed, implicit or
cancellation-based representations are outside this theorem's scope.

\subsection{A feasible exact-budget symmetric example}
One should not simply postulate equal large exact-list cardinalities. Suppose
each directed pair has an identical inventory consisting of $c$ copies of
each positive weight $w_0,\ldots,w_{J-1}$. Restrict those exponents to zero
or one. The $2^{cJ}$ choices have total weight at most
\[
 B_*=c\sum_{q<J}w_q.
\]
By the pigeonhole principle some exact weight $b_*$ has at least
$2^{cJ}/(B_*+1)$ choices. The identical inventories permit the same weight
and cardinality on every pair. The allocation is globally feasible whenever
$r(r-1)B_*<p$.

For fixed $r$, take weights $w_q\le A+4q$ with
\[
 c=\Theta\left(\frac{M}{r^2D}\right),\quad
 J=\Theta\left(\frac D{\sqrt u}\right),\quad
 A=o(D/\sqrt u),\quad p=\Theta(MD/u).
\]
With sufficiently small constants and enough available modes,
$r(r-1)B_*<p$, while
$cJ=\Theta(M/(r^2\sqrt u))$. When this last quantity dominates
$\log(p+1)$, the pigeonhole loss is lower order and
\[
 \log_2 Q=\Omega\left(\frac{M}{r^2\sqrt u}\right).
\]
Combining with \eqref{eq:width} gives explicit-table work
$\exp(\Omega(M/\sqrt u))$. Since the root taper has $u=O(\log s)$ and
$M=\Theta(s)$, merely changing trees or matrix multiplication does not
prove $\exp(o(s/\sqrt{\log s}))$ within this full-table inventory model.
This is an admissible abstract index-inventory example, not a claim that
every SAT input realizes identical inventories with nonzero coefficients.

\subsection{What a true order improvement still needs}
The reviewed taper and rank analyses already constrain simple retuning
\cite{precision,rank}. Our width theorem adds a matching obstruction for
dense explicit tables. A route around it must construct additional
structure, with full costs: an implicit representation of the actual group
functions closed under the required contractions; a structured mode-sum
algorithm using $\exp(O(M/u))$ admissible recursive calls; or a different
deformation/recovery interface that reduces precision or mode multiplicity.
This report does not construct those interfaces. In particular, neither
faster matrix multiplication nor a better tree-selection heuristic alone
establishes the conditional $s/\log s$ contraction bound.

\section{Reference implementation contract}

The primary selector accepts one positive integer list cardinality for every
directed pair. Zero cardinalities mean that an exact-weight allocation
contributes zero and must be skipped before selection. The selector compares
the integer fifth powers of predicted operation costs. It never computes a
floating-point logarithm to choose a branch.

For a merge with product dimensions $d_1,d_2,d_3$, this fifth power is
\[
 \frac{(d_1d_2d_3)^5}{\min(d_1,d_2,d_3)^3}.
\]
The division is exact. Equivalently it is the maximum of the three products
assigning exponents $2,5,5$ to the dimensions. The complete plan's score is
the maximum over its operations. The exact certificate bounds the selected
score over all admissible entropy vectors through weighted supports,
without assuming that small toy fixtures represent all asymptotic inputs.
