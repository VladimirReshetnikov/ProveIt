\section{Inherited exact interface and local evaluation model}
\label{sec:interface}

The analysis imports the exact deformation and recovery framework of
the OpenAI manuscript and the reviewed refinements
\cite{upstream,asymmetric,joint,tree,pairwise,affinejoint}.
The following are the precise parts used.

At an active call, let $M$ be the wire count, $r$ the fixed group count,
$u$ the taper range, and $a$ the cumulative packet exponent. Set
\[
 \kappa=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2},\qquad K(M,u)=\kappa\frac{M}{\sqrt u}.
\]
Nearly lossless padding gives child wire count $(2/r+o(1))M$ when the
packing gates are small. With $Q=r^a$ and current side degrees $R,C$,
sufficient gates are
\[
 QR/M=o(1),\qquad Q^2 C\,\polylog(s)/M=o(1),\qquad
 2^aQ^2/M=o(1).
\]
Both retained and freshly created degrees must be tracked.
For a retained valuation $i<u$, put $D=2^{a-i}$. The guarded working
precision is $(6+o(1))MD/u$. The sharp entropy estimate comes from the
joint Cauchy weights $a+1-i+4q-2\operatorname{popcount}(q)$ and at most
$(2+o(1))M/(r^2D)$ families of each mode index for every directed pair.

For an exact allocation of mode-weight budgets, the remaining finite sum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-sum}
 \sum_{\xi_{gh}\in\mathcal I_{gh}}
       \prod_{g=1}^r Z_g\bigl((\xi_e)_{e\ni g}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Empty-list allocations are skipped; the number of allocations is polynomial
for fixed $r$. Each entry of $Z_g$ is a genuine recursive contraction,
never a unit-cost oracle. Write
\[
 h_{gh}=\log_2|\mathcal I_{gh}|,
 \qquad g_{\{i,j\}}=h_{ij}+h_{ji}.
\]
For every fixed nonnegative directed vector $z$, the inherited inequality is
\[
 \sum_{g\ne h}z_{gh}h_{gh}
 \le (\|z\|_2/r+o(1))K.
\]
In particular, a fixed undirected support $v$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:undirected-entropy}
 v\cdot g\le(\sqrt2\|v\|_2/r+o(1))K.
\end{equation}
We use this only for finitely many fixed supports and sums, so the
uniform error applies. Exact list cardinalities are computed by the
usual finite unbounded-knapsack recurrence on positive integer weights.
The budget sizes and cardinality bit lengths are polynomial in $s$.
The same finite dynamic program supplies rank/unrank operations and
polynomial-delay enumeration of each mode list; these are inherited
interface obligations.

The input coefficient ring is
$(\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell)$ with
$1\le P_0,\ell\le s$. Ring rank, precision and coordinate bit lengths
remain polynomial in $s$.
Original entry routines may take
$\poly(s+\mathcal L)2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ time and polynomial space;
the equality and clause entries used for 3-SAT have polynomial-time access.
Every tensor keeps its immutable creation-time description and the
canonical representatives fixed at its creation precision. Fresh tensors
have degree at most twice their creation packet size, and their entry
cost is bounded by an exponential in that size with polynomial factors.

Each singleton group cache has $2(r-1)$ directed incidence classes,
so its entries require at most $2^{(\alpha_r+o(1))K}$ child evaluations,
where $\alpha_r=\sqrt{2(r-1)}/r$. If $\beta_r$ is the selected plan's
local time coefficient, the complete recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paid-recurrence}
 T_d\le\poly(s)\left[
  2^{(\alpha_{r_d}+o(1))K_d}T_{d+1}
  +2^{(\beta_{r_d}+o(1))K_d}\right].
\end{equation}
Local table storage and recursion stack storage are charged separately.

\subsection{The prior rectangular backend}

For sorted axis entropies $x\ge y\ge z\ge0$, use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangle}
 F(x,y,z)=\max\left\{(1+\varepsilon/2)(x+y),\quad
 x+(47/56+\varepsilon)y+(15/28)z\right\},
 \qquad\varepsilon=10^{-5}.
\end{equation}
The input/output-size term is retained. For unsorted axes the cost is
the maximum of nine linear forms, whose integer coefficient triples after
multiplication by $D_*=1{,}400{,}000$ are used below. Rearrangement is valid
because $1\ge47/56+\varepsilon\ge15/28$.

This backend is imported from the reviewed rectangular and weighted-pair
interfaces~\cite{pairwise}. The field bounds
$\alpha>3/10$ and $\omega<19/8$ suffice~\cite{matrix}.
Tensoring a fixed algorithm near the dual-exponent point with a square
algorithm, then blocking the largest dimension, gives the two branches
of \eqref{eq:rectangle}, with the fixed slack $\varepsilon$ covering
the finite algorithm choice. The coordinate/CRT bridge is essential over
the original finite coefficient rings: expand entries in polynomially
many integer coordinates, compute over suitable odd prime fields,
reconstruct bounded integers and reduce into the original ring.
It does not invert a nonunit there. The backend uses space bounded by a
polynomial factor times its largest input/output array. These facts are
imported theory, not implementations supplied by the small algebra tests.

A plan partitions the groups into three ordered blocks. It recursively
splits each block at the midpoint, multiplies the resulting boundary
tables, and contracts the final three block tables. Each operation has
the three edge classes of a tripartition $(A,B,O)$, where $O$ is the
remaining vertex set. A time support assigns a rectangular coefficient
to each crossing class and zero inside each part. A stored table has the
zero--one boundary support of its vertex subset. This includes singleton
caches, intermediate arrays and final block arrays.

\subsection{The coupled-pair argument}

For four plans, suppose their time-support families obey
\[
 \|v\|^2+\|w\|^2+6v\cdot w\le A_{ij}
 \quad(v\in\mathcal V_i,\ w\in\mathcal V_j,\ i<j).
\]
For any choices $v_j\in\mathcal V_j$, the exact identity is
\[
 \left\|\frac14\sum_jv_j\right\|^2
 =\frac1{48}\sum_{i<j}
       (\|v_i\|^2+\|v_j\|^2+6v_i\cdot v_j)
 \le\frac1{48}\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}.
\]
If $F_j(g)=D_*^{-1}\max_{v\in\mathcal V_j}v\cdot g$, then
\[
 \min_jF_j(g)\le\frac14\sum_jF_j(g)
 =\frac1{4D_*}\max_{v_j\in\mathcal V_j}
                         \left(\sum_jv_j\right)\cdot g.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:undirected-entropy}, this proves local coefficient
\[
 \beta^2=\frac{2\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}}{48D_*^2r^2}.
\]
The argument averages supports at the actual allocation and never
interchanges a minimum and a maximum. Applying it to time families
augmented by scaled boundary supports gives simultaneous time and storage
bounds, provided every mixed pair is also certified. These methods are
prior work~\cite{pairwise,affinejoint}; the finite supports below are new.

For independent reproduction, the inner product of two supports can also
be computed from their $3\times3$ contingency table. If
$n_{ab}=|A_a\cap B_b|$ and coefficients on distinct part pairs are
$c_{ab}$ and $d_{uv}$, the contribution for $a<b$ and $u<v$ is
\[
 c_{ab}d_{uv}\bigl(n_{au}n_{bv}+n_{av}n_{bu}\bigr).
\]
Summing these nine terms counts every edge crossing both tripartitions
once. Direct coordinate enumeration in the standalone checkers gives the
same finite integer quantities without relying on any numerical optimizer.
