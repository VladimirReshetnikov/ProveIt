\begin{abstract}
We review twenty refinement packets and the two primary manuscripts.
The five newest packets arrived through \texttt{docs/incoming} at snapshot
\texttt{fa6e904d109b}; the closing snapshot \texttt{dde01c4a63b8}
files them under SubexponentialSAT. The requested improvement in the logarithmic order of
general SAT is not established. We do obtain two new explicit exponential
upper-bound certificates in the common normalized tensor-contraction model.

For $s$ Boolean wires, maximum degree at most $\ceil{s^{9/10}}$, and the
specified entry-oracle and precision interface, our cached algorithm has
\[
 T\le\poly(s+\mathcal L)2^{(C_T+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},\qquad
 S\le\poly(s+\mathcal L)2^{(C_S+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},
\]
where
\[
 C_T=\frac\pi{\ln2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{105}}2+\sqrt{\frac{183}{40}}\right)
 =32.9158138649\ldots,\qquad
 C_S=\frac\pi{\ln2}\sqrt{\frac{244}{5}}=31.6617067862\ldots .
\]
The best displayed incoming cached certificate is $35.3545880584$ in time
and $34.2748030050$ in space. A second construction preserves polynomial
space and has time coefficient $50.3937257005$, compared with the best
displayed $55.7094783189$ and a stronger matched composition $54.1282952066$.

The new ingredient is a sharp four-block inequality for adaptive matrix
contraction, together with an adaptive four-cluster separator inequality.
The choices depend on exact mode-list sizes. Both are proved by elementary
finite-dimensional arguments; no compact-mode summation hypothesis is
assumed. The global schedules pay for every child contraction and preserve
the inherited packing and precision conditions. Supporting code verifies
the new finite identities and bounds over exact arithmetic. It does not
implement the full SAT algorithm or the asymptotically fast matrix backend.
The SAT orders $n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ and $n/\sqrt{\log n}$ remain
unchanged. All SAT consequences retain the explicitly listed upstream
deformation and reduction dependencies.

\section{Results and the comparison being made}
\label{sec:results}

The useful outcome is a proved change in how the mode graph is contracted.
The algorithm chooses among three ways of pairing four blocks after the
exact mode budgets have been fixed. Treating this choice as part of the
algorithm, rather than bounding a fixed contraction tree by its worst
allocation, reduces the leading coefficient.

\begin{theorem}[Cached normalized contraction certificate]
\label{thm:cached}
Assume the normalized interface in Section~\ref{sec:interface} and the
imported identities listed there. There is a deterministic exact contraction
algorithm with time coefficient $C_T$ and space coefficient $C_S$ displayed
in the abstract. In particular, eventual coefficients $33$ for time and
$32$ for space are valid. The algorithm uses subexponential space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial-space certificate]
\label{thm:ps}
Under the same interface, there is a deterministic exact contraction
algorithm using polynomial space and time
\[
 \poly(s+\mathcal L)2^{(C_P+o(1))s/\sqrt{\log_2s}},
 \qquad C_P=50.3937257005\ldots .
\]
An exact expression for $C_P$ is given in~\eqref{eq:ps-constant}; in
particular, the eventual coefficient $51$ is valid.
\end{theorem}

These are refinements of upper bounds. They are not measured ratios of
running times. The common size parameter $s$ counts normalized Boolean

\subsection{What the requested order improvement would require}

The strongest justified general-density envelope in the reviewed reports is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sat-envelope}
 \poly_k(n,m)\,
 2^{O_k\!\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
     \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}.
\end{equation}
It reduces to $2^{O_k(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ in the worst case and
$2^{O_{k,D}(n/\sqrt{\log n})}$ at fixed density $m\le Dn$.
The new engines retain these orders under the existing reductions.

The conditional $n\log\log n/\log n$ target in the precision packet
\cite{precision} would be a stronger order improvement. Its structured
summation algorithm is not supplied by that packet or by this report.
We do not count an unproved conditional proposal as an established upper
bound, and we do not claim to outperform it if its missing hypothesis is
eventually proved. Section~\ref{sec:barriers} records the unsuccessful order
routes investigated here and their precise obstacles.

\section{Normalized input and inherited exact interfaces}
\label{sec:interface}

Let $s\ge2$, $\lambda=\ceil{\log_2s}$, and let $\mathcal L$ be the total
graph and tensor-description length. The input is a bipartite Boolean
tensor network with at most $s$ wires and maximum degree at most
$\Delta=\ceil{s^{9/10}}$. Its contraction is
\[
 Z=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{E}}\prod_v T_v(x|_{\delta(v)}).
\]
The entries are in $(\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell)$, with
$P_0,\ell\le s$. Fixed multiples of $s$ give the same leading constants.
An original entry routine has time
$\poly(s+\mathcal L)2^{O(s^{9/10})}$ and polynomial space, with fixed
interface constants. Arbitrarily expensive tensor oracles are excluded.

During contraction the coefficient ring may be extended to
\[
 R_p=(\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[U,V]/(f(U),V^\ell),
 \qquad \deg f=O(\log s),
\]
where $f$ is monic and irreducible modulo two. Each ring entry has
polynomially many coordinates of polynomial bit length. The creation
precision of every tensor is part of its immutable description. A later
call evaluates that tensor at its creation precision and uses the same
canonical coordinate representatives. Canonical lifting is not treated
as a ring homomorphism.

The following exact ingredients are imported from the preprint and the
listed refinements \cite{openai,asymmetric,joint,adaptive,tree}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The polynomial Cauchy deformation, its integral evaluation at the
chosen nodes, exact Hermite recovery, and the child-network identity.
\item Asymmetric packet packing: with packet length $L=2^a$, $Q=r^a$
labels per side, row and column degree bounds $R,C$, nearly lossless
padding holds under $QR\polylog(s)=o(M)$,
$Q^2C\polylog(s)=o(M)$, and $LQ^2=o(M)$.
At the root the stronger symmetric term $Q^2\Delta\polylog(s)=o(M)$
is sufficient. Padded mass is $(1+o(1))M$, and each child has
$(2/r+o(1))M$ wires.
\item Below the root, the old side retains degree at most $\Delta$;
the newly created side has degree at most $2^{a_{d-1}+1}$.
\item Full crossing-block Cauchy modes, each local to one directed pair
of groups, with weights
\[
 w_q=a+1-i+4q-2\pc(q),\qquad q\ge0.
\]
At valuation level $i$, put $D=2^{a-i}$. Each directed pair has at most
$(2/r^2+o(1))M/D$ modes at any given index $q$.
\item Sharp recovery precision $p_t\le(6+o(1))MD/u$ once the inherited
precision is made negligible by an additive logarithmic buffer.
\end{enumerate}

We prove that our schedules satisfy these hypotheses. We also prove the new
mode-graph contraction identities and their resource bounds, rather than
assuming a faster oracle for the group functions.

\section{Exact mode lists and a Euclidean entropy bound}
\label{sec:entropy}

Fix one active call, interpolation node, and exact array of directed-pair
budgets $b_{gh}$ with $\sum_{g\ne h}b_{gh}<p_t$. The number of arrays is
polynomial in $s$ because $r$ is a fixed constant. Let $\mathcal I_{gh}$ be
the set of mode-exponent tuples on that pair of exact weight $b_{gh}$.
An empty list makes this array contribute zero. Otherwise write
\[
 h_{gh}=\log_2|\mathcal I_{gh}|\ge0,
 \qquad K(M,u)=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}\frac{M}{\sqrt u}.
\]
The contribution is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modegraph}
 \sum_{(\xi_{gh})\in\prod_{g\ne h}\mathcal I_{gh}}
 \prod_{g=1}^r Z_g((\xi_e)_{e\ni g}).
\end{equation}
Here an entry of $Z_g$ is obtained by contracting the prescribed child
network. It is a paid recursive call.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform entropy ball]\label{lem:entropy-ball}
For fixed $r$, uniformly over the exact arrays and active calls,
\[
 \|h\|_2\le\left(\frac1r+o(1)\right)K(M,u).
\]
Consequently, for any nonnegative vector $z$, including one chosen from
the actual lists, $z\cdot h\le(\|z\|_2/r+o(1))K$ when $\|z\|_2=O(1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Lower the actual weights to $\widehat w_q=1+4q-2\pc(q)$ and extend the
index range. Its counting function is $x/4+O(\log(x+2))$, giving
\[
 \sum_{q\ge0}-\ln(1-e^{-\theta\widehat w_q})
 =\frac{\pi^2}{24\theta}+O(\log^2(2+1/\theta))
\]
as $\theta\downarrow0$, with a corresponding uniform upper bound for all
$\theta>0$. The identity
$\int_0^\infty-\ln(1-e^{-x})\,dx=\pi^2/6$ supplies the coefficient.

For any $z\in[0,1]^{r(r-1)}$, use parameter $\theta/z_e$ for pair $e$,
omitting a pair with $z_e=0$, and multiply its logarithmic bound by $z_e$.
With $c=(2/r^2+o(1))M/D$, the result is
\[
 \ln2\,(z\cdot h)\le \theta p_t+
 \frac{c\pi^2}{24\theta}\|z\|_2^2
 +O_r(c\log^2(2+1/\theta)).
\]
Take $\|z\|_2=1$ and optimize the two leading terms. Using
$p_t\le(6+o(1))MD/u$ yields $(K/r+o(K))$ after division by $\ln2$.
The saddle has order $\sqrt u/D$. Our schedules give $D\ge\lambda^6$
up to a fixed factor, $u\le\lambda$, and $\log D=O(\lambda)$, so the
error relative to $M/\sqrt u$ is $O(\lambda^{5/2}/D)=o(1)$.
This estimate is uniform for every such $z$, even near a coordinate face.
Set $z=h/\|h\|_2$ if $h\ne0$. The zero case is immediate.
\end{proof}

There is also a way to apply the later arguments using only the source's
fixed-vector entropy lemma. The four-block proof produces just $216$
possible mean coefficient vectors. Apply the inherited estimate separately
to those fixed vectors and take the largest of their finitely many error
terms. The separator construction likewise produces a finite family of
fixed masks for each fixed arity. Thus the adaptive use does not require
an unproved extension of the source estimate to arbitrary variable weights.

\subsection{Counting, indexing, and making adaptive choices}

For mode weights $w_1,\ldots,w_v$, the dynamic program
\[
 A(j,b)=A(j-1,b)+A(j,b-w_j),\qquad A(0,0)=1
\]
with zero values at negative budgets counts the exact lists. It supports
rank, unrank, and enumeration with polynomial delay. Both the number of
modes and the budgets are polynomial in $s$; counts are bounded by
$(p_t+1)^v$ and therefore have polynomial bit length. No counting oracle
is needed.

The six aggregated domain sizes for four blocks are exact products of
integer list sizes. With matrix exponent $12/5$, the fifth power of a
merge cost is an integer quotient of products of those sizes. It can be
compared by cross multiplication. Alternatively, use ceiling binary
logarithms, computed by integer bit length: this changes the cost by only
$O_r(1)$, which is negligible in every displayed leading exponent. Thus
adaptation needs neither approximate real comparisons nor an unconstructed
optimization algorithm.

\section{Exact matrix multiplication and table storage}
\label{sec:mm}

The main theorem uses the same safe exponent $\omega=12/5$ as the incoming
cached-tree packet \cite{tree}. The primary matrix-multiplication literature
provides an exponent strictly below $12/5$ \cite{mm}. We need the exact
arithmetic bridge, since $R_p$ has nonunits and nilpotents.

Fix a finite rational bilinear multiplication algorithm whose recursive
exponent is strictly below $12/5$. Exclude the fixed finite set of primes
dividing its coefficients' denominators. A product of integer coordinate
matrices with shared dimension $N$ and entries below $2^p$ has entries
below $N2^{2p}$. Compute the product over polynomially many nonexceptional
small prime fields until their product exceeds this bound, reconstruct by
CRT, and reduce modulo $2^p$. The required number of primes and all
intermediate bit lengths are polynomial in $s$, because $p$ and $\log N$
are polynomial in $s$. Expanding ring entries in the fixed module basis
requires only polynomially many coordinate products; the integer structure
constants reconstruct their ring product. No division by a nonunit in
$R_p$ occurs.

A rectangular product with dimensions $N_1,N_2,N_3$ can be split into
square blocks of side comparable to $\min N_j$. The bit work is bounded by
\[
 \poly(s+\mathcal L)
 N_1N_2N_3(\min N_j)^{-3/5}.
\]
For index logarithms $x,y,z$, its exponent is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mm-cost}
 x+y+z-\tfrac35\min(x,y,z).
\end{equation}
A fixed bilinear base algorithm can process its recursive products
sequentially and accumulate outputs, with quadratic field-element storage.
The rectangular and CRT wrappers require a polynomial factor times the
input/output array sizes. Those sizes are bounded by the work exponent
in~\eqref{eq:mm-cost}.

Table records have polynomial bit length. The required orderings can be
obtained by sorting and scanning records in polynomial overhead per record.
The argument does not assume free random access to integers with
exponentially many bits. The finite code uses ordinary exact multiplication
to test the tensor identity, not the theoretical fast backend.

\section{Building the four tables and paying for child calls}
\label{sec:local}

For $r\in\{4,8,12\}$, first cache every $Z_g$ on its incident pair
indices. There are $2(r-1)$ incident directed pairs, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy-ball} bounds the child-call count by
\[
 2^{(\alpha_r+o(1))K},\qquad
 \alpha_r=\frac{\sqrt{2(r-1)}}r.
\]
The fixed factor $r$ and all exact weight arrays are polynomial overhead.

Partition the groups into four equal, predetermined blocks. For any subset
$B$, its table $T_B$ is indexed by its boundary directed pairs and sums
the product of the cached factors over the internal pairs. If disjoint
sets of sizes $a,b$ are merged in a graph on $r$ groups, the three index
classes of the matrix product have cardinalities
\[
 2a(r-a-b),\qquad 2ab,\qquad 2b(r-a-b).
\]
If these numbers are $v_1,v_2,v_3$, the weighted entropy estimate and
\eqref{eq:mm-cost} give coefficient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:merge-cardinality}
 \frac1r\sqrt{v_1+v_2+v_3-\frac{21}{25}\min(v_1,v_2,v_3)}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the cost is the maximum of three linear forms with coefficients
$1,1,2/5$ on the index classes; apply the entropy ball to each.

For $r=8$, each initial block has size two. Its classes are $(12,2,12)$,
and its squared numerator is $608/25$. For $r=12$, first form pairs and
then triples. The size-three merge has classes $(18,4,36)$ and squared
numerator $1366/25$; smaller merges cost less. For $r=4$, the four input
blocks are the already cached singleton factors. Thus
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{608/25}}8 < \beta,
 \qquad \frac{\sqrt{1366/25}}{12}<\beta,
 \qquad \beta=\sqrt{\frac{61}{150}}.
\]
The new adaptive four-block theorem controls the remaining contraction
by $2^{(\beta+o(1))K}$ work. It also bounds the chosen pair tables and
matrix temporaries, since every input and output size is at most that
matching's merge-cost bound. The fixed initial block tables fit within
the same envelope.

\begin{proposition}[Paid local recurrence]\label{prop:recurrence}
Suppressing polynomial factors per call, the new cached engine satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 T_d\le 2^{(\alpha_{r_d}+o(1))K_d}T_{d+1}
             +2^{(\beta+o(1))K_d},
 \qquad S_{d,\mathrm{local}}\le2^{(\beta+o(1))K_d}.
\end{equation}
All recursive group entries are charged by the first term. The second
term uses cached group values and makes no uncharged child call.
\end{proposition}

The identities hold in any commutative ring: the regrouping changes only
the order of finite sums and products. Fast matrix multiplication is an
optional implementation of those same products. Taking minima over the
three pairings does not alter the value of the contraction.

\section{A complete cached schedule}
\label{sec:cached-schedule}

For sufficiently large $s$, put
\[
 \Lambda=\lambda-\ceil{\sqrt\lambda},\qquad
 b_*=\ceil{6\log_2\lambda},\qquad H=\ceil{s/\lambda}.
\]
Handle the finite smaller range by direct summation. Set
$u_d=\floor{f_d\Lambda}$ and
$a_d=\sum_{j=0}^d(u_j+b_*)$. Use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cached-schedule}
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 d&r_d&f_d&A_d=\sum_{j\le d}f_j&m_d\\\hline
0&8&1/60&1/60&1\\
1&12&1/90&1/36&1/4\\
2&8&1/180&1/30&1/24\\
3+k&4&(1/160)(5/8)^k&<1/20&(1/96)2^{-k}
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Here $m_d$ is the limiting wire-count multiplier; the bounds will show
$M_d\le(1+o(1))^d m_d s$ uniformly on active paths.

\subsection{Packing, depth, and integer floors}

At the root, $2A_0\log_2r_0+9/10=1$. The retreat from $\lambda$ to
$\Lambda$ gives a negative $\Theta(\sqrt\lambda)$ in its binary-log
packing exponent. Below the root, the dominant old-side exponents are
\[
 \frac9{10}+\frac{\log_2 12}{36}<1\quad(d=1),\qquad
 \frac9{10}+\frac3{30}=1\quad(d=2),\qquad
 \frac9{10}+2A_d\le1\quad(d\ge3).
\]
Equality at the limiting power is again accompanied by the negative
$\Theta(\sqrt\lambda)$ retreat. For a rational strict certificate at
depth one, $12^{100}<2^{359}$ implies
$\log_2 12<359/100$, leaving a margin of $1/3600$.

The fresh-side term has leading power $2A_d\log_2r_d+A_{d-1}$, and
rounding has leading power $(1+2\log_2r_d)A_d$. Both are at most $1/4$
throughout this schedule (with a smaller bound at depth one). Additive
buffers, polynomial logarithmic factors, and $s/M\le\lambda$ cost only
$O((\log\lambda)^2)$ in their binary logarithms. They are absorbed by
the strict power gaps or by the $\sqrt\lambda$ retreat. New packet
degrees are at most $s^{1/20+o(1)}$, below the maintained $s^{9/10}$ cap.

For a noncircular depth argument, provisionally consider
$O(\log\lambda)$ depths with tail length at most
$\log_{20/11}\lambda+O(1)$. At all those depths the smallest taper is
\[
 \lambda^{\eta+o(1)},\qquad
 \eta=1-\frac{\ln(8/5)}{\ln(20/11)}>0,
\]
since $5/8>11/20$. Hence all $u_d$ diverge uniformly and the floors are
relative $o(1)$ perturbations. The packing inequalities just proved are
uniform on this provisional range. Their child ratios are eventually at
most $11/20$, with sharper limits $1/4,1/6,1/4,1/2$. Therefore every active
path fits inside the provisional range. This closes the bootstrap.

Padding errors are exponentially small in $\sqrt\lambda$ or a fixed
negative power of $s$, after polynomial logarithmic factors. Their product
over $O(\log\lambda)$ levels is $1+o(1)$. The asserted $m_d$ bounds and
uniform geometric estimates follow.

\subsection{Precision and entry evaluation}

The inherited precision is $O(s\lambda^2 2^{a_{d-1}})$. For retained levels
$i<u_d$, the additive buffer gives
$D=2^{a_d-i}\ge2^{a_{d-1}+b_*+1}$. Thus
\[
 \frac{p_{\mathrm{inherited}}}{MD/u_d}
 =O(\lambda^4 2^{-b_*})=o(1).
\]
The remaining jet-loss term is also lower order. The sharp budget
$p_t\le(6+o(1))MD/u_d$ is therefore uniform. All precisions,
interpolation degrees, extension ranks, and tensor-description sizes are
polynomial in $s$.

Leaves cost $2^{O(s/\lambda)}$. Original entry routines cost
$2^{O(s^{9/10})}\poly(s+\mathcal L)$. Packet routines cost at most
$\exp(O(2^{a_d}\log s))=\exp(s^{1/20+o(1)})$ times polynomial factors.
Each is $2^{o(s/\sqrt\lambda)}$. Creation-time representatives are retained;
the routine for an old tensor is not replaced by a recursively expanding
computation at each precision increase.

\subsection{Unrolling the recurrence without losing child costs}

Put $\kappa=\pi\sqrt2/\ln2$ and $\gamma=\sqrt{2/5}$. On the scale
$s/\sqrt\lambda$, the four leading $K$ values are
\begin{align*}
 k_0&=\kappa\sqrt{60}=49.6495177683\ldots,\\
 k_1&=\kappa\sqrt{90}/4=15.2019980635\ldots,\\
 k_2&=\kappa\sqrt{180}/24=3.5831453061\ldots,\\
 k_{3+j}&=(\kappa\sqrt{160}/96)\gamma^j
             =0.8445554480\ldots\gamma^j.
\end{align*}
Unrolling~\eqref{eq:recurrence} takes the maximum of each local
aggregation cost plus the preceding cache costs. The four finite starting
candidates and the infinite cache limit are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule Candidate & Coefficient\\\midrule
$\beta k_0$ & $31.6617067862$\\
$\alpha_8k_0+\beta k_1$ & $32.9158138649$\\
$\alpha_8k_0+\alpha_{12}k_1+\beta k_2$ & $31.4483967553$\\
$\alpha_8k_0+\alpha_{12}k_1+\alpha_8k_2+\beta k_3$
 & $31.3778492206$\\
$\alpha_8k_0+\alpha_{12}k_1+\alpha_8k_2+
       \alpha_4 k_3/(1-\gamma)$ & $32.2464016431$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The tail candidates have the form
\[
 P+\frac{\alpha_4 k_3}{1-\gamma}
 +\left(\beta-\frac{\alpha_4}{1-\gamma}\right)k_3\gamma^j,
\]
so their maximum is an endpoint or their limit. The second displayed
candidate is the largest; exact rational interval certificates in the
code verify every strict comparison. It equals the stated $C_T$.

Local stored tables have coefficient at most $\beta k_d$.
Ancestor storage adds, rather than multiplies: child calls are processed
one at a time while the partially filled parent cache is retained. Since
$k_d$ is largest at the root, the space coefficient is $C_S=\beta k_0$.
Polynomial work accumulated over $O(\log\lambda)$ depths contributes
$O(\lambda\log\lambda)=o(s/\sqrt\lambda)$ to the time exponent. This
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:cached}.

Here the per-level multiplicative overhead is polynomial in $s$, not a
fresh power of the entire original description length. Immutable tensor
descriptions reference original entry routines, and packet routines have
size polynomial in $s$. A single fixed polynomial in $s+\mathcal L$ pays
for original input handling and an original entry evaluation. Factoring
it out of the recurrence avoids a spurious $\mathcal L^{O(\log\lambda)}$
factor. This is the same description convention as the imported interface.

\section{A new adaptive separator bound in polynomial space}
\label{sec:ps}

Caching is not needed for a second, independent improvement. Condition on
the directed indices crossing a split, contract its two sides separately,
multiply their values, and sum over the crossing assignment. This is exact
distributivity over finite sums. A depth-first implementation stores only
the current assignments, ring accumulators, and recursion stack.

\subsection{The four-cluster inequality}

For a four-vertex graph let $w_{ij}\ge0$ be the combined weight of both
directions and let $W=\sum w_{ij}$. For a perfect matching $\{e,f\}$,
the largest root-to-leaf conditioning cost is $W-\min(w_e,w_f)$.
For the three opposite-edge pairs write $a_i$ for the smaller weight and
$b_i$ for the larger. Choosing the matching with largest $a_i$ gives
\begin{align*}
 W-\max_i a_i
 &\le \tfrac23\sum_i a_i+\sum_i b_i\\
 &\le\sqrt{\tfrac{13}3}\left(\sum_e w_e^2\right)^{1/2}.
\end{align*}
The constant is sharp: set every $a_i=2$ and every $b_i=3$.

For four predetermined clusters each of size $b$, each combined cluster
pair contains $2b^2$ directed coordinates. Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the
squared norm of their six aggregated weights by $2b^2$ times the directed
cross-cluster squared norm. Internal coordinates are disjoint. If $g(r)$
bounds the squared norm of a complete root-to-leaf path functional on
$r$ groups, the adaptive construction satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:g-recurrence}
 g(4b)\le\frac{26}{3}b^2+g(b).
\end{equation}
With $g(1)=0$, this gives
$g(4^j)=(26/45)((4^j)^2-1)$. In particular
\[
 g(4)=26/3,\qquad g(8)=110/3,\qquad g(16)=442/3.
\]
For $r=8$, use four clusters of size two and $g(2)=2$.
Fixed balanced binary trees have the larger respective values
$10,42,170$.

The cost bound for the full adaptive tree follows by combining its
cross-cluster and within-cluster norm bounds on disjoint coordinates.
For the actual entropy vector, the local time and number of child calls
are at most
\[
 2^{(\sigma_r+o(1))K},\qquad \sigma_r=\sqrt{g(r)}/r.
\]
There are only constantly many cluster choices for fixed $r$. Select the
matching by exact products of list sizes. Every leaf factor evaluation
is an actual recursive child call, and is charged by the same path bound.

\subsection{An admissible schedule and its full coefficient}

Use the same $\Lambda,b_*,H$ conventions, now with
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
d&r_d&f_d&A_d&m_d\\\hline
0&16&1/80&1/80&1\\
1&16&1/80&1/40&1/8\\
2&8&1/120&1/30&1/64\\
3+k&4&(1/160)(5/8)^k&<1/20&(1/256)2^{-k}.
\end{array}
\]
The root symmetric gate and the subsequent old-side gates have limiting
exponent one, with the negative $\sqrt\lambda$ retreat. Fresh-side and
rounding exponents are at most $1/4$. All arities are powers of two;
there is no irrational packing-capacity comparison. The previous depth,
precision, floor, and entry-cost argument applies verbatim.

The streamed recurrence is multiplicative,
$T_d\le\poly(s)2^{(\sigma_{r_d}+o(1))K_d}T_{d+1}$, so its coefficients
sum. The resulting exact expression is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ps-constant}
 C_P=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\ln2}
 \left[
 \frac9{128}\sqrt{80\cdot\frac{442}3}
 +\frac1{512}\sqrt{120\cdot\frac{110}3}
 +\frac{\sqrt{160\cdot(26/3)}}{1024(1-\sqrt{2/5})}
 \right].
\end{equation}
This is $50.3937257005\ldots$. Replace $442/3,110/3,26/3$ by
$170,42,10$ in the same expression to obtain the matched prior coefficient
$54.1282952066\ldots$. This separates the gain from the new adaptive
inequality from any gain due merely to schedule selection.

All mode indices, weights, stack frames, and ring entries have polynomial
bit length. Both recursions have logarithmic depth and retain only a
polynomial number of such objects when streamed. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:ps}.

\section{Comparison with compatible prior constructions}
\label{sec:comparison}

The five newest archives already combine much of the older work. Comparing
only with the filed $76.909$ coefficient would substantially overstate
the new result. The strongest displayed cached certificate is the
contraction-tree packet's $(35.354588,34.274803)$ time--space pair; the
strongest displayed polynomial-space certificate is the adaptive packet's

A stronger cached prior composition is also available. Use the paid
singleton caches and recursive block-table construction from the tree
packet, but choose a $3,3,2$ partition of eight groups minimizing crossing
entropy as in the adaptive packet. Each directed edge is crossing with
probability $3/4$, so the final direct crossing sum has coefficient
\[
 \beta_{\mathrm{old},8}=\tfrac34\frac{\sqrt{56}}8.
\]
Building the three blocks has smaller coefficient
$\sqrt{766/25}/8$. At arities twelve and four retain the prior fixed
three-block matrix construction, with coefficients $\sqrt{12/25}$ and
$\sqrt{208/25}/4$, respectively. On our cached schedule the root
dominates, with coefficient
\[
 C_{\mathrm{old,composed}}=
 \tfrac34\frac{\sqrt{56}}8\,\kappa\sqrt{60}
 =34.8321534202\ldots .
\]
The root storage in this composition is
$(\sqrt{30}/8)\kappa\sqrt{60}=33.9927010637\ldots$.
The supporting schedule checker recomputes these values rather than using
the rounded text as a certificate. This composition is prior machinery,
not counted as a new lemma.

Our cached bound is strictly below both the displayed and this composed
time certificate, and uses a smaller space certificate. The analogous
matched polynomial-space comparison was derived above. The guarantee is
about the specified common interface. It does not numerically dominate
unspecified constants in the separate Claude manuscript, low-degree bounds
with stronger hypotheses, or unproved conditional order targets.

Running a finite portfolio of the new and inherited engines preserves
the best valid bound in each input regime. For exact algorithms, ordinary
round-robin simulation stops when the first finishes; its overhead is
constant for a fixed portfolio. This also preserves special-case benefits
whose assumptions differ from the general-degree normalization. We have
not proved that our schedules are optimal over all possible portfolios or
all future combinations of the underlying framework.

\section{Why the stronger logarithmic targets remain unresolved}
\label{sec:barriers}

\subsection{Precision peeling is locally useful but not a new leading saving}

The incoming frontier packet already implements computing a contribution
of certified valuation $V$ at residual precision $P-V$. It also explains
that this does not reduce the leading recursive bound. Our audit confirms
the reason: every active child creates a fresh Hermite loss of order
$MD/u$, while inherited precision is only
$O(\lambda^4 2^{-b_*})MD/u=o(MD/u)$. Reducing an already subordinate
quantity leaves the leading term unchanged.

The peeling interface retains the same graph and ports. It does not
correlate large mode weight with a smaller child wire count or lower
deformation degree. Such an additional correlation could matter, but it
has not been proved. Treating peeling alone as a second leading saving
would repeat a limitation already documented in the incoming work.

\subsection{A short index is not a bounded-degree tensor description}

The precision packet compresses mode indices to
$S_\beta=O(M/\sqrt u+r^2)$ rank bits. The compressed network still has
only $r$ genuine group tensors, of degree on the scale $S_\beta/r$.
This fails the required sublinear maximum-degree condition when $r$ is
fixed or only polylogarithmic. Reapplying the same theorem is not licensed.

The quantitative tradeoff already in that packet is
$u=O(\log M/\log r)$ for the first pass and $u'=O(\log r)$ for a second
pass. Their putative savings multiply back to
\[
 \frac M{\sqrt{\log M/\log r}}\frac1{\sqrt{\log r}}
 =\frac M{\sqrt{\log M}}.
\]
This is a barrier for that particular proof route, not a complexity lower
bound for SAT or for every representation of these tensors.

\subsection{The exterior-power route needs more than reduced degree}

The earlier asymptotic-analysis packet explicitly asks for a closed
recursive interface combining deformation degree $K(2L-1)$,
$O(M/D)$ modes, $M/8$ compressed-wire children, and admissible descriptions
and entry costs. If supplied, it would yield
$\poly(s+\mathcal L)2^{O(\sqrt{sK/\log s})}$, hence
$2^{O(n\sqrt{\log\log n/\log n})}$ for its intended reduction.

The local exterior-power degree statement does not supply the required
compressed mode count: the ordinary separation scales with the original
incidence universe. Antisymmetry restricts occupation but does not itself
eliminate square-root partition entropy. A generic transfer of low
incidence degree to rank-bit degree is also false: the singleton
coefficient table of the degree-one polynomial $z_{2^b}$ is AND on its
$b$ binary index bits, with Boolean degree $b$.

The sharp local Smith profiles in the newest kernel archive further limit
improvements obtained just by increasing Cauchy-mode valuation weights.
The present report gains by summing an existing exact mode graph more
efficiently. It does not claim a superlinear valuation slope or a new
compact tensor compiler.
