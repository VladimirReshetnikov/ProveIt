INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE ADAPTIVE FOUR-BLOCK RESULT

Scope
-----
This audit checks the new local four-block inequality, the proposed global
schedule's numerical and packing accounting, and a finite implementation of
the exact regrouping identity. It does not certify the imported OpenAI SAT
reductions, implement the asymptotic fast matrix-multiplication backend, or
establish a smaller logarithmic order for unrestricted SAT.

1. Local coefficient certificate
-------------------------------
The six linear forms for each K4 matching arise correctly from the two
rectangular merges and the three possible smallest matrix dimensions.
Choosing the best matching can be bounded by the mean of one maximizing
linear form per matching. The uniform mean has squared norm at most 244/75.
An elementary proof is in ../k4_uniform_certificate.tex. The independent
exact check of all 216 choices is ../verify_k4_uniform_certificate.py.
No numerical optimization is needed for the proof. A star weighting of 4/5
versus 2/3 attains equality for all three matchings, proving sharpness for
this local strategy and this rectangular cost model.

2. Combining directed pair entropies
-----------------------------------
For four equal blocks of b groups, each undirected block-pair logarithm
sums 2b^2 directed pair logarithms, and these six index sets are disjoint.
Cauchy-Schwarz yields ||w||_2 <= sqrt(2)*b*||h||_2. The imported generating
product estimate is uniform for normalized weight vectors v in [0,1]^{r(r-1)};
choosing v=h/||h||_2 therefore yields ||h||_2 <= (1+o(1))*K/r. The resulting
new local coefficient is sqrt(61/150). The zero vector needs no division.
Uniformity of the imported error estimate should be stated in the report:
an assertion only for fixed v independent of the input would not suffice.

3. Proposed complete schedule
-----------------------------
Let Lambda=lambda-ceil(sqrt(lambda)). The proposed arities are 8,12,8,
followed by 4,4,... . The taper allocations are Lambda/60, Lambda/90,
Lambda/180, followed by (Lambda/160)*(5/8)^k. Their total is Lambda/20.
Floors and additive O(log lambda) buffers must be included as in the proof.

With child size (2/r+o(1))*M, the leading coefficients of K(M,u) on the
s/sqrt(lambda) scale are
  K0 = 49.64951776832136
  K1 = 15.20199806345429
  K2 =  3.58314530608443
  K3 =  0.84455544796968,
and the tail ratio is sqrt(2/5).
Singleton caching coefficients are sqrt(2(r-1))/r.

The resulting time candidates are
  depth 0 local work:      31.66170678619399
  depth 1 local work:      32.91581386492430
  depth 2 local work:      31.44839675525458
  depth 3 local work:      31.37784922057879
  limiting paid-oracle sum:32.24640164313060.
Every later local candidate lies between the depth-3 value and that limit.
Thus depth 1 is the maximum in this schedule.

4. Packing and memory accounting
--------------------------------
The retained-side packing exponents reach their boundary at root
  9/10 + 6/60 = 1,
at depth 2
  9/10 + 3/30 = 1,
and in the limiting r=4 tail
  9/10 + 2/20 = 1.
Lambda=lambda-ceil(sqrt(lambda)) supplies a 2^{-c sqrt(lambda)} margin,
which absorbs logarithmic active-size, padding, and buffer factors.
At depth 1 the retained-side exponent is
  9/10 + log2(12)/36 < 1.
Fresh-side and packet-rounding exponents are bounded by 1/4+o(1), safely
below one. The tail's coarse shrink bound may be taken as 3/5 eventually;
with leaf cutoff s/lambda this still gives a positive power of lambda for
the smallest active taper, since 1+ln(5/8)/ln(5/3)>0.

The space coefficient 31.66170678619399 is justified using the CHOSEN
adaptive matching: its boundary exponent C is <= its selected merge cost,
which is <= sqrt(61/150)*K. A fixed worst-case half-block boundary count
would instead give the weaker coefficient 35.10751069662202 at the root.
Initial four-block tables have coefficient sqrt(3/8), below sqrt(61/150),
as do the singleton caches. Matrix input/output arrays and ordinary blocked
fast-multiplication workspace fit within the selected merge cost. Ancestor
tables add their storage requirements; their sizes do not multiply.

5. Exact finite implementation
-----------------------------
adaptive_four.py caches each singleton oracle entry once, builds four block
tables using binary merges, compares all three matching cost models using
exact integer fifth powers, performs the two selected merges, and finishes
with an inner product. It uses only ring addition and multiplication in
factor values. A domain index belongs to both endpoint factors even though
it is labelled by an ordered pair. Missing domains have cardinality one.

The implementation intentionally uses naive cubic matrix multiplication.
It illustrates exactness and oracle accounting, not the fast-backend time
bound. The matching selector nevertheless compares the proved 12/5 cost
model exactly, without floating-point logarithms.

verify_adaptive_four.py passed 47 fixtures against independent full-assignment
summation, over Z/64Z and (Z/64Z)[epsilon]/(epsilon^2), for r=4,8,12. They
include active opposite-direction indices, a fully active directed K4,
cardinalities 1,2,3,4, all three matching choices at every arity, actual
nilpotents and zero divisors, and exact paid-oracle counts. The suite covers
17,174 global assignments; 36 fixture results are nonzero. No timing or
production SAT performance claim follows from these finite checks.
