## Compatibility and the actual combined frontier

### Shared interface

The generic normalized inputs have at most s Boolean wires, maximum degree <= ceil(s^{9/10}), polynomially described tensors over (Z/2^{P0}Z)[V]/(V^ell), 1<=P0,ell<=s, and entry access in poly(s+description) exp(O(s^{9/10})) time and polynomial space. The same unramified extension and immutable creation-precision representatives are retained. New entry routines do not recursively compose old tensor oracles: old tensors remain separate immutable vertices. The root-edge normalization and fixed entry-oracle constants must be held fixed in any coefficient comparison.

### Compatible ingredients, not independent multipliers

Near-lossless padding gives c_i <= (2+o(1)) M/D_i and children <= (2+o(1)) M/r; exact asymmetric packet accounting further removes the same-symbol orientation families, giving coefficient 2(1-1/r). Additive gaps make inherited precision negligible without spending an entire second taper block. Exact Hermite accounting still supplies P <= (6+o(1)) MD/u. The joint-Cauchy basis can replace slope three by slope four without multiplying mode-family count or enlarging source-group dependence. These modifications act at distinct interfaces and can compose, subject to preserving creation precision and exactly the full-half packet geometry proved by the joint packet.

The asymmetric packet itself already states the continuous fixed-r root/tail schedule optimization. For degree exponent gamma, root budget A0=(1-gamma)/(2 log_2 r), below-root cumulative budget 2A0, and c=2/r, the path functional is

    A0^{-1/2} [1 + c/(1-c^{2/3})^{3/2}],

provided the fresh-side and rounding constraints hold. It displays the r16, gamma=9/10 boundary coefficient 76.4327736858 (slope three), versus its feasible denominator-81 example 76.9089949516. Combining slope four multiplies a matched leading mode-based coefficient by sqrt(3)/2, but this is a combination of supplied mechanisms, not novelty. Optimizing the already printed fixed-r family also belongs to the combined baseline. A new contribution must improve that baseline, implement a genuinely new setup/extraction optimization, or prove a new asymptotic interface.

The exponential-space Strassen route is a distinct resource regime and must not be silently compared as if polynomial space. Its all-arrivals compatibility and optimized constants require an additional cache/recursion audit; the polynomial-space results alone do not dominate it on time.

### Sparse versus general-density degree

The low-degree coefficient 31.064132063 applies only for Delta<=ceil(s^{1/12}); bounded-degree direct clause networks satisfy it eventually. The general sparse-parity incidence reduction in the unchanged density packet uses J=ceil(n^{1/4}) groups, max degree O(n^{3/4} S sqrt(log n)) = s^{3/4+o(1)}, and s=n(1+ceil log(S+1)). It does not meet the low-degree premise. Its degree-array enumeration costs 2^{O(J^2 log n)}; simply increasing J to n^alpha with alpha<1/2 can lower degree exponent toward (but not below) 1/2 while preserving a lower-order enumeration exponent, but that is not the supplied s^{1/12} theorem. Numerical wire coefficients cannot be substituted directly as coefficients of n without tracking the full encoding wire constant and outer enumeration costs.

## Mathematical checks performed on the new packing proofs

* Balanced two-stage proof: greedy whole-row labeling gives R_a<=M/Q+Delta. Independent whole-column labels produce weighted Bernoulli cell loads. With rational z=1+1/(Delta lambda^2), the report's guard M>=4Q^2 Delta lambda^4 makes the sum of cell exponential moments <1. Its positive polynomial expansion after lambda=x+4 correctly certifies the stated gap. Conditional potentials are exact rational products; polynomial bit lengths and candidate count are explicitly justified. Parallel edges are bundled by vertex weights, not falsely treated as independent edges.
* Matching proof: greedy proper edge coloring uses at most 2Delta colors; indicators are independent inside each matching. Potential sum_{gamma,k} t^{-H_k} product_e(1+(t-1)p_e) is exact under partial labels. H_k=ceil((1+1/lambda)m_k/Q^2+2lambda tau) with tau=ceil log_2(8cQ^2) gives initial potential <=1/8. Whole-vertex conditional expectation fixes labels without increasing it. Common denominator bounds are polynomial; no numerical oracle is hidden.
* High-moment proof: Holder across matching colors controls the moment generating function despite inter-matching dependence; even 2t moment and conditional expectation yield the stated max-load bound. Ordered edge-tuple expansion correctly has denominator dividing R^{4t}, including repeated endpoints. Growing t=ceil log_2 s gives 2^{O(log^2 s)} setup, paid as lower order in the final exponent. Fixed moment t yields polynomial setup but increases the coefficient by sqrt((t+1)/t). The polynomial matching/two-stage alternatives already attain the same leading coefficient without this setup tradeoff.
* All three retain whole vertex labels and orientation packets, common padded class sizes, real-port grouping, and shrinking child edge counts. Adaptive schedules separately prove noncircular depth and taper-growth guards; their small-size fallbacks are explicit and not evidence of a practical threshold.

The Cauchy and asymmetric schedule interfaces are also being independently audited in companion round-three notes; this document does not turn their local proofs into an independently certified end-to-end solver.

## Practical-code novelty screen

The seven new packets supply exact Python component prototypes, local finite-test records, schedule checkers, and report sources. These are not full SAT solvers. Existing programs were read, not executed; recorded results are evidence of the packet authors' tests, not this review replaying them.

In particular, the packet-refinement implementation already caches row-power sums and suffix products:

    code/packet_packing.py, lines 125-163:
    score_b = sum_a suffix[a][v+1]
              * (row_sum[a] - z^load[a,b] + z^(load[a,b]+w[a,v])).

This uses O(n_columns Q^2) rational score arithmetic, not the O(n_columns Q^3) literal recomputation in the asymmetric packet's `code/balanced_packing.py` lines 75-85. Thus row-sum factoring alone is already present in the combined practical frontier. A sparse active-row/common-base implementation can still improve the dense O(n_columns Q^2) scan to O(Q sum_v |{a:w[a,v]!=0}|), with suitable initialization/state accounting, but should compare against this stronger existing implementation rather than only the asymmetric triple loop. The balanced-packing prototype recomputes the whole conditional potential; matching and high-moment programs implement different potentials and do not already provide this sparse factoring.

A full-text search of all seven top-level TeX reports found no root-of-unity/Fourier coefficient-extraction method. Their mentions of streamed extraction refer to the inherited finite-difference routine. The word homogeneous in the asymmetric report describes orientation packets, not a homogeneous Fourier extraction formula. Joint Cauchy supplies instead group caching and division-free Strassen multiplication. Novel extraction claims still need correct modular extension arithmetic and creation-precision accounting, but are not duplicates of the inspected reports.
