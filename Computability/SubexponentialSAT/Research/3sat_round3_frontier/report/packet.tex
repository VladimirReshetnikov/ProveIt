The following improvement is independent of the packing, taper and Cauchy-mode
certificates. It replaces only the packet-entry coefficient extraction, not the
mode sum or the SAT solver. The latest inspected reports retain the earlier
streamed forward-difference routine. We provide a same-interface alternative,
including the necessary characteristic-two precision accounting, an independent
implementation, and comparisons against a forward-grid implementation already
optimized by Gray traversal. The underlying polarization, Glynn, and root-filter
ideas are classical; no priority is claimed for these identities. For context,
see Glynn's \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2010.01.010}{\emph{The permanent of a
square matrix}} (2010) and Aaronson--Hance's
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.0025}{generalized Glynn treatment} (2012).

\subsection{Generic half-grid extraction with correct guard precision}
Homogenize a packet product of $R$ affine forms by adding a constant coordinate
of target exponent $R-|\beta|$; return zero if $|\beta|>R$. After dropping
zero-exponent coordinates, write
\[
 H(Z)=\prod_{r=1}^{R}\sum_{j=1}^{t}a_{rj}Z_j,
 \qquad \sum_{j=1}^t\beta_j=R,
 \qquad G=\prod_j(\beta_j+1),\quad B=\prod_j\beta_j!.
\]
The empty product $R=0$ is handled directly as the constant one. All $\beta_j$ are now positive. The coefficient ring is the source's finite free quotient
\[ A_p=(\mathbb Z/2^p\mathbb Z)[U,V]/(f(U),V^\ell), \]
with $f$ monic;
the argument allows nilpotents and does not require $A_p$ to be a field.

\begin{proposition}[Paired centered differences]
For $R>0$, let $\mathcal H$ contain one representative of each pair
$\{\gamma,\beta-\gamma\}$ in $0\le\gamma\le\beta$, omitting the central point
when every $\beta_j$ is even. Then
\[
 2^{R-1}B[Z^\beta]H
 =\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal H}
 (-1)^{R-|\gamma|}\prod_j\binom{\beta_j}{\gamma_j}
 H(2\gamma-\beta).                                      \tag{P1}
\]
There are exactly $\lfloor G/2\rfloor$ evaluations. If $v=v_2(B)$, the
coefficient modulo $2^p$ is recovered exactly by evaluating the right side at
precision $p+R-1+v$, dividing canonical integer coordinates by $2^{R-1+v}$,
and multiplying by $(B/2^v)^{-1}\bmod2^p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the difference of step two and order $\beta_j$ in each variable, starting
at $-\beta_j$. A monomial of total degree $R$ vanishes if any exponent is below
$\beta_j$; the only survivor is $Z^\beta$, with multiplier $2^RB$.
Homogeneity gives $H(-(2\gamma-\beta))=(-1)^RH(2\gamma-\beta)$, while the
binomial sign changes by the same parity. Paired summands are therefore equal.
The unpaired central value is $H(0)=0$. Dividing the integral identity by two
proves (P1). Its validity in the free integral quotient proves the stated
coordinatewise divisibility. Keeping the additional $R-1+v$ bits, rather than
illegally dividing a residue at precision $p$, recovers the answer.
\end{proof}

The representative set is a disjoint union of at most $t$ boxes: the first
noncentral coordinate is below its midpoint, earlier coordinates are central,
and later coordinates are unrestricted. Reflected mixed-radix Gray traversal
changes one coordinate by one at a time. Keeping the $R$ current row sums updates
them in $O(R)$ ring operations per evaluation, instead of recomputing $t$
terms per row. No grid-indexed table is retained. The input and preprocessing
are polynomial-sized, and auxiliary streaming state is polynomial in
$R,t,p,\deg f,\ell$. The extra precision is at most $2R-1$ bits. Both this
routine and the strong forward-Gray baseline use $O(RG)$ ring operations up to
polynomial setup; the half-grid saves evaluations, not a new exponential order.
The additional bit width means a wall-clock improvement is not a universal
consequence of the evaluation count.

\subsection{A cube-root profile using only unit division}
For a better special profile, suppose $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$ in $A_p$.
For positive target exponents $\beta_j\in\{1,2\}$, choose an index $k$ of minimum
exponent and put $Z_k=1$. Then
\[
 [Z^\beta]H
 =3^{-(t-1)}
 \sum_{\gamma\in\{0,1,2\}^{t-1}}
 \omega^{-\sum_{j\ne k}\beta_j\gamma_j}
 H\bigl(Z_k=1,\ Z_j=\omega^{\gamma_j}\ (j\ne k)\bigr).       \tag{P2}
\]
All arithmetic is at precision $p$: $3$ is a unit, so no factorial or
power-of-two division occurs. To prove (P2), apply character orthogonality to a
monomial $Z^\alpha$. A survivor has
$\alpha_j=\beta_j+3m_j$ with $m_j\ge0$ for $j\ne k$. Homogeneity forces
$\alpha_k=\beta_k-3\sum_{j\ne k}m_j$. Since $\beta_k\le2$, nonnegativity
implies every $m_j=0$. Thus precisely the requested coefficient survives.

For $\beta=(2,\ldots,2)$, (P2) takes $3^{t-1}=G/3$ evaluations, versus
$(G-1)/2$ centered evaluations and $G$ forward evaluations, with \emph{zero}
extra precision bits. For many exponents one, the ternary grid may instead be
larger; the implementation dispatches by the proven counts and never assumes
this special-profile benefit is universal. A single surviving coordinate is
handled directly by multiplying its row coefficients.

This does not require enlarging the source's coefficient ring. Its residue
field has degree $e=4\lceil\log_2s\rceil$, so it contains $\mathbb F_4$.
A nontrivial root of $X^2+X+1$ in that subfield lifts uniquely to every precision,
since its derivative $2X+1$ is a unit. If no root is supplied, exhaustive residue
search uses at most $2^e<16s^4$ candidates, hence polynomial time in $s$;
this is a conservative construction bound, not a claimed practical search.
Subsequent Hensel lifting is polynomial in the ring dimension and precision.
The supplied implementation accepts and verifies a root, and supplies a
bit-by-bit Hensel lift from a residue root. Benchmarks state explicitly whether
that lift is timed; they do not hide a residue-field search.

\textbf{Creation precision.} For both methods the row coefficients are first
canonically represented at the tensor's immutable creation precision $p$.
Guard bits in (P1) are internal computation only. Return canonical coordinates
modulo $2^p$, multiply the prescribed source-mode powers as before, and retain
that creation-time representative in later recursive calls. The root filter
works in the existing ring and changes no recursive tensor identity.

\subsection{Verification and honest same-operation measurements}
The independent code executes no imported report program. A direct coefficient
multiplication oracle checked 6,737 cases against three kernels, giving 20,211
exact equalities. Cases include exhaustive small binary matrices, noncanonical
integer inputs, free monic quotients, nilpotents, and mode-weighted synthetic
factors. Additional checks cover 180 cube-root cases, 32 degree-four Hensel
fixtures, 212 adaptive-dispatch cases, 710 antipodal partitions and 1,014 Gray
boxes. The same suite passes normally and under \texttt{python -O}. Negative
controls detect insufficient guard precision, an invalid character root and
changing creation precision. The exponential-table direct oracle is a
small-case verifier, not the streaming algorithm.

Measurements use Python 3.12.14 on an AMD EPYC 9V74 shared Linux executor,
one untimed warmup and the median of three timed calls, with alternating method
order. Generation of synthetic inputs is excluded; canonicalization,
preprocessing, extraction and final division are included. Timings are not
isolated-CPU measurements and have no asserted statistical significance.
The baseline already performs Gray row-sum updates; it is not a claim of a
best possible permanent implementation. Its zero-origin evaluation could
trivially be skipped, saving one out of $G$ points.

The cached-root cube routine is $2.71$--$3.00$ times faster than forward Gray and
$1.47$--$1.67$ times faster than the centered routine in these four fixtures.
The separate ``Cube + lift'' timings charge fresh Hensel lifting on every call;
residue roots are supplied in both cases. The fact that some lift-inclusive
medians are smaller illustrates executor/timing noise, not negative overhead.
A deliberately adverse eight-ones profile in the degree-two ring used 2,187
cubic evaluations versus 128 centered evaluations: the cubic routine took
0.07935 s versus 0.00416 s, about 19.1 times slower. The adaptive routine
correctly selected the half-grid. This negative case is why (P2) is selective,
not a universal replacement.

\textbf{Scope of the result.} These are measured improvements to a genuine,
exact packet-entry operation, with independently checked formulas and no
increase to the recursion's space class. They are not full-SAT timings, do not
show the frequency of favorable profiles in real SAT contractions, and do not
improve the $n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ global logarithmic order. In the current
asymptotic schedule packet-entry work is already lower order. A production
integration should benchmark the dispatch on actual generated packets rather
than extrapolate these synthetic speed ratios to a solver.
