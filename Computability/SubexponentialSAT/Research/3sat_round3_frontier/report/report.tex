\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{The combined normalized polynomial-space certificate improves, but the logarithmic order does not.}
After reviewing the canonical project and eight new incoming archives, we combine the already supplied asymmetric balancing and integral slope-four Cauchy modes, then improve the resulting schedule. A depth-varying grouping alphabet gives
\[
 T(s,\mathcal L)\le(s+\mathcal L)^{O(1)}
 2^{(63.75735555\ldots+o(1))s/\sqrt{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}}
\]
with polynomial space, below the $66.12047546\ldots$ retuned two-stage comparison from the compatible supplied ingredients. Thus coefficient $64$ is valid eventually on the shared wire-normalized interface. An optional subexponential-space cache gives eventual coefficients $38$ for time and $34$ for space. A stronger low-degree assumption gives polynomial-space coefficient $24$, or cached time $14.3$ and space $12.4$. We also provide exact component improvements: sparse incremental conditional-expectation scores, antipodal centered packet extraction and a favorable-profile cube-root filter, with independent correctness checks and explicitly scoped local measurements. These are not a complete SAT implementation, a proof-assistant certificate, a numerical coefficient directly on the number of SAT variables, or a smaller unrestricted logarithmic-order bound. All SAT consequences retain the source manuscript's exact algebraic and reduction dependencies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Results and scope}
Let $n$ be the number of declared variables, $m$ the number of input clauses and $k=3$ the fixed clause width. The source manuscript's general-density exponent is
$O(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})$, with $O_D(n/\sqrt{\log n})$ for $m\le Dn$ and fixed $D$ \cite{openai,reduction}.
The supplied density refinements give the uniform envelope
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 \poly_k(n,m)2^{O_k\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
 \min\{\log(2+m/n),\log\log n\}\right)}.
\end{equation}
The new results preserve this order. In particular, they do not establish
$o(n\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n})$ for unrestricted 3-SAT.

The numerical certificates below use the same normalized contraction size $s$: a bipartite Boolean tensor network with at most $s$ wires, polynomially many vertices, maximum degree at most $\ceil{s^{9/10}}$, and total description length $\mathcal L$. Parallel edges have separate ports. Entries lie in
\[
 (\mathbb Z/2^{P_0}\mathbb Z)[V]/(V^\ell),\qquad 1\le P_0,\ell\le s.
\]
Each original entry can be evaluated in $(s+\mathcal L)^b2^{As^{9/10}}$ time and $(s+\mathcal L)^b$ space for fixed $A,b$. All arithmetic is exact. Fixed constant multiples in the precision bounds do not change the leading results. The reduction has $s=O_k(n\log\log n)$ in general; its hidden constants preclude copying the coefficient $64$ onto $n\log\log n$.

\textbf{What is new here.} The main mathematical step is a sequence of different alphabets at successive depths, exploiting the evolving asymmetric degrees rather than paying for one root alphabet and one fixed tail alphabet. The practical steps refine two existing exact subroutines, not the contraction identities themselves. The sparse score update is compared with the already supplied row-sum-cached algorithm, not just the slower literal triple sum. The centered extraction identity is an application of classical polarization; its packet adaptation and exact 2-adic precision accounting are the contribution here.

\textbf{What is inherited.} Near-unit balanced packing, thin additive precision gaps, sharp Hermite taper loss, scalar parity lifts, density reductions, slope-four Cauchy modes, and cached block contraction were all in the supplied proposals. We verify compatibility rather than relabeling them as new. ``Derived'' throughout means an ordinary mathematical derivation relative to the unchanged source deformation, integrality, recovery and recursive-factorization identities. No upstream program or installer was run, no upstream repository was modified, and no complete SAT solver or proof-assistant build was executed.

\section{What remains open}\label{sec:open}
None of these results supplies the structured summation or compact factorization required by the earlier stronger-order targets. The precision proposal's $n\log\log n/\log n$ exponent requires a recursively valid summation algorithm for genuine high-arity group tensors. Small rank alphabets alone do not provide bounded-degree factors. The scalar parity proposal's $n(\log\log n)^{3/2}/\log n$ target requires its unsupplied compact-factorization interface, including exact invalid-index zeros and immutable creation precision. The prior degree-kernel route requires both a reduced global deformation degree and a mode-family count measured in the compressed wire universe, closed under every recursive child.

These obligations cannot be discharged by lowering a leaf threshold, quoting small-instance timing results, or splitting an arbitrary high-arity tensor into equality nodes. Nor is a non-polynomial cache a polynomial-space result. The alternate manuscript's graded crossing construction is a distinct route with the same fixed-width logarithmic order; its claimed unpublished formalization is not independently certified here. The present coefficient improvements are genuine upper-bound improvements on the stated normalized interface, not lower bounds on other possible schedules or algorithms.
