AFFINE-PLANE FOUR-PLAN SUPPORT BOUND: INDEPENDENT ANALYTIC PROOF

Setup. Label nine groups by the points of the affine plane F_3^2. Its four
directions give four parallel classes, each a partition into three lines of
three points. A plan first contracts inside the three lines by any binary
tree, then contracts the resulting triangle. Within a three-point line the
two binary merges have tripartitions of sizes(1,1,7) and(1,2,6). The final
triangle has tripartition(3,3,3). Internal ordering/singleton choice is
arbitrary and may differ across lines or directions.

For any operation tripartition(A,B,C), rectangular multiplication with
exponent12/5 gives three linear forms in the unordered pair entropies.
Multiply these forms by5. Each support vector v has coordinate0 on an edge
internal to one part, coordinate2 on the chosen discounted cross-class,
and coordinate5 on the other two cross-classes. Edges are unordered pairs
of the nine points. Denote operation types by:
  A: sizes(1,1,7), with the two singled points on one affine line;
  B: sizes(1,2,6), with the first two parts forming one affine line;
  C: the final three parallel affine lines.

Claim1. Every such vector has squared norm at most486.

For parts of sizes a,b,c, the cross-class sizes are ab,ac,bc. Discounting
one cross-class replaces squared coefficient25 by4, a decrease21 per edge.
Thus the largest squared norm is25(ab+ac+bc)-21 min(ab,ac,bc).
The three type maxima are:
  A:25(1+7+7)-21*1=354;
  B:25(2+6+12)-21*2=458;
  C:25(9+9+9)-21*9=486.

Claim2. If v,w belong to plans of distinct directions, then v.w<=288.

Here is a counting proof. Let S(v) be the set of coordinates on which v is
nonzero, and D(v) the discounted subset on which v equals2. Put
  c=|S(v) intersection S(w)|,
  a=|D(v) intersection S(w)|,
  b=|D(w) intersection S(v)|,
  t=|D(v) intersection D(w)|.
Then exactly
  v.w=25c-15(a+b)+9t,       t<=min(a,b).
The elementary function -15(a+b)+9 min(a,b) decreases when either a or b
increases. We can therefore use lower bounds on a,b in the cases below.

Two lines from distinct directions intersect in exactly one point. Each
line contains one point from each line of the other parallel class.

AA. An A support consists of every edge incident to a two-point subset
P of one line. If the corresponding two-point subset Q of a transverse
line is disjoint from P, the common edges are the four P-to-Q edges.
If P,Q intersect at x, the common edges are the eight edges incident to x,
plus the edge joining the two other selected points. Thus c<=9 and
v.w<=25*9=225.

AB. Ignore the one internal edge omitted from a B support; this can only
increase the common-support count. Intersect the incident-edge sets of a
two-point subset P of one line and a transverse three-point line L.
If disjoint there are six common edges. If they meet at x there are eight
x-edges and two edges from P\{x} to L\{x}. Thus c<=10 and v.w<=250.

AC. Let P be the pair underlying the A support. Its endpoints are in
different lines of the C parallel class. Each endpoint has six neighbors
in other C lines, and their mutual edge is counted twice. Thus c=11 and
v.w<=275.

BB. Let L,M be the transverse lines underlying the two B operations,
x=L intersection M, and let s in L, t in M be their singled vertices.
The two full incident-line edge sets have12 common edges: eight x-edges
and four edges from L\{x} to M\{x}. The omitted internal edge of L removes
one common edge exactly when s!=x; the omitted internal edge of M removes
one common edge exactly when t!=x. These removed edges are distinct.
Therefore c=12-1_{s!=x}-1_{t!=x}. Unless s=t=x, c<=11 and v.w<=275.
If s=t=x, c=12. For the L operation, its three possible discounted classes
meet the common support in respectively2,6,4 edges: x-to-(L\{x}),
x-to-complement(L), and(L\{x})-to-complement(L). The same holds for M.
Hence a,b>=2 and
  v.w<=300-15(2+2)+9*2=258<275.
In all BB cases v.w<=275.

BC. Let L be the line underlying the B operation. It meets each line of
the C parallel class once. The C-crossing edges incident to L number15:
three vertices each have six C-crossing neighbors, with the three internal
L edges counted twice. The B support omits one of those three internal
edges, giving c=14.
The C discounted class joins two of its parallel lines. Before the one
B omission, exactly five of its edges have an endpoint in L:3+3-1=5.
Thus its intersection with the B support has at least4 edges.
For B, its three discounted classes have respectively2,4,8 C-crossing
edges: the two singleton-to-pair internal L edges; the four cross-line
singleton-to-complement edges; or the eight pair-to-complement edges.
Consequently a,b can be ordered so that a>=4,b>=2, and
  v.w<=350-15(4+2)+9*2=278.

CC. Each parallel class has nine internal edges, and the two internal-edge
sets are disjoint, since two distinct points determine a unique direction.
Therefore c=36-9-9=18. A discounted class has nine edges, of which exactly
three lie within the lines of the other parallel class, one in each line.
Hence a=b=6. Choose the two lines defining each discounted class. Their
four intersections form a2-by-2 grid, and exactly its two diagonals cross
both pairs of lines, so t=2. Therefore
  v.w=25*18-15*(6+6)+9*2=288.
This equality holds for every choice of discounted classes.

The resulting valid pairwise upper-bound table is
         A    B    C
  A     225  250  275
  B     250  275  278
  C     275  278  288.
The table is deliberately an analytic upper certificate; some entries are
not the sharp finite maximum. In particular every cross-direction dot
product is at most288.

Claim3. For arbitrary supports v_1,...,v_4, one from each direction,
  ||v_1+...+v_4||_2^2 <=4*486+2*6*288=5400.
This follows by expanding the squared norm and using Claims1-2. Equality
is attained whenever all four supports are final-triangle supports, since
then every norm is486 and every pairwise dot product is288.

Consequence for the inherited exact-pair entropy interface.
Let g_{xy}=h_{xy}+h_{yx}, where h_{xy} is the logarithm of the exact mode
list cardinality on directed pair(x,y). For each direction j let
  F_j(g)=(1/5) max_{v in V_j} v.g.
Choose the least-cost of the four plans. Then
  min_j F_j(g) <=(1/20) max_{v_j in V_j}(sum_j v_j).g.
For each fixed support tuple, assign both directed orientations the weight
z_{xy}=(sum_j v_j)_{unordered{x,y}}/20. The squared directed norm is at
most(2/400)*5400=27. The inherited weighted entropy inequality is
  sum z_{xy} h_{xy} <=(||z||_2/9+o(1))*K(M,u),
where K(M,u)=(pi*sqrt2/ln2) M/sqrt(u).
Thus the selected plan's nonrecursive work and local storage have exponent
at most
  (1/sqrt3+o(1))*K(M,u).
The finite family ensures uniformity of the lower-order term. Every cached
singleton entry still requires a paid child contraction, with coefficient
alpha_9=sqrt(2*(9-1))/9=4/9. Therefore the fully paid local recurrence is
  T_d <=poly(s)[2^{(4/9+o(1))K_d} T_{d+1}
                  +2^{(1/sqrt3+o(1))K_d}].
Local table space is bounded by the selected operation cost exactly as in
the imported table method. Ancestor table sizes add along a recursion path.

Independent finite confirmation (supplementing, not replacing, the proof).
For the specific four naturally ordered parallel-class plans generated in

four-tuples. A separately reconstructed implementation found maximum5400,
