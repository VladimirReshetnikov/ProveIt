\section{Verified balanced source certificates}\label{sec:balanced}

\subsection{Removing a representation cost}

The ordinary source certificate is a list of checkpoint entries
\((P_i,Q_i,j_i,k_i)\). The paths \(P_i,Q_i\) name corresponding source sites;
\(j_i,k_i\) name the paired continuations of a decrement test. The existing
checker retrieves a continuation with
\code{nth\_error C (N.to\_nat j)}. A single access can traverse a linear
prefix of \(C\), even when its index is already represented in binary.
Repeated accesses create a quadratic worst-case indexing component.

The program semantics do not require a list. Nor does the soundness proof
need to trust an external random-access implementation. We replace storage
by a tree and prove equality with the original list lookup. This lets the
existing soundness theorem remain the specification. The incoming ordinary
checker and mirror theorem provide the semantic foundation \cite{incoming}.

\begin{definition}[Certificate tree]
A certificate tree is empty, a leaf containing one checkpoint entry, or a
node \(\operatorname{Node}(c,L,R)\) with binary natural cut \(c\).
Its flattening is the list of leaves in left-to-right order. Validity means
that every node's cut equals the number of leaves in its left subtree,
and that both subtrees are valid.
\end{definition}

Lookup at \(j\) follows \(L\) if \(j<c\), otherwise \(R\) at \(j-c\).
A leaf succeeds only for index zero; an empty tree returns no entry. Binary
\code{N} values are used for cuts and indices throughout lookup. In
particular, invalid indices retain the original failure behavior.

\begin{theorem}[Lookup refinement]\label{thm:lookup}
For every valid certificate tree \(T\) and every \(j\in\N\),
\[
 \operatorname{lookup}(T,j)=
 \operatorname{nth\_error}(\operatorname{flatten}(T),\operatorname{toNat}(j)).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on \(T\). Empty and singleton lists give the two base cases,
including out-of-range failure. At a node, validity identifies the cut with
the left list length. If \(j<c\), the list-append lookup law selects that
left list and the induction hypothesis applies. If \(j\ge c\), the other
append lookup law selects the right list at the subtracted index. The
binary-to-natural subtraction identity supplies exactly the original index.
No assumption that \(j\) is in range is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Checker refinement]
For valid \(T\) and arbitrary source decoder trees \(t_1,t_2\),
\[
 \operatorname{checkTree}(t_1,t_2,T)=
 \operatorname{check}(t_1,t_2,\operatorname{flatten}(T)).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Transport Theorem~\ref{thm:lookup} through the existing continuation,
segment, and entry checks. The tree-wide Boolean fold equals a list-wide
\code{forallb} over its flattening by structural induction. Combining these
identities gives the result, including the initial continuation check.
The segment interpreter and all register effects are unchanged.
\end{proof}

Consequently a true tree-validity check and a true forward certificate imply
the same halting equivalence as the original ordinary bisimulation theorem.
The incoming one-sided symmetry theorem supplies the reverse direction;
no second expensive mirrored certificate evaluation is introduced.

\subsection{Exact K215 theorem}

\code{Cert215Tree.v} constructs a concrete tree from \code{Plain215}.
The kernel checks its validity, exact flattening identity, 2,240 entries,
and height 13, counting the leaf. A general structural lemma bounds lookup
depth by tree height; the concrete bound therefore holds for every binary
index, including invalid indices.

\begin{theorem}[Compiled exact source equivalence]
For every natural-valued register file \(R\),
\[
 \operatorname{rm\_halts}(p_{215},([],R))
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{rm\_halts}(\mathrm{dagPerm},([],R)).
\]
\end{theorem}

The compiled name is \code{Source215Balanced.p215\_dagPerm\_balanced}.
It uses the exact current programs, not models with the same leaf counts.
The byte bindings for \code{Prog215.v}, \code{DagPerm.v}, and the original
offset certificate are recorded in the package. The new generic module has
194 lines, its exact tree wrapper 23, and the source theorem 21. These are
reusable additions; they are not advertised as a reduction in total source
lines. Their purpose is a simpler certificate representation with a verified
implementation boundary.

The certificate construction itself need not be trusted. Even if a generator
or the fuel-based builder produced a wrong tree, the exact flattening,
validity, and forward-check theorems would fail to compile. This is a useful
division of responsibility: the generator searches or compresses; the kernel
checks the resulting finite object against an explicit specification.

\subsection{Measured work and build-time limits}

The exact certificate contains 2,239 decrement checkpoints and one halt.
Its two branch accesses per decrement, plus the initial access, give 4,479
lookups. The independent census gives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}\toprule
Quantity & List representation & Balanced representation\\\midrule
Traversal nodes visited & 5,018,706 & 54,514\\
Mean nodes per access & 1,120.50 & 12.17\\
Maximum balanced depth & --- & 13\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The indexing traversal decreases by a factor of about 92.06. For balanced
\(n\)-entry trees and \(O(n)\) accesses, the node visits are
\(O(n\log n)\). Charging binary comparison and subtraction bit by bit
gives \(O(n\log^2 n)\) indexing work for in-range indices. This is an
analysis of the indexing component; source decoding, segment execution,
proof reconstruction, and kernel checking are additional work.

One matched observation in the shared environment produced:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}\toprule
Measurement & Plain & Balanced\\\midrule
\code{Time vm\_compute} transaction & 16.008 s & 11.314 s\\
Complete source-module compiler process & 33.103 s & 24.772 s\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Those are illustrative single observations, approximately 29.3\% and 25.2\%
lower respectively. The new tree-construction/exactness module itself took
13.159 seconds. Thus this experiment does \emph{not} establish a first-build
speedup, and it makes no whole-endpoint timing claim. A maintained generator
could emit the balanced tree directly and keep exact flattening as a
separate audit target; that design has not been measured here.

All three new modules compiled with closed assumption reports. A separate
recursive \code{coqchk} of the generic checker and its dependencies passed.
The concrete source theorem was kernel-checked during compilation, but was
not the target of that separate recursive invocation. The complete inherited
ZFC endpoint was not rebuilt.
