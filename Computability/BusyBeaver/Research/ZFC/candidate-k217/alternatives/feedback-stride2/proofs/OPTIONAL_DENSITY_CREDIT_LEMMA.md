# Optional uniform stack-credit theorem for the defined abstract biased scheduler

This is not needed when the startup stack is3, since that stack is entirely theorem-valued. It concerns only the abstract scheduler in COFINAL_ADJACENT_PAIR_PROOF.md, not the rejected upstream source. It gives a stronger resource result than a particular feedback-counter trace.

Let W(n) be the complete syntactic command word of candidate n, ignoring intended halts. Let s(n) count its S commands, and let g(0)=0 and g(n)=s(n) for n>0. Write f for the right Cantor projection. Exact decoding gives:

- W(P(0,y)) is S, followed by W(y-1) if y>0.
- W(P(1,y)) is MP, followed by W(f(y)) if f(y)>0.
- W(P(a,y)), a>=2, is AX, followed by W(f(f(y))) if that residual is positive.

## Counting theorem

For every N>=0,

    A(N) := sum_{n<N} s(n) <= N.

Proof by strong induction on N. The case N=0 is immediate, and s(0)=1. Let T_j=j(j+1)/2. For a nonnegative function u with u(0)=0 and prefix sums <=L-1 for all positive relevant lengths L, its pullback through f has prefix sums

    sum_{y<L} u(f(y)) <= L-r(L),

where r(L) is the number of nonempty Cantor diagonal blocks among indices y<L, equivalently the least r with T_r>=L. Indeed f enumerates 0;0,1;0,1,2;... . Each full or nonempty partial block loses at least one unit against its length. All terms involved are at smaller indices than the induction target.

Since sum_{j<L}g(j)=A(L)-1<=L-1 for L>0, this lemma applies to g and then to g composed with f. Denote the two pullback sums by H(L) and H2(L); both are <=L-r(L).

At a triangular boundary T_q, q>=2, decompose all complete diagonals by their root command. The exact recurrence above yields

    A(T_q) = q + A(q-1) + H(q-1) + sum_{l=1}^{q-2} H2(l)
           <= T_q - D_q,

where

    D_q = sum_{l=1}^{q-1} r(l) - (q-1) >=0.

The boundary q=1 is immediate. Now write N=T_q+k with 0<=k<=q. If k<=q-1, the additional prefix contains only AX-root candidates, and costs H2(k)<=k, proving A(N)<=N. The remaining case k=q includes those q-1 AX roots plus the MP root. It costs

    H2(q-1)+g(f(q-1)).

By induction, g(m)<=m for m>0: it is one term of A(m+1)-s(0)<=m; it also holds for m=0. Moreover f(q-1)<=r(q-1). Thus this additional cost is <=q-1, which is even less than k=q. The q=1 case is directly checked. This completes the induction.

## Uniform credit bound for every gap-one-or-two subsequence starting at1

Assign conservative command balances S=-1, MP=0, AX=+10. Actual successful MP pushes an additional theorem zero, so ignoring that push is safe. Consider any increasing candidate sequence starting at1 whose gaps are one or two. This includes the defined feedback scheduler but does not assume its exact lagged-bias rule.

Before its candidate n, at least floor((n-1)/2) earlier candidates have run. Among all indices <=n, at most 2q+1 have first command other than AX, where q=floor((sqrt(8n+1)-1)/2)<=sqrt(2n). Therefore at least floor((n-1)/2)-2q-1 earlier selected candidates have already supplied a first AX and hence ten credits. At any point in candidate n, the total S cost of all selected commands so far is at most A(n+1)<=n+1. Ignoring every additional AX and all successful-MP credit, the net balance from the beginning is bounded below by

    10 floor((n-1)/2)-20q-10-(n+1)
       >= 4n-20 sqrt(2n)-21.

This is positive for n>=64: it is positive at64 and increasing thereafter. Thus only candidates1 through63 need checking. The exact finite dynamic program minimizes each endpoint balance over predecessors n-1 and n-2 and also checks every internal command prefix. It gives minimum -3, attained at candidate5 along a permitted finite path. This is a proof over the finite remaining domain, not a finite extrapolation for the infinite theorem.

Consequently three initial theorem heads above an arbitrary opaque tail suffice for every such candidate schedule. In particular the actual safe stack3 requires no appeal to this estimate; its infinite decoded tail is already theorem-valued.
