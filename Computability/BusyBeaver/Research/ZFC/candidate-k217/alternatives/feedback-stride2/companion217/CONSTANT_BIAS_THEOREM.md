# Conditional calibration of the constant-bias stride-two repair

This theorem concerns the proposed global-bias companion, completed as a 217-working-state table. It does not establish the requested214/215 target. Source binding, all-natural binary runtime and the literal blank startup are separate obligations. In particular, this theorem does not repair the incorrect CC3 edge in the rejected215 table by assertion.

## Exact abstract interface required

The work-tuple commands are the directly proved short transfer S, unchanged MP and unchanged AX from `research214_theory/NIP_TRANSFER_COMPILER.md`. After every command, including S, the common footer leaves scratch X=1 and the other parser scratch registers zero. At a positive residual L, the scheduler decrements L and its first read adds the bias, so it unpairs L itself. The first read clears X; further fields are ordinary unpairings. At an empty residual, refill copies the old candidate counter N, increases N by2, and clears X. The first field of a new candidate is decoded directly from old N.

Assume the exact blank startup is a partial initial AX from logical registers (N,T,W,A,D,H,X,Y,L)=(1,0,3,0,0,0,0,0,0), and that completion returns through this common footer. The candidates then begin1,3,5,... . The startup stack is safe:3=P(2,0),2=Q_0(0) is a theorem, and stack0 has only theorem-zero heads forever. Therefore no protected-credit estimate is needed.

## Finite draining and word encoding

For positive L, f(L)<L for the right Cantor projection f. Each nonhalting command consumes one or more projections, so every candidate finishes after finitely many commands. The established command contracts provide finite work within a command.

Let W be the universally halting word AX(0;0,0);B_1;H supplied by a numerical-calculus refutation and the direct short-S compiler. The leading AX resets d=h=0 regardless of the incoming work store; the strong compiler handles arbitrary top and stack. H tests the produced theorem1. Thus W halts from every natural work tuple.

Concatenate the command fields of W into a finite nonempty list a_1,...,a_m. For any positive unused terminal residual r, encode

    E(r)=P(a_1,P(a_2,...,P(a_m,r)...)).

There are no successor wrappers: the constant bias exactly cancels every scheduler decrement. Every intermediate residual is positive because P(a,b)>=b and r>=1. Thus candidate E(r) executes W and halts before reaching the unused tail.

## Dyadic prefix lemma

For every fixed finite field list of length m, every k>=1 and every residue c modulo2^k, there is a residue r_0 modulo2^(k+m) such that

    E(r_0+2^(k+m)j) = c modulo2^k

for all natural j. In particular, successful candidates are cofinal in the odd numbers.

Proof. Let T_n=n(n+1)/2 and F(z)=T_(2z)=z(2z+1). The identity

    F(z)-F(w)=(z-w)(2(z+w)+1)

shows that F permutes residues modulo every power of two: its second factor is odd, so equality of outputs modulo2^k implies equality of inputs modulo2^k; finite injectivity implies surjectivity.

For a fixed a,

    P(a,b)=T_(a+b+1)-a-1.

To force P(a,b)=c modulo2^k, choose z modulo2^k with F(z)=c+a+1 modulo2^k, and require

    b=2z-a-1 modulo2^(k+1).

Then a+b+1 is congruent to2z modulo2^(k+1). Triangular numbers of congruent arguments modulo2^(k+1) are congruent modulo2^k, so the required output follows. Applying this inverse-residue step successively to the fixed outer fields raises the modulus exponent by one per field and yields the stated tail residue class.

Choose representatives with r>=1. Since E(r)>=r, the encoded successful candidates in the target residue class are unbounded. Taking k=1 and c=1 supplies a successful odd candidate beyond any initial cutoff. The stride-two scheduler reaches it unless it halts earlier.

## Equivalence, once source and runtime are bound

On a safe actual run, top, cache and every iterated stack head are theorems. Short S, MP and AX preserve this invariant; the scratch footer does not alter the work tuple. The only intended halt occurs when S tests theorem1. Thus every halt implies a numerical-calculus refutation.

Conversely, a numerical-calculus refutation supplies W and, by the dyadic lemma, a cofinal odd successful candidate. Finite draining and exact odd enumeration force a halt. Combining the two implications with the inherited numerical-calculus calibration gives the desired Con(ZFC) equivalence for any binary machine that realizes all the stated interface and startup hypotheses.

The dyadic-prefix argument is inherited from the earlier encoding-research report and is restated here to make this particular constant-bias application self-contained; the new application must not be represented as a new proof of that general lemma.
