# The canonical constructible order

[ConstructibleOrder.lean](../../Lean/BusyBeaver/ZFC/ConstructibleOrder.lean) constructs the canonical internal well-orders of the L hierarchy and proves `choice_of_VL`. Its hypotheses are the repository's six-field `ZFAxioms mem` bundle, actual Power, internal `Regular mem`, and `∀ x, InL H hP x`. Its conclusion is the exact existing `ChoiceSem mem`: every represented pairwise disjoint family of nonempty sets admits a selector meeting each member in exactly one point.

The keys consist of an internal formula code, an internal natural support bound, and a tuple of that length. Uniform `SatSupport.sat_support` covers every recognized internal code, including nonstandard ones. Restricting an environment to its support produces a tuple whose every completion defines the same subset. The construction never assumes a well-order of all omega-length environments.

The relevant keys form a set: if `CF` is the code-order field and `F` the old stage field, they lie in `CF × (omega × Power(omega × F))`. Tuple graphs have no nonpair junk, their domain is an internal natural contained in omega, and their values lie in F. Actual named Separation therefore collects all keys for a represented set of candidate elements. `key_exists`, restricted uniqueness, and least-key pullback well-order the new elements. The empty-field case is handled separately, since the universal environment condition alone would otherwise be vacuous.

`stepLOp` gives an actual named formula for the order-extension operation. `lord_wos` proves its recursive stages are internally well-ordered, and `lord_fld` identifies each stage field with the actual `L_a`. For a family of nonempty pairwise disjoint sets, a common stage contains the union of that family. A literal Separation instance selects its members' least points; disjointness and antisymmetry prove uniqueness.

These are internal well-orders: least elements are required for represented nonempty subsets. No external well-foundedness, standardness of omega or codes, object Choice, or transitive standard model is assumed. Meta-level `Classical.choice` names existing extensional sets and is distinct from the object-model Choice conclusion.
