# A syntactic consistency invariant for K + RAB + every identity

Author: opening_encoder. Ordinary proof draft for independent challenge; no new proof-assistant execution or machine claim.

## 1. Exact constructors and scope

Use the pinned Code254 constructors I(x,y)=4P(x,y), R(x,y)=4P(x,y)+1, Q_i(x)=4P(i,x)+2, E(x,y)=4P(x,y)+3. Let T=0, F=1=R(0,0), N x=I(x,F), Ex_i x=N(Q_i(N x)). Constructor tags are disjoint, I and Q are injective in their two arguments, every child is at most its I parent, R(a,b)>a, and R(a,b)=F implies a=b=T. The zero alias I(T,T)=T is retained.

The calculus contains truth, all exact rho1–6 and merged G rows, the restricted row

    H(a,b,c) = ((c → R(a,b)) → a) → ((a → c) → (b → c)),

all K rows a→(b→a), and **all identities x→x**, with MP and theorem-only GEN. It contains the actual gated C222 bundle. For the invariant we may admit its exact expression at all triples; this is a soundness overapproximation, not a change to the candidate calculus. In particular this larger system contains the proposed single extra axiom I(F,F)=16.

Write the actual literal core as follows:

    req(b,k,m) = Q_b(m → N(Q_b(m → N R(b,k))))
    Sub(a,b;k) = Q_k(R(k,a) → R(k,b))
    clos(a,b,k,m) = Q_b(R(b,k) → N(Q_a(m → N(Q_a(Sub(a,b;k) → req(b,k,m))))))
    nks(a,b,c) = Q_c(clos(a,b,c,c) → N req(b,c,c))
    V(a,b,c) = R(a,b) → Q_c R(a,b)
    Bundle(a,b,c) = (V(a,b,c) → nks(a,b,c)) → F.

All repeated labels and m=k=c occurrences are literal. Unlike the earlier RAB invariant, this argument DOES NOT replace nks with an arbitrary Q-headed formula. Such a replacement becomes inconsistent in the presence of K: any theorem Q_i T would provide V→Q_i T, which its generalized bundle would refute.

## 2. The grammar Safe (no MP constructor)

A finite Safe certificate is generated by:

1. every exact retained row rho1–6, G, H and Bundle above, and every K row;
2. Id(x): Safe(x→x), for every natural x;
3. Gen(i,s): from a Safe certificate s for p, a certificate for Q_i p;
4. Mono(i,s): from Safe(p→q), a certificate for Q_i p→Q_i q;
5. Weak(a,s): from Safe t, a certificate for a→t;
6. Lift(b,c,s): from Safe t, a certificate for (t→c)→(b→c).

There is no modus-ponens or arbitrary substitution constructor. Weak is already justified for actual theorems by K. Lift is justified by K plus H: from a theorem t, K gives (c→R(t,b))→t, and H(t,b,c) detaches the displayed Lift formula. These observations motivate the grammar but are not used circularly to prove its MP closure.

Every Safe code has tag 0 or 2. Thus F and every membership/equality code are not Safe. Every Safe certificate for Q_i p is literally a Gen certificate; stripping it gives a strictly smaller Safe certificate for p. All other constructors have tag 0, even when their result is the zero alias.

An implication Safe(p→q) is therefore one of the exact axiom forms, an identity, Mono, Weak, or Lift. The following inversions check every such possibility.

## 3. Four forbidden minors

### 3.1 Equality negation

There is no Safe(E(i,j)→F).

For exact rows, the antecedent/consequent tags rule it out: rho1 has Q antecedent; rho2 has I consequent; rho3/5 have I consequent; rho4 has Q consequent; rho6 has I antecedent; G/H/K have I consequent; Bundle has I antecedent. Id would require E(i,j)=F. Mono ends in Q. Weak would require a Safe certificate for F. Lift has I consequent. Gen cannot be an implication.

### 3.2 The rho6 antecedent

Set X(i,p)=Ex_i(Q_i p)=(Q_i(N(Q_i p))→F). There is no Safe X(i,p).

Only rho1 can match the exact outer Q→F shape among the nontrivial axioms: matching Q_j(N E(j,k)) with Q_i(N Q_i p) would force E(j,k)=Q_i p, impossible by tags. In rho6, a matching consequent p'=F leaves its I-headed X(j,F) antecedent equal to a Q-headed code, impossible. Bundle's antecedent is I-headed. All other exact rows have a wrong antecedent/consequent tag. Id would equate Q with F; Mono ends in Q; Weak would require Safe F; Lift ends in I; Gen has Q head.

### 3.3 Negated literal req

There is no Safe N req(b,c,c).

Again the target has Q→F shape. The potential rho1 match is

    Q_i(N E(i,j)) = Q_b(c → N(Q_b(c → N R(b,c)))).

Injectivity forces the consequent N(Q_b(...)) to equal F, impossible because it has implication tag 0. All other cases are exactly as in 3.2: rho6 would have I antecedent instead of Q, Bundle I antecedent, other axiom consequents have the wrong tag, Id/Mono/Weak/Lift are excluded. This uses the literal req body; it is not a general assertion that no quantified formula can be negated.

### 3.4 The literal nks core

There is no Safe(clos(a,b,c,c) → N req(b,c,c)), and hence no Safe nks(a,b,c).

Its antecedent is Q-headed and its consequent I-headed. Among the exact axioms, rho2 is the only remaining Q→I possibility: matching its consequent

    (Q_i p → Q_i q) = (req(b,c,c) → F)

would force Q_i q=F. K is another I-consequent possibility: matching x→(y→x) forces x=clos(a,b,c,c) and x=F, again impossible. Other rows have a wrong outer antecedent or consequent tag. Id would equate Q with I; Mono ends in Q; Weak requires Safe N req, excluded by 3.3; Lift has I antecedent; Gen has Q head. Finally Q-stripping would turn Safe nks into this forbidden minor.

Consequently there is no Safe(V(a,b,c) → nks(a,b,c)). Its antecedent is I-headed with R left child and Q right child, and its consequent is Q-headed. Of exact axioms, rho6 could have a Q consequent, but its X antecedent has Q left child and F right child, so it cannot equal V. H's antecedent, if matched with V, would force its I-headed inner left child to equal R(a,b). The remaining rows are excluded by outer tags or their consequent tags. Id would equate I with Q; Mono starts in Q; Weak would require Safe nks, just excluded; Lift ends in I. This excludes exactly the Bundle MP trigger.

## 4. The H trigger supplies a Safe consequent

If Safe(((c→R(a,b))→a)), then Safe a.

Check the outermost Safe certificate:

- Weak: its supplied child is exactly Safe a.
- rho6: matching its antecedent X(i,p) with c→R(a,b) and its consequent p with a forces R(a,b)=F. Hence a=b=T, and Id(T) supplies Safe T by I(T,T)=T.
- H(x,y,z): matching forces x=R(a,b), while a=(x→z)→(y→z) is at least x. This contradicts R(a,b)>a.
- K(x,y): matching forces a=y→(c→R(a,b)), which is at least R(a,b)>a.
- Bundle: matching its consequent with a gives a=F; the remaining equality would identify R(F,b) with the Q-headed nks core, impossible.
- Id: matching gives a=c→R(a,b), which is at least R(a,b)>a.
- Lift(d,e,s) with premise Safe t: matching gives a=d→e and e=R(a,b), again a≥R(a,b)>a.
- Mono has Q antecedent rather than I. Gen has Q head. rho1/2/4 have Q antecedents, and rho3/5/G have E antecedents, rather than the required I antecedent.

This exhausts the grammar. The resulting Safe-a certificate has at most the size of the trigger certificate: it is an immediate Weak subcertificate, or the one-node Id(T) certificate in the rho6 case.

## 5. MP closure by total certificate size

For Safe certificates s of p→q and t of p, prove Safe q by strong induction on size(s)+size(t), counting one per grammar constructor. This is not an assumed deduction theorem.

Inspect s:

- Id returns t.
- Weak returns its stored conclusion certificate.
- K returns Weak of t.
- rho1: Q-stripping t gives the forbidden equality negation, contradiction.
- rho2: Q-stripping t gives Safe(p'→q'); return Mono of it.
- rho3/5/G: their E antecedents cannot be Safe by tags.
- rho4: strip its two Q prefixes and reapply GEN in reversed order.
- rho6: its antecedent is forbidden by 3.2.
- Bundle: its antecedent is forbidden by 3.4.
- H(a,b,c): section 4 supplies Safe a from t; return Lift(b,c) of that certificate.
- Gen cannot be an implication by its tag.
- Mono(i,s0): t is a Gen certificate. Strip it to t0 for the unquantified premise. Apply the induction hypothesis to s0,t0 and then GEN. Both stored proof sizes have decreased.
- Lift(b,c,s0), where s0 proves Safe u: the minor t proves Safe(u→c). Apply the induction hypothesis to **t as major and s0 as minor**, obtaining Safe c, and return Weak(b) of it. The new measure is size(t)+size(s0), strictly below size(t)+size(Lift(b,c,s0)). This is the only swap and is why induction on the major certificate alone would be invalid.

All cases return a certificate of size at most size(s)+size(t). For the H/rho6 trigger case, the two one-node axiom certificates produce the two-node Lift(Id(T)); all other H cases return a Lift of a proper Weak subcertificate. The Mono and Lift recursive cases preserve the bound. The elimination procedure is therefore bounded by the input certificate sizes and is primitive recursive on finite encoded certificates.

## 6. Consistency consequence and remaining scope

Translate every derivation in the stated calculus to a Safe certificate by induction: truth uses Id(T), each axiom uses its Safe root, GEN uses Gen, and MP uses the constructive elimination of section 5. Every identity, including fixed code 16, is already admitted. Since Safe F is impossible by its tag, there is no derivation of F.

The constructor inequalities, certificate verification and bounded elimination are primitive recursive and their correctness is formalizable in PA. Thus even K + RAB + **all identities**, with every retained row and exact full gated bundle, has an elementary consistency proof. This rules out the desired ZFC-provable calibration, conditional on Con(ZFC), by the usual second-incompleteness argument. It does not suggest the extension is inconsistent; it proves the opposite. No positive derivation of all identities is needed for this conclusion, since they were freely added to the stronger consistent system.

This invariant does not include quantified stability QS. In particular, adding QS invalidates the simple Q-stripping lemma: rho6(i,F) is NN(Q_i(N(Q_i F))), and the QS instance can produce Q_i(N(Q_i F)). Therefore this proof makes no claim about the combined extension K+RAB+fixed16+QS. The earlier finite model establishes K+RAB+QS without fixed16; these two separate results must not be silently united.

No source pricing is justified by this negative result. No native formula-substitution or renaming rule, theorem equality, proof assistant run, or smaller machine is asserted.
