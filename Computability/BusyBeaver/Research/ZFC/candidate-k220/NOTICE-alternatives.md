# Attribution and scope

Prepared as a research contribution for Vladimir Reshetnikov's ProveIt work.
New prose, scripts, and certificates are supplied under the MIT No Attribution
terms in `LICENSE`. Existing third-party materials retain their own terms; no
repository-wide relicensing or replacement of third-party notices is asserted.

The exact public `K220_Construction_2026-10-20.zip` is retained unchanged in
`vendor/`, as read at ProveIt commit
a27b5391bbf3cfdc7d06cc7acb135f4930a03101. It contains its full attribution and
recursive K221, K222, K224, K225, and retained K226 evidence. The logical
interpretation also retains Elliot Glazer's K230 development pinned at
3142e4dd980f0be12f4abb636c4ae2528afc17eb. Their existing credits and MIT,
MIT X11, MIT No Attribution, and MPL 2.0 notices remain inside the unchanged
recursive deliveries.

The continuation rewrites and two independent macro/window checks adapt the
previously delivered `K221_Alternatives_2026-10-20` work to the exact new K220.
Every variant proof here is rerun against K220's own freshly derived invariant;
no inference is made merely because the edited rows are far from the K220 fold.
The macro checker uses corrected K220 filenames and status labels and adds
strict transition alphabet/direction parsing. The independent window checker
is unchanged. `portable/canonicalize.py` adapts K220's independent sparse-native
and dense-canonical startup checker to the three hash-pinned variant tables.

The native and canonical tables are new literal 220-state alternatives. They
are not new logical theories and not a state-count improvement. These ordinary
mathematical certificates do not create a newly compiled exact-byte K220
Rocq/Lean theorem or a global lower bound. Exploratory specimens are expressly
excluded from the certified core.
