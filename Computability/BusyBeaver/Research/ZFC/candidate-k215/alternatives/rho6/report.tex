\begin{abstract}
We construct two concrete binary, one-tape Turing machines with 215 working
states. Each differs from its accepted K215 parent in exactly two transition
rows and changes the sixth axiom generator from
$\Ex{A}{Q_A C}\imp C$ to $\Ex{A}{Q_A B}\imp B$.
The complete numerical axiom family is unchanged, including all undecodable
natural-number codes. A repaired axiom-selection compiler, the inherited native
calculus calibration, exact blank initialization, and a fresh quantified
primitive-runtime check give an ordinary proof, formalizable in ZFC, that
blank nonhalting is equivalent to $\Con(\ZFC)$.

Both machines pass all 6,176 guarded runtime cases. Their exact blank prefixes
take 95,714 and 96,284 transitions. A separate auditor checks source changes,
case records, positive clocks, every transition row, state renaming, and the
entire finite startup trace without importing the runtime checker.
Neither machine is a state renaming of the four accepted K215 presentations
included for comparison, nor of the other new machine.

No 214-state machine is claimed. The $\rho_6$ permutation itself has prior
Source163 provenance. The contribution is its exact two-row composition with
both accepted K215 command orders, complete fresh runtime verification,
explicit source/calculus proof, and a portable reproduction packet.
Additional results include a bounded exhaustive census of 279,936 row recipes,
controlled allocation experiments, and a 215-state command permutation that
passes primitive checks but halts from blank. No new proof-assistant endpoint
or unconditional consistency assertion is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Claim, conventions and provenance}

We use the usual deterministic two-symbol, one-tape convention. Tape positions
are integers, the blank symbol is 0, the initial head is at position 0, and
each transition writes one bit and moves exactly one cell left or right.
A distinguished halt label is not a working state. Entering that label halts.
There are two defined rows for every working state.

In the native presentations, start is 0, dispatcher is 84 and halt is 254.
The dense presentations relabel the sorted working-state set to
$0,\ldots,214$ and relabel halt to 215; their dispatcher is 83.
Every one of the 215 working states is reachable in the directed
transition graph from start. This graph statement does not assert that all
states occur on the actual blank computation.

\begin{theorem}[Ordinary mathematical endpoint]\label{thm:main}
For each of the two exact machines $M$ supplied in this packet,
the following equivalences have a proof formalizable in ZFC:
\[
 \ZFC\vdash
 \Halt(M,\mathrm{blank})\ \longleftrightarrow\
 \neg\Con(\ZFC),
 \qquad
 \ZFC\vdash
 \neg\Halt(M,\mathrm{blank})\ \longleftrightarrow\
 \Con(\ZFC).
\]
Here $\Con(\ZFC)$ means the usual syntactic consistency assertion for ZFC,
with the proof coding connected to the inherited native-calculus
calibration.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses the repository's accepted numerical-calculus calibration and
dominated-$\rho_8$ recovery theorem as predecessor results
\cite{primary,dominated,commands}. This packet supplies the changed
source-to-calculus argument and new exact binary checks. It does not
contain a newly compiled Lean or Rocq theorem for these two exact tables,
an internal-PA endpoint, a proof of $\Con(\ZFC)$, or a state lower bound.
In particular, the theorem is an equivalence; it does not assert
unconditional nonhalting.

All predecessor files are taken from ProveIt commit
\[
\pin.
\]
The two parents are the primary
\file{candidate-k215/} and its
\file{alternatives/commands120/} presentation \cite{commands120}. Paths to these candidates
in the bibliography are relative to

\paragraph{Prior work and the actual increment.}
The Source163 investigation already proposed replacing the formula parameter
of $\rho_6$ by $B$, with earlier 216/217 sources

\subsection{The literal source edit}

The source is the ordered list of primitive triples
\texttt{[binary path, opcode, operand]}. Its operations are increment,
guarded decrement, break, continue and halt. In the closed sixth-row
interval $[888,1171)$, interchange primitive register operands 3 and 5.
Only four occurrences actually change:
\[
 959,\quad 964,\quad 1137,\quad 1142
 \qquad\text{(zero-based primitive ordinals).}
\]
Each changes from register 3 to register 5. All paths, opcodes, other
operands and guarded successor ordinals remain identical to the respective
parent. There are 4,751 primitives, of which 1,425 are decrements; counting
both decrement guards gives 6,176 cases, including the halt case.
The nonhalting guarded control graph has 6,175 edges.

At this phase of AX, source register 4 stores $A$, register 5 stores $B$,
and register 3 stores $C$. These are phase meanings, not a renaming of
the outer logical-register vector. The closed constructor word changes as
follows, where lower-case letters load parameters and $I,Q,F$ denote
the corresponding numerical constructor operations:
\begin{lstlisting}
old: aacQFIQFIcI
new: aabQFIQFIbI
\end{lstlisting}
The change replaces two loads of $C$ by loads of $B$. In native decoder
terms, the $B$ and $C$ loads have entries 12 and 166, respectively.

Both sources have 202 distinct hash-consed subtrees, maximum depth 47,
and maximum zero run 6. A source-subtree count is not a physical
state count; the binary theorem below concerns the actual 215-state
tables.

\subsection{Source macro transfer on all natural inputs}

The source certificate reconstructs the inherited closed parameter-load and
constructor trees from the pinned source/table relation. It matches the
whole primitive word of the edited interval and independently parses its
postfix expression. The register-3/5 swap maps the complete $C$ load
macro to the existing $B$ load macro. The intervening constructors touch
neither parameter register.

Increment and guarded decrement commute with a bijective renaming of
register indices. Break, continue and halt do not inspect register values.
Consequently the inherited all-natural macro proof transfers to the
renamed load, with the same decreasing loop measures. This argument is
about arbitrary natural parameters, not bounded tests of formula codes.

The parameter registers, scratch registers, proof cursor and control
continuation are restored exactly. The current formula and theorem
stack change precisely by the specified row output. It would be false
to claim that the entire register vector is unchanged outside the row:
the changed formula code is intentionally retained there.
All other AX outputs and stack effects remain as in the parent.

\section{A numerical calculus that tolerates junk codes}

\subsection{Constructors and the zero alias}

All constructors in this section are total functions on $\Nat$:
\begin{align*}
 P(x,y)&=\frac{(x+y)(x+y+1)}2+y,&
 I(x,y)&=4P(x,y),\\
 R(x,y)&=4P(x,y)+1,&
 Q_i x&=4P(i,x)+2,\\
 E(x,y)&=4P(x,y)+3,&
 T&=0,\qquad F=1,\\
 N(x)&=I(x,1),&
 \Ex{i}{x}&=N\bigl(Q_i(N(x))\bigr).
\end{align*}
We write $x\imp y$ for $I(x,y)$. The equality $I(0,0)=0$
is a genuine numerical alias. The proofs never require every natural
code to decode as an ordinary first-order formula, and never replace
this alias by a free syntactic constructor.

For orientation, the eight retained rows are as follows; the final row
is directly enumerated only when $U\le A$:
\begin{align*}
 \rho_1(A,B)&=\Ex{A}{E(A,B)},\\
 \rho_2(A,B,C)&=Q_A(B\imp C)\imp(Q_A B\imp Q_A C),\\
 \rho_3(A,B,C)&=E(A,B)\imp(R(C,A)\imp R(C,B)),\\
 \rho_4(A,B,C)&=Q_A(Q_B C)\imp Q_B(Q_A C),\\
 \rho_5(A,B,C)&=E(C,A)\imp(R(C,B)\imp R(A,B)),\\
 \rho_6(A,C)&=\Ex{A}{Q_A C}\imp C,\\
 \rho_7(A,B,C)&=E(C,A)\imp
              \bigl(E(C,B)\imp Q_{R(A,B)}E(A,B)\bigr),\\
 \rho_8(A,B,C,U)&=((C\imp U)\imp A)\imp
                       ((A\imp C)\imp(B\imp C)).
\end{align*}
The seventh row is called \texttt{growG} in the source contract data.
The calculus also contains truth, the exact inherited gated theory
bundle, numerical modus ponens and theorem-only generalization.
The bundle and its gate are not changed. Its complete numerical
constructor tree, postfix word and source spans are retained in
\file{research/formalism/native_axiom_contracts.json}.

\subsection{The symmetry that matters for compilation}

\begin{lemma}[Row-local surjective reparameterization]\label{lem:family}
Suppose a numerical calculus has an axiom family
$\{f(p):p\in D\}$, and replace its enumeration by
$\{f(\sigma(q)):q\in D'\}$, where $\sigma:D'\to D$ is surjective.
If the other axiom families and inference rules are unchanged, the
set of derivable natural numbers is unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two sets of axiom codes are equal. Replace an axiom leaf in either
finite derivation by a parameter tuple giving the same literal code;
all inference nodes and conclusions remain identical. Induction on the
derivation gives both inclusions.
\end{proof}

The lemma is local to each axiom family. It does not assume that arbitrary
derived numerical codes admit a formula-substitution operation.
It also does not require all rows emitted by a single AX command to use
one common global parameter renaming.

The original sixth family is
$\{\rho_6(a,c):a,c\in\Nat\}$; the new family is
$\{\rho_6(a,b):a,b\in\Nat\}$.
They are literally equal sets of natural codes. Thus the old and new
native calculi derive exactly the same codes, a stronger property than
equiconsistency. The values emitted by a \emph{fixed} AX parameter tuple,
however, need not agree. Section~\ref{sec:compiler} repairs the proof
compiler accordingly.

The dominated-$\rho_8$ predecessor theorem recovers every unrestricted
$\rho_8$ instance through a fixed finite derivation, preserving its
literal numerical conclusion \cite{dominated}. Its 3,728-node recovery
certificate and native-calculus/ZFC calibration are inherited results.
They concern arbitrary numerical instances, including collapsed zero
implications. Nothing in our edit changes their premises, the theory
bundle or its private numerical codes.

\section{Command semantics and soundness}

Write a command boundary as $(t,S,d,h)$: current formula, Cantor-coded
stack, additive counter, and cached formula. Write $[x]S=P(x,S)$ and
extend this notation by nesting to the right. The command contracts are
those of the accepted command-permutation compiler \cite{commands}.

\begin{align*}
\MP(z):\quad b&=d+z,&
(t,S,d,h)&\longmapsto
 \begin{cases}
 (b,[0]S,b,0),&I(t,b)=h,\\
 (0,S,b,0),&I(t,b)\ne h;
 \end{cases}\\
\DROP:\quad &&
(t,S,d,h)&\longmapsto(h,[t]S,d,h).
\end{align*}
For $\NIP$, first test the \emph{incoming} $h$. If $h=1$, halt.
Otherwise let $(x,S')=P^{-1}(S)$ and return $(t,S',d,x)$.
The test occurs before, not after, the pop.

An AX command has wire fields $[c+3,y,x]$. On entry, set
\[
 U=d,\qquad A=d+x,\qquad B=h+y,\qquad C=c.
\]
It returns counter/cache zero, current $g(A,B,c)$, and stack
\[
 [\,\rho_1,\rho_2,\rho_3,\rho_4,\rho_5,
       \rho_6(A,B),\rho_7,\rho_8,Q_U(h),t\,]S.       \tag{1}
\]
The other rows use the inherited arguments; $\rho_8$ uses $(A,B,c,U)$.
The gate $g$ is zero if $c=A$ or $c=B$ and otherwise is the exact
inherited theory bundle. Since $A=d+x$, the condition $U\le A$
always holds. In (1), $h$ and $t$ are the incoming cache and current.

The ninth generated entry is a quantifier code, congruent to 2 modulo 4.
Every logical row and the bundle is an implication code, divisible by 4.
Thus all nine generated entries and the gated current are different
from falsity 1, even for junk inputs. There are ten pushes in total,
the last saving old current.

The wire tags are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
Presentation & tag 0 & tag 1 & tag 2\\
\midrule
\rhoa & NIP & DROP & MP\\
\rhob & DROP & NIP & MP\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
AX tags are $c+3$ in both. MP consumes its extra numeric field in both.

\begin{lemma}[Sound command execution]\label{lem:sound}
If $t$, $h$, and all successively decoded heads of $S$ are native
theorems, every nonhalting command preserves this property.
A halt from such a boundary implies that 1 is a native theorem.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The changed sixth row is an old allowed axiom instance by
Lemma~\ref{lem:family}. AX otherwise uses the unchanged axiom,
bundle and generalization rules. MP is precisely the numerical inference
test; its failure branch introduces truth 0. DROP and NIP only move
theorem codes. NIP's sole halt requires incoming cached theorem 1.
The zero stack has only truth heads under repeated unpairing, since
$P^{-1}(0)=(0,0)$.
\end{proof}

\section{A repaired completeness compiler}\label{sec:compiler}

\subsection{The strong contract and reset}

A strong block for theorem $X$ starts from \emph{any}
$(t,S,0,0)$ and either halts early or returns $(X,S,0,0)$.
Current and stack are arbitrary here; counter and cache must be zero.
The padding command used later handles fully arbitrary incoming tuples.

The inherited reset is
\[
 \Discard=\MP(1);\ \AX(1;x=0,y=0);\ \NIP^{10}.
\]
Against cache 0, $\MP(1)$ fails because $I(t,1)>0$.
Its result is $(0,S,1,0)$. The subsequent AX has
$U=A=c=1$, so its gate is zero. It pushes nine entries different
from 1, followed by the saved current 0. Ten NIPs recover the
original stack and cache 0. Thus Discard returns $(0,S,0,0)$,
even from arbitrary $t,S$.

\subsection{Exact row selection}

After Discard and an AX call with saved current 0, selecting stack
position $j$, $1\le j\le9$, uses
\[
 \NIP^j;\ \DROP;\ \NIP^{11-j}.                    \tag{2}
\]
Following AX with saved current 0, the first $j$ pops cache the
chosen entry. DROP moves it to current and pushes the gated current.
The remaining $11-j$ pops discard that safe gate, the remaining
generated rows, and the saved zero. The exact original stack is
restored and cache is zero. These identities use arithmetic
push/pop laws and remain valid when a push of 0 onto 0 aliases 0.

To produce a desired sixth axiom
$\Ex{a}{Q_a v}\imp v$, the new recipe is
\[
 \Discard;\
 \AX(c=0;x=a,y=v);\
 \NIP^6;\DROP;\NIP^5.                            \tag{3}
\]
The actual emitter has $A=a,B=v,C=0$; its sixth slot is exactly
the target natural code. Other rows emitted by that AX may differ
from those of an old compiler call, but they are safely discarded.
There is no need to preserve them.

Unchanged rows use their inherited parameter recipes and (2).
For $\rho_8$ with $U\le A$, first set the counter with
$J(0)=\text{empty}$ and $J(u)=\MP(u)$ for $u>0$,
then use $\AX(c=C;x=A-U,y=B)$ and position 8.
The empty $J(0)$ avoids a spurious successful $\MP(0)$ push.
An unrestricted $\rho_8$ leaf is first translated by the inherited
dominated-row recovery derivation.
Bundle leaves use their original gate witnesses and ten pops;
truth uses Discard.

\subsection{Inference blocks}

The GEN and MP blocks are unchanged. Their proofs depend on stack
positions, safe constructor tags and zero gates, not on the internal
values of the discarded sixth row. For explicitness, if $B_X$
is a strong block for $X$, generalization to $Q_i(X)$ uses
\[
 B_X;\DROP;J(i);\NIP;\AX(i;0,0);
 \NIP^9;\DROP;\NIP;\NIP.                         \tag{4}
\]
Before AX the cache is $X$, counter is $i$ and $U=A=c=i$.
The gate is zero and the ninth row is exactly $Q_i(X)$.

Given strong blocks for $X$ and $I(X,Y)$, modus ponens uses
\begin{align*}
 B_X;\DROP;B_{I(X,Y)};\NIP;\DROP;\NIP;\MP(Y);\\
 \AX(Y;0,0);\NIP^{10};\DROP;\NIP;\NIP.            \tag{5}
\end{align*}
The first line arranges $X$ and $I(X,Y)$ as current and cache
for a successful numerical MP. The second resets counter/cache
using the zero gate $U=A=c=Y$ and restores the underlying stack.
If a pop tests falsity earlier, the required alternative is already
halting. Equations (4)--(5) are the inherited all-natural identities,
including $X=0$, $Y=0$ and collapsed implication aliases
\cite{commands}.

Induction on a finite native derivation, with (3) for its changed
axiom leaves, gives a strong block for every native theorem.
Only wire encodings of the named NIP/DROP commands change between
the 210 and 120 presentations.

\subsection{Cofinality and the real initial cutoff}

To a strong block for theorem 1 append
$\DROP;\NIP;\NIP$. This either halts earlier or eventually tests
cached 1. Prepend the padding command
$\AX(0;x=1,y=Y)$. It resets counter/cache from any incoming tuple;
the following strong block accepts arbitrary current and stack.

Let $f_\pi(k)$ be the list of numeric fields for command $k$ in
wire order $\pi$, and let $\operatorname{Pair}(\vec a,r)$ right-fold
those fields using $P$, with tail $r$. Explicitly define
\[
 \operatorname{enc}_\pi([])=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{enc}_\pi(k::L)=
 1+\operatorname{Pair}(f_\pi(k),\operatorname{enc}_\pi(L)).
\]
Let $R=\operatorname{enc}_\pi(B_1;\DROP;\NIP;\NIP)$, where $B_1$
is the fixed strong refutation block. An initial enumerated candidate
has no outer successor, while noninitial command continuations do.
With the actual field order, the resulting candidate number is
\[
 N_Y=P\bigl(3,P(Y,P(1,R))\bigr)\ \ge Y.           \tag{6}
\]
This family is cofinal, so a successful refutation candidate occurs
above any finite initial cutoff.

The unchanged scheduler increments the next-candidate counter once at
each fetch. Positive proof-list pointers strictly drain between
commands, field readers return tails no larger than their inputs,
and the arithmetic macros terminate on all naturals.
Consequently a malformed nonhalting candidate cannot permanently block
later candidates \cite{commands}. Combined with
Lemma~\ref{lem:sound}, the source halts from the root
\[
 [N,T,W,A,D,H,X,Y,L]=[4,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0]
                                                               \tag{7}
\]
if and only if the native calculus derives 1. The formula-bearing
current, cache and stack in (7) are all zero. The cutoff 4 is handled
by (6), not by pretending the machine starts enumeration at zero.

\section{Whole-tape binary verification}

\subsection{What is quantified}

The unchanged inherited runtime engine is applied to the exact changed
table and exact changed primitive list. It proves a binary block for
each primitive and each decrement guard. The theorem quantifies over:
\begin{itemize}
\item all nine natural primary register values consistent with the guard;
\item any natural gap between the program field and register header;
\item any nonempty finite spare list of natural register values;
\item the complete tape, including the infinite zero exterior.
\end{itemize}
These are unbounded domains. A finite bound on \emph{symbolic proof
construction} is not a bound on natural values or represented runtime.

The register block for value $v$ is $B(v)=0\,1^{v+1}$.
During traversal the alternate form is $C(v)=1\,0\,1^v$.
The physical slots for logical indices $0,\ldots,8$ are
\[
 (6,2,0,4,3,7,8,5,1).
\]
The frame contains a left marker, binary program prefix, padding to
the header at distance $64+\mathrm{gap}$, nine register blocks and
the spare blocks. A logical source step may shift the head/frame
origin; the exact affine motion is checked with its successor prefix.

The proof engine uses literal transitions, unary induction for a
directional self-loop, and structural induction over a finite list
after proving each required one-block schema from the actual table.
Its four block schemas traverse $B$ to $B$ rightward, $B$ to $C$
rightward, $B$ to $B$ leftward, and $C$ to $B$ leftward. Each one-block
duration is $v+2$ and preserves arbitrary surrounding contexts.

Every nonhalt primitive returns to the exact successor frame.
The halt primitive reaches the distinguished halt state. Each proof
has an affine duration in thirteen slots: constant, gap, nine
primary values, first-spare value, and the width of the remaining
spare list. All coefficients are nonnegative and the constant is
strictly positive. Thus each block is finite, and infinitely many
source steps require infinitely many binary transitions.
This supplies both halting correspondence and divergence preservation.

The standalone \file{code/audit_candidate.py} imports no supplied

The independent replay checks every transition, not just a selected

every write is tracked, its final finite set of one cells also proves
the zero exterior. The exact set is in each audit JSON. Neither
altered row is visited anywhere in its startup prefix, so the changed
machine follows its parent's literal blank prefix.

We can now compose the proof:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The exact finite blank run reaches the safe source root (7).
\item The quantified primitive theorem simulates the whole source,
with positive durations and exact frame restoration.
\item Soundness, the repaired strong compiler and cofinality show
source halting exactly when the numerical calculus derives 1.
\item Row-family equality preserves that derivability condition.
The inherited dominated/native-calculus calibration identifies it
with the existence of a finite ZFC refutation.
\end{enumerate}
Each implication is arithmetical or an ordinary finite-derivation
argument formalizable in ZFC. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
The binary checker alone would not have supplied steps 1, 3 or 4.

\section{Material difference and its precise extent}\label{sec:distinction}

The auditor compares rooted action-labelled transition graphs. Starting
with start mapped to start, input symbol 0 or 1 forces both the action
and successor mapping. A forced-map conflict, injectivity conflict or
action mismatch rules out a bijective state renaming. All states are
graph reachable, so this deterministic traversal is sufficient.

Both new machines fail the isomorphism test against all four included
accepted native references: primary K215, commands120, composed, and
composed/source164. They also fail the test against one another.
The audit files include the forcing bit word and the conflicting
targets for each exclusion. For example, the order-120 machine versus
its parent forces a conflict along
\begin{lstlisting}
000000100100000000100001001110110
\end{lstlisting}
where the same forced left target 76 would have to match both 76 and
186 on the right. These bit words are paths in the transition graph,
not assertions that a physically consistent tape presents that word.

A broader saved corpus comparison for the order-210 machine found no
match among sixteen eligible 215-state tables in the checked-out ZFC
subtree, even allowing symbol complementation and left/right reflection.
This is a bounded corpus statement. It does not establish global
historical novelty, semantic inequivalence, or a different blank
halting condition. The substantive change is the source's use of a
different axiom parameter and its resulting binary routing.

\section{Experiments toward 214}

The positive construction stops at 215. The following experiments
identify finite search boundaries and possible next directions;
none is a lower-bound argument.

\subsection{All independent row-parameter permutations}

For the seven retained ungated rows, assign each of the six permutations
of registers 3, 4, 5 independently, preserving the accepted literal
source association. The exact search evaluates
$6^7=279,936$ recipes. The minimum number of distinct source subtrees
is 202, attained at exactly four recipes:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llr@{}}
\toprule
Recipe & Row changes & Edited primitive operands\\
\midrule
identity & none & 0\\
\texttt{joint24} & swap $B,C$ separately in $\rho_2,\rho_4$ & 16\\
\texttt{rho6} & use $B$ in $\rho_6$ & 4\\
\texttt{joint246} & both preceding changes & 20\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Every changed family is exactly the original numerical family.
For a desired $\rho_j(A,B,C)$ with $j=2,4$ in a joint-swap source,
the repaired recipe uses $\AX(c=B;x=A,y=C)$ after Discard.
The two additional source variants are supplied with their exact
source and control-flow certificates; no accepted 215- or 214-state
binary allocation is claimed for them.

This census only excludes a source-DAG improvement within the stated
finite family and fixed association. It does not exclude another
association, a different register program, a cheaper contextual decoder,
or a calculus that is merely equiconsistent rather than identical in
theorem codes.

\subsection{Local transition and source searches}

Seven ordinary direct-fold searches evaluated 4,431,512 specifications
across the primary, command-120, composed, new rho6 and three rotated
seeds. The proof-construction settings were radius 7 and fuel 97.
Additional searches considered 632,584 specifications with a blank
initial window, 168,324 and 168,314 shifted-head specifications on two
seeds, 94,600 unique-entrance eliminations, and 186,620
first-failure-pruned multiple-entrance probes.
No certified reduction survived.

These families overlap; adding their counts does not count distinct
machines. Failure of a bounded local proof search can mean that its
invariant or proof budget is too weak. It is not a semantic
counterexample and does not establish minimality.

A one-row rewrite search found 497 locally stuttering-equivalent
proposals among 371,090 alternatives, but no compatible paired fold
in the searched closure. Product constant propagation from the
calibrated logical seed found only two always-zero decrement sites;
the naive source replacement increased the subtree count from 202
to 209. A separate physical-rotation search visited 3,959 distinct
tables without dropping below 202 subtrees. These are exploratory
receipts; they are not additional accepted machine certificates.

\subsection{Controlled global allocation}

A corrected global contextual-role allocator fixes 207 inherited
engine/helper/context rows and lets source nodes move among existing
physical labels. Its source-identity control passes all 4,750
internal prefixes and retains 215 states. That control changes one
routing row, so it is not byte-identical to the baseline and has no
new full runtime certificate.

Within this fixed-row model, the \texttt{joint24} and
\texttt{joint246} trials are reported infeasible (17,711/17,718
role variables and 15,503/15,514 constraints). A broader model
fixes only 188 rows and gives nineteen contextual entries a
keep-or-reassign decision. Its \texttt{joint24} trial reached its
60-second solver limit, measured as 63.05 seconds, with no feasible
incumbent. This is a timeout, not an infeasibility result.
Earlier draft models that rejected the identity control are excluded
from these negative claims.

The portable allocation scripts, inputs, exact solver records and
attribution are included under \file{research/independent-encoding/}.
This family still restricts the machine architecture, so its failures
do not address general 215- or 214-state realizability.

\subsection{A useful rejected command permutation}

Changing primary K215 to NIP0/MP1/DROP2 by
\[
 134/1:\ 1R12\mapsto1R135,\qquad
 174/1:\ 1R135\mapsto1R12
\]
retains 215 states and passes all source-prefix checks. An observed
symbolic run also completed all 6,176 primitive cases. Nevertheless
the literal machine \emph{halts from blank after 646,553 transitions}.

Its first complete source root occurs at transition 494,760 with
\[
 [N,T,W,A,D,H,X,Y,L]=[2,0,4,0,1,0,0,0,0].
\]
The stack value $4=P(1,1)$ has falsity as its first head. Candidate
$2=P(0,1)$ encodes NIP;NIP under this tag order. The first NIP
loads the bad head and the second tests it. The finite rejection
is independently reproducible with a standard-library simulator;
the final tape contains exactly 409 one cells and zero exterior.
The rejection does not depend on the symbolic-run observation, whose
full per-case records are not retained in this supplement.

This example pinpoints why a correct primitive interpreter and
an equiconsistent source language do not alone prove the desired
blank-machine theorem. The initial theorem-stack invariant is
also essential.

\subsection{Why dropping a shared PUSH is not yet a formalism}

Bypassing native state 164's shared PUSH role would omit four AX
pushes as well as its other use. The load helper adds its parameter
to the uncleared current; it does not overwrite that current.
Consequently some leading quantifier indices become $A$ plus the
preceding row code, while other occurrences of $A$ remain unchanged.
For example, the sixth row becomes
\[
 \Ex{A+G}{Q_A C}\imp C,
\]
where $G$ is the preceding completed row code, rather than a uniform
reparameterization of $\rho_6$. AX would also lose four stack outputs.
There is no soundness/equiconsistency proof for that machine here.
The exact symbolic obstruction is retained in
\file{research/formalism/shared_push_obstruction.md}.

\paragraph{The next promising boundary.}
The symmetry census suggests searching the \emph{presentation} of
axiom families jointly with decoder context: permit row-local
surjections, then compile an explicit right-inverse selection recipe.
The two joint variants show that the logical freedom is larger than
the single $\rho_6$ change. To reach 214, an allocator likely needs
to free an additional contextual role or change source association,
or a new formalism needs a raw-code calibration robust to a more
substantial stack/constructor change. These are research directions,
not completed claims.

python3 -B code/reproduce.py --output fresh-audit
