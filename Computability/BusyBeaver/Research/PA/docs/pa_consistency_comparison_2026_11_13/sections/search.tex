\section{New grammar screens and their limitations}

The new split-head equivalence was tested as an actual changed emitter.
The macro word has 205 items instead of 201: the contraposed modal head
has the same constructor count as the old first head, while the separate
explosion family adds a four-item expression. Seven heads can be emitted
in different orders, and their parameter roles can be renamed. These are
sound source-level choices when the changed command selectors are used.

A second tested presentation uses the positive modal form
\[
 H\to U\bigl(D,U(D,H\to1)\to1\bigr)
\]
together with explosion. Substituting $H\to1$ into the contraposed form
and using $H\to((H\to1)\to1)$ derives this positive form. Conversely,
substitute $H\to1$ into the positive form and use double-negation
elimination, available with explosion and classical implication, under
the retained quantifier-monotonicity rule. Thus it is another safe
presentation with the same number of constructors but a different
emission order.

The third family pads the formula parameter in the first head with
formulas equivalent to it under the unchanged implicational basis.
Examples are $0\to H$, $(H\to H)\to H$, and $(H\to0)\to H$.
Code $0$ is already derivable truth, so these equivalences do not use
the first quantifier head. Quantifier monotonicity transports them
through its two quantifiers. Longer words can occasionally share more
subtrees, so this is a legitimate state-optimization question despite
the additional symbols.

The search uses the inherited repeated-subword dictionary and a randomized
pair-replacement heuristic with fixed seed 2026111342. Before each run,
the original compiler is checked to produce 196 states from the original
68-node body. The two split families never reached the heuristic's
72-node physical-allocation cutoff. Among the padding samples, 499
candidate parses reached that cutoff, but every one failed the imposed
maximum-six-zero-run or 46-bit-path screen. The padding family includes
the unchanged parameter, so its score of 68 is not claimed as a new
grammar improvement.

Consequently no layout in these three screens was accepted for physical
pricing. The recorded \file{best_physical} field is \file{null}; it is
not a numerical state bound. The report deliberately does not convert
74 raw grammar nodes into a purported minimum machine count. The
searches are finite heuristics, do not exhaust associations or logical
presentations, and do not rule out a better implementation of the
proved split-head calculus.

The portable script is \file{grammar/split_head_search.py}; its minimal
support sources and licenses are included. The exact receipts are under
\file{results/}. The two directions of the split-head proof are also
serialized in \file{logic/split_head_certificates.json}. A separate
checker reads those stored DAGs without importing their generator and
validates every axiom instance, assumption, prior-node reference, modus
ponens step, and final conclusion.
