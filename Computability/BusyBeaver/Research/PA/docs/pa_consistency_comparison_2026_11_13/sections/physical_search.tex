\section{Additional searches using source-derived invariants}
\label{sec:additional-local-search}

No state reduction was obtained in the searches described in this section.
Their value is to examine several transformations that are not covered by a
literal action-preserving quotient. The supplied programs, frozen inputs,
positive controls, adverse controls, and receipts are sufficient to rerun the
experiments without a network connection.

\subsection{A stronger finite context invariant}

A state merger need not work on every possible local tape word. It suffices
that its simulation contract holds on every local word that can occur in the
original blank computation. A bounded-window closure is one sound
approximation: after a head movement, the bit newly exposed at the edge of the
window is allowed to be either symbol. This deliberately forgets correlations
between the path reader and the register program.

We obtain a stronger approximation from the complete source-level coverage.
The inherited symbolic interpreter checks all $4,959$ zero/positive cases for
the $3,812$ source sites, with arbitrary natural register values and gap.
Every ordinary decoder transition exposes a finite symbolic tape prefix.
We enumerate the local words represented by that prefix, allowing all lengths
permitted by a variable unary run and all bit patterns at an opaque register
box. Correlations between variable lengths may be forgotten; this enlarges
the set and is safe. States used by an opaque engine contract, and states
whose self-loops may be skipped by the interpreter, retain their full coarse
blank-window contexts. Finally, all $58,154$ startup transitions are included,
and the exact final clean frame is checked.

The mathematical coverage argument is induction over the startup and the
source-instruction contracts. It is not a new kernel-checked invariant in this
package. In particular, the imported Python interpreter uses the established
register-engine contracts as an oracle. The construction does not infer a
universal property from sampled register values.

\subsection{Moving the alignment boundary}

A conventional local quotient identifies a deleted control $a$ with a retained
control $b$, then demands that a positive child block match one parent step
from either control. We also tried deleting $a$ at its predecessor boundary.
If $a$ has one incoming row, that row may be replaced by an arbitrary
write/direction/target triple. The child is compared with the old two-step
block across $a$, or with a later old endpoint. This allows a different
incoming write or head position without treating control $a$ itself as a
common boundary.

The unique-predecessor search considered $91$ states and $71,344$ replacement
actions. A further search considered all $195$ noninitial working states,
trying incoming rows separately and stopping a deletion when one predecessor
had no bounded witness. With radius-six coarse contexts and a $64$-step
bound, it tested $157,584$ individual actions and found no complete deletion.
This is not an exhaustive search of mutually dependent simultaneous changes
to several predecessor rows.

\subsection{Storing control information in the head or tape}

Two further templates change the representation of an intermediate
configuration. Write a configuration as $(q,h,T)$ and let $\pi(a)=b$, with
$\pi$ otherwise the identity. We exclude deleting the initial state.
Here $T$ is the set of cells containing $1$, and $[P]$ is $1$ when $P$ holds and $0$ otherwise.
Suppose all incoming transitions to $a$ from other states move in direction
$d\in\{-1,1\}$.

For a head-offset encoding use
\[
 \Phi(q,h,T)=\bigl(\pi(q),\ h-2d\,[q=a],\ T\bigr).
\]
Every incoming direction to $a$ is reversed. Its new head position is then
exactly the one specified by $\Phi$. One row of $b$ is synthesized, and the
finite checker requires a positive child block from $\Phi(C)$ to
$\Phi(C')$ for each permitted parent transition $C\to C'$.

For a neighboring-bit encoding use
\[
 \Psi(q,h,T)=
 \begin{cases}
 (b,h,T\mathbin{\triangle}\{h-d\}),&q=a,\\
 (q,h,T),&q\ne a.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here the symmetric difference complements that tape cell. Incoming rows to
$a$ complement their written bit, and one retained row is synthesized.
The child block must restore or correctly transport the tape difference.
These templates can carry a deleted state in physical information that an
ordinary same-head, same-tape quotient does not preserve.

The controls include explicit three-step examples for both encodings.
Changing the restoration bit, or the required helper direction, invalidates
the corresponding witness. For PA196, the recorded head-offset and
neighboring-bit searches each tested $52,590,720$ variants and found none.
The packaged full replay repeats both encodings at radius six with the same negative outcome; its combined receipt also records the positive and adverse controls.

\subsection{Results and limitations}

The expanded one-retained-row search at radius four tested $59,928,960$
patches with a $64$-step bound and found no survivor. At radius six, the
two-retained-row search examined $29,964,480$ first-row actions. Its
unknown-second-row prefilter retained $81,874$ choices; $64,189,216$
complete row pairs were checked, again without a survivor.

These results are restricted negative search outcomes. They do not establish
minimality of the $196$-state machine. A different axiom calculus, path layout,
register encoding, initialization, wider window, longer matching block, or
more extensive simultaneous rewrite remains outside these searches. No
$195$-state table is asserted by this package, and none of the receipts by
itself proves a PA-internal consistency equivalence.
