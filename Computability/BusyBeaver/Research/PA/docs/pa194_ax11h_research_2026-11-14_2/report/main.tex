\section{Scope, evidence, and the requested theorem}

\subsection{Three different kinds of statement}

A standard finite-run equivalence, an arithmetic theorem in PA, and an
arithmetic theorem in iPRA are different claims. A computation checked
externally for every represented source contract is valuable evidence
about a literal machine. A uniform theorem in the intended weak theory
also needs the soundness of every symbolic inference and the relevant
primitive recursive witness transformations. A PA proof of their
correctness cannot simply be relabeled as an iPRA proof.

Throughout this report, ``passed'' refers to an executable check with
the supplied inputs; ``written proof'' refers to the mathematical argument
given here or explicitly attributed; and ``checked in Lean'' refers only
to the specific existing repository result cited. No new Lean or Rocq
verification of either physical consistency theorem is claimed.

Throughout, $\Con(\PA)$ is the exact current endpoint
\file{nativePAConsistency}, the negation of
\file{nativePARefutation}, for the Foundation dependency
\file{32e1a0956a8622fad067328ca1959729a7634428}.
The mapped proof predicate and native syntax are checked explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:foundation-paeq-complete}. Ordinary PAeq and the
Cantor-coded numerical calculi are intermediate proof systems connected
by explicit transformations; their proof codes are not identified.
The target is the standard syntactic consistency sentence for PA. Searching
a smaller intuitionistic arithmetic calculus would change that target.
Likewise, changing the tape model, accepting a supplied initial register
file, or excluding a troublesome blank startup would change the machine
problem. The delivered tables retain the stated binary blank-start model.

\subsection{What the pinned formal development actually supplies}

The repository already proves much more than ordinary sample runs.
Its primary PA194 source coverage verifies all 3,673 primitive sites in
standard finite-run semantics. Its modeled scheduler development proves
halting equivalent to target provability in arbitrary $\IS$ models,
including nonstandard codes and run lengths. Its distinct-label gate
development discharges the formerly open gate premise and proves
$\Con(\PA)\to\neg\Halt$ for that \emph{modeled scheduler}
in native iPRA\cite{pa-status,fairness,gate}.

The machine-independent transfer theorem is also useful: an $\IS$
proof of PA-refutability equivalent to a bounded halting-history
existential, together with the supplied witness certificate, yields the
native iPRA nonhalting/consistency equivalence. Thus the missing machine
argument can be developed in arbitrary $\IS$ models; it need not be
attacked only in arbitrary candidate PRA models\cite{transfer}.

Two important boundaries remain in the pinned work. Its physical
controller and complete source frames are not connected for arbitrary
model-length runs, and its forward PA-to-numerical-calculus compiler is
incomplete over the weak base. The forward audit identifies inductions
quantifying over all smaller derivations and all environments, or over
all realization-table bounds. Those invariants have not been placed in
the permitted induction class\cite{forward}.

The new proofs resolve these boundaries for the present two machines.
The physical and source simulations use explicit primitive recursive
clocks and quantifier-free frame invariants. The logical compiler builds
finite trees of exactly the recursive requests it needs, then proves
coverage and correctness by bounded Boolean prefix invariants. Its
strata include term syntax, guards, value realization, arbitrary formula
realization, arbitrary induction matrices, and the outer PA proof.
The Foundation adapter separately checks the exact current syntax,
all ten proof rules, the induction recognizer, and the one changed
finite arithmetic axiom. No correctness theorem proved only in full PA
is silently reused as a weak-base theorem.

\subsection{What the novelty check establishes}

For every 194-state spanning graph, the supplied checker computes a
canonical action tuple over every spanning initial root, with global
tape reflection and global symbol complement allowed. State names are
discarded, and the write/movement on a terminal edge into the unique
halt label is ignored. Candidate equality is decided by full tuple
comparison, not by hash equality alone.

Neither new candidate matches any of the 82 recognized spanning
194-state classes, and the two candidates do not match each other.
Two additional recognized 194-state normalized tables lack any spanning
root. That invariant alone excludes an isomorphism to either new table,
whose full graph has a spanning root. Their files are included alongside
the 82 representatives, and the checker records this separate reason.

This is a precise corpus-relative structural novelty claim. It does
not prove novelty outside the supplied repository or exclude arbitrary
behavioral equivalences between proof-search machines. The stronger
reason these alternatives are materially different is the change in
their exact command interface and the required halting-word compiler,
described next.

\section{The two new literal constructions}
\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{Design choice and operational difference}

PA194AX11H combines the four-parameter, eleven-entry AX protocol with
the H cache-only halt guard and visible, no-push successful MP.
PA194QAX11H also uses the Q emitter's exchanged sixth head. The
individual ingredients are inherited; the new contribution is their
literal physical composition, exact verification, and the adjusted
command proof. This is not a claim to have invented AX11, H, or premise
exchange\cite{primary,ax11,h,q}.

These changes cannot be accounted for by a state renaming. In the old
sum-guard AX11 command, a store with $(t,h)=(1,0)$ halts under Shift;
the new Shift continues. At $(t,h)=(1,1)$ the old sum guard continues
and the new Shift halts. Relative to H, the new AX consumes an extra
field, stores the gate instead of returning it visibly, and extends the
stack by eleven entries. A correct old proof word is therefore not
automatically a correct new proof word.

\section{Ideas from the other requested developments}

\subsection{ZFC: charge the complete physical implementation}

The K215 ZFC proof packet is useful for command-local operand reading,
context-sensitive marker folds, and a local repair involving two
predecessor-bit cases. It also insists on checking the full runtime
and blank initialization\cite{zfc-k215}. The same discipline is used
here: the AX tail genuinely falls into a full field reader, so the
fourth operand is included in both the command equation and word
encoding; contextual folds are checked simultaneously; startup is a
separate complete-tape certificate.

The ZFC source is not a ready-made reduction of PA194. A physical
saving in one fixed program is not automatically present in another
program's control contexts. Its own distinction between an external
Lean/Rocq equivalence and an internal arithmetic endpoint also warns
against importing a stronger calibration claim than proved
\cite{zfc-status}.

\subsection{DiophantineConsistency: change the proof route, not the target}

The bounded-unary PRA representation result converts a bounded unary
predicate to an open PRA predicate through its primitive recursive
Boolean characteristic. It is about the stated bounded formulas,
not a blanket collapse of arbitrary $\Pi_1$ syntax
\cite{dio-bounded}. The associated $\IS$ transfer works when the
required open-universal PRA representations have been supplied
\cite{dio-transfer}. These ideas motivate the bounded Boolean
compiler invariant in Section~\ref{sec:ax11h-preservation} and the
separation of witness translation from syntax representation.

They do not give an immediate state reduction. A Diophantine search
would have to include the actual evaluator, all encoded constants,
the enumeration mechanism, and blank initialization in its binary
state count. No smaller complete implementation of those components
was obtained here. Replacing an existing PA proof-search engine by
the slogan ``enumerate a consistency polynomial'' is not a counted
construction.

\subsection{ConciseIPRA: derive the interface from fewer primitives}

ConciseIPRA derives richer formula transport and closure interfaces
from smaller primitive mechanisms. This suggests looking for
command composites that remove dedicated return paths while retaining
proof compilation\cite{concise}. AX11's fallthrough is an instance
of that design principle. The new Push, Pop, selector, and
generalization words recover the needed interface after adding H.

The metric in ConciseIPRA is the complexity of a complete proof
checker, not the number of states in these literal binary tables.
Searching a calculus bi-interpretable with iPRA would also address
its own consistency unless a PA interpretation and its exact
implementation were supplied. The useful transferable idea is
interface derivation, not an unsupported substitution of consistency
targets.

\section{Further reductions and formalization}
\label{sec:next}

The completed written proof leaves two useful directions for further
work. A new kernel formalization could encode the explicit finite-demand
query tables, their bounded invariants, the literal simulation rules,
and their composition with the pinned iPRA transfer. The included
source extracts and exact interface maps are intended to make those
proof obligations concrete. Finite Python certificates do not replace
that formalization.

A sub-194 construction must use a mechanism outside the recorded finite
families or a context those screens excluded. Concrete questions include
whether the fourth AX field can share more of an existing decode path,
whether a different complete implicational basis avoids the offset
parameter, and whether a repaired initialization can share a runtime-only
fold. Each proposal needs a complete literal table and both all-source
and full blank-start checks. The matrix countermodels explain why merely
restricting the old offset axiom does not supply a completeness proof.

None of the negative searches establishes that 194 is minimal. The
archive preserves the exact finite search spaces and their exclusions
so that later work can expand them.
