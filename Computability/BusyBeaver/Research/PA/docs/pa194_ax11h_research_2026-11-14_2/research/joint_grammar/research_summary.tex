\section{Additional search and a small propositional obstruction}
A fresh joint search crossed the AX11 protocol with 96 semantic emitter seeds:
two emitter-tree shapes, two equality orientations, the eight combinations of
curried-premise exchange in heads 3, 4 and 6, and three diagonal arguments.
Each of the six unguarded heads could independently permute its parameter
registers during the search. The old gate and its guard were retained.
The original and cached-only-H controllers were searched separately.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
Controller & Proposals & Accepted moves & Sub-194 hits \\
\hline
AX11 & 1,449,458 & 422,923 & 0 \\
AX11H & 1,462,701 & 431,735 & 0
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both 300-second runs retained a best admissible score of 60 effective
reader states, hence 194 total states with their 134 fixed controls.
This is a negative result for the stated search, not a state-count lower
bound. Every potentially qualifying sub-194 fold score was checked for
simultaneous compatibility. Larger seed scores are optimistic matching
bounds, as indicated explicitly in the CSV files. The captured inputs,
source, exact logs, and fixed-proposal replay commands are included.
No prospective lower-state winner was produced for physical verification.

The accompanying three-element logical matrices establish a separate
obstruction: neither the reflexive-only restriction $u=D$ nor the
truth-offset-only restriction $u=0$ of the dominated implicational head
proves weakening by itself. All 27 schema instances and modus-ponens
soundness are checked directly. This does not prove that the full
numerical calculus with its other axiom families loses completeness;
it identifies an additional proof obligation for that proposed
simplification of the offset protocol.
