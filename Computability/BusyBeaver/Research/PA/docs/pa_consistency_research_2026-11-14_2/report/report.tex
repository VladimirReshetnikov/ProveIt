\textbf{Outcome.} This investigation does \emph{not} construct a verified
binary Turing machine with fewer than 194 working states whose blank-tape
nonhalting is PA-provably equivalent to $\Con(\PA)$. The requested state
reduction remains unachieved. No different 194-state table is substituted
for that requirement.

\textbf{What is established here.} The comparison covers all 55 filed PA
research packets and the six incoming archives at the pinned snapshot.
It resolves 49 distinct 194-state transition graphs, separates reported
constructions from auxiliaries and rejected experiments, and identifies
several duplicate submissions and ambiguous names. Fresh structural and
source-aware searches do not yield a certified reduction.

The principal mathematical result is a uniform obstruction: with the
remaining supplied calculus unchanged, replacing the constructor coefficient
four by three and assigning equality \emph{any nonnegative constant offset}
produces a calculus deriving its contradiction code. Merely moving the
colliding equality tag cannot repair the known 193-state experiment.

\textbf{Proof status.} The new obstruction is an ordinary mathematical
proof with finite arithmetic checks and explicitly identified derived-rule
interfaces. The inherited PA calibration was audited as mathematics.
Neither this report nor its Python evidence is a newly compiled end-to-end
Lean or Rocq proof of a machine's consistency equivalence.

\section{Problem, conventions, and result boundary}

The requested object is a deterministic, single-tape binary Turing machine
$M$ with an all-zero, two-way infinite initial tape, such that
\[
 \PA\vdash\bigl(\neg\Halt(M,\mathrm{blank})\leftrightarrow\Con(\PA)\bigr),
 \qquad |Q_M|<194.
\]
Every transition writes a bit and moves exactly one cell left or right.
The terminal halt label is excluded from $Q_M$. There is no external
program tape, preloaded input, additional alphabet symbol, or uncounted
initialization mechanism. These are the conventions of the supplied
research, and they are retained throughout the comparison.

The equivalence concerns arithmetic assertions about the literal table.
External agreement of standard-model truth values would not establish it.
The convenient positive form is
\[
 \PA\vdash\left(\Halt(M,\mathrm{blank})\leftrightarrow
 \exists p\;\Prf_{\PA}(p,\ulcorner0=1\urcorner)\right).
 \tag{1}
\]
A successful construction needs both a proof-theoretic calibration and an
exact connection from that proof search to the chosen physical table.
Neither a short source program nor a long nonhalting simulation supplies
these two facts.

The result boundary is explicit: no new machine satisfying the strict
state target is claimed. The archive contains complete reference tables
for the 49 frontier graph classes, new search inputs and receipts, the
comparison ledgers, the mathematical obstruction, and this article's source.
The reference tables are evidence for comparison, not newly constructed
193-state solutions.

\subsection{Machine identity and designated starts}

For table comparison, a whole graph is canonically serialized by trying
all states that reach the complete working graph, traversing read-zero
before read-one, and choosing the least serialization. Thus changing
control names does not manufacture a new graph. This graph comparison
forgets the designated start; it is deliberately used only for comparison.
The separately retained start and its provenance remain part of every
machine record.

The start distinction matters. Some native-numbered tables use start 74,
while dense tables normally use start zero. An earlier packet renumbered
native states without making zero the start. Reading every table as if it
started at zero would therefore create false discrepancies or incorrect
blank-tape claims. Each of the 49 chosen representatives has a recorded
start and a graph-reachability check from that start. Some auxiliary starts
are inferred from the compiler convention; that inference is labeled and
is not used to approve a consistency theorem.

The full rows are in \file{comparison/frontier194_tables/}; exact hashes,
starts, start provenance, source paths, and descriptions are in
\file{comparison/frontier194.json}. The complete larger graph inventory is
in \file{comparison/all_graph_classes.json}. These files retain the detail
that would make a prose-only comparison hard to reproduce.

\section{What the different designs actually change}
\label{sec:families}

\subsection{The 208- and 196-state ancestry}

The older U208 packets share a unary-power-cover approach and several
contextual decoder reductions. Their main variations concern conditional
tape contexts, finite-history folds, entry contexts, source boundaries,
and register invariants. Many later deliveries compare these same tables
or record failed attempts below 208. They should not be counted as
independent successful designs simply because they arrived in separate
archives. The 61-entry ledger in Appendix~\ref{app:packets} identifies
each delivery and its contribution.

The PA196 development changes the arithmetic presentation, quantifier
coding, equality interface, axiom arrangement, and decoder layout. Its
imported formal work is substantially stronger than a bare table: it binds
source commands and literal execution, and supplies a PA proof in the
direction from nonhalting to PA consistency. The current repository
README explicitly distinguishes that result from the missing full
machine-specific internal equivalence in iPRA~\cite{status}.

The many 196-state research variants are useful because they isolate costs:
changing the order of heads, lowering source depth, shortening a macro,
or replacing a logical interface can each leave the compiled state count
unchanged, or even increase it. The larger inventory includes rejected
experiments and parent tables; the 102 graph classes at 196 states do not
represent 102 separately established consistency theorems.

\subsection{From 195 to the primary 194-state table}

The original 195-state line replaces a longer equality-vacuity interface
by a diagonal clause. For Cantor pairing
\[
 p(a,b)=\frac{(a+b)(a+b+1)}2+b,
\]
the coefficient-four constructors are
\[
 I(A,B)=4p(A,B),\quad M(a,b)=4p(a,b)+1,\quad
 U(a,B)=4p(B,a)+2,\quad E(a,b)=4p(a,b)+3.
 \tag{2}
\]
Code zero is truth and code one is the refutation target. The diagonal
clause $\Gamma(B)=B\to U(B,B)$ is valid because every variable label
occurring in the decoded nonzero formula $B$ is smaller than $B$; zero
is handled separately.

Separating this diagonal clause from the guarded gate changes the command's
stack interface but makes its emitter more compressible. The 195S source
has 190 emitter macro tokens and 3,673 resolved primitive sites. Its
reader allocation has 134 fixed controls, 67 axiom readers and six folds.
The primary PA194 retains that source and improves the compatible fold
set to seven~\cite{primary}:
\[
                134+67-7=194.
\]
The other principal allocation uses $134+68-8=194$. These are distinct
tables implementing the same resolved source graph. The A/D companion
changes physical register placement. It is another graph, not another
logical theory.

The primary table's SHA-256 is
\begin{center}\small\path{4bfea62b17aad7d8c8c5e682fe8942e0a86b93e4351f8e675e32705bdaae007f}\end{center}
Its source instructions, exact startup and two symbolic execution methods
make it the most convenient conservative reference for this investigation.
This choice is about a readily inspectable dependency chain, not a claim
that its runtime dominates every other 194-state machine.

\subsection{Command protocols: C, N, H and symmetric comparison}

The cached-controller variants change what successful modus ponens leaves
in the visible top and cached head, and which value Shift checks for
contradiction. The no-push variants remove a dummy-zero push on successful
modus ponens. These changes are substantive: a proof-word compiler must
be reconstructed for the altered store protocol. Removing a push is not
just an execution-speed optimization when later stack selectors refer
to its location.

The symmetric comparison variants change operand orientation in the
Shift guard and sometimes expose a controller fold. One family tests
only the cached head; another retains the original sum-based guard.
Those two meanings must not be conflated. In particular, the name
\texttt{PA194NS} was used for two different machines: a cache-only design
that is the same table as PA194HS, and a sum-guard design in a different
packet. The frontier ledger disambiguates them as NS(cache) and NS(sum).

Shorter source code does not automatically reduce the fixed control count.
The same physical control can serve an arithmetic routine, a source
reader and a contextual fold. A control that disappears from one macro
can remain necessary in another. This is why several command-level
simplifications remain at 194 states despite reducing primitive source
sites or particular command costs.

\subsection{Logical heads, numeric radix and return instructions}

The Q family exchanges the curried premises of the universal-distribution
head. Its correctness comes from an explicit implicational proof
translation, rather than a claim that the two formulas are literally the
same. The D family changes the separately emitted diagonal formula to
$\Gamma(D)$ and adjusts selectors. The J, ZR, F, A and CSZ families exploit
registers proved zero at selected returns. Such a decrement can implement
an identity jump in the relevant invariant; its correctness cannot be
extrapolated to a dirty store or the blank bootstrap.

The coefficient-five and coefficient-six families change the numerical
formula coding. Their correct arguments include translations of labels
and formulas, handling of unused residues, and a repair for the numerical
side condition on the restricted implicational head. In particular a
recoding need not preserve the usual numeric ordering, so simply applying
it to a dominated-head instance is insufficient. The supplied radix
calibration derives unrestricted implication templates and then reuses
them at the new coefficient~\cite{radix}.

The incoming compositions add Q5, Q6, QH6, QNS, QS and DHS to the older
frontier. Some combine several individually known ideas, but a valid
combined table still needs simultaneous fold and source-interface checks.
For example, coefficient-six and symmetric comparison can demand the
same helper control for different purposes. Savings from incompatible
folds cannot be added arithmetically.

\subsection{Duplicate deliveries and misleading names}

The following are concrete examples resolved by the table comparison:
\begin{itemize}
\item Incoming PA194NQ is the already filed PA194QN, not a new frontier graph.
\item The two incoming QNS deliveries contain the same primary dense table.
\item PA194CSZ and PA194X are identical dense tables.
\item The cache-only PA194NS and PA194HS are the same table, while NS(sum) is different.
\item Multiple PA194C packets re-deliver the same construction; the distinct report or file path is not a distinct machine.
\end{itemize}

Five rejected frontier graphs remain in the archive for accountability.
Three known-zero substitutions fail the actual blank bootstrap and halt
at step 1,361 in their supplied diagnostics. The append-zero transplant
fails the required initialization; the supplied analysis separates halting
starts from blank-ray behavior. The scanner variant fails its source
address contract. A failed runtime contract alone should not be misstated
as a proof about the complete altered machine's eventual halting.

highlighting the newest named examples.

\section{Audit of the PA consistency argument}
\label{sec:proofstatus}

\subsection{What was and was not verified}

The current repository says that neither complete machine-specific
internal reduction has yet been established in iPRA for the primary
PA194, original PA195 and PA196 endpoints~\cite{status}. Standard Lean
finite-run simulation results and a standard calculus/clock equivalence
are valuable components, but do not themselves supply that internal
arithmetical result. This does not refute the ordinary PA argument in
the delivered research articles; the user's requested theorem is in PA,
not the stronger formalization goal in iPRA.

The mathematical audit here examined the primary PA194 calibration and
the inherited original PA195 argument, including their delicate cases.
No invalid inference was found in the reviewed diagonal replacement,
equality-interface recovery, finite-comprehension proof supplier or store
invariant. This is an audit of those arguments, with explicit inherited
dependencies. It is not a fresh kernel check of the entire published
proof-producing compiler.

\subsection{The reusable chain of reasoning}

First, the imported PA196 construction supplies a translation of PA
refutations into the numerical calculus. The diagonal replacement recovers
the exact membership-rewriting, neighborhood and witness interfaces that
this compiler uses. For a desired reverse rewrite $C$, take the formula
$\varphi=N(C)$ and choose its numerical code as a fresh separator label.
Its labels are smaller than the code. Pointed separation and the diagonal
clause then yield the reverse implication. Coincident-label cases are
handled separately; they are not dismissed by an unjustified universal
freshness assumption.

Second, the reverse translation interprets $M(s,z)$ by the binary digit
predicate
\[
 \operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\equiv
 \exists u,v\,[s=(2u+1)2^z+v\;\wedge\;v<2^z].
\]
PA proves $\operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\to z<s$, hence refutes the translation
of $M(0,0)$ under every value of its source variable. For every arithmetic
formula $\theta(z,\vec u)$, PA has a proof of finite comprehension
\[
 \forall n\;\exists s<2^n\;\forall z<n\;
       (\operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\leftrightarrow\theta(z,\vec u)).
 \tag{3}
\]
A fixed induction-proof pattern either keeps the code or adds $2^n$ at
the new bit. Substituting the syntax of $\theta$ into that pattern is a
primitive-recursive proof-producing operation. This supplies proofs;
it does not assume an arithmetical truth predicate or reflection for PA.

Third, the proof-store invariant annotates both visible theorem codes
and every live element of the canonical Cantor stack with proofs of
their translations. The alias $p(0,0)=0$ matters: pushing zero onto zero
does not create an additional live canonical cell. The alias $I(0,0)=0$
also matters: the formula translation uses a proved equivalence at that
case, not an incorrect syntactic-disjointness assertion.

Fourth, the command compiler and enumeration argument must work from
arbitrary incoming annotated stores. The scheduler does not reset the
store between trial words. For a changed number of pushed heads or a
no-push modus-ponens command, the selectors and utility words must be
reproved. A word that succeeds only from an empty store is insufficient.

Finally, exact blank initialization reaches the intended clean frame.
Universal source contracts implement each source step with the right
whole-tape endpoint and a strictly positive duration. Primitive-recursive
finite-trace reasoning and determinism transfer both implications in (1)
to the literal table. A bounded tape run alone cannot replace these
unbounded arguments.

\subsection{Why the other suggested projects do not give a direct shortcut}

The ZFC work supplies useful methods: contextual folds, protected
continuations, exact initialization and all-store proof compilation.
Its consistency target is different. A ZFC consistency machine cannot
simply replace a PA consistency machine in (1)~\cite{zfc}.

ConciseIPRA illustrates how a larger presentation can be replaced by a
small derived interface with explicit proof translations~\cite{ipra}.
Its target is intuitionistic primitive-recursive arithmetic. The upper
calibration above uses full PA induction for arbitrary formulas in (3).
Replacing the searched proof system by iPRA without a new proof translation
would change the requested consistency assertion. The useful transfer is
methodological, not an automatic reduction in machine strength or size.

\section{Fresh searches and why they do not establish 193 states}
\label{sec:search}

\subsection{Physical state elimination beyond exact row matching}

The new physical screens examine sufficient finite simulation conditions.
They include an ordinary common-action merge; a state elimination that
changes the simulated head position by two cells and correspondingly
reverses entry movement; and variants that complement the predecessor
bit written on entry, restoring it during the simulation. A further
variant permits different survivor first actions when both original and
transformed entry contracts are met. These enlarge the search beyond
checking equality of two rows.

The full 49-class frontier was screened under bounded-window conditions.
The common-action, displaced-head and entry-bit screens used radius seven
and a 180-step local path limit; the more general survivor-action variant
used radius six and 180 steps. None returned a candidate. These results
are negative outcomes for named sufficient conditions, not a state lower
bound. A correct reduction that needs more distant tape information,
a different invariant or a nonlocal relation is outside those tests.

For PA194, H6, QH6 and QNS, the investigation also generated source-based tape
contexts from universal contracts, regular unary-file prefixes, self-loop
interiors and exact startup. The PA194, H6, QH6 and QNS context sets contain respectively
72,935, 74,314, 77,302 and 76,027 radius-eight windows. Five screen families were
run with 300-step local limits; no candidate was returned. The complete 328-screen finite replay, inputs and
positive controls are retained. The context derivation is experimental evidence
for these search domains; no successful reduction proof depends on it.

\subsection{Rotations of the full source decoder}

A separate experiment enumerated 23 single physical decoder rotations
whose associated source trees have exactly the same resolved primitive
control-flow graph and whose prefix decoders pass their tree relation.
Five also pass all 4,780 universal source outcomes and exact blank
initialization. The other 18 fail to close at least one inherited symbolic
contract. Several failures reach a wrong register effect, exposing a
second physical use of a control that the prefix decoder relation did not
capture.

This is a useful diagnostic distinction. Equality of the abstract source
graph and a correct reader-prefix map are necessary evidence, but cannot
justify mutating a state that also acts in arithmetic or return code.
The five passing rotations were subjected to the additional local screens,
including strengthened source contexts; none yielded a state reduction.
They are research inputs, not a replacement answer at 194 states.

\subsection{Alternative logical presentations}

The first quantifier head
$O(a,H)=(\exists_a U(a,H))\to H$ packages more proof-theoretic strength
than its superficial shape suggests. The Boolean countermodel in the
preceding section shows why replacing it by positive Brouwer or direct
specialization alone loses the established interface. A diagnostic
Brouwer-only association search made 5,798,229 proposal attempts in a
360-second run and reached reader objective 63. No calibration is claimed
for that diagnostic search.

The corrected Brouwer-plus-explosion search uses 288 templates, combines
three placements and three parameter choices for the extra explosion
head with the existing equality/premise variants, and has 194 macro tokens.
Its 300-second run made 4,904,863 proposal attempts and accepted 1,511,235
moves. Its best scanner-admissible reader objective was 65; adding the
134-control core gives an objective of 199. This is a heuristic grammar
objective, not a validated 199-state machine. No sub-194 hit occurred.
The finite proof equivalence explains why the logical repair is sound;
it does not turn a grammar score into a physical construction.

Further unbundling of the guarded gate into membership vacuity and a
separate negative separation head is propositionally possible. But the
old guard clears only the top entry. Naively deleting the combining
constructor leaks an invalid vacuity head onto the stack when the guard
fails. A concrete repair removes that extra stack entry on the disabled
branch. The improved direct pricing has 206 raw controls and six nominal
matching folds, giving objective 200, with unresolved scanner constraints.
No accepted physical machine is inferred from it.

\subsection{A disjoint mixed-radix repair}

The uniform obstruction does not rule out a nonconstant atom encoding.
One clean alternative has
\[
 I(A,B)=3p(A,B),\quad U(a,B)=3p(B,a)+2,
 \quad M(a,b)=6p(a,b)+1,\quad E(a,b)=6p(a,b)+4.
 \tag{4}
\]
The implication image has residue zero modulo three, the universal image
residue two, and the two atom images partition residue one modulo three.
Thus the tags are disjoint, unlike the rejected constant-offset shortcut.
Code zero retains its usual alias, and recursive formula children have
strictly smaller nonzero codes. The usual syntactic recoding strategy can
therefore be applied, with a separate treatment of the dominated
implicational interface.

Two concrete source profiles were generated and their free-algebra
emitter audits passed. A dedicated coefficient-six constructor shares
its arithmetic prefix with coefficient three but adds five reader
controls: its fixed budget is 139 and its emitter has 193 macro tokens.
A second implementation uses coefficient three followed by doubling the
top for atom formation. It adds three controls, with fixed budget 137
and 206 macro tokens.

Their separate 180-second grammar screens made 3,245,431 and 2,905,346
proposal attempts. The best optimistic allocations were respectively
$139+81-12=208$ and $137+80-12=205$. The twelve nominal folds were not
promoted to a jointly verified physical quotient. These are soundly typed
source alternatives whose straightforward implementations do not reach
the state target, not accepted new 205- or 208-state machines.

\section{Assessment and remaining mathematical work}

The existing 194-state constructions are a family of carefully related
implementations, rather than independent architectures with obviously
additive savings. Their diversity is useful for identifying which costs
are logical, which belong to the command protocol, and which arise from
physical decoder reuse. The comparison resolves duplicates and records
these dependencies rather than treating every new name as progress in
state count.

The strongest new mathematical exclusion concerns the entire
constant-offset repair family at radix three. Its scope is exact: all
nonnegative equality offsets fail while the other stated constructors,
heads and rules are retained. It does not exclude radix-three ideas with
a different interface, mixed encodings such as (4), or a completely
redesigned PA proof checker. It also says nothing about an absolute lower
bound of 194 states.

A successful next construction must still exhibit a complete table with
at most 193 working controls, identify the exact source calculus and store
protocol, prove both syntactic reductions to PA, and connect that source
to the literal table including startup. The finite search failures here
leave room for stronger invariants, coordinated changes to the fixed
controller, different arithmetic routines, and genuinely different proof
presentations. They do not justify declaring any of these approaches
impossible.

The strict requested construction remains open in this investigation.
The delivered material is intended to make the completed comparison,
new obstruction and failed reduction attempts inspectable and reusable,
without overstating their outcome.
