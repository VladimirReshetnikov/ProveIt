\section{Moving the diagonal clause within the guarded gate}
\label{sec:gamma-relocation}
Write $\bot$ for the distinguished formula code $1$ and
$\neg X=(X\to\bot)$.  Use the numerical constructors, six retained heads,
dominated propositional head, modus ponens, and closed generalization of the
PA195 calibration.  In particular, all the following are syntactic formula
codes; the parameters range over natural numbers, including coincident labels.
Put
\[
 \Gamma(B)=B\to U(B,B),\qquad R(a,b,c)=V(a,b,c)\to Q(a,b,c),
 \qquad G(a,b,c)=\neg R(a,b,c).
\]
Here $V,Q$ are exactly the PA195 formulas.  The old gate and two alternatives
are
\begin{align*}
 F_c(a,b,c)&=\neg\bigl(\Gamma(c)\to R(a,b,c)\bigr),\\
 F_a(a,b,c)&=\neg\bigl(\Gamma(a)\to R(a,b,c)\bigr),\\
 F_b(a,b,c)&=\neg\bigl(\Gamma(b)\to R(a,b,c)\bigr).
\end{align*}
All three use precisely the guard $c\ne a\land c\ne b$.  There is no
condition $a\ne b$.  A failed guard returns code zero, as before.

\paragraph{Fixed propositional proof operations.}
For arbitrary formulas $X,Y$, the common propositional fragment supplies
\begin{align*}
 \pi_1(X,Y)&:\neg(X\to Y)\to X,\\
 \pi_2(X,Y)&:\neg(X\to Y)\to\neg Y,\\
 \iota(X,Y)&:X\to\bigl(\neg Y\to\neg(X\to Y)\bigr).
\end{align*}
These need no semantic completeness assumption.  Here are finite natural
 deduction templates for them, translated by the existing syntactic deduction
operation.  For $\pi_1$, assume $f:\neg(X\to Y)$ and $n:\neg X$.
Under a further assumption $x:X$, $n(x)$ is absurd, hence implies $Y$;
discharge $x$ to obtain $X\to Y$ and apply $f$.  Discharging $n$ yields
$\neg\neg X$, and double-negation elimination yields $X$.
For $\pi_2$, assume $f$ and $y:Y$, form the constant implication
$X\to Y$, apply $f$, and discharge $y$.
For $\iota$, assume $x:X$, $n:\neg Y$, and $u:X\to Y$; the two
applications $u(x)$ and $n(u(x))$ give absurdity.  Discharge $u,n,x$.
Explosion, double-negation elimination, and deduction are the unchanged
finite proof-producing operations in the pinned PA196 propositional
infrastructure (\texttt{Logic/Deduction.lean} and
\texttt{Logic/Connectives.lean}).  Consequently these are fixed
primitive-recursive proof templates, whose checker equations PA proves.

\paragraph{Uniform translation of axiom instances.}
Let $\mathcal C_c,\mathcal C_a,\mathcal C_b$ be the calculi with the respective
gates and otherwise identical rules and heads.  Projection by $\pi_2$ gives
$G(a,b,c)$ from the gate of any one of them.  Every diagonal clause is
uniformly available in each calculus:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{calculus}&\text{gate instance}&\text{consequence by }\pi_1\\\hline
 \mathcal C_c&F_c(B+1,B+1,B)&\Gamma(B)\\
 \mathcal C_a&F_a(B,B,B+1)&\Gamma(B)\\
 \mathcal C_b&F_b(B,B,B+1)&\Gamma(B).
 \end{array}
\]
Both guard inequalities follow from the PA theorem $B+1\ne B$.
For clarity, the proof replacements for a guarded instance are as follows.
To obtain $F_a(a,b,c)$ in $\mathcal C_c$, project $G(a,b,c)$ from
$F_c(a,b,c)$, project $\Gamma(a)$ from $F_c(a+1,a+1,a)$, and combine
by $\iota(\Gamma(a),R(a,b,c))$.  Replace $a$ by $b$ for $F_b$.
Conversely, in either relocated calculus, project $G(a,b,c)$ from its
current gate and obtain $\Gamma(c)$ from its auxiliary gate at
$(c,c,c+1)$.  Apply $\iota(\Gamma(c),R(a,b,c))$ to recover
$F_c(a,b,c)$.

Translate a finite derivation recursively: preserve every common head,
propositional axiom, modus-ponens node, and generalization node, and replace
each gate node by the displayed finite proof.  The translation uses only
formula-code constructors, addition of one, and finite proof-tree
composition.  It is primitive recursive.  PA checks its output by induction
on the input proof tree, using the same propositional-template correctness
lemmas and the successor inequality.  If $T_{ij}$ denotes the resulting
translation, then, for $i,j\in\{a,b,c\}$,
\[
 \mathrm{PA}\vdash\forall p\,\forall A\;
 \bigl(\operatorname{Prf}_{\mathcal C_i}(p,A)
       \longrightarrow
       \operatorname{Prf}_{\mathcal C_j}(T_{ij}(p),A)\bigr).
\]
In particular the calculi have PA-provably equivalent refutation
predicates.  Combining this with the supplied PA195 calibration transfers
the same $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$ equivalence to either relocated
source calculus.  No assertion of PA's own consistency is used.

\paragraph{The machine-command parameter range.}
The actual arithmetic command is not an unconstrained oracle for a triple.
On a store $(t,w,d,h)$, its parameters are
\[
 \operatorname{AX}(c,x,y):\quad a=h+y,\quad b=d+x,
\]
and its result resets $d=h=0$ while pushing the same nine entries.
These are the equations of \texttt{PA196/Commands/Store.lean}; they remain
unchanged by moving the diagonal clause.  An initial
$\operatorname{AX}(0,0,0)$ always returns a store with $d=h=0$; AX itself
has no halting branch.  The original universal refutation compiler already
uses this reset before its compiled proof
(\texttt{PA196/Commands/Compiler.lean}, theorem
\texttt{haltWord\_universal}).  From the reset state every triple is
reachable by $\operatorname{AX}(c,b,a)$.
Thus the auxiliary relocated instance $(B,B,B+1)$ is produced with
$c=B+1,x=y=B$, and the auxiliary old instance $(B+1,B+1,B)$ with
$c=B,x=y=B+1$.  No negative increment or assumption about an arbitrary
live value of $d,h$ is hidden in either witness.  The same selector positions,
head count, failed-guard behavior, and normal reset protocol apply.

\paragraph{Scope of the finite audit.}
The accompanying independent free-algebra check verifies the complete
formula expression and pushed stack for all three 191-token words, rather
than checking a numerical sample.  An independent interval resolver agrees
with the compiler on each 3,700-site source graph and its 4,815 primitive
contracts.  The guard suffix is unchanged.  The 256 numerical range controls
and eight Boolean controls in its receipt are diagnostic checks; the uniform
range and proof-translation arguments are the ones above.  These results
justify a source-calculus search.  They do not certify a smaller physical
transition table, its scanner contracts, or a new kernel-checked endpoint.
The four finite deduction trees (including the antecedent-exchange template)
are supplied as \texttt{proof\_templates.json}.  The small explicit-rule
checker \texttt{check\_templates.py} checks their closed conclusions and a
negative control, including when Python assertions are disabled.  It uses
only implication introduction/elimination, falsity elimination, and classical
double-negation elimination.  This is a concrete certificate for the fixed
propositional part, not a replacement for the inherited numerical proof
compiler or its arithmetization.
