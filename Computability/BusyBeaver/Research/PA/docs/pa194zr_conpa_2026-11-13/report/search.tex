\section{Attempts to cross the 194-state frontier}
\label{sec:search}
No delivered search produced a verified machine with fewer than 194
working states. This is an experimental outcome, not a lower bound.
The searches separate syntactic size estimates, local tape-context
conditions, complete arbitrary-register contracts, and the actual blank
initialization. Passing an earlier gate is never reported as passing a
later one.

\subsection{Compiler and axiom-presentation searches}
The compiler searches vary the binary association of the axiom-emitter
word, its shared reader dictionary, and context-sensitive reader folds.
They also test permutations of emitted heads, parameter roles, selected
macro expansions, and the new zero-return control. The frozen trusted
record accounts for 90,819,000 mutation iteration slots. A slot is not
necessarily a distinct grammar or a complete candidate machine, and most
slots never reach whole-machine validation. The best resulting total
state-count score remains 194.

Two logical variants exchange the antecedents of $h_3$ or $h_6$. The
exchange transformation has an explicit propositional proof and is a
legitimate candidate for preserving the proof calculus. Its compiler
scores nevertheless remain above 194 in these runs. The accompanying
exchange-template checker is retained as exploratory work; neither
variant is substituted into the delivered machine.

A dynamic-normalization defect was discovered during exploration:
normalization preserved the emitted word immediately but reused leaf
position identifiers that a later mutation treated as distinct.
An apparent sub-194 result from the affected experiment was discarded.
The corrected optimizer renumbers the leaves and checks exact word
identity after normalization. The trusted accounting excludes all runs
affected by that defect, unlogged interrupted tails, and runs based on
the blank-invalid $R_6$ return variant. The final two bounded runs were
stopped at logged prefixes, with 4,743,000 and 4,503,000 accounted slots.
This provenance is necessary to interpret the negative record honestly.

\subsection{Physical reductions of the accepted machine}
The final PA194ZR table was also subjected to direct reduction attempts.
Native control 302 remains reachable through the zero branch of control
113 even after the nine source returns have been redirected. The
successful source transformation therefore does not by itself delete 302.

The context collections contain 272,471 and 3,129,859 contexts
respectively. These are finite inductive abstractions of tape behavior,
and a negative result can exclude only the tested actions under the
specified abstraction. It is not a proof that a different simulation
relation, source tree, scanner, or arithmetic kernel cannot save a state.

Earlier scanner experiments shorten the seven-zero delimiter test to
six, redirect scanner states, or merge scanner controls. They fail
universal contracts. The six-zero version fails 95 contracts: delimiter
safety cannot be inferred merely from a tally of source-address
substrings. The record includes the important $R_6$ negative control:
all source contracts pass, but the literal blank run halts at step 1,361.
It is a rejected design, never an alternative consistency machine.

\subsection{What remains open}
The accepted construction changes the shared return convention while
keeping the same total state count. Its two row changes could provide a
different starting point for future reader allocation or scanner work,
but no further saving is established here. A successful smaller machine
would still need a complete source binding, arbitrary-frame physical
contracts, exact blank initialization, and a PA refutation calibration.
The present evidence neither proves minimality at 194 nor rules out
a substantially different design.
