# Comparison of the pinned PA consistency-machine corpus

## Scope, identity, and evidence

This comparison fixes ProveIt at commit `4be5c6e3a10292a7466f5d3687e3b39615ff049b`. It covers all 61 filed packets under `Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/PA/docs` and all 11 current ZIP archives under `docs/incoming`: 72 deliveries in total. The packet ledger preserves every delivery, including negative and source-only investigations. The machine census independently checks literal tables and recognized explicit JSON table encodings throughout the PA tree and recursively inside archives. It does not execute arbitrary table generators or recover tables printed only in mathematical prose. That recognition boundary is stated so that “all” does not silently become a claim about every possible encoding.

The scan verifies 10,171 tracked candidate files against their pinned Git blob identities and inspects 15,220 file/member payloads. It encounters 25 distinct archive payloads through 37 references and recognizes 3,422 transition-table occurrences, 752 normalized table identities, and 638 complete one-halt action-graph classes admitting a spanning root. Six normalized identities have no spanning root and remain separately recorded. There are no archive-reading errors. The 535 entries in the repository's historical machine catalogue were also independently rehashed and all matched. The historical 7,130-table count describes original deliveries before duplicate removal; it has a different denominator from the fresh census.

For table identity, the census preserves both read branches, writes, and head directions, but forgets control names and the designated start. It canonically traverses from every state that spans the full graph. A machine theorem still requires its actual specified start. In particular, some native tables were renumbered from zero without making zero the correct blank start; treating every dense zero as the start would fabricate differences. The separate novelty test additionally permits tape reflection, global bit exchange, and arbitrary final writes/moves into halt. Bit exchange is an enlarged structural comparison, not an assertion that an all-zero blank tape remains unchanged.

The present frontier contains 82 distinct 194-state graphs. Their historical evidence categories are 40 reported principal constructions, seven supplementary constructions, five source-validated research rotations, 25 other auxiliaries, and five explicitly rejected designs. These are not 82 independently reproved consistency theorems. The project README distinguishes the research packets' ordinary mathematical PA calibrations from unfinished end-to-end Lean and iPRA reductions. A finite startup run, a Python certificate, and a theorem conditional on missing reductions do not independently establish the desired consistency equivalence. Conversely, an unfinished stronger iPRA formalization does not by itself refute a written PA argument.

## The larger predecessors and the 194-state budget

The U209/U208 lineage supplies the earlier enumerating register machine, unary register-file routines, finite-history sharing, and conditional/entry-context folds. Several apparently different U208 deliveries reproduce the same transition graph. The PA196 line changes the numerical logical interface as well as physical allocation: notably, the universal constructor reverses the arguments of Cantor pairing. Its imported development supplies important formal source and simulation components and a PA argument in one direction; the later ordinary two-way calibration must be distinguished from those components. The 105 currently recognized 196-state classes include research inputs and failures, not 105 separately established consistency results.

The original PA195, separate-head PA195S/C, combined-diagonal PA195DC, and scanner variant M195S8 are also distinct. Separating the diagonal-vacuity head from the guarded gate gives a compressible 190-token axiom word and changes the proof compiler's stack interface. Primary PA194 uses 134 fixed controls, 67 raw axiom readers and seven compatible history folds: `134 + 67 - 7 = 194`. A second implementation uses 68 readers and eight folds. Several later designs use 135 fixed controls and 59 effective readers. A shorter source word or fewer primitive source sites therefore need not reduce the literal state count: multiplier helpers, scanner constraints and incompatible folds can consume the saving.

The complete census retains larger parents, rejected tables and small diagnostic fragments. Named mathematical and operational designs are separately listed in the packet and frontier ledgers, so a class count is never substituted for a proof comparison.

## The independent design axes

The principal families share the same general proof-search architecture but vary four interfaces. First is the numerical calculus and axiom emitter. The primary arithmetic uses implication, membership, quantification and equality tags with coefficient four. M5/M6 and their descendants change that coefficient to five or six. This changes formula arithmetic throughout execution and needs hereditary recoding of both formulas and variable labels, treatment of unused residues, the exceptional zero code, and the dominated-basis side condition. Merely changing the multiplier is insufficient.

The Q axis exchanges the two curried premises of universal distribution. A fixed implicational exchange proof gives primitive-recursive translations in both directions without changing conclusions. The D axis emits the separate diagonal head at `Gamma(D)` instead of `Gamma(A)`, changing its selector. No accepted 194-state `Gamma(H)` construction is described in the pinned frontier. The letters H and H6 instead identify controller behavior. Earlier searches did explore other membership-premise exchanges, relation orientations, tag permutations and Gamma choices; those ideas cannot be described as newly proposed merely because their best saved physical realizations were larger.

Second is the command-store protocol. Original successful MP pushes a dummy zero. N omits that push and leaves the consequent visible in the top and offset registers. Its proof compiler must reserve a zero explicitly when a later Pop would otherwise consume the arbitrary underlying stack. C instead places the consequent in the cached head and clears top and offset. H combines N's visible result with a Shift test of the cached head alone. Original Shift tests the sum of visible top and cached head. These differences affect the final refutation word: a cache-only Shift cannot simply detect a contradiction still held only in the visible top.

Third is the destructive comparison algorithm. CS and HS reverse comparator arguments and avoid a restoration loop; SUMS and the sum-guard NS use symmetric comparison while retaining the sum test. QNS and QS combine that implementation with the exchanged quantifier head, with and without the dummy MP push. Fourth is source and decoder organization: J, A, F, ZR, CZ/CSZ and HA/HAZ replace selected jumps by decrements of registers proved zero, change register placement, or reassociate source trees. These transformations require their own invariants and complete startup checks. A correct clean-frame source identity need not hold during initialization.

The six new principal classes beyond the earlier 76-class audit are AX11, H5, QH5, QHS, QN5 and QN6. Most fill previously absent combinations of established logical, arithmetic and controller mechanisms. QHS changes the repeated Shift algorithm by removing a restoration loop. The supplied all-store compiler deliberately chooses the new operand zero for completeness while allowing arbitrary values in soundness. This is separate from a numerical-calculus change.

## Why the 193-state material does not settle the task

At the current pin there are 53 recognized 193-state graphs. Older reports saying “two” described older corpora. Their current descendants belong to a small number of unsuccessful mechanisms.

One is the coefficient-three shortcut. With the remaining supplied heads unchanged, numerical constructor collisions allow a derivation of contradiction code one. The original rejected example has a 73,393-step primitive refutation. A later report proves an obstruction for every nonnegative constant equality offset within that specific unchanged-head family. This is a logical failure even though the physical implementation passes its source checks; it is not a general theorem against all three-residue syntaxes or all 193-state machines.

The second is Runtime193: merge native 301 into 88, use `88/0 = 0 L 51`, and change `51/0 = 0 R 122`. All 4,780 primary-source outcomes pass, but no calibrated blank initialization results. The newest material adds AX11 and QN6 compositions, an append-zero experiment, a ZFC marker-relay transplant, and 49 quotient-transplant tables representing 47 further graphs. A fresh independent replay in this investigation tries every start of all 52 runtime-experiment graphs: all 10,036 runs resolve to 106 literal halts and 9,930 outward rays, with no cutoffs. Each ray has an exact finite prefix followed by a constant-state scan through a provably blank half-line. The coefficient-three graph is excluded from that replay because its obstruction is logical. The relay additionally has nine recorded source-outcome failures.

Prior coordinated startup searches are extensive but bounded. The AX11 packet checks a corrected pool of 327 source-context-screened row edits in encountered pairs and triples across starts, with 516,610 triple/start cases and over 50 billion transitions. No complete source frame is found, and 255,752 runs hit a bound. Those cutoffs do not prove nontermination, and those searches do not exclude other pools, four edits, other source grammars, or different initialization invariants. Likewise, the exact rational dual in the selector study proves a bound only for its frozen direct-selector/disjoint-fold family.

## The new PA194B and PA194G designs and transferable methods

PA194B changes the physical marker dispatch rather than the calculus, AX stack interface, command scheduler or formula coefficient. It factors two reader contexts through a dedicated selector while restoring the register-engine row borrowed by the failed Runtime193 construction. Its proof obligation is an exact local simulation and an inductive blank-start transport. This directly addresses why a source-correct reduced runtime may still break initialization, although retaining the selector leaves 194 controls.

Its structural difference has an especially short certificate. Every one of the 82 prior 194-state graphs has exactly 21 self-loop transitions, each on a different control. PA194B has 22 such transitions on 22 controls. Renaming, a different start, reflection, global bit exchange and changes only to terminal-edge effects all preserve this count. Thus none of those cosmetic transformations identifies it with an existing graph. The independent full canonical-tuple comparison reaches the same conclusion. Materiality also requires that the changed dispatch be used in actual execution; a fresh count alone would not justify changing an unused row and calling it a new algorithm.

PA194G changes the source interface instead. It combines the inherited Q head, no-push MP and original sum Shift with a new saved generalization `U(c,h)`, where `c` is the explicit AX operand and `h` is the unshifted incoming cached theorem. The old saved head was `U(d,h)`, using the offset register. This permits `Gen_i = Push; S; AX_{i,0,0}; S^10`, omitting the previous offset-setting command. The new all-store compiler and unchanged-calculus soundness argument are separate obligations from the physical source contracts. Its actual 194-state graph has 88 working controls of indegree one; the prior 82 graphs have counts 87, 89, 90, 91, 92, 93, or 94. This proves structural novelty even after forgetting all transition actions. PA194G's 21 self loops also distinguish it from PA194B. Both new calibrations compose with the identified inherited written PA proofs; neither is a new end-to-end kernel formalization.

The ZFC work supplies genuine implementation ideas: caller-history selection, tape-marker relays, protected return tails, source-frame contracts and all-store proof-word compilation. It does not permit substituting Con(ZFC) for Con(PA), or subtracting a ZFC state saving before checking the PA entry contexts and initialization. ConciseIPRA supplies the method of deriving a small interface by explicit proof translations, but its checker-size objective and iPRA strength differ from this literal binary PA-refutation search. Both suggest optimizing logic, proof certificates, source grammar and tape layout together; neither supplies a free state-count saving.
