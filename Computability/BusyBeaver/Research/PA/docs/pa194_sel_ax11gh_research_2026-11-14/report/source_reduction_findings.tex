\subsection{Source-level reductions examined in this session}

The six ordinary AX-head intervals are
$[18,35)$, $[35,54)$, $[54,70)$, $[70,86)$, $[86,102)$ and $[102,121)$
in the 190-token emitter. A direct free-constructor check shows that each
maps an arbitrary visible top and stack $(t,w)$ to $(h_j,[t;w])$ while
preserving the parameter registers $D,H,A$. Consequently their order can
be changed with a corresponding finite update of selector indices.
This provides an exact correctness condition for the parallel decoder
search, independent of its scoring heuristic. Earlier packets had also
searched head order and parameter permutations; this check does not claim
that the general search direction is new.

There are additional safe variants of the third and fourth heads. Each
has the shape $P\to(Q\to R)$ and can be replaced by $Q\to(P\to R)$.
The supplied restricted-rho/MP exchange certificate applies in both
directions, without treating the designated contradiction atom as an
extra logical constant. The two modified intervals each still have
16 macro tokens. Exact alternative 190-token words for either or both
exchanges were supplied to the decoder search. A changed formula word
is not itself a smaller machine; a complete table, compatible fold proof,
source contracts and blank startup remain necessary.

A first-head shortcut was rejected before use. Write
$X=U(D,N(U(D,H)))$ and let the old head be $I(N(X),H)$.
The tempting replacement $I(N(H),X)$ is equivalent if the symbol used
by $N$ is genuine falsity. The numerical calculus initially treats code
one as a distinguished atom, however, and the old head is what supplies
explosion. The earlier split-head packet had already exposed this exact
obstruction. With material implication, every membership atom true,
every equality atom false, and quantifiers interpreted as the identity,
the proposed replacement and all retained heads are valid while
$1\to3$ is false. Therefore the replacement alone is not a justified
equivalent calculus. Its exploratory emitter file is marked rejected;
no machine claim depends on it.

Likewise replacing the modal-symmetry head
$\exists_D\forall_D H\to H$ by the shorter
$\forall_D H\to H$ is not licensed by the existing completeness proof.
That proof uses symmetry to extend atomic membership vacuity through
negation before recovering equality substitution. Appealing to the
resulting substitution to justify the replacement would be circular.
No new separation-based derivation closing that gap was found.

These are limitations of the transformations examined, not a lower bound
of 194 states for PA-consistency machines.
