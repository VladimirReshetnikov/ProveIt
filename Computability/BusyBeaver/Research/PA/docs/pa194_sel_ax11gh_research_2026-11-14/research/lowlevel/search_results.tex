\section{Additional completed attempts to remove a state}
\label{sec:additional-reductions}

The following searches supplement the strict one-step contraction experiments.
They found no accepted machine below 194 working controls. Their negative
results apply only to the explicitly bounded synthesis families described
here; none is a lower bound on PA-consistency machines.

\subsection{Sharing one selector between two control pairs}

The primary table has 16 pairs of controls with two useful properties:
one control is always entered immediately after a right move that writes 0,
the other is always entered after a right move that writes 1, and exactly
one of their outgoing rows is common. There are 116 choices of two disjoint
pairs. A proposed reduction replaces their four controls by two retained
dispatch controls and one shared history selector, recycling one original
label and deleting the other.

The expanded synthesis tests both retained-label choices in each pair,
either recycled selector label, and all four assignments of the bit written
by the two dispatchers. In particular, the exceptional read symbols of the
two pairs need not agree: the original scanned symbol can itself distinguish
the two families. Each row of the shared selector may write either bit,
move either way, and target any retained working control or halt. Returning
to a patched control is permitted. Both rows are checked jointly after
partial-row filtering, including cases that reach the other, initially
unspecified selector row.

The complete run uses a radius-four tape window and at most 120 physical
steps per replacement fragment. Every bit assignment compatible with the
forced predecessor witness is tested; the exterior tape is arbitrary and
may not be read before the required endpoint is reached. There are
3,712 structural configurations, 4,109,696 single-row proposals and 8,016
joint row combinations. None survives. This includes the more restricted
two-pair shared-selector attempt, but does not cover arbitrary changes to
the tape representation or to other controls.

\subsection{Allowing a positive old prefix rather than one old step}

Requiring a replacement fragment to end exactly after one old step can be
unnecessarily restrictive. The extended criterion allows it to meet the
projection of any positive prefix of the old execution. Its parameters
$U,V$ bound the consumed old and new steps respectively:
\[
  1\leq u\leq U,\qquad 1\leq v\leq V,
  \qquad M^{u}(c)\sim N^{v}(\pi(c)).
\]
Here $\pi$ identifies the removed control with the retained control, and
$\sim$ requires the same head, complete tape and projected control at a
nonterminal endpoint. Terminal write and move decorations are irrelevant
once both computations halt. The candidate may revisit its retained control;
both replacement rows are synthesized jointly. Direct halt targets are
included.

The context set is a finite fixed point obtained from the blank initial
window, admitting either bit whenever a move exposes a previously untracked
boundary cell. This is an overapproximation of the actual blank execution.
Both local traces remain inside the prescribed window until their matched
endpoint, apart from the final head move. Equality on the window therefore
gives equality on the unchanged arbitrary exterior tape.

If such a certificate were accepted, its positive progress on both sides
would compose into a bounded-stuttering correspondence along the blank
executions. The finite window invariant and finite local checks would
support elementary arithmetization, with linear rescaling of halting
witness bounds. This observation motivates the search model; it is not a
new proof-assistant theorem and does not make the synthesis model complete.

The primary machine and the composite using providers $(98,19)$ and
$(8,59)$ were tested at radius eight with $(U,V)=(32,160)$. All five new
single-selector variants and all six two-selector combinations were also
tested at radius six with $(U,V)=(16,120)$.

Every row in this table has zero survivors. A smaller preliminary run on

than a timeout on an unfinished search.

The new matching rule has a positive control. Two old controls make
opposite two-step excursions---right then left, or left then right---and
reach the same unchanged-tape checkpoint. The replay explicitly requires
the contraction using two old steps and two new steps. Thus the positive
test exercises the broadened acceptance rule, not merely an ordinary
one-step duplicate-control elimination.

\subsection{Grammar and axiom-emitter experiments}

Four searches each completed a 600-second run. The baseline adds
occurrence-specific reassociation to the earlier transformations of all
occurrences of a shared subtree. The other three inputs exchange the
antecedents in the third numbered seed, the fourth numbered seed, or both.
These are instances of the purely implicational equivalence
\[
 (P\to(Q\to R))\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad(Q\to(P\to R)).
\]
They also allow independent bijective renaming of the three parameters in
each of the six numbered seed blocks. The frozen input words, macro
attributes, objective implementation and random seeds are included.

Adding the 134 fixed controls gives objectives 194, 196, 196 and 199.
These are compiler search scores: no newly accepted consistency machine
is claimed for the changed emitters, and their complete physical source
contracts were not audited. The simultaneous-fold objective has exact
local compatibility checks, whereas its scanner constraints remain a
screening model. The baseline retained the known 194-state objective and
did not improve it.

A proposed contrapositive replacement of the first seed was discarded.
Treating its distinguished falsity code as ordinary Boolean false would
silently assume explosion, which the replacement can lose in the actual
primitive implicational calculus. It is an already known rejected route
and is not counted as an accepted logical transformation. Interrupted
seeding experiments are likewise excluded from the completed counts above.

\subsection{Portable evidence}

The accompanying reduction-research folder contains source code, exact
inputs, original license notices and machine-readable receipts, including
\texttt{receipts/search\_results.json}. Its \texttt{replay.py} checks the
finite enumeration counts and empty survivor lists; the default quick
replay and the two-step positive control have passed. The optional grammar
replay uses explicit iteration caps and the original random seeds to replay
the recorded proposal prefixes without depending on the current computer's
wall-clock speed. Timing is not an acceptance criterion.
