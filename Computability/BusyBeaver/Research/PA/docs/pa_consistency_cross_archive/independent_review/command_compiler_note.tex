\section{A command compiler for the two cross-grafted emitters}
\label{sec:graft-command-compiler}

The diagonal cross-graft retains the combined H-only Shift and MP-in-H
commands and replaces AX by the diagonal-vacuity emitter. The separate-head
cross-graft additionally pushes the diagonal formula as an axiom head. It
is necessary to adjust the proof-command words in the latter case. This
section gives one argument covering both constructions.

Write a store as $\langle t,w,d,h\rangle$ and write
$[a_1,\ldots,a_k]::_Cw$ for successive Cantor cons operations, with $a_1$
at the top. The operations $\operatorname{hd},\operatorname{tl}$ are the
total Cantor projections, including their zero values. Put
\[
 I(a,b)=4p(a,b),\qquad U(a,B)=4p(B,a)+2.
\]
Shift and MP have the combined-program contracts
\[
 S\langle t,w,d,h\rangle=
 \begin{cases}\mathsf{halt},&h=1,\\
 \langle h,\operatorname{tl}(w),d,\operatorname{hd}(w)\rangle,&h\ne1,
 \end{cases}
\]
\[
 \mathsf{MP}_x\langle t,w,d,h\rangle=
 \begin{cases}
 \langle0,w,0,d+x\rangle,&I(t,d+x)=h,\\
 \langle0,w,d+x,0\rangle,&I(t,d+x)\ne h.
 \end{cases}
\]

\paragraph{The generic AX interface.}
Let $m=8$ for the diagonal cross-graft and $m=9$ for the separate-head
cross-graft. AX pushes $m$ generated formulas and the saved top, so the
total pushed-entry counts are respectively nine and ten. Put
$D=d+x$ and $H=h+y$. In both cases,
\[
 \mathsf{AX}_{c,x,y}\langle t,w,d,h\rangle
  =\langle G_m(H,D,c),[q_1,\ldots,q_m,t]::_Cw,0,0\rangle.
\]
The last two generated entries are
\[
 q_{m-1}=\rho_8(D,c,H,d),\qquad q_m=U(d,h).
\]
For $m=8$, $q_1,\ldots,q_6$ are the unchanged six heads, in reverse
emission order. For $m=9$, $q_1=\Gamma(c)=I(c,U(c,c))$, and the six old
heads occupy positions two through seven. The diagonal formula is pushed
unconditionally; a failed numerical guard only replaces the final top
$G_m$ by zero. All $q_i$ except $q_m$ have residue zero modulo four,
whereas $q_m$ has residue two. The guarded top also has residue zero.
Consequently none of these generated values is one.

The independent free-algebra check in \texttt{verify\_grafts.py} verifies
the entire emitted stack and top, the original order of the two operand
reads, the unchanged final guard/cleanup macros, and the exact expansion
of all 191 or 190 macro items into the candidate source. The updated AX
therefore still clears both $D$ and $H$ at command return.

\paragraph{Two total stack identities.}
For every incoming store, including $t=1$,
\[
 \mathsf{AX}_{c,x,y};S^m:
 \langle t,w,d,h\rangle\longmapsto
 \langle\rho_8(D,c,H,d),[t]::_Cw,0,U(d,h)\rangle,
\]
\[
 \mathsf{AX}_{c,x,y};S^{m+1}:
 \langle t,w,d,h\rangle\longmapsto
 \langle U(d,h),w,0,t\rangle.
\]
The first Shift tests the cleared zero cache. Each subsequent Shift up to
the $(m+1)$st tests one of the generated $q_i$, and therefore cannot halt.
The saved top is not tested until one further Shift. These facts prove
the identities directly by the total Cantor head/tail equations. They do
not require injectivity of a finite-list encoding; in particular
$p(0,0)=0$ is allowed.

\paragraph{Stack combinators and their exact scopes.}
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf{Push}_m&=\mathsf{AX}_{0,0,0};S^m;\mathsf{MP}_0,\\
 \mathsf{Pop}_m&=\mathsf{AX}_{0,0,0};S^{m+2},\\
 \mathsf{Discard}_m&=\mathsf{MP}_1;\mathsf{AX}_{1,0,0};
                       S^{m+1};\mathsf{MP}_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Their effects are
\[
 \mathsf{Push}_m\langle t,w,d,h\rangle
   =\langle0,[t]::_Cw,0,0\rangle,
\]
\[
 \mathsf{Pop}_m\langle t,[a]::_Cw,d,h\rangle
   =\begin{cases}\mathsf{halt},&t=1,\\
                  \langle t,w,0,a\rangle,&t\ne1,
     \end{cases}
\]
\[
 \mathsf{Discard}_m\langle t,w,0,0\rangle
   =\langle0,w,0,0\rangle.
\]
For Push, the comparison after $S^m$ is between an implication, residue
zero, and the cached universal code, residue two, so MP takes its failure
branch. For Pop, precisely its final Shift tests the saved top. For
Discard, the first comparison fails because $I(t,1)>0$, and the final
comparison fails because $I(U(1,0),0)>0$. Thus Pop is conditional on the
saved top, while Push is total on every store and Discard is total on the
displayed cache/offset interface. The compiler below permits an early halt
from a saved derived one and never assumes a stronger Pop contract.

Put $\mathsf{Offset}_0$ equal to the empty word and
$\mathsf{Offset}_i=\mathsf{MP}_i$ for $i>0$. From
$\langle0,w,0,0\rangle$ this returns $\langle0,w,i,0\rangle$.
Then
\[
 \mathsf{Gen}_{m,i}=\mathsf{Push}_m;\mathsf{Offset}_i;S;
                    \mathsf{AX}_{i,0,0};S^{m+1}
\]
returns $\langle U(i,a),w,0,0\rangle$ from
$\langle a,w,0,0\rangle$, including $a=1$.

\paragraph{Axiom selectors.}
For $1\le j\le m-2$, the word
\[
 \mathsf{Discard}_m;\mathsf{AX}_{c,D,H};S^{j+1};
      \mathsf{Pop}_m^{m-j}
\]
selects the $j$th generated primitive head and returns it as the top,
preserving the arbitrary underlying stack and clearing both caches.
After $S^{j+1}$ the top is $q_j$, the cache is $q_{j+1}$, and the remaining
explicit stack is $[q_{j+2},\ldots,q_m,0]$. The $m-j$ Pops consume exactly
this list and finish with cache zero. They cannot halt because $q_j\ne1$.
This includes the new unconditional $\Gamma(c)$ head when $m=9$.

For $u\le a$, the restricted propositional selector is
\[
 \mathsf{Discard}_m;\mathsf{Offset}_u;
 \mathsf{AX}_{b,a-u,c};S^m;\mathsf{Pop}_m.
\]
It returns $\rho_8(a,b,c,u)$. The guarded gate selector is
\[
 \mathsf{Discard}_m;\mathsf{AX}_{c,b,a};\mathsf{Pop}_m^{m+1},
\]
which returns $G_m(a,b,c)$. The gate has residue zero even when the guard
fails, so the Pops remain nonhalting. Soundness of the selected formulas
is the retained diagonal or separate-head calculus argument; this word
calculation does not replace that argument.

\paragraph{Modus ponens and proof compilation.}
The four-command suffix is unchanged:
\[
 \mathsf{MPSuffix}_b=S;S;\mathsf{MP}_b;S.
\]
On $\langle0,[a,I(a,b),0]::_Cw,0,0\rangle$ it halts if $a=1$ or $b=1$;
otherwise it returns $\langle b,w,0,0\rangle$. The dummy zero is essential
to preserve the underlying stack. This computation remains valid at
$a=b=0$, where $I(0,0)=0$.

Recursively compile a calculus proof $p$ into a word $W_p$. At an axiom
use its selector. At generalization append $\mathsf{Gen}_{m,i}$. For an
MP proof with minor proof $p_0$ of $a$ and major proof $p_1$ of $I(a,b)$,
use
\[
 W_p=\mathsf{Discard}_m;\mathsf{Push}_m;
      W_{p_1};\mathsf{Push}_m;W_{p_0};\mathsf{Push}_m;
      \mathsf{MPSuffix}_b.
\]
The first Push reserves the dummy zero. Induction on the proof tree proves
that, from every $\langle t,w,0,0\rangle$, $W_p$ either halts or returns
$\langle\operatorname{concl}(p),w,0,0\rangle$. The proof uses the exact
Pop contract above, so an intermediate derived one is handled as an early
halt rather than silently excluded.

For a proof of one,
\[
 \mathsf{AX}_{0,0,0};W_p;\mathsf{Pop}_m
\]
halts from every incoming store. The first AX clears arbitrary incoming
caches, the compiled proof preserves the arbitrary residual stack, and
the final Pop halts before examining that stack. This is the universal
halting-word form used by the unchanged exhaustive scheduler.

In the opposite direction, annotate the live top, cache, and decoded stack
entries by calculus proofs. AX introduces the retained axioms, zero, and
the generalization of its old cache; successful MP records a derivation
of its conclusion in the cache; failed MP returns zero; Shift moves proved
formulas and halts only when it exposes one. The unconditional extra
$\Gamma$ is a valid primitive head in the separate calculus even when the
final gate guard fails. Thus the annotation argument is unchanged apart
from one additional certified stack entry.

The word construction and annotation argument are primitive recursive and
use ordinary induction on finite proofs and runs. Composed with the
retained calculus-to-PA translations, scheduler proof, exact startup, and
positive complete physical instruction simulations, they provide the
ordinary mathematical calibration for the cross-grafts. They do not assert
a newly checked Lean endpoint. The independent finite-word checker verifies
64 symbolic identities for $m=8$ and 67 for $m=9$, with arbitrary opaque
stack tails; unknown arithmetic comparisons are errors rather than guessed
branches.

\paragraph{The zero-jump variants.}
The two additional variants change source site 26 from a jump to 34 into
\(\operatorname{dec}(R_6,34,27)\). Independent closure computations for
both the original and changed sources give identical inductive sets of
265,964 and 263,244 source-location/zero-pattern pairs, respectively. Every
pattern at site 26 has $R_6=0$. The two instructions therefore have identical
concrete effects whenever reached from the common clean seed. Induction
on source steps transports the command compiler unchanged. This is a
source-invariant argument; the separate exact physical checks bind the
changed row and the 58,168-step blank initialization to that source.
