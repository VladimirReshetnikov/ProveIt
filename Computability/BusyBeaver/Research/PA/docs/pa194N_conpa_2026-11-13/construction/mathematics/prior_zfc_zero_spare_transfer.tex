\subsection*{Why the tail can never be exposed}
Here is the unbounded scheduler argument; the full induction is included in \file{primary/proofs/protected_credit.md}. Let $f$ be Cantor's right projection, $T_s=s(s+1)/2$, $R(0)=\epsilon$, and $R(r)=W(r-1)$ for $r>0$. If $\operatorname{unpair}(n)=(a,y)$, the exact candidate words obey
\[
 W(n)=\begin{cases}
 S;R(y),&a=0,\\
 \mathrm{MP};R(f(y)),&a=1,\\
 \AX;R(f^2(y)),&a\ge2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Give $S$ balance $-1$, MP balance zero and AX balance ten. A residual $R(r)$ has at most $r$ commands, hence minimum prefix balance at least $-r$. Put $b(n)=\operatorname{bal}W(n)$, $B(k)=\sum_{n<k}b(n)$, $g(0)=0$, $g(r)=b(r-1)$ and $q(r)=g(r)+1$.

The values of $f$ occur in blocks $[0],[0,1],[0,1,2],\ldots$. Nonnegative prefix sums of $q$ therefore imply the same property for $q\circ f$ and $q\circ f^2$. Their needed input sums are $1+B(k-1)+(k-1)$. Joint induction on Cantor diagonals proves $B(k)+k\ge0$ and every command prefix within each diagonal $s\ge2$ nonnegative relative to that diagonal's beginning. Indeed, the first $k$ AX candidates have total balance at least $9k$. Within AX candidate $y$, all nonempty prefixes have balance at least $8y+10$. The MP candidate has lower bound $8(s-1)$; the final S candidate has lower bound $7s-9>0$. Only earlier candidate indices occur in the pullback, so the induction is not circular.

The exceptional words are $W(0)=[S]$, $W(1)=[\mathrm{MP}]$, $W(2)=[S,S]$. Their cumulative minimum is $-3$. Every later diagonal has nonnegative relative command-prefix balance. Thus enumeration from zero needs exactly three conservative safe heads. Eight initial heads leave a permanent margin of at least five.

For composite $S=\mathrm{DROP};\mathrm{NIP};\mathrm{NIP}$, DROP temporarily pushes the safe top; the first NIP removes that temporary head, and the second consumes the one safe head supplied by the bound. All tested values are theorems. AX thereafter adds ten theorem heads because its incoming top/cache are now safe. MP preserves the safe prefix and may add theorem zero. The chosen finite wrapper list remains valid even when $P(0,0)=0$; it counts explicit heads, not the magnitude or an injectively encoded length of the stack integer.

Hence every future halt witnesses a native refutation and $J$ is never exposed. Conversely, the complete transfer compiler works over arbitrary underlying stacks and provides successful candidates above every cutoff after an initial AX reset. Each candidate drains finitely. A native refutation therefore eventually causes a halt. Together with the inherited numerical/ZFC bridge, the new blank machine has the claimed consistency calibration. Completing the enormous AX numerically is neither attempted nor needed: its all-natural terminating contract supplies that step.

\section{Removing the successful MP zero push}
Using the same numerical constructors, a command-boundary store is $(t,w,d,h)$: top, stack, additive counter and cache. Retain the delivered transfer command $S$ and AX exactly. If $w=[x;v]$, then
\[
 S(t,w,d,h)=\begin{cases}\mathsf{halt},&h=1\text{ or }t=1,\\
 (h,v,d,x),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Only successful MP changes. With $y=d+x$, define
\[
 \MP(x)(t,w,d,h)=
 \begin{cases}
  (y,w,y,0),&I(t,y)=h,\\
  (0,w,y,0),&I(t,y)\ne h.
 \end{cases}
\]
The stack is unchanged in both branches. The old successful command returned $(y,[0;w],y,0)$. All arguments and stacks are arbitrary naturals; the numerical alias $I(0,0)=0$ is retained intentionally.

\subsection*{Soundness and structural blocks}
The safe invariant is unchanged: top, cache and each accessible stack head are native theorems. On success $\MP$ applies numerical MP to theorem $t$ and theorem $I(t,y)$; on failure it supplies theorem zero. Neither branch changes the stack. The already established soundness of $S$ and AX, including $S$'s top-falsity guard, therefore gives command-level soundness from safe or correctly protected seeds.

Put $\Delta=\AX(0;0,0)$. Substituting $\MP$ for MP preserves these structural contracts:
\begin{align*}
 H&=\Delta;S^{11},\\
 \PUSH&=\Delta;S^9;\MP(0),\\
 \DISCARD&=\MP(1);\AX(1;0,0);S^{10};\MP(0).
\end{align*}
$H$ halts or sends $(t,[x;w],d,h)$ to $(t,w,0,x)$. It uses no MP. $\PUSH$ sends every $(t,w,d,h)$ exactly to $(0,[t;w],0,0)$ without halting. After nine shifts, only saved $t$ remains over $w$; the final target is zero, and both branches of $\MP(0)$ now have the same top/cache/counter and leave that stack intact.

$\DISCARD$ sends every $(t,w,0,0)$ exactly to $(0,w,0,0)$ without halting. Its first comparison must fail since $I(t,1)>0$; the last $\MP(0)$ clears the top without adding a head. The counter-loading macro $J(u)$ may remain empty for $u=0$ and equal $\MP(u)$ otherwise. In fact $\MP(0)$ would now also be safe because its zero-alias success does not push anything.

Consequently the old strong blocks for truth, retained rows, the dominated row, the full gated bundle and generalization remain valid after this substitution. Only the inductive MP block needs a repair.

\subsection*{The repaired major first MP block}
For a native theorem $X$, require the universal strong contract
\[
 B_X(t,w,0,0)\quad\text{halts or returns}\quad(X,w,0,0)
 \tag{Strong}
\]
for arbitrary natural $t,w$. Suppose $B_A$ and $B_M$ satisfy this contract, with $M=I(A,B)$. Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 B_B={}&\DISCARD;\PUSH;B_M;\PUSH;B_A;\PUSH;S;S;\\
       &\MP(B);\AX(B;0,0);S^{11}.
 \end{split}
\]
The first two commands explicitly reserve the zero formerly created by successful MP. Ignoring permitted sound earlier halts, the exact intermediate stores are
\begin{align*}
 (t,w,0,0)&\longmapsto(0,[0;w],0,0),\\
 &\longmapsto(0,[M,0;w],0,0),\\
 &\longmapsto(0,[A,M,0;w],0,0),\\
 &\longmapsto(A,[0;w],0,M),\\
 &\xrightarrow{\MP(B)}(B,[0;w],B,0).
\end{align*}
The last tuple is exactly the delivered compiler's old post-MP tuple. $\AX(B;0,0)$ gates to zero, saves $B$ as its tenth generated head and clears counter/cache. Ten transfers give $(Q_B0,[0;w],0,B)$. The eleventh halts if $B=1$ and otherwise yields $(B,w,0,0)$, proving (Strong).

When $A=B=0$, $M=I(0,0)=0$ and the intended comparison succeeds. It inserts no extra zero; exactly the reserved zero is removed by cleanup. When $w=0$, a saved zero can numerically alias its suffix because $P(0,0)=0$. This does not invalidate the calculation: $\operatorname{unpair}(P(x,v))=(x,v)$ holds for every natural pair. The proof tracks an explicit wrapper list over a fixed suffix, not an injectively encoded stack length.

\begin{theorem}[Complete no push command compiler]
The modified three-command language compiles every native theorem with (Strong), and compiles a refutation into a successful candidate above every finite enumeration cutoff.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Truth, all axioms and generalization use the preserved strong blocks. The repaired block above handles the inductive numerical MP rule, so induction over finite proof trees gives (Strong). For a refutation use $B_1;H$. A first $\AX(0;1,Y)$ resets counter/cache and leaves an arbitrary top/stack, exactly the inputs admitted by the strong contract. The old field packing therefore gives cofinal candidates
\[
 N_Y=P(2,P(Y,P(1,R))),\qquad R=\operatorname{tail}(B_1;H).
\]
The unchanged positive predecessor-tail scheduler drains after each command, so every nonhalting candidate is finite and every sufficiently large successful candidate is eventually reached.
\end{proof}

\section{An adjacency obstruction for NIP DROP NIP}
Consider $R=\mathrm{oldNIP};\mathrm{oldDROP};\mathrm{oldNIP}$ with unchanged AX output order. For $w=[x;v]$ it halts if incoming $h=1$ or popped $x=1$, and otherwise
\[
 R(t,[x;v],d,h)=(x,v,d,t).
\]
This is not the earlier NIP--DROP variant. In particular its cache need not equal zero or its current top, so that earlier invariant cannot be reused.

Define a relation on naturals by
\[
 E(u,v)\iff\forall z\,\bigl(u=I(v,z)\ \Longrightarrow\ u=v=z=0\bigr).
\]
Maintain $E(h,t)$ and $E$ for every consecutive pair in the infinite decoded chain $[t;w]$. Both hold at the all-zero seed. The cache/top relation immediately forces every successful MP to have $t=y=h=0$.

\subsection*{AX preserves the relation by exact structural mismatches}
AX's output chain is
\[
 [G,R_1,R_2,R_3,R_4,R_5,R_6,R_7,L,Q_Uh,t;w],
\]
with cache zero. Thus $E(0,G)$ holds; the final saved-$t$ link to the old stack is inherited. Each remaining adjacency forbids a nontrivial implication match:
\begin{itemize}
\item For $G$ to have $R_1$ as antecedent, the full-bundle structure would force its quantifier-coded component to equal $1$. If $G=0$, the positive $R_1$ already prevents a match.
\item The antecedents of $R_1,R_2,R_4$ are quantifier-coded, whereas their following rows $R_2,R_3,R_5$ are implication-coded.
\item The antecedents of $R_3,R_5,R_7$ are equality-coded, whereas their followers $R_4,R_6,L$ are implication-coded.
\item $R_6=I(\exists_aQ_ac,c)$ cannot have $R_7$ as antecedent: injectivity would force $R_7$'s implication-coded consequent to equal $1$.
\item $L$'s antecedent is implication-coded but its next head $Q_Uh$ is quantifier-coded. That head itself cannot be an implication major for saved $t$.
\end{itemize}
These arguments range over all natural parameters. The implication constructor is injective on pairs even at the raw zero alias, so no syntactic well-formedness assumption is hidden in the tag comparisons.

If $R$ returns, its new cache/top pair was an old adjacent chain pair and the chain loses its first node. On MP failure, inserting a zero top/cache preserves $E(0,v)$; on success, the invariant forces all relevant values to zero, and the possible pushed zero preserves every adjacency. The same proof works when the successful-MP zero push is omitted.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed row order obstruction]
From the clean all-zero seed, every command sequence over $R$, MP and the unchanged AX has only trivial successful MP outputs. Its top, cache and decoded stack never contain $1$, and neither halt guard can fire.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The adjacency induction above forces successful MP output zero. Initially every accessible value differs from one. AX adds implication rows, a quantifier head, an implication-or-zero gate and the saved old top, all different from one. MP only produces or pushes zero; $R$ moves old values. Induction therefore preserves the non-one property as well as the adjacency invariant.
\end{proof}
The theorem rules out the intended nontrivial finite-refutation compiler for this fixed interface. It is not a state lower bound, an obstruction to every row permutation, or a nonhalting theorem for an unidentified physical blank initializer. An exposed unsafe tail or an entry before the invariant has been established is outside its clean-seed hypothesis.

Useful PUSH and CLEAN macros do not contradict the theorem. With $D_1=\AX(1;0,0)$, $D_1;R^9;\mathrm{MP}(0)$ returns $(0,[t;w],0,0)$, because the final implication comparison fails by its consequent. A subsequent $R$ restores the top or halts on $t=1$. Such macros never manufacture the missing nontrivial adjacent MP pair.

\section{Deleting AX saved top remains conditional}
Let $D,N$ be the old DROP and NIP, and let $\AX_{\rm del}$ delete only AX's first push-and-clear of incoming top. Two exact interface facts suffice for a completeness transport:
\begin{enumerate}
\item With incoming $h=0$, $D;\AX_{\rm del}$ has the full old AX effect: $D$ first creates the store that the deleted prefix would have created.
\item With incoming $t=0$, $\AX_{\rm del}$ has old AX's effect except that the final saved zero under the nine generated heads is absent.
\end{enumerate}
Every old strong-compiler AX call outside generalization has cache zero and can therefore be replaced by $D;\AX_{\rm del}$. Generalization needs the separate block
\[
 B_X;D;J(i);N;\AX_{\rm del}(i;0,0);N^9;D;N.
\]
After the first four components the store is $(0,w,i,X)$. The deleted AX supplies the eight implication rows and $Q_iX$ but no saved zero. Nine NIPs give $(0,w,0,Q_iX)$, without testing $X$. DROP and one NIP then return $(Q_iX,w,0,0)$. This also handles $X=1$ and $i=0$; a second final NIP would incorrectly over-pop the arbitrary suffix.

An independent review approves this completeness calculation \emph{conditional on the exact interface facts}. It does not establish raw-input soundness. A changed quantifier index such as $t+d$ is allowed by generalization, but the literal row/gate parameters, dominated condition $U\le A$, reset behavior and field arities still require checks. The tested 204-node / 217-state price is not a calibrated machine and gives no improvement at the requested ceiling.

\section{Complete dualization repairs NDN but costs more}
The fixed-interface adjacency obstruction must not be generalized to every NDN system. Let $J(t,w,d,h)=(h,w,d,t)$ swap top and cache. Changing \emph{both} logical commands to $\mathrm{MP}^{*}=J\mathrm{MP}J$ and $\AX^{*}=J\AX J$, while keeping $R$ unchanged, gives an exact dual normal-state interface:
\[
 J S J(t,[x;v],d,h)=(x,v,d,t).
\]
For $y=d+x$, $\mathrm{MP}^{*}(x)$ tests $I(h,y)=t$. Success returns $(0,[0;w],y,y)$ and failure returns $(0,w,y,0)$. AX generalizes incoming top, saves incoming cache, and returns the gate in the cache. Its complete equation is
\[
 \AX^{*}(c;x,y)(t,w,d,h)=
 (0,[r_1,\ldots,r_7,L(A,B,c,d),Q_dt,h;w],0,G(A,B,c)),
\]
where $A=d+x$ and $B=t+y$.

Ignoring only halt guards, conjugacy transports every exact compiled block to
\[
 (0,w,0,h)\longmapsto(0,w,0,X).
\]
Restoring $R$'s real guards can only interrupt it with an earlier halt. Appending $R$ to a compiled refutation catches cache one. Soundness follows because both guard operands are accessible theorems and successful MP caches a theorem conclusion. The same one/two/three field scheduler and balances $-1,0,10$ give cofinal completeness and conditional protected-tail soundness.

The guards of $R$ are not the conjugates of all the old $S$ guards, so this is an exact normal-map transport plus a separate halt-or-return argument, not equality of halt sets. Literal register swaps in the source cost 219, 219 and 220 source DAG nodes across the three tested layouts. No favorable physical allocation or blank initializer is claimed. Reversing only the MP comparison is not the proved construction.

\section{Prefix recoding has a complete logical calibration}
The independently reviewed Q-priority constructors are
\[
 I'(a,b)=2P(a,b),\quad Q'_ab=4P(a,b)+3,\quad
 R'(a,b)=8P(a,b)+1,\quad E'(a,b)=8P(a,b)+5.
\]
Their images partition all naturals into even numbers, $3\bmod4$, $1\bmod8$, and $5\bmod8$. Each constructor is injective; only $I'(0,0)=0$, and every positive value strictly exceeds both children. Also $R'(0,0)=1$, preserving the literal halt code.

Define $F(0)=0$. For positive $n$, uniquely decode $n=C(a,b)$ in the old four-tag algebra and set $F(n)=C'(F(a),F(b))$. The children are smaller, so the recursion is total. Define $G$ in the other direction. Induction proves both inverse identities; the constructor equations also hold at zero, where the self-decomposition is explicitly stopped. Thus this is an isomorphism on the \emph{entire numerical carrier}, not just parsed formula codes.

Every unrestricted finite proof transports under $F$: constructor-polynomial axiom expressions, exact numerical MP, theorem GEN and equality/disequality gates are preserved. A number serving simultaneously as a formula, binder and private label receives the \emph{same} image in every occurrence. The full private bundle is transported, not replaced by an unproved formula-only interpretation. Since $F(1)=1$, unrestricted refutations correspond in both directions.

\section{A two-constructor covering algebra collapses}
A separate negative theorem concerns $I(a,b)=2P(a,b)+2$ and $Q(a,b)=2P(a,b)+3$, whose images cover all codes at least two. Let $R,E$ be \emph{any} total numerical functions. Retain the intended full classical implicational strength, theorem GEN, distribution, existence, both membership substitutions, original $\rho_6$, and every original gated bundle. Then the calculus derives falsity one, independently of $R,E$.

Original $\rho_6$ first supplies explosion $1\to A$ for every natural $A$, and the existence/distribution rows give $Q_i1\to1$. Strong induction on raw codes then proves either $A$ or its native negation for every code: zero is truth, one has its identity negation, and every larger code decomposes into a strictly smaller implication or quantified matrix. No independent atomic generators remain. It follows that $Q_iA\leftrightarrow A$ for all $i,A$.

Existence now proves every equality code; the substitution rows make every membership code equivalent to $r=R(0,0)$. In the original gated bundle, the allowed instance $(a,b,c)=(1,1,0)$ reduces to $r$, while $(0,0,1)$ reduces to its native negation. MP derives one.

The full-strength hypothesis is essential. Because this algebra loses $I(0,0)=0$, the old dominated certificate must not be silently reused; a restricted basis lacking the intended implicational strength would need its own calibration. The theorem rules out this atom-free full-strength covering design, not arbitrary two-constructor calculi or variable-length encodings reserving atomic images.

\section{Modal B theorem equality fails but refutation equivalence is open}
Replacing original $(\exists_iQ_iA)\to A$ by $A\to Q_i\exists_iA$ does \emph{not} preserve the entire numerical theorem set. In the original raw algebra, interpret native zero as true, native one as true, all equality atoms as false, quantifiers as identity, and implication as Boolean implication. Membership atoms other than $R(0,0)$ may be false. The zero alias is respected. Every retained row, the proposed modal-B row and full gated bundle are true, but original $\rho_6$ at $A=E(0,0)$ is false.

The invalid earlier equivalence argument silently used unconditional double-negation elimination relative to native one. This countermodel \emph{does not refute equiconsistency}. A relative translation proves $Q_i1\leftrightarrow1$ and recovers double-negation elimination for regular formulas satisfying $1\to A$. Original $\rho_6$ is derivable for regular matrices. The unresolved obligations are transporting the full subset premise in its negative polarity and respecting the coupled matrix-code/private-binder identity.
