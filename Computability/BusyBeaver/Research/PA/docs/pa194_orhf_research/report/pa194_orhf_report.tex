\begin{abstract}
No justified binary blank-start consistency machine with fewer than 194 working states was obtained. This report supplies the requested fallback: two new complete 194-state tables, PA194ORHF and PA194ORHBF, with a written iPRA halting-equivalence proof and reproducible finite certificates. Both compose the supplied repaired operand-reader construction ORH with two exact three-to-one execution shortcuts; the second also composes the supplied dedicated history selector B. The mechanisms are attributed to their sources: the contribution is the new verified composition, its uniform complete-tape invariant, exact stuttering clock, comparison, and artifacts. The absolute consistency endpoint explicitly imports the revised AX11H written calibration. No new completed end-to-end Lean or Rocq proof is claimed. The comparison identifies 104 named distinct 194-state proposals, separates them from experimental graphs, and audits all 55 catalogued 193-state graphs. A fresh exhaustive register-allocation screen and broader macro-exposure searches did not produce a valid smaller table.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result, machine convention and proof boundary}
The objective is a deterministic single-tape machine $M$, started in its designated control on an all-zero two-way infinite binary tape, such that
\[
             \ipra\vdash \Con(\PA)\ \longleftrightarrow\ \neg\Halt_{\rm blank}(M).
\]
Working states exclude the separate terminal halt label. Every working state has exactly one transition on each bit, writes a bit, and moves exactly one cell left or right. There are no stay-put moves, extra symbols, input words, or uncounted initialization states.

The two tables in this packet use working labels $0,\ldots,193$, initial label 0, halt label 194, and initial head coordinate zero. Each file has 388 rows of the form \texttt{state read write L/R next}. Native labels are also supplied: start 74, source-boundary label 84, halt 254. In particular native label 194 is an ordinary working control. The explicit bijections in \texttt{labels.json} relate the native and dense tables.

\begin{theorem}[Written result with explicit inherited calibration]
Let $M_F$ and $M_{BF}$ be the two literal tables identified above. The finite transition facts and quantifier-free inductions in this report prove in iPRA that
\[
 \Halt(M_F)\leftrightarrow\Halt(\mathrm{ORH}),\qquad
 \Halt(M_{BF})\leftrightarrow\Halt(\mathrm{ORHB}),
\]
where ORHB is the four-row B composition of ORH. The supplied B and ORH relative proofs and the revised AX11H written calibration then give
\[
 \ipra\vdash \Con(\PA)\leftrightarrow\neg\Halt(M_F),\qquad
 \ipra\vdash \Con(\PA)\leftrightarrow\neg\Halt(M_{BF}).
\]
The PA-refutation predicate is exactly the fixed current Foundation predicate of that inherited calibration.
\end{theorem}

The new relative proof is a direct argument about literal transition tables. The absolute endpoint is a composition with an inherited ordinary mathematical theorem, whose ten proof components are retained and reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:inherited-calibration-audit}. We have not independently formalized every inherited proof template. The packet separates that dependency from finite executable checks. An unfinished formalization does not by itself negate a written proof, and a finite execution certificate does not by itself prove the consistency calibration.

The two new tables differ from ORH on two and six native rows, respectively. These differences alter recurring execution, not state names, comment formatting, or unreachable padding. The first table has a uniform time relation to ORH: each changed visit saves exactly two transitions. The companion changes the history-selector implementation as well and has its own bounded-time equivalence; it is not asserted to dominate ORHF in runtime.

\subsection{What the 193-state tables actually establish}
All 55 catalogued 193-state graphs were recovered and their canonical identities recomputed. A fresh literal blank-start screen covered every one of the 193 possible starting controls of each graph: 10,615 runs. It found 112 finite halts, 10,499 certified monotone outward rays, and four runs still capped at 100,000 transitions. The ray criterion is sound: once the head moves strictly into untouched blank tape in a repeated zero-transition control cycle, all later steps remain on new blank cells with the same outward movement.

The four capped cases belong to two already rejected constructions. One uses coefficient-three formula coding with the collision $E_3(1,0)=I_3(0,1)=6$ and an explicitly retained finite numerical refutation of the intended contradiction. The other is a constructor-loop reassociation whose positive branch never returns to its decrement test: it repeatedly increases $T$ by four and register 6 by one. Their source failures are distinct from the bounded screen's inability to classify the four executions. No cap is promoted to a consistency-machine result.

A separate known 193-state runtime core merges a decoder history state into the register machinery, but its complete blank startup fails. A prepared source frame is not an allowed input for the requested blank-start model. Reintroducing a valid initializer must be counted. No global 194-state lower bound follows from these failures.

\section{Construction of the two new tables}
ORH is the supplied repaired reassociation of the operand reader~\cite{orh}. Its source transformation is
\[
 ((A,(B,C)),D)\ \longmapsto\ (A,(B,(C,D))),
\quad
 (A,B,C,D)=(\texttt{node32},\texttt{node53},\texttt{node55},\texttt{node65}).
\]
The corresponding operand words change from $00,010,011,1$ to $0,10,110,111$. A separate repair preserves the reader's hidden use by a history selector. That repair is why the tempting uncorrected 193-state table is rejected. The complete ORH and AX11H source arrays have the same 3,671 primitive instructions, while 134 source addresses differ. The inherited ORH proof and its exact source/clock bindings are retained in \texttt{reference/ORH\_proof}.

Starting from the complete ORH native table, the principal builder changes only
\[
        (26,1):1R42\mapsto1R91,\qquad
        (101,0):0L26\mapsto0R17.
\]
For ORHBF, it first applies the four-row B transformation in Section~\ref{sec:b-companion} and then these same two patches. The two changes operate in recurring source execution, and the complete table still has 194 graph-reachable working controls. Both final tables preserve the precise AX11H instruction array and logical register allocation.

Exact blank initialization reaches the whole source frame at control 84 and head $-2$, at source site 25 with logical register vector $(2,0,\ldots,0)$ and 26 one-valued spare blocks. The checker compares every nonzero tape cell and therefore also establishes the zero exterior. It does not check only a tape window. ORHF takes 57,890 transitions, compared with ORH's 57,942; ORHBF takes 57,982, compared with ORHB's 58,034. Each startup uses 26 shortcut occurrences and saves exactly 52 transitions. These are startup comparisons. The uniform ongoing relation is the exact clock theorem proved next.

\section{New reduction attempts and their precise limits}
\subsection{Exhaustive register allocation within a fixed fold template}
A new C++ enumeration checked all $9!=362,880$ physical register allocations of the unfolded 201-control parent. For each allocation it recomputed all eligible one-history-bit fold edges, allowing eligible fixed non-box controls as well as the AX readers. An edge means the two controls have disjoint incoming history-bit contexts and their two old actions can be reproduced using the direct action or an existing three-step selector.

Every allocation has maximum bipartite matching size exactly seven. Matching is an upper bound on simultaneously compatible disjoint folds. Therefore an eight-fold reduction to 193 is impossible \emph{within this particular template}, independently of whether every seven-edge matching is jointly valid. This is a finite exhaustive result about the supplied parent and fold rule, not a lower bound for other compilers or machines. The exact input, code, hashes, and completed log are retained under \texttt{research/permutation\_research}.

\subsection{Exposing internal macros to the grammar search}
Two new search directions enlarge the old 190-token grammar vocabulary. One exposes the operand reader as \texttt{node32; node53; node55; node65}, reusing native entries 104 and 107 and the existing fixed pairs $(107,98)\mapsto185$, $(104,185)\mapsto92$, $(92,90)\mapsto213$. It retains the old fixed reader for the separate MP and hidden-selector uses. The AX word grows from 190 to 193 tokens but admits new cross-boundary sharing.

The other exposes repeated Push/Copy pairs:
\[
 \texttt{node55}=(\texttt{node49},\texttt{node54}),\quad
 \texttt{node58}=(\texttt{node49},\texttt{node57}),\quad
 \texttt{node70}=(\texttt{node49},\texttt{node69}).
\]
Its scorer recognizes every fixed reader whose two rows preserve the scanned bit and move right, instead of recognizing only the two- and four-increment macros. The actual binary tree, not a mere word length, determines the allocation score. The unchanged expanded seed is a definitionally expanded source; any changed tree would still require the complete resolved-instruction and physical checks before acceptance.

The original-fixed operand search completed at least 1,604,000 logged proposals before a memory termination, and the Push/Copy search completed at least 960,000 logged proposals before a memory termination. Neither produced a score below 60 effective reader controls, hence neither supplied a sub-194 candidate. Only flushed completed prefixes are counted; their requested durations are not reported as completed runs. The B-fixed operand search completed 2,216,629 proposals in 240.005 seconds, with 375,878 accepted moves, best effective score 60, and no strict hit. Its exact inputs, elapsed-time stopping rule and final receipt are retained separately. The five Catalan associations of the four closed operand routines were also evaluated: two score 194 and three score 195. The portable inputs and source are retained so a longer search can continue from a faithful starting point. Heuristic scores are not proof certificates.

\subsection{Local shortcuts and source-interface experiments}
A sound bounded backward-history search rediscovered only the two supplied shortcut mechanisms used here. Increasing the predecessor-history depth from three to six and the trace cap from thirteen to nineteen did not yield an additional certified row. The longer search used a finite symbolic-branch budget; it is exploratory evidence, not an exhaustive absence theorem beyond its completed branches.

At the source level, deleting standalone MP is invalid: the AX fallthrough MP sees zero top/cache operands and does not detach arbitrary implications. Executing genuine MP before AX suggests a fused stack command, but the generated head block obstructs clean access to old premises. No all-store completeness compiler was established for it. Similarly, splitting the guarded gate into two independent heads without moving the guard would expose an unsound unguarded head. These failures and the missing obligations are recorded explicitly instead of being hidden behind a low compiler score.

\section{Further research questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can a different invariant permit the known 193-control runtime to initialize directly from blank, while retaining the complete register/spare-list frame? The required result is a finite bootstrap identity and an inductive runtime simulation, not a long nonhalting prefix.
\item Can a compiler search share across the newly exposed Push/Copy and operand boundaries while preserving every structured jump? The present seeds expose new legal fixed-reader aliases; a strict score improvement would still need instruction-graph equality and literal source contracts.
\item Can folds with longer recorded history, non-disjoint state classes, or a changed tape representation evade the seven-edge matching bound? The fresh $9!$ result deliberately leaves those families open.
\item Can a fused MP--AX protocol recover a clean all-store axiom-selection and premise-access compiler? That is the concrete missing mathematical lemma before attempting to price its binary table.
\item Which small part of the direct invariant-and-clock theorem should be formalized first? The three-clause Boolean invariant and the two fixed three-step identities provide a much smaller new proof obligation than rebuilding the inherited PA translator.
\end{enumerate}
