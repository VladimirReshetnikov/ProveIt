Why the current one-bit lookback-fold model gains nothing
--------------------------------------------------------
The routine opportunities_fast from the prior search framework uses:
  (a) previous-bit contexts of bit-preserving right-moving incoming edges;
  (b) pure reader providers with rows(0,R,u0),(1,R,u1); and
  (c) equality of row actions in a fixed read-symbol column.

The 11 changed rows are all read 1/write 0, so they are neither the edges counted
in(a) nor rows of providers in(b). Their targets lie in the engine, outside the
set of candidate non-engine states. All context sets and provider graphs are
therefore unchanged. In column 1, the changed action family(0,R,SK[j]) is
disjoint from every unchanged row action. A permutation of j preserves all
equalities between members of this family. Thus every row-action equality used
in(c) is preserved as well. The resulting candidate-fold catalog is identical
under every one of the8! permutations fixing physical slot 6 for registerB.

The original catalog is empty when all 160 non-engine states are eligible.
One hundred random retargetings also reproduced the empty catalog, as a
diagnostic check of the invariant argument. This was NOT an exhaustive search
over all possible machines or all routing optimizations. More general tape
excursions, different source trees, changed engine rows, and longer history
encodings are outside this restricted-method invariance.

The successful A/D swap is exported in accepted196_register_swap. Its table is
an executable196-state alternative, not a solution to the requested strict
state bound and not a newly kernel-checked Lean development.
