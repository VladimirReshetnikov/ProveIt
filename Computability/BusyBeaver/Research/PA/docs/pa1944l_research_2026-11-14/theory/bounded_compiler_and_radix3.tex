\begin{abstract}
This supplement proves a bounded-input replacement for one full-PA induction
in the imported numerical-proof-to-command-word compiler. It also gives an
explicit primitive-recursive Boolean countermodel to an attractive radix-three
reduction which deletes the equality constructor and its three axiom heads.
A third result internalizes positive symbolic physical contracts in iPRA and
gives a relative halting theorem for PA1944L. These results do not supply an
end-to-end iPRA calibration of a physical machine to PA consistency.
All repository statements below refer to commit
\begin{center}\texttt{1c9524d8bf2e0a27b9a16ff622359927f536e663}.\end{center}
\end{abstract}

\section{Bounded induction for the word compiler}

The imported PA196 arithmetic module \texttt{CompilerInvariant.lean},
together with its realization, compiler-rule, and command-word definitions,
gives the following concrete situation. Exact source paths are listed in
the accompanying README.
At fuel $k$ and fixed table bound $N$, let $A_k(i)$ be the checker's row
value and $W_k(i)$ its compiled command word, for $i\le N$.
Value zero denotes rejection and $a+1$ acceptance with conclusion $a$.
Both tables are produced by primitive recursion on $k$ from the preceding
tables; lookup outside $[0,N]$ returns zero. The zero checker table has only
zero entries. For a word $w$, conclusion $a$, and clean store $(t,s,0,0)$,
write
\[
 R(w,a,t,s)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{run}(w,(t,s,0,0))=\mathsf{halt}
 \ \lor\
 \operatorname{run}(w,(t,s,0,0))=(a,s,0,0).
\]
The run here is over the finite command word, not an unbounded machine run.
Its value is primitive recursive. Early halting is allowed and absorbing.

The local rules, already proved in arbitrary $\isone$ models in the named
files, have this exact dependency pattern:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A leaf realizes its conclusion at every clean store, without a
      recursive row hypothesis.
\item Generalization queries one predecessor row at the same $(t,s)$,
      then appends a fixed generalization macro.
\item Modus ponens first queries the major predecessor at $(t,s)$.
      If it returns normally with formula $h=I(a,b)$, the minor predecessor
      is queried at $(0,\p(h,s))$. The fixed push and MP macros then return
      $b$ with original tail $s$, or halt earlier.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the second query's input depends on the major's \emph{formula},
not on an unbounded execution-time witness or on the contents of a returned
proof code. This point makes the bound below possible.

Fix the final fuel $F$, table bound $N$, and arbitrary requested inputs $t,s$.
All the following functions are primitive recursive:
\begin{align*}
 K&=\max\bigl(1,\{A_j(i):j\le F,\ i\le N\}\bigr),\\
 R_0&=\max(1,K,t,s),\\
 R_{j+1}&=(K+R_j+2)^2.
\end{align*}
Cantor pairing satisfies $\p(h,x)\le(K+x+2)^2$ for $h\le K$.
The sequence $R_j$ is nondecreasing.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-input compiler induction]\label{thm:bounded}
Under the displayed local word identities, the table compiler satisfies
\[
 A_F(i)=a+1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R(W_F(i),a,t,s)
 \qquad(i\le N)
\]
by quantifier-free induction in the primitive-recursive language. The same
argument therefore holds in $\isone$ under its representations of those
functions. It does not require full-PA induction on the universally quantified
predicate \texttt{TableRealizes}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $k\le F$, use the bounded invariant
\[
 P(k):\quad
 \forall i\le N\ \forall x,y\le R_{F-k},\quad
 A_k(i)>0\ \Longrightarrow\
 R\bigl(W_k(i),A_k(i)-1,x,y\bigr).
\]
Every displayed universal quantifier is bounded. Replace these finite
conjunctions by their primitive-recursive Boolean characteristic. The
matrix is a decidable equality/disjunction between primitive-recursive
values, so $P(k)$ is expressed by an equation in that language. Use a
guard $k\le F$ to make the induction total beyond $F$.

At $k=0$ every antecedent is false. Suppose $P(k)$ and $k<F$.
Take an accepted row $i$ at fuel $k+1$ and $x,y\le R_{F-k-1}$.
Leaves use their closed local identities. Generalization uses the induction
hypothesis for the same input; monotonicity gives
$x,y\le R_{F-k}$. In an MP row, the major input is the same and hence is
covered. On a normal major return, its conclusion $h$ is less than its
positive checker entry, so $h\le K$ by the definition of $K$.
The minor input satisfies
\[
 0\le R_{F-k},\qquad
 \p(h,y)\le(K+R_{F-k-1}+2)^2=R_{F-k}.
\]
It is therefore covered by $P(k)$ too. The local MP identity completes
the step, including either possible early halt. A lookup outside the table
cannot be an accepted premise, because its checker value is zero.
Thus $P(F)$ holds and covers the initially fixed $t,s\le R_0$.
Finally universally generalize the arbitrary parameters $F,N,t,s$.
This is universal generalization \emph{after} induction on an equation,
not induction on an unbounded universal formula.
\end{proof}

The theorem in $\pra$ or $\ipra$ is relative to the displayed local word
identities being available there. The imported realization lemmas supply
those identities in $\isone$, which is enough for the claimed repair of
the full-PA word-compiler induction in that theory. Merely using
quantifier-free induction in the last step does not automatically lower
the strength of unprovided lemma proofs.

\subsection{An explicit word-length bound}
For the imported nine-entry PA196 command interface the lengths are
\[
 |\mathsf{pop}|=11,\quad |\mathsf{push}|=10,\quad
 |\mathsf{discard}|=12.
\]
The head selectors have length at most $92$, the rho8 selector at most $33$,
and the gate selector $112$. Generalization appends at most $22$ commands;
MP concatenates its two predecessor words and $34$ additional commands.
Consequently, with $L_k=\max_{i\le N}|W_k(i)|$,
\[
 L_0=0,\qquad L_{k+1}\le 2L_k+112,\qquad
 L_k\le112(2^k-1).
\]
This is a bound on command count. Numerical word codes and arithmetic
register values can be much larger. For the separate ten-entry interface,
the adjusted words \texttt{pop=AX S\string^11},
\texttt{push=AX S\string^9 MP}, and
\texttt{discard=MP AX S\string^10 MP} have lengths $12,11,13$.
With the corresponding selectors, $134$ replaces $112$ as a safe local
constant. This latter application requires checking the adjusted selectors;
it is not a claim that the old nine-entry word is interchangeable with it.

\subsection{What the lemma does not settle}
The imported forward theorem also uses \texttt{RequestedCertificate}, a
statement quantifying over source environments, native label maps, and
freshness bounds. Its conclusion supplies a numerical certificate for an
arbitrary translated PA derivation. Fixing the word compiler does not
construct those certificates. In particular, the arbitrary induction-axiom
supplier still needs its own uniform proof-producing construction.
The upstream tree has numerous full-PA induction uses beyond the two
illustrative uses named in the forward-audit README.

A valid finite-table replacement for that remaining stage would require
the following concrete data. Given a node and requested parameter tuple,
list all predecessor queries, prove a primitive-recursive bound on each
query parameter independent of the missing predecessor certificate, and
supply every leaf and composition step in $\isone$. One can then use a
backwards-growing bound as above and collect finitely many certificate
witnesses by bounded $\Sigma_1$ collection. None of these data may be
replaced by the assertion that an external operation is ``primitive
recursive''. The present supplement has not supplied that complete
PA-axiom translator or its arithmetic calibration to the physical machine.

\section{An obstruction to equality-free radix three}

Consider Cantor pairing $\p(a,b)$ and the three injective, disjoint tags
\[
 I(A,B)=3\p(A,B),\qquad
 M(a,b)=3\p(a,b)+1,\qquad
 U(a,B)=3\p(B,a)+2.
\]
Treat code zero as truth, and code $1=M(0,0)$ as the proposed contradiction.
As before, $N(B)=I(B,1)$, conjunction is its classical implicational
abbreviation, and $\exists x_a B=N(U(a,N(B)))$.
Delete the equality constructor and the two equality-rewriting heads and
equality-witness head. Retain exactly the three heads
\[
 (\exists x_a U(a,C))\to C,\qquad
 U(a,U(b,C))\to U(b,U(a,C)),
\]
\[
 U(a,B)\to\bigl(U(a,B\to C)\to U(a,C)\bigr),
\]
as well as the dominated
propositional head, diagonal vacuity, guarded separation, MP, and closed
generalization. The same construction below works if equality is kept but
its single equality-witness head is deleted, by assigning every equality
atom value zero.

Define a Boolean value $v(c)$ by course-of-values primitive recursion:
\begin{align*}
 v(0)&=1,\\
 v(I(A,B))&=\neg v(A)\lor v(B),\\
 v(U(a,B))&=v(B),\\
 v(M(a,b))&=
   \begin{cases}0,&a=b,\\v(a),&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\end{align*}
All requested children are smaller than the nonzero code. The special
overlap $I(0,0)=0$ is consistent with the definition, since $1\to1=1$.
Thus $v$ is total primitive recursive and $v(1)=0$.

\begin{theorem}[A countermodel to deletion of the equality interface]
Every axiom and rule of the equality-free calculus described above preserves
Boolean value one. In particular code one is not derivable; this soundness
and consistency assertion can be proved in $\ipra$ by induction on coded
derivations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under $v$, both universal and existential quantifiers leave the body's
Boolean value unchanged. The head $(\exists x_a U(a,C))\to C$, quantifier
commutation, universal distribution, and diagonal $B\to U(B,B)$ therefore
evaluate to classical tautologies. The rho8 head
\[
 ((H\to d)\to D)\to((D\to H)\to(A\to H))
\]
is a propositional tautology for arbitrary truth values, so also when the
numeric dominance guard $d\le D$ is imposed. MP is Boolean implication
elimination, and generalization preserves $v(B)$ by definition.

It remains to check the guarded separation gate. Use the repository's
definitions
\begin{align*}
 V&=M(a,b)\to U(c,M(a,b)),&
 P&=c\to M(c,b),\\
 S&=U\bigl(b,(M(a,b)\to P)\wedge(M(c,b)\to c)\bigr),&
 Q&=U(c,S\to N(P)),\\
 G&=N(V\to Q).
\end{align*}
The gate guard includes $c\ne b$. Hence $v(M(c,b))=v(c)$, which gives
$v(P)=1$. Also $v(V)=1$, $v(S)=1$, and then $v(Q)=0$.
Therefore $v(G)=1$. If diagonal and gate are bundled as
$N(\Gamma(c)\to(V\to Q))$, this still has value one.
A failed guard returns truth and is harmless. These are all axiom cases.

For complete precision, encode a proof as a finite sequence $D$ of lines.
Each line carries either an axiom tag and its numerical parameters, or a
rule tag and indices of earlier premise lines. Let $\operatorname{OK}(D,i)$
mean that the first $i$ lines are accepted by this primitive-recursive
checker, and let $c_j$ be the conclusion code of line $j$. Define the
primitive-recursive Boolean
\[
 B(D,i)=\prod_{j<i} v(c_j).
\]
The induction formula is the quantifier-free implication
$\operatorname{OK}(D,i)=1\to B(D,i)=1$.
The Boolean factor lemma
$\prod_{j<i}v(c_j)=1\to v(c_r)=1$ for $r<i$ is itself a
quantifier-free induction on the finite product; thus the prefix invariant
supplies each earlier premise value without a hidden unbounded quantifier.
If line $i$ is an axiom, its value is one by the displayed numerical
identities. If it uses a rule, its earlier premise values are one by the
induction hypothesis, so its conclusion has value one by the local rule
check. Thus $\ipra$ proves the implication for every $i$; no truth predicate
or induction on an arbitrary arithmetic formula is involved. A final
accepted line of code one contradicts $v(1)=0$. A usual finite proof tree
can alternatively be flattened in postorder by primitive recursion.
\end{proof}

This is a Boolean algebra model of the \emph{coded proof rules}; it need
not be an ordinary first-order membership structure. That is precisely
why it detects the missing quantifier/equality interface.

\begin{proposition}[The witness head alone cannot be deleted]
In the original radix-four language, retain all the heads other than
$\exists x_a E(c,a)$. The resulting calculus has an $\ipra$ consistency
proof by the same method.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use radix four in the numerical recursion and set $v(E(a,b))=0$ for every
equality atom. The two remaining equality-rewriting heads
\[
 M(c,a)\to(E(c,b)\to M(b,a)),\qquad
 E(c,a)\to(M(b,c)\to M(b,a))
\]
have value one, because the equality antecedent has value zero.
Equality vacuity, if retained, also has value one. All other checks and
the proof-prefix induction are exactly those already given.
By contrast the omitted witness head has value zero for every pair of
labels, because the existential constructor preserves the body's value.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The proposed definable equality lacks a witness]
Replacing the removed equality by two-sided neighborhood equivalence
does not recover the missing witness axiom in this reduced calculus.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a fresh label $z$ define
\[
 E_z(a,b)=U(z,M(a,z)\leftrightarrow M(b,z))
       \wedge U(z,M(z,a)\leftrightarrow M(z,b)).
\]
Take $a=0$, $b=1$, and $z=2$. The first conjunct has value
$v(0)\leftrightarrow v(1)=0$; the second has value
$v(2)\leftrightarrow v(2)=1$. Thus $v(E_2(0,1))=0$ and
$v(\exists x_1 E_2(0,1))=0$. The soundness theorem rules out a derivation
of this instance of the proposed equality-witness schema.
\end{proof}

Consequently this specific deletion has not found a smaller checker for
the same refutation problem. It yields a calculus with an explicit weak
consistency proof. The result does not rule out every equality-free
presentation of PA: replacing the missing interface by a different, fully
calibrated rule remains possible, but its machine cost must be included.

\section{Internalizing the finite physical certificates}

This section concerns a relative compiler theorem. It does not identify the
source program's refutations with PA refutations. The four-increment
reassociation under investigation, PA1944L, retains every resolved source
instruction. Its independent verifier uses the following symbolic objects:
finite binary runs of affine length, the arbitrary finite spare-register
list, an infinite zero exterior, and arbitrary untouched contexts. There
are thirteen affine slots: a constant, the gap, nine primary-register
values, the first spare value, and the total width of the remaining spare
list. A claimed run length has nonnegative coefficients.

For $v\in\mathbb N$ set
\[
 B(v)=0\,1^{v+1},\qquad C(v)=1\,0\,1^v.
\]
For a finite list $A$, concatenate its blocks to form $B(A),C(A)$, and put
$\operatorname{width}(A)=\sum_{v\in A}(v+2)$. The word identity
\[
 C(A)1=1B(A)
\]
holds for the empty list and is preserved on adjoining one element, since
$C(v)1=1B(v)$. Its reversal follows by reversal of finite words.

\begin{lemma}[Arithmetic validity of the symbolic step rules]
Each of the following certificate operations has an $\ipra$ proof when
finite words, finite lists, tape zippers, and bounded machine iteration are
represented by their primitive-recursive codes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A literal transition performs its table row and adds one to time.
\item If a row writes $b'$ and moves repeatedly in one direction while
      remaining in the same state on $b$, it crosses $b^m$ in exactly $m$
      steps, replacing it with $b'^m$ and preserving both outside contexts.
\item The identities combining adjacent equal-symbol runs, deleting empty
      runs, absorbing a finite zero suffix into the blank exterior, and
      $C(A)1=1B(A)$ preserve the represented tape and head.
\item A checked one-block traversal from state $q$ back to $q$, in a fixed
      direction, mapping $B(v)$ or $C(v)$ to its declared output block in
      exactly $v+2$ steps, extends to an arbitrary finite list $A$ in
      exactly $\operatorname{width}(A)$ steps.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
One explicit canonical zipper is a pair of natural numbers $(l,r)$:
the low bit of $r$ is the current cell, and the low bit of $l$ its immediate
left neighbor. All exterior cells are zero. Writing $w$ and moving right
updates this pair to $(2l+w,\lfloor r/2\rfloor)$; writing $w$ and moving
left updates it to
\[
 (\lfloor l/2\rfloor,
 2(2\lfloor r/2\rfloor+w)+(l\bmod2)).
\]
Together with the finite state label, these are primitive-recursive
configuration functions for the delivered binary table. Canonical
zero-tail removal is built into the natural-number encoding.
The first item is a defining equation. For the second, use induction on
$m$ in the equation for the coded iterated step and concatenation. Empty
run, singleton run, and successor concatenation are the base and step;
contexts are free numerical parameters. Tape equality can be expressed
as equality of canonical finite zippers, so no unbounded quantifier over
tape positions is needed.

Each finite-word identity in the third item follows by induction on the
relevant run or list length in an equality of primitive-recursive codes.
For the fourth, the one-block proof is a fixed finite composition of the
first three items. Iterating it over the first $j$ entries of a fixed
coded list gives a quantifier-free equality of coded configurations at
time $\sum_{i<j}(v_i+2)$. Induction on $j$, with $j$ bounded by the given
list length, proves the list traversal. This also proves preservation of
arbitrary external contexts, because their codes remain free parameters.
All induction formulas are equations or Boolean combinations of equations.
\end{proof}

The independent checker rederives four one-block traversals directly from
the literal transition table. It records exact duration $v+2$, direction,
and preservation of arbitrary contexts. Its four entries use the label
conjugacy $73,95,48,112$ and transformations $B\to B$, $B\to C$,
$B\to B$, and $C\to B$, respectively. These are finite data, not
postulated register-engine oracles.

For each accepted source-site branch, composing the foregoing lemmas
along its finite symbolic trace gives an $\ipra$ theorem. Its duration is
the recorded affine polynomial $k_s(\vec v)$, with nonnegative coefficients
and strictly positive constant term. Coefficientwise checks make
$k_s(\vec v)\ge1$ immediate. The complete source has finitely many sites;
each decrement has its zero case and its positive case $v=x+1$. Thus the
finite list of local proofs covers every primitive source step.

This description gives an ordinary arithmetical soundness proof of the
certificate rules. A successful Python run checks the closed table and
certificate data; it is not itself an object-theory derivation. A new Lean
endpoint internalizing these rules has not been compiled here.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-segment halting transfer]\label{thm:physical}
Suppose a primitive-recursive source step $S$ and a literal machine $M$
have an $\ipra$-proved startup and the complete positive local contracts
just described. Then $\ipra$ proves
\[
 \operatorname{Halt}(M,\mathsf{blank})
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Halt}(S,s_0).
\]
The two directions have primitive-recursive witness transformations.
Consequently two literal machines with these contracts for the same
resolved source program have an $\ipra$-provable halting equivalence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Augment the abstract source configuration by the finite spare list, gap,
and head offset used by its frame. The local contracts specify
primitive-recursive updates of these auxiliary fields. They do not change
which instruction or numerical register value the source uses. The
initial spare list is nonempty; each source primitive preserves that
condition. This augmentation makes the exact encoded frame a function
$E(s)$ of a coded source state.

Let $b$ be the fixed startup duration. As long as no source halt has yet
occurred, define
\[
 T(0)=b,\qquad T(n+1)=T(n)+k(S^n(s_0)).
\]
Choose arbitrary total primitive-recursive values for the duration and
source update after halting. By induction on $n$ in a quantifier-free
implication guarded by the Boolean test that the first $n$ source steps
do not halt, the physical configuration at time $T(n)$ is $E(S^n(s_0))$.
The iterated-step composition equation is proved by quantifier-free
induction. Moreover $T(n)\ge b+n$ on such a prefix.

A source halt at step $n$ yields a physical halt by composing startup,
the first $n$ normal contracts, and the exact halt-site contract.
The resulting physical time is a primitive-recursive function of the
source prefix and its final local duration.

Conversely, suppose the physical machine halts at time $t$. The fixed
startup trace excludes an earlier startup halt. If none of the first
$t+1$ source states were a halt site, the preceding construction would
reach a nonhalting root frame at time $T(t+1)\ge b+t+1>t$.
In the totalized physical semantics a halt is absorbing, so this
contradicts the assumed halt at $t$. The partial semantics gives the same
conclusion because the halt state has no outgoing row. The finite source
prefix is decidable, and bounded minimization finds its first halt site.
This is a primitive-recursive witness extraction; it does not use Markov's
principle, unbounded search, or inverse decoding of intermediate tapes.
\end{proof}

For PA1944L, the independent check has passed for all $3,673$ sites and
$4,780$ branch outcomes, using $1,084,060$ symbolic steps, together with
the complete $58,320$-step startup and the exact source-instruction
comparison. The literal dense table's SHA-256 is
\begin{center}\small\texttt{c5787c6f2350e77183ca0d3ff25d2733ba87e8bb52cd54092d7fa26fcf4d5253}.\end{center}
Its duration records give the explicit uniform bounds
\[
 3\le k(s)\le
 534+8g+4\sum_{i=0}^{8}r_i+2a_0+2\operatorname{width}(A),
\]
where $g$ is the gap, $r_i$ the nine primary values, and $a_0,A$ the
first spare value and remaining spare list. A positive-decrement case
uses the parameter $r_i-1$ in its certificate, which is at most $r_i$.
These numerical bounds follow by taking the largest coefficient in each
of the thirteen slots across the $4,780$ records; the supporting JSON
summary is included. Therefore the relative theorem supplies an ordinary
mathematical proof in $\ipra$ for this exact physical implementation.
It transfers the source's halting problem and does not turn the
repository's still-open PA-refutation compiler into an established theorem.

\begin{corollary}[Relative iPRA equivalence of the two literal tables]
Let $M_0$ be the repository's primary PA194 table, with dense-file hash
\begin{center}\small\texttt{4bfea62b17aad7d8c8c5e682fe8942e0a86b93e4351f8e675e32705bdaae007f},\end{center}
and let $M_4$ be the PA1944L table pinned above. Both start in dense state
zero on the blank binary tape. Then
\[
 \ipra\vdash
 \bigl(\operatorname{Halt}(M_4)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Halt}(M_0)\bigr)
\]
and hence
\[
 \ipra\vdash
 \bigl(\neg\operatorname{Halt}(M_4)\leftrightarrow
       \neg\operatorname{Halt}(M_0)\bigr).
\]
This is an ordinary computer-assisted proof of a provability statement,
not a newly compiled Lean or Rocq theorem.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The source comparison checks equality of all $3,673$ resolved primitive
instructions, including every jump target. Both startups end at instruction
$25$ with logical primary registers $(2,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)$ and a nonempty
finite spare list. The independent checker has also passed the primary
table's $4,780$ cases, with $1,078,198$ symbolic steps, four rederived
list lemmas, minimum duration constant $3$, and its full $58,154$-step
startup. Therefore Theorem~\ref{thm:physical} applies to both tables and
the same initialized register-instruction program. Their auxiliary gaps
and path frames need not agree; each implementation has its own
primitive-recursive augmented-frame simulation, whose projection is the
same source program. Compose their halting equivalences. Negation
preserves an equivalence in intuitionistic logic.
\end{proof}

An equivalence of this source program with PA refutation is a further
premise. If the primary PA194 table is eventually calibrated to
$\operatorname{Con}(\pa)$ in $\ipra$, the displayed relative theorem
transfers that calibration to PA1944L immediately. The relative theorem
does not supply that missing premise.

\section{The final weak-arithmetic transfer}

Let $H=\exists t\,\operatorname{Run}_M(t)$ and
$R=\exists p\,\operatorname{Prf}_{\pa}(p,\bot)$ with decidable
primitive-recursive matrices. Once the actual machine satisfies
$\isone\vdash H\leftrightarrow R$, the repository's
\texttt{isigma1-transfer} applies. In the standard primitive-recursive
presentation, each implication is a $\Pi_2$ statement after placing the
decidable matrix in prenex form; the conservation step supplies the
corresponding classical PRA statement. G\"odel--Gentzen translation then
supplies the negative equivalence in intuitionistic PRA. Concretely,
intuitionistic logic proves $\neg\neg H\leftrightarrow\neg\neg R$
implies $\neg H\leftrightarrow\neg R$. No Markov principle is needed.

The remaining PA-to-source-calculus compiler and the formal assembly are
still required: neither a standard finite-run equivalence nor a
PA-provability theorem by itself establishes the displayed $\isone$ premise.
