The 194-state frontier
----------------------
The semantic frontier splits into 26 graphs presented as constructions in their
reports, nine auxiliary/search graphs to which this census does not assign a
complete equivalence argument, and four expressly rejected implementations. The
status labels record supplied paper and implementation evidence. This inventory
did not replay every historical universal checker or independently reprove all
historical logical calibrations.

The oldest three allocations, PA194-67, PA194-AD and PA194-68/8, share the separate
diagonal-head calculus and its ten-entry AX interface. The first uses 134 fixed
controls plus 67 AX readers minus seven contextual folds. AD changes physical
register allocation. The 68/8 variant changes decoder association; its extra reader
is compensated by an extra fold. These are different literal implementations of
essentially the same resolved program, not three unrelated proof systems.

The C family changes command semantics. Successful MP leaves its result in the
cached head H, and Shift tests H=1 instead of the original t+h=1 guard. The CS
family reverses destructive comparator operands in that cached-only protocol and
recovers a controller fold. C has 3,649 source sites; CS has 3,646. CZ, CSZ/X,
A, F, R, J, ZR and the auxiliary CSZ-A3/A5/A6, both-zero6 and I-tree variants
change zero-return conventions, source-address association, or both. Their
semantic invariants apply only at the designated source entries; the physical
startup must still be checked separately.

The N family removes the dummy-zero stack push after successful MP, leaves the
result in the visible top T, and retains the candidate as the numerical offset D.
The needed zero must then be reserved by the proof-word compiler. N uses the
original sum guard; the H/HS packet combines the no-push result with a cached-only
guard (H asymmetric, HS symmetric). This distinction matters: dropping a dummy
push is not justified by an old compiler that assumed the extra stack entry.
The successful no-push result is (b,w,b,0), not (b,w,0,0).

The radix machines M4R, M5 and M6 share a new multiplication-controller fold.
M4R is a physical reassociation with coefficient four. M5 and M6 change all formula
constructor coefficients to five and six and need explicit formula/variable-label
recoding. The stack Cantor pairing and proof-word scheduler do not become radix-five
or radix-six data structures. N5 and N6 compose those formula recodings with the
no-push MP interface. Constructor recoding does not preserve numerical order;
the restricted implicational condition therefore needs its supplied syntactic
translation argument, not a monotonicity assertion.

D changes the separately produced diagonal axiom to Gamma(D), with a different
selector and 69-reader/nine-fold decoder. Q exchanges the curried premises of a
quantifier-distribution head; a K/S exchange proof translates the altered axiom.
QN combines Q's emitter with N's no-push/original-sum controller. The novelty of
these designs is in the logical interface or its actual composition, rather than
in a relabeling of a table.

The latest HAZ packet uses the older auxiliary MPH semantics: MP caches its result
in H while Shift still has the original sum guard. HA adds A/D physical placement;
HAZ also replaces the final AX return with a known-zero decrement. Both have 3,650
source sites, with 4,750 and 4,751 outcomes. The initial H of that research is
already the old MPH auxiliary table; it is not the same designation as the new
H in the HS packet. Name-only comparison would confuse distinct graphs here.

The latest uppercase PA194NS_research packet is a further distinct family. SUMS
keeps the original sum guard and reverses its destructive comparison, eliminating
a transfer loop; an additional one-bit history fold absorbs the new trailer
control. Its NS companion also removes the successful-MP zero push. SUMS has
3,670 source sites and 4,776 outcomes; sum-NS has 3,651 and 4,751. Both initialize
in 58,004 physical steps. Their guard is semantically different from cached-only
HS/NS despite the duplicate name.

Larger designs
--------------
At 195 controls the census has 19 whole-graph classes. The principal reports cover
original PA195 (bundled diagonal/vacuity gate), 195S (separate diagonal head),
195C (cached command interface), the M195S8 scanner variant and diagonal-combined
PA195DC. Other classes include quotient parents, alternate logical/decoder
experiments, rejected contractions and the two latest QN MP-tail experiments.
The QN packet itself does not claim complete new consistency proofs for those
two tails, although it supplies physical checks. Thus 19 is not a count of
19 established consistency theorems.

At 196 controls there are 99 whole-graph classes. The upstream PA196 construction
changes quantifier coding, axiom-head organization and decoder allocation from
the 209-era source. Later designs explore separate and combined diagonal heads,
logical rebracketing, MP/Shift protocols, physical register permutations, zero
return branches and grammar changes. Some are rejected or intermediate inputs.
The shorter 18-site direct-negation macro has a 197-control verified allocation;
similarly, shorter source trees can have larger decoder budgets. Expanded-source
length and number of mathematical proof rules are not TM-state objectives.

The 197--207 classes mostly record larger controller/compiler/decoder experiments
and ancestors. The 208 collection has 142 classes, largely research candidates
and repeatedly quoted reference machines. The principal U208 constructions
reduce U209 with entry, finite-history and three-cell contextual folds. Imported
PA209 and the U209 parent are different tables and source lineages. Historical
nested ZIPs retain 209--222-control ancestors and additional entry-bypass/joint
experiments. None supplies a missing literal sub-194 design in this snapshot.
All state-count histograms and member paths are in the complete census.

Proof boundary
--------------
The current PA README explicitly reports that neither machine-specific direction
of its target iPRA consistency equivalence is complete for primary PA194, PA195
or PA196. It also describes substantial checked source coverage, simulation,
quotient, zipper and clock constructions. The filed papers separately present
ordinary mathematical arguments intended to be formalizable in PA, using
syntactic refutation translations. The absence of a new Lean/Rocq endpoint does
not refute or erase an ordinary PA paper proof; conversely, a standard finite-run
theorem or a conditional endpoint does not discharge the stated iPRA reductions.

The new construction should explicitly state which ordinary PA-calibration
lemmas it inherits and prove its changed command/source interface and exact
literal simulation, including blank startup and positive progress. A bounded
nonhalting run supplies neither direction of the logical biconditional. The
upstream PA196 statement Nonhalt -> Con(PA), by itself, does not supply the
reverse internal implication. Nothing in this inventory upgrades that one-way
statement into an end-to-end kernel biconditional.

Fresh negative controls
-----------------------
The independent finite-support simulator verify_negative_examples.py reproduced
halting at exactly 1,361 steps for all three distinct comparator-return dec-X
mutants (CS, alternate I-tree and ZR). Their universal source invariants can hold
while startup fails before the intended clean seed. This makes complete blank
initialization a concrete, necessary check for future zero-return changes. The
fourth rejected 194 graph is the shortened seven-zero scanner, documented as
failing source-frame alignment; no independent full replay of that certificate
is claimed here.

Next construction opportunities and constraints
----------------------------------------------
The current 39-class set leaves literal combinations of SUMS with Q or radix
constructors, or Q with cached-only no-push semantics, absent at the table level.
That observation is a search direction, not a proof of a 194-state allocation.
Changing formula coefficient, controller tail and physical register placement
together can alter which history folds remain compatible. A new combination
must be rebuilt and compared against auxiliary graphs as well as headline
designs. A change confined to an unreachable transition is not a material
improvement; recurring logical arithmetic, stack behavior or actually executed
return conventions provide stronger differences.

ZFC supplies useful no-push, command-local field-reader, history-fold and marker
ideas, but its state savings depend on that source interface. ConciseIPRA supplies
proof-checker and certificate-interface simplifications, not a free equivalence
between PA refutation search and iPRA refutation search. The usable transfer is
joint optimization of proof compilation, data representation, decoder and actual
tape behavior, with both PA refutation translations retained.
