\section{A complete command compiler for the changed emitter}
\label{sec:new-command-compiler}

This compiler applies to the no-push controller with Shift guard $t+h=1$.
It works for any of the exact head exchanges in the preceding section and
for either quantifier residue $2$ or $3$.  It does not describe the cached
or head-only Shift controllers.  The underlying no-push strategy and its
reserved-zero discipline are inherited from the supplied PA194N packet;
the present application changes which axiom codes the emitter produces.

Let $[a;w]=p(a,w)$ and use nested brackets with the leftmost item at the
head.  Cantor projections are total and satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{hd}[a;w]=a,\quad\operatorname{tl}[a;w]=w,
 \quad[0;0]=0,\quad\operatorname{hd}(0)=\operatorname{tl}(0)=0.
\]
These are numerical stacks, not freely generated lists.  The command store
$(t,w,d,h)$ consists of top formula, stack, offset, and cached formula.
With $y=d+x$, the no-push modus-ponens command is
\[
 \mathsf{MP}_x(t,w,d,h)=
 \begin{cases}(y,w,y,0),&I(t,y)=h,\\(0,w,y,0),&I(t,y)\ne h.\end{cases}   \tag{MP}
\]
Shift has the literal contract
\[
 \mathsf S(t,w,d,h)=
 \begin{cases}\mathsf{halt},&t+h=1,\\
 (h,\operatorname{tl}(w),d,\operatorname{hd}(w)),&t+h\ne1.
 \end{cases}                                                        \tag{S}
\]
The checked emitter gives
\[
 \mathsf{AX}_{A,x,y}(t,w,d,h)
   =(g(H,D,A),[q_1,\ldots,q_9,t;w],0,0),\qquad D=d+x,\ H=h+y,        \tag{AX}
\]
where $q_1=\Gamma(A)$ (or the separately checked $\Gamma(D)$ or
$\Gamma(H)$), and $q_2,\ldots,q_7$ are the six heads in reverse
emission order, $q_8=\rho(D,A,H,d)$, and $q_9=U(d,h)$.  A head exchange
changes the corresponding $q_j$ and nothing in this stack protocol.
All $q_1,\ldots,q_8$ and $g$ have implication residue zero; $q_9$ has
quantifier residue $2$ or $3$ and satisfies $q_9\ge2$.  None is code one.

Direct projection calculation proves
\begin{align*}
 \mathsf{AX}_{A,x,y};\mathsf S^9(t,w,d,h)
     &=(q_8,[t;w],0,U(d,h)),\\
 \mathsf{AX}_{A,x,y};\mathsf S^{10}(t,w,d,h)
     &=(U(d,h),w,0,t).                                  \tag{A10}
\end{align*}
Every Shift displayed here is nonhalting: it tests zero against a generated
non-one code, two such generated codes, or the universal code of value at
least two.  The identities hold even when a zero stack cell collapses.

Put $A_0=\mathsf{AX}_{0,0,0}$ and define
\[
 \mathsf{Push}=A_0;\mathsf S^9;\mathsf{MP}_0,\qquad
 \mathsf{Pop}=A_0;\mathsf S^{11},\qquad
 \mathsf{Discard}=\mathsf{MP}_0,\qquad
 \mathsf{Offset}_i=\mathsf{MP}_i.
\]
Their contracts are
\begin{align*}
 \mathsf{Push}(t,w,d,h)&=(0,[t;w],0,0),\\
 \mathsf{Pop}(t,[a;w],d,h)&=(t,w,0,a),\\
 \mathsf{Discard}(t,w,0,h)&=(0,w,0,0),\\
 \mathsf{Offset}_i(0,w,0,0)&=(0,w,i,0).                    \tag{P}
\end{align*}
For Push, the final comparison is between an implication code and a
universal code, so it fails.  Pop's eleventh Shift tests $U(d,h)+t\ge2$,
so Pop is total, including when $t=1$.  For Discard, both branches of
(MP) coincide at $y=0$.  For Offset, $I(0,i)>0$ if $i>0$, while the
successful zero case returns the same stated store.  Thus no false
assumption about $I(0,0)=0$ or $[0;0]=0$ is hidden in (P).

\paragraph{Axiom leaves.}
Starting from arbitrary $(t,w,0,0)$, first Discard.  For $1\le j\le7$,
\[
 \mathsf{Discard};\mathsf{AX}_{A,D,H};\mathsf S^{j+1};
                 \mathsf{Pop}^{9-j}
\]
returns $(q_j,w,0,0)$.  The Pops remove exactly the unselected generated
tail and the saved zero; the final cache is that saved zero.  If an emitted
head uses a permutation of its parameters, choose the AX arguments by the
inverse permutation for this one selected head. If $q_1$ is $\Gamma(D)$
or $\Gamma(H)$, set that freely chosen AX parameter to the desired code.
No simultaneous choice of parameters for the other unselected heads is
needed.  For $u\le a$, the word
\[
 \mathsf{Discard};\mathsf{Offset}_u;\mathsf{AX}_{b,a-u,c};
                    \mathsf S^9;\mathsf{Pop}
\]
returns $(\rho(a,b,c,u),w,0,0)$.  A permitted gate uses
$\mathsf{Discard};\mathsf{AX}_{A,D,H};\mathsf{Pop}^{10}$ and returns
$(g(H,D,A),w,0,0)$.  Truth uses Discard.

\paragraph{Generalization.}
From $(a,w,0,0)$, the word
\[
 \mathsf{Gen}_i=\mathsf{Push};\mathsf{Offset}_i;\mathsf S;
                         \mathsf{AX}_{i,0,0};\mathsf S^{10}
\]
returns $(U(i,a),w,0,0)$.  Its first three operations produce
$(0,w,i,a)$; then (A10) gives the conclusion.  Every Shift is safe,
including when the premise code is one.

\paragraph{Modus ponens.}
Suppose recursive words $C(a),C(I(a,b))$ return the displayed conclusion
with the stack tail preserved and offset/cache zero, or halt earlier.
Use
\[
 \mathsf{Discard};\mathsf{Push};C(I(a,b));\mathsf{Push};C(a);
 \mathsf{Push};\mathsf S;\mathsf S;\mathsf{MP}_b;\mathsf{Pop}.       \tag{C-MP}
\]
The initial Push reserves one zero.  Immediately before the two Shifts the
store is $(0,[a,I(a,b),0;w],0,0)$.  The first Shift exposes $a$ in the
cache; the second halts if $a=1$, otherwise giving
$(a,[0;w],0,I(a,b))$.  Successful MP gives $(b,[0;w],b,0)$ and total Pop
gives $(b,w,0,0)$.  These numerical projection equations also cover
$a=b=w=0$.  An early halt already fulfills the refutation-search goal.

Induction on a finite proof tree now proves that $C(p)$, from every
$(t,w,0,0)$, either halts or returns
$(\operatorname{concl}(p),w,0,0)$.  The proof word is primitive recursively
constructed.  From arbitrary incoming $d,h$, prepend $A_0$ to clear both.
If $p$ concludes code one, append Shift.  Thus
\[
                         A_0;C(p);\mathsf S                         \tag{W}
\]
halts from every incoming numerical store.  The derivation induction and
all displayed calculations are formalizable in PA.

\paragraph{The carried-store scheduler.}
The scheduler need not reset the store between candidates.  For a positive
continuation encoding $E(\epsilon)=0$ and
$E(c::w)=1+\operatorname{encodeCommand}(c,E(w))$, its AX code is
\[
 \operatorname{encodeCommand}(\mathsf{AX}_{A,x,y},z)
                  =p(A+2,p(y,p(x,z))).
\]
Let $z=E(W)$ for the universal halting word (W).  Prefixing it by
$\mathsf{AX}_{k,0,0}$ gives fresh candidate codes
\[
                       p(k+2,p(0,p(0,z))).
\]
They are strictly increasing and unbounded in $k$, and all such prefixes
are harmless because (W) works from every store.  Hence the scheduler,
whose fresh counter begins at two and increases through all later natural
numbers, eventually executes a halting word.  Positive continuations
strictly decrease and the source arithmetic macros terminate.  Candidate
coding, source-trace formation, and the finite arithmetic bounds used here
are primitive recursive; PA verifies the construction by induction.

\paragraph{Soundness and the exact conclusion.}
From the clean seed, annotate $t,h$ and every live canonical stack entry
with a proof in the changed calculus.  The zero stack supplies truth, and
a zero push on zero adds no canonical cell.  AX adds its checked axiom
heads, a generalization of the cached theorem, and the saved top.  MP
applies its two premise annotations or returns truth.  Shift transports
annotations; if it halts, $t+h=1$ says one of its two annotated codes is
one.  Therefore a source halt yields a changed-calculus refutation.
The two calculus transports above turn such a refutation into an inherited
calculus refutation and then a PA refutation.  Conversely, a PA refutation
passes through the inherited calibration, the new transport, the compiler
(W), and the scheduler to a source halt.

If the literal machine $M$ has the separately verified complete startup
and universal positive-duration source simulation, concatenate finite
segments in the forward direction.  In the reverse direction, a physical
halt at time $T$ cannot precede $T+1$ nonhalting source instructions, since
each consumes positive physical time and their simulations contain no
premature halt.  Determinism gives the required contradiction.  Thus PA
proves the two finite syntactic reductions and consequently
\[
 \mathrm{PA}\vdash
 \operatorname{Halt}(M,\mathrm{blank})
   \longleftrightarrow\exists p\,\operatorname{Prf}_{\mathrm{PA}}(p,\ulcorner0=1\urcorner),
\]
\[
 \mathrm{PA}\vdash
 \neg\operatorname{Halt}(M,\mathrm{blank})
   \longleftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA}).
\]
This conclusion uses the explicitly identified inherited calibration and
its ordinary mathematical proof; it does not upgrade an executable Python
certificate into a proof-assistant theorem, and it does not assume PA
consistency in order to prove the biconditional.
