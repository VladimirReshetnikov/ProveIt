\begin{abstract}
We compare the PA-consistency machine designs in ProveIt at commit
\nolinkurl{32d0d8b7d93a63ad4106c18cdb29b37927acdad2}.
The census covers 82 research deliveries and recognizes 94 distinct complete,
spanning, 194-working-state transition graphs. It also distinguishes the 53
193-state research graphs from accepted blank-start constructions.
We construct two new 194-state binary, one-tape machines: PA194AX11G,
whose eleven-entry axiom command uses an explicit generalization binder,
and PA194GH, which combines that binder protocol with a cached-only
contradiction test. Both have explicit complete tables, source bindings,
unbounded symbolic register contracts, and exact whole-tape blank startups.
Their recurring command behavior differs from the old designs, and their
graphs are new in the pinned corpus even under broad cosmetic equivalences.
The additional state-reduction searches did not yield an accepted machine
below 194 states.

The arithmetic proof boundary is essential. The pinned repository does not
yet establish either machine-specific direction of the full iPRA
consistency equivalence for its primary table. We give precise relative
transport statements and paper-level command proofs; we do not turn a
conditional endpoint, a standard-run theorem, or executable certificates
into a completed iPRA calibration. Thus the new physical constructions
advance the fallback design goal while the absolute iPRA theorem remains
an explicit inherited obligation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result, machine convention, and proof status}

The requested target is a literal machine $M$ with fewer than 194 working
states and
\[
 \ipra\vdash \Con(\PA)\leftrightarrow\NH(M).
\]
The fallback is a materially different 194-state design with the same
property. This investigation supplies two new physical designs and a full
comparison, but does \emph{not} establish a sub-194 machine or discharge
the remaining absolute iPRA calibration. That limitation is mathematical,
not merely the absence of a newly compiled Lean file.

Every delivered machine is deterministic, has one two-way infinite tape
over $\{0,1\}$, starts with every cell zero and the head at coordinate zero,
and writes one bit and moves one cell left or right at every nonterminal
transition. Dense working labels are $0,\ldots,193$, the initial label is
zero, and the terminal label is 194. There are 388 rows, with no row for
the terminal label. A native label such as 2066 is an identifier, not a
state count. Native start 74 and halt 254 are mapped explicitly to the
dense convention. No input or prewritten advice is part of the machine.

\input{metrics.tex}

The source changes are substantive. AX11G changes what an axiom command
consumes and what it leaves on the numerical theorem stack, while making
its explicit operand determine the binder of the saved generalization.
GH changes which theorem register can trigger halting and hence changes
the compiler's completion word. These are recurring command effects;
they are not state renamings, reflections, or different start labels for
an old graph. Their novelty is relative to the complete recognized pinned
corpus, not a global priority claim.

\subsection{The proof boundary found in the repository}

The top-level PA README explicitly states that neither machine-specific
iPRA direction has been established for the primary PA194, original PA195,
or PA196. The generic machine bridge isolates a premise
\texttt{Calibrated T h}, and its README says that no table is shown to
satisfy it. These are primary-source statements at the audited commit
\cite{pa-readme,isigma-machine}. They are more cautious than several
older packet titles and abstracts.

The current formal development has substantial reusable components:
standard finite-run source and table equivalences; an arithmetic clock
for the pinned table; source and register-operation equations in candidate
PRA models; modeled-scheduler fairness and its equivalence to numerical
derivability; and the implication from modeled-scheduler halting to an
internal PA refutation. The remaining physical/modelled-scheduler bridge
must work at arbitrary model times. The converse compiler must turn
arbitrary internally coded PA proofs into numerical-calculus derivations
in the intended weak arithmetic. The forward audit identifies uses of
full PA induction in imported proofs that are not automatically available
in $I\Sigma_1$ \cite{forward-audit}.

Accordingly, we distinguish four kinds of evidence throughout:
literal table facts; universal symbolic physical contracts; written
syntactic/calculus arguments; and internal object-theory provability.
A successful checker is evidence for its specified mathematical contract.
It is not a Lean kernel theorem and does not establish an unrelated
arithmetic calibration. Section~\ref{sec:ipra-transport} states exactly
what a verified relative simulation would transfer.

\subsection{Why the designs differ}

The dominant architecture is shared: a numerical theorem calculus feeds
a command enumerator, a binary tree implements its register program,
and a unary tape engine implements register operations. The relevant
allocation is
\[
 \#\hbox{fixed controls}+\#\hbox{raw AX readers}
       -\#\hbox{compatible folds}.
\]
The primary machine uses $134+67-7=194$; another first-generation design
uses $134+68-8=194$. Removing source instructions can leave this number
unchanged, and two independent-looking optimizations can compete for
the same physical helper or source history.

\subsection{Why the 193-state objects do not meet the target}

The coefficient-three shortcut retains four constructor tags and hence
creates a collision. For its unchanged definitions,
\[
 E(1,0)=3p(1,0)+3=6=I(0,1).
\]
The supplied derivation uses that collision to derive the false code one.
Later packet arguments rule out a specified family of nonnegative constant
equality offsets. They do not rule out every possible three-tag calculus.

The central Runtime193 construction identifies native 301 with 88 and
changes $(88,0)$ and $(51,0)$. Its 4,780 universal source-outcome contracts
can pass on canonical register frames while its inherited blank startup
enters an outward ray. A helper equivalence assumes that the next field
already contains a one; this is false during allocation of a blank field.
The remaining 193-state graphs are experimental transplants and repairs,
not an established new consistency machine. The distinction explains why
we require the entire blank-start tape and a semantic seed, not merely
a graph with 193 labels or a long bounded nonhalting run.

\section{Common numerical calculus and command representation}
\label{sec:calculus}

Let $p(a,b)=((a+b)(a+b+1))/2+b$ be Cantor pairing and let
$[a;w]=p(a,w)$. Nested stack brackets are top-first. The total projections
obey $\operatorname{hd}[a;w]=a$, $\operatorname{tl}[a;w]=w$, and
$[0;0]=\operatorname{hd}(0)=\operatorname{tl}(0)=0$. These are numerical
stacks; treating them as free lists would invalidate some edge cases.

The two new machines use
\[
 I(A,B)=4p(A,B),\quad M(a,b)=4p(a,b)+1,\quad
 U(a,B)=4p(B,a)+2,\quad E(a,b)=4p(a,b)+3.
\]
Zero is truth and one is the designated contradiction. Implication is
written $A\to B=I(A,B)$, and $N(B)=I(B,1)$,
$A\wedge B=N(I(A,N(B)))$, $\exists_aB=N(U(a,N(B)))$.
The common Q-oriented numerical calculus has the six heads
\begin{align*}
 L_1(D,H)&=(\exists_D U(D,H))\to H,\\
 L_2(D,c,H)&=U(D,U(c,H))\to U(c,U(D,H)),\\
 L_3(D,c,H)&=M(H,D)\to(E(H,c)\to M(c,D)),\\
 L_4(D,c,H)&=E(H,D)\to(M(c,H)\to M(c,D)),\\
 L_5(D,H)&=\exists_D E(H,D),\\
 L_6^Q(D,c,H)&=U(D,I(c,H))\to(U(D,c)\to U(D,H)),
\end{align*}
the dominated implication head
\[
 \rho(D,c,H,u)=((H\to u)\to D)\to((D\to H)\to(c\to H)),
 \qquad u\le D,
\]
and $\Gamma(c)=c\to U(c,c)$. For the guarded gate define
\begin{align*}
 P(a,b,c)&=c\to M(c,b),\\
 V(a,b,c)&=M(a,b)\to U(c,M(a,b)),\\
 W(a,b,c)&=U\bigl(b,(M(a,b)\to P(a,b,c))\wedge(M(c,b)\to c)\bigr),\\
 R(a,b,c)&=U\bigl(c,W(a,b,c)\to N(P(a,b,c))\bigr),\\
 g(a,b,c)&=\begin{cases}N(V(a,b,c)\to R(a,b,c)),&c\ne a,b,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\end{align*}
The inference rules are modus ponens and generalization of theorems.
These are explicit numerical axiom schemes, not assertions that every
numeric code represents a conventional well-formed formula.

Q exchanges the curried premises of the primary distribution axiom.
The existing finite restricted-implication exchange proof translates
between the orientations. The ordinary PA calibration of this calculus
is inherited from the supplied packets \cite{primary,generalizer}; its weak-arithmetic internalization
has the open obligations already identified. In the new constructions,
the genuine new proofs are the command protocols, literal source splices,
physical table bindings, and exact startup checks.

\section{New searches below 194 states}
\label{sec:searches}

The goal of these experiments was an accepted 193-state blank-start
construction. A low allocation score or local survivor would only nominate
a candidate: it would still need source, startup, and arithmetic checks.
The completed searches are reported with their exact scopes; none supplies
a lower bound on arbitrary Turing machines.

\subsection{Head-displaced control identification}

The existing joint-row merger screens largely require equal physical head
positions at corresponding controls. We also search representations in
which a removed control $a$ is represented by retained control $b$ with
head displacement $\delta\in\{-2,0,2\}$ and unchanged tape.
If an unchanged predecessor enters $a$ by move $d\in\{-1,1\}$, its new
move must be $d+\delta\in\{-1,1\}$. This condition allows a two-cell
shift when every relevant entering move can be reversed. A candidate
modifies those entering moves and synthesizes both rows of $b$ jointly.

For every supplied local context at $a$ and $b$, the synthesizer checks
the exact old one-step endpoint under the displaced representation.
It permits repeated visits to the retained control and requires positive
duration, unchanged tape outside the tested window, and exact nonhalting
endpoints. A terminal endpoint need not preserve an irrelevant final tape
decoration. The window radius is six; a proposed detour may use at most
160 steps without leaving the window. Source envelopes include interiors
of accelerated constant-symbol scans; a fresh 100,000-step literal blank
prefix supplements them. Arithmetic-box controls and the canonical source
dispatcher are protected, preserving the inherited box interface.

\input{offset_results.tex}

Failure in this finite synthesis model says only that no tested two-control
identification has the required local certificate. Wider windows, different
representations, changes to the arithmetic engine, multiple removed
controls, or different source programs are outside it.

\subsection{A longer repair search for Runtime193}

The latest supplied quadruple-edit startup screens stopped each trial at
20,000 steps; the primary accepted startup takes 58,154. We extended the
fixed-start all-source-frame screen to 200,000 steps, retaining the same
documented edit pool, complete-tape detector, and distinction between
certified rays and finite cutoffs. The detector allows arbitrary register
values and a nonempty spare tail, so a detected frame would still need a
semantic startup proof. We did not infer simultaneous runtime correctness
from individually neutral edits.

\subsection{New formula-tag dictionaries}

The standard radix family keeps constructor residues $0,1,2,3$. We
examined distinct universal/equality tags $(u,e)$ while keeping implication
tag zero and membership/contradiction tag one. The free-constructor check
retags all 15 relevant universal blocks and all three equality blocks,
including the equality embedded in the earlier helper. The tested pairs
include $(3,2),(2,4),(4,2),(4,3),(3,4)$ with admissible radices.
Appendix~\ref{sec:tag-reassignment} gives the mathematical recoding argument,
including the non-monotonicity issue for dominated implication axioms.

An apparent 195-state initial score for $(u,e)=(2,4)$ incorrectly treated
the old four-increment macro as free after changing the multiplier engine.
The corrected search charges for the actual macro and helper structure.
This is a useful example of why a macro-word score is not a machine count.

\subsection{Register reuse and already exhausted local models}

A fresh disjunctive zero/nonzero analysis of the primary 3,673-site program
reached 263,888 abstract contexts. All 36 pairs of the nine logical
registers can be simultaneously positive in that abstraction. Thus simple
aliasing justified by nonoverlapping positive lifetimes is not available
from this analysis. It does not prove that a redesigned eight-register
program is impossible. The only universally zero decrement found is the
known AX cleanup site already used in the HAZ family.

We did not present the primary shared-substring integer program as new
work: it already reports objective 67 with matching solver bound in the
supplied corpus. Nor did we repeat the old two/three-edit startup searches
as new results. Existing local-selector LP obstructions concern their
frozen grammar and selector family, not all Turing machines.

\section{What transfers from ZFC, Diophantine consistency, and concise iPRA}

The ZFC work contributes techniques that are already central to PA194:
dominated implication schemes, diagonal vacuity, source reassociation,
history-sensitive sharing, and separate exact startup certification.
It also records failed marker relays whose local identities did not extend
to all source exits. The lesson applied here is to verify the final
simultaneously folded table and the complete initialized tape. A theorem
about a ZFC table cannot serve as a PA theorem about a changed table
\cite{zfc-readme,zfc-transfer}.

The Diophantine project provides valuable examples of proving finite
syntactic reductions inside every relevant weak-arithmetic model and only
then invoking the iPRA endpoint. Its metrics concern polynomial variables,
degree, arithmetic operations, and fixed coefficients. They are not
binary-TM state costs. Using one of those equations here would require an
explicit enumerator/evaluator and a PA-strength calibration; no measured
sub-194 implementation follows merely from a compact equation
\cite{diophantine-readme}.

ConciseIPRA's scalar calculus optimizes the size of a complete proof checker
for a theory bi-interpretable with iPRA. Its native progress rule concerns
literal equations. Enumerating its contradictions would target consistency
of that theory, not automatically consistency of PA. Strengthening it to
the required induction strength and compiling it to this machine model
are substantive additional tasks. Its useful contribution here is the
insistence on explicit rules, exact coding, and complete checker scope,
not a justified state-saving replacement of the PA calculus
\cite{concise-readme}.

\section{Reproduction and conclusions}

The archive contains the complete tables, source trees, resolved instruction
maps, frozen inputs, builders, two implementations of physical verification,
command algebra checks, startup receipts, novelty comparator and all 94
comparison representatives. The top-level \path{README.md} gives the exact
commands. Construction and verification use Python's standard library;
optional searches use a C++17 compiler or SciPy/HiGHS. Assertions must
remain enabled. Frozen evidence and regenerated observations are kept
separate; timings are not mathematical acceptance criteria.

The experiments do not establish 194 as minimal. The most concrete open
routes remain a changed initialization/engine interface, a source design
with cheaper jointly compatible readers, or a representation that goes
beyond the tested local folds. The two new 194-state tables have exact
physical evidence and distinct command-level behavior. Completing the
absolute target still requires the inherited PA-refutation/calculus and
physical-model simulation obligations in the intended weak arithmetic.
