\section{Comparison of the supplied frontier}
\label{sec:frontier-census-new}

The comparison is pinned to ProveIt commit
\texttt{32d0d8b7d93a63ad4106c18cdb29b37927acdad2}.
There are 77 filed PA research packets and five current incoming ZIPs,
hence 82 deliveries. The supplied ledger records every delivery, README
hash, pinned URL, and attributed claim. A fresh scan checked all 12,551
tracked recognized-format inputs against their Git blob identities,
inspected 15,172 direct/archive payloads, and traversed 19 distinct ZIP
payloads through 31 references. There were no missing-input, parse, or
Git-identity errors. Historical proofs were catalogued, not all replayed.

Recognition found 3,606 table occurrences, 778 normalized labeled tables,
and 658 complete binary one-halt graphs having a root that reaches all
working states. The frontier includes 53 graphs at 193 states, 94 at 194,
27 at 195, and 105 at 196. Six nonspanning tables and 50 incidental
one-state JSON fragments are recorded separately. The format scope covers
five-column L/R tables, documented explicit JSON rows, and nested ZIPs;
arbitrary generators and prose-only tables are not automatically interpreted.
The broader filed-only catalogue's 662 classes also includes maximal
reachable components and other halt conventions, so its total is not the
same counting problem. Its 194-state frontier agrees with this scan.

The 94 frontier graphs comprise 51 reported principal constructions,
seven supplementary constructions, 25 research auxiliaries, four seeds,
and seven rejected objects. They remain distinct under arbitrary control
renaming, any spanning root, reflection, symbol exchange, and ignored final
halt effects. The comparison checks complete canonical tuples rather than
trusting fingerprints. Structural distinctness establishes neither startup
correctness nor a consistency calibration.

\subsection{What the designs change}

Most designs share a numerical proof calculus, command-word enumerator,
binary source tree, and unary register engine. Physical state count is
typically fixed controls plus AX readers minus compatible contextual folds.
The primary allocation is $134+67-7=194$; the first alternate PA194 uses
$134+68-8=194$. Source shortening, grammar sharing and contextual folding
interact, so their apparent savings cannot generally be added.

\subsection{Why the stored smaller tables are not solutions}

One of the 53 stored 193-state classes is a coefficient-three shortcut:
equality and implication collide, and the supplied calculus has a finite
refutation. The other 52 are initialized-runtime reductions or transplants,
chiefly based on identifying native controls 301 and 88. Their source-frame
contracts can hold while the unchanged blank bootstrap enters an outward
ray. Supplied all-start receipts classify tested starts as finite halts or
certified rays. A nine-trace argument covers every register permutation of
the fixed reduced runtime because only one permutation-sensitive selector
is reached before the ray. These are rejection certificates for particular
constructions, not a lower bound. A further even-scheduler/runtime
composition at 194 states enters a ray after 93 steps.

\subsection{Prior search and the remaining proof boundary}

Repeated-substring dictionary optimization by MILP is already present.
For the primary word, with free two- and four-increment macros, a stored
HiGHS run reports objective and bound 67 at zero gap. Its dictionary and
cost are independently checked; no formal optimality certificate is
supplied, and the objective omits scanner restrictions, simultaneous folds,
and startup. A model integrating those costs would be different.

Existing pair-quotient searches synthesize both survivor rows on primary194
and AX11Q6, with bounded source/bootstrap contexts and paths of at most
120 steps. AX11QH also repeats pair search after each of 357 neutral row
edits. P5 includes head-offset deletion and bypass searches but excludes
arithmetic-box controls as merge targets. Runtime193 repairs examine fixed
candidate pools of two, three, and four edits; 31 recent local guard hits
all fail full source checks. These searches do not cover every frontier
graph, arbitrary larger mergers, or all architectures. They establish no
general 194-state lower bound.

The repository's current PA README explicitly says that neither primary
machine-specific iPRA direction is established. Its generic endpoint takes
an all-model \texttt{Calibrated} premise, but no literal table has that
premise proved. The latest incoming texts likewise distinguish relative
weak-arithmetic simulation from absolute calibration. Consequently the
historical designs cannot be called completed iPRA-calibrated machines on
the evidence presently supplied. A new literal simulation can carry a
future calibration through an explicit reduction; it cannot provide the
absent logical premise merely by passing executable checks.

The complete evidence is in \texttt{packet\_ledger.csv},
\texttt{frontier194\_comparison.csv}, the retained 94 representatives, and
the portable census and novelty scripts. \texttt{FINDINGS.md} records the
precise scope, duplicate naming, relevant search paths, and provenance.
