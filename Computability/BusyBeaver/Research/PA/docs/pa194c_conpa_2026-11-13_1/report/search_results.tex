\section{The search below 194 states}
\label{sec:search}

The search objective was a complete physical machine with at most 193
working states. An optimistic reader count, a local fold in isolation,
or a long nonhalting blank prefix was not an acceptance criterion.
No below-194 result survived the required construction and proof checks.

\subsection{Association, history folds, and the 194-state plateau}

The two existing allocations suggest the numerical target of 59 effective
AX readers: 66 raw readers with seven folds, 67 with eight, or 68 with
nine, while retaining 134 fixed controls. We started independent searches
from both PA194 trees, preserving the 190-token emitter word. Candidate
scores used compatible history folds checked on the simultaneous quotient,
with scanner legality included. Further runs substituted the Combined
fixed rows, allowed mixed fixed-reader/AX-reader pairs, varied actual
head-parameter and ordering choices, and applied an additional unconditional
exact-row quotient after the contextual quotient.

Two finite traversals closed their recorded score-194 queues under the
stated single-occurrence and shared-subtree rotation moves: one used the
original controller and ordinary folds, the other Combined and mixed
folds. Each processed 11,472 parent grammars, evaluated 2,373,136 new
neighbors, and retained 11,470 nonseed successors. Neither reached the
20,000-entry queue cap. Both found neither a score below 194 nor a
raw-reader count below 67. Deduplication uses a 64-bit association hash;
queue closure is therefore a recorded computation, not a collision-free
mathematical exhaustiveness certificate. In particular, it is not a
global grammar or machine lower bound.

\subsection{Direct runtime searches}

We also searched the two supplied principal 194 tables and PA194C directly.
The local-action procedure considers replacing a transition by another
first action followed by a bounded detour through existing controls. It
then asks whether two non-box controls can share both replacement actions
without one detour relying on the other removed control. Every claimed
local match must restore the entire inspected tape window and the required
head displacement and destination.

Both conservative all-tape contexts and stronger contexts extracted from
all symbolic source outcomes plus exact blank startup were tried. The
radius-four runs use a nine-cell window and a 100-transition detour bound.
The PA194C radius-six run uses a thirteen-cell window and a 160-transition
bound. The extracted pattern counts were 8,338 for PA194-67, 8,873 for
PA194-68, 8,240 for PA194C at radius four, and 29,107 for PA194C at radius
six. No additional pair was emitted in these runs. The arithmetic-box
controls were excluded as merged controls so that their established
contracts remained applicable.

A separate raw-table scan enlarged the pair domain to fixed and AX readers,
using \emph{all} incoming rows to establish the previous bit. At the two
baseline grammars, this revealed no mixed fixed/AX fold beyond the known
AX-only opportunities. Later association searches applied the larger
domain to changing trees. Failure within these templates does not rule out
a different encoding, a larger simultaneous rewrite, or a stronger
reachable-configuration invariant.

\subsection{A specific rejected scanner shortcut}

Removing native scanner control 34 and redirecting its incoming transitions
to 75 would give 193 working controls. It shortens the header test by one
zero. In PA194C, however, 113 of the 4,749 arbitrary-register source
outcomes fail the existing generic simulation contract. For example,
source site zero has address \file{000000}, and the clean entry site 25
has address \file{1000000}; the shortened scanner cannot be justified by
the old address invariant. The complete failed-site list is preserved.

This rejects the shortcut under the proposed proof method. The failures
are not advertised as a proof that every failing frame is reachable from
blank or that the literal shortcut cannot have some other consistency
interpretation. A separate two-million-transition blank screen did not
halt, which supplies no missing correctness argument. The machine is
therefore not accepted as the requested smaller construction.

\subsection{Additional 194-state seed and remaining directions}

The supplementary PA194CZ table composes PA194C with the previously studied
zero-register jump at source site 26. It changes native row $(27,1)$ from
$(0,L,40)$ to $(1,R,21)$ and replaces that source jump by a decrement of
register six. A common inductive set of 263,244 site/positivity-mask pairs
proves that register zero test always takes the required branch there.
It has 4,750 physical branch contracts and a 58,168-step blank startup.
This is another 194-state search seed, not the principal result or an
additional state saving. It has the primary construction/invariant checks,
not a separately completed new independent physical replay.

The useful next changes would affect the joint source/decoder objective,
or a fixed-runtime invariant strong enough to share an additional control.
Deleting a logical head requires both soundness and a replacement
refutation compiler. Deleting a freshness comparison is particularly
unsafe because the guarded gate also contains membership vacuity, as
explained in Section~\ref{sec:logical-basis}. None of the negative searches
in this report establishes mathematical optimality of the current count.
