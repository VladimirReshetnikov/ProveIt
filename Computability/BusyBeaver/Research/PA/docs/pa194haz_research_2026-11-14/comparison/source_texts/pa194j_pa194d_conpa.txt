                        PA194J and PA194D:
              two new 194-state PA consistency machines
                       Corpus comparison, zero-register jumps,
               a changed diagonal emitter, and PA-provable equivalence

                        Research report for the ProveIt PA project

                               13 November 2026, Pacific time


                                            Abstract

    A machine with fewer than 194 working states was not obtained. We provide the requested
alternative: a materially different 194-state, two-symbol, single-tape machine, PA194J, with an
ordinary mathematical proof of PA ⊢ [¬ Halt(PA194J, blank) ↔ Con(PA)]. The new machine replaces
ten unconditional source jumps by decrements of registers proved zero at those locations. A finite
inductive abstraction with 263,180 location/status pairs establishes exact equality of the parent
and child source runs from their common seed. Three transition-row changes implement these
substitutions, retaining the symmetric Shift controller and the existing 67-reader decoder.
    The construction contains 194 working states and 388 transition rows. Its source has 3,646
primitive sites and 4,755 branch outcomes. A literal tape checker derives four generic list lemmas
from the actual rows and checks the source outcomes for arbitrary natural register values, gap,
and finite nonempty spare list. Exact blank initialization takes 60,062 transitions and reaches the
complete expected tape. The new graph differs from every existing 194-state graph in the pinned
corpus, even allowing state renaming, a different designated start, reflection, and global symbol
exchange.
    A second construction, PA194D, changes the logical emitter from Γ(A) to Γ(D), with a corre-
spondingly changed selector in the strong proof-word compiler. A new allocation of 69 decoder
readers admits nine simultaneous history folds, giving 134 + 69 − 9 = 194 working states. Its exact
blank startup takes 58,168 transitions. It is supplied as a separate literal table and construction,
with its own calibration argument and physical checks.
    The comparison covers 41 research packets at ProveIt revision 17f92105e2bc4317feae6c219c
b2143437b482ff: 36 filed packets and five newer incoming archives. The census recognizes 2,685
table occurrences, 620 byte forms, and 446 action-graph classes; rejected and diagnostic tables are
identified separately. The proof transfers the supplied ordinary PA calibration through new source
and physical preservation arguments. No new end-to-end Lean or Rocq theorem, iPRA equivalence,
state lower bound, or unconditional nonhalting is claimed.

1     Result and precise machine convention

The principal file is construction/machine/pa194j.tm. The machine starts in working state 0,
scanning cell 0 of a two-way infinite tape filled with zero. The alphabet is {0, 1}. Working states are
0, . . . , 193; label 194 is the terminal halt control, has no outgoing rows, and is excluded from the state
count. Each transition writes a bit, moves exactly one cell left or right, and enters its target state.
Every working state has exactly two rows. The five-column format is

                                stateread_bitwrite_bitL_or_Rnext_state.

All 194 working controls are reachable in the pointed transition graph. This finite graph fact does not
assert that the blank execution visits every control.
The authoritative dense table has SHA-256

      7c498ced0eb0dffed77ae2f68f4d80c98edfd8f7763ec1be68aa8cd7ea80eacd.

Its native-label presentation starts at 74 and halts at 254, with SHA-256

      31abf450ba25066c8ac3aad6e7eedb59386b770bb4917f006800145ebb622218.

The supplied label map conjugates every transition exactly. The entire dense table is printed in
Appendix A.

Theorem 1.1 (Main result). For the literal table specified above, with HPA194J the usual arithmetic
sentence asserting a finite halting trace from its blank start,

                   PA ⊢ HPA194J ↔ ¬ Con(PA) ,                        PA ⊢ ¬HPA194J ↔ Con(PA) .
                           (                               )                  (                            )


Here the proof is an ordinary mathematical proof formalizable in PA, with executable finite and symbolic
certificates. Its logical calibration is inherited from the supplied PA194/PA195 developments [2, 3] and
retained in references/logical_calibration/. The machine-specific work in this packet is the new
source invariant, exact table construction, independent physical simulation, and exact initialization.
The repository’s unfinished iPRA endpoints and the absence of a new kernel export remain distinct
from this ordinary proof; see Section 6.

sites register A is zero; at the scheduler site register X is zero; at the final AX return register D is zero.
At these ten sites a decrement can implement precisely the former jump. These substitutions create
new pure right-moving readers in the physical decoder.

2.3      Larger families and transfer of ideas

The U208 packets exploit a finite-history identification of controls 172 and 211 in a U209 ancestor. Many
deliveries are alternate presentations of the same graph. The relevant controls have already disappeared
in the PA196 development, so their saving cannot simply be applied once more to PA194. James
Chang’s PA196 source has 3,812 primitive sites; the later logical emitters and their layout searches
account for the transition to the current frontier.
The main 195-state families illustrate different routes to the same count. Original PA195 uses a
191-token bundled diagonal emitter and 3,700 sites. PA195S separates the diagonal clause, using 190
tokens and 3,673 sites, with 134 + 67 − 6 controls. PA195C adds the cached controller, reducing the
source to 3,649 sites. PA195DC combines the bundled emitter with that controller and has 3,676 sites.
M195S8 achieves eight folds but pays for an additional scanner control. These tradeoffs explain why a
source simplification or a larger fold count alone is insufficient.
NEG18 is another useful example. Its arithmetic identity shortens encoded negation from 47 primitive
sites to 18. In the separate-head source, replacing eight occurrences reduces 3,673 sites to 3,441, yet its
published physical implementation has 197 states. Reader sharing, legal return paths and compatible
local folds dominate the final cost. The new searches in this packet included mixed negation spellings
on the newer grammar seeds; none supplied a certified sub-194 table.
Appendix C gives an archive-by-archive comparison of all 41 packets. comparison/archive_ledger.csv
also records exact locators, hashes and evidence qualifications.

3     The new construction

3.1    Registers, source instructions and the common seed

The nine logical registers, numbered from zero, are

                                         (N, T, W, A, D, H, X, Y, L).

They are stored at physical unary positions (6, 2, 0, 4, 3, 7, 8, 5, 1), respectively. A source instruction is
one of inc(r, u), dec(r, z, p), jump(u), or halt. A decrement takes successor z and leaves the registers
unchanged when register r is zero; otherwise it subtracts one and takes successor p.
The clean source seed shared by PA194CS and PA194J is

                                       c∗ = (25, (2, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0)).

The source path of site 25 is 1000000. The physical clean frame has native control 84, head at −2,
header distance 48, the nine unary registers above, and 26 spare blocks of value one. Both machines are
initialized from a literal blank tape; their different finite startup traces are verified separately.

3.3    Why a reachable invariant is essential

The replacement at site 9 has positive successor 10, a halt site. Thus the two source programs are
not interchangeable on arbitrary register inputs. The proof must establish that the positive branch is
unreachable from the common seed. It does so for all ten replacements simultaneously. In contrast, the
physical simulation theorem checks every branch for arbitrary registers, including the positive branches.
These two different scopes fit together: the source invariant selects the actual branches, while the
physical theorem implements the complete new source.


4     An inductive proof of exact source-run preservation

For a natural register vector r = (r0 , . . . , r8 ), define its positivity mask
                                             8
                                    α(r) =         2j [ rj > 0 ] ∈ {0, . . . , 511}.
                                             ∑

                                             j=0

Write m \ j for clearing bit j and m ∪ j for setting it. Abstract states are pairs (i, m) of a source ordinal
and a mask. For an instruction at site i, define the abstract successors by

                                      Instruction              Abstract successors
                                        jump(u)                {(u, m)}
                                        inc(j, u)              {(u, m ∪ j)}
                                  dec(j, z, p), mj = 0         {(z, m)}
                                  dec(j, z, p), mj = 1         {(p, m), (p, m \ j)}
                                           halt                ∅.

The two positive-decrement possibilities reflect the distinction between an input equal to one and an
input greater than one. Discarding that distinction is a deliberate overapproximation.

Lemma 4.1 (Sound abstraction). Every concrete source step (i, r) → (i′ , r′ ) projects to an allowed
abstract step (i, α(r)) → (i′ , α(r′ )).

Proof. Jumps preserve the vector. An increment makes its component positive. A decrement of zero
preserves the vector and takes the zero target. If the component is positive, subtracting one either
makes it zero or leaves it positive, with every other bit unchanged. These cases are exhaustive over the
natural numbers.

Let P be the parent PA194CS source and P ′ the PA194J source. Compute the least abstract closure
R of (25, 1) under P . The included builder, a separate finite checker, and an independent review
implementation reproduce the following finite facts:

    1. |R| = 263180, and (25, 1) ∈ R.
    2. The abstract successors of every element of R lie in R.

    3. At every element of R, the P and P ′ abstract successor sets agree exactly. Recomputing the least

    4. At each changed site, the chosen register bit is zero in every mask in R at that site.

The canonical sorted-pair certificate has SHA-256

      9191fff6d156190b0eaa97f93dd5d10a23fbeac2d6505c0a269108e91c86f483.

The certificate is a finite object, not a bounded simulation of a source run. It says nothing about a
bound of 263,180 execution steps; the invariant applies at every step, for arbitrarily large register values.

Lemma 4.2 (Invariant and zero tests). Every source configuration reachable from c∗ , in either program,
projects into R. Consequently every inserted decrement takes its zero branch and leaves every register
unchanged.

Proof. The seed projects to (25, 1). Induct on the length of a concrete source run, using sound abstraction
and finite closure for the inductive step. At a modified site, the corresponding bit in the projected mask
is zero, which is equivalent to the natural register being zero.

Theorem 4.3 (Exact source identity from the seed). For every finite number of source steps, P and
P ′ , started at c∗ , have identical source ordinals and identical nine-register vectors, until and including
any halt. In particular,
                                       PA ⊢ Halt(P, c∗ ) ↔ Halt(P ′ , c∗ ) .
                                            (                             )


Proof. Induct on steps while using the preceding invariant. At an unchanged site the concrete instructions
agree. At a changed site the parent jumps to u, while the child decrements a register equal to zero and
takes precisely u, preserving the vector. The halt sites themselves are unchanged. Thus the concrete
states agree at the next step.
All definitions involved are primitive recursive. The finite closure claims can be expressed using a fixed
coded finite set and checked by bounded computations in PA. The abstraction lemma is elementary
arithmetic, and the remaining argument is ordinary induction on run length. The proof therefore
establishes the displayed internal PA statement, not only standard-model agreement.

The source proof depends on the seed. It is neither an all-tape equivalence between the literal machines
nor an arbitrary-register equivalence between the source programs. No such stronger statement is
needed for the blank-start theorem.


5     Binding the source to the literal binary table

5.1    Complete symbolic frames

A source frame represents a source path, zero padding, the header delimiter, nine unary registers and
a finite nonempty list of spare unary blocks. The block for a value v is B(v) = 0 1v+1 . The checker
also uses C(v) = 1 0 1v during sweeps. It represents the complete tape, including the zero exterior,
rather than a finite observed window. The base header distance is 48; its extra gap is an arbitrary
natural. The primary values, first spare value and remaining spare-list width are independent symbolic
quantities.

The source map is resolved twice, by an arithmetic path resolver and an interval resolver. Both give

5.2    Universal physical contracts

The independent checker derives four generic list-sweep lemmas from the literal transition rows, for
arbitrary values and arbitrary exterior contexts. Finite-list induction extends the one-block calculations
to all finite spare lists. Literal steps, monotone unary-loop summaries, these list lemmas, and exact
word normalization then prove the source contracts.
For each increment or jump there is one outcome; for each decrement, there are separate zero and
positive outcomes. A nonhalting outcome must reach the precise target source frame with the required
register effect and spare-list update. A halt outcome must reach the terminal control. Every duration is
a positive affine expression in the symbolic parameters. Consequently the simulation is productive: a
source step cannot be represented by a zero-length physical loop.

5.3    Exact blank initialization

A separate bytearray simulator executes precisely 60,062 literal transitions from native control 74 on
the all-zero tape. It obtains native control 84, head −2, and the frame of c∗ :

      path = 1000000,   header distance = 48,     (N, T, W, A, D, H, X, Y, L) = (2, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0).

Thus initialization is a complete configuration equality, not a check of only the register values or a small
neighborhood of the head.

Proposition 5.1 (Physical halting equivalence). For PA194J, PA proves

                                         HPA194J ↔ Halt(P ′ , c∗ ).

Proof. The finite initialization reaches the represented source seed without halting. Concatenate the
terminating positive physical simulations of source instructions. A source halt gives a finite physical
halting trace. Conversely, suppose a physical halt occurs after t further transitions. If no source
halt occurred, the first t + 1 source instructions would have terminating nonhalting simulations, each
of positive duration. Their concatenation extends beyond time t, contradicting the alleged halt.
Intermediate tape configurations need not have a canonical source decoding for this argument. The
explicit finite traces, unary and finite-list inductions, and induction on concatenated source steps are all
formalizable in PA.

The same established interface for the supplied PA194CS source [3] yields the corresponding parent
statement. Combining with Section 4 gives PA-provable blank halting equivalence between PA194J and
PA194CS.


6     The inherited PA consistency calibration

It would be insufficient to infer PA-provability merely from a claim that both machines do not halt
in the standard model. The supplied calibration instead provides syntactic transformations in both
directions. The relevant proof texts are retained in references/logical_calibration/, with the
cached-command correction and the ten-entry selector adjustments. The inherited symmetric-guard
proof is retained separately as proof_symmetric.tex; it establishes the precise cached command
interface of PA194CS [3].


6.1   Numerical formulas and commands

Let
                       (a + b)(a + b + 1)
           p(a, b) =                      + b, I(A, B) = 4p(A, B), M (a, b) = 4p(a, b) + 1,
                               2
                            U (a, B) = 4p(B, a) + 2,   E(a, b) = 4p(a, b) + 3.
The readings are implication, membership, universal quantification, and equality. Code zero is truth;
code one, M (0, 0), is contradiction. Negation is I(B, 1). Formula-code bounds make the diagonal clause
Γ(B) = I(B, U (B, B)) sound: variable xB is absent from the formula coded by B. The current emitter
pushes this clause separately from the guarded pointed-separation gate.
In the cached command system, stores are ⟨t, w, d, h⟩. Shift halts exactly when h = 1; otherwise it maps
the store to ⟨h, tl(w), d, hd(w)⟩. For y = d + x, modus ponens maps it to ⟨0, w, 0, y⟩ if I(t, y) = h, and
to ⟨0, w, y, 0⟩ otherwise. AX supplies ten pushed entries, counting the saved top, and clears the counter
and cache.
PA194CS implements the same command behavior through its symmetric destructive equality routine.
PA194J has exactly the same seeded primitive execution as PA194CS, so it needs no changed command
calculus, selector, axiom schema or proof-word encoding.

For a deterministic binary action graph with a chosen root, breadth-first discovery in read-bit order
produces a canonical numbering. To forget the root, the checker takes the lexicographically least
serialization over all controls from which the entire working graph is reachable. The unique terminal
control is marked separately. Two such graphs are isomorphic exactly when these canonical serializations
agree.

give a smaller machine for Con(PA). The present construction therefore preserves the PA-calibrated
calculus.


10    Reproduction and evidence boundaries

Python 3.10 or later and its standard library suffice for the accepted construction and checks. From the
archive root, run

     python3 -B verify.py

Assertions must be enabled; the scripts reject optimization mode. The full check rebuilds both literal
tables and sources, recomputes the finite invariants, runs both independent physical proofs, replays
the whole blank initializations, and checks corpus novelty and the changed logical emitter. It works
in temporary copies to preserve the frozen packet. The optional --quick mode omits the universal
physical replays; it retains the other checks. Optional C++ searches are not required. The saved
receipts give independently reviewable results but do not replace execution of the verifier.
To rebuild this article, run build_report.sh; it invokes pdflatex twice with failure-on-error flags.
The exact table, source files, supporting proof texts and finite receipts are included. SHA256SUMS
binds the delivery. The attribution and license files identify the inherited James Chang, ProveIt and
symbolic-engine material.
The claims should be read at their actual levels:

  • Fresh finite facts: state count, table hashes, source maps, graph distinctions, mask closure, and
    complete startup equality.
  • Fresh symbolic mathematics: arbitrary-register physical contracts, four list lemmas and positive
    simulation durations.
  • New ordinary theorem: exact seeded source preservation and the resulting PA-provable halting
    equivalence with the parent.
  • Inherited ordinary theorem: the numerical calculus, strong command compiler and scheduler are
    calibrated to PA consistency.
  • Unclaimed: a smaller machine, a general lower bound, a new end-to-end proof-assistant theorem,
    or an iPRA proof of the full consistency equivalence.


11    Further research questions

  1. Can the new helper pairs (39, 21), (108, 17), and (221, 144) support a reader allocation of effective
     size 59? With the current 134-control fixed part, that would give 193 states. A search must
     include the source invariant and all physical branch contracts, not just the new pair identities.
  2. Can one combine two different known-zero registers in a larger decoder gadget to replace three
     readers by two? The four certified final-register variants provide different interfaces for such a
     search. Longer detours would need a positive local simulation proof.

  3. Can the fixed controller be reassociated to shorten all return addresses, including startup and

  4. Can a smaller finite invariant or a structured loop invariant replace the 263,180-pair certificate?
