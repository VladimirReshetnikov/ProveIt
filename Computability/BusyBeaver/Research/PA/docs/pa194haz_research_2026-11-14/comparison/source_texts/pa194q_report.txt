                           PA194Q: a different 194-state
                             PA consistency machine
                      Corpus comparison, a changed axiom emitter,
                  and a second construction with an invariant certificate

                         Research prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov

                                 13 November 2026, Pacific time


                                               Abstract
    We construct PA194Q, a deterministic binary single-tape machine with 194 working states, whose
blank-tape nonhalting is equivalent to Con(PA) by an ordinary mathematical proof formalizable in
PA. No machine with fewer than 194 working states was established. The result meets the
requested alternative of a materially different 194-state design.
    PA194Q exchanges the two premises of the quantifier-distribution axiom emitted by the existing
separate-head source. The source transformation was proposed in earlier larger-machine research;
the new contribution is its complete 194-state realization, decoder and compatible folds, exact source
binding, and independent literal-tape verification. Its resolved source differs at 79 of 3,649 instructions
from PA194C. Both proof calculi have the same theorems by explicit, conclusion-preserving proof
translations. Its allocation is 134 + 67 − 7 = 194.
    The fresh comparison is pinned to ProveIt commit 17f92105e2bc4317feae6c219cb2143437
b482ff. It includes 41 delivered packets and recognizes 4,417 literal-table occurrences, 620 byte
forms and 446 action-graph classes. The eight existing 194-state graphs include a rejected scanner,
repeated copies of PA194C, and previously supplied symmetric, alternative-decoder and zero-jump
variants. PA194Q is absent even under arbitrary control renaming, change of start, tape reflection
and symbol complementation.
    All 4,749 arbitrary-register source outcomes pass two physical checks; the independent checker
rederives four list lemmas from literal rows. An exact 57,922-step blank initialization reaches the
intended complete source frame. A second new 194-state machine, PA194CSZ, combines symmetric
Shift with a zero-register jump using a 263,180-pair inductive certificate. The archive contains both
complete tables, deterministic builders, source maps, verifiers, certificates, comparison data, this
PDF and its complete LaTeX source. The logical PA calibration is explicitly inherited; no new
end-to-end Lean or Rocq theorem or completed iPRA endpoint is asserted.

1     Result and precise machine convention
The principal machine is machine/pa194q.tm. Its working states are 0, . . . , 193, initial state is 0, and
terminal halt label is 194. The single tape is two-way infinite, with alphabet {0, 1} and all initial cells
zero. The head initially scans cell zero. Each transition writes one bit and moves exactly one cell
left or right. The table has 388 rows, two for every working state and none for the halt label. All
working states are reachable in the pointed transition graph; this is not a claim that the actual blank
computation visits each state.
Each row has the form

                             state    read       write       L-or-R   next-state.

The authoritative dense-table SHA-256 is

      48e98b5504dc5b3f20a2d040cb54ed1c96bee4c5e32ea27ae98ffada4dce7ea1.

The optional second table is secondary/pa194csz/machine_csz/pa194CSZ.tm, with the same tape,
state, start and halt conventions. Its SHA-256 is

      62e93098caf7c09ff2938d07064f0dbbb47c71ec369892968258fff708592269.

Appendices A and B print every transition.
Let HM be the usual arithmetic sentence asserting a finite literal halting trace from the specified blank
start. The principal conclusion is
                                             (                          )
                                     PA ⊢ ¬HPA194Q ↔ Con(PA) .

The new preservation theorem is PA ⊢ HPA194Q ↔ HPA194C . It is proved through interderivable
numerical calculi and their source simulations, not by asserting that the two instruction graphs are
equal. The second construction instead has a step-for-step source preservation proof on an inductive
invariant. Section 5 separates these new results from the inherited PA calibration.

2.4   Formalization status

The current PA README explicitly states that the machine-specific iPRA consistency reductions

nonhalting. The supplied later manuscripts give an ordinary syntactic PA calibration addressing that
converse; this report uses it as an identified mathematical dependency, and does not relabel it as a new

3     The new emitter and its 194-state realization

3.1   A changed axiom at unchanged stack position

With I(a, b) = 4p(a, b) and U (D, A) = 4p(A, D) + 2, define

                         P = U (D, A),    Q = U (D, I(A, H)),    R = U (D, H).

The existing sixth head is P → (Q → R); PA194Q emits Q → (P → R). It remains the second
generated stack entry below the separate diagonal head. Every other emitted head, guard, command
and scheduler component is retained.
This is a different numerical computation. At A = 1, D = H = 0, (P, Q, R) = (6, 42, 2). The old head
has code 31,609,172; the new head has code 76,268. The emitted theorem families are interderivable,
but the machine does not enumerate identical codes in identical stores. Exactly 79 of the 3,649 resolved
source instructions change. All others, including their targets, agree with PA194C. The complete
190-token AX word differs only in its 19-token sixth-head block. The new source’s longest address has
length 36, compared with 37 for PA194C.
The premise exchange itself is not claimed as a new logical discovery. It appears among the supplied
earlier source-variant proposals, including the PA195 diagonal-combined research [4]. What was not in
the pinned corpus is this 194-state literal realization and its full source and physical certificate.

4     Physical verification and its logical scope
The logical registers are (N, T, W, A, D, H, X, Y, L), represented by nine unary blocks in a fixed physical
order. A source frame contains a binary source address p, a zero gap of length 48 + n − |p| with
n ≥ 0, a delimiter, these nine register blocks and an arbitrary finite nonempty spare list. Thus the
address-plus-gap length is 48 + n; no shorter-header contract is claimed. The checks quantify over
arbitrary natural register values, both decrement outcomes and arbitrary spare values and length. They
do not sample a bounded range of register values. Successor frames stay in this domain: the header
parameter never decreases, and an increment appends a one-valued spare while other primitive steps
retain the spare list.
The first checker uses the inherited arithmetic-block contracts and verifies all 4,749 primitive outcomes.
It takes 1,540,295 symbolic transitions, with at most 497 per outcome. The second checker imports
neither the optimizing compiler nor the first checker’s arithmetic-block oracle. It rederives four generic
list-sweep induction lemmas from the literal rows, resolves the source graph using two independent
methods, and then checks all 4,749 outcomes. Its replay uses 1,124,844 symbolic transitions, at most
403 per outcome. Every certified physical duration has a positive constant term, at least three.

continue nonhalting beyond t. This bounded argument is formalizable in PA, including its nonstandard
models. It does not assume the desired nonhalting conclusion.
The executable verifiers are supporting mathematical certificates and trusted programs, not a new
foundational proof kernel. The retained Lean files identify inherited interfaces and the existing
implicational exchange theorem. This archive does not claim that a new end-to-end Lean or Rocq
build proves the consistency biconditional.

5.2   A proof translation independent of quantifier distribution

The unchanged restricted implicational basis derives, uniformly in all numerical codes a, b, c, the
exchange formula                (            )    (           )
                                  a → (b → c) → b → (a → c) .                                  (Q3)
This is already the theorem Dominated.exchange in PA196/Logic/ImplicationBasis.lean. Its
construction uses only the restricted propositional head, modus ponens, reflexivity of the numerical
bound, and the two inequalities a, b ≤ I(a, b). In particular it does not use either version of the sixth
head. It also needs no rule treating code one as falsity.
For clarity, (Q3) is an intuitionistic implicational tautology. From assumptions f : a → (b → c), b,
and a, two applications of modus ponens give c; three applications of the implicational deduction
construction give (Q3). The K and S combinators used by that construction are derived from the same
unchanged restricted basis. The optional certificate supplied with this report checks a finite K, S, MP
derivation of this exact exchange formula.
Apply (Q3) at (a, b, c) = (Q, P, R) and use the new axiom Q → (P → R) as its minor premise. This
derives the old axiom P → (Q → R). Conversely, (Q3) at (P, Q, R) derives the new axiom from the
old one. These are uniform finite derivations for every triple of parameter values D, A, H.
Let C be the pinned separate-diagonal-head calculus and CQ the calculus with (Q1)’s replacement.
Transform a C derivation recursively: retain every unchanged axiom; replace a sixth-head leaf by
the new sixth-head axiom followed by the first exchange derivation above; recursively transform the
premises at modus ponens and generalization nodes. Every conclusion is preserved literally. The
converse transformation uses the other exchange derivation. Thus there are primitive-recursive proof
transformations F, G whose correctness PA proves:
                                     Prf C (e, B) =⇒ Prf CQ (F (e), B),
                                                                                                    (Q4)
                                    Prf CQ (e, B) =⇒ Prf C (G(e), B).
There is no substitution of arbitrary formulas into a freshness-restricted gate. Only the numerical
parameters of the unrestricted implicational exchange template are instantiated. In particular (Q4)
proves, inside PA, equivalence of the two existential refutation predicates with conclusion code one.

The code-zero alias. These are numerical derivations, so they include zero among the allowed
formula codes. The identity I(0, 0) = 0 never violates a modus-ponens step: its code equality remains
exactly the equality required by the rule. In the arithmetic interpretation of formulas, T (B) denotes
the formula produced by a syntactic translation, not an arithmetic truth predicate. There is a uniform
proof-producing construction of the instancewise PA equivalence

5.4    All-store completeness and syntactic soundness

For a finite CQ proof e, compile axiom leaves by their selectors, generalization by the preceding word,
and an MP node with major proof e1 of I(a, b) and minor proof e0 of a by

                            Discard; Push; We1 ; Push; We0 ; Push; S; S; MPb ; S.

Induction on the finite proof tree proves the exact invariant: from every ⟨t, w, 0, 0⟩, the word We either
halts or returns ⟨concl(e), w, 0, 0⟩. Therefore, if e proves one, the word

                                              AXQ
                                                0,0,0 ; We ; Pop                                      (Q10)

halts from every incoming store. This all-store assertion handles the inherited scheduler’s retention of
the store between unsuccessful trials. An assumption that every trial resets the store would be wrong.
The unchanged exhaustive scheduler eventually tests the word (Q10), so a refutation yields a source
halt.
Conversely annotate the visible top, cached head, and each live canonical stack entry by derivations
in CQ . Zero has the fixed derivation ρ8 (0, 0, 0, 0) = 0, so a single zero proof supplies the total stack’s
padding. AX emits its own axiom heads, the saved theorem, and a generalization of the cached theorem;

7.2   A finite common invariant proves source preservation

For a concrete register vector r ∈ N9 , define its positivity mask
                                            8
                                            ∑
                                  µ(r) =          2i [ ri > 0 ] ∈ {0, . . . , 511}.
                                            i=0

The finite abstract transition system acts on pairs (p, m) of a source site and a positivity mask. A
jump retains the mask. Incrementing ri sets bit i. A decrement with bit i zero takes the zero successor
and retains the mask; with bit i one it takes the positive successor, with bit i either retained or cleared.
A halt has no successors. Every concrete step projects to one of these abstract steps. In the positive
decrement case, the two abstract possibilities distinguish ri = 1 from ri > 1; no bounded-register
assumption is involved.
Starting with (25, 1), the two source graphs have the same abstract reachable closure R, containing
exactly 263,180 pairs. The delivered finite certificate checks all of the following:
   1. (25, 1) ∈ R;
   2. R is closed under every abstract successor for both graphs;
   3. the two abstract successor sets agree at every element of R;
   4. the 32 masks occurring in R at site 26 all have bit 6 zero.
The first checker computes the two closures independently. The second checker reads the finite relation
and verifies its seed, range, closure, common successors, and site-26 property without importing the
builder.
The concrete clean source seed is

                                    (p, r) = (25, (2, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0)),

whose mask is 1. Induction on execution length and abstract-step soundness therefore prove r6 = 0
whenever either reachable execution visits site 26. At that site, the new decrement consequently leaves
the complete register vector unchanged and goes to site 34, exactly like the old jump. All other source
instructions are equal. A second induction shows that the two concrete source executions have identical
program counters and register vectors at every finite step, including the same halting outcome.
This proof makes no consistency assumption and uses no empirical observation of a long blank run.
The masks overapproximate unbounded register values. The relation is finite and explicit, and the
projection and induction are primitive recursive arithmetic reasoning available in PA.
