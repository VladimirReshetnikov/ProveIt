\section{Concrete lessons from ZFC and concise iPRA}

\paragraph{Partial selectors can expose a missed physical fold.}
The accepted ZFC K216 reparses a 4,174-instruction region as
$((A,X),(B,Y))$. Only the zero branches $A,B$ are prescribed by the
selector; $X,Y$ remain live and require verification. Native states 164
and 183 share their read-one transition, while incoming edges leave
different predecessor bits. Existing helper 201 reads that marker,
chooses the appropriate old endpoint, and restores the tape in three
steps. This removes state 183 without a new helper. Earlier searches
limited such reparses to 600 leaves, so their negative results did not
cover this construction. This gives a concrete reason to search larger
partial-provider regions in PA while checking every retained branch.\footnote{\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/4be5c6e3a10292a7466f5d3687e3b39615ff049b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/candidate-k216/README.md}{ZFC/candidate-k216/README.md}, ``What saves the state'' and ``Attribution and prior search scope''.}

\paragraph{Read fields only where a command needs them.}
The command-permuted K216 moves the shared counter reader into individual
commands. Its primary small-command tags are NIP0, DROP1, MP2;
permuting these closed bodies also changes blank initialization to reach
a clean theorem seed. The three-step replacement
$81/0:0L92\mapsto0L111$ frees the old $92/1$ role. K215 combines this
reader schedule with the partial selector through a fresh allocation;
its 202 source subtrees become 215 physical states, rather than a state
count inferred from source size.\footnote{\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/4be5c6e3a10292a7466f5d3687e3b39615ff049b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/candidate-k216/alternatives/permuted/README.md}{ZFC/candidate-k216/alternatives/permuted/README.md} and \href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/4be5c6e3a10292a7466f5d3687e3b39615ff049b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/candidate-k215/README.md}{ZFC/candidate-k215/README.md}.}
The applicable PA question is which field readers, return paths and
initialization roles can share controls. Source savings alone answer
none of those physical obligations.

\paragraph{Fuse proof rules with a proved recovery translation.}
ConciseIPRA uses iterator terms $I(n,a;u.h)$ and formulas built from
equations, $Q_yB=B(0)\to\forall yB(y)$ and
$D_{x,y}B=\exists x(B(x,0)\to\forall yB(x,y))$.
Its \texttt{E3Prf} calculus has three rule families: Use, Intro and
Discharge, together with a fixed reflexivity clause. Discharge both opens
a witness and performs one deterministic evaluation step; a compound
auxiliary formula selects identity evaluation and recovers ordinary
elimination. Lean proves \texttt{e3prf\_iff\_vprf} for every clause
context. This supplies a concrete model for combining interfaces while
proving that each original operation remains expressible.\footnote{\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/4be5c6e3a10292a7466f5d3687e3b39615ff049b/Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/NativeEvalDischarge.lean}{ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/NativeEvalDischarge.lean}.}

\paragraph{Keep the theory and formalization claims separate.}
The concise native calculus has a completed Lean bi-interpretation with
iPRA, including \texttt{native\_inconsistent\_iff}; the README reports
no \texttt{sorry}, extra axioms or \texttt{native\_decide}. K215 likewise
has exact-table external Lean and Rocq endpoints, while its internal PA
endpoint remains open.\footnote{\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/4be5c6e3a10292a7466f5d3687e3b39615ff049b/Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/NativeBiInterpretation.lean}{ConciseIPRA/Lean/ConciseIPRA/NativeBiInterpretation.lean}; \href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/4be5c6e3a10292a7466f5d3687e3b39615ff049b/Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/README.md}{ConciseIPRA/README.md}, ``Lean formalization''; K215 README above.}
These results establish reusable proof methods. A concise iPRA checker
does not itself recognize PA refutations: a PA calibration and a complete
binary implementation, including syntax and initialization costs, are
still needed for this task.
