\begin{abstract}
The strict target of this investigation is a binary, single-tape machine
with fewer than 196 working states whose blank-tape nonhalting is equivalent
to the consistency of ordinary first-order Peano arithmetic. No such machine
is established here. We compare the literal 208-, 209-, and 196-state
constructions, provide two new explicit 196-state reader layouts with complete
startup and executable symbolic checks, and give small certificates excluding
specified repairs of five earlier near misses. A new equivalent axiom emitter
uses 199 macro tokens and 3,773 primitive instructions instead of 201 and
3,812, with a uniform proof-translation argument verifiable in PA. An explicit
67-rule grammar is supplied; its shortest verified tape implementation here
has 198 states and does not meet the target. A new source search permits jump depths to change while
preserving the exact ordinal control graph.
We distinguish these results from a smaller-machine construction and from
an exported PA proof of the full consistency biconditional.
\end{abstract}

\section{Target, conventions, and status}

We use the same model as PA196: a deterministic Turing machine on a two-way
infinite tape over $\{0,1\}$, initially all zero, with one designated initial
working state and one separate halting control. Each transition writes a bit,
moves exactly one cell left or right, and changes control. The halt control
is excluded from the working-state count. We do not change the alphabet,
preload a nonblank program, or count only states seen in a finite simulation.

For a literal machine $M$, let $H_M$ be its arithmetical blank-input halting
sentence and $N_M=\neg H_M$. The strongest requested endpoint is
\[
 \PA\vdash N_M\leftrightarrow\Con(\PA),\qquad |Q_M|<196.
\]
A finite nonhalting prefix proves neither half of this statement. A
state-count score for a source grammar is also insufficient: the physical
interpreter, startup, and complete tape endpoints must still work.

The archive includes a fresh literal comparison, two explicit reader layouts
in \emph{rotated196}, an equivalent numerical axiom emitter and its grammar
witness, and finite obstruction certificates for extending fixed failed folds. The
rotation preserves 196 working states. Its symbolic checks use the
unchanged register-engine contracts of the upstream development; they have
not been exported as a new Lean build or an internal PA derivation.
This qualification applies wherever the changed machine is discussed.

\section{What the 196-state construction already saves}

The published PA196 construction changes the axiom command and its layout,
while retaining the 36-state tape engine of PA209. Its physical state budget
is
\[
 \underbrace{36}_{\text{engine}}+
 \underbrace{13}_{\text{leaf routers}}+
 \underbrace{147}_{\text{readers}}=196.
\]
Its reader states implement 159 distinct internal source nodes. The existing
context-dependent sharing is already counted in 147; its six axiom-body
folds cannot be subtracted a second time. See the upstream paper for the
logical recovery of the removed axiom head~\cite{pa196paper}.

Let $p(a,b)=\binom{a+b+1}{2}+b$. PA196 uses
\[
 I(a,b)=4p(a,b),\quad M(a,b)=4p(a,b)+1,\quad
 U(a,B)=4p(B,a)+2,\quad E(a,b)=4p(a,b)+3.
\]
Relative to PA209, the universal constructor transposes the pairing
arguments. The axiom command drops the old seventh head, adds guarded
equality vacuity, rearranges the other heads, and changes some antecedent
orders. Its body has 201 macro tokens, represented by a 68-node binary
grammar and 62 physical reader states after sharing. The complete source
shrinks from 3,948 to 3,812 primitive occurrences.

U208's last one-state saving uses a different local pattern. In its native
U209 parent, one predecessor enters the state to be deleted from opposite
head directions, and a helper uses that entry history to select the proper
continuation. A direct scan of all PA196 rows finds no state whose two rows
write $0$ and move left, respectively write $1$ and move right, into the
same working target. Thus the exact mixed-entry U208 pattern cannot be
transplanted by renaming. This finite fact does not rule out a more general
history-dependent fold.

\section{The consistency theorem: the exported direction matters}

The public theorem inventory for PA196 explicitly includes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:known-direction}
 \PA\vdash N_{196}\to\Con(\PA).
\end{equation}
Classically, this is equivalent to a refutation of PA yielding a halting
trace of the literal machine. The published development also establishes
external nonhalting and independence, with the stated metatheoretic
consistency and soundness assumptions~\cite{pa196result,pa196paper}.
The analogous PA209 inventory has the same internal direction.

The checked inventory does not supply the other internal implication
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remaining-direction}
 \PA\vdash\Con(\PA)\to N_{196},
 \quad\text{equivalently}\quad
 \PA\vdash H_{196}\to\neg\Con(\PA).
\end{equation}
This is an audit of the exported statements, not a claim that the converse
is false or unprovable. ProveIt's existing \file{PA-BRIDGE.md} and November
13 manuscript already discuss this distinction~\cite{proveitbridge}.
It is confirmed here rather than presented as a new discovery.

An external semantic argument about each ordinary finite formula cannot
simply be read as a single internal theorem for arbitrary coded formulas
and traces in every model of PA. A satisfactory internal converse can
instead use syntax: extract a finite command derivation from an arbitrary
coded literal halting trace, translate its formulas and axiom instances
into PA proofs, and verify the transformation uniformly. PA induction can
then reason about the codes without a truth predicate for all formulas.
The totality and correctness of the extraction and proof transformations
are proof obligations, not consequences of a long finite run.

For a descendant machine, a PA-formalizable halting-preserving simulation
would transport whatever endpoint is proved for its parent. It does not
create a missing parent theorem. The present artifact neither exports a
new internal biconditional nor treats a Lean metatheorem as a PA derivation.

The executable symbolic verification checks 4,959 instruction/branch

The checker retains arbitrary natural gap lengths and complete symbolic

all tape contents and the head, rather than merely checking a return to the

This gives a much stronger validation than a finite blank prefix. The
ordinary composition argument is as follows. The clean configurations
represent the same source seed. At each source site, both physical layouts
execute a positive-length block to the source successor's full frame.
The retained source control graphs agree exactly. By induction, finite
source executions are represented in both layouts, with the same halt
instruction. Positive block lengths and total primitive blocks are needed
to turn this into both directions of halting preservation; they must not be
replaced by endpoint guesses.

The certificate computation uses the generic register contracts transcribed

executable symbolic induction checker, not a new proof-assistant kernel
check. Neither an end-to-end Lean proof of this literal changed table nor a
PA consistency biconditional is claimed. The generated source definition is
supplied for subsequent formal integration.

\subsection{Why this rotation was worth constructing}

The interpreter's return scan distinguishes its left marker using seven
zeros. A proposed six-zero version removes one scanner state. In literal
labels this would redirect row $23/0$ from $(0,L,34)$ to $(0,L,69)$;
in native labels the proposed new target is 75. It fails on the unmodified
address layout, so it is \emph{not} applied to the delivered machine.

The relevant source-address obstruction is the pattern \texttt{1000000}:
a run of six zeros following a path one can imitate a marker. Six leading
zeros can end at the actual left marker; seven leading zeros require
separate exclusion. Checking only the maximum address length misses this
issue. Address constraints are a search guide, not a substitute for the
full primitive runtime checks.

Before the rotation, source site 3693 has address
\texttt{1010101000000}. Afterwards it has
\texttt{101010100000}. This removes the sole offending path outside the
axiom body. Eighteen offending axiom-body addresses remain. The delivered
196-state table therefore keeps the original seven-zero scanner.

\section{New jump-retargeting source search}\label{sec:retarget}

The prior directional-rotation search preserves structured jump depths.
We enlarge the local search by permitting a jump's depth argument to change,
while retaining its exact ordinal destination. For each reassociated tree:
\begin{enumerate}
\item retain the primitive instruction order and check every decrement's
zero and positive successors;
\item for each forward or backward jump, enumerate the appropriate ancestor
boundaries and choose the depth that reaches its old destination;
\item reject a missing destination or a required depth above 3;
\item rebuild the tree and compare its complete resolved ordinal graph with
the original graph.
\end{enumerate}
The source transformation is therefore a preservation of the resolved
register program, not an assumption that tree associativity always
preserves control flow.

There are 246 first global binary rotations of the distinct internal
subtrees. Exactly 173 survive the full control-flow check. Of these, 12
require nontrivial jump retargeting beyond the prior criterion. Their
internal-node/maximum-zero-run census is:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrr@{}}
\toprule
Internal nodes & Maximum zero run & Accepted first rotations\\
\midrule
159&6&36\\
159&7&32\\
160&6&85\\
160&7&19\\
163&6&1\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The 73 rejections comprise 46 changed decrement branches, 23 unrepresentable
jump destinations, and four required depths above 3. None of the accepted
first rotations has fewer than 159 internal nodes.

A second screen starts from the 11 newly retargeted first variants with at
most 160 internal nodes and tests every second global rotation, applying
the same repair and full graph comparison. It checks 2,706 ordered cases
and finds no source below 159 internal nodes. This is not an exhaustive
search of all second rotations from all first variants, and it says nothing

allocation trials of selected variants produced no accepted smaller table;
timeouts were not treated as unsatisfiability proofs.

\section{Two-trace certificates for fixed near misses}\label{sec:obstruction}

Five earlier one-row near misses were used as fixed starting points for a
new repair screen. The question is deliberately narrow: after making the
specified state identification and keeping its first patched row fixed,
can changing one additional transition repair every required local block?

The contexts come from the radius-six blank-start window overapproximation,
which contains 270,067 state/windows. A block must match one projected
parent step in at most 128 positive child steps, remain within the 13 cells
$[-6,6]$, and restore the complete represented tape and the required head
position. The closure admits abstract halt, so it is not a nonhalting proof.

\begin{tabular}{@{}rrlrr@{}}
\toprule

\midrule

96&56&$56/1\mapsto(1,R,67)$&96&203, 3915\\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are the original PA196 literal labels. Mask bit $i+6$ is the bit at
tape offset $i$.

\begin{lemma}[Necessary intersection for a one-row repair]
Fix a failed block and the exact required initial and final configurations.
Any change to one transition address that alters the block before its
specified failure must change an address queried by that block.
Consequently, a single additional row capable of repairing several failed
blocks must belong to the intersection of their queried-address sets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the changed address is not queried, induction on executed steps gives
exactly the same control, head, and tape at each step up to failure. The
modified execution therefore has the same failure. Apply this argument to
each required block.
\end{proof}

For each row of the table, two failed blocks have address-set intersection
contained entirely in the already fixed patch. No eligible additional row
can therefore repair both. The small JSON certificates retain the complete
traces, parent endpoints, and window membership. A dictionary-based trace
generator is checked by a separately implemented bit-vector replay.
\file{repair_obstructions/repair_obstruction.py} recomputes the closure and
verifies all five certificates.

The conclusion concerns these fixed first patches and this certificate
template. It is not a lower bound on 195-state machines, an exhaustive
unrestricted two-row search, or proof of different eventual halting behavior.
Sharper reachable invariants, a wider matching region, multiple parent steps,
a different tape representation, or two more changed rows remain outside it.

\subsection{Two folds sharing a new helper}

The eighteen additional U208 machines suggest a different way to spend
a helper state: remove two old states and introduce one shared helper,
so that $196-2+1=195$. The delivered \file{shared_helper/} component
examines this mechanism directly on PA196. There are fourteen eligible
pairs of states with opposite one-step predecessor tags and one common
transition row, giving 88 disjoint pair choices. Both retained-state
orientations are considered independently. Each helper target ranges over
all 196 old working controls and the helper itself.

With bit-preserving, right-moving helper rows, the complete count is
\[
 88\cdot4\cdot197^2=13,660,768
\]
combined patches. Allowing both write bits and both movement directions
independently in each helper row gives sixteen action patterns and
\textbf{218,572,288 patches}. No patch survives the exact whole-tape,
two-cell, positive-block test with a 64-step bound. The broader family
includes the narrower one; these counts should not be added as disjoint
cases. The C++20 source and pinned-input Python replay wrapper are included.
The search does not require either individual fold to succeed in isolation.
It therefore tests actual interaction through the shared helper, including
helper self-visits. Longer blocks, larger footprints, different entry
predicates, or more changed rows remain outside this bounded result.

\section{Using the concise-iPRA work without changing the target}

The concise-iPRA material~\cite{ipra} is useful as a source of compilation ideas:
explicit syntax, restricted proof interfaces, grammar reuse, and a small
trusted checker boundary. Its axiom system cannot simply replace PA's
axiom system in this task. A contradiction search for a different theory
has that theory's consistency as its natural endpoint; an appropriate
uniform calibration to ordinary PA would be an additional theorem.

Two mechanisms in that work are particularly relevant. Its
\emph{one-context} and \emph{one-sided transport} presentations recover
general equality reasoning from restricted transport rules. Its
\emph{direct closure} presentation replaces a witnessed closure rule by a
direct universal closure, with an explicit recovery proof. These suggest
looking for redundant logical interfaces before optimizing their emitted
instruction trees. The weak-cover analysis in the appendix develops this
approach for reverse membership rewriting, including the bootstrap that a
mere semantic-equivalence argument would omit. The distributed gate in
Section~\ref{sec:distributed-gate} gives a source saving while retaining
the original PA196 numeric calculus and guard.

These are transfers of proof-design methods. They do not identify the
iPRA checker with the PA contradiction search. A shorter high-level
checker, a smaller abstract syntax tree, or fewer macro tokens does not
automatically produce fewer tape states; the complete binary machine
still has to be compiled and verified.

\section{Reproduction and evidence boundaries}

The mathematical, source, startup, and symbolic-frame checkers use Python
3.10 or later and the standard library. The shared-helper enumeration also
requires a C++20 compiler, invoked by its Python wrapper.
Run them without \texttt{-O}; assertion-based symbolic checks must remain
enabled. From the extracted archive directory:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 verify_all.py
pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error report.tex
pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error report.tex
\end{verbatim}
The archive includes the report source and compiled PDF, full transition
tables, reference hashes and notices, exact source programs, verification
scripts, and recorded results. The original upstream proof libraries are
referenced at their pinned revisions; they are not represented as newly
rebuilt in this investigation.

The evidence separates four different levels: literal table and startup
checks; complete source control-flow checks; executable all-natural
symbolic frame contracts using inherited engine lemmas; and end-to-end
formal arithmetic theorems. The first three are supplied for the successful
reader rotations and the new 198-state compilation. The fourth is not supplied for those changed tables, and
no strict state-count improvement is claimed.

\section{Further research questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Can the remaining eighteen six-zero axiom addresses be eliminated
while keeping at most 62 physical axiom-body readers and preserving all
startup and primitive contracts? This would make the one-state scanner
reduction a concrete next candidate.
\item Can source reassociation and retargeting be optimized jointly with
helper-row allocation, rather than fixing old reader assignments before
searching? Existing compatible-helper restrictions exclude some possible
layouts.
\item Can the distributed-gate grammar be allocated with enough compatible
folds to beat PA196? The source saving and the 198-state compilation are
established here; the lost sharing is now the concrete obstacle. A joint
objective must include entry-bit conditions and transient address changes,
not only a binary grammar's rule count.
\item Can two or more coordinated helper edits repair a different
195-state quotient with a complete, independently checkable simulation?
The two-trace certificates identify why one further row is insufficient
for the five fixed first patches; they do not close this larger space.
\item Can the new weak-cover free-instance interface in the next section
be compiled with fewer states than the current pointed-separation emitter?
The logical recovery should be kept separate from numerical layout scores.
\item Can the reverse PA trace-to-refutation transformation be completed
uniformly for the original literal PA196 table, then transported to any
verified descendant? This would close the internal biconditional independently
of a state-count improvement.
\end{enumerate}
