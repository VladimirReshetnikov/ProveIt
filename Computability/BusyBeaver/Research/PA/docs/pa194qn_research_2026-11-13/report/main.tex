\section{Outcome and exact machine convention}

The primary target was a machine with fewer than 194 working states.
The completed searches did not establish one. The fallback result is the
fully specified machine \M. Its state count is
\[
134\text{ fixed controls}+67\text{ axiom-reader controls}
             -7\text{ simultaneous folds}=194.
\]
The tape alphabet is $\{0,1\}$, the tape is two-way infinite, and every cell
is initially zero. The head begins at coordinate zero in state zero.
Working states are $0,\ldots,193$; label 194 is terminal and has no outgoing
rows. Every defined transition writes one bit and moves exactly one cell,
left or right. All 194 working controls are reachable in the transition
graph. Graph reachability is not a claim that every control is visited on
the actual blank computation.

The authoritative file is \file{machine/machine.tm}. Its SHA-256 is
\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
d1cf0f6e7a3c8f999d045694efc107dd1f9a03d45dd6c15e3d197ff7754c7231
\end{center}
Appendix~\ref{app:table} prints the entire table. A row in the machine file
has the form \texttt{state read write direction next}. Native labels,
the dense-label bijection, the unfolded 201-control parent, and every
source instruction are also supplied.

\subsection{What is new, and what is inherited}

The Q emitter\cite{q} exchanges the two curried premises of the universal
distribution head. The N controller performs successful modus ponens without
pushing a dummy zero, retaining the result in the visible top and in the
offset\cite{n}. \M\ combines these two mechanisms while retaining N's original
Shift guard $t+h=1$. Neither constituent idea is claimed as new in isolation.
The new object is their exact 194-state realization, its compatible source
and command interface, its preservation proof, and its verified table.

The combination is substantively different from both parents. Relative to
PA194N, it changes an emitted formula and 79 source instructions. Relative
to PA194Q, it changes both the MP result convention and the Shift guard.
For example, $I(0,2)=20$, and MP with parameter two sends
\[
(0,w,0,20)\longmapsto(2,w,2,0)
\]
in \M, whereas Q's cached controller sends that store to $(0,w,0,2)$.
Also, \M's Shift halts from $(1,0,0,0)$, whereas the cached-only guard
does not. These are differences of command behavior, in addition to the
literal graph distinction.

The finite novelty check compares complete transition tuples, allowing
arbitrary state renaming, any graph-spanning root choice, left/right
reflection, and symbol complementation. It finds no match among all 34
recognized existing 194-state graph classes. This statement is explicitly
scoped to the finite literal-table corpus; it does not assert that the
combination was absent from every possible informal idea or source-only
encoding. In particular, a prior NS search already screened Q and D
emitters under the different NS controller.

\section{Numerical formulas and the changed axiom}
\label{sec:logic}

Use Cantor pairing and constructors
\[
\begin{split}
p(a,b)&=(a+b)(a+b+1)/2+b,\qquad I(A,B)=4p(A,B),\\
M(a,b)&=4p(a,b)+1,\quad U(a,B)=4p(B,a)+2,\quad E(a,b)=4p(a,b)+3.
\end{split}
\]
Code zero denotes truth; code one is the contradiction $M(0,0)$.
Write $N(B)=I(B,1)$, $B\wedge C=N(I(B,N(C)))$, and
$\exists_a B=N(U(a,N(B)))$. The zero alias $I(0,0)=0$ is real and must
be handled as an equality of numerical constructors, not denied by an
assumption that formulas are a free term algebra.

For parameters $D,A,H$, the six ordinary emitted heads, in emission order,
are
\begin{align*}
L_1&=I(\exists_D U(D,H),H),\\
L_2&=I(U(D,U(A,H)),U(A,U(D,H))),\\
L_3&=I(M(H,D),I(E(H,A),M(A,D))),\\
L_4&=I(E(H,D),I(M(A,H),M(A,D))),\\
L_5&=\exists_D E(H,D),\\
L_6'&=I(U(D,I(A,H)),I(U(D,A),U(D,H))).
\end{align*}
The old sixth head was
\[
L_6=I(U(D,A),I(U(D,I(A,H)),U(D,H))).
\]
The additional heads are the diagonal instance
$\Gamma(A)=I(A,U(A,A))$ and the dominated implication instance
\[
\rho(D,A,H,d)=I(I(I(H,d),D),I(I(D,H),I(A,H))),\qquad d\le D.
\]
The guard and separation gate are unchanged. In fully explicit notation,
\begin{align*}
V&=I(M(H,D),U(A,M(H,D))),& P&=I(A,M(A,D)),\\
B&=U(D,I(M(H,D),P)\wedge I(M(A,D),A)),&
Q_s&=U(A,I(B,N(P))),\\
g(H,D,A)&=\begin{cases}N(I(V,Q_s)),&A\ne H\text{ and }A\ne D,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\end{align*}

\begin{lemma}[Primitive-recursive logical transport]
Replacing the universally instantiated head $L_6$ by $L_6'$ leaves the
numerical theorem set unchanged. There are conclusion-preserving
primitive-recursive translations of finite derivations in both directions,
and PA proves their correctness.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The unchanged dominated implication basis derives K, S, and premise exchange
\[
I(I(P,I(Q,R)),I(Q,I(P,R))).
\]
One obtains the reverse implication by interchanging $P$ and $Q$.
Substitute $P=U(D,A)$, $Q=U(D,I(A,H))$, and $R=U(D,H)$, then apply MP.
At a changed axiom occurrence, replace that leaf by this fixed proof template;
preserve all other proof constructors. Structural recursion and induction
on the coded proof give the stated translations. The derivation of exchange
uses only the dominated implication basis and does not use either version
of universal distribution. It is therefore not circular.

The archive includes a separately replayed 61-line exchange certificate:
17 K instances, 14 S instances, and 30 MP steps, with no assumptions.
The inherited derivations of K and S are identified in
\file{references/ImplicationBasis.lean} and
\file{references/RestrictedImplication.lean}. Because the proof template
uses only numerical implication formation and MP, it remains valid in
the case $I(0,0)=0$.
\end{proof}

The formula change is numerically visible. For $D=A=0,H=1$ the three
subformulas $(P,Q,R)$ have codes $(2,146,6)$, and $L_6$ and $L_6'$ have
different codes. The change is not a global permutation of formula variables
or a relabeling of the TM's controls.

\section{The no-push command interface and its proof compiler}
\label{sec:commands}

Let $[a;w]=p(a,w)$, with total projections $\operatorname{hd}$ and
$\operatorname{tl}$. Thus $[0;0]=0$ and both projections of zero are zero.
Iterated brackets have their leftmost item at the top. A store is
$(t,w,d,h)$: visible top, proof stack, numerical offset, and cached head.

For $b=d+x$, the MP and Shift commands are
\begin{align*}
\MP_x(t,w,d,h)&=
\begin{cases}(b,w,b,0),&I(t,b)=h,\\(0,w,b,0),&I(t,b)\ne h,\end{cases}\\
\Shift(t,w,d,h)&=
\begin{cases}\mathsf{halt},&t+h=1,\\
(h,\operatorname{tl}w,d,\operatorname{hd}w),&t+h\ne1.
\end{cases}
\end{align*}
MP success preserves $w$ exactly. No cell is implicitly pushed by this
operation. For $D=d+x,H=h+y$, AX returns
\[
\AX_{A,x,y}(t,w,d,h)
 =\bigl(g(H,D,A),[q_1,\ldots,q_9,t;w],0,0\bigr),
\]
where
\[
(q_1,\ldots,q_9)=
(\Gamma(A),L_6',L_5,L_4,L_3,L_2,L_1,\rho(D,A,H,d),U(d,h)).
\]
All of $g,q_1,\ldots,q_8$ have residue zero modulo four, and
$q_9=U(d,h)\ge2$ has residue two. None is one, for arbitrary input
parameters, without assuming consistency. The free-algebra AX check verifies
the entire stack and gate, field reads and cleanup; it does not merely
check these residues.

\subsection{Total stack identities}

Words execute from left to right. Direct calculation gives
\begin{align*}
\AX_{A,x,y};\Shift^9(t,w,d,h)&=(q_8,[t;w],0,U(d,h)),\\
\AX_{A,x,y};\Shift^{10}(t,w,d,h)&=(U(d,h),w,0,t).
\end{align*}
These Shifts are safe because their tests include generated non-one
residues, or the universal code of value at least two.

Put $A_0=\AX_{0,0,0}$ and define
\[
\Push=A_0;\Shift^9;\MP_0,\quad
\Pop=A_0;\Shift^{11},\quad
\Discard=\MP_0,\quad\Offset_i=\MP_i.
\]
Their exact contracts are
\begin{align*}
\Push(t,w,d,h)&=(0,[t;w],0,0),\\
\Pop(t,[a;w],d,h)&=(t,w,0,a),\\
\Discard(t,w,0,h)&=(0,w,0,0),\\
\Offset_i(0,w,0,0)&=(0,w,i,0).
\end{align*}
For Push, the final MP compares an implication with a universal code and
therefore fails with zero offset. Pop's last Shift tests $U(d,h)+t\ge2$,
so Pop is total even when $t=1$. Discard works for arbitrary old cache:
with $d=x=0$, both outcomes of MP equal $(0,w,0,0)$. Offset works also
at $i=0$, where the same two outcomes coalesce.

These are numerical projection identities. They remain true when a zero
push collapses into an empty numerical stack. In particular,
$\Pop(t,0,d,h)=(t,0,0,0)$. No proof below depends on a nonexistent
distinguishable zero stack cell.

\subsection{Selectors, generalization, and MP}

For an arbitrary incoming $(t,w,0,0)$ and $1\le j\le7$, the selector
\[
\Discard;\AX_{A,D,H};\Shift^{j+1};\Pop^{9-j}
\]
returns $(q_j,w,0,0)$. The initial Discard makes the saved old top zero.
The Shifts select $q_j$; the Pops remove the unselected tail and saved zero.
The gate uses $\Discard;\AX_{A,D,H};\Pop^{10}$.
For $u\le a$, the dominated-head selector is
\[
\Discard;\Offset_u;\AX_{b,a-u,c};\Shift^9;\Pop.
\]
It returns $(\rho(a,b,c,u),w,0,0)$. Truth is supplied by Discard.
The exchange translation in Section~\ref{sec:logic} supplies the old
sixth head whenever a proof calls for it.

Generalization is compiled by
\[
\Gen_i=\Push;\Offset_i;\Shift;\AX_{i,0,0};\Shift^{10}.
\]
It sends $(a,w,0,0)$ to $(U(i,a),w,0,0)$, including $a=1$.
Before its AX, the store is $(0,w,i,a)$; all the displayed Shifts are safe.

At an MP node with minor $a$, major $I(a,b)$, and recursive proof words
$C(a)$ and $C(I(a,b))$, use
\[
\Discard;\Push;C(I(a,b));\Push;C(a);\Push;
                    \Shift;\Shift;\MP_b;\Pop.
\]
The first two commands reserve a zero below the premise computations.
If no recursive execution has halted, the suffix begins at
$(0,[a,I(a,b),0;w],0,0)$. Two Shifts either halt on $a=1$ or establish
$(a,[0;w],0,I(a,b))$. MP returns $(b,[0;w],b,0)$, and the final total Pop
returns $(b,w,0,0)$, including $b=1$. The final Pop is essential:
the single Shift appropriate to Q's cached-result compiler would not
implement this interface.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform proof compiler]
For every finite derivation $p$ in the changed calculus, the recursively
compiled word $C(p)$, starting from any $(t,w,0,0)$, either halts early
or returns $(\operatorname{concl}(p),w,0,0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the proof tree using the displayed selectors, generalization
identity, and reserved-zero MP suffix. Each recursive word preserves the
arbitrary underlying stack. Early halting is preserved by word extension.
The calculations include the cases $I(0,0)=0$ and $[0;0]=0$.
\end{proof}

If $p$ proves one, the word $A_0;C(p);\Shift$ halts from \emph{every}
incoming store. AX first clears the offset and cache. If no earlier halt
occurs, the final Shift tests $1+0=1$. This all-store contract is needed
because the scheduler carries its store between failed trials.

\section{From finite proofs to a PA consistency equivalence}
\label{sec:calibration}

\subsection{The exhaustive scheduler}

The source scheduler is inherited unchanged. With positive continuation
coding $E(\epsilon)=0$ and $E(c::w)=1+B_c(E(w))$, its command encodings are
\[
B_{\Shift}(z)=p(0,z),\quad B_{\MP_x}(z)=p(1,p(x,z)),\quad
B_{\AX_{A,x,y}}(z)=p(A+2,p(y,p(x,z))).
\]
Let $z=E(A_0;C(p);\Shift)$. Prefixing this all-store halting word by
$\AX_{k,0,0}$ gives fresh-candidate codes
\[
n_k=p(k+2,p(0,p(0,z))).
\]
They are strictly increasing and unbounded. The fresh counter begins at
two, so omission of zero and one loses none of this cofinal family.
For a positive continuation $l$, the next decoded residual is at most
$l-1$. Each trial therefore finishes or halts; the inherited arithmetic
macro loops have primitive-recursive terminating bounds. Finite induction
through earlier candidates reaches a selected $n_k$, unless a halt has
already occurred. The outer fresh-candidate code and the positive
continuation code differ by the displayed convention; conflating them
would invalidate this argument.

\subsection{Soundness of the command execution}

Annotate the visible top, cached head, and every live canonical stack
entry with a numerical-calculus derivation. Code zero has a fixed truth
derivation; it also annotates the virtual zero tail. For a nonzero Cantor
stack, its tail is strictly smaller than the stack code, giving a bounded
primitive-recursive construction of the canonical annotation. No theorem
annotation is imposed on the offset $d$.

AX adds valid heads, a generalization of the annotated old cache, and the
saved old top. On successful MP, the annotations of $t$ and $I(t,b)=h$
give a derivation of $b$; failure returns truth. Shift transports the
annotations. If its guard succeeds, $t+h=1$ implies that one of the two
annotated values is code one. Thus a source halt yields a finite refutation
in the numerical calculus. The proof updates are primitive recursive and
are verified by ordinary induction in PA.

\subsection{The inherited numerical-calculus calibration}

The supplied separate-head calibration\cite{cal} provides PA-verifiable proof-producing
translations between PA refutations and derivations of numerical code one.
Its mathematical texts are included in \file{references/logical/}, with
their source identities. This is an explicit dependency of the result,
not something inferred from finite simulation. The pertinent parts are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Interpret $M(a,b)$ by finite binary membership
\[
\operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\equiv
\exists u,v\,[s=(2u+1)2^z+v\ \wedge\ v<2^z],
\]
and $E$ by equality. PA proves $\operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\to z<s$,
hence refutes the translation of code one.
\item For each arithmetic formula $\theta$, induction produces a PA proof
of finite binary comprehension
\[
\forall n\,\exists s<2^n\,\forall z<n\,
(\operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\leftrightarrow\theta(z)).
\]
Substitution into this proof template is a syntactic primitive-recursive
operation. It does not assert a uniform arithmetic truth predicate.
\item Every variable label in a decoded formula of code $B$ is below $B$;
the case $B=0$ is handled separately. Therefore $\Gamma(B)$ has the
required fresh binder. This supports the replacement of the bundled
diagonal gate by its separate head.
\item The separation translation distinguishes the coincident-label case
from distinct labels. When labels coincide, the binder changes both
occurrences; the finite-comprehension matrix must change accordingly.
\item The completeness translation derives reverse membership rewriting
and neighborhood equality with fresh diagonal binders, then uses the
original refutation compiler. The removed equality-vacuity schema is not
silently assumed.
\item $I(0,0)=0$ is reconciled by fixed proof templates relating truth and
truth implying truth. It is not treated as literal free-syntax injectivity.
\end{enumerate}

The new exchange translation preserves these calibrations. The all-store
compiler and the scheduler then give, in PA, a finite source halt from
every PA refutation. Conversely, the annotation construction and inherited
arithmetic translation turn a source halt into a PA refutation. Thus
\[
\PA\vdash\left(\Halt(R_{QN})\leftrightarrow
          \exists p\,\Prf_{\PA}(p,\ulcorner0=1\urcorner)\right).
\]
No consistency assumption or reflection principle for PA is used to prove
this biconditional. It is a statement about finite proof and computation
codes. The actual nonhalting of the resulting machine is conditional on
the consistency of PA.

\section{Binding the source to the exact 194-state table}

\subsection{Source change and allocation}

The inherited source has nine registers $(N,T,W,A,D,H,X,Y,L)$. The

has 3,654 sites. Relative to N, exactly 79 instruction rows change, all within
indices 1,953 through 2,067 inclusive. All other instruction rows coincide.

The N splice replaces
\[
\texttt{node163=(node6,node55)},\quad
\texttt{node55=(node49,node54)}
\]
by \texttt{node163=(node6,node54)}. It deletes a 19-site dummy-zero push
interval $[3634,3653)$ from the corresponding push-based Q source.
The interval has one external entry, from old instruction 3628.
The builder checks every surviving instruction and successor after exact
index relocation. The native physical edit is
\[
(164,1):(1,R,98)\longmapsto(1,R,114).
\]

At successful MP, the retained copy loop begins with $D=b,T=X=0$.
Its first loop maintains $(T,D,X)=(j,b-j,j)$ for $j\le b$;
the second maintains $(T,D,X)=(b,j,b-j)$. It exits with $(b,b,0)$
in $7b+2$ primitive steps. This explains the exact result and offset

The grammar compiles to 67 readers atop 134 fixed controls.

\begin{lemma}[PA-verifiable finite simulation]
The delivered table halts from its blank initial configuration exactly
when the initialized QN register source halts. The finite trace translations
are primitive recursive, with correctness formalizable in PA.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The exact seed establishes the source-frame relation. Each source step
has a finite, positive-duration physical segment to the prescribed next
frame, or the terminal state for a source halt. The generic unary and list
lemmas are proved by induction on their coded lengths and lists; the finite
symbolic path certificates supply the remaining compositions. Concatenation
simulates every finite source trace.

For reflection, suppose the physical computation first halts at time $T$.
If the source has not halted within its first $T+1$ steps, concatenating
those positive nonhalting segments after the startup yields a deterministic
physical trace past time $T$, a contradiction. This is a bounded finite
argument and does not infer halting equivalence from a simulation in only
one direction.
\end{proof}

\section{Attempts below 194 and their exact limits}

No bounded search in this work yielded a verified sub-194 machine.
The accepted result does not depend on running heuristic discovery again.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{All eligible reader folds on the QN parent.} Among its 201
controls, 130 have the required strict incoming-bit context. The inherited
three-step helper template yields seven opportunities and maximum matching
seven, exactly the seven used. There is no additional cross-fold with a
fixed control under this template.
\item \textbf{Contraction searches on PA194HS.} A radius-six conservative
window abstraction contains 269,607 configurations. The all-pairs search
examines 28,755,456 trial row actions; none is accepted, and no pair is
skipped. A radius-nine search restricted to pairs involving native root
guard 68 has 10,719,423 abstract configurations and 297,984 trial actions,
again with zero accepted and zero skipped. Both use a 64-step local horizon
and require simulations to stay in their observed window.
\item \textbf{Two new logical-emitter screens.} Replacing the equality-witness
head $\exists_D E(H,D)$ by $\exists_D E(A,D)$ preserves its universally
instantiated axiom family but changes the emitted correlation. A bounded
adaptive search ends at a projected 195 states after 2,613,698 mutations.
Combining Q's exchanged sixth head with $\Gamma(D)$ and the documented
known-zero conventions ends at projected 195 after 2,475,004 mutations.
Neither numerical score is promoted to a consistency-machine theorem.
\item \textbf{Simpler MP outputs.} A direct transfer yielding
$(b,w,0,0)$ is physically realized by adding a reader for
$(\mathrm{dec}\ D,\mathrm{inc}\ T;\mathrm{continue})$. It reaches 195
states, not 193 or 194. A cleaner command contract alone is therefore
insufficient to improve the physical count.
\end{enumerate}

These are finite negative results for particular rewrite, layout, and
contraction classes. They establish no global lower bound. Prior rejected
scanner deletions are also not general impossibility results: they fail
specific source-frame obligations, sometimes after a local quotient has
already passed. The report does not treat agreement on a finite blank run
as evidence of nonhalting or correctness of an optimization.

\subsection{Ideas from the ZFC and concise iPRA programs}

The ZFC work contributes the useful no-push/explicit-reserved-zero proof
compiler and the practice of stating command contracts over arbitrary
carried stores. Those ideas were already imported into PA194N, so their
reuse is credited rather than presented as a new transfer. Contextual
decoder folding and known-zero source specializations likewise have an
established history in these programs.

The concise iPRA program suggests treating rule selection and proof
representation as algorithm-design choices instead of optimizing formula
notation in isolation. However, replacing the PA refutation enumerator
by an iPRA refutation enumerator does not preserve the requested target:
it changes the consistency statement. A useful transfer must retain a
PA-verifiable refutation translation and improve the compiled binary
machine, not only shorten a high-level proof checker.

\section{Further research questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Can the root guard be specialized using its reachable input invariant
without adding a reader state? In the symmetric controller one operand is
always one. Generic pair-contraction failures do not exclude a change to
the source and its frame relation together.
\item Can a shorter origin scanner be obtained by jointly redesigning the
source tree, temporary markers, and initialization? Bounding only the
zero runs in clean source addresses misses transient-marker hazards.
\item Can a redesigned MP result convention and an AX decoder share the
extra transfer reader? The 195-state direct-transfer experiment gives a
concrete target whose interface is simpler than its physical allocation.
\item Can changes in different axiom heads be optimized jointly with
multi-state contextual quotients? The Q+$\Gamma(D)$ search reaches a
195-state score but does not exclude a better association or a broader
folding template.
\item Can the new proof-to-command compiler and inherited syntactic
PA-calibration maps be expressed in the repository's explicit arithmetic
coding framework, closing the current machine-specific formalization gap?
This would improve assurance without confusing a shorter proof script
with a smaller Turing machine.
\end{enumerate}
