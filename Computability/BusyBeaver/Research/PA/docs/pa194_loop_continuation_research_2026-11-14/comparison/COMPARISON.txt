2. What is a design, and what is only an experiment?

The accompanying annotations attribute the packets' own historical evidence categories to freshly recomputed graph identities. They classify the 94 graphs as 51 reported principal constructions, seven supplementary constructions, 25 research auxiliaries, four search seeds and seven rejected constructions. These labels do not certify 51 iPRA consistency equivalences. The additional nonspanning synthetic diagnostic has 194 working controls but no root seeing the whole graph; it is outside this 94-graph count.

The common architecture has four layers: a numerical proof calculus; an enumerating register-machine scheduler and store protocol; a binary source-tree/reader implementation; and the literal unary-register tape engine with blank initialization. A reduction at one layer need not reduce the final number of controls, and proof transfer must account for all layers changed.

3. Principal mechanisms and their interaction

The primary PA194 uses the separate diagonal emitter, ten-entry AX interface and original sum guard. Its allocation is 134 fixed controls + 67 raw AX readers - seven contextual folds. An alternate decoder uses 134+68-8=194. PA194-AD changes the physical positions of A and D without changing the abstract source. These graphs are distinct implementations of the same central architecture.

C/CS change the store convention. Successful modus ponens caches its result, and Shift tests the cached head. CS reverses the destructive comparator's operands. N instead keeps a visible consequent while omitting the old dummy-zero push; its proof compiler requires a reserved zero. H adds a cache-only contradiction guard to the no-push protocol. HS uses its symmetric comparison. SUMS and the uppercase NS design use a symmetric implementation of the original sum guard. The lowercase pa194ns packet is the same graph as HS and must not be confused with the uppercase sum-guard NS.

Q reverses the two curried premises of the universal-distribution axiom head. Its combinations QN, QNS, QH and QHS combine this logical emitter change with corresponding store/guard protocols. The transformation requires an explicit primitive logical proof of premise exchange; resemblance of formula strings is insufficient. G changes the saved generalized cached theorem from U(d,h) to U(c,h), using AX's first explicit operand as the binder. Its word compiler changes even though its state count stays194.

M5/M6, N5/N6, H5/H6, Q5/Q6, QN5/QN6 and QH5/QH6 alter the arithmetic coefficient of formula constructors from four to five or six. Their meanings change throughout the execution, including 61 constructor bodies in the supplied sources. This is not merely a state renumbering. Unused residues, the zero-code alias and the interpretation of every emitted head must be handled by the recoding proof.

D uses Gamma(D) in place of Gamma(A) for the diagonal-vacuity head. DHS combines that change with the H/symmetric controller. Its allocation is135+69-10=194. Such counts demonstrate why a larger raw tree can sometimes produce an equally small final table after better folds.

J, F, A, CSZ/X, ZR and HAZ replace selected unconditional jumps by decrements of registers known to be zero at those source sites. Their correctness requires an inductive source invariant. The guard does not protect blank initialization, which uses the same physical controls outside the invariant. R/M4R and other source rotations reassociate the binary source tree, changing physical addresses while preserving the resolved source graph. Many saved rotations are search seeds rather than fully reconstructed released machines.

AX11 pushes the final guarded AX head and falls into the full MP field reader. Its command consumes an additional fourth field z and creates an eleven-entry prefix. Completeness can select z=0, but soundness must hold for every z, including the Cantor zero-stack alias. AX11QH5, AX11Q6, AX11H, AX11QH and AX11S are compositions of this changed command interface with earlier logical/arithmetic/controller mechanisms.

The newer physical-only alternatives have narrower proof obligations. B merges two historical reader roles and reuses the freed control as a dedicated selector; it changes four rows and permits an all-tape short-step transfer. TDY cycles three physical register locations, P5 moves five registers, and HX exchanges H/X blocks. PA1944L changes the recurring association of four increments to ((i,(i,i)),i); the cost becomes135+67-8=194. These retain the primary resolved source but require fresh frame and whole-tape startup checks.

Earlier PA195, 195S,195C,195DC and195S8 tables are genuine different source/controller/scanner ancestors. Original PA195 has a bundled191-token diagonal emitter; PA195S uses the separate190-token interface. MP196 immediately detects contradiction after a successful MP instead of waiting for Shift. The even-candidate scheduler with H/X placement costs195. These mechanisms may be valuable for future redesign but do not meet the requested state bound as supplied.

6. The arithmetic proof boundary

At the pinned revision the authoritative PA README states explicitly that neither machine-specific direction of the physical PA194/iPRA consistency equivalence is established. Standard finite-run Lean theorems, Python certificates and a conditional iPRA endpoint are separate from a discharged internal-arithmetic calibration. The corpus contains ordinary written PA arguments and substantial formal components; those do not automatically lower the base theory to iPRA.

The modeled scheduler now has all-model fairness and a target-derivation correspondence in I-Sigma1 models, and the source-to-PA direction has substantial guarded-gate/separation support. The remaining machine-specific work includes the uniform physical/controller frame simulation at internal times and the arbitrary-internal-PA-proof compilation into the numerical calculus. The generic I-Sigma1/conservation/intuitionistic transfer is already available, but no table has its complete Calibrated premise discharged.

All five latest incoming packets state this distinction explicitly. Several give written relative iPRA halting equivalences or relative source-equivalence proofs to the primary machine. These are useful preservation results. They do not supply the missing absolute premise. Any new candidate proved only equivalent to the primary must be labeled conditional for the requested absolute Con(PA) theorem, unless that separate calibration is actually completed.
