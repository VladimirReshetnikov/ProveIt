UNIFORM CONTRACTS OF THE SEVEN REUSED EMITTER MACROS
=================================================

This note is a new ordinary proof explanation for the supplied U machine.
The accompanying .v files are verbatim upstream templates. No new Rocq,
MM0, or other proof-assistant compilation is asserted by this note.

Register conventions and statement
---------------------------------
Let T=register1, W=register2, C=register3, X=register6 and Y=register7.
All registers have arbitrary natural values. At macro entry X=Y=0.
Registers not mentioned in a contract retain their exact original values.

P(a,b)=(a+b)(a+b+1)/2+b, I(a,b)=4P(a,b), R(a,b)=I(a,b)+1,
Q(a,b)=I(a,b)+2, N(a)=I(a,1).

The node names in the following table refer to the pinned DagPerm.v.
The ordinal ranges refer to the pinned candidate-k215/sites215.json.
Every interval includes its first primitive and excludes its last bound.

All seven terminate normally, restore X=Y=0, and preserve every other
register. These are all-natural statements, not consequences of testing
small input tuples.

1. Increment-only transfer loops
--------------------------------
A loop that decrements register r until zero and increments distinct
target registers by fixed multiplicities each iteration terminates after
its initial value of r iterations. If the initial value is k, the source
ends at zero and each target j has increased by m_j*k. Induction on k
proves the statement, including k=0. The targets do not change r.

ProgLibPerm.sem_while is precisely this induction principle. Its w117,
w193, w201, w208, w211, w212, w215, w216, and w217 instances are the
transfer loops needed here. Multiple increments in the same iteration
may be interchanged without changing their net effect.

2. Pairing loop (node162, lemma pair162)
---------------------------------------
This loop has the following exact pseudocode, with break and continue
interpreted by the structured semantics of RM.v:

  repeat:
    while T>0: T--; X++; Y++
    if X=0: exit
    X--; Y++
    while X>0: X--; T++

Its total contract, even for arbitrary initial X,T,Y, is

  (X,T,Y)=(a,b,z) -> (0,0,z+P(a,b)).

Induct on k=a+b. At k=0, the first loop is empty and the test exits.
At k=s+1, the first transfer gives (X,T,Y)=(s+1,0,z+b).
The next decrement and increment, followed by the transfer from X to T,
give (0,s,z+b+1). The induction hypothesis contributes P(0,s).
The identity

  P(a,b)=b+1+P(0,s)  when a+b=s+1

proves the result. Both inner loops terminate by their decreasing source
register; the repeated outer call decreases a+b. All other registers
are untouched. This is the proof recorded as pair_step and pair162.

3. Unpairing loop (nodes115/116)
--------------------------------
For every unit consumed from input register Z, node154 performs:

  Y++
  if X>0: X--
  else: while Y>0: Y--; X++

Thus the pair (X,Y) advances by the function

  (a+1,b) -> (a,b+1),    (0,b) -> (b+1,0).

Starting at (0,0), after z iterations it is unP(z). Indeed P(0,0)=0,
and each displayed transition increases the Cantor value by exactly one.
The converse identities unP(P(a,b))=(a,b) follow from this diagonal
enumeration. Induction on the input Z proves termination and the exact
output, with Z cleared. Lemmas ubody154, unpair115, unpair116 and
iter_unP record this argument. Node115 uses input register8; node116
uses W. The latter is the one used by the expression constructors.

4. Composition into the seven macros
------------------------------------
PUSH0 (node171) first transfers T to X, then W to T, computes P(X,T)
into Y by node162, and transfers Y to W. Thus (t,w) becomes
(0,P(t,w)) with both scratch registers zero. This is pushwff171.

PUSH1 (node177) is PUSH0 followed by one increment of T.

PAR3 (node168) is PUSH0, then a loop clearing C into both T and X,
then a loop transferring X back to C. It leaves T equal to the old C,
restores C exactly, and clears both scratch registers.

CONS/I (node119) unpairs W=P(a,w) into X=a,Y=w,W=0, transfers Y
back to W, computes P(X,T) into Y with node162, and transfers Y to T
with multiplicity four. The result is T=4P(a,t), W=w, X=Y=0.

R (node181) and Q (node180) append one and two increments of T,
respectively, to node119. N (node218) is PUSH1 followed by node119:
PUSH1 saves the old t, and CONS then constructs I(t,1) while restoring
the original stack w. No further loop principle is required.

5. Binding these proofs to the actual source intervals
------------------------------------------------------
DagPerm.v is pinned by SHA-256
ba2b28487ae978bd27b0d804e9b2bde6d9adcd1dd575de55572465c050b86aa1.

audit_source.py --macro-dag references/logic/DagPerm.v parses this
literal DAG independently, expands each of the seven nodes, resolves
all branch destinations, and compares the result with the corresponding
interval of the separately pinned accepted sites215.json.

The finite normalization is deliberately narrow:

* Every resolved branch destination must be identical. A CB/CC count
  may differ only because a changed binary parenthesization names the
  same resolved destination by a different ancestor count.
* The only other permitted difference is two adjacent increments in
  opposite order. Both must fall through, and the checker rejects any
  external control edge entering the second increment. At the pair's
  exit both register files are identical, so induction through successive
  control boundaries transfers the uniform macro contract.
* Every other instruction, register operand, and control edge is exact.

This comparison succeeds for all seven macros. It is not a probabilistic
test or an inference from their small-value runs. The recorded finite
normalization steps, together with the elementary commutation identity,
transfer the all-natural proofs above to the exact reused source bytes.

The same audit then binds all 737 new primitives and every new branch
target to the selected postfix expression built from these intervals.
All 3154 retained primitives and translated control edges are checked
unchanged. Structural induction on a postfix expression E gives the
uniform contract (T,W)->(eval(E),P(T,W)), with all parameters preserved
and scratch cleared. In the final source the atom n is PAR3, so n=C.
The expression evaluates exactly to H_C, with lower clause first.

RM.v supplies both the path and structured source-language semantics.
RMEquiv.v gives their generic equivalence for well-formed programs.
The ordinal CFG resolver is the direct finite form of the same rules:
CD-zero/CB strip the indicated zero ancestors and go past that block;
CC strips one ancestors and returns to the selected block's start.

6. PA formalizability
---------------------
The proof uses induction on a natural counter, elementary pairing
identities, finite source-graph comparison, finite composition, and
structural expression induction. All run witnesses can be constructed
primitive recursively from the inputs and loop iteration counts. PA
proves their totality and the stated contracts. The new literal TM
simulation and blank startup remain separate proof obligations and are
not established by this macro note.
