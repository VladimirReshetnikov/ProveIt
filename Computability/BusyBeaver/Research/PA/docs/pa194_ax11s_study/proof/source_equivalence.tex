\section{An iPRA proof of the relative source equivalence}
\label{sec:relative-ipra}

The new construction changes the implementation of the sum test within
Shift. Its command semantics remain exactly those of PA194AX11, including
the nonhalting case with both visible formulas equal to one. This section
proves the relative statement in a weak base; it does not assume or prove
the missing calibration of the parent binary machine to PA consistency.

\paragraph{Literal source boundary.}
Use zero-based primitive ordinals. The parent has 3,672 instructions and
the new source has 3,669. The first 3,667 instructions agree literally.
The final five parent instructions are replaced by two new instructions.
The affected command block begins at ordinal 3,660. Its only incoming edge
from outside the block is the edge from ordinal 48. The only entries into
the comparison block, ordinals 0 through 10, are the final instructions of
these two versions of Shift. The comparison has the same exit ordinal 11
in both programs. These are finite graph assertions checked directly
against the two instruction arrays, independently of their grammar.

Write $t,h,x$ for the incoming contents of registers 1, 5, and 6, and put
$s=t+h+x$. Every other register is arbitrary and unchanged throughout the
following comparison block. In particular, this is not a bounded-value
claim or an assumption that the incoming scratch register is zero.

The first loop transfers register 1 into register 6. The second transfers
register 5 into both registers 1 and 6. Their exact common result is
\[
                  (r_1,r_5,r_6)=(h,0,s).
\]
The parent trailer transfers $r_6$ into $r_5$ and sets $r_6$ to one; the
new trailer merely increments $r_5$. Thus the comparator receives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &r_1&r_5&r_6\\\hline
 \text{parent}&h&s&1\\
 \text{new}&h&1&s.
 \end{array}
\]

\paragraph{Comparator lemma.}
Starting at ordinal 0 with $(r_5,r_6)=(a,b)$, the common comparator
decrements the two counters together while both are positive. It then
clears either remainder. If $a=b$, it reaches the halt instruction at
ordinal 10. Otherwise it reaches ordinal 11. In both cases the final two
counters are zero, and no other register changes.

Counting primitive transitions up to arrival at ordinal 10 or 11, the
exact duration is
\[
 C(a,b)=
 \begin{cases}
 3a+2,&a=b,\\
 2a+b+4,&a>b,\\
 3b+2,&a<b.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{CE}
\]
For example, in the $a>b$ case, the initial $b$ paired decrements cost
$3b$ transitions. The first unequal comparison costs three more and
leaves $a-b-1$ in the first counter. Clearing it costs $2(a-b-1)+3$,
giving the middle line of (CE). The other two cases follow directly
from the same loops. These calculations prove the lemma by induction
on natural counters; they do not use a symmetry assumption about the
comparator's timing.

Applying the lemma to $(s,1)$ and $(1,s)$ shows that both versions halt
exactly when $s=1$. Otherwise both return at ordinal 11 with the identical
entire register store, namely $(r_1,r_5,r_6)=(h,0,0)$ and all other
registers preserved. When they halt, their stores at ordinal 10 also
agree. Their step counts need not agree.

\paragraph{Exact durations and an arithmetic bound.}
The shared prefix costs $3t+4h+2$ transitions. The parent trailer costs
$3s+3$, and the new trailer costs two. Hence the complete affected blocks
have durations
\[
 D_{\rm old}=3t+4h+3s+5+C(s,1),\qquad
 D_{\rm new}=3t+4h+4+C(1,s).                                  \tag{D}
\]
Their difference is
\[
 D_{\rm old}-D_{\rm new}=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&s=0,\\
 4,&s=1,\\
 2s+4,&s\geq2.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{Gain}
\]
All durations are positive and both are at most $9s+10$. Including
execution of the final halt instruction, if that convention is desired,
changes the applicable duration and bound by one. On a well-formed command
frame $x=0$, so the exact guard is $t+h=1$; the shared continuation gives
\[
 \mathsf S(t,w,d,h)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathsf{halt},&t+h=1,\\
 (h,\operatorname{tl}(w),d,\operatorname{hd}(w)),&t+h\ne1.
 \end{cases}
\]
The total Cantor-stack conventions remain
$\operatorname{hd}(0)=\operatorname{tl}(0)=0$ and $p(0,0)=0$.
No free-list assumption is introduced. In particular, the sum at
$(t,h)=(1,1)$ is two and does not trigger either implementation.

\paragraph{Why the relative theorem holds in iPRA.}
Encode finite register stores, primitive instructions, and bounded runs
by fixed primitive-recursive functions. The loop invariants above are
equalities and decidable comparisons between these functions. With the
incoming parameters retained, they are quantifier-free formulas; ordinary
induction on each remaining counter proves them in iPRA. The displayed
linear bounds make each local witnessing trace primitive recursive.

Replace the common source block by one macro step at its entry. The
finite graph check ensures that an ordinary run beginning at the common
initial source entry cannot enter a strict block interior by another
edge. Expanding every macro step by its old or new trace is primitive
recursion on the macro-step count. Both expansions end at identical
boundary stores and preserve halting. Conversely, a first halting
primitive trace cannot occur in a nonhalting block interior: the block
proof identifies the only halt and its exact exit condition. Positivity
of every expansion duration lets one use at most $n+1$ macro boundaries
when analyzing a putative first halt at primitive time $n$.

Thus, for the explicit register-program halting predicates from their
common initial source frame, ordinary mathematical reasoning gives
\[
 \mathrm{iPRA}\ \vdash\
       H_{\rm AX11\ source}\ \leftrightarrow\
       H_{\rm AX11S\ source}.                               \tag{Relative}
\]
This is a proof of relative source semantics, not a newly compiled Lean
or Rocq theorem. It does not require the numerical calculus to be sound,
nor does it assume $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$.

\paragraph{The remaining absolute-calibration boundary.}
The new source therefore inherits exactly the parent's numerical-calculus
and scheduling arguments: its four-field AX command, eleven-entry stack
interface, no-push successful MP, and exhaustive candidate decoder are
unchanged. At the literal binary-table level the packet separately checks
all primitive simulation contracts, simultaneous state folds, and the
complete blank startup. Their mathematical interpretations must be stated
at the strength actually proved.

The repository's existing all-model endpoint still requires a calibration
of the physical table in every model of $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$. The new
source proof does not fill the inherited model-uniform controller/frame
simulation or arbitrary-internal-PA-proof compiler gaps. Consequently
(Relative) must not be silently substituted for the requested absolute
statement $\mathrm{iPRA}\vdash\neg H\leftrightarrow
\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$.
