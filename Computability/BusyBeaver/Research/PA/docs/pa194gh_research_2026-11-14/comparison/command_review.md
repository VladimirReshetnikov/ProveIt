# Independent review of the GH command argument

Reviewed inputs: `innovation/PA194GH/proof.tex` and `check_commands.py`, the frozen G emitter, and the five native fixed-control changes defining GH. This note is an independent mathematical review, not a Lean/Rocq kernel certificate.

The reviewed word identities are consistent with the displayed Cantor stack convention and cache-only Shift. AX followed by nine Shifts leaves the dominated head visible, the saved generalization cached, and the old visible top on the stack. Ten Shifts expose `U(c,h_old)` and cache the old top. The eleventh Shift therefore halts precisely when that old top is one. This proves the stated Pop behavior, including the empty-stack zero alias.

Push does not accidentally halt. Its first nine Shifts inspect only the cleared cache and generated heads, and its final MP cannot identify a residue-zero implication with the residue-two universal head. `MP(0)` with zero offset clears the top/cache in both its success and failure branches. The generalization word `Push; Shift; AX_i; Shift^10` puts the original theorem in the cache, consumes it in AX before Shift can test it, and returns `U(i,a)` with unchanged underlying stack and cleared caches. This remains valid when `a=1` and when the binder is zero.

The MP compiler reserves a zero below both premise values. Its suffix `Shift; Shift; MP_b; Pop` either detects a premise/conclusion equal to one or returns the consequent with the original old stack. Without the reserved zero the last Pop would destroy arbitrary old stack data. The zero-pair and zero-implication aliases are covered directly by the numerical equations.

The compiler-length accounting checks: the longest axiom selector uses 122 commands; generalization adds 23; an MP node adds 49 beyond its two premise words. Hence `|W_pi|<=122*nodes(pi)`, and the universal halting word with its initial AX reset and final Pop has length at most `122*nodes(pi)+13`. A lone final Shift would be incorrect for the new guard.

The initial wording of the quantifier-free induction argument was tightened during review. Having primitive-recursive characteristic functions alone does not justify an unbounded all-store induction. The revised argument fixes the proof and input store as free parameters, computes the finite absorbing-halt execution and actual recursive call intervals, and inducts over a bounded conjunction of their local identities. This explicitly avoids using an unbounded universal store statement as the induction formula. The cofinal-candidate arithmetic and its decoding convention were independently checked against the pinned `pa194-work/pa194-model-fairness/ConPAScheduler.lean` (`seed n l = if l=0 then n else l-1`, with nested second projections for the residual) and `pa194-work/pa194-command-source/PA194FetchCommand.lean` (`parse`, with AX argument order c, x, y). A fresh candidate therefore has no outer +1, whereas a nonempty continuation does. This validates the displayed n_k and E conventions; the unchanged literal fetch implementation remains covered by the construction/source certificates.

The remaining distinction is substantive. The command proof gives witness transformations between the displayed abstract scheduler and the common numerical calculus. Transferring them to the literal machine in iPRA requires internalized physical/source simulation and the inherited PA-calibration premises. The new fragment states this limitation explicitly. It does not infer an iPRA theorem merely because an earlier paper used PA or because a Python symbolic interpreter passed.
