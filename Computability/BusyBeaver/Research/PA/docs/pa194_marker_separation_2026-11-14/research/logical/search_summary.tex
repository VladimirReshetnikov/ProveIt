\subsection{Additional source-grammar experiments}
These experiments did not produce an accepted machine below $194$ states.
The finite replay contains $4{,}222$ scored trees, including one existing
positive control and explicitly unproved protocol candidates. Its prices
subtract a maximum disjoint matching of contextual fold opportunities;
they are optimistic prices, not complete machine certificates.

Each timed run used a fixed recorded seed and a $600$-second allowance.
The objective includes $134$ fixed controls, or $136$ in the coefficient-seven
experiment. Near the frontier the search also checks simultaneous local folds,
but the objective is not a full source, startup, or consistency verification.
The recorded loops and bounded screens imply no lower bound for arbitrary
$193$-state machines.

The antecedent-exchange idea is attributed to the existing
\texttt{pa194\_sel\_ax11gh\_research\_2026-11-14} work. The new finite sweep
applied three such exchange patterns to $1{,}191$ historical grammar shapes,
for $3{,}573$ cases. A separate fixed-shape screen tested $108$ combinations
of exchanges and parameter permutations. Neither improved the state frontier.

A restricted-generalization proposal replaces the saved head by
$U(N(c),h)$, where $N(c)=I(c,1)$, and was priced over all $429$ Catalan
associations of its eight-leaf prefix; the least optimistic price was $197$.
Renaming proof variables into negative-code labels may be useful, but its
internal proof compiler, contradiction-target handling, and complete tape
correspondence remain unproved. An additive binder $U(c+d,h)$ was tested
in two evaluation orders and $84$ prefix associations, also with minimum
price $197$. These are research observations, not accepted constructions.
