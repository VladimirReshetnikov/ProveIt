\section{Additional logical and command-protocol investigations}
\subsection{Safe contraposition variants of the emitter}
The first quantifier head, which also participates in the explosion
bootstrap, was retained. For each of the third, fourth, and sixth heads,
write its ordinary curried form as $A\to(B\to C)$ and consider the six
classically equivalent presentations
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A\to(B\to C),&B\to(A\to C),&\neg C\to(A\to\neg B),\\
A\to(\neg C\to\neg B),&\neg C\to(B\to\neg A),&B\to(\neg C\to\neg A).
\end{array}
\]
The two positive presentations had already been searched in the corpus.
The new search used every combination containing at least one of the
four negative presentations, with two inherited grammar shapes:
$2(6^3-2^3)=416$ initial trees.  Local and shared reassociations were
scored using the inherited fold scorer, with unchanged
AX11H controls. The scorer checks simultaneous compatibility when the
optimistic reader score is at most 64. Parameter permutations were disabled because the new
word lengths change the old fixed offset intervals. A 300-second run
made 4,294,014 proposals and reached optimistic reader score 65. With the
134 fixed controls this is an unverified 199-state price, before any
additional compatibility or physical-simulation obstruction. No sub-194
machine resulted. This is a finite search record, not a global lower
bound, and its best score was not promoted to a verified physical table.

These transformations use fixed classical propositional proof templates
after the retained calculus has supplied its ordinary implicational and
explosion rules. The latter has a direct bootstrap using only the
retained first head and the restricted propositional basis: under the
assumption $1$, $K$ gives
$U(0,N(U(0,A)))\to1$, which is $\exists_0 U(0,A)$;
the first head then gives $A$. Deduction supplies $1\to A$, and Peirce
supplies the remaining classical templates. Thus this argument does
not presuppose a changed third, fourth, or sixth head. They do not replace the first head by the weaker modal
reflexivity schema $\forall_x H\to H$. The latter does not by itself
supply the symmetry content of $(\exists_x\forall_xH)\to H$.

\subsection{Modus ponens that stores its result}
Let a command store be $(t,w,d,h)$, with Cantor stack coding
$[a;w]=p(a,w)$ and $p(0,0)=0$. For an MP operand $x$, put $b=d+x$.
The ordinary comparison is $I(t,b)=h$. When it fails, retain the usual
output $(0,w,b,0)$. Three alternative successful outputs are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
\text{protocol}&\text{successful output}&\text{successful source tail}\\\hline
\mathrm{DD}&(b,[b,0;w],b,0)&\mathrm{copyD};\mathrm{copyD}\\
\mathrm{HD}&(b,[0,0;w],b,0)&\mathrm{copyH};\mathrm{copyD}\\
\mathrm{DH}&(0,[b,0;w],b,0)&\mathrm{copyD};\mathrm{copyH}.
\end{array}
\]
Here each preserving copy first pushes the old visible top. At successful
comparison entry, $t=h=0$ and $d=b$, so the displayed equations follow
for arbitrary $b,w$, including their zero aliases. DD supplies a reusable
copy of the new theorem; DH puts its result only on the theorem stack.
They change recurring computation and the proof-command protocol, not
only state names.

There is a simple universal compiler for these protocols with the old
ten-entry AX and sum-based Shift. The inherited words
\[
 \mathrm{Push}=\mathsf A_{0,0,0};\mathsf S^9;\mathsf{MP}_0,
 \qquad
 \mathrm{Pop}=\mathsf A_{0,0,0};\mathsf S^{11}
\]
still obey
\[
\mathrm{Push}(t,w,d,h)=(0,[t;w],0,0),\qquad
\mathrm{Pop}(t,[a;w],d,h)=(t,w,0,a).
\]
Push's final comparison is forced to fail: its proposed implication
has residue zero and its cached universal head has residue two.
Consequently its behavior is unaffected by the new successful branch.
Pop is safe even for $t=1$: the final tested sum contains a universal
code at least two. In particular these are identities on arbitrary
natural-number stacks, not merely on nonempty free lists.

Define
\[
 \mathrm{Erase}=\mathrm{Push};\mathrm{Pop};\mathrm{Push};\mathrm{Pop}.
\]
Successive stores are
\[
(t,w,d,h)\mapsto(0,[t;w],0,0)\mapsto(0,w,0,t)
 \mapsto(0,[0;w],0,0)\mapsto(0,w,0,0).
\]
For numerical offset $u$, use the empty word when $u=0$, and
$\mathsf{MP}_u$ when $u>0$. From $(0,w,0,0)$ the latter comparison
fails since $I(0,u)>0$, and its output is $(0,w,u,0)$.
For DD or HD, follow a successful MP by two Pops: the two newly
pushed entries are removed and the output is $(b,w,0,0)$.
For DH use Pop followed by Shift: this either yields $(b,w,0,0)$ or
halts when $b=1$. The existing axiom selectors and generalization
compiler can be adjusted using these identities; all additional stack
entries are supported by theorem proofs whenever the successful MP is.
The script \texttt{mp\_tail\_words.py} checks these free-term identities,
the reserved-zero MP suffix, zero aliases, and the cases $b=0$, $b=1$
and $b\ne1$. It additionally checks all ten axiom selectors,
theorem-only generalization, and the dominated-$\rho_8$ selector.
It does not by itself establish any physical simulation.

A particularly direct stack-only tail is
$\mathrm{copyDWithoutPush};\mathrm{push}$, giving
$(0,[b;w],b,0)$. A fully reconstructed 195-state table for this variant
passed all 4,780 construction symbolic contracts and reached its exact
complete blank initialization frame in 58,154 steps. It introduces
native reader 330, whose rows are $0\mapsto0R114$ and
$1\mapsto1R19$, and changes the successful comparison edge $(164,1)$
to that reader. The seven inherited AX folds remain valid. No immediate
opposite-history fold eliminates the added reader.
The term checker calls this protocol SP. Its proof compiler starts MP
with the reserved-zero stack, so the successful output is
$(0,[b,0;w],b,0)$; Pop followed by Shift yields $(b,w,0,0)$ or halts
when $b=1$. That suffix must not be claimed to normalize arbitrary
carried stacks without the reserved zero.

For DD, HD and DH, a second search installs the respective new tail
reader as a shared two-macro pattern in the AX grammar as well. Its
price is 135 fixed controls, so effective AX score 59 is required merely
to reach 194. Search scores, literal-table validation, and final proof
status must remain separate; a lower grammar score alone is not a new
consistency machine.

Each family used 49 seeds made from primary and Q grammar shapes,
positive antecedent exchanges in the third, fourth and sixth heads,
and the three already calibrated diagonal choices for $\Gamma$.
The 180-second shared-reassociation runs examined the following numbers
of proposals:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{crrr}
Protocol & Proposals & Accepted proposals & Best AX score\\\hline
DD & 1,150,813 & 361,831 & 60\\
HD & 1,244,403 & 377,848 & 60\\
DH & 1,067,950 & 328,391 & 60
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For each protocol, a second deterministic screen selected ten final
grammar shapes and tried all $6^6=46,656$ independent permutations of
the three parameters in the six ordinary heads. Thus each protocol
received 466,560 further scored cases, for 1,399,680 total. Every best
score remained 60; no score 59 occurred. These scores use the inherited
compatible local-fold scorer with the extra tail pattern available as
a shared macro. They are scoped negative results for those seeds,
shapes and transformations, not a lower bound for all grammars,
all fold mechanisms, or all machines. No 194-state physical table for
these changed successful-MP protocols is claimed.

\subsection{A fixed-label separation idea remains unproved}
A possible larger logical change is to restrict the separation gate's
set and element labels to 0 and 1, and use the property code
$U(A,A)\geq2$. The diagonal freshness theorem makes this property
classically equivalent to $A$ and makes the numerical guard automatic.
However, an argument invoking arbitrary first-order variable transport
at this point is circular: the existing diagonal calculus uses its full
gate to obtain reverse membership rewriting and the equality/binder
transport interfaces. No completeness proof for the restricted gate
was obtained, so this idea is not used to claim a smaller machine.

\subsection{What transfers from the three additional research directions}
The following comparisons refer to the pinned repository source, rather
than to an assumption that its current main branch is unchanged.

The ZFC research is directly useful at the compiler and logical-interface
levels. Its history-sensitive reader sharing, closed source rotations,
register allocation, command-local operand reading, and exact complete
startup checks are applicable techniques. Its dominated-$\rho_8$ and
alternative propositional bases also help separate a merely shorter
axiom expression from a fully calibrated calculus. Several of those
ideas are already ancestors of the PA packets and cannot be counted as
new savings. In particular, the ZFC \texttt{k218-hf-efq} report proves a
closed-theorem double-negation elimination rule for one proposed weaker
core but explicitly leaves its full consistency calibration open. That
rule is not a proved formula-level double-negation axiom or an
unrestricted deduction theorem. The report's logical gap is a reason
not to delete a PA axiom head solely on the strength of that bootstrap.
The ZFC README's 215-state exact-table endpoint and its separate internal
PA status also concern a different consistency target; changing that
target does not establish the requested equivalence with
$\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})$.

The DiophantineConsistency project supplies another possible search
architecture: enumerate integer or natural tuples and halt at a zero
of one fixed polynomial. Its reported iPRA proof-existence endpoints
are valuable examples of how to internalize a consistency reduction,
including the domain and exact expression binding. Its maintained
target is the repository's ZFC refutation predicate. A PA replacement
would first require the corresponding PA polynomial and its own
primitive-recursive reductions in both directions. More fundamentally,
the reported counts are polynomial variables, arithmetic-circuit
operations and degrees, rather than binary TM states including data
generation. For example, the described compact44 tuple contains the
coefficient $\beta=2^{590069}-1$. A small number of algebraic operations
does not by itself provide a small blank-tape constructor for the literal
coefficient tuple and a small evaluator. No such sub-194-state PA
realization was constructed in this investigation; no impossibility
claim about the architecture is made.

The ConciseIPRA project measures complete Python proof checkers by
normalized source tokens, AST nodes, and UTF-8 bytes. Its native scalar
core has zero, successor and an iterator, equality, and two closure
operators. Its restrictions of equality transport, single introduction
rules, deterministic witnesses, and source-directed dispatch are useful
ideas for reducing a proof checker's case analysis. However, its stated
calibration is a presentation of iPRA. Replacing the PA refutation search
requires a separate consistency calibration to PA, in addition to the
existing presentation theorem. Substitution, fresh-variable management,
iterator syntax and proof hypotheses must also be implemented and
charged when passing from a concise Python checker to a literal
one-tape binary table. Neither the Python size metric nor the small
axiom inventory supplies that cost automatically. We therefore did not
claim a PA state saving from those counts.

These three directions inform the search and its proof discipline.
They do not supply an uncharged oracle, an omitted constant initializer,
or a replacement consistency target in the delivered PA machine.
