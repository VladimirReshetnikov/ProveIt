\begin{abstract}
No valid machine with fewer than 194 working states was obtained. This
report supplies the requested alternative: a new complete binary Turing
machine, PA194ORH, with 194 working states, together with a written
$\ipra$ proof of its nonhalting equivalence to the calibrated PA194AX11H
machine. Composing with the reviewed, revised AX11H written calibration
gives $\ipra\vdash\Con(\PA)\leftrightarrow\neg\Halt_{\rm blank}(\mathrm{PA194ORH})$.
The result is a mathematical proof and executable certificate package;
it is not a new completed Lean proof.

The change reassociates a closed operand routine, recodes 134 source
addresses, and separates a hidden history-selector use of its entry
control. A tempting 193-state version fails exactly because it omits that
separation. Independent literal-table replay checks all 4,778 source
outcomes for arbitrary registers and a symbolic finite spare list, and
reproduces the complete 57,942-step blank initialization. An explicit
polynomial clock translates halting witnesses in both directions.

The comparison covers 97 research packets at a pinned repository commit,
including all four incoming archives: 83 named principal or supplementary
designs in 29 mechanism families. A fresh explicit-table census finds
520 spanning 194-state graph classes. PA194ORH is distinct from every
one under a broad structural equivalence. This census includes experiments
and rejected candidates; its graph classes are not 520 proved consistency
machines. The archive includes the full transition table, construction,
proofs, independent verification, comparison ledgers, and failed reduction
experiments with their actual scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result, conventions, and the exact claim}

The principal deliverable is \file{machines/PA194ORH/machine.tm}.
Its five-column rows are $(q,b,w,d,q')$: in control $q$, reading bit $b$,
write $w$, move one cell in direction $d\in\{L,R\}$, and enter $q'$.
The tape is two-way infinite, with alphabet $\{0,1\}$ and initial symbol
zero everywhere. The initial control is 0 and the initial head position
is 0. Working controls are exactly $0,\ldots,193$; control 194 is the
separate halt label and has no outgoing row. Thus the machine has 194
working states and 388 transitions. Its underlying directed graph spans
all 194 working controls from the initial control. This graph statement
does not assert that every control occurs on the blank computation.

The native-label table is conjugate to the dense table by
\file{labels.json}. Native initial, source-boundary, and halt controls
are 74, 84, and 254, respectively. All transition-level arguments below
use these native labels; Appendix~\ref{app:table} prints the complete
dense table.

The parent is the exact PA194AX11H table from the revised second AX11H
packet, with dense SHA-256
\begin{center}\small
\nolinkurl{c5dfe1026d077d3e062eca11edb789597bfbbced4f6040f8828ae5d467b1167a}.
\end{center}
The relative theorem established here concerns those exact two tables.
The absolute PA conclusion uses the revised parent's written calibration,
whose frozen archive is included with SHA-256
\begin{center}\small
\nolinkurl{93ee082ef035b2d32e71d6af5239b25b4bea1773f03bea4d2c39810a7fe15075}.
\end{center}
This archive is the one imported by several later supplied designs, not
an unspecified claim about a machine sharing the AX11H name.

\begin{theorem}[Main result, with its proof dependency made explicit]
Let $M$ be the delivered PA194ORH table and $P$ the exact parent just
identified. There are primitive recursive translations of halting witnesses,
whose correctness is provable in $\ipra$, establishing
\[
 \ipra\vdash\Halt_{\rm blank}(M)\leftrightarrow\Halt_{\rm blank}(P).
\]
Together with the revised AX11H written theorem, this yields
\[
 \boxed{\ipra\vdash\Con(\PA)\leftrightarrow
                     \neg\Halt_{\rm blank}(M).}
\]
Here $\Con(\PA)$ is the current Foundation PA-refutation predicate
used by the pinned ProveIt consistency endpoint, with the explicit
proof-presentation adapters of the inherited calibration.
\end{theorem}

The relative part is proved below from the exact source graph and literal
machine contracts, including a positive-clock argument. The absolute
part is a composition with the reviewed parent theorem. No consistency
assumption about PA is needed to prove this equivalence. No new
kernel-checked end-to-end Lean endpoint is claimed. The repository's
ongoing formalization and the written mathematical theorem are separate
statuses, and neither finite replay nor graph distinctness settles the
full arithmetic calibration by itself.

The archive also supplies PA194OR, the operand reassociation applied to
the original 3,673-site PA194 source and its original command protocol.
It has 194 states, passes 4,780 independent physical outcomes, and
initializes in 58,170 steps. Its separate relative theorem and polynomial
witness clocks appear in Section~\ref{sec:secondary-or-relative}.
PA194ORH is the main absolute-calibration result of this report because
its exact AX11H written dependency was independently reviewed here.

\subsection{What the corpus does not establish}
The 54 classes with 193 controls do not supply an accepted reduction.
The widely studied $301\mapsto88$ runtime splice preserves a fragment
of the computation but breaks blank initialization; later packets give
explicit outward-ray counterexamples, including an exhaustive test of
the specified selector-only family over all $9!$ register permutations.
A proof that a candidate simply runs away forever supplies no calibrated
PA-refutation search. Such exclusions are local to the stated families.
Neither the existing literature in the repository nor the new searches
here establishes a general lower bound of 194 states.

\section{Inherited architecture and calibration}

\subsection{The common search program}
The source has nine primary natural-number registers,
\[
 (N,T,W,A,D,H,X,Y,L)=(r_0,\ldots,r_8),
\]
represented in physical order $(6,2,0,4,3,7,8,5,1)$, together with a
finite spare register list and an affine tape-gap parameter. A structured
binary source tree controls increment, conditional decrement, forward
break, backward continuation, and halt instructions. Source-tree paths
are also physical control addresses, so reassociation is not a purely
syntactic operation on the Turing machine.

At a higher level the scheduler enumerates encoded finite command words
and runs them on a carried store. A numerical calculus supplies the
axioms and the MP/generalization operations needed to search for a PA
refutation. The carried store is not automatically reset between every
candidate. Consequently completeness uses an all-store compiler and
cofinal encodings of successful command words; a compiler proved only
from an empty stack is insufficient.

For orientation, the inherited coefficient-four numerical constructors
are
\[
 p(a,b)=\frac{(a+b)(a+b+1)}2+b,\qquad
 I(A,B)=4p(A,B),\qquad M(a,b)=4p(a,b)+1,
\]
\[
 U(a,B)=4p(B,a)+2,\qquad E(a,b)=4p(a,b)+3.
\]
Zero is the numerical truth code and one the contradiction code.
The first quantifier head is $(\exists_D U(D,H))\to H$; replacing it by
the superficially shorter $U(D,H)\to H$ loses logical content used in
the bootstrap. The gate, diagonal head, restricted implicational
basis, and all numerical guards are inherited without modification by
PA194ORH. The complete definitions and their proof transformations are
in the included AX11H calibration archive.

AX11H uses four AX operands, eleven emitted stack entries, no-push
visible MP, and cached-only Shift. These exact choices matter: a
separate all-store Pop/exposing word is used to turn a visible numerical
contradiction into the cached contradiction detected by Shift. The
zero-alias identity $p(0,0)=0$ is respected throughout; the encoded
stack cannot be treated as a free datatype in arguments about arbitrary
natural inputs.

\subsection{Why this parent was selected}
The revised AX11H packet provides the strongest directly reusable
written calibration encountered: literal-table to source simulation,
source to command simulation, an all-store command compiler, numerical
proof transformations, an adapter for the current Foundation PA
presentation, and the weak-arithmetic consistency transfer. Its ten
mathematical proof files match the frozen archive included here
byte-for-byte. The parent was chosen on the basis of that exact package,
not because the repository currently has a completed machine-specific
Lean theorem.

The independent review checked the following potentially delicate parts.
First, physical clocks are zero after canonical source halt; positivity
must only be asserted before halt. Second, the forward numerical
compiler replaces illicit induction on arbitrary PA formulas by finite
demand trees with decreasing request ranks and primitive recursive
construction order. Third, the Foundation adapter distinguishes the
current PA proof coding, finite axioms, and induction recognizer from
the historical ordinary-PAeq intermediate presentation. Finally, the
negative/open-matrix transfer is applied to the actual atomic halting
predicate; it does not assert an intuitionistic positive existential
from its double negation.

The finite replays verified 698 pinned source instances and 202
Foundation artifact identities, 8,828 primitive Foundation AST nodes,
both 3,671-instruction source graphs, both 4,778-outcome physical
interfaces, and both complete 57,922-step parent startups. No concrete
fatal flaw was found in the written calibration. This is a review of a
mathematical proof with supporting finite checks. It does not materialize
every enormous relational proof list or supply a new kernel verification
of the complete absolute endpoint. The detailed audit is included as
\file{comparison/calibration_review.txt}.

\section{The new operand-reader construction}
\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{Reassociate a closed routine}
Let the four closed subroutines be
\[
 A=\texttt{node32},\quad B=\texttt{node53},\quad
 C=\texttt{node55},\quad D=\texttt{node65}.
\]
They unpair the residual operand, transfer the recovered value,
perform the preserving copy, and execute the coefficient-four
constructor, respectively. The old \texttt{node75} tree is
\[
       ((A,(B,C)),D),
\]
whereas the new tree is
\[
       (A,(B,(C,D))).
\]
The order of primitive source operations is unchanged. More strongly,
resolving every decrement target, break, and continuation yields exactly
the same 3,671 primitive instructions, including all target indices.
There are 134 changed physical source addresses; the maximum address
length remains 37. The address changes are recorded in the source
artifacts and independently recomputed from the trees.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccll}

Routine & Native entry & Parent address suffix & New address suffix\\
\midrule
$A$ &104&\texttt{00}&\texttt{0}\\
$B$ &107&\texttt{010}&\texttt{10}\\
$C$ &98&\texttt{011}&\texttt{110}\\
$D$ &90&\texttt{1}&\texttt{111}\\
\bottomrule

The last pair $(C,D)$ is already present in the axiom grammar. Giving
that pair fixed native label 333 shares its two rows between the closed
operand routine and the axiom emitter. This is an actual reader change:
the binary dispatch codewords, native transitions, and recurring physical
execution differ from the parent.

\subsection{Repair the hidden history entry}
The first attempt removed a reader and had 193 working controls. It
preserved the entire resolved source graph and passed the seven local
fold certificates, but it failed 30 source outcomes. The reason is
structural: control 213, the apparent entry of the closed routine,
is also entered from $(69,0)$ as a history selector. A compiler which
only rewrites declared macro entry edges misses this second use.

The repair gives that history use its own entry at 92. The relevant
fixed engine rows, before the axiom-reader relabeling and quotient,
are as follows. Rows of 111 are unchanged.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rll}
\toprule
Native control & Reading 0 & Reading 1\\
\midrule
69 & $0L92$ & inherited\\
92 & $0R111$ & $1R90$\\
213 & $0R104$ & $1R185$\\
185 & $0R107$ & $1R333$\\
333 & $0R98$ & $1R90$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Here is the complete local argument, including its entry condition.
Suppose 69 reads zero at position $p$, and let $b$ be the bit at $p-1$.
For $b=0$, the old trace is
\[
  (69,p)\longrightarrow(213,p-1)\longrightarrow(92,p)
                \longrightarrow(104,p+1),
\]
and the new trace is
\[
  (69,p)\longrightarrow(92,p-1)\longrightarrow(111,p)
                \longrightarrow(104,p+1).
\]
Both preserve the two zero cells; the last transition uses the unchanged
row $(111,0)\mapsto0R104$. For $b=1$, both traces take two steps to
$(90,p)$ and preserve both cells. No other tape cell is read or written.
This is an unconditional finite local identity under the stated
triggering edge, not an inference from a finite sample of the blank run.
All changed controls lie outside the register-box state set, and all
46 register-box rows are unchanged.

The repair costs the state saved by the naive construction. The actual
count is
\[
  \underbrace{135}_{\text{fixed controls}}+
  \underbrace{66}_{\text{axiom readers}}-
  \underbrace{7}_{\text{simultaneous reader folds}}=194.
\]
The parent's corresponding decomposition is $134+67-7=194$. The raw
new table has 201 working controls. Each of the seven folds identifies
a pair whose incoming edge records a distinguishing history bit. The
checker verifies all four history/current-bit cases for every pair on
the final simultaneously folded table, for a total of 28 local cases.
It also checks the exact incoming-edge conditions. No fold is justified
only on a previously unfolded or independently modified table.

\subsection{Initialization and novelty}
The new dense table has the SHA-256 printed on the title page. Starting
from the completely blank tape it reaches the exact clean frame at
native control 84, head position $-2$, after 57,942 transitions. The
source address is \texttt{1000000}, corresponding to source site 25,
with logical registers $(2,0,\ldots,0)$ and 26 spare ones. Whole finite
tape support is compared, not only a decoded register window. The
parent reaches the same logical frame after 57,922 transitions.

The novelty check compares complete canonical transition tuples against
all 520 spanning 194-state corpus classes. It permits arbitrary state
renaming, every spanning root, tape reflection, global symbol exchange,
and changes to the write/move component of an edge entering the halt
label. The new graph matches none. This is stronger than a dense-file
hash mismatch. The intentionally broad comparison is a structural test:
symbol exchange or root changes need not preserve the blank-zero
computation. We do not use this novelty equivalence to infer semantic
correctness.

\section{Executable evidence and what it proves}

Two independently organized physical checks are supplied. The
construction checker performs 4,778 symbolic contracts and uses the
inherited register-box interfaces. Its 1,516,115 symbolic events have a
maximum of 506 per outcome. The independent checker imports no
construction compiler; it reads the literal transition rows, re-proves
four finite-list lemmas from those rows, and resolves the source
control graph in two separate ways. It validates all 4,778 outcomes
with 1,102,204 symbolic events and maximum 403. It also checks the
dense/native conjugacy and the complete blank startup.

These event counts are \emph{not} uniform bounds on ordinary TM time.
Symbolic runs and list recursions summarize arbitrarily long unary
register blocks. Their certificates contain affine ordinary-step
durations, and it is those durations, together with the event-calculus
induction proof, that support the uniform arithmetic simulation.

The independent outcomes range over nine arbitrary natural registers,
both branches of every decrement, an arbitrary nonempty finite spare
list, and the affine gap parameter. They close the complete tape frame,
not just the visible register values. There are 4,777 nonhalting frame
endpoints and one halt outcome. Every nonterminal source step has
ordinary duration at least 3. The following section states the exact
arithmetic bridge and its finite-data checks.

\section{Composition with the written PA calibration}

The preceding relative theorem gives primitive recursive finite-witness
maps in both directions between the exact new and parent machines.
Intuitionistic contraposition therefore gives
\[
 \ipra\vdash\neg\Halt_{\rm blank}(M)
                \leftrightarrow\neg\Halt_{\rm blank}(P).
\]
Compose this with the revised AX11H mathematical theorem from
\file{proof/consistency_equivalence.tex} in
\file{reference/calibration_AX11H.zip}. This proves the boxed main
statement without changing the PA predicate or silently identifying
different proof encodings.

For clarity, the inherited calibration chain has five distinct pieces:
the actual binary machine simulates the primitive register source;
that source implements the abstract scheduler and commands; an all-store
compiler turns numerical derivations of contradiction into cofinally
encoded halting words; current Foundation PA proofs are transformed
through the specified ordinary-PAeq presentation into numerical proofs;
and the pinned open-matrix/negative transfer obtains the two $\ipra$
consistency implications. The soundness direction also tracks proof
support for stored numerical formulas. The parent packet explicitly
provides primitive recursive clocks for the candidate drain, the
source-command expansion, and the physical implementation.

The present construction leaves the parent's resolved primitive
instruction graph unchanged. Consequently none of its logical rules,
AX guards, scheduler semantics, all-store command words, proof
presentation adapters, or consistency-transfer hypotheses are weakened.
The added work is the exact source-frame recoding and the physical
clock relation, which are proved and checked here. This is the reason
the parent's substantial calibration can be reused rather than merely
postulated for an unrelated machine.
