\begin{abstract}
A fixed, independently replayed syntactic proof permits an exchange of the
two antecedents inside the quantified negative gate of the coefficient-four
PA calculus. This supplies a sound alternative source formula. Two bounded
grammar searches did not produce a machine with 194 states or fewer for the
tested source changes. Their best discovery scores project to 197 and 200
states. The present note records this mathematical result, the negative
search evidence, its limitations, and the remaining work. It makes no new
machine claim and does not establish the inherited absolute equivalence
with the consistency of PA.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and notation}

The repository snapshot is
\texttt{32d0d8b7d93a63ad4106c18cdb29b37927acdad2}.
The source templates used in these experiments come from the incoming
PA194AX11QH packet listed in Section~\ref{sec:provenance}.
Numerical implication and universal quantification have the inherited
definitions
\[
 I(A,B)=4p(A,B),\qquad \U(c,A)=4p(A,c)+2,
 \qquad p(a,b)=\frac{(a+b)(a+b+1)}2+b.
\]
We write $A\imp B$ for $I(A,B)$ and $\bot$ for the designated contradiction
code $1$. In particular, $I(0,0)=0$ remains an allowed numerical alias.
No free-syntax injectivity assumption is needed by the proof below.

The proof interpretation used here has modus ponens, generalization of
closed proofs, universal distribution, and the dominated implicational
schema
\[
 \rho(a,b,c,u)=((c\imp u)\imp a)\imp((a\imp c)\imp(b\imp c)),
 \qquad u\le a.
\]
The imported proof terms derive the ordinary $K$, $S$, and exchange
schemas from this restricted schema and modus ponens. The checker expands
and rechecks that derivation; it does not assume an unrestricted classical
implication axiom in place of $\rho$.

The two distribution orientations relevant to the comparison are
\begin{align*}
 D(c,A,B)&=\U(c,A)\imp
       (\U(c,A\imp B)\imp\U(c,B)),\\
 D^{\mathrm Q}(c,A,B)&=\U(c,A\imp B)\imp
       (\U(c,A)\imp\U(c,B)).
\end{align*}
They interderive by one instance of exchange and one application of modus
ponens. The certificate is replayed separately with either orientation
as the primitive distribution schema.

\section{The alternative gate}

For the inherited PA gate, abbreviate its subexpressions as
\begin{align*}
 P&=c\imp\M(c,b),\\
 V&=\M(a,b)\imp\U(c,\M(a,b)),\\
 S_0&=\U\bigl(b,
       (\M(a,b)\imp P)\mathbin{\wedge}(\M(c,b)\imp c)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Here the conjunction is the inherited negative encoding; nothing in the
following argument depends on its implementation. More generally,
$P,V,S_0$ may be arbitrary numerical formula codes. Set
\begin{align*}
 Q&=\U(c,S_0\imp(P\imp\bot)),&
 G&=(V\imp Q)\imp\bot,\\
 Q'&=\U(c,P\imp(S_0\imp\bot)),&
 G'&=(V\imp Q')\imp\bot.
\end{align*}
The change under investigation is $G\rightsquigarrow G'$. It exchanges
the two premises immediately inside the outer quantifier. It leaves the
distinct-label guard unchanged.

\paragraph{Fixed derivation.}
The exchange theorem gives
\[
 (S_0\imp(P\imp\bot))\imp(P\imp(S_0\imp\bot))
\]
and its converse. Generalize each closed theorem by $c$, then use universal
distribution to obtain $Q\imp Q'$ and $Q'\imp Q$.
To derive $G\imp G'$, suppose $g:G$ and $h:V\imp Q'$.
For $v:V$, the term $h(v)$ gives $Q'$, and the theorem $Q'\imp Q$ gives
$Q$. Thus $v\mapsto (Q'\imp Q)(h(v))$ establishes $V\imp Q$; applying
$g$ gives $\bot$. Discharging $h$ and $g$ gives $G\imp G'$.
The reverse direction is identical with $Q$ and $Q'$ interchanged.
The checker eliminates these temporary hypotheses by the explicit
$K,S$ abstraction algorithm, leaving only closed proof rows.

\paragraph{Why the numerical side conditions survive.}
Every dominated-schema leaf in the expanded $K,S,$ exchange derivation
has its parameter $u$ as an implication subterm of its parameter $a$.
The constructor inequalities
$x\le I(x,y)$ and $y\le I(x,y)$ imply $u\le a$ under every numerical
substitution. This remains true when some codes are zero or coincide.
The finite proof therefore supplies universal numerical proof templates,
not merely a truth-table equivalence for well-formed positive codes.

\paragraph{What can be transferred.}
If two otherwise identical numerical calculi use respectively $G$ and
$G'$ as their guarded axiom family, replace each use of one gate axiom
by the other gate axiom, the appropriate fixed implication template, and
modus ponens. Retain the same guard and all remaining proof rules.
This gives explicit primitive-recursive translations of finite derivations
in both directions. The construction can be verified by quantifier-free
induction on the length of the input derivation, using the fixed template
checks and the primitive-recursive syntax constructors.

This is a relative logical statement. To turn it into the requested
machine theorem one must still implement and verify the changed emitter,
derive its literal physical simulation in $\iPRA$, and connect the
reference numerical calculus to PA proof codes. No such new physical
machine was accepted in these experiments. The supplied Python replay
is not a Lean kernel check and does not discharge the absolute PA
calibration obligation.

\section{Finite certificate and replay}

Run from this directory:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 check_gate_exchange.py
\end{verbatim}
The script reads the imported Lean source as proof-term data and expands
the dependencies of $K,S,$ and exchange. Each resulting row is checked
against the dominated schema or modus ponens. It then constructs and
audits the small outer gate certificate. In addition to reporting success,
it writes the complete outer certificates and content hashes under
\nolinkurl{evidence/}.

The imported implication derivation contains 55,585 distinct proof rows
and 88,006 distinct implication terms: 26,427 dominated-schema leaves
and 29,158 modus-ponens rows. All domination checks succeed by subterm
inclusion. With ordinary distribution, the outer gate certificate has
123 proof rows and 144 terms. The receipt gives the corresponding counts
for the exchanged-distribution replay and hashes both artifacts.

This is a concrete finite syntactic check. It does not test the truth of
PA, prove that PA is consistent, or prove that a proposed physical TM is
nonhalting. Nor does the check certify any of the search scores as literal
transition-table state counts.

\section{Recorded source searches}

Both searches retain the 190-token emitter word length. They explore
binary grammar reassociations while holding their selected emitted
word fixed. The score is the number of reachable raw reader shapes minus
the number of simultaneously compatible two-context folds, plus penalties
for source-depth and orientation constraints. Only the final
zero-penalty minima are reported below. Adding the inherited fixed-state
term 134 gives a \emph{projected} total; this is a discovery score pending
full compilation and literal validation.

The first experiment exchanges
$\M(a,b)\imp(c\imp\M(c,b))$ with
$c\imp(\M(a,b)\imp\M(c,b))$ inside $S_0$.
It is included as negative search evidence only: this exchange was
already present in the prior PA194G exploratory inputs. The prior inputs
also already considered swapping the two conjuncts in $S_0$.
These ideas are not claimed as new designs here.

The second experiment makes the $Q\rightsquigarrow Q'$ change from
Section~2 and uses eight initial seeds: the primary and Q distribution
grammars, each with four combinations of the prior inner-$S_0$ premise
exchange and conjunction order. No global novelty claim is made for
the logical equivalence itself.

The searches are heuristic, not exhaustive. The first retained an
inherited seed-pool admission threshold of score at most 62; its observed
score-63 improvement was saved, but not admitted to that pool. The second
used the adaptive threshold ``score at most current best plus one.''
Both disabled head reordering and parameter renaming. The logs and source
are retained to make those restrictions visible. A time budget and random
seed reproduce the search procedure, but not an identical number of
iterations on another computer. The retained best grammars and logs are
the evidence for the reported runs.

Neither run supplies a lower bound on any machine class. Neither run
produced an accepted 194-state machine, and neither result should be
described as a 197-state or 200-state verified machine construction.

\section{Further research questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Can a joint search over gate orientation, head order, parameter
renaming, reader association, and compatible folds realize $G'$ in at
most 194 states? The fixed logical proof means that such a search would
not need a new PA-calibration argument for the gate replacement itself.
\item Can the primitive logical proof-template transformations be added
as Lean declarations over the repository's actual numerical derivation
type? This would turn the retained finite certificate into a reusable
formal library lemma rather than leaving it as an external replay.
\item Can a different guard be justified by a primitive-recursive proof
translation while reducing the physical cost of the present two
inequality tests? A suggested inequality such as $c>a+b$ is not a
replacement theorem: it would require a complete padding argument and
an actual smaller implementation before it could be accepted.
\item Can AX push its gate and enter the Shift continuation more cheaply
than the existing AX11 fallthrough into MP? A simple reassociation of
the outer dispatch tree introduces an extra continuation depth; no free
physical helper for that depth was identified. This remains a concrete
implementation question, not an accepted design.
\item Most critically, can the inherited proof-code translations and
literal simulation be completed inside $\iPRA$ with all nonstandard
instances covered? Relative gate equivalence and finite execution checks
do not replace that absolute calibration theorem.
\end{enumerate}
