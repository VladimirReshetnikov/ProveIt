\section{An exact obstruction to repairing the 193-state core by register allocation}

Fix the supplied 193-state core with native start 74 and halt 254.  It is the
primary table with controls 301 and 88 identified and the two replacement rows
$(88,0):0L51$ and $(51,0):0R122$.  Let the register selectors be precisely the
external transitions of the primary table whose target is one of
\[
51,36,25,48,32,33,22,55,60.
\]
A register allocation permutes these target labels according to a permutation
of the nine field positions.  All other rows, including the new decoder
helper $(88,0)$, remain fixed.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
For every one of the $9!=362{,}880$ allocations in this specified family, the
machine started in control 74 on blank tape reaches an outward right ray in
control 35 after at most 93 transitions, and never halts.

\paragraph{Proof.}
For each $k\in\{0,\ldots,8\}$, choose the allocation exchanging positions 7
and $k$.  Replay its literal blank-start trace until reaching control 35 to
the right of the complete finite support.  The nine checked prefix lengths
are $85,86,\ldots,93$.  In each prefix the only executed row whose action can
depend on the register allocation is $(45,1)$, which selects physical field
7 before permutation.  Therefore an arbitrary allocation with image $k$ of
field 7 has exactly the same whole-tape trace as this representative.
Formally, induction on the prefix length gives equality of control, head and
complete finite tape: the next row is either unchanged or is $(45,1)$ with
the same selected target.  No other allocated selector is reached.

At the resulting configuration, the next square and every square to its
right are blank, and row $(35,0)$ is $1R35$.  Each subsequent transition writes
one and moves right to the next blank square in the same control.  A second
induction proves that every future control is 35.  The halt control 254 is
never reached.  The finite prefix certificates and every literal row check
are supplied in the accompanying JSON and Python replay.\hfill$\square$

This result concerns only this runtime core, this start, and these selector
rewrites.  It is not a lower bound on arbitrary machines, and it does not
exclude other modifications of the bootstrap or data representation.
