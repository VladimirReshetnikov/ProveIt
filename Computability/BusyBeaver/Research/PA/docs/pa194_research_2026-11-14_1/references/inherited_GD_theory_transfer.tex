\subsection{What transfers from the three related research programs}
\label{sec:theory-transfer}

The three suggested directories offer useful ideas at different layers.
Their size measures and proof endpoints must be distinguished before an
idea can count as a reduction of the present blank-start binary machine.
All source paths below refer to the pinned repository revision.

\paragraph{ZFC machines.}
The closest match is the K215 compiler architecture: command-local field
readers, source reassociation, predecessor-bit folds, and separate proofs
for arithmetic commands, primitive source, and physical execution.
The K215 documentation reports compiled external Lean and Rocq
halting/refutation equivalences for the exact 215-state table; the ZFC
research README separately says that its internal PA endpoint remains
open, while the exact 240-state machine has an internal PA equivalence.
Those are distinct formalization claims.\footnote{\raggedright\path{Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/candidate-k215/README.md}
and \path{Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/README.md}.}
We checked the concrete K215 replacement
$81/0:0L92\mapsto0L111$: it is already present in the primary PA194
native table, so it cannot supply a second deletion here. The
protected-tail scheduling argument also provides a useful way to weaken
startup requirements, but the PA collection already investigates that
route; it does not repair a failed literal startup by itself.
The parameter-free-MP study has an ordinary all-natural compiler proof
using additive AX and comparison residuals. Its saved additive allocation
costs 234 states under its conservative policy, against 215 for its
identity control, and it explicitly claims no new complete runtime or
blank initialization.\footnote{\raggedright\path{Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/k215-residual-parameter/README.md}
and \path{Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/k215-residual-parameter/proof.md};
the protected-tail result is identified in the ZFC research README under
\path{k217-protected-tail-seed/}.}
This is useful evidence that removing a source operand or reducing a DAG
does not automatically reduce physical controls. The GD construction
retains the established engine and prices its new emitter literally.

\paragraph{Diophantine consistency.}
This project supplies compact equations for the repository's ZFC
refutation predicate, together with substantial Lean proof-existence
work in iPRA. Its maintained README distinguishes native refutation
negation, standard or equality-only presentations, and selected
G\"odel--Gentzen-translated consistency sentences; it also separates
finite polynomial checks from logical endpoints and external-file
bindings.\footnote{\raggedright\path{Computability/DiophantineConsistency/README.md}
and \path{Computability/DiophantineConsistency/IPRA_PROVABILITY_PLAN.md}.}
These distinctions are directly relevant to the calibration audit here.
They do not give a smaller PA machine merely by counting variables or
arithmetic operations. For example, the documented compact44 tuple uses
$\beta=2^{590069}-1$. An equation-search machine would need an explicit
coefficient generator, witness enumeration, exact arithmetic, a PA
calibration, and a complete binary startup. Large coefficients do not
prove that such a machine must be large, but their generation cannot be
omitted from its state accounting. No such smaller compiled PA machine
was constructed in this work.

\paragraph{Concise iPRA.}
The concise-calculus program measures complete Python proof checkers by
normalized tokens, syntax-tree nodes, and bytes. Its README describes
small native calculi and precise Lean equivalences, including weakened
one-term transport and direct equation closure, while explicitly marking
other presentation combinations as unformalized.\footnote{\raggedright\path{Logic/PrimitiveRecursiveArithmetic/ConciseIPRA/README.md},
especially ``The native scalar core'' and ``Lean formalization''.}
This assists the design and formalization of finite proof transformations
and restricted induction arguments. However, replacing the searched
calculus by one equivalent to iPRA would target consistency of iPRA;
the present task requires consistency of full PA. Recovering that target
would require a separate calibration accounting for full PA induction.
Moreover, a shorter Python checker is not a priced two-symbol
transition table. We therefore use these materials as formalization and
architecture guidance, and claim no additional physical-state saving
from them. The new physical certificates apply to the three literal GD tables.
