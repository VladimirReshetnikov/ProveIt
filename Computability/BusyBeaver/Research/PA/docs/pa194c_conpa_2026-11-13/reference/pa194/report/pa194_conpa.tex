\begin{abstract}
We construct a deterministic, binary, one-tape Turing machine with
\textbf{194 working states}, started on the all-zero tape, whose nonhalting
is provably equivalent in PA to $\Con(\PA)$. The state count excludes the
separate terminal halt control. The construction keeps the supplied
separate-diagonal-head numerical calculus and its entire 3,673-instruction
register program. A new binary association of the unchanged 190-token
axiom emitter admits 67 raw reader controls and seven compatible
history-sensitive folds. Thus the complete count is
$134+67-7=194$, improving both existing 195-state allocation patterns.

Every one of the 4,780 arbitrary-register source contracts passes, and
literal blank execution reaches the exact complete source frame after
58,154 transitions. A separate symbolic literal-tape checker rederives its
generic list lemmas from the actual table and independently checks every
source branch, dense-table conjugacy and complete blank initialization.
The archive contains every transition, deterministic builders, finite
certificates, source and graph comparisons, search provenance, and this
mathematical proof. A second 194-state table permutes the physical locations
of registers $A$ and $D$.

The proof establishes finite-trace equivalence with the supplied
separate-head source and composes it with its explicit PA calibration:
$\PA\vdash(\neg\operatorname{Halt}(\MX)\leftrightarrow\Con(\PA))$.
This is an ordinary mathematical proof of a PA-provability statement,
supported by executable certificates. A newly compiled end-to-end Lean
or Rocq theorem is not claimed. The comparison covers all 32 incoming
archives at the pinned revision, recursively including nested archives,
and distinguishes three positive 195-state families from six rejected
diagnostic families.
\end{abstract}

\section{The result and the literal machine}
\label{sec:result}

The principal machine is \file{construction/tables/pa194.tm}.
Its working controls are $0,\ldots,193$, its start is control $0$, and
its unique terminal halt label is $194$. The tape is two-way infinite,
has alphabet $\{0,1\}$, and is initially zero everywhere, with the head
at cell zero. Each transition writes one symbol and moves one cell left
or right. There are \textbf{388 transition rows}; there is no row for
the halt control. All 194 working controls are reachable in the pointed
transition graph.

The native presentation starts in 74 and halts in 254. The supplied
\emph{dense-to-native} list is an exact conjugacy of every transition.
The dense table's SHA-256 is

Let $H_M$ be the usual arithmetic sentence asserting a finite halting
trace of a literal machine from its specified blank start. The conclusion is
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
       \PA\vdash\bigl(\neg H_{\MX}\leftrightarrow\Con(\PA)\bigr).}
\]
Actual nonhalting follows if PA is consistent. The biconditional is a
statement about what PA proves and does not add $\Con(\PA)$ as an axiom.
Graph reachability is not a claim that every control is visited by the
blank execution. No global minimality claim is made for 194.

\section{The saving: 67 readers with seven compatible folds}
\label{sec:decoder}

\subsection{A fixed source program, a new binary association}

The principal source is the supplied separate-head calculus used by 195S.
It has an axiom-emission word of 190 macro tokens and a complete register
program with 3,673 primitive instruction sites. The selected construction
uses exactly that word in exactly that order. Its six independent axiom-head
parameter permutations are all the identity; no axiom formula, guard,
command selector, stack position or logical register assignment changes.

The frozen new grammar changes how that same word is parenthesized and
shared as a binary tree. It has 67 raw reader controls. The 134 fixed
controls are retained, giving a raw 201-state table, and the seven folds
listed below reduce it to 194. The builder regenerates the parent 195S
from its original grammar and folds and binds it to the exact supplied
native table before checking the new construction.

Checking the flattened word alone would be insufficient. This tree language
also encodes control flow: a \file{breakTo} or \file{continueTo} leaf counts
ancestors of one direction, so reassociation can silently change its
successor. The certificate therefore compares the entire resolved register
instruction map, indexing sites by their left-to-right ordinal. It checks
the opcode, register operand and every successor, including both decrement
branches, every structured jump and every halt. The independent verifier
uses a second interval/ancestor resolver for the same comparison.

\begin{lemma}[Source identity]
\label{lem:source}
The new register program and the supplied separate-head program have
identical executions from every initial vector of nine natural registers,
when counters are identified by primitive-site ordinal. PA proves the
corresponding finite-trace identity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Their complete finite instruction maps are equal. Hence their coded step
functions make the same finite case distinction, register update and
next-counter choice. Equality of the maps is a finite arithmetic fact;
induction on the trace length gives the assertion in PA.
\end{proof}

A replayable occurrence-level reassociation certificate is included. It
connects the parent and new macro trees through a common right comb.
The certificate establishes endpoint word identity; intermediate trees
are not asserted to meet the physical scanner constraints. The endpoint
source-map and runtime certificates are checked separately.

\subsection{The seven folds in full}

The raw-to-folded identifications, in native compiler labels, are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 2016\mapsto2052,\quad 2018\mapsto2027,\quad
 2022\mapsto2032,\quad 2024\mapsto2046,\\
 2037\mapsto2015,\quad2055\mapsto2019,\quad2060\mapsto2014.
\end{gathered}
\]
Each pair has two distinct entry contexts. The removed member is entered
after a symbol-preserving right move across zero; the retained member is
entered after the corresponding move across one. The shared control can
therefore recover the missing state information by inspecting the previous
bit. The replacement actions are:

All incoming targets are projected by the same seven identifications.
Every other transition is retained from the raw 201-state construction.
The pairs are disjoint and none contains the start or halt.

The local checker replays all four context/read-bit cases for each pair
against the \emph{final simultaneously folded table}. It requires the
complete local tape, head displacement and projected destination control
to agree, with arbitrary untouched exterior tape. This is 28 finite
contracts. Helpers may be affected indirectly by other folds, so an
optimistic matching of individually feasible pairs would be insufficient.
The full source-frame checker then executes the resulting 194-state table
for every primitive source case.

\begin{proposition}[The new raw quotient]
The raw 201-state table and its folded 194-state table have equivalent
halting behavior from native state 74 on every initial tape. The local
simulation uses between one and five folded steps for each raw step.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All 23 incoming rows into exceptional fold controls establish the required
previous bit. The start is not exceptional. At an ordinary control the
transition commutes with the state projection; at an exceptional control
one of the 28 exact two-cell contracts applies. Thus induction constructs
a positive finite simulation of each raw trace on its complete tape.
For the converse, a folded first halt after $T$ steps is inconsistent with
$T+1$ nonhalting raw steps and their positive simulations, by determinism.
The argument is also arithmetizable for coded initial tapes and traces.
\end{proof}

This quotient is of the newly compiled 201-state table. The result is not
obtained by deleting one control from the literal supplied 195S table;
transport from 195S uses the common source program.

\subsection{Why this improves the two previous allocations}

The first positive PA195 kept 68 raw readers and saved seven controls by
folding. The later 195S reduced the raw count to 67 but supported only six
compatible folds. The new tree has the lower raw count and the larger fold
set. It does not remove an already-counted fold a second time: its literal
201-state raw table and seven explicit identifications are reconstructed
and counted from scratch.

An independent cross-combination first produced a useful fallback: deleting
the outer diagonal-combination token from the original 195 grammar gave
a separate-head realization with 68 readers and seven folds. The successful
194-state run, however, was seeded directly from the supplied 195S grammar,
not from that crossed fallback. It focused on the best retained layouts
with a lower annealing temperature and more frequent continuation from
near-optimal candidates. This run found the 67-reader/seven-fold endpoint.
The delivered frozen grammar, rather than the stochastic search, defines
the final machine.

\section{Physical implementation and exact blank startup}
\label{sec:physical}

The source register order is $(N,T,W,A,D,H,X,Y,L)$. The principal machine
keeps the supplied logical-to-physical map
\[
                        (6,2,0,4,3,7,8,5,1).
\]
A physical block $0\,1^{v+1}$ represents natural value $v$.
The full frame consists of a left marker, the encoded binary source path,
a gap, a right delimiter, the nine logical blocks in physical order and
an arbitrary nonempty spare tail. For source path $p$ the right delimiter
is at distance $48+n$ from the source head, with $n\ge0$ arbitrary; the
zero gap therefore has length $48+n-|p|$. The verification uses this
explicit frame family, not arbitrarily shorter headers. The arithmetic-box rows and fixed
controller are unchanged. In particular, the inherited increment
interface includes appending a one-valued spare register; omitting that
effect would invalidate full-frame simulation.

Starting from the all-zero tape, literal execution reaches the exact clean
frame after \textbf{58,154 transitions}, in native state 84, with head at
$-2$, source counter 25 and logical vector
\[
                         (2,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0).
\]
The endpoint comparison includes the complete finite support of the tape,
not merely its head and control. It includes all markers, source bits and
26 one-valued spare blocks. An independent bytearray simulation verifies
the same complete tape equality.

\subsection{A 194-state register-allocation companion}

The archive also supplies $\MAD$, using the same decoder and source graph
but physical map
\[
                        (6,2,0,3,4,7,8,5,1).
\]
The $A$ and $D$ blocks are exchanged. In native labels the only three
edits are
\[
 (17,1):0R32\mapsto0R48,\quad
 (144,1):0R48\mapsto0R32,\quad
 (161,1):0R32\mapsto0R48.
\]
The physical register arithmetic routines remain unchanged. This is a
permutation of tape data locations, not a relabeling of controls. If
$\sigma$ exchanges file positions three and four, then
$\sigma(v)_{p'(r)}=v_{p(r)}$ for every logical register $r$.
Increment, zero test and positive decrement commute with this store
identification, and the spare tail is unchanged.

The clean seed has $A=D=0$ and keeps $N$ at physical position six.
The companion reaches its exact full clean frame after 58,122 literal
steps. The 32-step saving is an initialization fact; no uniform speedup
for all later computations is claimed. This register-layout technique
was already used in a 196-state report~\cite{register}; the new contribution
here is its verified composition with the 194-state decoder.

\section{PA-internal finite-trace preservation}
\label{sec:preservation}

Let $R_S$ be the common finite separate-head register program, started at
site 25 with the clean logical vector above. Let $H_R$ assert that it has
a finite halting source trace. Both source and physical trace predicates
have the usual primitive-recursive finite-trace checkers.

\begin{lemma}[Exact positive simulation]
Every nonhalting primitive step of $R_S$ from a well-formed frame has a
terminating, positive-length physical simulation by $\MX$. It ends at
the exact intended next frame, applies the specified register update,
preserves the remaining represented register values and untouched exterior
tape, realizes the specified next-address and spare-tail effects, and has
no premature halt.
A source halt reaches the terminal physical control. These facts have
PA proofs for the coded operations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All 4,780 source cases are checked, including both branches of every
decrement. The symbolic frame includes an arbitrary natural parameter $n\ge0$ in the header distance $48+n$
and the arbitrary complete physical register file. The construction checker
instantiates unchanged arithmetic-box contracts with their exact row
premises; the independent checker rederives the generic register-list
lemmas from the literal table and executes every source case.

The certificate language uses literal transitions, finite concatenations,
constant-symbol scan loops and register-list scans and updates. Each has
an elementary arithmetic correctness proof and a finite bound in the
represented block lengths. The physical segment codes are primitive
recursively constructible, with PA-provable correctness. The verifier's
positive-progress condition requires a literal transition before a
source contract can close; normalization steps do not count as physical
progress. The final symbolic checker also provides a positive-duration
certificate for its compressed list operations.

Replaying the finite certificates instantiates these arithmetic proofs.
This argument quantifies over all frame parameters and does not infer a
universal conclusion from bounded samples of register values. The source
identity and independent successor checks supply the intended destination
for every case.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-trace equivalence]\label{thm:transport}
For the principal table and the supplied separate-head machine $M_{195S}$,
\[
 \PA\vdash H_{\MX}\leftrightarrow H_R,
 \qquad
 \PA\vdash H_{\MX}\leftrightarrow H_{M_{195S}}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Given a finite halting source trace, prepend the verified blank startup
and concatenate its exact physical simulation segments. A recursion on
the source trace constructs a finite halting tape trace; PA proves this
construction correct by induction.

Conversely, suppose the physical run first halts after $T$ transitions.
The verified startup cannot contain a halt. Iterate the total source
step function for $T+1$ instructions from the clean seed, treating a halt
as an absorbing event. If no source halt occurs, concatenate these
positive-length nonhalting physical segments. Their combined length
already exceeds $T$ before adding startup. Determinism contradicts the
alleged first physical halt. Thus the bounded source iteration contains
a halt.

The bounded iteration and finite concatenations are primitive recursive;
PA proves their correctness and the determinism contradiction by ordinary
induction. The same argument for the supplied 195S eliminates the common
source predicate. No assumption of actual nonhalting and no reflection
principle for PA occurs in this preservation proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and the formal proof boundary}
\label{sec:verification}

\input{verification_summary.tex}

The two physical checkers have independent representations and control-flow
resolvers. The construction checker uses the documented arithmetic-box
contracts after confirming their exact rows. The independent checker uses
symbolic literal tape lists and rederives its generic list lemmas from
the candidate table. Its parameters are nine independent arbitrary natural
register values and an arbitrary finite nonempty list of spare values.
Both decrement outcomes are covered. Their symbolic-step totals measure
verification work, not physical runtime on arbitrary register inputs.

The release also checks all 388 rows, the 194 working controls, complete
graph reachability, the unique halt target, exact dense-to-native
conjugacy, the full source map, the 28 simultaneous local fold cases,
whole-tape blank startup and the source-emitter word. The fresh corpus
census contains no earlier recognized 194-state consistency table.
The two new 194-state graphs are compared independently of control names.

The imported PA196 formal package exports the implication
\[
       \PA\vdash\operatorname{Nonhalt}(PA196)\to\Con(\PA).
\]
It does not export a reverse PA-internal consistency implication for
these new tables. The reverse direction used here comes from the explicit
ordinary syntactic calibration summarized in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}
and retained in the reference files. The supplied original-PA195 formal
quotient libraries prove increasingly strong all-tape preservation results,
while its iPRA/PRA/PA endpoint assembly remains conditional on its
machine-specific bridges.

Accordingly, the result delivered here is an ordinary mathematical proof
of the stated PA biconditional, with executable machine-specific
certificates and inherited logical proof constructions. The metadata
marks the new endpoint as \file{kernel_checked: false}. A newly compiled
end-to-end Lean or Rocq theorem is not being asserted.

\section{Search provenance and failed approaches}
\label{sec:search}

\input{search_summary.tex}

The successful search jointly scored raw decoder size and compatible
folds. It used shared-subtree rotations, optional axiom-head parameter
permutations, the physical scanner constraints and simultaneous local
verification in its fitness. The selected 194-state result has identity
parameter permutations, so its mathematical transport reduces to source
identity and physical simulation. The search was stopped after preserving
the successful construction and its certificates; no smaller-state claim
is made.

Earlier searches in this session had reached distinct, fully verified
195-state alternatives. Those include a crossed 68-reader/seven-fold
separate-head decoder and physical $A/D$ register permutations. They were
useful starting points and remain in the discovery record. The final
194-state theorem does not depend on the negative searches below.

The recorded failed searches include:
\begin{enumerate}
\item bounded-window control contractions and first-action synthesis,
      including alternative write bits, directions and existing controls;
\item two-, three- and four-bit reader-entry contexts, with a four-bit raw
      67-reader search of 1,798 disjoint-context pairs recovering only the
      existing six pairs for that \emph{particular} supplied tree;
\item contexts extracted from all arbitrary-register source contracts and
      complete startup of supplied 195S, yielding 8,758 radius-four windows
      over 172 non-box controls from 9,760 distinct symbolic frame observations;
\item complete closure of two fixed raw tables under a bounded local
      pair-contraction template, including alternate helper actions;
\item all $7!=5,040$ orders of seven unconditional heads on each of two
      fixed tree shapes, with no sub-195 score for those shapes.
\end{enumerate}
These are finite or heuristic searches in stated families, not lower
bounds. The successful new tree itself demonstrates why failure on a
fixed association cannot be promoted to a general obstruction.

Register permutations alone do not enlarge the current three-step
pure-reader opportunity relation: their adjustable router rows write zero
on reading one and move right, so they cannot be pure reader helpers or
match either allowed AX reader-1 action. The reader-0 actions are fixed.
This observation is limited to that fold language and says nothing
against more general coupled changes.

The corpus also supplies concrete warnings against apparent reductions.
An interior AX zero-run bound does not cover the first source path and
transient increment frames, so it does not alone justify deleting a
return scanner. Exposing membership vacuity as an unconditional stack head
leaves unsound formulas available when the guard fails. Replacing the
searched calculus by iPRA requires a new PA calibration, not just a smaller
proof checker.

\section{Relation to the ZFC and concise-iPRA ideas}

The transferable ZFC principle is joint optimization of the source tree,
reader allocation and physical helpers. A shorter source can eliminate a
useful helper and increase the physical state count. The new 194 machine
realizes the complementary outcome: one shared tree supports both the lower
raw-reader count and the larger fold set. ZFC return/decoder substitutions
also motivated the local action searches, although the accepted saving
here occurs in the AX reader allocation.

The concise-iPRA material encourages reducing proof-checker interfaces by
deriving some schemas and keeping the remaining dependencies explicit.
The diagonal-code freshness simplification is already part of the supplied
PA research; this report does not claim it as a new discovery or attribute
it to a different theory without evidence. Proof-checker byte count and
binary TM working-state count remain different objectives, and iPRA
consistency is not interchangeable with PA consistency. The new machine
keeps the existing PA proof calibration throughout.

\section{Reproduction and further research questions}

The top-level \file{README.md} gives exact commands. Python 3 and its
standard library suffice to reconstruct the tables and replay the finite
and symbolic certificates. Optional discovery searches require C++17.
The report compiles with a standard LaTeX installation. The final frozen
grammar and folds are included; stochastic discovery need not be repeated.

The most concrete next questions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can a 66-reader layout preserve seven folds, or can a 67-reader
      layout support eight, giving 193 states with the fixed controller?
\item Can jointly changing a helper and several reader classes save a
      control beyond pairwise fold matching?
\item Can a three-context, two-history-bit decoder be certified as a
      whole replacement with a net state saving?
\item Can the complete outer control flow be reorganized to permit a
      shorter return scanner at every source and transient frame?
\item Can membership vacuity be split with a correctly guarded stack
      protocol whose physical cost is below the saved emitter cost?
\item Can the existing formal quotient and arithmetic-transport libraries
      be assembled into a kernel-checked endpoint for the exact 194-state
      table using these finite certificates?
\end{enumerate}
