\section{The weak-arithmetic obligation and its present status}
\label{ipra-status}

The requested conclusion is a theorem \emph{in iPRA} about the blank run
of one specified literal table.  A state count, an external simulation,
and even a PA-provable consistency equivalence do not by themselves give
that theorem.  The pinned PA README explicitly leaves both
literal-machine-specific iPRA directions open for its primary PA194,
original PA195, and PA196.  The incoming mathematical calibrations state
PA-provability.  These are statements about the available arguments, not
evidence that the desired iPRA equivalence is false.\cite{pa-readme}

The distinction is not ``written proof versus Lean proof.''  An adequate
ordinary mathematical proof that a particular argument is formalizable in
iPRA would meet the mathematical requirement without a new Lean build.
What remains missing here is the complete weak-arithmetic correctness
argument for the relevant proof compiler and literal simulation.  Neither
the word ``primitive recursive'' nor an existing correctness proof using
unrestricted induction automatically supplies it.

\subsection{What the current interfaces establish}
\label{ipra-interfaces}

The generic endpoint is already available.  For a numerical transition
table $T$ and halt label $h$, its hypothesis \texttt{Calibrated}$\,(T,h)$
requires, in \emph{every} model $V\models I\Sigma_1$,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Prov}_{\mathrm{PA}}^V(\bot)
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists t\in V\;\operatorname{state}(\operatorname{run}_T(t))=h.
 \label{ipra-calibrated}
\end{equation}
Both proof codes and times may be nonstandard.  The repository transfers
this calibration to the syntactic iPRA consistency/nonhalting theorem;
its PA witness-certificate obligation has been discharged.  The name
\texttt{ConPAUnconditional} concerns that generic certificate, and does
not discharge the table-specific premise \eqref{ipra-calibrated}.
No literal table is calibrated in that generic directory.\cite{ipra-generic}

The modeled scheduler has a stronger result than a standard-model
semidecision argument.  In every relevant $I\Sigma_1$ model its halting is
equivalent to internal refutability in the numerical calculus.  The proof
uses a tracked execution on an empty stack, with a height/no-underflow
witness, and a frame theorem to extend it to arbitrary underlying stacks.
This makes the existence-of-execution invariant $\Sigma_1$.
The modeled-halt-to-PA-refutation direction is also established, including
the distinct-label gate supplier; it yields an iPRA theorem that PA
consistency excludes a halt of that \emph{auxiliary scheduler}.
Neither result identifies that scheduler with the literal binary machine
at arbitrary internal times.\cite{pa194-fairness,pa194-modeled}

Two complete bridges are still needed: an internal PA-refutation-to-calculus
compiler, including all axiom suppliers, and the model-uniform physical
connection from the actual blank tape through its controller and arithmetic
macros.  Current logical proof transport covers the LK rules, including
cut, under explicit supplier hypotheses; it does not supply every
translated PA induction axiom.\cite{fullpa-audit,pa-readme}

\subsection{An explicit sufficient criterion}
\label{ipra-criterion}

Fix a primitive-recursive PA refutation checker $R(p)$ and a
primitive-recursive predicate $H_M(t)$ saying that the specified machine
has reached its absorbing halt state by time $t$.  Set
\[
 \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA})\equiv\forall p\,\neg R(p),
 \qquad \operatorname{Nonhalt}(M)\equiv\forall t\,\neg H_M(t).
\]
The absorbing convention changes no finite halting event.

\paragraph{Witness criterion.}
Suppose primitive-recursive functions $F,G$ have correctness proofs in
iPRA:
\begin{equation}
 R(p)\longrightarrow H_M(F(p)),\qquad
 H_M(t)\longrightarrow R(G(t)).
 \label{ipra-witness-maps}
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 \mathrm{iPRA}\vdash
 \operatorname{Nonhalt}(M)\longleftrightarrow
 \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{PA}).
\]
\begin{proof}
Assume nonhalting and $R(p)$.  The first implication gives the forbidden
instance $H_M(F(p))$, so $\neg R(p)$; generalize over $p$.
Conversely, under consistency, $H_M(t)$ gives the forbidden instance
$R(G(t))$; generalize over $t$.  These are intuitionistically valid
arguments with explicit witnesses.  They use neither Markov's principle,
double-negation elimination, nor reflection for PA.
\end{proof}

This is a sufficient target, not an assertion that its two correctness
proofs have been completed.  Alternatively one can establish the
$I\Sigma_1$ calibration \eqref{ipra-calibrated} and use the existing
transfer.  A primitive-recursive program together with an unrestricted-PA
proof of its correctness is not yet either target.

\subsection{Where full induction occurs, and what it does not imply}
\label{ipra-induction-audit}

The inherited \texttt{original\_pa195\_calibration.tex} describes a concrete
calculus-to-PA proof translator: decode formulas, insert fixed logical
templates, generate a finite bit-comprehension proof for each guarded
gate, and translate proof steps.  The comprehension proof uses one PA
induction instance for an arbitrary input formula.  That induction belongs
to the \emph{generated PA proof}.  Checking syntactically that a line is
a PA induction instance does not require the checking metatheory to accept
that instance as true.  Thus arbitrary induction in the output is not an
obstruction to weak-arithmetic verification of the supplier.  This is
consistent with the newer modeled soundness result.\cite{pa194-modeled}

The forward PA-to-calculus direction in the inherited argument instead
imports the PA196 compiler and replaces certain equality templates.  Its
displayed internal conclusion is PA-provable existential refutation
transport.  The replacements and radix recodings are explicit finite
syntactic constructions; they do not lower the induction strength of the
entire imported compiler.  In particular, the inspected correctness
arguments include the following unbounded invariants:\cite{fullpa-audit}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \texttt{ordinary\_derivation\_requested} quantifies over smaller
 derivations and all environments/allocation parameters, and then asserts
 the existence of requested certificates.  Its induction predicate is not
 supplied in $\Sigma_1$ form.
 \item \texttt{fuel\_invariant} quantifies over every table bound and a
 table-realization condition containing unbounded store/environment
 requirements.  Again, the imported proof invokes unrestricted induction.
\end{itemize}

The accompanying syntactic audit records 88 files containing explicit
\texttt{C := Set.univ} inductions in the imported
\texttt{PA196/Arithmetic} and \texttt{M6PA/Calibration} trees:
32 transport, 12 scheduler, 10 machine, 3 certificate, 1 compiler-invariant,
and 30 calibration files.  The JSON manifest records representative sites.
This is a count of files exhibiting a syntactic pattern, not 88 independent open problems,
not a lower bound on repair work, and not a claim that every use is
essential.  Some are syntax identities amenable to weak induction; some
already have newer $I\Sigma_1$ replacements.  It does show why replacing
only the two outer wrappers would not constitute a complete audit.

Accordingly, we do not infer that the compiler is impossible in iPRA.
We also do not infer its weak-arithmetic correctness merely from the fact
that each standard instance has a finite output.  The missing step is a
uniform construction and verification at the required base theory,
covering the actual suppliers, environments and nonstandard proof codes.

\subsection{A concrete repair: bounded request tables}
\label{ipra-request-tables}

The problematic shape is ``for every environment there exists a
certificate.''  A useful alternative fixes the input and requested
environment, and records the finite graph of the recursive requests
actually made.  Each entry stores its source subcode, environment, child
indices and resulting certificate.  Validity of a proposed finite table
is primitive recursive; in the usual arithmetic presentation it is
$\Delta_1$, and existence of such a table is $\Sigma_1$.  An induction
constructing these witnesses can therefore be an $I\Sigma_1$ induction,
provided its local construction and bounds are proved.  A separate
renaming or frame lemma recovers the desired uniform interface.

There is a particularly explicit pattern in the imported NNF certificate
construction.  Its quantifier case changes a parameter $a$ only to
$\operatorname{advance}(a)$, while each source child $q'$ is strictly
smaller than its parent $q$.  For input code $p$, use table entries
\[
 (q,k)\quad\text{with}\quad q+k\le p,
 \qquad\text{at parameter }\operatorname{advance}^{k}(a).
\]
Process $q$ in increasing order.  A Boolean child requests $(q',k)$;
a quantifier child requests $(q',k+1)$, and
\[
 q'<q\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q'+k+1\le q+k\le p.
\]
Thus every dependency is an earlier entry of the same triangle, containing
at most $(p+1)(p+2)/2$ entries.  Local certificate constructors and
translation equalities give a bounded table checker.  This is a concrete
way to replace the unbounded-parameter induction, including internally
for nonstandard $p$ once the coding lemmas are established.  It is not a
claim that all actual interfaces have already been ported.

For the full compiler one must also bound every parameter change and
local proof constructor.  A sufficient scheme bounds request depth by
the source code $d$, bounds parameter updates by iteration of a monotone
primitive-recursive function, obtains a resulting branching bound $B$,
and bounds the number of request nodes by $(B+1)^{d+1}$.  Iterating a
monotone primitive-recursive majorant for local certificate constructors
through that many nodes gives a primitive-recursive output bound.  The
hypotheses must be checked: arbitrary nested recursion does not become
primitive recursive merely because its individual operations are.
The inspected transport preserves cut, so cut elimination is not a
required intermediate step.  Large formula codes, or towers of
exponentials with primitive-recursively bounded height, are consequently
not by themselves the obstruction.\cite{fullpa-audit}

\subsection{The physical bridge can use positive segments}
\label{ipra-physical}

Here is a useful conditional lemma for exact machine simulations.
Suppose source configurations have a primitive-recursive tape embedding
$E$, and each valid nonhalting source step has a generated exact physical
trace of length $\tau(c)\ge1$ to the next embedded configuration.
Require primitive-recursive generators and weak-arithmetic proofs of
these contracts, correspondence of terminal configurations, preservation
of valid frames, and a verified finite blank startup into $E(c_0)$ with
no earlier halt.

Forward translation concatenates the segments of a given source halting
trace.  For the converse, given a physical halting time $T$, generate at
most $T+1$ source steps and their segments, stopping if a source halt
occurs.  If none occurs, positivity makes the total physical duration
greater than $T$, and the endpoint is nonhalting.  Determinism and the
absorbing halt convention contradict the alleged physical halt.  Bounded
search therefore supplies a source halting witness.  This avoids an
unbounded inverse search through arbitrary intermediate tapes.

All iterations in this argument are primitive recursive under the stated
contracts.  The same reasoning can prove relative halting equivalence
between two literal tables, independently of PA calibration.  But the
contracts must cover the entire register file, all valid gaps, controller
layout, and startup.  A finite symbolic certificate may express a
universally parameterized contract; a collection of successful numerical
sample runs does not.  The AX11H artifacts supply the executable bindings
and finite symbolic checks, while a complete weak-arithmetic treatment of
their inherited physical interpretation remains part of the stated
obligation.\cite{pa-readme}

\subsection{What transfers from the three related projects}
\label{ipra-related}

The ZFC research supplies useful physical ideas: command-local reader
orders, coordinated source rotations, predecessor-bit folds, and omission
of a redundant successful-MP push.  Several already occur in the inspected
PA designs.  Savings competing for the same control roles do not simply
add, and changes still need exact startup and source bindings.  A ZFC
machine's calibration, or a stronger-base arithmetic endpoint there, does
not calibrate a different PA table in iPRA.\cite{zfc}

The DiophantineConsistency work is relevant through explicit data-valued
proof constructors, proof checkers, and weak-arithmetic witness machinery;
the generic PA transfer already uses related infrastructure.  A small
polynomial's variable count or degree is not a binary-TM state count:
enumeration, coefficient representation and arithmetic implementation
still require controls.  Nor can a consistency equation for ZFC replace
the required PA refutation checker.\cite{dio}

ConciseIPRA illustrates smaller derived interfaces and translations proved
on a specified public image.  This is a useful possible approach after a
raw-schema reduction fails: a restricted translation might suffice even
when arbitrary raw formulas cannot be transported.  For the guard-padding
proposal that remains an open route, not a constructed translation.
Finally, substituting a concise checker for \emph{iPRA proofs} changes the
searched theory; it does not establish the requested PA-consistency
equivalence.\cite{concise}

The appropriate claim for PA194AX11H is therefore an explicit 194-state
candidate with a proved relative command-calculus construction and
documented physical certificates, together with the common unresolved
weak-arithmetic calibration.  The witness criterion above states exactly
what would turn that candidate into a solution of the requested iPRA
problem; this report does not assert that the criterion has been met.
