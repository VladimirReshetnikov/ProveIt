\section{Further searches below 194 states}
\label{sec:additional-search}

The experiments in this section produced no validated blank-start machine
with fewer than 194 working states. They record concrete searches and rejected
repairs, with enough information to reproduce their scope. Their failure
establishes no general lower bound on consistency machines.

\subsection{Repairing the inherited 193-state runtime}

The sub-194 packet supplies a 193-control runtime obtained by identifying
native control 301 with 88 and changing two transitions
\cite{sub194packet}. Its source-step certificate covers 4,780 symbolic
outcomes on canonical source frames. The critical arithmetic change is
\((51,0):0R16\mapsto0R122\). When the next cell contains 1, the original
and changed paths have the same two-step endpoint, because both
\((16,1)\) and \((122,1)\) are \(0L1\). The blank initialization can instead
encounter a following zero. The unchanged reduced table therefore fails
initialization; a runtime certificate alone does not supply the required
blank-start consistency machine.

The AX11 packet already screened pairs and triples from a fixed pool of
327 proposed transition detours outside the generic arithmetic boxes
\cite{ax11packet}. We extended that experiment to four distinct transition
rows. A hit had to display the \emph{entire} finite support of an encoded
source frame: control 84, the left marker, the source address, zero padding,
a register-file marker, all nine working registers, and a nonempty spare
list. Register values were unrestricted in this preliminary screen.
The minimum header offset was relaxed from 48 to 36 to admit more potential
hits; no runtime theorem at offset 36 is asserted.

Each run was limited to 20,000 literal transitions, the implementation's
finite tape window, and 20,000 cells containing 1. Directly recognized
outward blank self-loops were classified separately. The main experiment
used every one of the 193 existing controls as a possible initial control
and source entry sites 25--44. Two further experiments used native start
74 and admitted every one of the 3,673 source addresses; one also changed
\((198,0)\) from \(1L40\) to \(0L40\).

The main experiment performed 6,106,954 runs including its preparatory
phases, and 79,714,470,105 literal transitions. Of those runs, 3,983,144
reached a search bound, 2,123,805 reached a recognized outward ray, and five
halted. The bounded runs remain unresolved beyond the cutoffs. The other
two experiments performed respectively 1,093,967,014 and 969,547,469
transitions. These experiments overlap in their preparatory phases, so
their totals should not be added as disjoint coverage.

The quadruple counts use the recorded row-encounter filters and deduplicate
quadruple/start combinations. They do not count all formal four-element
subsets of the pool. Moreover, simultaneous semantic correctness of the
individual detours was never assumed: a startup hit would still have
required complete runtime and logical verification.

\subsection{Joint decoder guards and a late counterexample}

A separate synthesis experiment permitted an arbitrary replacement of
\((122,0)\) or \((137,0)\), together with a replacement of one non-box row
encountered along the first modified test trajectory. The local screen used
radius-12 windows from the symbolic source execution: 48 windows for
\((122,0)\) and 177 for \((137,0)\). It sought a rejoining configuration
among the first 64 original local steps, using at most 128 candidate steps
and remaining inside the window.

Of 8,544,225 tested pairs, 31 passed these targeted local checks. Every one
of the 31 failed the complete 4,780-outcome source checker. The closest
repairs set
\[
 (122,0)\mapsto0L174,\qquad
 (174,0)\mapsto0R30
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad 0R47.
\]
They pass 4,623 outcomes before failing at source site 3554: the zero branch
of a decrement of the logical continuation register \(L\). They are
therefore rejected constructions, despite their extensive local agreement.
The inherited symbolic checker uses generic arithmetic-box lemmas; these
experiments do not constitute a new end-to-end proof in Lean or
\(\mathsf{iPRA}\).

\subsection{Alternative terminal grammars}

A second route changed the AX11 terminal store-push fragment to a fragment
copying one of \(D,H,A\), with two source-tree associations, yielding six
frozen input templates. The semantic adequacy of these substitutions was
not established. The experiment consequently tested an optimistic
compiler-allocation opportunity, rather than a verified replacement
calculus.

The supplied AX11 annealer and compatible-fold scorer were reused unchanged
\cite{ax11packet}; parameter renaming is disabled in that source. Active
mutations reassociate binary trees. The score combines the effective number
of reader nodes with penalties for structural and scanner constraints.
With 134 fixed controls, a nomination for at most 193 controls required an
effective reader count at most 59 and zero penalty.

A 600-second run with random seed \texttt{202611142001} recorded
11,424,577 iterations and 584,235 accepted moves. It produced no qualifying
nomination; its best penalized score was 60. At zero penalty, the count
\(134+60=194\) would retain the existing state bound. The iteration count
is not a count of distinct grammars or verified machines. The elapsed-time
cutoff also makes exact final iteration counts hardware-dependent.

\paragraph{Reproduction and interpretation.}
The source, inputs, receipts, rejected runtime table, and detailed commands
are under \texttt{research/runtime193/}; see its \texttt{README.txt} and
\texttt{results.json}. The six frozen grammar templates, unchanged inherited
C++ sources, full run log, and one representative unverified grammar sample
are under \texttt{research/terminal\_grammar/}. Its \texttt{provenance.json}
identifies the source archive members and their hashes. Both directories
include only the evidence needed for their stated experiments. Different
tape encodings, source invariants, bootstrap strategies, proof calculi, and
transition edits remain outside these finite searches.
