Concrete mathematical preservation theorem.

For a register tuple r=(r0,...,r8), define
  alpha(r)=sum(2^j : rj>0).
The abstract semantics is a sound overapproximation of the concrete one:
  an increment forces the selected bit to one;
  a zero decrement follows the zero branch and preserves the mask;
  a positive decrement follows the positive branch, with the selected bit
  either zero or one afterward;
  jumps preserve the mask.
This elementary lemma holds for arbitrary natural register values, including
nonstandard values in any model of PA. The relation R(pc,mask) is a fixed
finite table. Its seed and closure are finite arithmetic facts checked by
the certificate. PA proves by induction on source time that every concrete
configuration reachable from (25,(2,0,...,0)) satisfies R(pc,alpha(r)).

At an unchanged source instruction, the two programs have the same concrete
step. At a changed instruction, R forces the tested register to zero; the
new zero-decrement therefore follows precisely the old jump target and
preserves the entire register tuple. Induction gives exact pointwise equality
of the two concrete source runs, including whether and when the halt source
site is reached. No soundness or consistency assumption is used. Thus PA
proves source-halting equivalence from the designated seed.

This is a reachable-source theorem. It does not claim equivalence from every
arbitrary initial register tuple or on arbitrary physical tapes. The masks
are an overapproximation of all future reachable source configurations, not
a bounded concrete test. Exhaustive closure is what makes the invariant
unbounded in source execution time.
