\begin{abstract}
We compare the 51 delivered PA-machine research packets present in
\texttt{VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt} at commit
\texttt{3cc7fbf75ba111a0da696ef2f6001dd528891bd5}. The recognized 194-state
frontier has 34 distinct control graphs: 21 accompanied by reported ordinary
PA-calibration arguments, nine auxiliary or search designs, and four rejected
designs. Several headline names are duplicates.

No machine below 194 states with the required consistency equivalence is
established. The accepted alternative is an explicit new 194-state binary,
one-tape, blank-start machine, PA194NQ. It combines no-push modus ponens with
the exchanged quantifier-distribution emitter. These changes commute at the
resolved source level. They alter recurring numerical and storage behavior;
the resulting table is absent from all 34 prior graph classes, even after
state renaming, root changes, reflection, and symbol exchange.

Using the supplied ordinary syntactic calibration, we give a PA-formalizable
proof of
\[\PA\vdash\neg\Halt(\mathrm{PA194NQ})\leftrightarrow\Con(\PA).\]
Both physical checkers pass all 4,755 primitive source outcomes, and an
independent checker rederives the four register-list lemmas from the literal
table. The complete blank startup is checked at 58,154 transitions. The proof
is an ordinary mathematical proof with executable certificates, not a newly
compiled end-to-end Lean or Rocq theorem.

A second result explains why a physically valid 193-state coefficient-three
candidate is unsuitable: its numerical calculus has an explicit fourteen-line
refutation, also expanded into 73,393 independently checked primitive steps.
This failure occurs at the logical layer, despite successful source and
startup checks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result, conventions, and proof boundary}

\subsection{The accepted literal machine}

The authoritative table is \file{machine/machine.tm}. Its working states are
\(0,\ldots,193\), its initial state is 0, and its separate terminal halt label
is 194. The halt label has no outgoing rows and is excluded from the state
count. The tape is two-way infinite over \(\{0,1\}\), initially all zero,
with the head at position zero. Every transition writes one bit and moves
exactly one cell left or right. There are 388 transitions.

Each line has the format
\[
        \texttt{current\_state\quad read\_bit\quad write\_bit\quad L/R\quad next\_state}.
\]
The table's SHA-256 is
\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
d1cf0f6e7a3c8f999d045694efc107dd1f9a03d45dd6c15e3d197ff7754c7231.
\end{center}
The same machine in sparse compiler labels is
\file{machine/machine.native.tm}, with initial label 74 and halt label 254.
\file{machine/labels.json} gives the exact bijection from dense labels to
native labels, including halt. Every working state is graph-reachable from
the designated start; this is a graph fact, not a claim that each control is
visited on the actual blank run.

\begin{theorem}[Ordinary mathematical PA calibration]\label{thm:main}
For the exact table specified above,
\[
 \PA\vdash
 \bigl(\neg\Halt(\mathrm{PA194NQ},\mathrm{blank})
                   \leftrightarrow\Con(\PA)\bigr).
\]
Here halting is the usual existence of a coded finite run from the stated
blank configuration, and \(\Con(\PA)\) means that no finite PA proof derives
\(0=1\), for the same ordinary effective presentation of PA used in the
inherited calibration.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is proved by finite syntactic transformations and source/tape
simulation below. Its logical calibration depends on identified supplied
mathematical results, whose texts are retained in \file{references/logical/}.
We do not turn a conditional Lean theorem into an unconditional one, nor
infer PA derivability from a standard-model execution experiment.

\subsection{What is and is not new}

The separate-head numerical calculus, no-push MP rule, premise-exchange
theorem, compiler, and physical proof engines are supplied prior
work~\cite{primary,nopush,exchange,upstream}. The
new accepted object is their particular 194-state composition, with exact
source commutation, a complete preservation argument, an updated full-corpus
comparison, and fresh independent physical checks. This is not a claim of a
new axiom basis, a state-count breakthrough, or global minimality.

The current repository pursues the stronger endpoint
\(\mathrm{iPRA}\vdash\Con(\PA)\leftrightarrow\neg\Halt(M)\), and explicitly
states that neither machine-specific direction is complete for its primary
PA194 endpoint~\cite{repo}. That limitation is respected here. An ordinary proof about
PA derivability is a different result from an end-to-end proof-assistant
export or a proof in iPRA. The Python certificates expose their trusted
mathematical rules; they do not constitute a foundational kernel.

\section{The numerical calculus and the exchanged head}\label{sec:logic}

Let
\[
 p(a,b)=\frac{(a+b)(a+b+1)}2+b,
 \quad I(a,b)=4p(a,b),\quad M(a,b)=4p(a,b)+1,
\]
\[
 U(i,B)=4p(B,i)+2,\qquad E(a,b)=4p(a,b)+3.
\]
Code 0 denotes truth, code 1 is \(M(0,0)\), and \(N(B)=I(B,1)\) denotes
negation. Write \(A\to B=I(A,B)\),
\(A\wedge B=N(I(A,N(B)))\), and
\(\exists x_i B=N(U(i,N(B)))\).
All these expressions denote natural-number operations. In particular,
\(I(0,0)=0\); the proof never assumes a freely generated injective formula
syntax at that exceptional code.

For arbitrary natural parameters \(D,A,H\), the six ordinary AX heads in
emission order are
\begin{align*}
 f_1&=(\exists x_D\,U(D,H))\to H,\\
 f_2&=U(D,U(A,H))\to U(A,U(D,H)),\\
 f_3&=M(H,D)\to(E(H,A)\to M(A,D)),\\
 f_4&=E(H,D)\to(M(A,H)\to M(A,D)),\\
 f_5&=\exists x_D E(H,D),\\
 f_6&=U(D,A)\to\bigl(U(D,I(A,H))\to U(D,H)\bigr).
\end{align*}
PA194NQ replaces only the last head by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exchanged-head}
 f_6^{Q}=U(D,I(A,H))\to\bigl(U(D,A)\to U(D,H)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The separate diagonal head is
\(\Gamma(A)=I(A,U(A,A))\). The dominated implicational head is
\[
 \rho(D,A,H,d)
 =I\bigl(I(I(H,d),D),I(I(D,H),I(A,H))\bigr),\qquad d\le D.
\]

For completeness, the remaining guarded gate uses
\begin{align*}
 V&=M(H,D)\to U(A,M(H,D)), & P&=A\to M(A,D),\\
 S&=U\bigl(D,(M(H,D)\to P)\wedge(M(A,D)\to A)\bigr),\\
 Q_0&=U(A,S\to N(P)).
\end{align*}
Its value is
\(g(H,D,A)=N(I(V,Q_0))\) when \(A\ne D,H\), and 0 otherwise.
Truth, these heads, modus ponens, and generalization of theorems define the
supplied separate-head numerical calculus \(\C\). Replacing \(f_6\) by
\(f_6^Q\) defines \(\CQ\).

\begin{lemma}[Uniform premise exchange]\label{lem:exchange}
The calculi \(\C\) and \(\CQ\) have primitive-recursive proof translations
in both directions preserving every numerical conclusion. PA proves the
correctness of these translations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The unchanged restricted \(\rho\) basis derives the implicational K and S
schemas and hence the theorem
\[
 (P\to(Q\to R))\to(Q\to(P\to R)).
\]
\file{mathematics/exchange_certificate.py} constructs and independently
replays a 61-line parametric certificate: 17 K instances, 14 S instances,
and 30 applications of MP, with no hypotheses. Substituting
\[
 P=U(D,A),\quad Q=U(D,I(A,H)),\quad R=U(D,H)
\]
converts \(f_6\) to \(f_6^Q\). Interchanging P and Q gives the reverse
conversion. Replace each affected axiom line by this fixed proof template;
all other axioms and inference rules are unchanged. Instantiating a finite
template and concatenating coded finite derivations are primitive recursive.
Their verification is an induction on the input derivation, formalizable in
PA. Since this argument evaluates identities in the numerical implication
operation, it does not require disjoint syntax at code zero.
\end{proof}

The compiler's emitter is also checked independently as a free-constructor
calculation before numerical evaluation. Its complete word has 190 macro
tokens. Only positions 102 through 120, in zero-based numbering, change.
The old and new blocks have the same parameter use and stack effect; their
final formulas are exactly the two displayed heads. For example, at
\((A,D,H)=(1,0,0)\), their codes are respectively 31,609,172 and 76,268.
This is a recurring numerical difference, not a change of labels.

\section{Commands, carried stores, and proof compilation}\label{sec:commands}

\subsection{Exact contracts}

A command store is \((t,w,d,h)\), consisting of visible top, Cantor-coded
stack, numerical offset, and cached theorem. Write \([a;w]=p(a,w)\), and
use iterated brackets with the leftmost entry on top. The total projections
satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{hd}[a;w]=a,\quad \operatorname{tl}[a;w]=w,
 \quad [0;0]=0,\quad \operatorname{hd}(0)=\operatorname{tl}(0)=0.
\]
The numerical stack is not a free list.

With \(y=d+x\), PA194NQ's MP contract is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mp}
 \MP_x(t,w,d,h)=
 \begin{cases}
 (y,w,y,0),&I(t,y)=h,\\
 (0,w,y,0),&I(t,y)\ne h.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Successful MP leaves the stack unchanged, instead of inserting a dummy
zero. It retains the consequent in both visible top and numerical offset.
Shift is the original operation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
 \Sh(t,w,d,h)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathsf{halt},&t+h=1,\\
 (h,\operatorname{tl}(w),d,\operatorname{hd}(w)),&t+h\ne1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

Put \(D=d+x\), \(H=h+y\). The AX operation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ax}
 \AX_{A,x,y}(t,w,d,h)
 =\bigl(g(H,D,A),[q_1,\ldots,q_9,t;w],0,0\bigr),
\end{equation}
where
\[
 (q_1,\ldots,q_9)
 =(\Gamma(A),f_6^Q,f_5,f_4,f_3,f_2,f_1,
                 \rho(D,A,H,d),U(d,h)).
\]
Thus nine generated entries and the saved old top are pushed. Heads
\(q_1,\ldots,q_8\) and the gate are multiples of four; \(q_9\) has residue
two and is at least two. None is code one, independently of any consistency
assumption. The guard and cleanup suffix are unchanged.

The following proof is an explicit specialization of the supplied N
compiler. The exchanged head changes the interpretation of selector
\(q_2\), but not any stack count, residue argument, or command word.

\subsection{Uniform stack words}

Let \(A_0=\AX_{0,0,0}\), and define
\[
 \Push=A_0;\Sh^9;\MP_0,\qquad
 \Pop=A_0;\Sh^{11},\qquad
 \Discard=\MP_0,\qquad \Offset_i=\MP_i.
\]
Their exact contracts are
\begin{align}
 \Push(t,w,d,h)&=(0,[t;w],0,0),\label{eq:push}\\
 \Pop(t,[a;w],d,h)&=(t,w,0,a),\label{eq:pop}\\
 \Discard(t,w,0,h)&=(0,w,0,0),\label{eq:discard}\\
 \Offset_i(0,w,0,0)&=(0,w,i,0).\label{eq:offset}
\end{align}
Push and Pop are total on all natural stores, including false top code one.
To see this, AX followed by nine Shifts gives
\((q_8,[t;w],0,U(d,h))\); ten Shifts give \((U(d,h),w,0,t)\).
All these Shifts are safe by the displayed residues. The eleventh tests
\(U(d,h)+t\ge2\), so it also cannot halt. The MP in Push compares an
implication with a residue-two universal code and returns truth. In
Discard, both MP branches coincide because the proposed consequent is zero.
For Offset, \(I(0,i)>0\) when \(i>0\), so comparison with cached zero
fails; the case \(i=0\) has the same stated result.

These equalities also cover the collapsed zero-pair cases: Pop from a zero
stack returns cached zero, and Push of zero onto zero returns zero stack.
The symbolic word checker expressly includes these cases.

\subsection{Axiom selectors and generalization}

From a store \((t,w,0,0)\), selector
\[
 \Discard;\AX_{A,D,H};\Sh^{j+1};\Pop^{9-j}
 \qquad(1\le j\le7)
\]
returns \((q_j,w,0,0)\). The remaining \(9-j\) items are precisely the
unselected tail and the saved zero; the final Pop installs that zero in
cache. In particular selector two returns \(f_6^Q\), not the old \(f_6\).
For \(u\le a\), the dominated-head selector is
\[
 \Discard;\Offset_u;\AX_{b,a-u,c};\Sh^9;\Pop,
\]
which returns \((\rho(a,b,c,u),w,0,0)\). The gate selector is
\(\Discard;\AX_{A,D,H};\Pop^{10}\). Truth uses Discard alone.

Generalization is compiled by
\[
 \Gen_i=\Push;\Offset_i;\Sh;\AX_{i,0,0};\Sh^{10}.
\]
From \((a,w,0,0)\) it returns \((U(i,a),w,0,0)\). Immediately before AX,
the store is \((0,w,i,a)\); the final ten Shifts apply the AX identity
above with saved top zero. This word remains total when \(a=1\).

\subsection{The missing zero is reserved by the proof compiler}

Suppose recursively compiled proofs return \(a\) and \(I(a,b)\), each
preserving its input stack and clearing offset and cache, or halt early.
The MP proof word is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compile-mp}
 \Discard;\Push;C(I(a,b));\Push;C(a);\Push;
                         \Sh;\Sh;\MP_b;\Pop.
\end{equation}
Before the two consecutive Shifts the store is
\((0,[a,I(a,b),0;w],0,0)\). The first Shift exposes \(a\) in cache.
The second either halts if \(a=1\), or gives
\((a,[0;w],0,I(a,b))\). Successful MP then returns
\((b,[0;w],b,0)\), and the total Pop returns \((b,w,0,0)\).
The zero is inserted once at the compiler level, below the premise
computations. Nothing assumes that a numerical zero pair has a distinct
list representation.

\begin{lemma}[All-store refutation compiler]\label{lem:compiler}
There is a primitive-recursive map from a \(\CQ\)-derivation \(p\) to a
finite command word \(C(p)\) such that, from every \((t,w,0,0)\), it either
halts or returns \((\operatorname{concl}(p),w,0,0)\). If \(p\) concludes
code one, then \(A_0;C(p);\Sh\) halts from every natural store.
PA proves these assertions about coded finite executions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the coded derivation. The axiom cases use the selectors above;
the generalization case uses \(\Gen_i\), and MP uses
\eqref{eq:compile-mp}. Earlier halting remains halting when a suffix is
appended. AX first clears an arbitrary offset and cache, so the refutation
word works from arbitrary carried stores. If no earlier halt occurs, its
last Shift sees \(1+0=1\). The command words are fixed finite templates,
and their arithmetic correctness is uniform in the displayed parameters.
The recursive proof traversal and finite trace construction are formalizable
in PA without using a reflection principle or assuming consistency.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Soundness and the fair scheduler}

For soundness, annotate the current top, cached formula, and every live
canonical stack entry by a \(\CQ\) derivation, or by a PA proof of its
arithmetic translation. The offset need not be a theorem code. AX emits
valid instances, a generalization of the old cached theorem, and the old
top. On MP success, the two theorem annotations give a proof of the
consequent; failure gives truth. Shift transports annotations. If its halt
test succeeds, one of its two annotated naturals is one. This supplies a
refutation. For \(w>0\) the Cantor tail is strictly smaller than \(w\),
so canonical stack annotations have a bounded primitive-recursive
construction. Numerical stack zero supplies the fixed truth proof.

The source scheduler is unchanged. It enumerates finite command words and
carries the store between trials. A single word that works only after a
reset would therefore be insufficient; Lemma~\ref{lem:compiler} provides
the needed uniformity. For an explicit cofinal family, put
\(E(\epsilon)=0\) and
\(E(c::v)=1+\operatorname{encodeCommand}(c,E(v))\). The AX command code is
\[
 \operatorname{encodeCommand}(\AX_{A,x,y},z)
             =p(A+2,p(y,p(x,z))).
\]
If \(z=E(A_0;C(p);\Sh)\), fresh candidate codes
\(p(k+2,p(0,p(0,z)))\) describe the same universal halting suffix with
an arbitrary initial AX. They are strictly increasing and unbounded in
\(k\). Starting the fresh counter at two loses none of this family.
Decoded positive continuations decrease, and the fixed arithmetic macro
loops terminate by their natural counters. PA verifies the resulting finite
enumeration and trace construction.

\section{Source construction and literal tape verification}\label{sec:physical}

\subsection{Two changes commute}

The builder begins with the attributed PA196 numerical register program
and the supplied separate-head PA194 emitter. Its two edits are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item replace the 19-token block for \(f_6\) by the equal-length block for
      \(f_6^Q\), keeping all other 171 macro tokens unchanged;
\item in the successful MP branch, replace source subtree
      \(\texttt{node55}=(\texttt{node49},\texttt{node54})\) by
      \texttt{node54}, bypassing the dummy-zero push and retaining the copy.
\end{enumerate}
In native physical labels, the second edit changes
\((164,1):1R98\) to \((164,1):1R114\).
The source comparison checks the following square directly:
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 R_{\C,\mathrm{oldMP}}&\xrightarrow{\text{19-site deletion}}&R_{\C,N}\\
 \big\downarrow\scriptstyle{f_6\mapsto f_6^Q}&&
 \big\downarrow\scriptstyle{f_6\mapsto f_6^Q}\\
 R_{\CQ,\mathrm{oldMP}}&\xrightarrow{\text{19-site deletion}}&R_{\CQ,N}.
\end{array}
\]
Both upper/lower full sources have 3,673 sites before the deletion and
3,654 afterward. The deletion interval is \([3634,3653)\); its only
external incoming source edge is \(3628\to3634\). All remaining
instructions and destinations agree under the explicit relocation map,
on both sides of the square. The axiom exchange changes exactly 79 of the
3,654 instructions relative to PA194N. The changes are disjoint; agreement
of a flattened word alone is not used as evidence of preserved structured
jump destinations.

\begin{lemma}[PA-formalizable source/tape transport]\label{lem:transport}
The literal blank machine halts if and only if the initialized PA194NQ
source scheduler halts. PA proves the equivalence for coded finite runs.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The exact finite startup establishes the initial source frame. Each
primitive source outcome has a terminating physical realization with the
exact successor frame, or the designated source halt. The generic unary
and list lemmas prove these contracts uniformly. Concatenate their finite
traces to map a source halt to a physical halt. Conversely, let a first
physical halt occur at time \(T\). Iterate the totalized source step function
\(T+1\) times. If a source halt occurs, reflection is established. Otherwise
concatenate the verified positive, nonhalting primitive traces after the
nonhalting startup. Their total duration exceeds \(T\), contradicting
determinism and the assumed first physical halt. This is a bounded finite
construction, not an assumption of an infinite source execution.
The integer polynomials, list induction,
finite case splits, source recursion, and trace concatenations are all
formalizable in PA. No assumption of consistency is used.
\end{proof}

\section{Consistency calibration and inherited dependencies}\label{sec:calibration}

The supplied separate-head calibration gives uniform proof-producing
reductions between PA refutations and \(\C\)-derivations of code
one~\cite{primary,nopush}.
We retain its detailed texts and distinguish that mathematical dependency
from the newly run physical checks. The two directions require different
arguments; the upstream exported implication alone is insufficient.

\subsection{PA proofs produced from numerical derivations}

Translate \(M(a,b)\) to finite binary membership
\(\operatorname{Bit}(x_a,x_b)\), \(E(a,b)\) to equality, and \(U(a,B)\)
to universal quantification of the translated formula. One suitable formula
is
\[
 \operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\equiv
 \exists u,v\,[s=(2u+1)2^z+v\ \wedge\ v<2^z].
\]
PA proves \(\operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\to z<s\), hence the negation of the
translation of code one. Decode zero as truth; the numerical equality
\(I(0,0)=0\) is handled by a fixed truth-equivalence proof.

The substantive axiom supplier is finite binary comprehension. For every
arithmetic formula \(\theta(z,\vec u)\), PA proves
\[
 \forall n\,\exists s<2^n\,\forall z<n\,
          (\operatorname{Bit}(s,z)\leftrightarrow\theta(z,\vec u)).
\]
Induct on \(n\), retaining the old code if \(\neg\theta(n,\vec u)\) and
adding \(2^n\) otherwise. Substitution into this fixed induction-proof
pattern gives a primitive-recursive function producing PA proofs, whose
correctness is itself verified in PA. This is a syntactic proof supplier,
not a truth predicate for all arithmetic formulas.

Variable-label bounds in formula codes justify the diagonal instance
\(B\to U(B,B)\). The separation supplier also handles collisions: when
the two relevant labels coincide, the binder changes both occurrences,
and the first membership antecedent becomes \(\operatorname{Bit}(z,z)\).
The comprehension matrix must then be changed. The retained proof does
not incorrectly hold that bound variable fixed. Other heads have uniform
first-order or propositional proof templates. Lemma~\ref{lem:exchange}
provides the exchanged head's template. Applying these suppliers to the
annotation invariant in Section~\ref{sec:commands} maps a source halt to
a PA refutation.

\subsection{Numerical derivations produced from PA refutations}

The inherited PA196 proof compiler uses two-way membership rewriting,
neighborhood equality, and an equality witness. The separate diagonal
calculus derives those interfaces without the deleted equality-vacuity
head. Its explicit replacements use a formula's own code as a fresh
separator label, pointed separation, and fixed classical implication
templates. The supplied separate-head documents retain the detailed
derivations, including coincident-label cases and the change to a ten-entry
axiom interface. These are the logical dependencies behind the forward
proof translation; source simulation alone would not establish them.

Compose that PA-to-\(\C\) proof translation with
Lemma~\ref{lem:exchange}, then with the all-store compiler
Lemma~\ref{lem:compiler} and the unchanged fair scheduler. This constructs
a finite source halt from each PA refutation. Every transformation is
primitive recursive and its correctness is proved by PA induction on the
appropriate coded derivation or trace.

Thus, writing \(H_R\) for source halting, the two syntactic directions give
\[
 \PA\vdash H_R\leftrightarrow
      \exists p\,\Prf_{\PA}(p,\ulcorner0=1\urcorner).
\]
Compose with Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} and negate the existential
refutation predicate to obtain Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The same reasoning
also proves, in PA, equality of the blank-halting conditions of PA194NQ
and PA194N. It does not prove unconditional nonhalting in PA. In the
standard model, nonhalting follows if PA is consistent.

\section{Why the alternative is materially different}\label{sec:novelty}

Graph novelty by itself is insufficient: one can change unreachable rows
or merely renumber states. The comparison therefore has two parts.

First, \file{comparison/check_novelty.py} verifies the frozen comparison
tables' hashes, canonicalizes their complete transition tuples at every
graph-spanning root, and compares tuples exactly. It additionally allows
left/right reflection and global symbol exchange. There is no match to
PA194NQ among any of the 34 classes, including rejected and auxiliary
ones. Hashes identify files but are not the sole graph-equality test.

Second, the changed behavior is visible at repeatedly used semantic
interfaces. Compared with PA194N, AX emits \(f_6^Q\) instead of \(f_6\),
with 79 resolved primitive instructions changed. Compared with PA194Q's
cached controller, successful MP has a different storage effect. For
example, \(I(0,2)=20\), so from \((0,0,0,20)\), \(\MP_2\) returns
\((2,0,2,0)\) in NQ, whereas Q's cached MP returns \((0,0,0,2)\).
These are actual command semantics. NQ retains the original Shift test
\(t+h=1\), and its proof compiler reserves the MP zero below both premises.
It is therefore a new composition of two prior mechanisms, with explicit
interaction and preservation proofs, rather than a new name for an old
table. We claim novelty relative to the pinned corpus, not all possible
unpublished machines.
