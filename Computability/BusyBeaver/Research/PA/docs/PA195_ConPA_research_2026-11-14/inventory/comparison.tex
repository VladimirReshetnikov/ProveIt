\section{Comparison of all supplied PA machine designs}

\subsection{Scope and principal finding}

The source snapshot is ProveIt commit \nolinkurl{906367250a7f96c923147b10df392294bced7be5}.
The census reads every \nolinkurl{.tm} file in \nolinkurl{Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/PA}
and every \nolinkurl{.tm} member of every incoming ZIP, following nested ZIPs and
scanning each byte-distinct ZIP payload once. It executes no submitted code.
It separately checks the designated initial controls and halt controls for
the curated final tables. The complete census includes ancestors, rejected
experiments, raw intermediate compilations, and synthetic controls; its broad
counts must not be advertised as counts of valid consistency machines.

The supplied source corpus contains \textbf{no accepted sub-196 construction}.
There are six root-free graph classes of explicit 195-state diagnostics,
all rejected or unproved by the source reports. Two separate rejected
196-state layouts actually halt on the blank tape; those finite halts were
independently replayed here. There are more positive same-size designs than
the older repository catalogue records: the updated explicitly started
catalogue has 23 distinct 196-state graphs, comprising 19 baseline or
report-positive designs, two explicitly experimental MP designs, and two
blank-halting controls.

This comparison is not a new proof of the PA equivalence of any table.
The term \textbf{report-positive} records the source manuscript's claim of a
successful construction, with the stated executable checks. The exact proof
scope is kept separate below.

The new 195-state constructions obtained during the present work are outside
this pinned-source census and are described separately. Their existence does
not change the historical counts or turn the six supplied diagnostics into
accepted constructions.

\subsection{Proof boundaries}

The precise imported formal PA196 result is

\texttt{PA $\vdash$ Nonhalt(PA196) $\to$ Con(PA)}.

The imported Lean project also establishes external nonhalting in its
metatheory. Its theorem inventory does not export the reverse implication
inside PA. The same distinction applies to imported PA209. External
soundness, independence in both directions, and a finite simulation prefix
do not supply the reverse implication by themselves.

Incoming manuscripts contain substantial ordinary mathematical arguments
for the converse: translate membership to binary-bit membership, equality
to arithmetic equality, and the transposed universal constructor directly;
generate PA proofs of finite comprehension by substituting the input formula
into a full-induction proof template; preserve code0's truth alias; handle
coincident source labels; and carry proofs through the live store and stack.
Their positive-length exact macro simulations plus determinism are intended
to extract a source refutation from any physical halt. Absence of a new Lean
certificate does not alone refute these mathematical arguments. Conversely,
the existence of the manuscripts does not mean every exact proof-code and
machine-binding obligation has already been checked.

The integrated PA directory includes useful standard-Nat prerequisites:
canonical certificate-stack treatment of \nolinkurl{pair(0,0)=0}, bindings to actual
command definitions, a numeric native checker, and checker-rule audits.
The documentation keeps the arbitrary-PA-model trace extraction and the
Foundation model-valued proof-code bindings as separate obligations. A
replacement table must preserve the appropriate command compiler, complete
runtime contracts, and exact blank startup as well as the logical theory.

ConciseIPRA supplies proof-checker design principles. Consistency of iPRA
cannot simply replace consistency of PA; an encoding through an
intuitionistic checker requires the full PA-strength calibration. ZFC K215
supplies useful physical techniques, but its numerical control labels and
code conventions do not identify states in the PA196 table.

\subsection{Opportunities identified in the supplied designs}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The separate-head 190-item emitter has substantially fewer source sites
while still costing196 controls. Its final all-helper compatibility
checker is a stronger search base than a matching-size estimate alone.
The earlier optimistic70-reader/nine-fold195 count failed because of
invalid macro entry sides and interacting helper paths. This direction
led to the new construction: an unchanged 190-token macro word is
reassociated from68 distinct readers to67, retaining six valid
simultaneous folds and giving134+67-6=195 working controls.
\item The scanner-oriented separate-head layout has only six static internal
address obstructions:197,584,786,867,1759,2068. Removing these is not by
itself a correct scanner deletion; dynamic address bits, reader aliases,
and startup require a joint invariant.
\item The runtime-valid U208/PA196 hybrid isolates blank initialization as a
concrete unsolved issue. Its initial-control screens and unused-row
census are supplied. Any successful bootstrap must also preserve the
universally quantified runtime cases.
\item Source-26 zero-jump, closing-jump specialization, H-only Shift,
MP-result-in-H, and the diagonal/separate-head calculus involve different
source regions but share physical helper paths. Their composition is a
promising question only after full simultaneous verification; savings
cannot be summed arithmetically.
\end{enumerate}

No negative result cited here proves a global lower bound of 196.
