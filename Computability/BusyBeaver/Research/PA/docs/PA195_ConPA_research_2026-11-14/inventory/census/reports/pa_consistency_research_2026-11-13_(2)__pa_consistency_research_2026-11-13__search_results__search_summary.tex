\subsection{Previous work that is already in the supplied archives}

The incoming collection already contains extensive negative experiments.
For example, it reports 23,040 fixed-association head variants with no
improvement on the original 68 distinct AX concatenation nodes, and
46,656 head-independent label permutations with no improvement on the
original 159 distinct internal program nodes. Its same-count helper
screen reports 8,061,300 trials without the desired saving. These are
supplied results, not new searches of this investigation~\cite{incoming}.

The same caution applies to earlier source simplifications. Removing
known dead or constant branches can lower the source-site count from
3,812 to 3,807 while increasing the distinct-node count to 162 and giving
a best reported 197-state allocation. Negation-head peepholes remove
many more occurrences but still give 197 states in the supplied compiler.
The distributed-gate branch already gives a 198-state table. The new
198-state table in this report belongs instead to the diagonal-vacuity
branch. None of these counts combines additively with the contextual
U208 saving.

\subsection{Fresh association and reader-rotation searches}

Each of three fresh AX reassociation searches used a 240-second wall-clock
budget. For the original 201-token word and zero-run bound six, the run
recorded 91,792 sampling attempts and a best screening cost of 62.
With bound five, it recorded 99,064 attempts and a best cost of 73.
For the diagonal 191-token word and bound six, 92,720 attempts found
69 distinct new readers with five potential folds, giving screening
cost 64. That last witness was subsequently reconstructed and separately
verified as the delivered 198-state machine.

The screening cost subtracts a maximum matching of possible context folds;
it does not itself prove that all those folds can coexist. The zero-run
calculation includes the temporary trailing zero introduced by the
increment-one router. The attempt counter includes repeated samples and
selected nodes with no admissible rotation; it does not count distinct
machines. Fixed seeds and the full receipts are supplied, but repeating
a wall-clock-bounded run on another machine or Python version can change
its attempt total and the best result reached before the cutoff. These
are heuristic search observations, not exhaustive grammar optimizations
or state-count lower bounds.

A separate direct physical reader search produced the twelve-rotation
196-state layout. It expanded 412 queued layouts and accepted 11,866
prefix-decoder checks. A subsequent continuation expanded 573 layouts
and accepted 16,470 prefix checks without improving the fourteen
remaining zero-scanner obstruction sites. These are queue expansion
counts; repeated paths can reach the same physical layout, and a prefix
pass is not itself a universal primitive certificate.

A third rotation search explicitly computed action-preserving state
partitions for 12,966 candidate tables after prefix checks. It found
no nontrivial state quotient and no source-node count below 159 in that
family. It expanded 487 queued layouts. The search is bounded by its
time and queue strategy; these numbers do not assert exhaustive coverage
of all tree associations or all equivalent transition tables.
