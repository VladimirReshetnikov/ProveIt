\subsection{An eighteen-instruction source macro for negation}
\label{sec:negation18}

Write $T=R_1$, $W=R_2$, $S=R_6$, and $Y=R_7$ for the accumulator,
encoded stack, and two scratch registers. All registers range over
$\mathbb N$. Let
\[
 \Delta(n)=\frac{n(n+1)}2,\qquad
 p(a,b)=\Delta(a+b)+b.
\]
The source encoding uses $I(a,b)=4p(a,b)$ and falsity code $1$.
Consequently the negation constructor is $N(t)=4p(t,1)$.

\paragraph{The general pairing core.}
The exact raw subtree \texttt{node64} contains sixteen primitive source
instructions. Its effect, including arbitrary initial scratch values, is
\begin{equation}
 (T,S,Y)=(t,s,y)
 \quad\longmapsto\quad
 \bigl(4(y+p(s,t)),0,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:general-pairing-core}
\end{equation}
It preserves $W$ and every other register. The only written registers in
its primitive control-flow graph are $1$, $6$, and $7$.

Here is a terminating-loop proof of~\eqref{eq:general-pairing-core}.
The first loop repeatedly decreases $T$ and increases both $S$ and $Y$.
It terminates with
\[
 (T,S,Y)=(0,s+t,y+t).
\]
Its natural variant is $T$ and its preserved quantities are $S+T$ and
$Y+T$. The next loop has $T=0$ at its header. If $S=u>0$, it decreases
$S$ once, increases $Y$ once, drains the remaining $u-1$ units of $S$
into $T$, and runs the first loop again. The resulting header state is
\[
 (0,u,v)\longmapsto(0,u-1,v+u).
\]
The inner drain has variant $S$; the outer loop also has variant $S$,
measured at its header. Its invariant is $Y+\Delta(S)$, because
$\Delta(u)=u+\Delta(u-1)$. It therefore exits with
\[
 (T,S,Y)=(0,0,y+t+\Delta(s+t))=(0,0,y+p(s,t)).
\]
Finally \texttt{node63} drains $Y$ into $T$, adding four to $T$ per
decrement of $Y$. Its variant is $Y$ and its invariant is $T+4Y$.
This proves~\eqref{eq:general-pairing-core}, including termination and
preservation of the complete unrelated register frame.

This derivation is also supported by the existing general lemmas
\texttt{AXPair.drain\_t\_xy}, \texttt{AXPair.pair\_loop}, and
\texttt{AXTimes4.times4}. In particular, the first two accept arbitrary
initial $y$; the special zero-$y$ hypothesis of the published
\texttt{AXPair.pair} statement is unnecessary for the generalized effect
above. The accompanying independent checker reads the raw source and
checks the loop invariants as exact rational polynomial identities.

\paragraph{The new macro.}
Suppose $S=Y=0$ at macro entry. Execute
\[
 \texttt{INC7};\quad\texttt{INC7};\quad\texttt{node64}.
\]
After the first two instructions $Y=2$. The general core gives
\[
 T'=4\bigl(2+p(0,t)\bigr)=4p(t,1)=N(t),
 \qquad S'=Y'=0,
\]
using
\[
 p(t,1)=\Delta(t+1)+1=\Delta(t)+t+2=p(0,t)+2.
\]
Thus the macro computes negation and preserves the stack and other
registers. The clean-scratch condition is essential; for arbitrary
initial $S=s,Y=y$, the same eighteen instructions give
$4(y+2+p(s,t))$, which need not equal $N(t)$.

The complete eighteen-instruction graph is below. A decrement tests for
zero; on a positive register it subtracts one before taking its positive
successor. Label $18$ is the macro exit, not a halt instruction.

The exact source-step count of the general core is
\[
 10t+13\Delta(s+t)-2(s+t)+6y+3.
\]
For the new macro on a clean store this becomes
$13\Delta(t)+8t+17$. These formulas follow from the three drain counts
$4t+1$, $3s+1$, and $6y+1$, and the outer-loop recurrence
$C(0)=1$, $C(u)=C(u-1)+7u-2$ for $u>0$.

\paragraph{Comparison of the five negation spellings.}
All five spellings below compute $N(t)$ on a clean macro store. The
published push and cons contracts justify the original stack-based
spelling; the generalized core proves the four alternatives directly.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\hline
Source spelling & Primitive instructions\\
\hline
\texttt{push; incT; cons} & 47\\
\texttt{node37; incT; node64} & 20\\
\texttt{node64; node61; node61} & 24\\
\texttt{INC7; INC7; node64} & 18\\
\texttt{INC7; node64; node61} & 21\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here \texttt{node37} drains $T$ into $S$, and \texttt{node61} adds four
to $T$. Primitive source size is distinct from physical TM state count:
reusing decoder controls determines the latter.

\paragraph{Executable certificate and binding.}
The portable checker \texttt{negation18/verify.py} imports no optimizer
or source compiler. It resolves the primitive graph in two ways: by
leaf-path subtree intervals and by recursively maintaining break and
continue contexts. The resolvers agree on the original 3812-site
source, all five macro expansions, and the new 3441-site source.
The checker independently establishes the native macro entries by
following complete known prefixes in the upstream transition table:
all 64 occurrences of \texttt{node64} enter state $106$, and all 346
occurrences of \texttt{INC7} enter state $82$.

For the supplied 197-state seed, the checker reconstructs the entire
source from the new grammar and the raw outer source. It finds exactly
eight copies of the eighteen-instruction graph, at source ordinals
\[
 584,\ 657,\ 1701,\ 1774,\ 2891,\ 2936,\ 3142,\ 3269.
\]
An independent exhaustive joint zero/positive abstraction has 245112
reachable source-site/mask pairs from source site $25$, with register
$0$ positive and the other registers zero. A positive decrement is
allowed to leave either a zero or positive result, so this finite
closure is a sound overapproximation of all represented natural
register values. It forces both scratch registers to zero at every
one of the eight macro entries. There are 40 possible masks at site
$2891$ and 32 at each other listed entry. This supplies the clean-store
condition for the exact changed source.

There are additionally 819 finite controls for arbitrary-$Y$ core
inputs and 455 finite full-register-frame comparisons of the five
spellings. Those runs are regression controls; the universal arithmetic
argument is the terminating-loop proof and its checked polynomial
identities. The separate whole-machine certificate checks physical
instruction contracts and concrete blank startup for the 197-state
seed. This source improvement does not by itself establish a machine
with fewer than 196 states or a new formalized PA consistency endpoint.
