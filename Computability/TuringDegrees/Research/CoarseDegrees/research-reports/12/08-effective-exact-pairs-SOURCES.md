## Primary-source literature reviewed

1. D. R. Hirschfeldt, C. G. Jockusch, Jr., R. Kuyper, and P. E. Schupp,
   *Coarse reducibility and algorithmic randomness*, JSL 81 (2016), 1028–1046.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01707
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.01707
   Relevant: Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.7. The latter was checked in the PDF
   page image (PDF page index 9, printed page 10) to verify the upper-density
   bound and the nonreducibility directions. It is the imported theorem only
   in the alternative short realization proof.

2. J. N. Y. Franklin and R. Solomon,
   *Lowness for isomorphism, countable ideals, and computable traceability*.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06003v1
   https://arxiv.org/html/1909.06003v1
   Relevant: Section 2, row-finite requirements in the exact-pair construction
   and the no-disagreement verification. Cited to make clear that this
   classical mechanism and ordinary exact-pair existence are not new here.

3. C. G. Jockusch, Jr., and P. E. Schupp,
   *Generic computability, Turing degrees, and asymptotic density*.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.5212
   Relevant: context for the dyadic coding/limit phenomenon; its needed form
   is proved within the delivered article and was already present in ProveIt.

4. P. M. Gerdes,
   *Comparing Notions of Dense Computability on omega^omega and 2^omega*.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06925v1
   https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06925v1
   Relevant: coarse and effective-dense conventions and Question 7. This
   draft does not claim to settle its general effective-dense component.

5. D. R. Belanger,
   *Conservation theorems for the Cohesiveness Principle*.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13011
   Relevant: contemporary primary-source context on Friedberg jump-inversion
   methods. The elementary relative inversion actually used is fully proved
   in Appendix B, and is not imported from a variant with different hypotheses.

## Novelty boundary

The source review identifies clear classical ingredients and the explicit
repository target. It is not a systematic bibliographic priority determination.
The package contains no copied external paper or font file. The mathematical
proofs, coding presentation, and supporting verifier were written for this task.
