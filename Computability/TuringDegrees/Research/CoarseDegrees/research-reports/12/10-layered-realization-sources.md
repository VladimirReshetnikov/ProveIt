## Published primary sources

1. Denis R. Hirschfeldt, Carl G. Jockusch, Jr., Rutger Kuyper, and Paul E. Schupp.
   *Coarse reducibility and algorithmic randomness*. Journal of Symbolic Logic
   81 (2016), 1028–1046.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/jsl.2015.70
   Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01707
   PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.01707
   Checked especially: Section 2 coarsenings, Definition 3.1, Theorem 3.7,
   and its application to arbitrarily accurate computable approximants.

2. Carl G. Jockusch, Jr., and Paul E. Schupp.
   *Generic computability, Turing degrees, and asymptotic density*.
   Journal of the London Mathematical Society (2) 85 (2012), 472–490.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jdr051
   Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.5212
   PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.5212
   Checked especially: Theorem 2.19 (dyadic code and the halting problem)
   and the adjacent limit-lemma discussion. The preprint title differs from the
   published title by using “Turing reducibility”.

3. Damir D. Dzhafarov and Gregory Igusa.
   *Notions of robust information coding*. Computability 6 (2017), 105–124.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.3233/COM-160059
   Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2982
   Background for uniform/nonuniform robust reducibilities; not a source of the
   claimed new ideal-realization theorem.

4. Richard Friedberg.
   *A criterion for completeness of degrees of unsolvability*.
   Journal of Symbolic Logic 22 (1957), 159–160.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/2964177
   Original publisher page:
   https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/article/criterion-for-completeness-of-degrees-of-unsolvability/BFECDB6FB6FB2AAA39F125E4F5251413
   The publisher's extract verifies the original theorem and metadata.
   The exact relativized version used here is proved in Appendix A; no uninspected
   detail of the original proof is needed. This is not the later review article,
   which has a different DOI.

## Scope of the comparison

The literature checks were targeted rather than exhaustive. The manuscript's
strongest priority statement is a resolution of the question as left open in the
specified repository snapshot. It makes no claim that a global search has
established the nonexistence of equivalent results elsewhere.
