## Primary published/preprint sources

Peter M. Gerdes, *Comparing Notions of Dense Computability on omega^omega and
2^omega*, arXiv:2508.06925v1 (9 August 2025).

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06925v1
- https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06925v1
- https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06925v1

Verified: the coarse-description and reducibility conventions in Definitions

not claim to settle the separate effective-dense question. The PDF question

Denis R. Hirschfeldt, Carl G. Jockusch, Jr., Rutger Kuyper, and Paul E. Schupp,
*Coarse Reducibility and Algorithmic Randomness*, Journal of Symbolic Logic
81(3) (2016), 1028–1046; preprint arXiv:1505.01707.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01707
- https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.01707
- https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~drh/Papers/coarsereducibility.html

Verified: Definition 3.1 introduces the invariant called I_X in this note;
Theorem 4.3, attributed to Igusa, says that coarse computability bound one
implies trivial invariant, including non-coarsely-computable examples.
The author's publication record confirms the 2016 journal citation.

Inference explicitly proved in Section 1.1: trivial invariant plus absence
of a computable coarse description already excludes a least representative
in either the uniform or nonuniform coarse class. Thus the literal negative
answer is not presented as newly resolving an open problem.

## Mathematical work developed here

The metric and prefix-search proofs, the all-or-meager argument, simultaneous
exact partners, perfect fusion, the explicit guarded construction, and the
resulting coinitiality and relative statements are written out in the article.
Their proof validity and their historical novelty are separate questions.
The article does not claim a comprehensive literature priority determination.

Targeted searches included combinations of “coarse descriptions”, “exact
pair”, “perfect”, “minimal pair”, and “least Turing degree”. They did not
establish a matching prior statement or its absence. Search silence is not
evidence of originality. The reference list is deliberately small: no
unrelated search results are used as mathematical authority.
