                                                 Abstract
         We fuse the fixed-coefficient construction of Research Report 47 with the two dense spatial
     Horner expressions in the initializer of Research Report 44. A three-band cyclic-index identity
     factors the operation corrections into twelve short spatial factors and twenty-four rotated
     occurrence factors. Exact repeated-block grouping compresses the remaining fixed spatial profiles,
     with division-free, fully paid geometric weights. The resulting literal-1/3 sources have 14,658,934
     arithmetic operations for two positive raw inputs and 14,658,944 for one. They compute exactly
     the same complete integer polynomial, on every assignment of the same coordinates, as the
     frozen predecessors. The positive witness counts remain 465 and 467, with one equation and
     exact variable degree 2,304,000. We prove the new polynomial identities, audit the two precise
     source replacements and every retained consumer, give the complete arithmetic ledger, and
     distinguish algebraic proof, finite checks and source reconstruction. No quotient-ring identity,
     nonzero assumption on the spatial variable, global optimality or operation-record claim is used.


1    Result and comparison
Reports 44 and 47 specify a fixed Diophantine polynomial source arising from a periodic-ant
realization of the pinned U15 machine [1, 2]. Report 47 reduces the cost of constructing the fixed
coefficients. The present refinement avoids first constructing all spatial coefficients separately and
then evaluating two dense polynomials from them. Instead, it constructs those two polynomials
directly from the same exact coefficient identities.
   An arithmetic gate is a binary addition, subtraction or multiplication of already available
expressions. We write M for multiplication count and A for the combined addition/subtraction
count. The only constant leaves are 1 and 3. Fixed loop indices, mask bits and exponents are
compilation data; they do not supply free integer leaves. Aliasing an available expression is free,
while every emitted arithmetic operation, including operations on zero or one, is paid. Gates are
expressions, not extra quantified witnesses.

7.2   Topological binding and polynomial equality

The input-independent prefix is generated first. The old main is then regenerated by the authenticated
owned arithmetic generator, whose complete old canonical hash must match the frozen target for
each arity. Its declarations are preserved. Fusion is inserted just before old gate 1264, after the
referenced W coordinate is available.
    Each retained old gate is mapped to a new gate with the same opcode. Its operands bind to an
earlier retained gate, an unchanged declared coordinate, one of the 598 paid constant aliases, or
one of the two proved fused outputs. There is no residual C: operand in the resulting source. The
profile-cache closure described in Section 6 ensures the fused construction cannot silently append a
late constant to the already copied prefix state.

Proof of Theorem 1.1, equality and invariants. All prefix aliases have the prescribed old integer
values by the inherited occurrence and profile identities and the explicit paid constant chains.
Proposition 5.1 supplies exactly the old polynomial at each removed interval’s final output. Induct
on retained old gates: if all mapped operands have the old polynomial values, applying the unchanged
opcode gives the same polynomial. The mapping is topological by construction and no removed
interior value is referenced. The same induction verifies (19), all later initializer consumers, all
residual operands and every finalizer gate.
    For m = 285 in the two-input version, or m = 286 in the one-input version, the retained finalizer
computes each residual difference ri = ai − bi , squares it, and sums all squares. Thus it retains
mM + (2m − 1)A, namely 285M + 569A or 286M + 571A. Residual pairs are metadata, not extra
asserted equations. The single final equation has that sum on the left and paid gate 0 = 1 − 1 on the
right. Coordinate declarations and positive domains are identical, so no new witness is introduced.
   The old polynomial’s exact degree is 4S = 2, 304, 000: its only degree-2S residuals have leading
homogeneous term −W 2S , giving leading final part 2W 4S , as established in Report 44. Equality of
the complete polynomial preserves that exact degree. We do not infer it from syntactic intermediate
degrees of the factored source, which can ignore cancellations. The operation counts completing the
theorem are derived in Section 8.

10      Further questions suggested by this construction
The result leaves concrete opportunities, none of which are included in the present count.
    1. Fuse the occurrence compiler more deeply. The retained occurrence construction uses
       13,656,984 gates, about 93% of the new complete source. A direct bivariate or block-valued run
       construction might avoid materializing every Ta,s . It would need an exact ordinary-polynomial
       invariant at the cyclic boundaries and a paid source ledger.
    2. Share rotated factors algebraically. The twenty-four long Horner expressions are exact
       and simple but do not exhaust possible sharing. Rotation formulas using prefixes and suffixes
       might lower their cost; division by W and reduction modulo W S − 1 would not be valid
       substitutes for an ordinary-polynomial proof.
    3. Refine the paid profile and anchor grammar. This source deliberately retains the
       complete old profile cache and all anchor rows. A narrower cache, shared addition chains or a
       proved anchor factorization may help, provided every remaining consumer and constant port
       is still paid and independently checked.
    4. Evaluate alternative band widths and block decompositions. The present 600-column
       split matches physical slots. Other exact decompositions may trade short-factor cost against
       occurrence-factor length or cache reuse. Any improvement must include the new powers and
       geometric weights rather than treating them as free.
    5. Separate arithmetic size from execution cost. Source-generation memory, validation
       time and bit complexity of numerical evaluation merit distinct analysis. A lower gate count
       alone does not settle any of them or establish a lower bound on the best possible source.
The stopping point of this report is the independently reproduced 14,658,934/14,658,944-gate
construction and its exact polynomial identity. The frozen earlier reports remain available as
independently valid constructions and as the provenance of the inherited mathematical assumptions.
