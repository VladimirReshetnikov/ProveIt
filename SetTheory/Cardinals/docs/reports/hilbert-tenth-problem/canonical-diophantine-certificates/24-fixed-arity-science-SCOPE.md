# Fixed-arity binary sandpile research

This work constructs an integer-only, horizon-free certificate for existence of finite total stabilization with binary odometer in the threshold-six lattice. It deliberately does not certify target firing, a prescribed odometer, earliest ranks, unique witnesses, or real-exactness.

The central simplification is least action: a finite-support binary stabilizing supersolution bounds the actual legal odometer, which therefore exists, is finite, and is binary. Supersolutions may have unnecessary topplings. This preserves stabilization existence only.

Planned complete input interface: three positive periods; a base-32 periodic tile code; three positive finite-addition box dimensions; and a base-32 finite-addition code. Heights in the periodic tile are 0 through 5 and added heights are 0 through 15. All accepted binary-stabilizing configurations necessarily have initial height at most 11, so rejected larger added heights cannot cause a false negative within valid encodings. The finite patch is anchored at the origin. A finite list of Cantor pairing equations can combine the eight parameters into one ordinary integer input.

Construction: centered unknown period-aligned padded prism; Lucas-parity masks paid by three explicit power relations each; all powers expanded by a fixed fifteen-equation constructive Pell macro; geometric repetition and two masked block-spreading steps per input tensor; one carry-free packed conservation equation. No time horizon, bounded universal quantifier, variable conjunction, floor operation, or generic MRDP theorem is to remain in the final source.

Literal-loader scope remains inherited from Reports 35/36: the reports' loader claims one-shot legal dynamics and halting iff finite total topplings. The generic certificate can encode its periodic tile and finite perturbation as ordinary integers, but reconstructing the particular multimillion-node graph or the program-to-tape compiler is outside this new arithmetic theorem.

No repository writes, publishing, or upstream-code execution are authorized or performed. Newly authored finite checkers may run after inspection.
