\begin{abstract}
A rational signal machine computes by the collisions of finitely many kinds of particles moving at fixed rational speeds on a line. This report prints two independent manuscripts that prove one theorem by two routes: once a complete finite collision history is fixed, its realizations are exactly the natural zeros of a sum of squares of integer affine residuals, of degree two, with exactly one witness tuple for each realization. In both, typed endpoint conditions on maximal adjacency intervals exclude unrecorded earlier collisions, omitted simultaneous inputs and omitted simultaneous sites, and causal depth controls the denominators of the event coordinates.

Part~I (source~07) eliminates every event coordinate. The realizing initial-gap vectors form a relatively open rational polyhedral cone cut out by at most $3(n-1)+(3m+1)E$ linear conditions, and the only witnesses are the slacks of the strict rows. The chamber geometry yields small integral realizations, an NP upper bound for bounded synthesis, a robustness--codimension criterion, a canonical first-hit quartic for a fixed event budget, exact quasi-polynomial seed counts, an exactly solved first-collision tournament and a four-speed accumulating clock with exponentially growing denominators. Part~II (source~11) keeps batch times and event positions as natural witnesses on a fixed rational grid, adds halting semantics, proves that the witness matrix has full column rank (so the certificate is strictly convex in its witnesses), gives linear witness and residual ledgers and a full-layer reference construction, and bounds the canonical witnesses by $60^K\max(1,M_0)$ for the four-speed interface of Durand-Lose's universal machine.

Neither Part compiles unbounded halting into one fixed-arity polynomial, transcribes the published universal machine, or improves the repository's 75- and 87-operation bounds. Part~I's convex-quadratic barrier is a special case of Part~XI of the collection's report \emph{canonical-diophantine-certificates}. The report is AI-assisted and unrefereed; nothing in it is formalized in Lean or Rocq.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The two sources}\label{smc:sec:parts}

This report is built from two AI-assisted research manuscripts that arrived in one delivery as batch~78 of ProveIt's incoming reports (manuscripts~07 and~11; Appendix~\ref{smc:app:provenance} gives the full provenance). They are called \emph{source~07} and \emph{source~11} after their batch-78 manuscript numbers, which are also the file prefixes of their shipped programs and data. Neither cites the other, and a text-overlap check at placement found almost no shared wording (at most 0.5 per cent of either text's eight-word sequences occur in the other). They nevertheless prove the same theorem, by the same mechanism, in two different witness formats (Section~\ref{smc:sec:routes}).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Part~\ref{smc:part:cg} (source~07, the base), \emph{Collision geometry is linear}.} Arbitrary rational speeds, of either sign. Every event time and position is eliminated as a rational linear form in the initial gaps, so a complete chronological skeleton becomes a homogeneous rational chamber (Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:chamber}) and a convex quadratic with unique slack witnesses (Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:quadratic}). The chamber geometry then gives denominator and height bounds, small integral realizations, an NP upper bound, robustness and codimension, finite-horizon semilinearity, a canonical first-hit quartic, exact Ehrhart counts, two exact examples and a convex-quadratic barrier.
\item \textbf{Part~\ref{smc:part:sd} (source~11), \emph{Direct Diophantine certificates}.} Speeds and positions normalized to natural numbers by an external Galilean change of coordinates. Batch times and event positions stay in the certificate as natural witnesses on the grid $B=D^K$, and the certificate has prefix and halting versions (Theorem~\ref{smc:sd:thm:main}). Its additions are the halting semantics with terminal rays, full column rank of the witness matrix and hence strict convexity in the witnesses (Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:rank}), an explicit witness and residual ledger, a full-layer reference construction with exact counts (Section~\ref{smc:sd:sec:fulllayer}), the witness-height bound for Durand-Lose's four-speed interface (Theorem~\ref{smc:sd:thm:height}) and a positive-witness translation.
\end{itemize}

Source~07 is the base because it assumes less (signed rational speeds, no normalization interface) and proves more about the chamber; source~11 is printed in full as the second route together with its additions. Each Part keeps its source's definitions, letters and numbering of statements. The common theorem is stated in both Parts, because its two statements have different inputs and witnesses; where a lemma of Part~II coincides with one of Part~I, a \wtag\ note at the lemma says so, and source~11's proof is kept as a marked second route.

\subsection{One theorem by two routes}\label{smc:sec:routes}

\textbf{The common theorem.} Fix a rational signal machine, an initial label word and a finite chronological collision history (a \emph{skeleton} in Part~I, a \emph{schema} in Part~II). There is an effective sum of squares of integer affine residuals, in the input coordinates and natural witnesses, whose natural zeros are exactly the realizations of that history, with exactly one witness tuple for each realization; its degree is at most two. This is Theorems~\ref{smc:cg:thm:chamber} and~\ref{smc:cg:thm:quadratic} of Part~I and Theorem~\ref{smc:sd:thm:main} of Part~II (for prefix semantics).

\textbf{The common mechanism.} Both sources record each pair of adjacent signals once, over the maximal interval during which it stays adjacent, and impose a \emph{typed} condition at each end of that interval: equality exactly when both segments are born (or die) at the same collision there, and strict positivity otherwise, including for a pair that survives the last listed batch. Affineness of the gap then excludes every interior meeting (Part~I, Lemma~\ref{smc:cg:lem:endpoint}; Part~II, Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:interior}), and the strict conditions at a batch boundary make the declared blocks exactly the collision sites (the soundness part of the proof of Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:chamber}; Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:boundary}). The two sources then prove the same auxiliary statements: the lifetime count $(n-1)+\sum_e(q_e+1)$ (\eqref{smc:cg:eq:Jbound} and \eqref{smc:sd:eq:Lbound}, where Part~II writes $|\out(e)|$ for $q_e$), causal-depth denominators dividing $D^h$ (Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:denominator}, Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:denom}), and semilinearity of the inputs accepted under a fixed event bound (Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:semilinear}, Proposition~\ref{smc:sd:prop:semilinear}). Table~\ref{smc:tab:routes} compares the two formats.

\textbf{Normalizations.} The two definitions of $D$ agree. Part~I multiplies all speeds by an integer $L$ and leaves their signs; Part~II subtracts a common speed $c$ and multiplies by an integer $m$. A common shift does not change speed differences, so with $m=L$ both take $D$ to be the least common multiple of the same nonzero integer differences. Part~II needs nonnegative speeds and an origin because its inputs are natural absolute positions; Part~I needs neither because its input is the gap vector. Part~I's integer seeds come from homogeneity (scaling a rational chamber point); Part~II fixes the scale outside the witness tuple and warns that a scale witness or variable denominators would not preserve its canonical quadratic claim without a new argument (Section~\ref{smc:sd:sec:normalize}).

\textbf{Two shuttles, one mechanism.} Part~I's clock and Part~II's shuttle are both a particle reflected between two converging walls, each round trip shrinking the remaining time to the accumulation by a factor three, so that the reduced denominators grow exponentially in the number of events while their bit lengths grow linearly. They are different machines: Part~I's has a stationary left wall and a right wall of speed $-1$, and its every finite prefix has the whole positive quadrant as chamber (robust chronology with exponentially growing precision); Part~II's lives entirely in $0<t<2$, $0<x<6$ and is stated for its natural-speed interface. Neither source gives the accumulation point a continuation. Source~07 credits the accumulation phenomenon to Becker et al.~\cite{accumulations}.

\textbf{Two missed simultaneities, one failure mode.} In both examples a proposed batch omits a second, spatially separate collision that happens at the same time, and in both the omitted pair is an unchanged adjacent pair whose gap reaches zero exactly at the last listed boundary. The strict upper endpoint of a surviving pair rejects it: in Part~I through the chamber row that must be positive there, in Part~II through the residual $G(T_1)-h^+-1=-h^+-1$. Part~II adds the observation that weaker conditions (two nonnegative endpoint gaps with positive sum) would accept the false schema.

\textbf{Two meanings of ``sparse''.} In Part~I ``event-sparse'' counts geometric conditions: the bound \eqref{smc:cg:eq:Rbound} is linear in $n$ and $E$, but a propagated intercept may depend on every initial gap, so the rows can be dense and storing them can take $O(dR_\sigma)$ coefficients (the paragraph after Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:rows}). In Part~II the sparse certificate also has residuals of bounded width, because event coordinates are witnesses; its coefficients then contain the grid constant $B=D^K$. \emph{Neither} size statement is a constant arithmetic-operation count for an unbounded computation, as both sources say.

Three words need the same care.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Skeleton} (Part~I) and \textbf{schema} (Part~II) carry the same information: an ordered list of batches, each a set of disjoint contiguous blocks of the live list. A skeleton lists only the collision blocks (at least two members); a schema partitions the whole live list and marks singletons as persisting signals. A \emph{site} of Part~I is an \emph{event} of Part~II.
\item \textbf{Realization.} Part~I's realization and Part~II's prefix realization are the same notion: exactly the listed batches as the first batches, nothing asserted afterwards. Part~II's \emph{halting} realization additionally excludes every future collision. The ``halting relation'' of Part~I's Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:barrier} is the undecidable halting of a universal machine, a different thing.
\item \textbf{Convex.} Part~I's certificate is convex (positive semidefinite Hessian) jointly in seed and slacks; Part~II's is \emph{strictly} convex in the witnesses with the input held fixed, and Part~II states that joint strict convexity is neither needed nor generally true.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relation to the repository and to the literature}\label{smc:sec:relation}

Neither source cites the related material below; this subsection supplies the cross-references (pins and dates in Appendix~\ref{smc:app:provenance}). Reports of the collection are cited by label name, since their labels do not resolve in this file.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Report \emph{canonical-diophantine-certificates}, Part~XI}~\cite{cdc}, ``Order-free self-assembly and a positivity-restricted degree threshold''. Its Lemma \texttt{cdc:of:lem:affinezero}, Theorem \texttt{cdc:of:thm:cubicsemilinear} and Theorem \texttt{cdc:of:thm:classification} ($\mathrm D^+_2(k)=\mathrm D^+_3(k)=\mathrm{SL}(k)$) prove, for polynomials of degree at most three that are nonnegative only on the real nonnegative orthant, that the full natural zero set is a finite union of natural solution sets of rational affine systems, hence semilinear and effectively so. \item \textbf{Report \emph{liveness-beyond-halting}}~\cite{lbh}. Its Part~V prints a question of its source~11, ``Optimal degree under stronger geometric restrictions'', which asks for the minimal degree and dimension of a witness-free, binary-branching local polynomial that is globally nonnegative on all real inputs, and remarks that the globally nonnegative quadratic case ``has a much more rigid affine geometry''. Part~I's Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:rigidity} (and, more generally, Part~XI of~\cite{cdc}) states that geometry: such a quadratic has a rational affine zero set. This report does not settle that degree question.
\item \textbf{The Hilbert-tenth-problem programme has already reviewed both manuscripts.} The review of the delivery of 2 October 2026~\cite{review808}, with its detailed signal-machine part~\cite{reviewsignal}, replayed both suites from the original archives, read both articles in full and reports ``PASS for both reports' stated finite-schema, exact-domain theorems and generated examples'', with no mandatory correction. It also proves and checks two reductions that neither source claims: deleting source~11's identically zero same-birth rows and its same-death rows (differences of retained flight rows), and erasing the initial-order slacks when $K>0$ or the certificate is halting, which takes the worked annihilation from six residuals and five witnesses to four and four (a bijection of natural zero sets, not of nonnegative rational ones, with a rational counterexample); and deleting redundant strict rows, with computed slacks, from the five exported packets of source~07. Those reductions are the review's, are not re-proved here, and do not change any theorem of either Part. This report's intake is therefore not a first review; it adds the merge, the cross-references and the credit of Remark~\ref{smc:cg:rem:credit}.

A sibling audit~\cite{blockca} notes that ``finite particles can in principle store unbounded values in their separation'' and that ``a new construction would have to establish that interface''. Durand-Lose's halting universal signal machine~\cite{durandlose} uses a finite initial configuration and a halting event (Part~II, Section~\ref{smc:sd:sec:purpose}), so signal machines are a candidate for such an interface, and both Parts compile its finite collision histories in principle; but neither transcribes its universal rule table or its input loader (Part~I, Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:implementation}; Part~II, Question~\ref{smc:sd:q:universal}), so this report does not establish that interface.
\item \textbf{The repository's universal bounds.} The project README~\cite{repo} still states the established bounds of 75 operations for a complete certificate and 87 for one universal polynomial. Neither Part improves or bears on them; both say so.

\subsection{Status and what is not claimed}\label{smc:sec:status}

The report is AI-assisted and unrefereed, and \textbf{nothing in it is formalized in Lean or Rocq}. Both sources give conventional proofs together with finite exact-arithmetic checks that are regression evidence, not proofs. Every limitation stated by either source is kept in its Part; collected here, neither Part claims
\begin{itemize}
\item historical priority, or exhaustive novelty, for its constructions (Part~I, Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:intro}; Part~II, Section~\ref{smc:sd:sec:purpose}); Part~II adds that a general linearizability of signal-machine evolution is already in Durand-Lose's linear real-register simulation~\cite{DL07};
\item one fixed-arity polynomial for unbounded halting, a single-fold or finite-fold Diophantine representation of c.e.\ sets, or a fixed-universal arithmetic-operation bound: the skeleton or schema, the machine and the input length are compiler data, and the row and residual counts are not operation counts;
\item that the published universal machine (13 meta-signals, 21 non-blank rules) or its input loader has been transcribed, executed or certified;
\item any semantics at or after an accumulation point; the clock and the shuttle are tests of finite prefixes, and the clock is not a claim to have discovered accumulation;
\item NP-hardness (Corollary~\ref{smc:cg:cor:NP} is an upper bound), an efficient enumeration of first-hit chambers or of their volumes, or an optimized exhaustive enumerator in the code;
\item that the denominator-growth examples are lower bounds for arbitrary Diophantine representations: Part~II's is a lower bound for common-grid encodings;
\item that integer scaling of a rational seed preserves a prescribed physical length or deadline;
\item that the finite checks are exhaustive, or that the shared simulator of Part~II's two compilers is independently verified;
\item any Lean or Rocq verification; neither source's code is a verified compiler.
\end{itemize}

\section{The question and the contribution}
\label{smc:cg:sec:intro}

\subsection{A concrete gap between universality and arithmetic}

The MRDP theorem guarantees that a computably enumerable acceptance relation
has a Diophantine representation. That existence theorem does not itself give
a transparent arithmetic description of a particular computational substrate.
A useful substrate-specific compiler should explain what its witnesses mean,
why they cannot describe a counterfeit computation, and which resource bound
is actually being measured. The distinction is especially important when the
substrate has a geometric, asynchronous, or apparently continuous dynamics.
For background on the classical theorem and a machine-based formalization,
see Matiyasevich~\cite{matiyasevich} and Bayer et al.~\cite{afp}.

The \texttt{ProveIt} Hilbert-tenth-problem material motivates precisely this
separation. Its reversible finite-history audit distinguishes cheap local
relations from a complete finite-input computation certificate, and points
to additional obligations concerning wiring, initialization, boundaries,
and halting observations~\cite{repo,reversible}. This article develops an
analogous complete \emph{geometric frontend} for a different substrate:
one-dimensional rational signal machines. It does not attempt another
operation-count reduction for the repository's universal polynomial.

\wtag\ The repository has since reviewed this manuscript, and the related manuscript printed as Part~\ref{smc:part:sd}, in its Hilbert-tenth-problem research tree (Section~\ref{smc:sec:relation}). The reversible-history audit cited here is unchanged from this source's pin to the write.


Signal machines are not merely collections of unrelated finite circuits.
Durand-Lose gives a halting universal construction with 13 signal types and
21 collision rules, obtained through cyclic tag systems~\cite{durandlose}.
This established universality is the reason to study the substrate. We do
not reproduce or independently certify that universal machine's full rule
table and input loader here. Instead we prove a generic finite-history
compiler whose interface applies to the rational fixed-speed model.

The central question addressed is the following.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Can an explicitly proposed finite collision history be
represented arithmetically without introducing arbitrary event coordinates,
without overlooking an earlier collision, and without splitting a genuinely
simultaneous event into a false sequence?}
\medskip

Our answer is affirmative. In fact, the required geometric residuals can be
linear rather than quadratic, and their sum of squares can therefore be
quadratic rather than quartic. The essential work is not squaring equations;
it is showing that a small set of order constraints certifies the
\emph{complete} chronology.

\subsection{Results and their logical scope}

For a fixed initial label word and a fixed discrete skeleton $\sigma$, we
construct integer matrices $\mathsf E_\sigma,\mathsf G_\sigma$ such that the
real initial-gap vectors realizing that skeleton are exactly
\begin{equation}
 \Ccal_\sigma=
 \{g\in\R^{n-1}:\mathsf E_\sigma g=0,
                         \ \mathsf G_\sigma g>0\}.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:mainchamber}
\end{equation}
Initial gap positivity is included among the strict rows. Every event time
and position is a rational linear form in $g$. Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:sparse}
proves the event-sparse bound
\begin{equation}
 R_\sigma\le 3(n-1)+(3m+1)E
 \label{smc:cg:eq:mainbound}
\end{equation}
for the number of raw linear residuals and inequalities.

The corresponding natural-number certificate is
\begin{equation}
 P_\sigma(g,z)=
 \sum_i(\mathsf E_{\sigma,i}g)^2+
 \sum_j(\mathsf G_{\sigma,j}g-1-z_j)^2.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:mainpoly}
\end{equation}
Its degree is at most two, and its entire auxiliary tuple is forced to be
$z_j=\mathsf G_{\sigma,j}g-1$. This uniqueness concerns a fixed externally
supplied skeleton, not a fixed universal MRDP polynomial.

The same linearization gives several additional conclusions. A collision at
causal depth $h$ has coordinate denominators dividing $D^h$ after integer
speed normalization, where $D$ is the least common multiple of the nonzero
speed differences. Every nonempty chamber has a small positive integer
point. A chamber's synchronization equalities determine its exact
codimension and its perturbation behavior. For a fixed event budget,
first-acceptance seeds form a finite disjoint union of such chambers, and
counting them in growing integer boxes gives exact quasi-polynomials.

These conclusions solve specific representation, realizability, precision,
and counting questions formulated here. They are not presented as the
settlement of a named longstanding conjecture. The literature review
establishes the underlying signal-machine model and related accumulation
results; it does not establish historical priority for every combination of
lemmas in this article. The proofs are intended to stand on their own and
to be suitable for further independent review.

\section{Rational signal machines and chronological skeletons}
\label{smc:cg:sec:model}

\subsection{Particles, velocities, and collision rules}

\begin{definition}[Machine]
A rational signal machine consists of a finite label set $M$, a velocity map
$v:M\to\Q$, and a deterministic rule for every admissible incoming collision
set under consideration. An incoming set contains at least two labels with
pairwise distinct velocities. Its output is a possibly empty set of labels
with pairwise distinct velocities. A signal of label $a$ moves along a
straight line of slope $v(a)$ until it participates in a collision.
\end{definition}

We use a transparent default rule when an explicit rule is absent: the
incoming labels reappear as outgoing labels, ordered by increasing velocity.
Such a crossing still counts as a collision. Alternatively, one may specify
all relevant rules explicitly and reject a skeleton requiring an undefined
rule. The attached implementation supports both conventions.

At a collision all incoming signals are consumed and the prescribed outputs
are born at that space-time point. Newly born outputs are ordered by
increasing velocity in the right-hand time limit. This makes them separate
rather than immediately collide again. At each positive time, all spatially
distinct collisions occurring at that time are processed together.

This is the fixed-speed straight-line model used in abstract geometrical
computation~\cite{durandlose}. Our arithmetic interface restricts initial
configurations to finitely many signals at distinct positions and considers
ordinary finite prefixes. An infinite sequence accumulating at a finite time
has no automatically assigned continuation in this article.

\subsection{Initial coordinates and normalization}

Fix $n\ge2$ initial labels $a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1}$, in increasing spatial order,
and write $d=n-1$. Translation invariance lets us put
\begin{equation}
 x_0=0,\qquad x_i=g_1+\cdots+g_i\quad(1\le i\le d),
 \qquad g_i>0.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:gaps}
\end{equation}
Repeated labels at distinct positions are permitted. We call $g$ the
\emph{seed geometry}; the label word is separate discrete input.

The principal chamber theorem allows real $g$. The Diophantine theorem uses
integer $g$. A rational gap vector can be multiplied by a positive common
denominator to become integral. Scaling all positions by $c>0$ scales all
event times by $c$ without changing any collision rule or chronological
skeleton. Thus integral seeds suffice for existence of a rational
realization, but they need not preserve a separately specified physical
length or time bound.

When all velocities are rational, choose an integer $L>0$ such that $Lv(a)$
is integral for every label. In the time coordinate $\tau=t/L$, all speeds
are integers $Lv(a)$. Denominator bounds below are stated in this normalized
time coordinate. An arbitrary common translation in velocity is not needed.

\subsection{Batches and stable signal identities}

\begin{definition}[Chronological skeleton]
A finite skeleton is an ordered list of nonempty batches. Each batch is an
ordered list of pairwise disjoint contiguous blocks in the current live
signal list, each block having at least two members. The blocks are listed
from left to right. Each block is a collision site, with its rule and outputs
determined by the incoming labels. New signal identities are assigned by
batch, then by site, then by increasing output velocity.
\end{definition}

The initial identities are $0,\ldots,n-1$. A consumed identity cannot be used
again; a noninitial identity must have been generated by an earlier batch.
Within a single batch, outputs cannot serve as inputs of another site.
These are discrete well-formedness conditions, not arithmetic constraints
left for a witness to evade.

Let $B$ be the number of batches and $E$ the total number of collision sites.
At site $e$, write $p_e$ and $q_e$ for the numbers of incoming and outgoing
signals. Let $m$ be the number of distinct velocities. Then
\begin{equation}
 2\le p_e\le m,\qquad 0\le q_e\le m,\qquad B\le E.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:arity}
\end{equation}
The quantities $B$, $E$, and causal depth are different. Many sites may share
a batch; many batches may contain events unrelated to a given causal chain.

\begin{definition}[Realization]
A positive seed $g$ realizes a skeleton $\sigma$ when its actual execution
has exactly the specified batches as its first $B$ batches, with exactly the
specified incoming blocks and outputs at every listed site. In particular,
no earlier event and no simultaneous site or extra incoming signal may be
omitted. Nothing is required about events after the final listed batch.
\end{definition}

The empty skeleton is realized by every positive seed. It states only that
no initial portion has yet been requested; it does not state that the
machine never collides. A malformed skeleton is assigned the empty
realization set. One can encode a statically rejected skeleton by the
constant polynomial $1$ if a total polynomial-producing interface is desired.

\section{Eliminating every event coordinate}
\label{smc:cg:sec:linear}

\subsection{Intercept propagation}

For each signal segment $s$, let $v_s$ be its fixed velocity and write its
supporting line as
\begin{equation}
 x_s(t)=b_s+v_st.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:line}
\end{equation}
Its intercept $b_s$ need not be its birth position: the birth may occur at a
positive time. Initially, the $b_s$ are the partial sums in
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:gaps}, hence homogeneous integer linear forms in $g$.

Suppose an event contains two inputs $s,r$ with $v_s\ne v_r$. Their formal
meeting time and position are
\begin{equation}
 t_e=\frac{b_r-b_s}{v_s-v_r},\qquad
 x_e=b_s+v_st_e.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:meet}
\end{equation}
For an outgoing label of velocity $w$, its intercept is
\begin{equation}
 b_{\mathrm{out}}=x_e-wt_e.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:out}
\end{equation}
The denominator $v_s-v_r$ is fixed by the machine, not a variable.
Consequently these formulas preserve rational linearity.

\begin{lemma}[Coordinate elimination]
For every well-formed finite skeleton, all formally reconstructed intercepts,
event times, and event positions are homogeneous rational linear forms in
the initial gap vector. An event with more than two incoming signals requires
only additional homogeneous rational linear equalities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the ordered batches. Initial intercepts are linear. Equations
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:meet} and \eqref{smc:cg:eq:out} use only addition and multiplication or
division by fixed rational constants. An additional incoming signal $u$
requires $b_u+v_ut_e=x_e$, again a homogeneous linear equality. Every new
intercept is then available for subsequent batches. No output of the current
batch is used by another event in that batch, so this induction is acyclic.
\end{proof}

All event coordinates are therefore \emph{computed data}. They need not
appear as free Diophantine witnesses. A certificate containing unconstrained
coordinate variables would obscure both the low degree and the uniqueness
that result from this elimination.

\subsection{Local incidence does not imply a real computation}

The incidence equations alone are insufficient. Three signals starting at
$0,u,u+v$ with respective speeds $2,0,-1$ formally give the first two a
meeting time $u/2$. If $u=3$ and $v=1$, however, the second and third signals
collide already at time $1$, before $u/2=3/2$. An equation witnessing the
later formal intersection cannot establish that the two original segments
are still alive there.

There is also a boundary failure. When $u=2v$, all three signals meet at
once. A purported binary event containing only the first two signals has
perfectly consistent incidence coordinates but the wrong incoming set.
A compiler must distinguish strict separation from weak separation at
precisely these boundaries.

For events in the same batch, choose the leftmost site's formal time as
$t_k$. Equate all other site times in that batch to $t_k$, and require
\begin{equation}
 t_k-t_{k-1}>0,\qquad t_0=0.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:batchorder}
\end{equation}
These conditions are necessary but still do not exclude an unlisted earlier
collision. The missing ingredient is a complete spatial-order certificate.

\section{A sparse certificate for complete collision chronology}
\label{smc:cg:sec:sparse}

\subsection{Maximal adjacency intervals}

Between collisions the live signals have a strict spatial order. The
skeleton tells us how that order is updated: replace each incoming block by
its output block, in increasing velocity order. This replacement changes
only a bounded number of adjacent pairs.

\begin{definition}[Adjacency interval]
A maximal adjacency interval records an ordered pair $(s,r)$, left signal
first, that remains adjacent in the live list from one batch boundary to a
later batch boundary. The interval begins when the pair first becomes
adjacent and ends when it ceases to be adjacent or when the requested prefix
ends. An unchanged pair is not closed and reopened merely because an
unrelated event occurs elsewhere.
\end{definition}

Intervals created at the final batch have zero duration and need not be
checked: their output order is already determined by the distinct velocities.
Every recorded positive-duration interval has an affine gap
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{sr}(t)=b_r-b_s+(v_r-v_s)t.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:gapline}
\end{equation}
At its initial boundary this gap is prescribed to be zero exactly when both
segments are born at the same collision there. Otherwise it is prescribed
to be strictly positive. At its final boundary it is prescribed to be zero
exactly when both segments are consumed at the same collision there.
Otherwise it is strictly positive.

Both endpoints cannot be prescribed zero for a positive-duration interval.
Indeed, two distinct straight-line segments cannot separate after a common
birth and then meet again without one undergoing an intervening collision.
Such a proposal is rejected statically. Each surviving interval contributes
exactly two linear conditions before redundant zero rows are removed.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint lemma]\label{smc:cg:lem:endpoint}
Let $a<b$ and let $f$ be affine on $[a,b]$. If $f(a),f(b)\ge0$ and at least
one of them is positive, then $f(t)>0$ for every $a<t<b$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $t=(1-\lambda)a+\lambda b$ with $0<\lambda<1$,
$f(t)=(1-\lambda)f(a)+\lambda f(b)$ is a positive convex combination of
nonnegative numbers, at least one of which is positive.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Soundness and completeness}

\begin{theorem}[Exact chronological chamber]
\label{smc:cg:thm:chamber}
Fix a rational signal machine, an initial label word, and a finite skeleton
$\sigma$. There is an effective construction of rational homogeneous linear
equalities and strict inequalities whose solution set is exactly the set of
real seed geometries realizing $\sigma$. All simultaneous collisions and all
extra incoming signals are accounted for. Every nonempty solution set is a
relatively open rational polyhedral cone in the kernel of its equality
matrix.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First reject malformed identities, overlapping or noncontiguous input
blocks, undefined rules under the nontransparent convention, and incoming
blocks whose velocities fail to decrease strictly from left to right.
The latter condition is necessary because immediately before a multiple
meeting, two incoming lines $s,r$ ordered left to right have gap
$(v_s-v_r)(t_e-t)>0$ and hence $v_s>v_r$.

Propagate every intercept and event coordinate using
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:meet}--\eqref{smc:cg:eq:out}. Add the additional incoming-incidence
equalities, equal-time conditions within each batch,
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:batchorder}, initial gap positivity, and all adjacency-interval
endpoint conditions described above. Every resulting expression is a
homogeneous rational linear form in $g$.

For soundness, suppose all these conditions hold. On each positive-duration
adjacency interval, the endpoint lemma gives strict separation in its
interior. Hence no adjacent pair meets at an unlisted intermediate time.
A meeting of nonadjacent signals would force an adjacent meeting: at the
first failure of strict spatial order, at least one consecutive gap is zero.
Thus no collision of any arity can occur between the listed batches.

At a batch boundary, the incidence equalities put the signals of each
specified block at its reconstructed common site. Every adjacent gap not
belonging to that block is strictly positive there. Consequently no additional
incoming signal can join the site. The same strict conditions separate the
sites of distinct blocks. They also exclude an omitted disjoint collision
elsewhere in the configuration: an omitted site would have a zero gap at a
boundary where strict positivity is imposed, or would lie in the interior of
an unchanged adjacency interval, where positivity has just been proved.

The deterministic rule is therefore applicable to exactly the listed
incoming set at each site. Its outgoing order is increasing in velocity,
so outputs separate in the right-hand time limit. Static identity validation
ensures that every segment has a legitimate birth and is consumed at most
once. Induction on batches now proves that the reconstructed execution is
exactly the actual initial prefix.

For completeness, consider an actual execution realizing $\sigma$. The
coordinate propagation agrees with its genuine intersection coordinates.
The extra incidences and batch-time equalities hold, batch times strictly
increase, and each endpoint gap is zero precisely at a common birth or
common death of the pair. Every other endpoint gap is positive. A pair of
straight segments with a common birth and a later common death cannot occur.
Thus the execution satisfies every generated condition.

Finally, a finite intersection of homogeneous equality subspaces and open
linear half-spaces is a relatively open convex cone in that subspace. If
nonempty, its strict inequalities leave a neighborhood in the equality
subspace around each point. All coefficients are rational.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The event-sparse bound}

\begin{theorem}[Linear number of chronology conditions]
\label{smc:cg:thm:rows}
Let $J$ be the number of positive-duration maximal adjacency intervals used
by the construction. Then
\begin{align}
 J&\le (n-1)+\sum_{e=1}^E(q_e+1)
       \le (n-1)+(m+1)E,\label{smc:cg:eq:Jbound}\\
 R_\sigma&\le 3(n-1)+(3m+1)E.\label{smc:cg:eq:Rbound}
\end{align}
These bounds apply before deduplication or removal of identically zero
equalities.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Initially there are $n-1$ adjacent pairs. Replacing a collision block by $q_e$
outputs creates at most $q_e-1$ internal output pairs and two external pairs,
for a total at most $q_e+1$ when $q_e\ge1$. If $q_e=0$, it creates at most one
bridge pair between the surviving neighbors. At a boundary or when neighboring
blocks change simultaneously, fewer pairs may be created. Existing unchanged
pairs need no new interval. This proves \eqref{smc:cg:eq:Jbound}.

There are $d=n-1$ initial gap inequalities, $B$ positive batch-time
increments, $\sum_e(p_e-2)$ additional incoming-incidence equalities,
$E-B$ equalities synchronizing the sites within batches, and $2J$ endpoint
conditions. Therefore
\begin{align*}
 R_\sigma
 &\le d+B+\sum_e(p_e-2)+(E-B)+2J\\
 &=d+\sum_ep_e-E+2J\\
 &\le d+(m-1)E+2d+2(m+1)E\\
 &=3d+(3m+1)E.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\wtag\ The first inequality of \eqref{smc:cg:eq:Jbound} is Part~II's Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:lifetimes}; Part~II's own size ledger \eqref{smc:sd:eq:rawcounts} counts witnesses and residuals in its event-coordinate format.

The word \emph{sparse} here refers to the number of geometric conditions,
not to the number of nonzero coefficients in each eliminated linear form.
A deeply propagated intercept may depend on every initial gap. Storing all
rows densely can take $O(dR_\sigma)$ coefficient slots; compiling them may
have additional bookkeeping cost. Neither \eqref{smc:cg:eq:Rbound} nor degree two
is a constant arithmetic-operation bound for an unbounded computation.

An implementation may scan the live list at each batch for simplicity.
That does not change the mathematical row bound. A linked-list implementation
can update adjacencies locally, while the attached small implementation
prioritizes clarity and exact arithmetic over this engineering optimization.

\section{Quadratic Diophantine certificates with unique slacks}
\label{smc:cg:sec:quadratic}

\subsection{Positive denominator clearing}

Every nonzero rational row can be multiplied by a positive common denominator
and then divided by the positive greatest common divisor of its integer
coefficients. For equality rows the sign may subsequently be normalized;
for strict inequality rows it may not. Negating a strict row would reverse
its meaning.

Delete identically zero equality rows. An identically zero strict row makes
the chamber empty. Remove duplicate rows of the same kind. Denote the
resulting integer matrices by $\mathsf E$ and $\mathsf G$, with $r$ equality
rows and $s$ strict rows. Their matrix presentation is a complete, explicit
description of the polynomial, even when its expanded quadratic has many
monomials.

\begin{theorem}[Unique-slack quadratic representation]
\label{smc:cg:thm:quadratic}
For a nonrejected skeleton, define
\begin{equation}
 P(g,z)=\|\mathsf E g\|_2^2+
        \|\mathsf Gg-\mathbf1-z\|_2^2,
 \qquad (g,z)\in\N^{d+s}.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:P}
\end{equation}
Then $P$ is an integer polynomial of total degree at most two, globally
nonnegative and convex on $\R^{d+s}$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \exists z\in\N^s\ P(g,z)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 g\in\Z^d\cap\Ccal_\sigma.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:bijective}
\end{equation}
For every accepted $g$, the auxiliary tuple is unique:
\begin{equation}
 z=\mathsf Gg-\mathbf1.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:uniqueslack}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each summand is the square of an affine linear form with integer
coefficients. Thus $P$ is a quadratic sum of squares and has Hessian
$2A^{\mathsf T}A\succeq0$, where $A$ is the combined coefficient matrix of
these affine forms. Over the reals, and hence over the integers, a sum of
squares vanishes precisely when every squared form vanishes. Its zeros
therefore require $\mathsf E g=0$ and
$\mathsf G_jg=1+z_j\ge1$ for every strict row. Because these expressions are
integers, this is equivalent to $\mathsf Gg>0$. The chamber theorem proves
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:bijective}. The same equations force \eqref{smc:cg:eq:uniqueslack}, which
is a nonnegative integer tuple whenever $g$ is accepted.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ Theorems~\ref{smc:cg:thm:chamber} and~\ref{smc:cg:thm:quadratic} together are the common theorem of the two Parts (Section~\ref{smc:sec:routes}). Part~II proves it again as Theorem~\ref{smc:sd:thm:main}, with batch times and event positions kept as natural witnesses, adds halting semantics, and strengthens uniqueness to full column rank of the witness matrix (Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:rank}).

All event coordinates are reconstructed from $g$ and are absent from the
auxiliary tuple. This avoids arbitrary rational encodings with multiple
numerator--denominator representatives. If the seed itself is given as a
rational geometry in a different interface, one must separately choose and
validate an encoding or a scale; the uniqueness theorem should not be
silently transferred to a noncanonical rational encoding.

\subsection{What uniqueness does not assert}

The polynomial depends on the externally supplied skeleton. Its number of
rows and slack variables is not fixed independently of the requested
history. Even though each member of this family has unique auxiliary
witnesses, placing an arbitrary unbounded skeleton inside one fixed
Diophantine equation is a different task. An unspecified application of
MRDP to a skeleton verifier does not preserve this uniqueness for free.

The present result is therefore not a proof that all computably enumerable
sets admit single-fold or finite-fold Diophantine representations. Its
stronger-than-usual feature is narrower and fully explicit: for each fixed
collision skeleton, projection of \emph{all} natural zeros of \eqref{smc:cg:eq:P}
onto the initial gaps is a bijection onto the actual integer realizations.
There are no additional zeros corresponding to incomplete local histories.

\section{Causal depth controls denominators and coefficient size}
\label{smc:cg:sec:depth}

\subsection{A denominator invariant}

Work in the integer-speed normalization of Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:model}, and set
\begin{equation}
 V=\max\bigl(1,\max_{a\in M}|v(a)|\bigr),\qquad
 D=\lcm\{|v(a)-v(b)|:v(a)\ne v(b)\}.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:VD}
\end{equation}
The least common multiple of an empty set is taken as $1$; in that case no
collision is possible from a simple initial configuration.

Assign depth zero to initial segments. The depth of an event is one plus
the maximum depth of its incoming segments. Every output segment inherits
the depth of its birth event. This is causal depth, not the chronological
index of the batch.

\begin{theorem}[Causal denominator bound]
\label{smc:cg:thm:denominator}
At an event of depth $h$, each coefficient of its time and position forms
belongs to $D^{-h}\Z$. The same holds for every outgoing intercept. At an
integer seed, the event coordinates therefore have reduced denominators
dividing $D^h$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Initial intercept coefficients are integral. Suppose an event has depth $h$.
Its incoming intercepts have denominators dividing powers of $D$ no larger
than $D^{h-1}$. Put them over the common denominator $D^{h-1}$. Formula
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:meet} divides their difference by a nonzero integer velocity
difference dividing $D$. Its time coefficients thus belong to $D^{-h}\Z$.
The position and all output intercepts use only addition and multiplication
by integer velocities, preserving that lattice. Evaluation at integer gaps
preserves the same denominator divisibility.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ Part~II proves the same divisibility for event coordinates as Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:denom}, with its own proof.

Only primes already present in the speed differences can appear in these
coordinate denominators at an integer seed. At a rational seed, primes in
its initial common denominator may also occur. Parallel collisions elsewhere
do not increase the denominator exponent along an unrelated causal chain.

This is an upper bound, not an assertion that all factors of $D^h$ occur.
Cancellations and special velocity configurations can make the actual
precision requirement much smaller. Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:clock} exhibits an
explicit case in which exponential denominator magnitude is nevertheless
unavoidable.

\subsection{An explicit coefficient bound}

For a rational linear form, use the $\ell^1$ norm of its coefficient vector.
Put $K=2+4V$. Initial intercepts have norm at most $d$.

\begin{lemma}[Growth of propagated forms]
\label{smc:cg:lem:norm}
Every intercept of depth at most $h$ has coefficient norm at most $dK^h$.
Every event time and event position of depth at most $h$ satisfies the same
bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume the input intercept norms at a depth-$h$ event are at most
$M=dK^{h-1}$. A nonzero integer speed difference has absolute value at least
one, so \eqref{smc:cg:eq:meet} gives $\|t_e\|_1\le2M$. Consequently
\[
 \|x_e\|_1\le(1+2V)M,\qquad
 \|b_{\mathrm{out}}\|_1\le(1+4V)M.
\]
Each factor is at most $K$. Induction proves the claim; forms of smaller
depth obey the larger bound as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Integer row height]
\label{smc:cg:prop:height}
If the maximum event depth is $h$, each primitive integer chamber row can
be chosen with every coefficient of absolute value at most
\begin{equation}
 A=2d(1+V)(DK)^h.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:A}
\end{equation}
In particular, for a fixed machine, row coefficient bit lengths are
$O(h+\log d)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An endpoint gap is a difference of two intercepts plus a velocity difference
times a batch time. Its coefficient norm is at most
$2dK^h+2VdK^h=2d(1+V)K^h$. Additional incidence residuals and differences of
event times satisfy the same bound. Initial gap rows also do.
All these coefficients have denominators dividing $D^h$ by
Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:denominator}. Multiplication by $D^h$ bounds every resulting
integer coefficient by \eqref{smc:cg:eq:A}. Passing to a primitive integer row can
only decrease this bound.
\end{proof}

The exponent in \eqref{smc:cg:eq:A} bounds coefficient \emph{magnitude}; its
logarithm is linear in depth. Exact rational arithmetic can therefore remain
polynomial in the externally bounded history length even when ordinary
fixed-precision floating-point simulation becomes unreliable. This is not
an assertion that the total number of events before acceptance has a
computable bound in the initial input length.

\section{Small integral realizations and finite-horizon synthesis}
\label{smc:cg:sec:small}

\begin{theorem}[Small positive integer seed]
\label{smc:cg:thm:small}
Let $\Ccal=\{g\in\R^d:\mathsf E g=0,\mathsf Gg>0\}$ be a nonempty chamber
whose strict rows include $g_i>0$. Suppose all matrix coefficients are
integers of absolute value at most $A\ge1$. Then $\Ccal$ contains a positive
integer point satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \max_i g_i\le d^{d/2}A^d.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:small}
\end{equation}
Thus every realizable collision skeleton has a realization with a seed
encoding of polynomial bit length in the explicit skeleton and initial
label word, for a fixed machine.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Homogeneity permits a positive scaling of any point in $\Ccal$ so that every
strict expression is at least $1$. Hence the closed rational polyhedron
\[
 Q=\{g:\mathsf E g=0,\mathsf Gg\ge\mathbf1\}
\]
is nonempty. It lies in $[1,\infty)^d$. Minimizing $\sum_i g_i$ over $Q$
attains an optimum: intersecting with a nonempty objective sublevel gives
a compact set. The optimal face is a nonempty compact polytope and has a
vertex; that point is also a vertex of $Q$.

At such a vertex choose $d$ independent active constraints, including
sufficient equality rows. They give a nonsingular integer matrix $B$ and a
right-hand side $c$ with entries in $\{0,1\}$. The vertex is $B^{-1}c$.
Let $\delta=|\det B|$, a positive integer. Cramer's rule shows that
$g=\delta B^{-1}c$ is integral. It is positive, satisfies the homogeneous
equalities, and has every strict row at least $\delta\ge1$.

Each coordinate of $g$ is, up to sign, a determinant in which one column of
$B$ has been replaced by $c$. Hadamard's inequality bounds its magnitude by
$d^{d/2}A^d$; using the sharper bound on the replaced column is unnecessary.
This proves \eqref{smc:cg:eq:small}. Combining it with \eqref{smc:cg:eq:A} gives the stated
bit-length conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Real, rational, and integer realizability coincide]
A fixed skeleton is realizable by a positive real seed if and only if it is
realizable by a positive rational seed, if and only if it is realizable by a
positive integer seed. Realizability is decidable by rational linear
feasibility, using $\mathsf E g=0,\mathsf Gg\ge\mathbf1$.
\end{corollary}

The equivalence is specific to homogeneous rational chambers. It should not
be transferred to arbitrary nonlinear geometry. Nor does the scaled integer
seed necessarily satisfy extra nonhomogeneous requirements such as an
absolute prescribed distance or a fixed deadline.

\begin{corollary}[An NP upper bound for bounded synthesis]
\label{smc:cg:cor:NP}
Fix a rational signal machine and a distinguished output label. Given an
initial label word of length $n$ and a unary bound $T$, the problem of whether
some positive seed produces the distinguished label within a complete prefix
containing at most $T$ collision sites is in NP.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A skeleton of at most $T$ sites has at most $T$ batches and at most $n+mT$
created signal identities. Since $m$ is fixed, its discrete description has
polynomial length in $n+T$. Guess such a skeleton, check its identities and
rules, and check that the distinguished label appears in its prefix.

If it is realizable, Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:small} supplies a positive integer seed
with polynomial bit length: $d=n-1$, $h\le T$, and
$\log A=O(T+\log n)$ for the fixed machine. Guess that seed as well.
Propagating rational forms and verifying every chamber row then takes a
polynomial number of operations on polynomial-length integers. This is a
polynomial-time verifier. No search over exponentially long real numbers is
required.
\end{proof}

Only an upper bound is claimed. There is no NP-hardness result here. The
budget counts total sites, including all sites in the accepting batch, not
elapsed physical time. A physical-time bound can contain arbitrarily many
events and even an accumulation, so it is not interchangeable with $T$.

\section{Robustness and the rank of synchronization constraints}
\label{smc:cg:sec:robust}

The chamber description distinguishes two different sources of numerical
difficulty. A trace can be sensitive because it demands an exact equality
among initial gaps, or its exact coordinates can require large denominators
even while its combinatorial pattern is robust. The latter phenomenon will
occur in the accumulating clock.

\begin{theorem}[Certified perturbation radius and codimension]
\label{smc:cg:thm:robust}
Let $\Ccal=\{g:\mathsf E g=0,\mathsf Gg>0\}$ be nonempty, with zero strict
rows removed, and let $g^*\in\Ccal$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \rho(g^*)=\min_j\frac{\mathsf G_jg^*}{\|\mathsf G_j\|_1}>0.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Every perturbation $\eta\in\ker\mathsf E$ with
$\|\eta\|_\infty<\rho(g^*)$ preserves the skeleton. The chamber has relative
dimension $d-\rank\mathsf E$. It has nonempty ambient interior if and only
if $\rank\mathsf E=0$; otherwise it has ambient Lebesgue measure zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The equality constraints persist for $\eta\in\ker\mathsf E$. For every
strict row, H\"older's elementary norm inequality gives
\[
 \mathsf G_j(g^*+\eta)
 \ge \mathsf G_jg^*-\|\mathsf G_j\|_1\|\eta\|_\infty>0.
\]
Hence the perturbed point stays in the chamber. This proves relative
openness in the full equality kernel, whose dimension is
$d-\rank\mathsf E$. A proper linear subspace has empty interior and measure
zero in $\R^d$. Conversely, when the rank is zero, the finitely many strict
inequalities define an open neighborhood of each accepted point.
\end{proof}

The radius in \eqref{smc:cg:eq:rho} is a certified lower bound, not necessarily the
largest possible radius in the equality subspace. Restricting perturbations
to that subspace can increase the true distance to a forbidden boundary.

One must also distinguish genuinely independent synchronization equations
from identities already forced by previous dynamics. Some formal
coincidences yield an identically zero row and impose no additional
codimension. Therefore the statement ``every multiple collision is fragile''
is too strong. What matters is the rank of the \emph{nontrivial} conditions
on the input geometry. When a loader or problem specification already
restricts the admissible inputs to a subspace, robustness should be measured
relative to that allowed input space.

The theorem provides a useful diagnostic for a proposed geometric computer.
A simulator can report not merely a diagram, but also an exact equality
matrix, a synchronization rank, and a rational certified tolerance at an
integer seed. Those objects separate an intended robust mechanism from an
accidental coincidence created by a particular numeric sample.

\section{Finite-horizon acceptance, semilinearity, and counting}
\label{smc:cg:sec:counting}

\subsection{Disjoint first-acceptance chambers}

Fix a machine, an initial label word, a marked accepting label, and a site
budget $T$. Acceptance means the first appearance of that label in a batch
such that the total number of processed sites through that entire batch is
at most $T$. If the initial word already contains the marked label, every
seed accepts with zero events.

There are only finitely many candidate skeletons with at most $T$ sites.
Indeed, at every stage there are at most $n+mT$ identities; the rule table
and possible contiguous input blocks are finite. Enumerate those skeletons
that end at the first accepting batch and reject all statically invalid
ones. Distinct nonempty chambers in this first-acceptance list are disjoint:
for a fixed seed, deterministic complete chronology has just one first
accepting prefix.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon semilinearity]
\label{smc:cg:thm:semilinear}
The positive integer seed geometries accepted within site budget $T$ form
an effectively constructible semilinear set. More specifically, they are a
finite disjoint union of the integer points of rational homogeneous
chambers of the form \eqref{smc:cg:eq:mainchamber}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The enumeration above is finite and effective. For each skeleton,
Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:chamber} gives a complete linear characterization. On integer
points, a strict integer inequality is equivalent to being at least $1$,
so each chamber is definable in Presburger arithmetic. Finite union remains
Presburger-definable. The effective equivalence with semilinear sets is the
classical theorem of Ginsburg and Spanier~\cite{ginsburgspanier}.
\end{proof}

This use of the classical equivalence is not a new proof of that theorem.
The new substrate-specific input to it is the exact finite union of
collision chambers, including chronology and simultaneous-event boundaries.


\wtag\ Part~II proves the same statement for its fixed schemas and event bounds, prefix and halting, as Proposition~\ref{smc:sd:prop:semilinear}.

\subsection{A single quartic for an unknown bounded first-hit skeleton}

The skeleton need not remain externally supplied when the event budget is
fixed. Enumerate the nonrejected first-acceptance skeletons
$\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_K$ and let their matrices be
$\mathsf E_k,\mathsf G_k$. Empty chambers may remain in this list without
harm. Introduce natural selectors $b_1,\ldots,b_K$ and a separate slack
vector $z_k$ for each chamber. Define
\begin{align}
 Q_T(g,b,z)
  ={}&\left(\sum_{k=1}^K b_k-1\right)^2
  +\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_i\bigl(b_k\mathsf E_{k,i}g\bigr)^2\notag\\
  &+\sum_{k=1}^K\sum_j
       \bigl(b_k(\mathsf G_{k,j}g-1)-z_{k,j}\bigr)^2.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:quarticunion}
\end{align}
For an empty family, use $Q_T=1$.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical first-hit quartic]
\label{smc:cg:thm:quarticunion}
Equation \eqref{smc:cg:eq:quarticunion} is a globally nonnegative integer polynomial
of degree at most four. Its natural zeros project bijectively onto the
integer seeds accepted within the fixed event budget. No Boolean equations
for the selectors and no additional inactive-slack constraints are needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a natural zero, $\sum_kb_k=1$ forces exactly one selector to be $1$ and
all others to be $0$. For the selected chamber, the remaining equations are
precisely $\mathsf E_kg=0$ and
$\mathsf G_kg-\mathbf1=z_k\ge0$. For an unselected chamber, each slack
residual becomes $-z_{k,j}$, forcing all of its slacks to zero. Thus the
polynomial accepts exactly the union of the chamber integer points.

Those chambers are disjoint by canonical first-acceptance chronology.
Therefore an accepted seed determines exactly one selected skeleton, its
active slacks are uniquely determined, and every inactive slack is zero.
Every residual has degree at most two before squaring, proving the quartic
bound. The construction is a sum of squares and hence globally nonnegative,
but it is not in general convex.
\end{proof}

The same construction works for any finite disjoint family of chambers. If
chambers overlap, it still represents their union, but a seed in several
chambers has several selector witnesses. First-hit canonicalization is
therefore mathematically important, not cosmetic.

\begin{remark}[\wtag\ Relation to a single-fold quadratic of another report]\label{smc:cg:rem:singlefold}
The accepted set of Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:quarticunion} is a finite union of chamber integer points, hence semilinear (Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:semilinear}). The collection's report \emph{canonical-diophantine-certificates}~\cite{cdc} proves in its Part~XI (Theorem \texttt{cdc:of:thm:singlefoldsl}) that every semilinear set has a representation by an integer polynomial of degree at most two that is nonnegative on the real nonnegative orthant, with exactly one natural witness for each member, effectively from a Presburger formula. So, if only orthant nonnegativity is required, the first-hit set has a single-fold quadratic certificate. Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:quarticunion} gives something different: an explicit construction from the chamber matrices, globally nonnegative on all real inputs, whose selector and slacks have the chamber meaning. Within the globally nonnegative class a quadratic has a rational affine zero set (Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:rigidity}), and the selector products of \eqref{smc:cg:eq:quarticunion} leave that class, as the paragraph after Corollary~\ref{smc:cg:cor:barrier} says. Whether a globally nonnegative quadratic suffices for a given first-hit family is not decided here.
\end{remark}

This theorem removes external skeleton choice for a \emph{bounded} problem;
it does not remove the bound. The number of candidate skeletons, and hence
the number of selectors and slacks, may be very large and grows with $T$.
The attached implementation provides the matrix-based quartic constructor,
and checks a two-chamber first-hit example. It does not include an optimized
exhaustive enumerator for all skeletons of an arbitrary event budget.

\subsection{Exact quasi-polynomials rather than merely leading asymptotics}

Let $A_T(H)$ count the accepted seeds in $\{1,\ldots,H\}^d$, with $H\ge1$.
For a single chamber put
\[
 \Pi_\sigma=\Ccal_\sigma\cap(0,1]^d.
\]
Its closure is a bounded rational polytope in its equality subspace.
The original set is obtained from that closed polytope by excluding finitely
many rational faces where one or more required strict expressions vanish.
The upper box facets remain included.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-horizon counting]
\label{smc:cg:thm:ehrhart}
For fixed machine, initial word, accepting label, and event budget $T$,
$A_T(H)$ is an exact quasi-polynomial in $H$ for all positive integers $H$.
It has degree at most $d$. Its ordinary generating function is rational and
has poles only at roots of unity. Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{H\to\infty}\frac{A_T(H)}{H^d}
 =\sum_{\sigma}\vol_d(\Pi_\sigma)\in\Q,
 \label{smc:cg:eq:density}
\end{equation}
where the sum ranges over the first-acceptance chambers, and
lower-dimensional terms have zero $d$-dimensional volume.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Homogeneity gives
$\Ccal_\sigma\cap(0,H]^d=H\Pi_\sigma$.
For a nonempty chamber, the closure of $\Pi_\sigma$ is obtained by replacing
its strict homogeneous inequalities by weak ones. To see equality with this
weak closure, take a small strictly feasible point with every coordinate
less than $1$ and form convex combinations with any weakly feasible point.
Those combinations converge to the latter while preserving all strict
conditions and the box.

A forbidden equality $\mathsf G_jg=0$ cuts out a face of the weak closure,
since $\mathsf G_jg\ge0$ throughout it. Inclusion--exclusion over these faces
expresses the lattice-point count in $H\Pi_\sigma$ as a finite integer
combination of counts in dilates of closed rational polytopes. Ehrhart's
rational-polytope theorem makes each term a quasi-polynomial; see
Beck and Robins~\cite{beckrobins}. This also covers lower-dimensional faces.
Disjointness of first-acceptance chambers permits summing these exact counts.

A quasi-polynomial of degree at most $d$ and period $p$ has a rational
generating function whose denominator divides a power of $1-z^p$.
This proves the generating-function assertion. The normalized leading
coefficient of a full-dimensional rational polytope count is its volume;
removing proper faces does not change it. Equivalently, the same limit follows
from Riemann sums for a bounded polyhedral set. Rational polytopes have
rational volume by a rational triangulation and determinant formulas.
Summing over the disjoint chambers proves \eqref{smc:cg:eq:density}.
\end{proof}

Thus a finite-horizon seed-counting problem has a particularly rigid
asymptotic form: a polynomial in $H$ with periodic coefficient functions,
not an arbitrary irregular error term. Its leading density is rational.
If the initial gaps are instead sampled independently and uniformly from
$(0,1]$, the probability of finite-horizon acceptance is exactly the same
rational volume sum.

The theorem does not promise an efficient procedure for enumerating all
chambers or computing all their volumes. Their number can grow rapidly with
$T$. It also does not justify interchanging an unbounded-time limit with the
box-size limit. Removing the event bound can leave an infinite union of
chambers, outside the finite quasi-polynomial argument.

Because each integer seed in one chamber has one slack tuple, its chamber
count is also the number of complete natural zeros of its quadratic
certificate with $1\le g_i\le H$, even when no separate bound is placed on
$z$. Across different skeletons this statement uses a tagged disjoint family
of certificates, not a single unqualified polynomial.

\section{An exact first-collision tournament}
\label{smc:cg:sec:tournament}

\subsection{Three chambers and a complete polynomial}

Consider labels $A,B,C$ with respective speeds $2,0,-1$, initially at
$0,u,u+v$, where $u,v>0$. Introduce two stationary output labels $H,R$ and
rules
\[
 \{A,B\}\longrightarrow\{H\},\qquad
 \{B,C\}\longrightarrow\{R\},\qquad
 \{A,B,C\}\longrightarrow\{R\}.
\]
Use transparent rules otherwise. The question here is whether $H$ is
produced at the \emph{first} collision batch.

The possible first events are exactly
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{event}&\text{condition}&\text{time}\\\hline
 AB&u<2v&u/2\\
 BC&u>2v&v\\
 ABC&u=2v&v=u/2.
 \end{array}
 \label{smc:cg:eq:tournament}
\end{equation}
For instance, the formal $AC$ meeting time is $(u+v)/3$. If $u<2v$, it is
later than $u/2$; if $u>2v$, it is later than $v$. It cannot be a different
first event. The equality case is the triple collision.

The complete accepting chamber has the particularly small certificate
\begin{equation}
 P_{AB}(u,v,z_1,z_2,z_3)=
 (u-1-z_1)^2+(v-1-z_2)^2+(2v-u-1-z_3)^2.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:tournamentpoly}
\end{equation}
All five variables range over $\N$. At $u=v=1$, for example, the unique
auxiliary tuple is $(0,0,0)$. At $(u,v)=(3,1)$, the third strict condition
fails, correctly rejecting the incidence-only counterfeit. At $(2,1)$ it
also fails, correctly rejecting an omitted simultaneous third input.

For the triple-collision skeleton the compiler instead produces
\begin{equation}
 P_{ABC}(u,v,z_1,z_2)=
 (u-2v)^2+(u-1-z_1)^2+(v-1-z_2)^2.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:triplepoly}
\end{equation}
Its equality rank is one. The two binary-event chambers have rank zero and
are open in the positive quadrant; the triple-collision chamber is
one-dimensional and requires exact synchronization.

\subsection{Counting, generating function, and inversion}

Let $C(H)$ count the accepting gap pairs with $1\le u,v\le H$, and set
$C(0)=0$. Direct summation gives
\begin{align}
 C(H)&=\sum_{v=1}^H\min(H,2v-1)\notag\\
 &=\begin{cases}
 3m^2,&H=2m,\\
 3m^2+3m+1,&H=2m+1,
 \end{cases}\notag\\
 &=\floor{\frac{3H^2+1}{4}}
  =\frac{3H^2}{4}+\frac{1-(-1)^H}{8}.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:count}
\end{align}
For $H=2m$, split the sum after $v=m$; the odd-number sum contributes $m^2$
and the remaining $m$ terms contribute $2m^2$. The odd case follows by the
same split after $m+1$.

The first values are
\[
 0,1,3,7,12,19,27,37,48,61,75,91,108,127,147,169,\ldots.
\]
No assertion about an OEIS identification or novelty of this elementary
sequence is needed. It is included as an exactly solvable counting test for
the compiler and as an illustration of the general quasi-polynomial theorem.
Its ordinary generating function is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{H\ge0}C(H)z^H
 =\frac{z(1+z+z^2)}{(1-z)^3(1+z)}.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:gf}
\end{equation}
This follows from the standard geometric-series derivatives applied to
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:count}, or by expanding the displayed rational function.

There is also an exact inverse threshold. For every positive integer $y$,
\begin{equation}
 \min\{H\ge0:C(H)\ge y\}
 =\ceil{\sqrt{\frac{4y-1}{3}}}.
 \label{smc:cg:eq:inverse}
\end{equation}
Indeed, because $y$ is integral,
$\floor{(3H^2+1)/4}\ge y$ is equivalent to $3H^2+1\ge4y$.
The smooth expression inside the ceiling has the expansion
\begin{align}
 \sqrt{\frac{4y-1}{3}}
 &=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}y^{1/2}
 -\frac{1}{4\sqrt3}y^{-1/2}
 -\frac{1}{64\sqrt3}y^{-3/2}
 -\frac{1}{512\sqrt3}y^{-5/2}
 +O(y^{-7/2}).
 \label{smc:cg:eq:inverseasymptotic}
\end{align}
The binomial series supplies all further orders. The ceiling in
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:inverse} is essential: omitting it would incorrectly replace an
integer inverse's bounded rounding oscillation by a decaying error.

\subsection{Independent simultaneous sites}\label{smc:cg:sec:simultaneous}

Take the transparent two-speed machine with $v(P)=1$, $v(N)=-1$, and initial
word $P,N,P,N$. With gaps $(2,5,2)$, the two disjoint pairs $(0,1)$ and
$(2,3)$ collide simultaneously at time $1$. A correct first batch must
contain both sites. A proposed batch containing only $(0,1)$ is rejected by
the unchanged adjacency interval of $(2,3)$, whose gap would vanish at the
final boundary where positivity is required.

For general positive gaps $(g_1,g_2,g_3)$, this simultaneous first-batch
skeleton has the single nontrivial equality $g_1=g_3$. Changing the last gap
from $2$ to $3$ destroys that equality. This example tests a different
failure mode from the omitted third input of a multiple collision: the
missing event is spatially separate, not an extra signal at the same site.

\section{A four-speed clock with exponentially growing denominators}

\subsection{Rules and a closed-form execution}

Consider the four labels
\[
 v(L)=0,\qquad v(P)=2,\qquad v(R)=-1,\qquad v(N)=-2
\]
with rules
\begin{equation}
 \{P,R\}\longrightarrow\{N,R\},\qquad
 \{L,N\}\longrightarrow\{L,P\},
 \label{smc:cg:eq:clockrules}
\end{equation}
and transparent defaults. Initially put $L$ at $0$, $P$ at $u$, and $R$ at
$u+v$, with $u,v>0$. The left boundary is stationary, the right boundary
moves left at speed $1$, and a particle alternately travels right at speed
$2$ and left at speed $2$, reflecting at the boundaries via the two rules.

Write $S=u+v$ and $W=3u+2v$. The collision times are
\begin{align}
 t_{2k+1}&=S-\frac{W}{3^{k+1}},&&k\ge0,\label{smc:cg:eq:clockodd}\\
 t_{2k}&=S-\frac{W}{2\cdot3^k},&&k\ge1.\label{smc:cg:eq:clockeven}
\end{align}
Odd collisions occur on the right boundary at position $S-t_{2k+1}$;
even collisions occur at position zero.

\begin{proposition}[Clock correctness and common chamber]
\label{smc:cg:prop:clock}
For every $u,v>0$, equations \eqref{smc:cg:eq:clockodd}--\eqref{smc:cg:eq:clockeven}
describe the actual alternating execution. The collision times strictly
increase to $S$. Every finite alternating prefix has the whole positive
$(u,v)$ quadrant as its seed chamber.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first rightward meeting occurs at $t_1=v/3$, which equals
$S-W/3$. At a right-boundary collision at time $t$, the remaining boundary
distance is $S-t$. The new left-moving particle takes $(S-t)/2$ to reach
zero, leaving time-to-boundary-meeting $(S-t)/2$. From there the right-moving
particle closes the remaining gap at relative speed $3$, leaving two thirds
of that remaining time when it next hits the right boundary. Hence a full
right-to-right bounce multiplies $S-t$ by $1/3$, with the intermediate
left collision having half the previous remaining time. This proves the
formulas inductively.

The first time is positive. Every subsequent difference is positive, and
all times are less than $S$. Between collisions the moving particle lies
strictly between the two boundaries. The boundaries themselves meet only at
$S$, not at any finite indexed event. There is therefore no unlisted
collision in any finite prefix. The labels alternate exactly as specified
by \eqref{smc:cg:eq:clockrules}, independently of the positive gaps. This proves
both correctness and the common-chamber statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A denominator lower bound without fragile synchronization}

For the particular integer seed $(u,v)=(1,2)$, $S=3$ and $W=7$, giving
\[
 \frac23,\frac{11}6,\frac{20}9,\frac{47}{18},\frac{74}{27},
 \frac{155}{54},\frac{236}{81},\frac{479}{162},\ldots.
\]
At event $2k$, the reduced denominator is exactly $2\cdot3^k$:
\[
 t_{2k}=\frac{6\cdot3^k-7}{2\cdot3^k},
\]
and the numerator is odd and is not divisible by $3$. The causal depth of
event $j$ is $j$, since the reflected particle links every event to its
predecessor. Thus denominator magnitude is exponential in causal depth,
while its bit length is linear in depth.

Here the integer speed differences have least common multiple $D=12$,
so Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:denominator} correctly gives a divisible upper bound
$12^j$. The example does not make that upper bound sharp in its base, but it
rules out a depth-independent denominator bound or a polynomial-magnitude
bound for general rational signal machines.

At the same time, every finite prefix has equality rank zero and remains
valid throughout the positive quadrant. Growing exact precision is therefore
not evidence of a fragile collision pattern. Robust symbolic chronology and
large rational denominators are compatible.

Accumulations in signal machines are established phenomena. Becker et
al.~\cite{accumulations} distinguish the behavior of small speed sets and
show, in particular, that four speeds permit accumulation whereas the
three-speed case with rational speed and initial-distance ratios does not.
The clock is an explicit test case and a denominator-growth illustration,
not a claim to have discovered accumulation. Our compiler verifies every
ordinary finite prefix before $S$; it assigns no transition or computational
power to the limiting point at $S$.

\section{Why a fixed universal convex quadratic cannot follow}
\label{smc:cg:sec:barrier}

A natural question is whether the quadratic frontend can be compressed into
one fixed polynomial with a fixed number of witnesses for arbitrary
unbounded computations. Within the globally nonnegative quadratic class,
there is an elementary obstruction stronger than a failure of the present
construction.

\begin{theorem}[Linear zero-set rigidity]
\label{smc:cg:thm:rigidity}
Let $Q(w)$ be a quadratic polynomial with rational coefficients that is
nonnegative for every $w\in\R^N$. Its real zero set is either empty or a
rational affine subspace. The projection of its nonnegative integer zeros
onto any subset of coordinates is an effectively semilinear, decidable set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 Q(w)=w^{\mathsf T}Aw+2b^{\mathsf T}w+c
\]
with $A$ rational and symmetric. Global nonnegativity implies $A\succeq0$.
It also implies $b\perp\ker A$: otherwise the restriction to an appropriate
kernel direction would be a nonconstant affine function unbounded below.
Thus $b\in\operatorname{range} A$ and the consistent rational system
$Aw_0=-b$ has a rational solution.

Completing the square gives
\[
 Q(w)=(w-w_0)^{\mathsf T}A(w-w_0)+\mu,
 \qquad \mu=c+b^{\mathsf T}w_0\ge0.
\]
If $\mu>0$, there are no zeros. If $\mu=0$, positive semidefiniteness gives
$Q(w)=0$ exactly when $A(w-w_0)=0$, equivalently $Aw=-b$. This is a rational
affine subspace.

Clearing denominators gives finitely many integer linear equations.
Intersecting with $\N^N$ and projecting coordinates is an existential
Presburger definition. Effective semilinearity and decidability follow from
Ginsburg--Spanier and Presburger arithmetic~\cite{ginsburgspanier}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Convex-quadratic compression barrier]
\label{smc:cg:cor:barrier}
An undecidable halting relation cannot be represented by one fixed globally
nonnegative rational quadratic with a fixed finite number of natural
parameters and witnesses, even after a computable input-loading map.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The projected zero relation would be decidable by
Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:rigidity}. Its preimage under a computable input-loading map
would then be decidable as well, contradicting the assumed undecidability.
\end{proof}

This theorem applies to global nonnegativity on \emph{all real variables},
not merely nonnegativity on a special natural-number input slice. It does
not classify arbitrary quadratic equations, arbitrary nonconvex
polynomials, or cubic Diophantine representations. For example,
$x-y^2=0$ is a quadratic equation with a nonlinear zero set, but its
polynomial is not globally nonnegative and is outside the theorem.

The quartic first-hit construction in \eqref{smc:cg:eq:quarticunion} leaves the
convex-quadratic class by multiplying selectors and input-dependent linear
forms. Its finite-horizon relation remains decidable for other reasons.
A truly unbounded compiler must additionally encode an unbounded amount of
discrete chronology into finitely many integer variables. That compression,
not the local collision geometry, is where the universal Diophantine
problem re-enters.

\begin{remark}[\wtag\ Credit]\label{smc:cg:rem:credit}
Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:rigidity} and Corollary~\ref{smc:cg:cor:barrier} are a special case of results already in Part~XI of the collection's report \emph{canonical-diophantine-certificates}~\cite{cdc}, which source~07 does not cite. There, Lemma \texttt{cdc:of:lem:affinezero} shows that a real polynomial $p$ of degree at most three that is nonnegative on an open convex set $O$ and vanishes at $a\in O$ vanishes on $O$ exactly along $a+\ker\nabla^2p(a)$; with $O=\R^N$ this is the affine zero set of Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:rigidity}. Theorem \texttt{cdc:of:thm:cubicsemilinear} shows that the full natural zero set of a \emph{rational} polynomial of degree at most three that is nonnegative only on the real nonnegative orthant is a finite union of natural solution sets of rational affine systems, and Theorem \texttt{cdc:of:thm:classification} that the coordinate projections of such zero sets are exactly the semilinear sets, $\mathrm D^+_2(k)=\mathrm D^+_3(k)=\mathrm{SL}(k)$, effectively. Those results assume less (nonnegativity on the orthant only, degree up to three, no convexity or sum-of-squares hypothesis) and give the same decidable, semilinear conclusion; that report itself notes that its theorem is stronger than the observation that a globally nonnegative polynomial cannot have odd highest degree. Corollary~\ref{smc:cg:cor:barrier} therefore also holds for orthant-nonnegative polynomials of degree at most three. No novelty is claimed here for either statement. Source~07's proof by completing the square is kept as a direct second route for the globally nonnegative quadratic case, and its clause ``even after a computable input-loading map'' is its own: a computable preimage of a decidable set is decidable.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The classical unbounded existence result remains available}

For a finite rational initial configuration, an exact event-driven simulator
can find the next collision batch by computing all positive pairwise meeting
times and taking the smallest. It can then apply all rules at the sites of
that batch. Repeating this procedure semidecides the appearance of a marked
signal in an ordinary finite prefix. An infinite accumulating run simply
causes the simulator to execute indefinitely when no earlier acceptance has
occurred; no post-accumulation convention is needed for semidecidability.

Consequently the pre-accumulation finite-prefix acceptance relation, with
machine and rational input encoded by natural numbers, is computably
enumerable. MRDP supplies a fixed-arity Diophantine representation of that
encoded relation~\cite{matiyasevich,afp}. This last existence step is
classical. Neither MRDP nor the present frontend automatically supplies
unique witnesses, an explicit small expanded universal polynomial, or the
repository's particular operation ledger.

For a fixed universal machine with an undecidable marked-halting interface,
there can be no total computable function of the encoded seed that bounds
the number of sites needed by every accepting run. Such a function, followed
by exact bounded simulation, would decide halting. This explains why the
bounded chamber theorems and the universal existence theorem coexist without
contradiction.

\section{Implementation, reproducible checks, and trust boundaries}
\label{smc:cg:sec:implementation}

\subsection{What the attached code computes}

The accompanying standard-library Python implementation uses exact
\texttt{Fraction} arithmetic for every geometric calculation. The essential
interfaces are:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
Machine(speeds, rules, transparent_default=True)
simulate(machine, initial_labels, gaps, max_batches=20)
compile_skeleton(machine, initial_labels, skeleton)
UnionCertificate(tuple_of_chamber_certificates)
\end{lstlisting}
A skeleton is a tuple of batches; each batch is a tuple of input-ID tuples.
For example, \texttt{(((0, 1), (2, 3)),)} denotes one batch with two
independent simultaneous sites. Fresh identities are generated canonically
by increasing outgoing velocity.

The simulator uses an all-pairs minimum-time calculation. The symbolic
compiler uses intercept propagation and adjacency intervals. Their different
chronology mechanisms provide a useful cross-check: the regression suite
compares exact numerical prefixes against the regions predicted by the
symbolic compiler on many other initial seeds.

A compiled \texttt{Certificate} contains the complete integer matrices,
exact rational event forms, causal depths, interval count, and raw row
ledger. Its \texttt{witnesses(gaps)} method returns the unique natural slack
tuple, while \texttt{evaluate(gaps, witnesses)} evaluates the full polynomial.
The \texttt{UnionCertificate} implements \eqref{smc:cg:eq:quarticunion}. Its
constructor does not prove that a supplied family is globally disjoint;
that is a mathematical precondition for uniqueness. At a supplied seed,
its witness routine rejects multiple accepting chambers rather than
silently claiming a unique answer.

The machine constructor copies and freezes the speed and rule tables.
This prevents a caller's later dictionary mutation from changing the
meaning of an already constructed machine. Input validation rejects unknown
labels, repeated-speed outputs, malformed groups, use of dead signals,
noncontiguous blocks, overlapping sites, and inappropriate incoming speed
orders. These checks are part of the semantic contract.

The exported JSON matrices specify full polynomials, not just sample zeros.
For example, \texttt{tournament\_left.json} contains all rows of
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:tournamentpoly}, while
\texttt{first\_hit\_quartic\_union.json} supplies every matrix and the exact
selector formula for a two-chamber union. Expanding these expressions into
monomials is optional and provides no extra mathematical information.

\section{Further research questions and a concrete development path}
\label{smc:cg:sec:future}

\wtag\ Table~\ref{smc:tab:questions} pairs these directions with the questions of Part~II. The first two coincide with Part~II's Questions~\ref{smc:sd:q:formal} and~\ref{smc:sd:q:universal}.

\subsection{Formalizing the chronology theorem}\label{smc:cg:q:formal}

The most direct formalization target is the endpoint lemma followed by the
sweep invariant: the live list is in strict spatial order on every open
interval between batches, and its equal-position blocks at each boundary
are exactly the declared sites. Stable IDs make consumption and birth
finite-map properties. A Lean development should keep the real semantic
statement separate from rational coefficient computation, then prove that
evaluation commutes with the intercept compiler. Finally, the sum-of-squares
zero lemma and natural slack uniqueness can be linked to the repository's
Diophantine infrastructure.

A practical first milestone is a small checker for exported matrices and
rational event forms. A second is a proof-producing compiler rather than a
trusted Python translator. Tests that compare two unverified programs should
remain auxiliary evidence, not be promoted to kernel verification.

\subsection{An audited universal signal-machine frontend}\label{smc:cg:q:universal}

Instantiate a published finite-input universal construction with its exact
labels, velocities, all required collision rules, transparent defaults, input
encoding, and marked acceptance convention. The 13-type construction of
Durand-Lose~\cite{durandlose} is a concrete candidate. The task is not merely
to copy a picture: simultaneous three-input cases, finite workspace supply,
and the loader's correspondence with the simulated machine must be audited.
The eventual theorem should state an exact equivalence between an ordinary
integer program input and the existence of a compiled accepting prefix.

This would turn the present generic geometry theorem into a fully explicit
end-to-end substrate result. A fixed-arity universal polynomial would still
require a separate outer compression step and its own complete resource
ledger.

\subsection{Sharper denominator invariants}\label{smc:cg:q:denominators}

The factor $D^h$ treats all speed differences uniformly. Can one use a
velocity-labelled dependency graph to compute exact prime-valuation bounds
or a smaller denominator ideal for each event? Which speed sets force
uniformly bounded denominators on all integral seeds, and which permit
exponential growth? The clock supplies one lower-bound mechanism, while the
small-speed accumulation results~\cite{accumulations} suggest that the
interaction between arithmetic precision and global dynamics is subtler
than a least-common-multiple bound alone.

A particularly concrete project is to propagate prime-valuation vectors
through \eqref{smc:cg:eq:meet} and detect systematic cancellation at each rule.
Any claimed lower bound must use reduced denominators, not merely the
unreduced fractions produced by a particular implementation.

\subsection{From few rows to small arithmetic circuits}\label{smc:cg:q:circuits}

The event-sparse theorem controls row count but permits dense coefficient
vectors. Can common subexpressions in intercept forms give a substantially
smaller arithmetic circuit than dense matrix evaluation? An appropriate
cost model must distinguish fixed machine numerals, input-dependent
arithmetic, equality tests, and the conversion from a residual system to one
polynomial. The original intercept dependency graph already provides a
sharing structure; the challenge is to preserve its certified semantic
meaning while optimizing its arithmetic schedule.

A useful result would be a proved tradeoff among witness count, maximum
degree, coefficient height, and literal addition/multiplication count.
No one-dimensional comparison such as ``quadratic beats a degree-169
polynomial'' is meaningful when arity and unbounded-history coding differ.

\subsection{Avoiding exhaustive first-hit enumeration}\label{smc:cg:q:firsthit}

The quartic in \eqref{smc:cg:eq:quarticunion} is explicit but can contain an enormous
number of chambers. Can a local selector circuit encode a bounded unknown
skeleton with polynomially many variables while retaining canonical first-hit
uniqueness, including all simultaneous events? A promising direction is to
encode changes of adjacency directly rather than a rectangular array of all
particle positions. The main obstruction is that a locally plausible
selection must still describe one globally ordered and complete execution.

Even a polynomial-size bounded compiler would not solve fixed-arity
unbounded compression. Keeping those two improvements distinct will make
future operation claims interpretable.

\subsection{Robust universality and minimal synchronization rank}\label{smc:cg:q:robust}

For a given computational simulation, determine the rank of its required
input-geometry equalities. Can the same computation be realized on a
full-dimensional chamber, or does its interface genuinely require exact
ratios among certain gaps? The answer must be relative to the legal input
space rather than to coordinates the loader never permits to vary.

One possible invariant is the minimum independent synchronization rank
among equivalent signal-machine implementations with a fixed speed set.
The chamber theorem makes that invariant concrete for each finite trace,
but extending it uniformly over a universal family needs a precise notion
of simulation and allowed perturbation.

\subsection{Counting complexity and asymptotic structure}\label{smc:cg:q:counting}

Determine when first-hit chamber counts can be computed without enumerating
all skeletons. Are there useful machine classes for which a recurrence on
adjacency types produces the Ehrhart generating function directly? When do
quasi-polynomial periods collapse, and which restrictions imply polynomial
counts? The exact inverse in the tournament example is simple; in higher
degree, inverse asymptotics must retain residue-class corrections and
rounding effects rather than replacing them by an unjustified smooth error.

For unbounded acceptance, investigate convergence of finite-horizon rational
densities. The increasing accepted sets give monotonicity, but a rational
limit is not automatic. Quantitative tail estimates would be needed for a
useful approximation theorem and for interchanging input-box and time limits.

\subsection{Algebraic velocities and nonlinear motion}\label{smc:cg:q:algebraic}

With velocities in a fixed algebraic number field, formal event coordinates
remain linear over that field. However, positivity is no longer a rational
half-space condition in the same elementary form, and integer realization
by scaling is not automatic. A number-field sign certificate might extend
part of the method, but the rational-cone, Presburger, and Ehrhart conclusions
would require new arguments or additional hypotheses.

If signals accelerate or their velocities depend on variable parameters,
\eqref{smc:cg:eq:meet} ceases to divide only by a fixed constant. This identifies a
precise boundary of the present linearization. Studying which broader motion
laws still admit low-degree complete chronological certificates is a more
informative problem than assuming every geometric model behaves like the
fixed-speed case.

\subsection{Accumulation limits without fictitious infinite witnesses}\label{smc:cg:q:accumulation}

The clock gives a finitely described accumulation time, but a generic finite
prefix certificate cannot assert all events of an infinite run. A separate
notion of a certified self-similar tail could perhaps prove a limit formula
or a no-collision property beyond a given recursive pattern. Such a notion
must specify the dynamics and limit semantics explicitly. It must not
silently treat an infinite sequence of successful finite checks as one
ordinary existential Diophantine witness.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{smc:cg:sec:conclusion}

For rational fixed-speed signal machines, the continuous coordinates of a
finite computation are not the difficult nonlinear part. Once a discrete
chronological skeleton is fixed, they are rational linear forms in the
initial gaps. The nontrivial certificate obligation is completeness of
chronology: excluding unlisted earlier meetings and accounting for every
signal and every site at a simultaneous event. Maximal adjacency intervals
solve that obligation with a linear number of endpoint conditions.

The resulting exact chamber description yields quadratic natural-number
certificates with unique slack tuples, and finite disjoint first-hit unions
yield quartics with unique selector and slack tuples. It also yields useful
results about denominator growth, small integral realizations, perturbation
stability, and seed-counting quasi-polynomials. The executable examples test
these claims against independent exact simulation and expose the boundary
cases that incidence-only encodings miss.

The unresolved universal task is sharply separated: finitely encode an
unbounded discrete skeleton together with the ordinary input interface.
The convex-quadratic rigidity theorem explains why this cannot be achieved
by merely hiding more geometry inside one fixed globally nonnegative
quadratic. (\wtag\ The rigidity theorem is a special case of Part~XI of \emph{canonical-diophantine-certificates}; Remark~\ref{smc:cg:rem:credit}.) The present contribution is a complete and auditable geometric
frontend, together with a clear account of which further arithmetic and
formalization steps remain.

\section{Purpose and relation to prior work}\label{smc:sd:sec:purpose}
Turing-complete substrates can store and transform information in very different ways. Signal machines use a finite collection of particle types moving continuously on a line and interacting at collisions. A direct arithmetization of their geometry should explain both the prescribed events and the absence of all other events. Merely writing down line intersections leaves the latter obligation unresolved.

This article establishes that obligation for each fixed finite collision schema. Its contribution is an explicit integer certificate, including denominator control, canonical witnesses, sparse size bounds, and exact endpoint conditions. No general claim that signal-machine evolution is newly linearizable is intended. Durand-Lose's linear real-register simulation already computes the next delay, updates gaps, and processes simultaneous events by finitely many linear branches \cite[Section~4]{DL07}. Our arithmetic constraints begin after a particular finite branch history has been fixed.

The substrate itself is already Turing universal. Durand-Lose gives a halting construction with 13 meta-signals and 21 \emph{non-blank} collision rules; its four speeds are $-2,0,1,2$ \cite[Theorem~5 and Figure~4]{durandlose}. The construction uses a finite initial configuration and is separate from the paper's weaker, non-halting cellular-automaton simulation with an infinite background. After a Galilean shift the speeds become $0,2,3,4$. The rational normalized placements used in the halting arrangement can be scaled to integer positions, as proved generally below. We have not transcribed or executed that universal collision table. The accompanying replay uses small exact test machines.

The results are deliberately bounded in a precise sense. The machine, input length and labels, schema, and number of batches are compiler data. The polynomial's arity grows with the schema. Nothing here packs unbounded execution into one fixed-arity quadratic, resolves the single-fold Diophantine representation problem, improves a universal-polynomial arithmetic-operation bound, or formalizes the proof in a proof assistant. A targeted literature check supports the stated relation to earlier work; it does not establish exhaustive novelty.

\section{Model and exact theorem}\label{smc:sd:sec:model}
Throughout, $\NN=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. Inputs and witnesses are natural numbers unless explicitly stated otherwise.

\begin{definition}[Normalized signal machine]
A machine $\cM$ consists of a finite set of labels, a fixed speed $v(\ell)\in\NN$ for each label, and a deterministic collision table. A legal incoming set has at least two labels with pairwise distinct speeds. Its outgoing set also has pairwise distinct speeds and may be empty. An initial configuration consists of labels $\ell_0,\ldots,\ell_{n-1}$ at positions
\[
 0\le p_0<p_1<\cdots<p_{n-1},\qquad p_j\in\NN.
\]
Between collisions a signal follows a straight line of its label's speed. At a collision every incident signal is consumed and the designated outgoing set is emitted at that point.
\end{definition}

If an incoming set has no rule, the collision is forbidden, unless the model explicitly completes the table by a transparent rule. Such a rule consumes the incident segments and emits the same labels, ordered by speed. The theorem works for either convention after the machine is fixed. The replay scripts use identity completion, so an omitted rule in their input is transparent, not forbidden.

We distinguish labels from \emph{segment identities}. Each initial signal has its own identity, and each emitted signal receives a fresh one, even if a transparent rule reproduces the same label. A segment has one birth and at most one death. Initial signals are born at time zero. This distinction is essential for endpoint typing.

\begin{definition}[Finite collision schema]
Fix the machine and the initial label list. A schema $\cS$ specifies $K$ nonempty chronological batches. In batch $i$, a partition of the ordered pre-batch live list into consecutive blocks designates each nonsingleton block as an event and each singleton as a persisting signal. Every event uses a legal rule, and its incoming speeds strictly decrease from left to right. Its outputs are ordered by strictly increasing speed. All replacements in a batch are performed atomically. Segment births precede their deaths by at least one batch.
\end{definition}

The schema fixes no event coordinates or durations. Its events within a batch are ordered from left to right by their incoming blocks. Zero-output events are retained as events. A schema with $K=0$ has no events. No initial collision markers, infinite initial support, or accumulation events are included.

A \emph{prefix realization} has exactly these first $K$ nonempty collision batches and ends immediately after batch $K$. The $K=0$ prefix is the initial configuration alone. A \emph{halting realization} additionally has no future collision. Halting does not require all signals to disappear or stop moving. There is no arbitrary terminal observation time.

Let $E$ be the number of events, $I=\sum_e|\inset(e)|$ the number of incoming incidences, $r$ the largest incoming arity, and $s$ the largest fanout. Set $\nminus=\max(n-1,0)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:modulus}
 D=\lcm\{|v-w|:v,w\text{ are machine speeds},\ v\ne w\},\qquad B=D^K,
\end{equation}
with the empty least common multiple equal to one.

\noindent\wtag\ Theorem~\ref{smc:sd:thm:main} is, for prefix semantics, the theorem of Part~I's Theorems~\ref{smc:cg:thm:chamber} and~\ref{smc:cg:thm:quadratic}, in a different witness format: natural absolute positions as input, and batch times, event positions and slacks as witnesses on the grid $B$. Its halting version, the full column rank and the size ledger \eqref{smc:sd:eq:rawcounts}--\eqref{smc:sd:eq:linearcounts} are not in Part~I. Source~11's proof is printed in full as the second route.

\begin{theorem}[Sparse canonical certificate]\label{smc:sd:thm:main}
There is an effective compiler which, for every such fixed schema, produces integer affine residuals $r_1,\ldots,r_R$ in the input coordinates $\mathbf p$ and witnesses $\mathbf y\in\NN^W$, and a polynomial
\[
 P_{\cS}(\mathbf p,\mathbf y)=\sum_{a=1}^R r_a(\mathbf p,\mathbf y)^2.
\]
For prefix semantics, a natural zero exists exactly when $\mathbf p$ realizes the schema. For halting semantics the same statement holds after the terminal conditions below are included. For every admissible input the witness is unique. The witness coefficient matrix has full column rank; consequently, if $W>0$, the Hessian in the witness variables is positive definite.

If $L$ denotes the number of recorded adjacency lifetimes, including terminal rays when present, then
\begin{align}
 L&\le \nminus+\sum_e(|\out(e)|+1),\label{smc:sd:eq:Lbound}\\
 W&\le E+2K+2L+\nminus,\qquad
 R\le I+K+2L+\nminus.\label{smc:sd:eq:rawcounts}
\end{align}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:linearcounts}
 W\le(2s+5)E+3\nminus,\qquad
 R\le(r+2s+3)E+3\nminus.
\end{equation}
The polynomial has degree at most two, and degree exactly two whenever its witness vector is nonempty.
\end{theorem}

Syntactically illegal schemas are rejected before compilation. For the halting version, a final live order with a negative adjacent relative velocity is likewise rejected as impossible. One can instead output the constant polynomial one for these cases; the stated resource ledger concerns the structurally admitted construction. At $K=0$, the prefix compiler may use only input-order checks. For $n\le1$ it has no witnesses and returns zero. This harmless degenerate case is not a strictly convex function on a positive-dimensional space.

\section{Rational normalization and denominator control}\label{smc:sd:sec:normalization}
\subsection{Normalization is an external interface}\label{smc:sd:sec:normalize}
Suppose the original speeds are rational numbers $u(\ell)$. Choose a rational $c\le\min_\ell u(\ell)$ and a positive integer $m$ such that every $m(u(\ell)-c)$ is an integer. For a fixed rational input choose a rational translation $a$ and positive integer $q$ so that every $q(x_j+a)$ is a nonnegative integer. The invertible coordinate change
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:normalize}
 X=q(x+a-ct),\qquad T=\frac{q}{m}t
\end{equation}
preserves simultaneous incidence, spatial order at each time, positive time direction, and the collision rules. Its speeds are
\[
 \frac{dX}{dT}=m(u(\ell)-c)\in\NN,
\]
and its initial coordinates are natural numbers. Thus every finite rational configuration can be put into the theorem's interface.

The constants $a,c,m,q$ are chosen \emph{outside} the existential witness tuple. For a varying input family with fixed denominator and shift, the natural input $p_j=q(x_j+a)$ is the declared coordinate encoding. Allowing an arbitrary additional scale as a witness produces many proportional encodings; allowing raw variable numerators and denominators introduces products when denominators are cleared. Neither change preserves the present canonical, jointly quadratic claim without a new argument. Inputs at arbitrary real coordinates are outside this interface.


\wtag\ Part~I needs no Galilean shift: it allows signed integer speeds after the time rescaling $\tau=t/L$ (Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:model}) and takes the gap vector as input. With $m=L$ the two normalizations give the same speed differences, hence the same $D$ (Section~\ref{smc:sec:routes}).

\subsection{Causal depth and a fixed grid}
Assign depth zero to initial births. The depth of event $e$ is one plus the maximum depth of the birth events of its incoming segments, with initial births contributing zero. Every birth lies in an earlier batch, so the maximum event depth $H$ satisfies $H\le K$.

\begin{lemma}[Denominator divisibility]\label{smc:sd:lem:denom}
(\wtag\ The same as Part~I, Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:denominator}, there stated for the coefficient forms in the initial gaps; source~11's proof is kept as a second route because it works with event-coordinate witnesses.) Every event time and position of depth at most $h$ has reduced denominator dividing $D^h$. In particular $D^H$, and hence $B=D^K$, clears every event coordinate and time. It also clears the position of any live segment sampled at an event time.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Initial birth positions and times are integers. Suppose an event of depth $h$ has incoming lines
\[
 x=b_1+v_1(t-a_1),\qquad x=b_2+v_2(t-a_2),\quad v_1\ne v_2.
\]
All birth coordinates have denominators dividing $D^{h-1}$ by induction. The intersection time is
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:intersection}
 t=\frac{b_2-v_2a_2-b_1+v_1a_1}{v_1-v_2}.
\end{equation}
The numerator's denominator divides $D^{h-1}$, while $|v_1-v_2|$ divides $D$. Thus the reduced denominator of $t$ divides $D^h$. Substitution into either line gives the same assertion for its position. A multi-signal event is pinned by any two incoming speeds; the remaining incidences are additional equalities. Finally a sampled position is an integer-linear combination of a birth position, birth time and sample time, all cleared by $D^H$.
\end{proof}

This lemma bounds denominator divisibility, not numerator magnitude. Its practical advantage is that $B$ is a computable constant of the schema. The compiler can use the sharper $D^H$ if desired; the supplied implementation uses $D^K$.

\section{The sparse construction}\label{smc:sd:sec:sparse}
\subsection{Geometry variables and flight equations}
Use a natural variable $T_i$ for the scaled time of batch $i$, with $T_0=0$, and a natural variable $X_e$ for the scaled position of each event. Introduce $d_i\in\NN$ and require
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:time}
 T_i-T_{i-1}-d_i-1=0\qquad(1\le i\le K).
\end{equation}
Thus each batch occurs at a strictly later time on the $B$-grid.

For a segment $\sigma$ of speed $v_\sigma$, let $b(\sigma)$ be its birth batch. Define its scaled birth-position expression $A_\sigma$ to be $Bp_j$ for initial segment $j$, and $X_f$ if it is emitted at event $f$. Its position at any relevant batch time is the affine expression
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:position}
 Z_\sigma(T_i)=A_\sigma+v_\sigma\bigl(T_i-T_{b(\sigma)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
If $\sigma$ is consumed by event $e$ in batch $i$, impose the flight residual
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:flight}
 X_e-Z_\sigma(T_i)=0.
\end{equation}
There are $I$ such residuals. All coefficients are integers because the speeds and $B$ are fixed integers. No product of two unknowns appears.

Input order is encoded, rather than silently assumed, by natural slacks $u_j$ and residuals
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:input}
 p_{j+1}-p_j-u_j-1=0\qquad(0\le j<n-1).
\end{equation}
An explicitly restricted ordered-input domain permits these residuals and witnesses to be omitted. We keep them in every count.

\subsection{Maximal adjacency lifetimes}
Maintain the ordered live list combinatorially. A consecutive ordered pair of segment identities has a lifetime beginning at the first boundary at which it becomes adjacent and ending at the boundary at which it ceases to be adjacent. A pair that remains adjacent across several batches has one lifetime, not a new copy in every slab. Perform all replacements in a simultaneous batch before creating new pairs; pairs appearing only during an arbitrary sequential processing order are never recorded.

For a prefix, close each still-active positive-duration lifetime at the final cut $T_K$. A pair newly born at batch $K$ has no positive-duration finite lifetime and is omitted. For halting semantics, still-active pairs receive infinite terminal lifetimes instead, including pairs born at the final batch. The initial $K=0$ prefix needs no finite lifetimes.

For a pair $(\sigma,\tau)$, left to right, its scaled gap is the affine function
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:gap}
 G_{\sigma\tau}(T)=Z_\tau(T)-Z_\sigma(T),
\end{equation}
whose slope is the fixed relative velocity $v_\tau-v_\sigma$.

\begin{lemma}[Lifetime count]\label{smc:sd:lem:lifetimes}
(\wtag\ The same as the first inequality of \eqref{smc:cg:eq:Jbound} in Part~I, Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:rows}; source~11's proof is kept as a second route because it works with event-coordinate witnesses.) The number $L$ of recorded lifetimes satisfies \eqref{smc:sd:eq:Lbound}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are at most $\nminus$ initial adjacent pairs. Replacing one consecutive event block by $k$ outputs creates at most $k+1$ new pairs: at most $k-1$ internal output pairs and two outer-neighbor pairs when $k\ge1$, or at most one bridge pair when $k=0$. Boundary events can create fewer. A sequential accounting of the disjoint replacements in a batch therefore counts at most $\sum_e(|\out(e)|+1)$ creations. Some counted pairs may be transient and disappear within the same batch; atomic processing removes those and cannot increase the count. Every lifetime has exactly one creation, including any terminal lifetime. Closing a lifetime does not create an additional one.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Typed endpoint constraints}\label{smc:sd:sec:endpoints}
For a finite lifetime from batch $a$ to batch $b>a$, impose two constraints:
\begin{enumerate}
\item At the lower endpoint, impose $G(T_a)=0$ if and only if both segments were emitted by the \emph{same event} in batch $a$. Otherwise impose $G(T_a)=h^-+1$ with a fresh $h^-\in\NN$.
\item At the upper endpoint, impose $G(T_b)=0$ if and only if both segments are consumed by the \emph{same event} in batch $b$. Otherwise impose $G(T_b)=h^++1$ with a fresh $h^+\in\NN$.
\end{enumerate}
The ``if and only if'' here specifies which residual the compiler emits from the schema; it is not a new logical connective in the polynomial. Permitted equality means an equality residual, not an arbitrary nonnegative gap. In particular, a surviving pair at the final cut receives a \emph{strict} upper gap.

For a terminal ray starting at batch $a$, require its relative velocity to be nonnegative, a check on machine constants. Impose a zero initial gap only for two same-event outputs at that starting boundary; impose $G(T_a)=h+1$ otherwise. Same-event outputs have strictly increasing speeds, so a zero-gap terminal pair automatically has strictly positive relative velocity. A terminal pair with zero relative velocity must therefore start with positive gap.

Every stated condition is either an integer affine residual or a finite compile-time structural check. Define $P_\cS$ by squaring and adding the residuals \eqref{smc:sd:eq:time}, \eqref{smc:sd:eq:flight}, \eqref{smc:sd:eq:input}, and all endpoint residuals.

\section{Proof of geometric correctness}\label{smc:sd:sec:proof}
\subsection{Why endpoint types suffice}
\begin{lemma}[Positive interior gap]\label{smc:sd:lem:interior}
(\wtag\ The same as Part~I, Lemma~\ref{smc:cg:lem:endpoint} (the endpoint lemma) together with the static rejection of a common birth and a common death that precedes it; source~11's proof is kept as a second route because it works with event-coordinate witnesses, and it adds terminal rays.) Every finite lifetime satisfying its endpoint residuals has strictly positive gap in its open time interval. Every admitted terminal lifetime has strictly positive gap at every time after its start.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A finite gap is affine. Its endpoint values are nonnegative. If either is strictly positive, every proper convex combination of the endpoint values is strictly positive. If both are zero, the endpoint types say that the pair shares its birth event at the lower boundary and its death event at the upper boundary. Birth ordering gives $v_\sigma<v_\tau$, so
\[
 G(T_b)=G(T_a)+(v_\tau-v_\sigma)(T_b-T_a)>0,
\]
a contradiction. Thus at least one endpoint is strict, and no extra endpoint-sum residual is needed. On a terminal ray the nonnegative slope preserves a strict initial gap; a zero initial gap has strictly positive slope and becomes positive immediately.
\end{proof}

It is important that this argument uses segment identities and the exact start and end boundaries. Merely allowing two nonnegative endpoint gaps, even with positive sum, does not exclude an unrecorded meeting at the upper endpoint.

\begin{lemma}[Complete boundaries]\label{smc:sd:lem:boundary}
At every batch satisfying the residuals, each designated incoming block meets at its event point, distinct designated events have distinct ordered positions, and no persisting signal or signal of another event also meets that point. The post-batch order is valid; its only zero gaps are between outputs of the same event.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Before the batch, every adjacent pair belongs to a lifetime that has existed for a positive duration. For a pair in one event block, the flight equations place both segments at that event. Any pair across two distinct pre-batch blocks is not consumed by the same event. If its lifetime ends at this boundary, the strict upper endpoint gives a positive gap. If it persists across the boundary and this is an interior point of its lifetime, Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:interior} gives a positive gap. If the boundary is the final cut, a surviving pair has a strict upper endpoint, or lies on an admitted terminal ray. These cases exhaust the pre-batch neighboring pairs.

All positions in an incoming block are equal by the flight equations, whereas successive blocks are strictly separated. Hence the designated blocks are precisely the maximal equality classes at that time. In particular a third incident signal cannot be omitted, two designated events cannot coincide, and a completely erased event still has a position in the strict block order. Replacing each event block at its own point by the prescribed speed-ordered outputs therefore preserves weak spatial order. Across distinct surviving blocks the gap stays strict; within one output fan the gap is zero and the increasing speeds separate it immediately. If erased blocks disappear, the new bridge gap spans one or more former strict block separations, so is positive. The replacements are atomic and introduce no additional intermediate configuration.
\end{proof}

This proof also explains why final-born prefix pairs can be omitted. Their boundary order is already certified by the incoming block partition and flight equations. Immediately after that boundary, a common output fan separates and all other gaps remain positive for a sufficiently small interval. A prefix does not assert the absence of collisions later in that interval's future.

\subsection{Soundness and completeness}
\begin{proof}[Proof of the existence assertion in Theorem~\ref{smc:sd:thm:main}]
For soundness, suppose $P_\cS(\mathbf p,\mathbf y)=0$ with natural inputs and witnesses. Each square is nonnegative, so every residual is zero. The input packet gives strict initial order. Time residuals give strictly increasing batch times. Divide all scaled times and event positions by $B$ and draw the prescribed segments using \eqref{smc:sd:eq:position}.

On each open slab, the combinatorial live list is fixed and every neighboring pair is in an active lifetime. Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:interior} excludes all adjacent meetings. A meeting of nonadjacent signals would force an adjacent equality in the ordered list at or before that meeting, so there are no other meetings either. At the next batch, Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:boundary} proves that the designated events are exactly all collisions and contain every incident signal. Their legal rules are the fixed schema rules. Induction across the $K$ batches gives precisely the prescribed prefix. For halting semantics every final adjacent gap remains positive for all future time by the terminal-ray argument, so no future collision occurs.

For completeness, start with an actual realization. Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:denom} puts every event time, event position and endpoint sample on the $B$-grid. Assign their scaled values to $T_i$ and $X_e$, and set $d_i=B(t_i-t_{i-1})-1$. All are natural. Input gaps determine natural $u_j$. Each lower or upper endpoint at which the schema prescribes equality is an actual common birth or death point. Every other endpoint is strictly separated by the definition of a complete collision history; its positive scaled integer gap minus one supplies the unique natural slack. An actual halting configuration has nonnegative adjacent relative velocities, since a negative one would eventually cause a meeting, and its terminal initial gaps have exactly the required types. All residuals vanish.
\end{proof}

The argument covers simultaneous multi-signal collisions, disjoint events in the same batch, annihilation, transparent collisions and empty final configurations. Its dependence on complete blocks and typed boundaries is substantive, not a cosmetic choice of encoding.

\section{Canonical witnesses and strict convexity}\label{smc:sd:sec:canonical}
Uniqueness of integer witnesses alone would not prove strict convexity. We establish the stronger linear-algebra statement directly.

\begin{lemma}[Full column rank]\label{smc:sd:lem:rank}
Write the residual vector as
\[
 \mathbf r(\mathbf p,\mathbf y)=A\mathbf y+C\mathbf p+\mathbf c.
\]
For every structurally admitted schema, the matrix $A$ has trivial kernel over $\R$, even if the schema has no realization for the chosen input.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $A\Delta\mathbf y=0$ and hold the input fixed. Initial birth positions have zero variation. Induct on batches. Assume all earlier times and event positions have zero variation. Choose any event $e$ in batch $i$ and two of its incoming segments $\sigma,\tau$ with distinct speeds. The homogeneous flight equations are
\[
 \Delta X_e-v_\sigma\Delta T_i=0,\qquad
 \Delta X_e-v_\tau\Delta T_i=0,
\]
because both segments were born in earlier batches. Their difference gives $(v_\sigma-v_\tau)\Delta T_i=0$, hence $\Delta T_i=0$ and $\Delta X_e=0$. Every other event in the batch then has zero position variation from any one of its flight equations. Equation~\eqref{smc:sd:eq:time} gives $\Delta d_i=0$. Induction fixes all geometry variables and time slacks.

Each remaining witness is an input-order or endpoint slack with coefficient $-1$ in its own defining residual. With the geometry and input fixed, that residual forces its variation to be zero. Thus $\Delta\mathbf y=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Canonical zero and Hessian]\label{smc:sd:cor:hessian}
For fixed input there is at most one real solution of the residual system, and hence at most one natural zero of $P_\cS$. The Hessian in witness coordinates is $2A^{\mathsf T}A$, positive definite when $W>0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Two solutions differ by a vector in $\ker A$, which is zero by Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:rank}. Moreover $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}(2A^{\mathsf T}A)\mathbf z=2\|A\mathbf z\|^2>0$ for every nonzero $\mathbf z$. Existence for admissible inputs was proved above.
\end{proof}

Strict convexity is asserted in the witness variables with the input held fixed. Joint strict convexity in input and witness coordinates is neither needed nor generally true. The compiler introduces no witness for a schedule choice, denominator choice or arbitrary final observation time. Adding any of those could destroy the canonical property.

\begin{proof}[Proof of the size assertions in Theorem~\ref{smc:sd:thm:main}]
Geometry uses $E$ event positions, $K$ batch times and $K$ time slacks. A finite lifetime needs at most two slacks and two residuals; a terminal lifetime needs at most one of each. Input order uses $\nminus$ slacks and residuals. Flight and time residual counts are $I$ and $K$. This proves \eqref{smc:sd:eq:rawcounts}. Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:lifetimes}, $I\le rE$, $K\le E$ and $|\out(e)|\le s$ give \eqref{smc:sd:eq:linearcounts}. Squaring affine residuals gives degree at most two. If $W>0$, full column rank forces a nonzero quadratic part.
\end{proof}

The ledger counts variables and affine residuals. With a fixed machine, every residual has bounded width: an endpoint gap uses only the birth positions and times of two segments, one endpoint time, and possibly a slack. A linear-size arithmetic circuit follows if integer constants are treated as available coefficients. This does not give a literal gate ledger under a model that charges separately for constructing large constants, input loading, multiplication conventions or natural-to-positive conversion. In particular, no improvement to previously studied 75-operation or 87-operation constructions is asserted here.

\section{A full-layer reference construction}\label{smc:sd:sec:fulllayer}
A denser construction is useful for cross-checking. In slab $i$, let $N_i$ be the number of pre-batch signals and $g_i$ the number of blocks, including singleton through-signals. Introduce natural scaled end coordinates $C_{i,j}$, a duration slack $d_i$, and one gap slack between successive blocks. The scaled start coordinate $A_{i,j}$ is $Bp_j$ initially or an alias of an earlier endpoint. Require
\begin{align*}
 C_{i,j}-A_{i,j}-v_j(d_i+1)&=0 &&\text{for every live signal},\\
 C_{i,j}-C_{i,\mathrm{first}(b)}&=0 &&\text{inside each collision block }b,\\
 C_{i,\mathrm{first}(b')}-C_{i,\mathrm{first}(b)}-h-1&=0
 &&\text{for successive blocks }b,b'.
\end{align*}
All blocks, including zero-output collisions, participate in the last row. The exact counts, with input-order checks, are
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:baselinecounts}
 W=\nminus+\sum_i(N_i+g_i),\qquad
 R=\nminus+\sum_i(2N_i-1).
\end{equation}
Indeed each slab uses $N_i+1+(g_i-1)$ witnesses, and $N_i+(N_i-g_i)+(g_i-1)$ residuals.

The two boundary lists have nonnegative adjacent gaps, with strict separation across ending blocks. A pair with zero gap at its starting boundary is a same-event output pair with increasing speeds; it cannot meet again at the ending boundary after positive time. Affineness therefore excludes all interior collisions. The full ending partition supplies completeness at each boundary. Conversely the denominator lemma gives a witness from every realization. The same two-incoming-speed induction fixes each duration, then all endpoints and gap slacks, and proves full witness rank. A halting certificate additionally checks that the final live speeds are nondecreasing; the final boundary order is already certified.

This reference compiler stores coordinates for every live signal at every layer, even if that signal survives unchanged through many layers. The sparse compiler avoids these repetitions by storing only event coordinates and maximal adjacency lifetimes. Both implementations share an exact simulator and small arithmetic utilities; agreement between them is useful evidence, but is not independent formal verification of the common simulator.

\section{Worked examples}\label{smc:sd:sec:examples}
\subsection{One annihilation with its complete quadratic}
Consider labels $R,S$ with speeds $2,0$ and the rule $\{R,S\}\mapsto\varnothing$. Let the initial positions be $p_0<p_1$, and specify one event. Here $D=B=2$. Write $T,X,d$ for its scaled time, position and duration slack, $u$ for input order and $h$ for the lower adjacency slack. The compiler gives
\begin{align}\label{smc:sd:eq:annihilation}
 P={}&(p_1-p_0-u-1)^2+(T-d-1)^2\notag\\
 &+(X-2p_0-2T)^2+(X-2p_1)^2\notag\\
 &+(2p_1-2p_0-h-1)^2+(2p_1-2p_0-2T)^2.
\end{align}
The last row is the zero upper gap of the two segments consumed by the same event. Its redundancy with flight equations is harmless. Every $p_0<p_1$ has the unique witness
\[
 T=p_1-p_0,\quad X=2p_1,\quad d=u=p_1-p_0-1,
 \quad h=2(p_1-p_0)-1.
\]
For $(p_0,p_1)=(0,1)$ the tuple $(T,X,d,u,h)$ is $(1,2,0,0,1)$, corresponding to physical time $1/2$ and position $1$. There are five witnesses and six residuals. The final state is empty, so the prefix is also halting. Changing any one witness while fixing the others cannot keep all residuals zero.

\subsection{A hidden collision exactly at the final cut}\label{smc:sd:sec:finalcut}
Use the four-speed interface $\{0,2,3,4\}$, with labels $a,b,c,d$ respectively, and the annihilation rule $\{d,a\}\mapsto\varnothing$. Start with
\[
 (\ell_0,\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)=(d,a,d,a),\qquad
 (p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3)=(0,4,10,14).
\]
There are two simultaneous collisions at physical time $1$, at positions $4$ and $14$. Now submit a false one-batch schema containing only the first event and marking the last two signals as through-signals. Since $B=12$, the first event's flight equations force $T_1=12$ and $X_0=48$.

The omitted pair's gap is
\[
 G(T)=12(14-10)-4T=48-4T.
\]
It is positive before the cut, has lower value $48$, and reaches zero at the cut. Thus conditions of the form $G(0)\ge0$, $G(T_1)\ge0$, and $G(0)+G(T_1)>0$ would accept its endpoints. Our upper endpoint is strict because the pair survives rather than dying in one designated event. Its residual becomes
\[
 G(T_1)-h^+-1=-h^+-1,
\]
which cannot vanish for $h^+\in\NN$. The false schema has no certificate. The correct simultaneous schema consumes both pairs atomically and has event positions $48$ and $168$ on the scaled grid.


\wtag\ Part~I's independent simultaneous sites (Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:simultaneous}) are another instance of this failure mode: an omitted, spatially separate simultaneous collision, rejected by the strict endpoint of the surviving pair (Section~\ref{smc:sec:routes}).

\subsection{An outgoing fan at a finite cut}
Replace the annihilation rule in the preceding setup by $\{d,a\}\mapsto\{b,c\}$. Each collision emits speeds $2$ and $3$. At the final cut the two members of each fan have zero gap and immediately separate; they need no zero-duration finite lifetime. In a halting certificate they instead receive terminal rays with zero starting gap and positive relative velocity. The gap between the two different fans is strict at the cut. Its relative velocity is $2-3=-1$, so that terminal ray is rejected: the two fans will interact later. This separates valid finite-prefix geometry from the stronger no-future-collision requirement.

\section{Witness height for the four-speed interface}\label{smc:sd:sec:height}
Let the normalized machine speeds lie in $\{0,2,3,4\}$, and let $M_j$ be the largest live position immediately after batch $j$, with $M_0=\max_j p_j$ and $\max\varnothing=0$. Let $t_j$ be elapsed physical time, with $t_0=0$.

\begin{lemma}[Geometric growth]\label{smc:sd:lem:growth}
Whenever batch $j+1$ exists, its delay satisfies $\delta_{j+1}\le M_j$. All positions at that boundary, including erased event positions, are at most $5M_j$. Consequently
\[
 M_j\le5^jM_0,\qquad
 t_j\le M_0\frac{5^j-1}{4}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A next collision must be approached by an adjacent closing pair. The pair has a strictly positive gap $g$ and positive integer closing speed $c\ge1$. Because all live positions are nonnegative, $g\le M_j$; its meeting delay is $g/c\le M_j$. The earliest collision delay is no larger. During this interval every signal advances by at most $4M_j$, so every pre-collision position is at most $5M_j$. Outputs remain at those event positions and deletion cannot increase the maximum. Iterate this bound and sum $\delta_i\le5^{i-1}M_0$ to obtain the stated geometric series.
\end{proof}

The argument concerns an existing next batch. Empty or singleton configurations have none. Nor does a valid configuration whose maximum position is zero: any coincident outgoing fan has strictly increasing speeds and separates. This avoids dividing by a nonexistent positive gap.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical height]\label{smc:sd:thm:height}
For $B=12^K$, every canonical input coordinate and witness of the sparse or full-layer compiler is at most
\begin{equation}\label{smc:sd:eq:height}
 60^K\max(1,M_0).
\end{equation}
Each such integer therefore has bit length $O(K+\log(M_0+1))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the full speed set, the nonzero absolute differences are $1,2,3,4$, whose least common multiple is $12$. The same $12$ is a valid clearing modulus for a subset. Up to batch $K$, every live sample and every event position is at most $5^KM_0$, including positions subsequently erased. Multiplication by $12^K$ bounds them by $60^KM_0$. Every endpoint gap is the nonnegative difference of two positions in this range, so its slack is smaller. Input-order slacks are at most $M_0$. The time bound is
\[
 12^Kt_j\le12^KM_0\frac{5^K-1}{4}\le60^KM_0,
\]
and the scaled duration minus one is no larger than the corresponding scaled time. The empty and all-zero cases are covered by $\max(1,M_0)$; if $K=0$, all remaining slacks are initial-gap slacks bounded by $M_0$. Taking binary logarithms proves the bit bound.
\end{proof}

This bounds actual zero witnesses, not arbitrary trial assignments. Degree two alone would supply no such height guarantee. Likewise, the bound is exponential in batch count $K$ as a numerical magnitude while linear in $K$ as a bit length. For a fixed machine with maximal speed $V$, the same argument gives $M_j\le(1+V)^jM_0$; the four-speed constant above is the concrete interface relevant here.


\wtag\ Part~I bounds the chamber's row coefficients (Proposition~\ref{smc:cg:prop:height}) and the size of \emph{some} realizing seed (Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:small}); Theorem~\ref{smc:sd:thm:height} bounds the canonical witnesses of every realizing input. The two bounds measure different things and neither implies the other.

\subsection{Exponential denominator growth can be necessary}
\begin{proposition}[A bounded geometric shuttle]\label{smc:sd:prop:sharpness}
There is a fixed four-label machine with integer initial positions whose $2k$-batch prefix contains a time of reduced denominator $3^{k-1}$, although every event lies in $0<t<2$ and $0<x<6$. Thus a common integer grid for exact event geometry may require exponentially large scale in the number of batches.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Give labels $L,R,F,B$ speeds $3,2,4,0$, respectively, and use rules
\[
 \{F,R\}\longmapsto\{B,R\},\qquad
 \{L,B\}\longmapsto\{L,F\}.
\]
Start with $L,F,R$ at positions $0,1,2$. The two walls follow $x_L(t)=3t$ and $x_R(t)=2+2t$, so their separation is $2-t$ until they meet at $t=2$. The first right collision occurs at $t_{R,1}=1/2$ and the first left collision at $t_{L,1}=1$. At a left collision at time $a<2$, the outgoing speed-four shuttle catches the right wall after $(2-a)/2$, giving right time $r=1+a/2$. Its speed-zero replacement is then caught by the left wall after $(2-r)/3$, giving the next left time
\[
 a'=r+\frac{2-r}{3}=\frac43+\frac a3.
\]
These positive delays place the shuttle strictly between the walls between alternating collisions; there are no other earlier meetings. The recurrence and first collision give
\[
 t_{L,k}=2-3^{1-k},\qquad
 t_{R,k}=2-\frac32\,3^{1-k}\qquad(k\ge1).
\]
The first six batch times are $1/2,1,3/2,5/3,11/6,17/9$. At the $k$th left event the numerator of $(2\cdot3^{k-1}-1)/3^{k-1}$ is coprime to three, so the reduced denominator is exactly $3^{k-1}$. Event positions lie on a wall before time two and hence are below six. Every finite prefix is valid.
\end{proof}

This example supplies a lower bound for common-grid geometry encodings: after $2k$ batches a clearing scale is divisible by $3^{k-1}$, and its scaled last time requires $\Omega(k)$ bits. It makes the linear event-count dependence in the witness-bit bound qualitatively sharp for this type of encoding; it is not a lower bound on arbitrary Diophantine representations. The collision times approach a Zeno accumulation at $t=2$. We assert nothing about a continuation at or beyond that accumulation, which is outside the finite-schema theorem. The replay checks 64 batches with exact fractions.


\wtag\ Part~I's four-speed clock (Section~\ref{smc:cg:sec:clock}, Proposition~\ref{smc:cg:prop:clock}) is the same shuttle mechanism on a different machine, with speeds $0,2,-1,-2$ and reduced denominators exactly $2\cdot3^k$ at event $2k$ for the seed $(1,2)$. The worked-example receipt shipped as \path{data/11-signal-certificates-expected-worked_examples_receipt.json} records the last left denominator of the 64-batch prefix as $617673396283947=3^{31}$, which is $3^{k-1}$ for $k=32$.

\section{Fixed-schema arithmetic and the unbounded boundary}\label{smc:sd:sec:fixed}
\begin{proposition}[Semilinear feasible inputs]\label{smc:sd:prop:semilinear}
(\wtag\ The same as Part~I, Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:semilinear}, for prefix semantics; source~11's proof is kept as a second route because it works with event-coordinate witnesses, and it adds the halting case.) For a fixed normalized machine, fixed initial label list and fixed schema, the set of natural input positions admitting a prefix certificate is semilinear. The same holds for the halting certificate. For a fixed total-event bound, the union over all schemas with that bound is also semilinear, provided the initial length and labels are fixed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A zero of a sum of squares is exactly a common zero of its affine residuals. Existence of natural witnesses is therefore an existential formula using only integer constants, addition, order and equality. Such Presburger-definable sets are semilinear \cite[Theorem~1.3]{ginsburgspanier}. Terminal slope checks depend only on the fixed schema; they either remove it or leave the same argument.

For a total-event bound $E_0$, at most $n+sE_0$ segment identities can have been created. A finite label set and bounded finite list admit finitely many consecutive partitions, legal rule choices and batch assignments with at most $E_0$ events. Thus there are finitely many schemas. Finite unions of semilinear sets are semilinear.
\end{proof}

More concretely, a semilinear set is a finite union of sets $\mathbf a+\sum_{j=1}^m\NN\mathbf b_j$. The proposition describes a strong boundary: a fixed externally supplied finite schema reduces to integer linear feasibility despite its quadratic packaging. Strict convexity does not import undecidability into that fixed finite system.

Enumerating all finite schemas gives an enumerable family of quadratics covering finite-event halting, with varying arities and coefficients. In a deterministic machine an actually halting run has a definite finite sequence of complete nonempty batches. That fact does not encode its arbitrary length into one fixed tuple. A single fixed-arity representation of unbounded halting requires an additional history-packing construction, with its own decoder, arithmetic costs and witness-multiplicity proof. Generic Diophantine representability results do not automatically preserve this compiler's quadratic degree, sparsity, or single witness. Nor does the construction certify continuation through a Zeno accumulation; no finite schema contains infinitely many events.


\wtag\ For a fixed event budget, Part~I's Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:quarticunion} packs the finite union over all first-hit skeletons into one quartic with unique selector and slack witnesses; it does not remove the budget either.

\subsection{Strictly positive witnesses}
If a convention requires witnesses in $\Z_{>0}$, replace every $y_j$ by $Y_j-1$ and define
\[
 Q_{\cS}(\mathbf p,\mathbf Y)=P_{\cS}(\mathbf p,\mathbf Y-\mathbf1).
\]
This is an integer polynomial of the same degree. The coordinatewise translation is a bijection from positive witness tuples to natural witness tuples and preserves uniqueness and the Hessian. The input-coordinate convention remains unchanged unless it is translated separately and explicitly. This substitution is exact but is not an assertion that conversion is free in an arithmetic-operation ledger.

\subsection{Questions with concrete next steps}\label{smc:sd:sec:questions}
\wtag\ Table~\ref{smc:tab:questions} pairs these questions with the research directions of Part~I: Questions~\ref{smc:sd:q:universal} and~\ref{smc:sd:q:formal} coincide with Sections~\ref{smc:cg:q:universal} and~\ref{smc:cg:q:formal}, and Questions~\ref{smc:sd:q:ledger} and~\ref{smc:sd:q:compress} overlap Sections~\ref{smc:cg:q:denominators}, \ref{smc:cg:q:circuits} and~\ref{smc:cg:q:firsthit}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{smc:sd:q:universal} \textbf{Instantiate the universal table and input map.} Transcribe the 13-label, 21-non-blank-rule machine with an explicit convention for every blank collision. Verify the cyclic-tag input encoding and rational halting gadget, then compile and replay complete finite examples. This would connect the present implementation to the cited universal machine rather than only to its speed interface.
\item\label{smc:sd:q:conservative} \textbf{Exploit conservative bounded populations carefully.} A reversible conservative rational construction is already Turing universal \cite{DL06}. Its positive energy assignment bounds the number of simultaneously live signals for fixed total energy, but not the number of collisions. A direct unbounded representation would need to instantiate its finite transition branches, encode unbounded histories of rational distances, and pay for strict guards and input loading. Conservation alone neither bounds runtime nor excludes accumulation.
\item\label{smc:sd:q:ledger} \textbf{Sharpen the denominator and cost ledger.} Causal depth can replace batch count in $B$. Further denominator cancellation may depend on the actual branch structure. A useful next result would make such improvements canonical and give a literal arithmetic-gate ledger, including the construction of coefficients and any conversion of input conventions. (\wtag\ Causal-depth clearing itself is proved: Lemma~\ref{smc:sd:lem:denom} shows that $D^H$ suffices, and Part~I, Theorem~\ref{smc:cg:thm:denominator}, gives $D^h$ event by event. What remains open is a canonical choice compatible with the witness format, and the gate ledger.)
\item\label{smc:sd:q:compress} \textbf{Compress histories without losing canonicality.} The sparse certificate removes repeated geometry, not the unbounded number of events. Can a restricted family of repeated collision motifs be packed with a unique decoder witness and explicit degree, height and operation bounds? Any answer should separate verification of a supplied history from existence of an outcome.
\item\label{smc:sd:q:formal} \textbf{Formalize geometric completeness.} The most valuable proof-assistant target is the typed-endpoint soundness theorem, including atomic simultaneous replacement and disappearing event blocks. The replay data provide regression fixtures, but the formal objects must model the real semantics, not only the executable's output.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{smc:sd:sec:conclusion}
A finite signal-machine schema admits a direct, canonical Diophantine encoding because its collision geometry can be expressed by affine integer constraints on a predetermined rational grid. Sparse adjacency lifetimes make the certificate linear in event count and initial support. Correct endpoint types are what turn line-incidence equations into a complete execution certificate. The resulting quadratic is strictly convex in its witness variables, and the four-speed case has an explicit linear witness-bit bound. The remaining unbounded problem is to encode arbitrary schema length without losing the quantitative and multiplicity properties that make this finite construction useful.
