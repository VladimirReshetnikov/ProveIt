                                                   Abstract

          We give five explicit positive-integer POWER modules with 22, 16, 14, 13 and 12 leaves,
      including the output. Their sums of squares have respectively 15, 9, 7, 6 and 5 residual slots and
      fixed-base degrees 12, 12, 16, 16 and 20. For a variable base the last two degrees are 20 and 24. The
      reductions distinguish sign normalization, bijective elimination, and restriction to a strict quotient
      domain followed by rational integer recovery. Every restored variable is proved integral and positive.
      All nonempty complete fibers remain infinite.
          For the retained three-witness bounded counter compiler, we prove an exact degree law: degree
      two at horizon zero, 4(T +1)2 −4 at positive odd horizons, and 4(T +1)2 −8 at positive even horizons.
      We identify the complete top homogeneous term and sharpen the support ceiling. Composing
      the 12-leaf module twice with native gap decoding and the bounded compiler gives 29 positive
      witnesses, three external positive inputs, 18 residual slots and degree equal to the maximum of 20
      and the compiler degree. The other variants give explicit alternative resource ledgers.
          The all-exponent POWER theorem depends on a precisely pinned constructive Pell theorem pair.
      The finite-horizon compiler is separately compiled for each horizon and pays for its finite acceptance
      table. No new Lean verification, finite-fold representation, optimality, priority or fixed-polynomial
      unbounded-halting claim is made.


1    Conventions and principal results

All existential variables in this report range over Z>0 . We write N = {0, 1, . . .}. A natural alias z is
the expression z+ − 1 in its own distinct positive leaf z+ . Thus a zero alias is represented by leaf 1.
Aliases are expanded substitutions, never additional variables or defining equations. A shifted Pell
parameter is α = α+ + 1, so it is at least 2. Every final equation is literally a sum of the listed squared
integer-polynomial residuals.
Fix b ∈ Z, b ≥ 2, and C, o ∈ Z>0 . The intended POWER relation is o = bC−1 . Its Pell index is
C, including when the semantic exponent is zero. A module leaf count includes o and excludes the
supplied index C. A fixed base is a coefficient. If the base is variable, supply an additional positive
input B and put b = B + 1; that external input is not concealed in a module count. Degree is joint in
all independent inputs and positive leaves, after adapter expansion and before imposing equations. The
results cover independent or affine base conventions as stated, not arbitrary nonlinear substitutions.

2.2   The exact normalization maps

From an old tuple preserve all other leaves and define
                    qα = α2 − α1 ,    qσ = σ2 − σ1 ,     qτ = τ2 − τ1 ,   qr = r2 − r1 .
The new leaves are qα + 1, qσ + 1, qτ + 1, qr + 1. Their positivity follows from the preceding proof.
Conversely set the first member of every old natural pair to zero and the second to its new quotient.
The old positive leaves are therefore 1 and q + 1, never zero. These maps prove existential equivalence,
and the reverse map is a section. They are not inverse on all old tuples: adding an arbitrary common
natural shift to both members of each pair changes the old tuple without changing its quotient. The
old ledger 13 + 2 + 11 = 26 becomes 13 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 22, retaining all fifteen residual slots.

The source uses natural subtraction. For natural a, c, the statement a − c = 1 with truncated
subtraction is equivalent to a = c + 1. The inner square-minus-one differences here are nonnegative,
and the proof above establishes α > b. Thus the ordinary subtraction α − b agrees with its natural
counterpart. No domain strengthening or rational-witness replacement is hidden in this translation.

All-exponent equivalence for the 22-leaf module. Slots 1–9 in (2) imply (7) with k = C. The zero
branch is impossible because y ≥ C ≥ 1. Hence (x, y) = (XC (α), YC (α)). Slots 10–15 supply (8) with

other inequalities supply the three natural slacks. Represent signed congruence quotients by pairs of
naturals, then apply the normalization maps just proved. Every positive adapter exists, including at

4    A genuine infinite family in each accepted fiber

The recurrence (6) proves by induction

                        Xn (z)2 − (z 2 − 1)Yn (z)2 = 1,   Xn (1) = 1,      Yn (1) = n.

For the norm, substitution into one recurrence step multiplies the previous norm by z 2 − (z 2 − 1) = 1.
For z ≥ 2, n ≥ 1, both coordinates are positive by the same recurrence. Integer polynomials preserve
congruences of their arguments.
Fix an accepted (b, C, o) and one 22-leaf solution. Hold α, w, M, g, x, y, u, v, qv , J, the three slacks and
qr fixed. For every k ∈ N, put

              βk = β0 + 4yuk,                  sk = XC (βk ),                      tk = YC (βk ),
             qb,k = qb,0 + uk,               qα,k = qα,0 + 4yk,
             qσ,k = (sk − x)/u,               qτ,k = (tk − C)/(4y).                                       (9)

Here βk ≡ α (mod u) and βk ≡ 1 (mod 4y), so sk ≡ x (mod u) and tk ≡ C (mod 4y). Lemma 2.1,
using 0 < x ≤ u and 1 ≤ C < 4y, makes the two quotients natural. All fifteen equations hold. The
new pair at k = 0 need not equal the originally chosen (s, t); only the preserved data and congruences
are required. The leaf qb,k is strictly increasing because u > 0, proving infinitely many distinct tuples.
This is genuine parameter variation after the four common-shift redundancies have been removed.

9     The retained three witness bounded compiler

Fix a finite deterministic two-counter program M, initial state q0 and halt state H. Each instruction
increments one counter with a specified successor, or tests it for zero with a specified zero successor and
otherwise decrements it with a specified positive successor. The other counter is unchanged. States may
repeat. After H use virtual absorbing steps only for native bookkeeping. These semantics are explicit;
related standard instruction conventions and their importance are discussed by Dudenhefner [2].


9.1   Exact clipping on all input sizes

Lemma 9.1 (Clipping). Replacing native initial counters (a, b) by (min(a, T ), min(b, T )) preserves
all control states through time T and all selected branches at entry times 0, . . . , T − 1. Therefore it
preserves first halt by T and first halt exactly at T .

Proof. At time zero states agree. Suppose branches agree for the first t < T transitions. Corresponding
counters have received identical changes. If the original initial counter was below T , its representative
is identical. Otherwise its representative began at T and is at least T − t > 0 at this entry time; the
original is this value plus its nonnegative initial excess. Their zero-test outcomes agree. Increments
and virtual halt steps agree as well. This proves the induction through transition T . At T = 0 there
are no branches, and only the initial state matters.

The strict bound is needed before the last transition, not after it. Final counters and their zero status
need not agree. The uniform native threshold cannot be reduced for this argument: a loop which halts
on zero and otherwise decrements first halts at step T from a = T − 1, and at step T + 1 from a = T .
Clipping at T − 1 would merge different answers. This is a clipping-threshold fact, not a polynomial
lower bound.

11.3       Effective construction and its limits

The table has exactly N Boolean entries for the selected predicate. Constructing it in principle
costs at most N T native transitions. A straightforward algebraic generator builds each basis product
by repeated linear-factor multiplication using O(N 2 ) integer operations per product, hence O(N 3 )
overall. Scaling, adding and ordinary convolution to form the six squares fit within another O(N 3 )
integer operations. Product and triangle bounds as above keep intermediate magnitudes within
O(N log(N + 1)) bits. These are arithmetic-operation bounds, not unit-cost bit-complexity assertions.
The generator is uniform and effective in program and horizon, but each horizon selects different
coefficients and a different degree. If T is supplied in binary, these costs need not be polynomial in its
bit length. The construction does not give a faster single-instance bounded-halting decision procedure
than ordinary simulation. Existentially saying that some horizon accepts does not internalize varying
factorials, products and acceptance tables in a single fixed polynomial. No such uniform arithmetic
encoding is supplied, and no unbounded single-fold consequence follows from unique bounded triples.

14     Physical interpretation and semantic boundaries

The arithmetic POWER and clipping arguments do not need a physical simulator. The separately
retained five-live-signal compiler theorem uses stationary markers L = 0, X = g1 , Y = g1 + g2 , R = D
and an instruction messenger. On precisely the encoded inputs (43), it identifies each native transition
with one ordered instruction section, preserves D, and uses at most 32 binary collisions and duration
strictly between D and 10D per nonhalting transition.
Conditional on that retained theorem, the certificates here also express encoded-input bounded
instruction-section reachability. If first halt occurs at k > 0, the physical prefix has elapsed time
strictly between kD and 10kD and at most 32k collisions. At k = 0 it is already at the halt section.
A collision-count horizon is not an instruction horizon. The arithmetic absorbing halt edge is padding
only, not an assertion that the physical messenger keeps executing after halt.
Neither the three-witness compiler nor these module replacements classify arbitrary unencoded physical
executions. The present work does not re-audit the physical theorem, create a universal counter
program, or establish a new five-signal universality result. Its finite-table coefficients depend on the
fixed program and horizon; its physical interpretation remains limited to the explicitly inherited
interface.

16     Context and questions left open

Pell-based Diophantine exponentiation is established prior mathematics. Pąk and Kaliszyk [4] describe
formalizations and carefully distinguish free parameters, explicit unknowns and additional unknowns
hidden in subrelations. That distinction is essential here: twelve module leaves include the output,
exclude the supplied index, and cost eleven auxiliaries after the output is fixed. The native-gap
composition must pay for both shifted counters, both complete modules and the compiler witnesses.
The comparison in Table 1 is between specified presentations of one relation. It is not a comparison
with arbitrary Diophantine equations, prime-representing polynomials or universal polynomials. No
exhaustive literature search, novelty, priority, minimum degree, minimum witness count, arithmetic-
gate improvement or small-height Pell-witness result is claimed. Every displayed full POWER and
native-gap fiber is infinite; eliminating obvious common-shift freedom does not make these formulas
finite-fold.
Several bounded questions remain meaningful. Can another explicitly justified elimination improve a
listed witness/degree pair? Can the coefficient and support ceilings be tightened without changing the
literal formula? Can useful height bounds be proved for reconstructed positive Pell witnesses? Can
finite-table construction exploit a particular program without treating acceptance as free data? Each
would require a separately stated construction or proof. None is settled by the exact counts in this
report, and none turns the horizon-indexed family into one fixed polynomial for unbounded halting.
