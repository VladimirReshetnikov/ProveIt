                                  Diophantine compilers
                    Two witnesses at linear degree; one witness by disjoint cells

                                                  4 October 2026

                                                    Abstract
          A predicate on positive integer pairs that depends only on clipping each coordinate at a fixed
      threshold has a direct Diophantine representation with a unique positive witness pair. Cleared
      tensor interpolation gives five residual squares and, at horizon T ≥ 1, exact degree max{2(T +
      1), 4, 2 deg FS } ≤ 4T . One fixed two-zero-test program attains 4T for every by-horizon T ≥ 2 in this
      presentation. A separate product of disjoint-cell sums of squares has one unique positive witness
      and exact degree 2nI + 4T nE + 8T nC . We prove an exact zero-versus-one native auxiliary-variable
      classification for every fixed finite clipping table, with no degree restriction: zero variables suffice
      exactly when each accepted tail cell’s full coordinate-flat closure is accepted.
          All domains, empty and full tables, horizon zero, coefficient heights, expanded support, storage,
      factored gates and table preprocessing are paid explicitly. Composing with the accepted twelve-leaf
      POWER module gives 28 positive witnesses and 17 squares, or 27 witnesses with a nonnegative
      product block; replacing that block by its square gives 13 squares and doubles its compiler-degree
      contribution. Native witnesses and decoded projections are unique, but the full composed fibers are
      infinite. The results concern horizon-indexed families, not a fixed polynomial for unbounded halting,
      and make no global minimality or priority claim.

1.1    Why bounded counter programs give such tables

Fix a finite deterministic two-counter program M , initial state q0 and halt state H. A nonhalt
instruction either increments one counter and takes its specified successor, or tests a counter for zero,
taking its zero successor when zero and otherwise decrementing it and taking its positive successor.
The other counter is unchanged. Both counters are natural integers; arbitrary loops are allowed. Make
H virtually absorbing with no counter change solely to describe a finite prefix.

Lemma 1.2 (Clipping through the last tested entry). The runs from (a, b) and (min(a, T ), min(b, T ))
have the same control states q0 , . . . , qT and the same branches in their first T transitions. Thus first
arrival at H at or before T is preserved.

Proof. Suppose the first t transitions agree, where t < T . A counter initially below T began identically
and received identical changes. A counter clipped from at least T began at T , so its representative
value at the next instruction entry is at least T − t > 0. The original equals that value plus its fixed
nonnegative initial offset along the common branches. Consequently every tested counter has the same
zero/nonzero status, and the next instruction agrees. Induction proves the claim, with the T = 0 case
vacuous. A representative may become zero after transition T while the original remains positive;
there is no next test in the specified prefix.

Positive inputs A, B represent native counters A − 1, B − 1. The shift converts the native threshold T
to K = T + 1. Define S by testing representatives (i − 1, j − 1) either for reaching H by T or for first
reaching H exactly at T . Lemma 1.2 proves (1) for all positive input sizes in both cases. If q0 = H,
the by-T table is always full, and the exact-T table is empty for T > 0. At T = 0 both predicates are
full exactly when q0 = H.
There are K 2 table bits. A direct finite construction examines the initial state and at most T transitions
from each representative: at most K 2 T native transitions, with each representative counter at most
2T . This is an effective finite preprocessing bound, not an executed interpreter in this report. It is
not free advice and does not imply a faster bounded-halting algorithm. The horizon indexes different
polynomials, with different products, factorials and table coefficients.

Each factor is nonzero. Their exact degrees, in the order of (12), are 2, 4T, 4T, 8T : pK has leading
term x2T , and its squared corner product has leading term A4T B 4T . Total degree is additive under
products of nonzero polynomials. Thus (2) is exact. The ceiling uses nI ≤ T 2 , nE ≤ 2T and nC ≤ 1,
and is attained by the full-table displayed product. For nonempty S, the coefficient of w2|S| is one, so w

distributing their product gives precisely

literal square slots: pick one squared residual from each factor and square the product of those residuals.

4     Exactly when zero native witnesses suffice

For a clipped cell a = (a1 , a2 ) define its tail positions J(a) = {ℓ : aℓ = K}. Its coordinate-flat closure
in the table consists of all cells obtained by replacing any or all coordinates in J(a) by arbitrary indices
in {1, . . . , K}, while keeping the other coordinates fixed.

Theorem 4.1 (Exact zero-versus-one native classification). Fix K ≥ 1 and S ⊆ {1, . . . , K}2 . The
following are equivalent:
  1. An integer polynomial R(A, B), with no witnesses, vanishes on positive integer inputs exactly on
     US .
  2. Every accepted cell’s coordinate-flat closure is contained in S.
  3. If (i, K) is accepted then every (i, j) is accepted; if (K, j) is accepted then every (i, j) is accepted;
     an accepted (K, K) forces the full table.
Within one polynomial equality, with fixed table-dependent coefficients and no degree bound, the minimum
number of positive existential auxiliary variables is zero when these conditions hold and one otherwise.

The quantified indices in the last condition range from 1 to K. Joint replacement at the corner is
essential. This is not the usual coordinatewise order-ideal condition: fixed coordinates smaller than K
need not be replaced.

Necessity. Suppose R vanishes on all positive integer points of an accepted cell. Fix its non-tail
coordinates at that cell’s exact values. The resulting polynomial in the tail coordinates vanishes on the
entire Cartesian product {K, K + 1, . . .}|J(a)| .
A polynomial zero on a product of infinite real sets is identically zero. For one variable this is the
finite-root theorem. Inductively, view it as a polynomial in its last variable; fixing all earlier coordinates
in their infinite sets forces every coefficient to vanish there, and the induction hypothesis makes those
coefficient polynomials zero. Therefore the restriction of R to the entire coordinate flat is identically
zero. In particular, each former tail coordinate may take any positive value below K as well. Exact
representation requires the corresponding jointly replaced clipped cells to be accepted. This argument
even allows real coefficients and makes no SOS assumption on R.

Sufficiency and the minimum. Let X1 = A, X2 = B and define

An empty sum is zero and an empty product is one. A factor vanishes exactly on its coordinate flat. If
the closure condition holds, every positive point on that flat is accepted; conversely an accepted point
makes its own cell’s factor zero. This proves exact representation. For the empty table, RS = 1. An
accepted all-tail cell forces a full table and contributes the zero factor, yielding RS = 0, as required.
If S is nonempty and has no all-tail cell, each factor is a nonzero degree-two polynomial, so the displayed
RS has degree exactly 2|S|. That degree need not be minimal. Necessity rules out zero variables if
closure fails, while Proposition 3.1 supplies one. These two observations establish the exact minimum
in the stated class.

At K = 1, both possible tables have minimum zero, represented by 0 and 1. Finite interior-only tables
also satisfy closure. For example, a single interior point admits (A − i)2 + (B − j)2 = 0 with no witness.
A single edge cell at K ≥ 2 fails closure: its tail ray cannot be cut off below K by a witness-free
polynomial equality. These examples explain why a uniform one-witness construction is not minimal
for every table.

5.2   Tensor factored gates and coefficient generation

Let m = K 2 − |S| be the rejected-cell count. Reuse the 2K factors u − i, v − i. Computing every
one-dimensional basis by multiplying its K − 1 factors and its signed binomial coefficient uses at most
2K(K − 1) multiplications. The two ranges cost 2(K − 1) more, the two classification products two
more, the rejected tensor products m, and the five final squares five. Thus

                                   multiplications ≤ 2K 2 + m + 5,                                  (23)
                           additions/subtractions ≤ 2K + 10 + max(m − 1, 0),

including u, v, factors, shifted slacks and the final sum. A uniform combined ceiling is 4K 2 + 2K + 14.
There is no division. Signed binomial constants have O(K) bits each, nodes have O(log K) bits, the
table has K 2 bits, and a straightforward gate-index encoding uses O(K 2 log(K + 1)) bits in all. These
counts do not treat large integer operands as fixed-bit objects.
For one explicit expanded-construction procedure, build the K univariate bases by successive linear
multiplication in O(K 3 ) integer coefficient operations. Sum their outer products across the table
in O(K 4 ), and square the bivariate polynomial by naive convolution in O(K 4 ). Precompute the
powers of A − r + 1 and B − s + 1 through 2K − 2. Substitute one coordinate by a separable
coefficient transform in O(K 4 ) operations, leaving O(K 3 ) entries, and then the other in O(K 5 ). The
remaining four squares are smaller. This gives O(K 5 ) integer coefficient operations after the table is
known. Triangle/product bounds, even with the coarse affine-expansion factor 34K , keep coefficient
intermediates at O(K log(K + 1)) bits. Index handling is separate; no unit-cost bit-complexity or
polynomial-in-log T assertion follows.

Form A − h, B − h and their squares for 1 ≤ h ≤ T , then multiply the T squares in each coordinate.
This costs 2T subtractions and 4T − 2 multiplications. Preparing the four witness squares in (12) costs
four subtractions and five multiplications. Interior factors add twice, edge factors once, and the corner
is already prepared. For N = |S| ≥ 1,

                                    multiplications ≤ 4T + 3 + max(N − 1, 0),                       (27)
                            additions/subtractions ≤ 2T + 4 + 2nI + nE .

The combined ceiling is 3T 2 + 10T + 7. If S is empty, return the constant one with no arithmetic.
Squaring QS adds one multiplication. The table, constants and gate indices admit an O(K 2 log(K + 1))-
bit factored description. Input-dependent values such as pK (A) require full precision, so these gates
are not a bit-time claim.
For an explicit conservative expansion bound, interior factors have at most seven monomials; edge
factors at most 6T + 5; the corner at most 1 + (2T + 1)2 + (4T + 1)2 . This follows by expanding
w2 − 2wp + p2 and the corner analogue. Their support bounds sum over S to O(K 2 ). Build pK
in O(K 2 ) coefficient operations. Each intermediate partial product has at most DS3+3 = O(K 6 )
monomials, so successive naive monomial convolutions and collection use O(K 8 ) coefficient operations,
with O(K 2 log(K + 1))-bit coefficient intermediates. A further naive self-convolution for Q2S uses
O(K 12 ) operations. These are deliberately conservative bounds, with indexing/bookkeeping separate.

7     Context, evidence, and the exact boundary

7.1    Elementary ingredients and literature context

The scaled basis in (3) is ordinary Lagrange interpolation with a common factorial denominator cleared.
NIST’s Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, Section 3.3(i), records the standard nodal formula
and basis identities [1]. Tensoring those bases prescribes a Boolean-valued table on a rectangular grid.
The underlying finite-grid vanishing principle is classical; Alon’s primary paper [2, Lemma 2.1] gives
its familiar degree-bounded finite-set form and an induction proof. The infinite-grid implication used
here follows from the same one-variable root argument, proved explicitly in Section 4.

Report 69   Low-arity finite-clipping compilers                                           4 October 2026


Conjunction by a sum of real squares and disjunction by a product of nonnegative factors are
elementary algebraic encodings. In the one-witness construction, uniqueness depends additionally on
the disjointness of the clipped cells and the positive-integer domain. These contextual observations
are not an attribution of the specific formulas or ledgers to the cited works. No exhaustive literature
survey, novelty or priority conclusion is offered.


7.2   What is and is not represented

The native theorems quantify over all positive inputs and positive integer witnesses, not a finite
sample box. Their proofs establish that domain-wide result. For bounded counter programs, clipping
transports both by-horizon and exact-first-halt semantics, including loops and initially halted programs.
The table itself remains fixed data obtained by finite preprocessing.
In particular T is an index of the family. Writing “there exists a horizon T ” outside the family does
not encode its varying acceptance table, factorials or products inside one fixed polynomial. This
report supplies no unbounded-halting fixed polynomial or single-fold MRDP result. It gives no global
witness minimum, minimum degree, minimum coefficient height, universal-polynomial record, efficient
POWER witness-height bound, or new physical simulation theorem. The exact native zero-versus-one
classification is the narrower theorem proved in Section 4; it does not classify the minimum arity of the
gap compositions.
Earlier Reports 66–68 retain their own constructions, resource bounds and scopes. The new formulas
are replacements available for comparison, not retroactive recounts or corrections to those reports. No
claim about arbitrary unencoded physical trajectories, collision horizons or physical resource costs is
made here.
