# Sparse orbit geometry and distinct-site counting at mass at most four

Exploratory successor packet, 3 October 2026. This packet assumes the reviewed orbit classification in `../higher-dimensional-particle-threshold-research-20261003/PROOF.md`; it does not modify that proof or any prior release. The conclusions below are corollaries and explicit examples, not a novelty claim. No reversibility is assumed or claimed for the examples.

## 1. Definitions and statement

Let F satisfy the classification's hypotheses: a deterministic translation-equivariant finite-radius CA on Z^d, finite alphabet, unique weight-zero vacuum, positive integer nonvacuum weights, conserved finite total mass, at most one weight-one symbol, and a fixed finite input c of mass at most four. When the unit symbol exists, let δ be its isolated displacement and set G=τ_{−δ}F. Thus G fixes isolated units and F^t=τ_{tδ}G^t. When there is no unit symbol, use G=F and δ=0.

Write

- S=⋃_{t≥0}supp(G^t(c)), the set of **distinct** visited sites
- C(N)=#(S∩[−N,N]^d), N∈N
- V(T)=#⋃_{0≤t≤T}supp(G^t(c)), T∈N

The same statements on effective definability apply to the sites visited with any specified nonzero label or subset of labels, but sharp degree statements below concern the union of all occupied sites. Repeated visits to one site contribute only once. None of these counts is a count of particle-time occurrences.

A rational affine plane means a translate of a real vector subspace spanned by integer vectors. “Finite width” means contained in that plane plus a fixed bounded set. Its rank is the subspace dimension; rank zero is allowed.

### Theorem A: stationary-frame geometry and counts

Effectively from the CA and input:

1. S is Presburger-definable, equivalently semilinear. Its characteristic generating functions are rational after partitioning Z^d into sign orthants and reflecting each into N^d. In particular, the site set is a rational subset of the additive group Z^d. Raw two-sided Laurent series need not have a common formal-series expansion, which is why the orthant convention is specified.
2. S lies in a finite-width rational affine plane of rank at most two. More precisely, it lies in finitely many exact affine translates of one rational subspace of rank at most two.
3. C(N) is effectively eventually quasipolynomial, of degree 0, 1, or 2 for a nonempty orbit. Degree zero means a finite site set. If S is infinite, then C(N)=κN^r+O(N^{r−1}), where κ>0 is computable and rational, and r∈{1,2}. The empty orbit has the identically zero counting function.
4. For independently variable integer lower and upper box endpoints, the distinct-site count is effectively **piecewise** quasipolynomial. One should not replace “piecewise” by a single multivariate quasipolynomial. Any fixed one-parameter affine family of finite integer boxes therefore has an eventual quasipolynomial count, of degree at most two, possibly identically zero or of smaller degree.

The classification distinguishes the degree precisely:

- An independent tail or a whole translated-periodic tail has degree one if any eventual occupied-site ray has nonzero drift, and degree zero otherwise
- An expanding shuttle has degree r=rank_Q{E,ν}∈{1,2}, where ν is its fixed primitive flight direction and E is the anchor displacement per complete repeated section cycle

Two nonparallel independent rays can require a rank-two containing plane while their count still has degree one. A containing-plane rank is not generally a counting degree.

### Theorem B: expanding shuttles

Choose the effective section origin so x_n=x_0+nΔ, L_n=L_0+nE, Δ>0, and let

    T_n=T_0+c n²+b n,       c>0.

Every cycle-n support lies within a fixed distance of the segment joining L_0+nE and L_0+n(E+Δν). Consequently, if E and ν are independent, the tail is within finite width of the pointed rational cone generated by E and E+Δν. In their plane this is a sector of opening strictly below 180 degrees.

Let D(K) count sites visited through the first K complete eventual cycles, including the fixed earlier prefix. Then D(K) is effectively eventually quasipolynomial and

    D(K)=αK^r+O(K^{r−1}),    α∈Q_{>0}, r=rank_Q{E,ν}.

Consequently

    V(T)=α c^{−r/2} T^{r/2}+O(T^{(r−1)/2}).

The convention at a section endpoint changes only lower-order terms. In particular the two possible orders are √T with O(1) error, or T with O(√T) error. There is no assertion that V(T) is eventually quasipolynomial as a function of ordinary time.

The first-visit time τ(y), on the domain S, is effectively piecewise rational quadratic on a finite semilinear partition. As visited sites escape every bounded set,

    τ(y)=Θ(||y||_∞²).

Thus the maximum first-visit time among S∩[−N,N]^d is Θ(N²), for an expanding shuttle. This is a coverage time for the sites that this particular orbit will ever visit, not for all lattice sites in the box.

### Theorem C: restoration of the original frame

For expanding shuttles the original-frame site set lies in finite width of span_R{E,ν,δ}, of rank at most three; rank three can really be necessary. If δ≠0, its centered-box count is Θ(N), although it need not be eventually quasipolynomial and its visited set need not be semilinear. Explicit permitted CAs below prove both failures. Independent and translated-periodic original-frame tails remain finite unions of arithmetic rays and hence semilinear.

These distinctions are essential: affine drift restoration multiplies the section cycle's quadratic clock by δ. It is not an affine change of the untimed spatial variables (n,j).

## 2. The complete phase form and the common plane

The assumed classification gives a finite prefix followed by one of three alternatives. In an expanding tail, all successful long flights in the uninterrupted repeated excursion use the same unoriented rational line. Their drift vectors may differ, including opposite signs and different speeds, but each is m_iν for some nonzero integer m_i. The finite mode retains all phases, transverse marker offsets and local encounter prefixes.

At edge i of cycle n, the two markers have positions

    L_0+nE+e_i,
    L_0+nE+e_i+(x_0+nΔ+d_i)ν+z_i,

where e_i,d_i,z_i are fixed finite data. The emitting packet starts at a bounded offset from one of these two markers. At a retained free-flight phase its sites therefore have the form

    p_i+n(E+ε_iΔν)+j m_iν,                 (2.1)

where ε_i is 0 or 1, with any constant gap/phase offsets absorbed into p_i. Its integer domain is given by finitely many linear inequalities/congruences in n and j, with 0≤j≤a_i n+b_i after splitting finitely many residues and boundary phases. Each genuine full-flight phase has a_i>0. Local encounter-prefix sites and the two passive markers have the same form without j. Finite unit labels are not assumed to be distinguishable: “left,” “right,” and the head roles here are geometric bookkeeping only.

Every vector in (2.1) belongs to p_i+span_R{E,ν}. This proves the exact finite-affine-plane statement. It also proves finite width by taking the maximum norm of the finite offsets p_i from any one reference plane. A finite initial prefix is absorbed by increasing that bound.

More is true. A long free flight starts a bounded distance from one marker, ends a bounded distance from the other, and moves by a fixed nonzero multiple of ν per period. All retained phase offsets are bounded. Hence its period samples, and its bounded within-period deviations, stay within fixed distance of the segment between the two markers. The finite prefix at an encounter has fixed duration and fixed relative support offsets, so satisfies the same bound. Markers at different edges differ from the cycle section markers by bounded vectors. This proves the segment assertion in Theorem B, without assuming that every microscopic packet step is monotone.

If E,ν are independent, the generators E and E+Δν are independent and a real linear functional can take value 1 on both. Their positive cone is therefore pointed and contained in an open half-plane of their two-dimensional span. If E is parallel to ν, the cone can degenerate to a ray or line; only rank one is asserted.

For an independent tail, taking a common phase period gives finitely many rays p+kD_i. There are at most two genuinely mobile objects: a mobile object in the stationary-unit frame has mass at least two. Thus at most two nonzero drift directions occur, although there may be many finite phase offsets. Their span has rank at most two. In the no-unit case there are at most two occupied sites and the same reasoning applies. For a translated-periodic whole tail there is one common drift, so its plane rank is at most one. This explicitly covers terminal missed flights, two independently translating dimers, compact triples that never meet their remote unit, zero-drift periodic objects and completely stationary inputs.

## 3. Semilinearity and distinct-site enumeration

Introduce y as the spatial free variable and existentially quantify n,j and the finite phase choice in (2.1), together with its linear integer domain. This is a Presburger formula for S. Use disjunction, not addition of parameter counts. Distinct choices of phase, n or j can describe the same site; existential quantification removes these multiplicities. Prefixes and arithmetic-ray tails are Presburger as well.

Effective Presburger elimination yields a finite union of lattice-polyhedral pieces or linear sets. Partitioning into sign orthants gives rational characteristic generating functions, using the classical equivalences recorded precisely in Woods [W, Theorem 1.5]. For the parameter N, the formula

    y∈S and −N≤y_i≤N for every i

has finite fibers. Presburger counting [W, Theorem 1.10] gives a piecewise quasipolynomial in N; in one nonnegative parameter the unbounded interval pieces combine into an eventual quasipolynomial. Replacing ±N by independently variable lower/upper endpoints gives the multivariate piecewise statement. These are effective constructions; no efficient complexity bound is asserted.

Every finite-width rational rank-r plane has O(N^r) lattice sites in a radius-N box: choose r coordinate projections that are independent on its direction subspace, and use bounded transverse offsets to bound the number of lattice preimages of each projected tuple. A finite union has the same bound. In an independent or translated-periodic tail, each nonconstant ray contributes Θ(N), while constant rays contribute finitely many points; this proves the degree claims there.

For an expanding tail, choose one actual free-flight phase occurring for every sufficiently large cycle, or for one fixed positive-density residue class. Its period range has length ≥an−b, a>0. All its sites with n≤ηN lie inside the box for a sufficiently small fixed η>0, except an immaterial finite start.

- If E and ν are independent, the affine map (n,j)↦p+n(E+εΔν)+jmν is injective. Summing Θ(n) sites over Θ(N) cycle values gives Ω(N²)
- If E is parallel to ν, fix one n comparable to N within that residue class. Distinct j give Θ(N) distinct sites because mν≠0. No injectivity across different cycles is used

This proves the matching Ω(N^r) lower bound. Each eventual quasipolynomial constituent therefore has degree r. Its leading coefficient is common across residues: monotonicity C(N)≤C(N+1) implies the leading coefficient at one residue is at most the next, and going once around the residue cycle forces equality. It is positive and rational. The error term follows.

The same argument applies to D(K): constrain n<K (and include any section boundary by a separate affine phase). The counted variables remain spatial y, not the cycle/flight witnesses. The formula has finite fibers, its range is O(K^r), and one long phase supplies the matching lower bound. D(K) is monotone, so again it has one common positive rational leading coefficient α.

## 4. Time and inversion, with floors retained

For a given T, let n(T) be the largest n with T_n≤T. After the finite initial interval,

    n(T)=floor((−b+sqrt(b²+4c(T−T_0)))/(2c)).

Thus n(T)=sqrt(T/c)+O(1). The sites through time T lie between the unions through adjacent complete sections. Inserting D(n(T)) and D(n(T)+1) into the preceding expansion proves Theorem B's formula for V(T). One does not obtain a smooth expansion to all orders: section and within-cycle floor oscillations remain.

There is also an exact effective representation. Within cycle n, each phase's elapsed time is affine in (n,j): all previous edge lengths are affine in n and a retained packet phase contributes pj+r. Let u=T−T_n. Count distinct sites from all earlier cycles together with current-cycle sites whose affine elapsed time is ≤u. This is a two-parameter Presburger counting function Q(n,u), and is piecewise quasipolynomial. Therefore

    V(T)=Q(n(T), T−T_{n(T)})

after adding the fixed prefix. It is Q, not the composition with the quadratic-root clock, that is asserted piecewise quasipolynomial.

For first arrival, let R(y,n,h) be the effective Presburger relation saying that site y is occupied h steps after section n, where h is the within-cycle affine phase time. Use half-open cycles to avoid duplicated section endpoints. The least n for which some h exists is a Presburger-definable function n_min(y): impose existence and absence at all earlier n. With this n fixed, the least h is also Presburger-definable. A finite-valued Presburger-definable function is piecewise rational affine on an effective finite semilinear partition. For completeness, decompose its graph into finitely many linear sets with generators (v_i,w_i), separating input and output coordinates. Every integer relation among the v_i must hold among the w_i: split the relation into its positive and negative parts, giving two graph points with the same input, and apply functionality. Thus v_i↦w_i extends to a rational linear map on their span, proving affineness on that component. Boolean refinement gives a partition. This also proves the needed effective linear bound on the first-cycle function, rather than assuming it. Hence

    τ(y)=T_0+c n_min(y)²+b n_min(y)+h_min(y)

is piecewise rational quadratic, adding singleton cells for earlier-prefix first visits.

The support bound ||y||≤K(1+n) forces n_min(y)≥||y||/K−1. Piecewise affine definability supplies n_min(y)≤K'(1+||y||), since there are only finitely many affine formulas. Within-cycle duration is O(n). These two inequalities prove τ(y)=Θ(||y||²) for unbounded visited sites. A positive rational leading coefficient in C(N) ensures that S has sites at distance at least εN inside the radius-N box for every sufficiently large N. Taking the maximum τ gives the Θ(N²) box-coverage assertion.

A simpler spatial inverse is useful: the least N with C(N)≥M is (M/κ)^{1/r}+O(1). This follows directly from C(N)=κN^r+O(N^{r−1}) and monotonicity. No claim of an eventual quasipolynomial inverse, or a floor-free all-orders expansion, is made.

## 5. Restoring δt: what survives and what does not

Every original-frame flight position equals (2.1) plus δ times its actual time. That time is T_n plus an affine function of n,j. Hence all positions lie in finite width of span_R{E,ν,δ}; the polynomial degree in n is at most two. This proves the rank-three upper bound for expanding tails. For independent/translated-periodic tails the time is affine in their common-period parameter, so merely changes the ray drift vectors; their original-frame sets stay semilinear.

If δ≠0, the expanding support satisfies

    y_F(t)=tδ+O(sqrt(t))

uniformly over its finitely many occupied sites. A site in [−N,N]^d can consequently occur only at times t=O(N). Since there are at most four occupied sites at each time, the distinct-site count is O(N).

For the matching lower bound choose a fixed small η>0 and T=floor(ηN), so every occupied site up to T lies in the radius-N box once N is large. Pick any occupied site at time T. Quiescence and radius R imply it has an occupied predecessor within R at time T−1; repeat back to time zero. This finite occupied-site ancestry path stays in the box and has bounded jumps. In a coordinate with δ_i≠0 its endpoint displacement is Θ(N), so the path contains Ω(N) distinct sites. This proves Θ(N), without assuming uniquely identifiable particles or falsely counting all particle-time occurrences as distinct.

Neither this linear-order bound nor the rank-three bound implies semilinearity or eventual quasipolynomiality. The next examples show their failure for actual globally conservative CAs.

## 6. An explicit globally conservative rank-two shuttle

Work first on Z². Use alphabet {0,u,E,W}, with weights 0,1,2,2. Here E and W are head states, not weight-one velocities; u is the unique unit symbol. Let e_1=(1,0), e_2=(0,1).

A head at a is **eligible** iff no other head occurs at max-norm distance at most 4. Each eligible head applies one of these rewrites, with all coordinates not listed left unchanged:

1. E at a, 0 at a+e_1: replace these by 0 at a and E at a+e_1
2. E at a, u at a+e_1, 0 at a+2e_1+e_2: replace by W at a, 0 at a+e_1, u at a+2e_1+e_2
3. W at a, 0 at a−e_1: replace by 0 at a and W at a−e_1
4. W at a, u at a−e_1, 0 at a+e_2 and a−e_1+e_2: replace by 0 at a and a−e_1, E at a+e_2, u at a−e_1+e_2

If its applicable guard fails, a head does nothing. Conditions 1/2 are exclusive, as are 3/4. An ineligible head does nothing. Extra units are never implicitly cleared: every written destination required to be empty is tested explicitly. Symbols outside the listed write sites, including extra noise and inactive heads, are unchanged.

This is a well-defined radius-6 CA. Every rewrite writes inside a+[-2,2]². Distinct eligible heads have distance greater than 4, so these write cubes are disjoint. An output at x need only inspect candidate heads within distance 2 and their radius-4 head-isolation tests, all within distance 6 of x. Each rewrite preserves the exact sum of weights of its written sites. Simultaneous disjoint rewrites therefore preserve the total weight on every finite configuration, including malformed configurations with many heads or extra units. Vacuum is quiescent and an isolated u is fixed. This supplies the full-shift conservation argument; no unproved “particle compiler” is being invoked.

Start with u at (0,0), u at (k,0), E at (1,0), where k≥3. There is always one head, so eligibility never fails. Inductively the cycle sections are exactly

    L_n=(0,n),    R_n=(k+n,n),    head E at (1,n).

The eastward head takes k+n−2 free steps, applies rule 2 once, takes k+n−2 westward free steps, then applies rule 4 once. Thus the cycle duration is 2(k+n−1) and

    T_n=n²+(2k−3)n.

Every row n eventually has exactly the visited x-coordinates 0,1,...,k+n. The right marker may reach the next row before the other two objects, but it lands at that next row's endpoint. Hence the **entire distinct visited set** is exactly

    S={(x,y)∈Z²: y≥0, 0≤x≤k+y}.             (6.1)

This realizes E=e_2, ν=e_1 and Δ=1. It proves degree two and rank two are sharp. For N≥k,

    C(N)=Σ_{y=0}^N (min(N,k+y)+1)
        =[N²+(2k+3)N+2+k−k²]/2.             (6.2)

No finite union of fixed-width lines can cover (6.1), since their box counts are O(N), whereas (6.2) is quadratic. The example has positive planar density 1/8 in centered square boxes. It does not explore a half-plane.

## 7. Degenerate degree examples and full-set nonsemilinearity

Degree zero is realized by one stationary unit. Degree one is realized by an isolated E moving east under the rule above. These examples have mass one and two respectively.

For a rank-one **expanding** example on a horizontal line, change both rule 2's vacancy test and its new-u destination from a+2e_1+e_2 to a+2e_1. Change rule 4 to require W at a and u at a−e_1, then switch W at a directly to E without moving that neighboring u; remove the two former destination-vacancy tests, since this is now an in-place switch. Leave the eligibility convention and the guards of rules 1 and 3 unchanged. These are the complete changed guards and writes. The same disjoint-rewrite proof applies. Its sections are L_n=(0,0), R_n=(k+n,0), head E at (1,0), with the same T_n. Its distinct visited set is the nonnegative horizontal ray, and V(T)=sqrt(T)+O(1). Therefore V(T) is not an eventual quasipolynomial even in the stationary frame: a nonzero quasipolynomial cannot have genuinely square-root order.

Now compose this horizontal-rule CA G with the vertical shift F=τ_{e_2}G. Its isolated unit drift is δ=e_2, so normalizing F recovers G. Its full visited set is

    S_F={(x,t): x∈supp(G^t(c)), t≥0}.

Every horizontal row has exactly three sites, x≥0, all x=O(sqrt(t)), while x is unbounded. This set is not semilinear. Indeed in any nonempty linear component of a putative semilinear decomposition, a period vector (a,b) cannot have b<0 because S_F has no negative rows. If b=0 it must have a=0 because each row is finite. If b>0 then a must be zero because along its multiples |x|/t→|a|/b, contradicting the uniform sublinear bound. Thus every period is vertical, and finitely many linear components would bound x, contradicting unboundedness. The argument concerns the full occupied-site union, not only a selected rare label or section subsequence.

The count of this particular nonsemilinear set can nevertheless be eventually polynomial. Nonsemilinearity alone must not be cited as a counterexample to box quasipolynomiality; a separate example follows.

## 8. Exact nonquasipolynomial box count in the original frame

Return to the planar wedge CA of §6 and set F=τ_{e_2}G, shifting **within the same plane**. At a time t in cycle n, put σ(t)=0 before that cycle's right bounce and σ(t)=1 afterward. The three original-frame positions have heights

    left: t+n,       head: t+n,       right: t+n+σ(t).

Each object's height is strictly increasing; it advances by 2 at that object's one upward move per cycle, and by 1 otherwise. The head and left marker have identical height sequences and different x-coordinates (head x≥1, left x=0).

There are no cross-time overlaps with the right-marker trajectory either. Before its right bounce, its height is the head's height at the same time and its x-coordinate is strictly larger. After its right bounce, its height can equal only the head's height at the next time, or be a missing head height at the final left bounce. In the former case the right marker's x-coordinate is k+n+1, while the head's next x-coordinate is at most k+n−1. The head does not move onto either marker. Thus the three full visited trajectories are pairwise disjoint, not merely distinct at the same time.

The skipped heights for the left marker and head are

    a_n=T_n+n−1=n²+(2k−2)n−1,        n≥1.

The right bounce finishes at t=T_n+k+n−1, giving skipped right-marker heights

    b_n=T_n+k+2n−1=n²+(2k−1)n+k−1,  n≥0.

Each height sequence starts at 0 and contains every nonnegative integer except its displayed skipped sequence. For sufficiently large N the box's horizontal constraint is inactive at all heights ≤N. An explicit sufficient threshold is N≥max(k+1,2): if a right-marker x-coordinate is k+j then its height is at least (j−1)²+(2k−1)(j−1)+k, so x>N cannot occur below height N when N≥k+1; the head's x-coordinate is no larger. The more conservative threshold N≥2k+4 is also sufficient and is used by the audits.

Define

    A(N)=max(0, floor(sqrt((k−1)²+N+1))−(k−1)),
    B(N)=max(0, 1+floor((−(2k−1)+sqrt((2k−1)²+4(N−k+1)))/2)).

For N≥2k+4, the exact distinct-site count is

    C_F(N)=3(N+1)−2A(N)−B(N).                 (8.1)

The square-root expressions count the quadratic skipped-height sequences, including their stated n ranges. It follows that

    C_F(N)=3N−3sqrt(N)+O(1).                  (8.2)

This is not eventually quasipolynomial. If it were, its linear growth and limit C_F(N)/N→3 would force every constituent polynomial to be 3N plus a constant. The difference C_F(N)−3N would then be bounded, contradicting (8.2). Equation (8.1), with its bounded floor oscillations, is the exact statement; no floor-free higher-order expansion is implied.

This gives a single actual finite-range conservative CA with unique unit symbol and mass-four input whose original-frame occupied-site set is nonsemilinear and whose centered-box count is not eventually quasipolynomial.

## 9. Rank-three sharpness after drift restoration

Embed the planar wedge CA in Z³ with its rewrites in e_1,e_2 directions, using the same radius-4 head-isolation convention in max norm on Z³. The full conservation/locality proof is unchanged. Compose with shift δ=e_3. At the section times T_n its support contains

    (0,n,T_n),       (k+n,n,T_n).

Suppose its full visited set were within bounded distance of an affine plane of rank at most two. There would be a nonzero linear functional ℓ(x,y,z)=ax+by+cz bounded on the site set (constant affine offsets do not affect boundedness). On the first displayed sequence,

    ℓ(0,n,T_n)=bn+c[n²+(2k−3)n]

is bounded only if c=b=0. On the second sequence the remaining value a(k+n) is bounded only if a=0. This contradicts nonzero ℓ. Thus no finite-width rank-two affine plane suffices. The statement rules out every such plane, not merely one guessed orientation.

In fact, even finitely many bounded-width rank-two planes do not suffice. The eastward flight contains (1+j,n,T_n+j), 0≤j≤k+n−2. On these points a nonzero normal ℓ gives c n²+(b+c(2k−3))n+(a+c)j+a. If a+c≠0, a fixed bounded strip for ℓ contains only O(1) values of j for each n. If a+c=0, the expression is an unbounded nonconstant polynomial in n, since otherwise a=b=c=0, so the strip contains none of the flight for all sufficiently large n. A finite collection of strips therefore covers only O(1) of the Θ(n) flight sites. Every bounded-width affine plane lies in one such strip. The rank-three upper bound is sharp even against finite unions of rank-two tubes.

## 10. Relation to the close two-pebble literature

These geometric consequences should be situated alongside the older pebble results, not advertised as unprecedented geometry. Lacalle–Gajardo [LG, printed p.2] explicitly describe earlier two-pebble trajectories as confined to a conical region, with two-dimensional zigzag coverage; their p.5 discusses expanding oscillations versus periodic behavior, and their conclusion on p.17 extends their geometric arguments to Z^d. Their main theorem concerns real-time trace recognition, a different statement from this packet's fixed-input site counting and CA classification.

Blum–Sakoda [BS, printed p.148] state sector exploration below a half-plane, and impossibility of exploring an entire half-plane with one finite automaton and two pebbles. The pointed-cone result in §2 is consistent with and close to that geometry. Delorme–Mazoyer [DM] is the central historical reference; its publisher abstract and the explicit [LG] account were checked, but its complete text was not retrieved for this packet. No priority inference is justified by that limitation.

Our mass accounting and global examples use one finite-state mass-two head and two **identical** mass-one markers. The previous CA proof covers more general splitting/fusion and independent-packet tails; it must not be silently identified with a pebble model without that argument. Conversely, the standard semilinearity-to-counting step is existing Presburger mathematics, not a new counting theorem.

## 11. Reproducibility and limitations

`audit.py` implements both explicit rule variants, a cellwise radius-6 evaluation strategy that reuses the same action predicates, cycle-clock and exact-site checks, malformed-configuration weight-conservation samples, and the exact count (8.1). `audit-results.json` and `audit-results-optimized.json` record ordinary and `python -O` runs. All correctness checks use explicit `RuntimeError` guards, including a deliberate failing-guard self-test, and the two typed JSON receipts are byte-identical. The cellwise/global agreement tests check evaluation strategy and locality; they are not independently reimplemented transition-rule logic. Finite tests are audits of transcription and arithmetic; the disjoint-write argument is the unrestricted conservation proof.

The examples are weighted-alphabet CAs, not binary CAs and not reversible CAs. They do not prove binary or reversible sharpness for the rank-two cone or nonquasipolynomial count. All effectivity statements are fixed-input and inherit the classification's promise assumptions. No polynomial-time bound, uniform timed Presburger description, universal exploration algorithm, or new universality threshold is claimed.
