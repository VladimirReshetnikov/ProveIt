                   A smaller sufficient radius with exact trajectory preservation

                                                4 October 2026

                                                    Abstract

          We separate a short geometric recognition radius from a longer contextual-control radius in
      a parallel conservative involution. If local involutions write radius b, recognition reads radius a,
      and control reads radius r, with b ≤ a ≤ r, isolation at distance H = max{2(b + a), b + r} and a
      finite prospective recognition test give a full-shift involution of radius at most b + a + H. The proof
      preserves all recognition and selection decisions, including initially rejected anchors and cooperative
      candidate births.
          Applying this lemma to the literal binary five-particle compiler, while retaining its phase block,
      gives sufficient radius 108D + 149 + 3J in place of Report 26’s 180D + 258 + 9J. The encoding, every
      admissible forward and inverse trajectory, exact clocks, reflections, halt observer and clean-target
      construction are unchanged. The inherited universal-source ledger yields 55 027 013 instead of
      91 711 698, approximately a forty percent reduction in the proved upper bound. This is a new
      full-shift rule; an explicit four-particle input distinguishes it from Report 26. A separate complete
      finite certificate leaves only the five shifts among the 428 conservative binary five-input tables.
      Neither result is a priority or minimum-radius claim. No new-rule simulator, universal template
      array, arithmetic evaluator or universal-source execution is supplied.


1    Objects and principal result

A configuration is x ∈ {0, 1}Z . A particle is a site whose bit is one. A cellular automaton (CA)
is a translation-equivariant map with a finite, uniform local radius. Number conservation means
preservation of the ordinary number of ones on every finite-particle configuration. We do not equate
divergent sums on infinite inputs.
The starting point is Report 26’s two-block rule F26 = P26 ◦ E26 . Its finite endpoint templates come
from the earlier guarded-swap compiler. We retain those literals, their type-specific write windows,
their guards and their signed five-particle encoding. We change only the edge block’s full-shift selection
mechanism, and retain P26 exactly.

2.3   Recognition invariance on arbitrary inputs

Fix any potential recognition anchor v, whether initially recognized or absent. Its read interval Ia (v)
meets at most one selected write interval: meeting those at distinct u, w would imply

                               |u − w| ≤ |u − v| + |v − w| ≤ 2(b + a) ≤ H,

contradicting isolation. After the simultaneous update, the word read by ρ( · , v) is therefore its original
word or exactly its word after a single selected Tu . In the latter case the prospective test at u preserves
its value. Consequently R(Ax) = R(x) on the full shift. This explicitly excludes cooperative births or
deaths caused jointly by different selected replacements.


2.4   Every selection decision is invariant

Consider any recognized isolated anchor u, including one whose prospective test fails. Every selected
w ̸= u is another recognized anchor and hence satisfies |w − u| > H. Since

                                      H ≥ b + r,          H ≥ 2b + 2a,

its write interval misses both Ir (u) and Ib+2a (u). Therefore every other selected update leaves the full
local-control and prospective-comparison data at u unchanged.
If u is unselected, no update at u occurs either. Its prospective test remains false, so it stays unselected.
If u is selected, the test data after all updates are those in Tu x. Because Tu is an involution, testing
there interchanges the two sides of
                                             R(Tu x) = R(x).
The equality remains true. Recognition invariance preserves isolation. Nonisolated recognized anchors
remain nonisolated, and absent anchors remain absent. Hence S(Ax) = S(x), including every rejected
status.
A recognized isolated anchor with Tu x = x passes prospectivity and is selected, but writes nothing. Its
local identity choice remains unchanged because other selected writes miss Ir (u). Thus identity actions
are covered rather than tacitly removed from the selected set. The proof does not assert preservation
of every internal guarded-template label; it preserves exactly the recognition and selection data needed
for inversion.

2.5      Involution conservation and radius

At the second application, the same anchors are selected. The local-control word at each selected u
contains its own first image and no modification from any other selected write. The involution Tu
therefore restores that write block. All other sites remain fixed, proving A2 = id.
Disjoint equal-weight finite replacements conserve ordinary finite mass. In particular, on a finite input
a nonidentity replacement requires at least one input particle in its block; only finitely many such
disjoint blocks can act. Translation covariance is inherited from the predicates and translated local
maps.
For an output coordinate i, only anchors in Ib (i) can write it. Isolation at such an anchor reads radius
H + a, local control reads r, and prospectivity reads max{r, b + 2a}. But

                                       H + a ≥ r,      H + a ≥ b + 2a.

Thus the output depends only on Ib+H+a (i), proving (6). This finishes Theorem 2.1. No finite-support
reduction, source semantics, empirical trajectory argument or proof-assistant execution is used.

4     The source and five particle geometry

4.1   The complete source interface

The source is a finite deterministic partial injection on Q × Z2≥0 . Its m controls include a designated
halt control h with no outgoing branch. A branch e : q → q ′ selects the left or right counter, with
side v ∈ {−1, +1} and index j, changes it by ∆ ∈ {−1, 0, +1}, and has a Boolean domain guard Ge .
Guards use finitely many tests cj = k or cj > k. Enabled updates have natural outputs. Branch
domains at each source control are disjoint, and actual branch images at each target control are disjoint,
on all natural-counter IDs, including unreachable ones.
The exact image predicate is

                               Ie (c′ ) = Ge (c′ − ∆ej ) ∧ (c′ − ∆ej ∈ Z2≥0 ).                            (9)

Fix J ≥ 0 such that every domain and every exact image predicate is determined by the classes
0, 1, . . . , J, > J of both counters. Write p for the number of nonzero-update branches and α for the
number of zero-update branches. We reserve a for the recognition radius. Other controls may be stuck
on some IDs; stuckness at a nonfinal control is not designated halting.
These are the authenticated compiler’s mathematical source assumptions. In particular, an exact
reverse guard cannot be replaced by the forward guard. The construction below specifies finite Boolean
predicates directly; no source parser or compiler program is executed to justify them.


4.2   Signed modes and encoding

signed mode a distinct gap d(u± ) ∈ {1, . . . , D}, where

                  D = 2m + 4p,         S = 2D + 2,         B2 = D + 1,        L = 3D + 4,
                                                                                                         (10)
               b = B3 = L + D + 1 = 4D + 5,            K = 3b + 1,        Z = 10b + 10 + 2J.

A head at anchor x consists of {x, x + d(u± )}. Its anchor is always the leftmost head particle, also
during leftward motion. At origin zero the home encoding is

                         Enc(q, cℓ , cr )± = {−(Z + cℓ ), 0, Z + cr , S, S + d(Hq± )}.                   (11)

The three singletons are unlabelled left endpoint, origin and right endpoint. Their gaps are at least Z.
The head is the unique pair at separation at most D; all head-to-marker distances exceed D. The
configuration has exactly five particles in a binary zero background. Neither an extra track nor a
spatial parity pattern, external phase schedule or clock particle is present.

 1. Dispatch the home head from o + S to mode Oe at o + vS, using Ge on old counters
 2. Move the outbound anchor by v until it reaches m − vS
 3. Replace marker m by m′ and change to Ie at m′ − vS

 4. Move the inbound anchor by −v until it reaches o + vS

The unsigned admissible set contains all homes in Q×Z2≥0 and only the strict intermediate configurations

The doubled set contains both signs of every such node and all translations.
Intermediate nodes retain the branch label. Outbound modes retain old counters, inbound modes

unsigned micrograph is therefore a partial injection. For every unsigned edge c → c′ , its doubled edge

6     Agreement on every admissible state

The global involution proof is independent of source semantics. To retain the intended computation,
we must additionally prove that all intended local transitions pass the new filters. Guarded type
uniqueness alone does not prove the required guard-free anchor statement. We establish it directly
from the literal templates.


6.1   Every raw match uses the actual head

At every admissible node there is exactly one particle pair with gap at most D, the head. Markers
are separated by at least Z; head-marker distances are at least S − D = D + 2 > D. Every endpoint
template has exactly one close pair, so every raw match must use that actual head. Its distinct gap
determines the signed mode. A triple’s remaining displayed particle must be one of the three markers.
A fixed triple has diameter at most 2b < Z, but that observation alone would not exclude different
types using different nearby markers. The literal formulas give the stronger fact that every triple
places its singleton within L + 1 of the actual head anchor. Because Z > 2(L + 1), at most one marker
can lie in that range. The signed mode, side and distance then determine the possible travel type,
subject to the partitions below. The third marker cannot supply a remote alternative.
Other markers lie outside the intended triple’s exactness window. For a current-marker anchor their
distances are at least Z > K. For a reverse endpoint’s old-marker anchor they are at least Z − 1 > K,
since the actual marker moved by at most one. All intended triple exactness conditions therefore hold.
Free-pair exactness is addressed separately by its own radius L.


6.2   Homes and same anchor branch alternatives

At a plus home Hq+ , only forward dispatch or direct types with source q have the correct signed gap.
Their home endpoint has head anchor u + S and singleton u, forcing the integer anchor to be the
origin. At a minus home Hq− , only reverse commit or direct types with target q match, again at that
origin. No moving free pair, moving travel triple or other interaction has the same signed gap.

The raw recognized set is empty or the singleton origin. Its guarded set Co (x) is empty or a singleton:

If reverse commit is enabled at post counters c′ , predicate (9) supplies a genuine natural predecessor

is identity, which is harmless.

6.4   Interaction walls and old marker anchors

The four moving walls have these forced interactions:
 1. Outbound minus at t = S: reverse dispatch at the origin A
 2. Outbound plus at t = N − S: forward endpoint at the old selected marker m = C
 3. Inbound plus at t = N − S: forward commit at the origin C
 4. Inbound minus at t = S: reverse endpoint at the old marker coordinate m = m′ − v∆
The last anchor is not generally the actual current marker m′ . In (16), its singleton has offset v∆ and
its head anchor has offset v∆ − vS. Both force the same old anchor in the reverse orientation.
For the matching mode, the candidate singleton location forces precisely the stated wall. Reverse
outbound dispatch requires a marker at x − vS, forward outbound endpoint at x + vS, inbound commit
at x − vS, and reverse inbound endpoint a current marker at x + vS. In the oriented corridor those are
respectively the behind or ahead wall indicated above. The third marker cannot occupy a competing
contact location. Branch-specific signed Oe /Ie gaps exclude all other branch interactions. The corridor
tables exclude travel at the same wall.
Reverse dispatch retains the old counters of its enabled source edge, so Ge is true there. Forward
commit retains that edge’s post counters, so its exact Ie is true. Endpoint interactions need no
contextual guard. Thus every admissible moving node has exactly one raw anchor and exactly its
intended guarded type there. The definition of admissibility, restricting intermediates to enabled
subdivisions, is essential to this guard argument.


6.5   The two endpoints keep the same recognized anchor

Every intended microedge has two admissible signed endpoints. Their invariant raw anchor is:
• The predecessor head anchor for free travel
• The contacted unchanged marker for triple travel
• The unchanged origin for dispatch, commit and direct transitions
• The old endpoint-marker coordinate for endpoint transitions in both orientations
At an intended edge of type g and anchor u, the previous arguments give

                        R(x) = {u} = R(τg,u x),        Cu (x) = {g} = Cu (τg,u x).

At homes several raw types can coexist, but they still count as just this one anchor. The local mutual-
uniqueness test therefore chooses the intended transposition. Isolation and prospective raw-anchor
invariance pass automatically. At a missing edge the local maps are identity whether or not raw
recognition remains. Hence E∗ equals the intended doubled edge matching on every admissible input.
For the retained phase block, a home has its unique home-phase triple. A moving state has the
free phase pair when every marker is farther than L from its unchanged head anchor, and otherwise

10.1    Conservation is an integer graph potential

The binary order-four de Bruijn graph has sixteen four-bit vertices. A five-bit word abcde is an edge
from abcd to bcde, with weight
                                     g(abcde) = f (abcde) − c.
The graph is strongly connected: appending the four bits of a target vertex reaches it from any vertex.

Lemma 10.2 (Conservation criterion). The binary table f is number-conserving on finite configurations
if and only if there is an integer potential p on the vertices, normalized by p(0000) = 0, such that

                                      f (abcde) = c + p(bcde) − p(abcd)                                (25)

on every edge.

Proof. Conservation first forces f (00000) = 0, since the zero configuration must remain zero. Fix a
periodic binary word and place M copies between infinite zero tails. Except at a bounded number of
boundary sites, the difference of output and input particle counts is M times the sum of g over one
period. Boundary contributions are bounded independently of M . Conservation gives 0 = M δ + O(1)
for all positive M , so δ = 0.
Every directed closed walk of the graph gives overlapping windows of a periodic binary word, so its
total weight is zero. Define p(v) as the weight of a directed path from 0000 to v. If two paths are
chosen, append the same directed return path to 0000. Both closed walks have zero weight, and the
path weights coincide. Thus p is well-defined and integer-valued. Appending one edge proves (25).
Conversely, the zero self-loop in (25) gives f (00000) = 0. For a finite configuration, summing output
minus input bits telescopes the differences of consecutive four-bit-window potentials. Both tails
eventually equal 0000, so the sum is zero. This proves finite number conservation.

10.4    What the classification does and does not imply

A nontrivial computationally universal binary, uniform, time-independent, number-conserving, full-
shift-reversible CA in this symmetric-radius convention must therefore have radius at least three. This
does not construct a radius-three universal example, classify larger neighborhoods or close the gap
to the sufficient upper bound in (1). It does not apply unchanged to partitioned, time-dependent,
second-order, nonuniform or larger-alphabet models.
Schranko and de Oliveira’s 2011 paper [6] studies reversible number-conserving rule derivation and
representation. A bounded renewed search verified its bibliographic entry on the author’s publication
list but did not obtain its full text; the publisher route was unavailable. Accordingly this report does
not attribute a precise five-input classification to that paper or claim priority over it. The result here
is an independently certified project appendix. Morita’s 96-state four-neighbor construction [7] is a
distinct model and supplies no binary five-particle radius bound.

13     Further mathematical directions

The remaining large scale comes primarily from unary gap encoding of the signed head modes:
D = 2m + 4p. Reducing the source mode count or replacing that finite-mass head encoding would be
a different construction and could change exact clocks. The present result improves the coefficient
without changing those objects.
Two immediate technical directions remain. First, type-dependent geometric separation might reduce
a common exclusion bound further, but any argument must protect all isolated rejected controls and
exclude cooperative recognition births, not merely keep current writes disjoint. Second, a practical
evaluator or arithmetic certificate should prove complete candidate discovery on arbitrary malformed
multihead inputs; valid-orbit simulation tests alone would not establish that contract. Formalizing the
anchor and signed-corridor arguments could help prevent confusion between current and predecessor
head anchors or between current and old marker anchors.
The completed conclusion is narrower and exact: the same five-particle admissible dynamics admits a
new conservative binary full-shift extension, composed of two involutions, with the sufficient radius
in (1). The universal number remains an inherited-ledger substitution and the radius-two result
remains an independent finite classification.
