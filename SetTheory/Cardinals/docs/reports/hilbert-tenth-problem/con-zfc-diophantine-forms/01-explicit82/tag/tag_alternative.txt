4. FIXED BINARY TAG APPENDANT, WITHOUT MATERIALIZING ITS BITS
Let kA=419662080, lambda=2kA+2=839324162,
b=30lambda=25179724860,
sigma=1+(300(lambda+1)+1)(b-1)=6340185447580320129960,
a=b sigma=159644125131448413623112242805600.
For a positive integer X, write G_n(X)=sum_{i=0}^{n-1} X^i, with G_0=0.
This is an exact finite sum; when X>1 it equals(X^n-1)/(X-1).
Let X=3^b.
For each letter x<kA let p(x)=[p_0,...,p_{m_x-1}] and
ell_x=m_x(lambda+1)-1.
For x!=1 define
V_x=sum_{j=0}^{m_x-1} X^[j(lambda+1)+2(p_j+1)-1]
       + X^ell_x G_{sigma-ell_x}(X).
All exponents and range bounds are nonnegative fixed integers by the production length bound.
Define the fixed appendant content
U0=G_sigma(X) G_{b/2-kA}(9)
   +sum_{0<=x<kA, x!=1} 3^[b-2(x+1)] V_x.
This is a finite sum of nonnegative integers, with completely specified finite productions and bounds.
It is exactly the ternary content (least significant first) of the binary appendant u.
Its length is a. Its first bit is1; hence U0 mod3=1.

Derivation:
Every odd phase has a zero track. The even phases0,2,...,b-2kA-2 have all-one tracks.
The phase b-2(x+1) has the track enc(p(x)).tail followed by enough ones to reach sigma, except x=1 whose track is all zero.
A letter y encodes as0^{2(y+1)} 1 0^{lambda-2(y+1)}, of length lambda+1, so the track's one positions are exactly those used in V_x.
Rows interleave tracks, so phase phi and row j contribute3^[phi+bj].
This proves the displayed U0 formula directly from TagBinary91.lean lines75-103.

Proof of the additional elimination:
Original RH+1=H+Rv gives R(H-v)=H-1>=0, hence t=H-v>=0.
Substitution yields H=Rt+1 and v=(R-1)t+1.
Conversely these formulas imply RH+1=H+Rv identically. K0>=9 and D>=1 make R>=9, so H,v,q are positive for every t>=0.
The other eliminated quantities are positive by section8.
Sum of squares vanishes over integers iff each residual vanishes.
This reduction is elementary algebra, newly supplied here, not a claimed source Lean theorem.
There is no claim of absolute minimality among all Diophantine representations.
For solutions over all integers rather than naturals, replace each x_l by four new squares, giving88 integer variables; this is usually worse than specifying the natural-number domain explicitly.
