\begin{abstract}
We combine the repository's blank-input K215 compiler, signed Pell
arguments, and root-amalgamation constructions.  Four witnesses---the
main-root quotient and three Pell roots---are replaced by a single
integer primitive element.  The result is an explicit polynomial in
\textbf{twelve strictly positive unknowns}, of degree \textbf{2601},
evaluated by \textbf{167 binary arithmetic operations}.  A three-equation
version has maximum degree 2592 and 163 operations.  The proof includes
a rational-ordinate integrality lemma and explicit separation estimates
for all sixteen algebraic branches; a resultant alone would not suffice.
We also give unrestricted-integer variants and reduce the local compiler
alphabet from 37,075 to 5,252 tiles.  Compact synchronization anchors
reduce the fixed base from $2^{5^{18}}$ to $2^{5^{16}}$, a factor of 25
in the bit length of $B-1$.  With one coherent tuple of the four fixed
coefficients, ZFC proves that absence of solutions is equivalent to
$\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})$.  This is a mathematical reduction using explicitly
identified repository theorems, accompanied by exact finite checks; the
new Diophantine bridge has not been formalized in Lean or Rocq.  No
global optimum or proof of consistency is claimed.
\end{abstract}
