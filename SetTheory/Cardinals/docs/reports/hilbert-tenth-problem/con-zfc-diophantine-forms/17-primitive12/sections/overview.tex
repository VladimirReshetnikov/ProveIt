\section{Result, comparison, and proof boundary}
\label{sec:overview}

The goal is an equation with \emph{fixed integer coefficients}, whose
solvability is equivalent to the existence of a finite ZFC refutation.
We work at the immutable repository snapshot
\begin{center}\small\texttt{f0ae4e0991ca44a7bb4e5671d42e33d775e48035}.\end{center}
The seven packages in the supplied comparison directory are numbered
09--15 \cite{RepoComparison}.  They already combine a number of independent improvements,
so a useful successor must specify which size criterion improves.

\begin{theorem}[Main positive-domain result]
\label{thm:main}
The coefficient recipe in Section~\ref{sec:compiler}, and the finite
arithmetic recipe in Section~\ref{sec:primitive}, specify a polynomial
$\mathcal P_{12}\in\Z[J,F,\alpha,z,h,i,T,s,w,\eta,\zeta,t]$ such that
\[
\mathrm{ZFC}\vdash
\left(\neg\exists (J,F,\alpha,z,h,i,T,s,w,\eta,\zeta,t)
 \in\Z_{>0}^{12}\;\mathcal P_{12}=0\right)
\ \longleftrightarrow\ \Con(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
The polynomial has total degree 2601 and a division-free circuit of
167 binary operations.  Multiplication by a fixed numeral is counted;
reuse of an already computed value is free.  The equivalent
three-equation system has 163 operations and maximum degree 2592.
\end{theorem}

Here and below, ``ZFC proves'' refers to the ordinary mathematical
proofs, internalized in ZFC and composed with the repository's calibrated
K215 machine theorem.  We supply no newly checked proof-assistant
derivation of the Diophantine construction.  In particular, the argument
does not infer a ZFC theorem merely from the external soundness of Lean.

The comparison uses the reported predecessor figures, together with

a system means its maximum residual degree.

The reduction from thirteen to twelve unknowns is a genuine improvement
within this explicit compiler family.  It costs degree and operations.
The 67-operation polynomial remains the preferred expression when
arithmetic evaluation size is the objective; it can use the new
coefficient tuple unchanged.  Likewise, this work does not supersede
the 32-variable quartic obtained by summing the squares of the existing
23 quadratics.

There is a separate coefficient improvement.  A local pruning argument
and new synchronization positions give
\[
37\,075\longrightarrow 5\,252\quad\text{tiles},\qquad
B=2^{5^{18}}\longrightarrow 2^{5^{16}}.
\]
The exact bit length of $\beta=B-1$ decreases from
$3\,814\,697\,265\,625$ to $152\,587\,890\,625$.
Both coefficient tuples remain astronomically large; the archive
contains their exact finite definitions, not their expanded numerals.

\subsection{The inherited interface}

The four constants are $\beta=B-1$, $K=D_C+BD_R$, $M_C$, and
$M_F^*=M_F^{\rm nat}+B-1$.  The last port is the \emph{shifted} mask.
They must be taken from one complete, proved coefficient recipe.
Their numerical mask conditions include
\begin{gather*}
B=2^d,\quad d\ge4,\qquad
0<M_C,M_F^{\rm nat}<B-1,\\
M_C\equiv2\pmod4,\qquad M_F^{\rm nat}\equiv4\pmod8,\qquad
\pc(M_C)+\pc(M_F^{\rm nat})=d.
\end{gather*}
These conditions alone do not make an arbitrary tuple a compiler:
the local-language, convolution, synchronization, and completeness
proofs are also needed.  Section~\ref{sec:compiler} supplies the adapter
for the new tuple.

The normalized positive core has fifteen witnesses
\[
J,F,\alpha,z,f,h,i,T,s,w,\tau,\eta,\zeta,y,\gamma>0.
\]
Its first and main Pell equations, strong equation, and auxiliary
equation are
\begin{align}
\tau^2-\ell(\ell+k)&=1, &
(X+ac+\gamma H)^2-\Delta c^2&=1,\label{eq:old-first-main}\\
f^2-\Delta i^2c^4&=1, &
Q V^2-(Q-1)y^2&=1,\label{eq:old-strong-aux}
\end{align}
where all abbreviations are defined in the next section.  The remaining
conditions are the index and transport equations $N_k=N_t=1$.
The established blank-input compiler says that this core is solvable
if and only if K215 halts from its blank tape.

Root reconstruction need not recover positive $f,\tau,y,\gamma$.
For that reason we use the signed \emph{ordinary} core proved in package
12, \texttt{ORDINARY\_BLANK\_PROOF.md}.  It allows
$h,f,T,\tau,y,\gamma\in\Z$ while keeping
$J,F,\alpha,z,i_{\rm ord},s,w,\eta,\zeta$ positive.  Its strong equation is
\[
(i_{\rm ord}c^2)^2=\Delta(f^2-1).
\]
The normalized core maps into it by $i_{\rm ord}=\Delta i$.
Its product theorem also permits the two outer factors to be equal
signs in $\{1,-1\}$ at an intermediate stage.  That theorem subsequently
recovers the positive signs and the actual blank computation.  This
precise interface is used below; the stronger canonical restrictions
``$i$ odd'' and ``$w$ a square'' are needed only for the later
unrestricted-integer constructions.

\subsection{What the result does and does not settle}

``Simplest'' is not a total ordering of Diophantine descriptions.
Number of unknowns, degree, circuit size, coefficient height, and proof
length conflict.  The new construction improves two of these criteria
and supplies explicit tradeoffs for the others.
Classical nine-unknown representation results already exist
\cite{Jones1982,BayerDavid2025}; twelve is therefore not a global record
for existential representability.  The contribution here is a fully
specified specialization of the inspected K215/native compiler, with
concrete finite coefficient data and reproducible arithmetic.
Nor does the equivalence prove consistency or inconsistency of ZFC.
Under the usual hypotheses, consistency prevents ZFC from proving its
own consistency; a two-sided undecidability claim requires additional
care about soundness and is not asserted here.
