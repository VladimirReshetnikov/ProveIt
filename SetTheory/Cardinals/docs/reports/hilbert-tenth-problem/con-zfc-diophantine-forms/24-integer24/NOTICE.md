# Attribution and provenance

This is an AI-assisted mathematical synthesis prepared in response to
Vladimir Reshetnikov's request for simpler Diophantine formulations of the
consistency of ZFC. It is intended as a reviewable research contribution
for the ProveIt repository. The preparation date is 22 October 2026.

The new exposition, circuit refactorings, coefficient adapter, scripts and
proof arguments were produced with ChatGPT assistance. They have not been
independently peer reviewed by a named human mathematician. The separate
arithmetic and mathematical audits included in the package are part of
that AI-assisted research process.

The inherited material comes from
<https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt> at commit
`ae3007ca4467011e0e051c84acb4a1dd13971b0e`. The exact source paths and hashes
are in `evidence/SOURCE_PINS.json` and
`evidence/BASELINE_PROVENANCE.json`. Byte-identical source copies are
preserved separately from the newly bound or optimized circuits.

The ProveIt repository's MIT No Attribution license is retained in
`licenses/LICENSE.ProveIt`. The new contribution is supplied for use and
incorporation on those same permissive terms, to the extent any rights
attach to it. This does not replace the licenses or attribution of
third-party material.

The calibrated K215 machine and its proof lineage include material with
additional attribution. The upstream files `licenses/NOTICE_K215.md`,
`licenses/LICENSE.Wade`, `licenses/NOTICE-normalized-source.txt` and
`licenses/NOTICE-original245.txt` are preserved without alteration. Their
terms and attribution continue to apply to the associated material.

No new end-to-end proof-assistant verification is claimed. The copied Lean
endpoint is a provenance record; its surrounding project was not rebuilt
as part of this delivery. The report distinguishes the new ordinary
mathematical proofs, exact finite algebra checks, numerical diagnostics,
and inherited semantic dependencies.
