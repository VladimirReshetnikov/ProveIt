\begin{abstract}
\wtag\ Three manuscripts represent a whole terminating computation by a
\emph{fixed number of finite polynomials} that satisfy fixed linear
identities (in one case quadratic identities that become linear once a
single shared stride is fixed). In each, a clock forces the complete
witness tuple to be unique whenever it exists, every coefficient of that
witness is zero or one, and its degree or support is an exact running-time
function for which no computable input-only bound exists.

Part~I (source~06) compiles a deterministic radius-one cellular automaton
with $s$ symbols into affine-linear systems over $\N[X,Y]$ with
$s^3+s+5$ polynomial unknowns and $3s+3$ rows, or
$3\lceil\log_2s\rceil+6$ unit-coefficient rows, or nine weighted rows, all
with the same full solution tuples; a solution exists exactly at a first
singleton acceptance. Part~II (source~15) answers Part~I's one-coordinate
question: coefficient-bounded univariate linear systems are finite-state
decidable, so no universal linear compiler over $\N[X]$ has computably
bounded witness heights; eleven identities of degree two with one shared
stride $S$, forced to equal $X^{m+2h+2}$, replace it, with witnesses in
bijection with Part~I's. Part~III (source~01) compiles a two-counter
machine with $q$ states and $d$ zero tests into $q+d$ linear identities in
$q+2d$ polynomial unknowns with omitted-variable sorts, valid over every
nonzero commutative ring; it then derives an exact integrality gap
(integer minimum $1$, rational infimum $0$) and a Hilbert-space operator
with dense nonclosed range whose basis-vector membership is computably
enumerable complete.

Polynomial unknowns are not ordinary integer witnesses: nothing here is a
fixed-arity single-fold or finite-fold Diophantine representation over
$\N$, and the repository's 75- and 87-operation bounds are unchanged.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The three Parts}\label{pwh:sec:parts}

This report is built from three research manuscripts delivered with
batch~79 of ProveIt's incoming reports (manuscripts~06, 15 and~01;
Appendix~\ref{pwh:sec:provenance} gives the full provenance). They are
called \emph{source~06}, \emph{source~15} and \emph{source~01} after their
batch-79 manuscript numbers, which are also the file prefixes of their
shipped programs and data. Each carries the author line ``Research report
prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov'' and names no assistant. Source~15 is a
direct sequel of source~06: it cites source~06's file by name and answers
one of its research questions. Source~01 was written independently of both,
from another snapshot of the repository, and shares no lemma with them.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Part~\ref{pwh:part:uh} (source~06), \emph{Unique histories
from linear equations}.} For a deterministic radius-one cellular automaton
with $s$ symbols, a quiescent blank and an accepting alphabet, a matrix
over $\Z[X,Y]$ independent of the input and of the running time has a
solution $U\in\N[X,Y]^{s^3+s+5}$ exactly when the evolution has a first
singleton acceptance; the whole tuple is then unique, Boolean-coefficient,
with exact degrees $h$, $m+2h+1$ and $m+3h+1$
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:main}). The direct form has $3s+3$ rows; an
exact marginal projection and injective features give
$3\lceil\log_2s\rceil+6$ unit-coefficient rows or nine weighted rows with
the same solution tuples (Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:features},
Corollary~\ref{pwh:uh:cor:feature-counts}). A fixed matrix is
$\Sigma^0_1$-complete on an unambiguous family
(Corollary~\ref{pwh:uh:cor:hard-intro}); a single quadratic over the same
semiring has the same solutions (Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:quadratic}).
\item \textbf{Part~\ref{pwh:part:oc} (source~15), \emph{The price of one
coordinate}.} Bounded-height solvability of univariate affine-linear
systems over $\N[X]$ is decidable by a finite-state argument
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:finite-state}), so no universal univariate
linear compiler can guarantee a computably bounded witness height
(Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:no-linear}): Part~I's one-coordinate question
(Section~\ref{pwh:uh:q:onecoord}) has a negative answer. Eleven identities
of degree at most two in $s^3+s+7$ unknowns over $\N[X]$, every nonlinear
monomial containing one stride unknown $S$, replace the linear design; two
of them force $S=X^W$ with $W=m+2h+2$ (Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:main}), and
the full solutions are in bijection with Part~I's
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:bijection}).
\item \textbf{Part~\ref{pwh:part:bt} (source~01), \emph{Linear boundary
transport}.} Over any nonzero commutative ring $R$, a deterministic
transition graph has a finite-support transport equation whose fibre is the
halting history plus the cycle-constant kernel
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:unclocked}); a clock indeterminate $Z$ makes it
injective and the $0/1$ history unique (Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:clocked}).
Two-counter machines with $q$ states and $d$ zero tests compile to $q+d$
linear identities in $q+2d$ polynomial unknowns whose sorts omit the tested
variable (Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:compiler}), and a row tag $W$ makes one
equation with a computably enumerable complete accepted set
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:fixed}). The associated energy has integer
minimum~$1$ but rational infimum~$0$ on nonhalting sources
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:gap}), and the Hilbert-space operator has dense
nonclosed range with c.e.-complete basis-vector membership
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:range}).
\end{itemize}

Each Part keeps its own definitions, letters and numbering of statements.
Parts~I and~II share the cellular semantics, the ray-clock mechanism and
the worked four-symbol example; Part~III has a different substrate, a
different witness ring and a different uniqueness mechanism, and is placed
here because of the spine of Section~\ref{pwh:sec:spine}.

polynomials whose degrees and supports grow with the running time, and the
identities they satisfy are linear in them (Part~II: of degree two, with
every product through one stride unknown, and linear in the remaining
unknowns once the stride is fixed, Proposition~\ref{pwh:oc:prop:fibre}).

Three features recur. First, the no-bound results
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:nobound}, Corollaries~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:nobound}
and~\ref{pwh:bt:cor:bound}) are all the bounded-search argument of
Proposition \texttt{cdc:bd:prop:nobound} in~\cite{cdc}, with polynomial
degree, stride or support in place of an integer witness height; no novelty
is claimed for the argument. Second, every Part separates its normal form
from the older undecidability of linear equations over polynomial semirings
(Narendran~\cite{uh:Narendran}; Dong~\cite{uh:Dong} for a decidable single
homogeneous equation) and does not claim it. Third, every Part warns that a
fixed number of polynomial unknowns is not a fixed number of ordinary
integer unknowns; the research programme's reviews repeat the warning
(Section~\ref{pwh:sec:relation}).

Watch for these readings:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fixed arity.} ``A fixed number of unknowns'' counts
\emph{polynomials of unbounded support}, never integers. A polynomial
unknown hides an unbounded list of exponents and coefficients.
\item \textbf{Unique, single-fold.} Uniqueness is uniqueness of the
complete polynomial tuple, including every clock, mask and boundary
auxiliary. It is not an ordinary single-fold Diophantine representation:
canonically coding the tuple as an integer and applying MRDP can introduce
auxiliary integer witnesses of uncontrolled multiplicity (Sections~\ref{pwh:uh:sec:barriers}
and~\ref{pwh:bt:sec:mrdp}).
\item \textbf{Boolean coefficient.} Every coefficient is $0$ or $1$;
arithmetic is ordinary semiring or ring arithmetic, not arithmetic in a
Boolean ring. In Parts~I and~II Boolean coefficients are a
\emph{conclusion} of the shape lemmas, never a hypothesis.
\item \textbf{Linear.} Parts~I and~III are linear in the unknowns. Part~II
is quadratic, every product passing through the stride $S$; its fixed-stride
fibres are linear and decidable (Proposition~\ref{pwh:oc:prop:fibre}).
\item \textbf{Degree.} The \emph{witness degree} (coordinate degree of a
solution) grows with the running time; the \emph{unknown-degree} of the
identities is $1$, $2$ or~$4$. Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:no-linear} is a
statement about unknown-degree~$1$ in one coordinate.
\item \textbf{Clock.} In Parts~I and~II a clock is a pair of unknown
polynomials (endpoint and prefix) forced by the ray lemma. In Part~III the
clock is an indeterminate $Z$ of the coefficient ring, not an unknown.
\item \textbf{Diophantine.} The title of source~01, \emph{Linear
Quasi-Diophantine Universality}, refers to polynomial-valued unknowns with
prescribed sorts, not to ordinary integer unknowns (its abstract and
Section~\ref{pwh:bt:sec:mrdp} say so).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relation to neighbouring reports, the formal project and the research programme's reviews}\label{pwh:sec:relation}

This report sits in the collection's \texttt{hilbert-tenth-problem}
category. No earlier report of the collection treats polynomial-semiring or
polynomial-sort witnesses; its neighbours have natural-number unknowns.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Canonical Diophantine certificates~\cite{cdc}, Part~IX.} The
one-history relation (\texttt{cdc:eq:onehistory}), the equivalence of
finite-fold representability with a finite-fibre universal halting
polynomial (Theorem \texttt{cdc:bd:thm:universal}) and the absence of a
computable witness-height bound (Proposition \texttt{cdc:bd:prop:nobound})
are the boundary that all three Parts stand outside. Part~III's
Section~\ref{pwh:bt:sec:mrdp} restates that boundary without citing it,
and the three no-bound results are the polynomial analogues of
\texttt{cdc:bd:prop:nobound}; editorial notes in the Parts say so.
Part~IV of~\cite{cdc}, \emph{Polynomial trajectories}, shares only a name:
its trajectories are integer-valued polynomial functions of time with
natural-number witnesses.
\item \emph{Stochastic and thermal exactness~\cite{ste}.} Batch-79
manuscripts~10 and~14 (archives \path{Coercive_Green_Diophantine.zip} and
\path{Well_Conditioned_Diophantine_Computation.zip}) were placed there by
commit \texttt{bbaf322e5}, for its Part~V on coercive Green operators. They
build fixed sparse operators from counter machines in the opposite regime
to Part~III's Section~\ref{pwh:bt:sec:hilbert}: a uniformly coercive operator
instead of one with zero in its continuous spectrum. A note after
Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:range} records this.
\item \emph{Smooth Diophantine finalizers~\cite{sdf}}, opened by the same
placement as this report, post-processes finite systems of degree at most
two over $\N$ into one quartic with a hypersurface smooth over $\Z$. Such
systems occur here only as the externally bounded exports of
Proposition~\ref{pwh:uh:prop:bounded} and
Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:boundedexport}; neither source uses it, and it does
not apply to equations whose unknowns are polynomials.
\end{itemize}

\emph{The research programme's reviews.} The Hilbert's-tenth-problem
research programme (\path{Computability/HilbertTenthProblem}, read-only for
this report) reviewed all three manuscripts, byte for byte the archives
placed here; this report does not present its placement as a first review.
\begin{itemize}

written construction on its stated domain, replays the three author suites,

\item Source~01: \cite{reviewBS}, commit \texttt{49bc4c654}. It finds the

history); the tested repair is
\path{linear_boundary_exact_boolean_guards.patch}. The theorems, stated for
the two Boolean modes, are unaffected.
\item Both reviews are indexed in~\cite{reviewIX} (commit \texttt{c00ce825a}).
\item Source~15 had only been inventoried as pending (commit
\texttt{b927b2290}) when it was placed here; it was reviewed afterwards

\end{itemize}

\section{The question and the result}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:intro}

A finite computation can be certified by introducing variables for each
successive state. The number of variables then grows with the chosen time
horizon. A different question is whether an entire, arbitrarily long
computation can be represented by a fixed number of algebraic objects while
retaining a precise correspondence between computations and complete
witnesses. Here the algebraic objects are finite polynomials: the number of
unknown polynomials stays fixed, whereas their degrees and supports grow
with the computation.

The main technical issue is not merely to encode local transition rules.
It is to prevent additional tiles, detached components, multiplicities,
extra blank padding, independently chosen clock lengths, and arbitrary
accepting continuations from creating spurious or duplicate witnesses.
The construction below resolves these issues within a particularly simple
syntax: affine-linear equations over the semiring $\N[X,Y]$.

\subsection{Main normal-form theorem}

Throughout, $\N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. An accepting event means that at least one
cell carries a symbol from a designated set $\F$. A \emph{first singleton
acceptance} is a first accepting event at which exactly one cell is
accepting. This condition is automatic for the usual one-head simulation
of a deterministic accepting Turing machine, but is not automatic for an
arbitrary cellular automaton.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical linear history compiler]
\label{pwh:uh:thm:main}
Let $\A$ be a finite alphabet of size $s$, with blank symbol $0$. Let
$f:\A^3\to\A$ satisfy $f(0,0,0)=0$, and let
$\F\subseteq\A\setminus\{0\}$. There is an explicitly constructible matrix
\[
  \mathsf A_{f,\F}\in
  \Z[X,Y]^{(3s+3)\times(s^3+s+5)}
\]
and an effective encoding of each nonempty finite word $w$ as a vector
$\mathsf b(w)$ of zero--one coefficient polynomials such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\mathsf A_{f,\F}U=\mathsf b(w)$ has a solution
      $U\in\N[X,Y]^{s^3+s+5}$ precisely when the evolution from $w$, with
      blank exterior, has a first singleton acceptance.
\item Whenever a solution exists, the complete tuple $U$ is unique and
      every coefficient in every component of $U$ is zero or one.
\item If the first acceptance time is $h$ and the input length is $m$, then
\[
 \max_i\deg_Y U_i=h,\qquad
 \max_i\deg_X U_i=m+2h+1,\qquad
 \max_i\deg U_i=m+3h+1.
\]
\item Every nonzero matrix entry has at most two monomials, with
      coefficients in $\{-1,1\}$ and total degree at most three.
      The matrix depends only on $f$ and $\F$, not on $w$, $m$, or $h$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem is proved in Sections~\ref{pwh:uh:sec:compiler}--\ref{pwh:uh:sec:correctness}.
It is a statement about all solutions, not only about a specially
constructed class of well-behaved solutions. No coefficient bounds,
monomial predicates, support bounds, or time indices are added as hidden
side conditions.

\begin{corollary}[A fixed unambiguous hard instance family]
\label{pwh:uh:cor:hard-intro}
For one fixed choice of $f$ and $\F$, deciding whether
$\mathsf A_{f,\F}U=\mathsf b(w)$ is solvable over $\N[X,Y]$ is
$\Sigma^0_1$-complete. Every input word in this family has either no solution
or exactly one solution, and any solution has only zero--one coefficients.
\end{corollary}

The direct form of Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:main} is convenient for understanding
soundness. Section~\ref{pwh:uh:sec:features} proves two stronger row counts with
\emph{exactly the same complete solution tuples}: $3\lceil\log_2s\rceil+6$
rows retaining unit coefficients, or nine rows allowing coefficients of
magnitude at most $s-1$ for $s\ge2$. All conclusions about uniqueness,
Boolean coefficients, and degrees transfer unchanged.

The proof uses a direct one-head Turing-machine simulation, not an
assumption that an arbitrary cellular rule is universal. The construction
of that simulation is given in Section~\ref{pwh:uh:sec:universality}.

\subsection{What is and is not new here}

Narendran proved undecidability of systems of linear equations over
polynomial semirings, already with one indeterminate, in 1996
\cite{uh:Narendran}. Thus neither polynomial-semiring undecidability nor
monomial forcing is claimed as a new idea. In particular, the elementary
power-forcing equations used below have a predecessor in his construction.

Lohrey and Steinberg encode Turing machines by tilings to obtain
undecidable subsemimodule membership for finite-rank free
$(\Z\times\Z)$-modules, and related group-theoretic results
\cite{uh:LohreySteinberg}. Their zero-tiling-sum formulation permits multiple
tiles at a location. It is a close conceptual precedent for interpreting
linear equations as translated local constraints.

The contribution developed here is the combined, explicitly counted
normal form: a fixed low-degree matrix, exact shape recovery from
conservation, first-acceptance canonicalization, uniqueness of every
auxiliary polynomial, forced zero--one coefficients, and an exact degree
ledger. These properties are proved directly. We have not established that
this precise combination is absent from all earlier literature, and do
not present the theorem as a resolution of a named longstanding
Diophantine conjecture.

Dong's work provides a useful contrast: a single homogeneous linear
equation over $\N[X]$ admits polynomial-time decision procedures for the
nonzero-solution problem he studies \cite{uh:Dong}. This is not a decision
procedure for arbitrary inhomogeneous systems. The contrast here is
therefore not a claimed one-variable/two-variable undecidability threshold.
Two coordinate indeterminates are used because they retain the actual
space--time geometry transparently.

\section{Semantics and notation}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:semantics}

\subsection{Polynomials are the unknown values}

Let
\[
  \Sring=\N[X,Y]\subset\Z[X,Y].
\]
An element of $\Sring$ is a finite sum
$P=\sum_{i,j\ge0}p_{ij}X^iY^j$ with $p_{ij}\in\N$.
The symbols $X,Y$ are fixed coordinate indeterminates, not existentially
quantified numbers. An equation is an identity of polynomials, equivalent
to equality at every coefficient.

A linear system means
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}(X,Y)U_j(X,Y)=b_i(X,Y),
  \qquad 1\le i\le r,
  \label{pwh:uh:eq:linear-semantics}
\end{equation}
where the $a_{ij},b_i$ are supplied integer-coefficient polynomials and
$U_j\in\Sring$ are unknown. There are no products $U_jU_k$ in a linear
system. Negative coefficient terms in~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:linear-semantics} may be
moved to the opposite side, so this is equally an equality between two
expressions formed with addition and multiplication by fixed elements
of $\Sring$.

We call a representation \emph{single-fold over $\Sring$} when every
admitted input has exactly one full tuple of $\Sring$-witnesses, and every
other input has none. This terminology does not change the domain to
$\N$. One polynomial variable contains an unbounded finite amount of data.

Write
\[
 \rep{r}=1+X+\cdots+X^{r-1},\qquad \rep{0}=0.
\]
For a nonnegative polynomial, its mass is
$\wt(P)=P(1,1)$, the sum of all coefficients. A polynomial is called
\emph{Boolean-coefficient} when all its coefficients belong to
$\{0,1\}$; no Boolean-ring arithmetic is intended. We use $\deg 0=-\infty$ for total
and coordinate degrees, so maxima over tuples with zero components are
unambiguous.

\subsection{The cellular evolution}

Let $w=w_0\cdots w_{m-1}\in\A^m$, $m\ge1$. The initial configuration is
\[
 c_0(i)=\begin{cases}w_i,&0\le i<m,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \qquad i\in\Z.
\]
Subsequent configurations satisfy
\begin{equation}
 c_{t+1}(i)=f(c_t(i-1),c_t(i),c_t(i+1)).
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:ca}
\end{equation}
Quiescence gives the elementary light-cone bound
\begin{equation}
 c_t(i)=0\quad\text{if }i<-t\text{ or }i>m-1+t.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:cone}
\end{equation}
Indeed, a cell outside the larger interval at time $t+1$ has three blank
predecessors at time $t$. This proves~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:cone} by induction.

We distinguish \emph{configuration rows} from \emph{transition rows}.
Transition row $t$ contains the triples whose centres are
\[
 i=-t-1,-t,\ldots,m+t.
\]
There are
\begin{equation}
 w_t=m+2t+2
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:width}
\end{equation}
such triples. The two extreme centres are blank guard cells at time $t$;
their outputs are allowed to become nonblank at time $t+1$. We encode the
centre $i$ in this row at horizontal exponent
\begin{equation}
  j=i+t+1,\qquad 0\le j<w_t.
  \label{pwh:uh:eq:shear}
\end{equation}
Thus a cell remaining at physical position $i$ shifts one place to the
right when moving to the next encoded row. This is the source of the
factor $XY$ in the vertical equations.

\section{A finite nonnegative flow forces a clock}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:clocks}

The following elementary lemma is the main mechanism for obtaining exact
shape information from linear equations. Its power-forcing special cases
are familiar from polynomial-semiring undecidability constructions
\cite{uh:Narendran}; the translated form is convenient for the present proof.

\begin{lemma}[Ray-clock lemma]
\label{pwh:uh:lem:ray}
Let $S=X^aY^b$ and $M=X^pY^q$, where $a,b,p,q\in\N$ and $p+q>0$.
For $U,V\in\Sring$, the identity
\begin{equation}
 U+V=S+MV
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:ray}
\end{equation}
holds if and only if, for some $h\in\N$,
\begin{equation}
 U=SM^h,\qquad V=S\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}M^t.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:ray-sol}
\end{equation}
The integer $h$ is uniquely determined, and equals $\wt(V)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Evaluation at $(1,1)$ gives $\wt(U)=1$. Hence $U$ is a monomial with
coefficient one. Regard the coefficients of $V$ as nonnegative integer
flows along directed edges
\[
 (i,j)\longrightarrow(i+p,j+q)
\]
in $\N^2$. Equation~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:ray} says that the unique source is at
$(a,b)$ and the unique sink is at the exponent of $U$. Every vertex has at
most one predecessor and one successor, and total degree strictly
increases along every edge.

For completeness, this flow description can be checked purely by
coefficient induction. Before the source, and on every chain not meeting
the source, the incoming coefficient is zero. A positive outgoing
coefficient cannot appear there without a source; a sink there would
force a negative coefficient. At the source the outgoing coefficient is
one unless the sink is the source itself. That coefficient remains one
along its unique successor chain until the sink is reached. Since $V$
has finite support, this chain must terminate at the sink. There can be
no other flow. It follows that the sink is $SM^h$ and that precisely the
first $h$ edges of this ray carry coefficient one. This is
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:ray-sol}. Conversely, those expressions satisfy
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:ray} by telescoping. Their mass and uniqueness statements follow
immediately.
\end{proof}

Two aspects matter. First, finiteness of support excludes an infinite
unterminated flow. Second, nonnegativity excludes a compensating signed
flow on another chain. Ordinary scalar evaluation of the equation does
not retain either of these coefficientwise arguments.

\section{The complete compiler}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:compiler}

We now list every unknown and every equation. The list itself is the
compiler; no separate validity predicate is attached to its output.

\subsection{Unknown polynomials and linear forms}

For each triple $\tau=(\ell,c,r)\in\A^3$, introduce a polynomial
$Z_{\ell c r}$. Its intended coefficient at $X^jY^t$ is one when that
triple occurs at transition site $(j,t)$, and zero otherwise. Introduce
$T_a$ for every $a\in\A$ to record the final configuration, with one blank
guard at either end. Introduce five further polynomials
\[
 D,\ Q,\ B,\ V,\ J.
\]
These are all the unknowns, giving $s^3+s+5$ in total.

For $a\in\A$, use the following abbreviations for \emph{linear forms},
not additional unknowns:
\begin{align}
 \LL_a&=\sum_{\ell=a}Z_{\ell c r}, &
 \CC_a&=\sum_{c=a}Z_{\ell c r},\label{pwh:uh:eq:marginals1}\\
 \RR_a&=\sum_{r=a}Z_{\ell c r}, &
 \OO_a&=\sum_{f(\ell,c,r)=a}Z_{\ell c r}.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:marginals2}
\end{align}
Indices not specified under a sum range independently over $\A$.
Also put
\begin{equation}
 I_a(X)=\sum_{\substack{0\le j<m\\ w_j=a}}X^j,
 \qquad G=V+B+Q+D.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:input-ghosts}
\end{equation}
The $I_a$ are supplied data, and $G$ is another linear form.

\subsection{Equations}

The two clocks are
\begin{align}
 D+Q&=X^{m+1}+X^2YQ, \tag{C1}\label{pwh:uh:eq:C1}\\
 B+V&=1+YV. \tag{C2}\label{pwh:uh:eq:C2}
\end{align}
The left-neighbour marginal equations, one for every $a\in\A$, are
\begin{equation}
 X\CC_a+\epsa V=\LL_a+\epsa XQ.
 \tag{L$_a$}\label{pwh:uh:eq:L}
\end{equation}
The right-neighbour marginal equations are needed only for nonblank
symbols:
\begin{equation}
 X\RR_a=\CC_a\qquad (a\in\A\setminus\{0\}).
 \tag{R$_a$}\label{pwh:uh:eq:R}
\end{equation}
The vertical equations, one for each symbol, are
\begin{equation}
 \CC_a+T_a=XI_a+XY\OO_a+\epsa G.
 \tag{V$_a$}\label{pwh:uh:eq:V}
\end{equation}
Finally, acceptance is enforced by
\begin{align}
 \sum_{a\in\F}\CC_a&=0, \tag{F0}\label{pwh:uh:eq:F0}\\
 \sum_{a\in\F}T_a+J&=B+XJ. \tag{F1}\label{pwh:uh:eq:F1}
\end{align}
All equalities are polynomial identities, and every listed unknown ranges
over $\Sring$.

\subsection{Why the matrix is fixed}

Move the supplied terms $X^{m+1}$, $1$, and $XI_a$ to the right-hand
vector, leaving every unknown on the left. The remaining coefficient
polynomials depend only on the alphabet, the rule table, and the
accepting set. Typical entries are $1-X^2Y$, $1-Y$, $X-1$, $1-XY$,
$1-X$, $X$, and $-1$. For an individual tile variable there is at most
one positive and one negative occurrence in any row. Thus each entry has
at most two monomials with coefficients of magnitude at most one, and
total coordinate degree at most three.

The apparent input-dependent coefficient in a naive boundary construction
would be $X^{m+1}Q$. It does not occur here. Instead, the right-boundary
clock is \emph{started} at $X^{m+1}$ in the supplied constant term of
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:C1}. All subsequent shifts are multiplication by the fixed
monomial $X^2Y$. This is the step that makes the matrix independent of the
input length.

\section{Shape and soundness}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:correctness}

\subsection{The clocks synchronize without a separate equation}

\begin{lemma}[Forced boundary and transition region]
\label{pwh:uh:lem:shape}
Every solution of the complete system has a uniquely determined
$h\in\N$ for which
\begin{align}
 D&=X^{m+1+2h}Y^h,
 &Q&=\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}X^{m+1+2t}Y^t,\label{pwh:uh:eq:DQ}\\
 B&=Y^h,
 &V&=\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}Y^t.\label{pwh:uh:eq:BV}
\end{align}
Moreover, the total tile polynomial $H=\sum_{\ell,c,r}Z_{\ell c r}$ is
\begin{equation}
 H=\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}Y^t\rep{m+2t+2}.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:Hshape}
\end{equation}
Consequently, at every site in this region exactly one tile polynomial
has coefficient one, and all tile coefficients outside the region vanish.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:ray} separately to \eqref{pwh:uh:eq:C1} and
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:C2}. This gives~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:DQ} for one integer $h$ and gives
$B=Y^k$, $V=\sum_{t<k}Y^t$ for a possibly different integer $k$.

Summing all equations~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:L} over $a$ uses
$\sum_a\CC_a=\sum_a\LL_a=H$ and yields
\begin{equation}
 XH+V=H+XQ.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:sumL}
\end{equation}
Evaluation at $(1,1)$ gives $k=h$. Thus the two clocks have the same
height without a separately paid synchronization variable or row.

Rearrange~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:sumL}:
\[
 (1-X)H=V-XQ
  =\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}Y^t(1-X^{m+2t+2}).
\]
Since $(1-X)\rep{r}=1-X^r$, the displayed expression for $H$ satisfies
this equation. It is the only such polynomial, because $1-X$ is not a
zero divisor in $\Z[X,Y]$.

Every coefficient of every $Z_{\ell c r}$ is nonnegative. Their sum is
one at each site of~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:Hshape}, and zero elsewhere. Therefore
exactly one tile is present at each permitted site, with coefficient one;
no tile can occur elsewhere or with multiplicity greater than one.
\end{proof}

This argument establishes occupancy rather than assuming it. In
particular, it rules out disjoint extra computations and translated closed
components: their nonnegative coefficients would alter the forced total
$H$.

\subsection{The terminal region is also forced}

\begin{lemma}[Forced top row]
\label{pwh:uh:lem:top}
Every solution satisfies
\begin{equation}
 K:=\sum_{a\in\A}T_a=Y^h\rep{m+2h+2}.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:topshape}
\end{equation}
At each site of this row exactly one terminal symbol occurs with
coefficient one; every terminal coefficient elsewhere is zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each triple has exactly one central symbol and exactly one output under
$f$. Hence $\sum_a\CC_a=\sum_a\OO_a=H$. Also,
$\sum_a I_a=\rep{m}$. Summing~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:V} gives
\begin{equation}
 H+K=X\rep{m}+XYH+G.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:sumV}
\end{equation}
By~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:DQ}--\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:BV},
\begin{equation}
 G=\sum_{t=0}^{h}Y^t(1+X^{m+2t+1}).
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:Gshape}
\end{equation}
The coefficient row at $Y^0$ on the right of~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:sumV} is
$X\rep{m}+1+X^{m+1}=\rep{m+2}$. For $1\le t\le h$, that row is
\[
 X\rep{m+2t}+1+X^{m+2t+1}=\rep{m+2t+2}.
\]
There are no other rows. Subtracting the already known $H$ leaves exactly
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:topshape}. This argument includes $h=0$, when $H=0$ and the
initial guarded row is already the top row. Nonnegativity now proves the
one-symbol-per-site assertion just as in Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:shape}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Neighbour matching is exact}

The omitted blank right-neighbour row is not a missing constraint.
Summing~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:R} over $a\ne0$ and subtracting that sum from
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:sumL} gives
\begin{equation}
 X\RR_0+V=\CC_0+XQ.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:Rzero}
\end{equation}
Thus, with the blank boundary terms restored, right-neighbour matching
holds for every symbol.

\begin{lemma}[The tile triples are genuine consecutive windows]
\label{pwh:uh:lem:windows}
Fix a transition row $t<h$, and write its unique tile at position $j$ as
$(\ell_j,c_j,r_j)$, for $0\le j<w_t$. Then
\[
 \ell_0=c_0=0,\qquad c_{w_t-1}=r_{w_t-1}=0,
\]
and for $1\le j<w_t$,
\[
 \ell_j=c_{j-1},\qquad c_j=r_{j-1}.
\]
In particular, the tile at each site is the actual length-three window
of the row of centre symbols, with blank exterior.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At an interior position $j$, coefficient comparison in \eqref{pwh:uh:eq:L}
gives the equality of the one-symbol indicators of $c_{j-1}$ and
$\ell_j$. The right-neighbour rows, including~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:Rzero}, give
the equality of the indicators of $r_{j-1}$ and $c_j$. Since the
occupancy lemma has already proved that these are single symbols, the
indicator equalities imply the asserted equalities of symbols.

At position zero, \eqref{pwh:uh:eq:L} forces $\ell_0=0$ and the right-neighbour
rows force $c_0=0$. At the coefficient immediately after the last tile,
the boundary monomial in $XQ$ forces $c_{w_t-1}=0$ in the left rows and
$r_{w_t-1}=0$ in the right rows. All boundary exponents agree with
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:width} by~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:DQ}. Finally, the interior equalities also
give $r_j=c_{j+1}$ wherever that expression is internal to the row.
\end{proof}

It would not be sound to deduce equality of joint symbol pairs from
marginal counts before proving occupancy. Here occupancy comes first;
there is exactly one tile, not a distribution or a multiset, at a site.

\subsection{Vertical matching recovers the actual evolution}

\begin{lemma}[Semantic reconstruction]
\label{pwh:uh:lem:reconstruct}
For each $t<h$, the coefficient of $X^jY^t$ in $\CC_a$ is one exactly
when
\[
 c_t(j-t-1)=a,\qquad 0\le j<w_t.
\]
The coefficient of $X^jY^h$ in $T_a$ is one exactly when
$c_h(j-h-1)=a$, for $0\le j<w_h$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
When $h>0$, the $Y^0$ part of~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:V} is
\[
 \CC_a\big|_{Y^0}=XI_a+\epsa(1+X^{m+1}).
\]
This is precisely the input configuration with the two source guards.
By Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:windows}, every tile in this row contains the true
neighbourhood of its centre. Its assigned output is therefore the true
cellular output. Multiplication by $XY$ moves that output to the next
encoded source row and shifts its exponent by one, as required by
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:shear}. The two new boundary terms in $G$ supply the blank
source guards for that row. Inducting in $t$ recovers all transition
rows.

At time $h$, there are no centre terms because $H$ has no $Y^h$ row.
Equation~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:V} therefore places the last outputs, together with
the guards, in the $T_a$. This proves the terminal assertion. If $h=0$,
the same equation directly reads
$T_a=XI_a+\epsa(1+X^{m+1})$, which proves the assertion without a
transition row. Bound~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:cone} and quiescence ensure that the
omitted infinite exterior is genuinely blank, not an unverified part of
the computation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Acceptance and all auxiliary witnesses}

\begin{lemma}[First acceptance and the horizontal marker]
\label{pwh:uh:lem:acceptance}
Every solution encodes a first singleton acceptance at time $h$. If its
accepting cell has encoded horizontal position $j_*$, then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{a\in\F}T_a=X^{j_*}Y^h,
 \qquad J=Y^h\rep{j_*},
 \qquad 1\le j_*\le m+2h.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:J}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All summands in~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:F0} are nonnegative. Thus no centre row before
$h$ has an accepting symbol. Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:reconstruct} identifies
these with the genuine configurations, including their blank exterior.

Apply Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:ray} to~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:F1}, with source $B=Y^h$,
multiplier $X$, and sink $\sum_{a\in\F}T_a$. It yields exactly the first
two formulas in~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:J}. There is consequently one accepting cell
at time $h$, not merely a selected cell among several. The two top
boundary cells are blank by the vertical reconstruction and $0\notin\F$,
which gives the stated bounds on $j_*$. The marker $J$ is uniquely forced;
there is no slack in its choice.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completeness and uniqueness}

\begin{proof}[Completion of the proof of Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:main}]
Suppose first that the genuine evolution has a first singleton acceptance
at time $h$. Define $D,Q,B,V$ by~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:DQ}--\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:BV}. At every
transition site $(j,t)$ with $t<h$, put coefficient one in the unique
$Z_{\ell c r}$ corresponding to the actual neighbourhood of physical
centre $j-t-1$. Put coefficient one in $T_a$ at $(j,h)$ according to the
actual guarded final row. Define $J$ by~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:J}. All unspecified
coefficients are zero.

The clock equations telescope. The spatial equations express exact
adjacency and the blank source boundaries. The vertical equations express
the input row, the deterministic rule, and the two new guards at each
stage. The absence of earlier acceptance gives~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:F0}, and the
single terminal accepting cell gives~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:F1}. Thus this tuple is a
solution, proving completeness.

Conversely, the preceding lemmas reconstruct a genuine first singleton
acceptance from every solution. The first accepting time of a
deterministic evolution is unique. Hence every solution must have the
same $h$. Its clocks are then fixed, every transition tile and terminal
symbol is fixed by the actual evolution, and $J$ is fixed by the location
of the accepting cell. There is therefore exactly one full solution
tuple. All its components are Boolean-coefficient by the construction and
by the shape and clock lemmas. The matrix-size and coefficient assertions
were checked in Section~\ref{pwh:uh:sec:compiler}; the exact degree assertions
are proved in Section~\ref{pwh:uh:sec:resources} below.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two accepting cells are not one accepting certificate]
If the first accepting configuration contains two or more accepting
cells, the system has no solution. A later time with only one accepting
cell cannot help, because \eqref{pwh:uh:eq:F0} detects the earlier event. For
arbitrary cellular automata this restriction is part of the specified
language, not an omitted case in the theorem. One-head machine encodings
satisfy it automatically.
\end{remark}

\section{An exact marginal projection}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:projection}

A more direct tile formulation would use the ordered pair of symbols on
each side of a tile as its horizontal colour. Define
\[
 W_{ab}=\sum_{(\ell,c)=(a,b)}Z_{\ell c r},\qquad
 E_{ab}=\sum_{(c,r)=(a,b)}Z_{\ell c r}.
\]
The pair-colour spatial equations are
\begin{equation}
 XE_{ab}+\delta_{a,0}\delta_{b,0}V
   =W_{ab}+\delta_{a,0}\delta_{b,0}XQ,
 \qquad a,b\in\A.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:pair}
\end{equation}
Together with the same clocks, vertical equations, and acceptance rows,
these give $s^2+s+4$ equations on exactly the same witness tuple.

\begin{theorem}[Full-tuple preservation under marginal reduction]
\label{pwh:uh:thm:projection}
The pair-colour system and the reduced system \System{} have identical
solution sets in $\Sring^{s^3+s+5}$. Replacing~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:pair} by
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:L}--\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:R} removes exactly $(s-1)^2$ equations and changes
neither the meaning nor the value of any witness coordinate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Sum~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:pair} over $b$ for fixed $a$. The resulting equation is
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:L}. Sum it over $a$ for fixed $b$ to obtain the complete
right-neighbour equation for $b$; discarding its blank instance gives
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:R}. Thus every pair-colour solution is a reduced solution.

For the reverse implication, Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:shape} proves that each site
contains one tile, and Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:windows} proves equality of both
components of the adjacent pair. Therefore the ordered pairs themselves
agree. At the left and right boundaries the pair is $(0,0)$, and there
are no tiles outside the prescribed region. Comparing coefficients at
every site gives~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:pair}. The equation-count difference is
\[
 (s^2+s+4)-(3s+3)=(s-1)^2.
\]
No reconstruction map or fresh auxiliary variable is involved.
\end{proof}

This theorem is a zero-set equivalence over nonnegative polynomials, not
an off-zero identity between residual vectors. The proof of the reverse
direction uses forced unit occupancy. In a signed or fractional setting,
equality of two marginal distributions does not generally force equality
of the joint pair distributions.


\section{Injective features: logarithmically many rows, or nine}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:features}

The pair-to-marginal reduction still assigns a channel to each symbol.
Once the shape equations force exactly one label at each site, a full
one-hot vector is unnecessary: any injective numerical description of a
symbol suffices. The important order of argument is to force unit
occupancy \emph{before} using the injectivity of this description.

\subsection{The complete feature system}

Let $d\ge0$ and let
\[
 \chi:\A\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,B_0\}^{d}
\]
be injective, with $\chi(0)=0$. Here $B_0\ge1$ is an ordinary fixed integer,
not the clock polynomial $B$. For $1\le k\le d$, define the linear forms
\begin{align*}
 L^{(k)}&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}\chi_k(\ell)Z_{\ell c r}, &
 C^{(k)}&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}\chi_k(c)Z_{\ell c r},\\
 R^{(k)}&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}\chi_k(r)Z_{\ell c r}, &
 O^{(k)}&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}\chi_k(f(\ell,c,r))Z_{\ell c r},\\
 T^{(k)}&=\sum_{a\in\A}\chi_k(a)T_a, &
 I^{(k)}&=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\chi_k(w_j)X^j.
\end{align*}
As before, $H=\sum Z_{\ell c r}$, $K=\sum T_a$, and
$G=V+B+Q+D$ are abbreviations, not additional unknowns.

Retain the two clock equations~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:C1}--\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:C2} and the two
acceptance equations~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:F0}--\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:F1}. Replace all the spatial
and vertical symbol equations by the following two aggregate equations
and $3d$ feature equations:
\begin{align}
 XH+V&=H+XQ, &
 H+K&=X[m]_X+XYH+G,
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:feature-shape}\\
 XC^{(k)}&=L^{(k)}, &
 XR^{(k)}&=C^{(k)},
 \qquad 1\le k\le d,
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:feature-spatial}\\
 C^{(k)}+T^{(k)}&=XI^{(k)}+XYO^{(k)},
 &&1\le k\le d.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:feature-vertical}
\end{align}
Thus the complete feature compiler has $s^3+s+5$ unknown polynomials and
exactly $6+3d$ affine-linear equations. In particular, the aggregate
shape equations are explicitly paid for; they are not free consequences
of a family of feature rows whose total might have been lost.

\begin{theorem}[Same-tuple injective-feature compression]
\label{pwh:uh:thm:features}
For every injective $\chi$ as above, the feature system and \System{} have
identical solution sets in $\N[X,Y]^{s^3+s+5}$. The feature matrix is
independent of the input word and horizon. Every entry has at most two
monomials, coefficient magnitude at most $B_0$, and coordinate total
degree at most three. The supplied right-hand side has coefficient
magnitude at most $B_0$ as well.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First suppose the original system holds. Summing its left-marginal rows
gives the first aggregate equation; summing its vertical rows gives the
second. Weighting the left-marginal rows by $\chi_k(a)$ eliminates both
blank boundary terms, since $\chi_k(0)=0$, and gives the first feature
spatial equation. Weighting the nonblank right-marginal rows gives the
second. Weighted vertical rows give~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:feature-vertical}. The
unchanged clock and acceptance rows require no argument. This proves
inclusion of the original zero set into the feature zero set using no
new witnesses.

Conversely, the clocks and the first aggregate equation are exactly the
hypotheses used to derive synchronized clocks and the transition mask in
Section~\ref{pwh:uh:sec:correctness}. The second aggregate equation then forces
the same terminal mask. Nonnegativity therefore gives exactly one tile
at each source site and exactly one terminal symbol at each top site,
with zero multiplicities elsewhere.

Write $(\ell_j,c_j,r_j)$ for the unique source triple at position $j$ of
one row. Coefficient comparison in the feature spatial equations gives,
for every $k$, the boundary equalities
\[
 \chi_k(\ell_0)=\chi_k(c_0)=0,\qquad
 \chi_k(c_{\rm last})=\chi_k(r_{\rm last})=0,
\]
and the interior equalities
\[
 \chi_k(c_j)=\chi_k(\ell_{j+1}),\qquad
 \chi_k(r_j)=\chi_k(c_{j+1}).
\]
These equalities hold for the whole feature vector. Injectivity and
$\chi(0)=0$ recover both blank boundaries and the genuine overlapping
windows. At each initial site, the feature vertical equations identify
the central symbol with the corresponding input symbol, with zero
features on the two guards. At each later source or terminal site, they
identify that symbol with the deterministic output of the preceding
row's unique tile. Again injectivity is applied only to a single symbol
on each side, never to arbitrary sums of symbol codes. The recovered
configuration history satisfies every original symbol equation. Since
the clock and acceptance equations were retained verbatim, the complete
original system holds on the very same tuple.

The matrix count and bounds follow by inspection. A source variable can
occur in any one feature row only at its two specified coordinate shifts;
its coefficients are feature values with signs. Aggregate rows have
unit coefficients. The sole degree-three shift is the inherited $X^2Y$
in the first clock. Input feature coefficients occur at distinct supplied
positions. This proves all claims.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Three exact coefficient--row tradeoffs}

Enumerate the alphabet as $0,1,\ldots,s-1$.
\begin{corollary}[Binary and weighted forms]
\label{pwh:uh:cor:feature-counts}
The same complete natural-polynomial witness relation admits:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $3\lceil\log_2s\rceil+6$ rows whose matrix coefficients lie in
      $\{-1,0,1\}$ and whose input vector has zero--one coefficients;
\item nine rows with coefficient magnitude at most $s-1$, for $s\ge2$;
\item $3\lceil\log_b s\rceil+6$ rows with coefficient magnitude at most
      $b-1$, for every fixed integer $b\ge2$.
\end{enumerate}
The number of unknown polynomials remains $s^3+s+5$. Every conclusion
about complete-witness uniqueness, Boolean coefficients, exact degrees,
and support size remains unchanged.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the fixed-length binary digits of each symbol for (i), the single
feature $\chi(a)=a$ for (ii), and its fixed-length base-$b$ digits for
(iii). Each map is injective and sends blank to zero. Apply
Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:features}. When $s=1$, the empty feature vector is
injective on the singleton alphabet and the zero-feature form has six
rows; its accepting set is empty, as required by the standing convention.
\end{proof}

For a fixed universal automaton, part (ii) gives a fixed \emph{nine-row}
coefficient matrix with undecidable solvability on the effective word
input family, while every solvable member has a unique Boolean-coefficient
full witness. The constant number of rows is not a constant number of
unknown scalar coefficients or an ordinary nine-variable Diophantine
claim.

Within the stated feature alphabet $\{0,\ldots,B_0\}^d$, the elementary
counting bound
\[
 s\le(B_0+1)^d
\]
forces $d\ge\lceil\log_{B_0+1}s\rceil$. This is only a lower bound on the
length of an injective bounded-coordinate code, not a lower bound on all
possible algebraic compilers or even on all same-witness equation systems.
Feature compression also need not reduce the size of a fully expanded
sum of squares: fewer but denser rows can produce more cross terms. The
worked example below exhibits this tradeoff explicitly.

\section{Universality without a black-box cellular rule}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:universality}

\subsection{An explicit machine-to-cellular construction}

Fix a deterministic, one-tape, two-way-infinite Turing machine. Its tape
alphabet is $\Gamma$ with blank $\square$, its state set is $Q$, and it
has a designated accepting state $q_{\rm acc}$. For every nonaccepting
state and tape symbol, let
\[
 \delta(q,a)=(q',b,d),\qquad d\in\{L,R,S\}.
\]
A rejecting halt may be replaced by a nonaccepting stationary loop.
The accepting head is retained forever after its first appearance.

Take the cellular alphabet
\begin{equation}
 \A=\Gamma\ \sqcup\ (Q\times\Gamma),
 \qquad s=|\Gamma|(1+|Q|).
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:tm-alphabet}
\end{equation}
An unmarked symbol $a\in\Gamma$ represents a tape cell without a head;
$(q,a)$ represents the same tape cell carrying the head in state $q$.
Identify the unmarked blank with the cellular symbol $0$.

Define the radius-one rule by the following ordered cases. If the centre
is an accepting head, keep it. If the centre is a nonaccepting head with
transition $(q',b,d)$, output $(q',b)$ when $d=S$ and the unmarked $b$
when $d=L$ or $R$. If the centre is unmarked and its left neighbour is a
head instructed to move right, put that head's new state on the centre's
existing tape symbol. Otherwise, if the centre is unmarked and its right
neighbour is a head instructed to move left, do the analogous update.
Otherwise retain the centre's unmarked symbol. Neighbourhoods with
multiple inconsistent heads are resolved by the stated order of cases;
they do not occur from a valid one-head encoding.

This description determines a finite total table $f:\A^3\to\A$.
In particular, an all-blank neighbourhood remains blank. Set
\[
 \F=\{(q_{\rm acc},a):a\in\Gamma\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Step-for-step simulation]
\label{pwh:uh:prop:TM}
From a finite encoding with exactly one head, the cellular automaton
continues to have exactly one head and performs one machine transition
per cellular step, until and including the first accepting step. Its
first acceptance, if any, has exactly one accepting cell.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The old head either remains in place with its new state or writes the
prescribed unmarked symbol and leaves its position. In the moving case,
exactly its indicated neighbour receives the new head state. All other
unmarked cells remain unchanged. Since there is one old head, there is
no competing incoming move. These statements prove the one-step claim
and preserve the one-head invariant. Induction proves the simulation.
An accepting state belongs to the unique head, which proves the
singleton property.
\end{proof}

An empty machine input is represented by the single blank cell carrying
the initial head, so the compiler's condition $m\ge1$ causes no loss.
For a nonempty input, put the initial head on its first tape symbol and
leave the remaining tape symbols unmarked.

\subsection{Fixed-matrix hardness and effective input loading}

A fixed universal machine can interpret a finite program followed by its
input; the classical universality construction supplies such machines
\cite{uh:Turing}. Apply Proposition~\ref{pwh:uh:prop:TM} to one such fixed machine.
This fixes $f$, $\F$, $s$, and hence the entire matrix
$\mathsf A_{f,\F}$. Only $\mathsf b(w)$ changes with the encoded program
and input. The input map forms the symbol-indicator polynomials $I_a$
and the endpoint marker $X^{m+1}$; it does not simulate the machine or
compute its accepting time.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{pwh:uh:cor:hard-intro}]
The reduction from the universal machine's acceptance problem is the
finite input encoding just described, followed by the compiler.
Proposition~\ref{pwh:uh:prop:TM} and Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:main} prove equivalence and
uniqueness. Thus the problem is $\Sigma^0_1$-hard. It is computably
enumerable because finite tuples of nonnegative integer polynomials can
be enumerated, and any candidate tuple can be checked by finitely many
exact coefficient operations. This proves completeness.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, one obtains a fixed finitely generated $\Sring$-subsemimodule
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M=\{\mathsf A_{f,\F}U:U\in\Sring^{s^3+s+5}\}
       \subseteq\Z[X,Y]^{3s+3}
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:subsemimodule}
\end{equation}
with undecidable membership even on the structured right-hand-side family
$\mathsf b(w)$. On that family, the representation by the fixed generators
is unique whenever it exists. Uniqueness is not asserted for arbitrary
vectors outside this input family.

This is an effective general compiler, not a numerical smallest-universal
cellular-automaton claim. The package instantiates and tests several finite
rules, but does not supply an independently audited numerical transition
table for a particular small universal machine. Once such a table is
chosen, the construction above generates its cellular table and fixes the
numerical matrix size without any further mathematical existence step.

\section{Exact resources and a noncomputable degree bound}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:resources}

\subsection{Degrees, support size, and what fixed arity means}

\begin{proposition}[Exact witness ledger]
\label{pwh:uh:prop:ledger}
Let the input length be $m$, the first singleton acceptance time be $h$,
and the final accepting position in the sheared coordinates be $j_*$.
For the unique complete witness,
\begin{align}
 \max_i\deg_Y U_i&=h,\label{pwh:uh:eq:dy}\\
 \max_i\deg_X U_i&=m+2h+1,\label{pwh:uh:eq:dx}\\
 \max_i\deg U_i&=m+3h+1.\label{pwh:uh:eq:totaldegree}
\end{align}
The total number of nonzero coefficients across all witness polynomials is
\begin{equation}
  h^2+mh+5h+m+4+j_*.
  \label{pwh:uh:eq:fullsupport}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All tile supports lie at $0\le t<h$ and
$0\le j\le m+2t+1$. All terminal supports lie at $t=h$ and
$0\le j\le m+2h+1$. The clocks and the marker obey
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:DQ}, \eqref{pwh:uh:eq:BV}, and \eqref{pwh:uh:eq:J}. These give the upper
bounds in~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:dy}--\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:totaldegree}. The monomial
$D=X^{m+2h+1}Y^h$ attains all three maxima simultaneously.

The total tile mass is
\[
 \wt(H)=\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}(m+2t+2)=h(m+h+1).
\]
The terminal mass is $m+2h+2$. The masses of $(D,Q,B,V,J)$ are
$(1,h,1,h,j_*)$. Since every coefficient is zero or one, adding these
masses counts all nonzero coefficients and gives~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:fullsupport}.
\end{proof}

The compiler therefore has fixed \emph{polynomial arity}, not bounded
certificate length. The canonical light cone contains quadratically many
sites in $h$ for fixed input length. Sparse storage still records those
sites. No exponential acceleration of ordinary witness checking is
claimed, and no computation disappears into a bounded-size integer tuple
by a mere change of notation.

\subsection{No computable global degree estimate}

\begin{theorem}[Degree-bound obstruction]
\label{pwh:uh:thm:nobound}
For the fixed universal compiler of Corollary~\ref{pwh:uh:cor:hard-intro}, there
is no total computable function of the input word that bounds the largest
$Y$-degree of its solution whenever a solution exists. The same holds for
the largest total degree, the largest $X$-degree, and the total number of
nonzero witness coefficients.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A computable bound on the $Y$-degree would, by~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:dy}, bound the
first accepting time on every accepting input. Simulating the fixed
machine for that many steps would then decide acceptance, including a
negative answer when it did not accept by the bound. This contradicts
Corollary~\ref{pwh:uh:cor:hard-intro}.

A bound on either of the other degrees gives a time bound by
\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:dx} or~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:totaldegree}. A support bound also bounds $h$:
for $h>0$, the polynomial $Q$ alone has $h$ nonzero coefficients, and
$h=0$ is harmless. These reductions are effective, so all the claimed
bounds are impossible.
\end{proof}

A bound depending only on the size of the encoded input would be an even
stronger bound and is therefore impossible as well. This remains true
although the number of equations, the number of polynomial variables,
the matrix entries, and the witness coefficient alphabet are all fixed.
The unbounded computational resource is the polynomial support and its
degree.

\wtag\ The proof is the bounded-search argument of Proposition
\texttt{cdc:bd:prop:nobound} in~\cite{cdc}, with the $Y$-degree in place of
an integer witness height; Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:nobound} and
Corollary~\ref{pwh:bt:cor:bound} are its counterparts in Parts~II and~III.

\section{From many linear equations to one quadratic}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:quadratic}

Let $L_1(U),\ldots,L_{3s+3}(U)$ be the left-minus-right residuals of the
complete reduced system. Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal P_{f,\F,w}(U)=\sum_{i=1}^{3s+3}L_i(U)^2.
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:sos}
\end{equation}
This is a polynomial in the unknown polynomial values, with coefficients
in $\Z[X,Y]$.

\begin{lemma}[Sum-of-squares identity test]
\label{pwh:uh:lem:SOS}
For $p_1,\ldots,p_r\in\Z[X,Y]$,
$\sum_i p_i^2=0$ as a polynomial identity if and only if every $p_i=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reverse implication is immediate. In the forward direction, evaluate
the identity at any real pair $(x,y)$. A sum of nonnegative real squares
can vanish only if every $p_i(x,y)$ vanishes. A real polynomial vanishing
at every point of $\mathbb R^2$ is the zero polynomial. Applying this to
each $p_i$ proves the result. Real evaluation is used only to prove an
identity lemma; it does not change the domain of the witness variables.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Single quadratic polynomial-semiring representation]
\label{pwh:uh:thm:quadratic}
The equation $\mathcal P_{f,\F,w}(U)=0$, with $U\in\Sring^{s^3+s+5}$,
has exactly the same complete solutions as \System{}. It is of degree
two in the unknown polynomial values. Its solution, when it exists, is
unique and Boolean-coefficient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every residual is affine-linear in the unknowns. Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:SOS}
shows that its sum of squares vanishes precisely when all residuals
vanish. The uniqueness and coefficient assertions are therefore inherited
without any additional variables. The degree is exactly two: for example,
the coefficient of $D^2$ receives a nonzero square contribution and cannot
vanish as a sum of coefficient-polynomial squares.
\end{proof}

One may regard the input endpoint and the $I_a$ as polynomial parameters.
Then~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:sos} is one fixed quadratic expression for fixed $f,\F$,
with those parameters specialized to valid word encodings. This is the
sense in which the construction gives a universal quadratic expression
over a polynomial semiring. The parameter values are not ordinary scalar
numerals, and validity of the word-encoding slice is part of the input
specification.

\subsection{A bounded-degree ordinary quadratic family}

\begin{proposition}[Coefficient extraction at an external bound]
\label{pwh:uh:prop:bounded}
Fix a nonnegative external bound $H_0$ and put $d=m+2H_0+1$. Replace each
unknown polynomial by
\[
 U_i=\sum_{0\le p\le d}\sum_{0\le q\le H_0}u_{i,pq}X^pY^q,
 \qquad u_{i,pq}\in\N.
\]
Coefficient comparison gives a finite affine-linear system in
\begin{equation}
 (s^3+s+5)(d+1)(H_0+1)
 \label{pwh:uh:eq:scalararity}
\end{equation}
ordinary natural-number variables. Its sum of squared scalar residuals is
an ordinary quadratic polynomial. It has a unique natural zero precisely
when a first singleton acceptance occurs at some time $h\le H_0$; otherwise
it has no natural zero. Fixing $B=Y^{H_0}$ restricts this to first acceptance
at exactly $H_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The matrix shifts increase $X$-degree by at most two and $Y$-degree by at
most one. It suffices to compare all coefficients with
$0\le p\le d+2$, $0\le q\le H_0+1$ in each residual. These are ordinary
linear equations in the supplied coefficient variables.

Any coefficient solution is a genuine polynomial solution and therefore
has acceptance time $h=\deg_Y B\le H_0$. Conversely, the unique witness
for any first singleton acceptance at $h\le H_0$ satisfies the imposed
degree bounds by Proposition~\ref{pwh:uh:prop:ledger}. Coefficients give a
bijection between the bounded polynomial tuple and the scalar tuple, so
uniqueness is preserved. The scalar sum-of-squares equivalence follows
from nonnegativity of integer squares. The additional equation on $B$
fixes the clock time and is again linear in its coefficients.
\end{proof}

There are at most $(3s+3)(d+3)(H_0+2)$ coefficient residuals before optional
clock fixing. These loose rectangular counts include coefficients that
the shape proof forces to zero; they are not optimization claims.
Crucially, the scalar arity in~\eqref{pwh:uh:eq:scalararity} depends on the
external bound. Taking the union of all such bounded families does not
produce one fixed-arity ordinary quadratic Diophantine equation.

\section{Why domain changes do not preserve the theorem for free}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:barriers}

\subsection{Signed polynomial witnesses}

\begin{proposition}[Nonnegative kernel and signed ambiguity]
\label{pwh:uh:prop:sign}
For the fixed compiler matrix, the homogeneous system
$\mathsf A_{f,\F}U=0$ has only the zero solution in
$\Sring^{s^3+s+5}$. In contrast, every solvable input system has infinitely
many solutions when witnesses are allowed in $\Z[X,Y]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the homogeneous version of the first clock,
$D+Q=X^2YQ$. Evaluation at $(1,1)$ gives $D=0$ because $D$ is nonnegative;
then the integral-domain property gives $Q=0$. The second clock similarly
gives $B=V=0$. Summing the homogeneous left-neighbour rows yields
$XH=H$, hence $H=0$ and all nonnegative tile polynomials vanish. The
vertical rows now give $T_a=0$ for every $a$, and the final acceptance row
gives $J=XJ$, hence $J=0$.

For the signed assertion, the matrix has $n=s^3+s+5$ columns and
$r=3s+3$ rows, with
$n-r=s^3-2s+2>0$ for every $s\ge1$. Over the field $\Qfield(X,Y)$,
it therefore has a nonzero kernel vector. Clearing polynomial and integer
denominators gives a nonzero kernel vector $K\in\Z[X,Y]^n$. From any
signed solution $U$, all vectors $U+tK$ with $t\in\Z$ are distinct signed
solutions. They need not be nonnegative.
\end{proof}

The absence of a nonzero nonnegative homogeneous kernel is not, by itself,
a general uniqueness criterion for inhomogeneous semiring systems: the
difference of two nonnegative solutions may be signed. The specific
uniqueness theorem still needs semantic reconstruction and first
acceptance.

\wtag\ Part~III restores a unique witness over every nonzero commutative
ring, signed coefficients and zero divisors included, by a different
mechanism: unknowns restricted to subrings that omit a tested variable, and
a clock indeterminate (Theorems~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:clocked}
and~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:compiler}).

\subsection{Numerical evaluation destroys the clock predicate}

Consider the elementary clock
\[
 D+Q=1+X^2YQ.
\]
As a polynomial equation over $\Sring$, Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:ray} forces
$D=(X^2Y)^h$. After specialization $X=b\ge2$, $Y=c\ge1$, the scalar
natural values
\[
 Q=2,\qquad D=2b^2c-1
\]
always satisfy the numerical equation. Yet
\[
 b^2c<2b^2c-1<(b^2c)^2,
\]
so this $D$ is not a power of $b^2c$. Thus even the simplest clock loses
its intended semantics under raw scalar evaluation. This is a
counterexample to the clock specialization, not a claim that every full
compiled system acquires a spurious solution in this way.

Likewise, $Y\mapsto X^k$ is not injective on all of $\Z[X,Y]$ for any
fixed $k$: it identifies $Y$ with $X^k$. It becomes injective on a
suitably bounded strip of $X$-exponents, but our accepting degrees have
no computable input-dependent bound. This blocks a naive fixed-stride
flattening argument. It does not rule out a more sophisticated
univariate compiler; general univariate linear-semiring undecidability
was already established by Narendran \cite{uh:Narendran}.

\subsection{Ordinary Diophantine representations}

A finite polynomial tuple has a canonical integer encoding, and its
validity can be checked effectively. That observation explains why the
represented input language is computably enumerable. It does not turn
all coefficientwise equalities into a fixed-arity ordinary polynomial
system with unique auxiliary integer witnesses.

Applying an MRDP theorem to the resulting enumerable language establishes
ordinary Diophantine representability, but the theorem does not by itself
preserve the uniqueness of a chosen computation certificate. The
additional witnesses used to arithmetize coefficient access, support,
exponentiation, bounds, and verification must all be accounted for in any
single-fold claim. This report supplies none of those steps as a hidden
assumption. It therefore makes no claim to settle ordinary single-fold
or finite-fold Diophantine representation questions.

The distinction is especially important when comparing with native
integer-packing compilers in \repo{}: their paid typing and bounding
conditions cannot be discarded merely because the intended polynomial
witnesses in this report happen to have Boolean coefficients.

\section{A parsimonious nondeterministic extension}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:nondeterministic}

The proof separates local consistency from deterministic uniqueness.
This permits a precise extension that is stronger than an existence-only
translation.

Let $\Delta\subseteq\A^4$ be a finite allowed-transition relation.
A quadruple $(\ell,c,r,d)$ means that a cell with neighbourhood
$(\ell,c,r)$ may produce $d$. Assume every neighbourhood has at least one
allowed output and that the all-blank neighbourhood has exactly the output
zero. A finite history is legal when each of its cell updates belongs to
$\Delta$. Acceptance and the light-cone convention are unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Parsimonious history correspondence]
\label{pwh:uh:thm:nondet}
Replace triple variables by $Z_{\ell c r d}$ for
$(\ell,c,r,d)\in\Delta$, and define the four marginal forms by their
respective coordinates. Keep all the equations of
Section~\ref{pwh:uh:sec:compiler}. The resulting system has
$|\Delta|+s+5$ unknown polynomials and $3s+3$ equations. Its solutions
are in bijection with finite legal configuration histories whose final
row is their first singleton acceptance. Every solution has Boolean
coefficients. For a fixed such history every auxiliary polynomial is
unique.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each allowed quadruple has one left, central, right, and output symbol,
so all total-mass identities remain valid. The clock, shape, and occupancy
proofs are unchanged. Neighbour matching reconstructs the actual source
windows, and vertical matching reconstructs each succeeding configuration.
At every site the unique tile certifies that its output is allowed by
$\Delta$. Acceptance has the same first-singleton meaning.

Conversely, a legal configuration history specifies each quadruple at
each site, together with its length, boundaries, final row, and accepting
position. The same canonical construction supplies its unique solution.
The two constructions are inverse. Determinism is needed only to conclude
that there is at most one legal accepting history for a fixed input.
\end{proof}

The objects counted here are \emph{configuration histories}. Distinct
syntactic transition labels with identical source and destination are not
counted twice, because $\Delta$ is a set of quadruples. To count such
labels separately, they must be represented in the local state or tile
alphabet. The provided executable compiler implements the deterministic
special case; the nondeterministic statement is proved in the article but
is not claimed as implemented in that package.

\section{Reproducibility and validation}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:validation}

\subsection{Executable scope}

The package contains a dependency-free Python implementation using sparse
integer-coefficient dictionaries. It accepts any total finite deterministic
radius-one rule with quiescent blank, validates the alphabet and accepting
set, and emits the entire system, including both clocks and both
acceptance equations. It supports the reduced marginal form, the
unreduced pair-colour form, and arbitrary validated injective-feature maps. Polynomial equality is checked coefficientwise;
no floating-point arithmetic or numerical specialization is used.

The witness constructor takes a \emph{supplied external horizon} for the
purpose of generating or testing a candidate. The emitted mathematical
system contains no such horizon. The constructor deliberately returns a
candidate even when acceptance fails; the full residual check must then
reject it. This separation tests the acceptance conditions rather than
assuming them in the verifier.

Input rule tables are copied into immutable mappings. This prevents later
mutation of a caller's table from silently changing the meaning of an
already compiled system.

\section{Further research questions and concrete next steps}
\label{pwh:uh:sec:research}

The following questions are proposed extensions of this construction.
They are not all asserted to be previously published open problems;
precise priority and existing special cases should be checked before a
separate novelty claim is made.

\subsection{A numerical universal matrix with a verified source table}\label{pwh:uh:q:numerical}

Choose a particular universal Turing-machine table with documented tape,
halt, and input conventions. Generate the cellular rule by
Proposition~\ref{pwh:uh:prop:TM}, then emit its fixed matrix and input encoder.
A useful deliverable would contain the table, the simulation theorem,
all matrix entries, and a certificate checker, together with a numerical
value for $s^3+s+5$ and the chosen row count (in particular nine
in the weighted form). This would replace the generic universality
instantiation by a fully auditable concrete object. Minimizing $s$ is
secondary to obtaining a correct ordinary-input interface.

\wtag\ The same target is posed for Part~II's eleven identities
(Section~\ref{pwh:oc:q:numerical}) and for Part~III's fixed tagged equation
(Section~\ref{pwh:bt:q:concrete}, \emph{A numerical universal table}); no
source instantiates one.

\subsection{Equation counts versus coefficient size}

The feature theorem gives an explicit tradeoff between the number of rows
and coefficient magnitude, without changing the witness tuple. Can the
nine-row weighted form be reduced further while retaining the fixed matrix,
canonical geometry, and full-tuple zero-set equivalence? For unit
coefficients, is there a substantially better alternative to
$6+3\lceil\log_2s\rceil$ rows? Any lower-bound problem must specify the
allowed fixed coordinate shifts and whether clock, geometry, and acceptance
constraints may be combined. The counting bound on feature-vector length
is not a lower bound for unrestricted linear systems. A finite-family
Pareto analysis should also count matrix density and expanded quadratic
size, which need not decrease with the number of rows.

\subsection{A one-coordinate compiler with the same uniqueness guarantees}\label{pwh:uh:q:onecoord}

General linear-system undecidability over $\N[X]$ is known. The concrete
question here is whether one can retain a fixed matrix, a transparent
word-input map, Boolean-coefficient full witnesses, and first-history
single-foldness using only one coordinate indeterminate. A successful
construction must avoid the collisions of a fixed Kronecker substitution
and must prove uniqueness of any width or stride auxiliaries. This is a
sharpening target, not a proposed proof of a one-variable undecidability
threshold.

\wtag\ \emph{Answered in Part~\ref{pwh:part:oc} (note of 2~October 2026).}
In the form asked here the answer is negative: coefficient-bounded
univariate affine-linear systems are decidable
(Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:finite-state}), so no universal univariate linear
compiler can guarantee even one Boolean-coefficient full witness per
accepted input (Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:no-linear}, with bound $B_x=1$).
Eleven identities of degree two with one shared stride keep the full unique
Boolean history (Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:main}), in bijection with this
Part's witnesses (Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:bijection}). The research
programme's review of source~15 adds a ten-identity projection (note at
Section~\ref{pwh:oc:q:rows}).

\subsection{Packing with an exact auxiliary-fibre ledger}\label{pwh:uh:q:packing}

Can the canonical semiring witness be transferred to an ordinary integer
or exponential-Diophantine representation with a proved bijection, or at
least a controlled finite fibre, over the full auxiliary tuple? A useful
first milestone is a bounded version with explicit carry bounds and a
proof that every integer solution has a unique digit decomposition and
satisfies the polynomial coefficient equations. The unbounded extension
must confront Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:nobound}; it cannot assume a computable
input-only degree limit.

\wtag\ Part~II asks the same for its stride witnesses
(Section~\ref{pwh:oc:q:packing}) and Part~III for its sparse polynomial
checker (Section~\ref{pwh:bt:q:arithmetic}, \emph{An explicit integer bridge
with controlled fibers}). The obstruction named here is, for all three,
the bounded-search argument of Proposition \texttt{cdc:bd:prop:nobound}
in~\cite{cdc}.

\subsection{Parsimonious branching compilers}

Implement Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:nondet} with labelled transition choices and
prove a root-counting correspondence for a carefully specified
nondeterministic machine semantics. Distinguish configuration histories,
labelled execution paths, and different schedules of independent actions.
These choices determine whether the target should count traces, events,
or equivalence classes. The existing equations already identify the point
at which local multiplicity becomes genuine computational branching.

\subsection{Smaller canonical regions}

The fixed expanding light cone is simple but can be much wider than the
actually visited tape interval. Can one use the minimal visited interval
while retaining unique nonnegative linear witnesses and a fixed matrix?
A naive freely chosen rectangle introduces padding ambiguity. Any
improvement needs a canonical extremum condition, with all witnesses
uniquely determined, rather than an existentially generous space bound.
A successful version could substantially reduce support size even when
it does not reduce the number of polynomial variables.

\subsection{Output-preserving and compositional interfaces}

The terminal polynomials already contain the whole final tape. A next
step is to specify a canonical output projection and prove that composing
two compilers preserves a bijection of complete witnesses. Input length,
head position, blank padding, and return-to-origin conventions must be
part of the interface. A composition theorem would make the normal form
useful as a library component rather than only as a universal acceptance
reduction.

\subsection{Kernel-checked support and reconstruction proofs}\label{pwh:uh:q:kernel}

A practical Lean or Rocq development should begin with finitely supported
maps $\N^2\to\N$, the coefficientwise ray-clock lemma, and the implication
``sum of natural coefficients equals one $\Rightarrow$ unique occupied
label.'' Next come the two shape identities, the missing blank marginal
row, and induction recovering the cellular evolution. Only after these
parts should one formalize the Turing-machine bridge and the exact count
ledger. The proof naturally separates algebraic identities, nonnegative
coefficient reasoning, finite-state semantics, and the universality input
reduction. No unbounded numerical search is required for these lemmas.

\wtag\ Parts~II and~III propose the same formalization programme for
their constructions (Sections~\ref{pwh:oc:q:kernel} and~\ref{pwh:bt:q:analysis},
\emph{A fully verified compiler pipeline}); nothing of it exists in the
repository at this write.

\section{Conclusion}

The central construction answers a precise design question: a deterministic
finite computation can have a fixed-arity, affine-linear certificate over
$\N[X,Y]$ in which the \emph{whole} witness, including clocks, boundaries,
and accepting-position marker, is unique. Nonnegative conservation forces
unit occupancy and a canonical light cone. Marginal matching then suffices
to reconstruct the local windows, and first acceptance removes arbitrary
post-acceptance extensions. Injective features compress the very same
witness relation to $6+3\lceil\log_2s\rceil$ unit-coefficient rows, or to
nine rows when symbol-sized coefficients are allowed. The reduction is
justified by already-forced one-hot occupancy, not by a generally false
claim that weighted marginals determine arbitrary multisets.

The resulting fixed-matrix problem is $\Sigma^0_1$-complete on
an unambiguous input family, even though every actual witness coefficient
is zero or one. The exact degree equals a linear function of the running
time, and no computable input-only bound on that degree is possible. A
single quadratic polynomial-semiring equation preserves all of these
properties; a bounded ordinary quadratic family preserves them only with
arity growing with the external bound.

The useful mathematical advance of this manuscript is the explicit
normal form and its witness-preserving reductions. Its limitations are
just as concrete: the unknowns are polynomials, the universal numerical
rule is not instantiated in the package, the main theorem is not yet
kernel-checked, and historical priority for the combined refinement
remains to be investigated. These boundaries leave a well-defined path
from the present proofs to a verified, compositional compiler and to
carefully audited ordinary-arithmetic packing experiments.

\section{The question, its obstruction, and its replacement}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:intro}

The computational-substrate program in \code{ProveIt} has developed many
ways of turning executions into arithmetic certificates. A central issue is
not merely whether a certificate exists. One also wants to know what every
solution means, whether the auxiliaries introduce artificial multiplicity,
and whether an apparently fixed compiler hides an externally selected
running-time or space bound. The repository's Hilbert-tenth overview makes
these distinctions explicit and separately records its natural-number
75-operation certificate and 87-operation universal-polynomial bounds
\cite{oc:ProveIt}. The present work changes neither bound.

The preceding manuscript \emph{Unique Histories from Linear Equations}
\cite{oc:Precursor} gives a canonical compiler over $\N[X,Y]$. Its two
indeterminates retain space and time. A proposed extension in its further
research section asks for one coordinate while preserving a fixed
\emph{linear} matrix, a transparent word-input map, Boolean-coefficient full
witnesses, and first-history uniqueness. This is a precise question about a
representation, rather than a claim that undecidability over $\N[X]$ is
unknown.

\wtag\ The preceding manuscript is Part~\ref{pwh:part:uh} of this report;
the question is its Section~\ref{pwh:uh:q:onecoord}.

This report answers that question negatively and supplies a degree-two
replacement. The negative statement is stronger than required: even a
computably varying matrix cannot represent an undecidable language if every
solvable instance has some witness with a computably bounded coefficient
height. The positive statement keeps the desirable semantic properties and
uses only a single shared nonlinear parameter.

\subsection{Two main theorems}

All natural numbers in this report include zero. A polynomial is
\emph{Boolean-coefficient} when every coefficient is zero or one; arithmetic
still takes place in the ordinary semiring, not in a Boolean ring.

\begin{theorem}[The univariate linear obstruction]
\label{pwh:oc:thm:obstruction-intro}
Given an affine-linear system $A(X)u(X)=b(X)$ over $\N[X]$ and a natural
number $B_0$, it is decidable whether there is a solution all of whose
coefficients are at most $B_0$. If the system has $n$ unknown polynomials,
$\deg A\le d$, and $\deg b\le M$ with $M\ge0$, then any solvable bounded
instance has a bounded solution with
\[
 \max_i\deg u_i\le M+(B_0+1)^{nd}.
\]
Consequently, no effective reduction of an undecidable language to
univariate affine-linear systems can guarantee a computable bound on the
coefficients of some solution to every solvable instance.
\end{theorem}

The proof is given in Section~\ref{pwh:oc:sec:linear}. It is an elementary
finite-state argument and should not be confused with a decision procedure
for unrestricted univariate polynomial-semiring linear systems.

For the positive theorem, a \emph{first singleton acceptance} means that the
first configuration containing any designated accepting symbol contains
exactly one such cell. This holds automatically in the usual one-head
simulation of a deterministic accepting Turing machine. It is a genuine
restriction for an arbitrary cellular automaton.

\begin{theorem}[Self-sizing univariate quadratic compiler]
\label{pwh:oc:thm:main}
Fix a deterministic radius-one cellular automaton with alphabet
$\A=\{0,\ldots,s-1\}$, $s\ge2$, quiescent blank $0$, total local rule
$f:\A^3\to\A$, and accepting set $\F\subseteq\A\setminus\{0\}$.
For each nonempty finite input word $w$ of length $m$, there is an effective
system $\Sys$ of eleven polynomial identities with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item There are exactly $s^3+s+7$ unknowns in $\N[X]$. Every identity has
      degree at most two in those unknowns. Every nonlinear monomial
      contains one distinguished stride unknown $S$.
\item The nonconstant-in-the-unknowns terms are fixed by $f$ and $\F$.
      Their coefficients lie in $\Z[X]$, have coordinate degree at most
      two, and have coefficient magnitude at most $s-1$. Only the
      constant terms load the input word.
\item The system has a solution precisely when the evolution from $w$,
      with blank exterior, has a first singleton acceptance. In that case
      the complete polynomial witness tuple is unique, and all its
      coefficients are zero or one.
\item If the acceptance time is $h$, then
\[
 S=X^W,\qquad W=m+2h+2,
\]
      and the maximum degree among all witness components is
\[
 D_{\max}=\max\{W,\,W(h+1)-1\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
The equations contain no externally supplied running time, stride,
polynomial degree bound, occupancy condition, or coefficient bound.
\end{theorem}

We list the whole system in Section~\ref{pwh:oc:sec:compiler} and prove all-solution
soundness, completeness, and uniqueness in Section~\ref{pwh:oc:sec:proof}.
The theorem is not just a correct recipe for generating some valid
certificates: it characterizes the entire natural-polynomial zero set.

\subsection{What the degree boundary means}

A fixed universal machine, translated to a one-head cellular automaton,
gives a fixed instance of Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:main} whose word-input solvability
problem is $\Sigma^0_1$-complete. Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:obstruction-intro} rules out
the analogous affine-linear compiler with computably bounded witness
coefficients. Thus degree two is both sufficient and necessary \emph{for
systems in this coefficient-bounded representation class}. It is not a
claim that unrestricted univariate linear systems are decidable, nor that
eleven equations or $s^3+s+7$ variables are optimal.

The previously available two-coordinate linear construction and the present
one-coordinate quadratic construction illustrate two different ways to
retain unbounded computational geometry. A second coordinate permits
fixed linear shifts in time. With one coordinate, the present construction
pays for a variable shift by multiplication with an unknown monomial.

\subsection{Literature and contribution ledger}

Narendran proved undecidability of systems of linear equations over
polynomial semirings, already over $\N[X]$, in 1996 \cite{oc:Narendran}.
The unbounded coefficient heights permitted there are essential to the
contrast with our obstruction. Dong's study gives a different positive
result: polynomial-time decidability for a \emph{single homogeneous}
linear equation in which every unknown polynomial is required to be
nonzero \cite{oc:Dong}. Neither result settles the canonical, automatically
Boolean, full-witness formulation considered here.

Finite-state reasoning about finitely supported objects belongs to the
classical automata tradition exemplified by B\"uchi \cite{oc:Buchi}.
Our coefficient-window construction is proved directly rather than
attributed as a new general automata principle. Encoding computations by
translated local constraints also has close precedents, including the
Turing-machine tiling constructions of Lohrey and Steinberg
\cite{oc:LohreySteinberg}. Kronecker substitution is a standard way of
encoding multivariate polynomial data by separated univariate exponent
blocks; see, for example, Harvey \cite{oc:Harvey}. None of these general
techniques is claimed as an invention of this report.

The particular contribution is the following combination: an explicit
negative answer to the precursor's one-coordinate linear design question;
a positive-stride polynomial equation; the exact calibration $SB=XD$;
eleven complete low-degree identities; reconstruction of every solution;
uniqueness of all stride, clock, boundary, and position auxiliaries; and
an exact resource ledger. The proofs below establish these claims without
assuming an unexamined universal equation or an unproved repository lemma.
A broader literature review would be needed to establish historical
priority for the combined normal form.

\section{Coefficient-bounded univariate linear systems are finite-state}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:linear}

\subsection{Definitions and the finite-memory recurrence}

Let $n,r\ge1$ and consider
\begin{equation}
 A(X)u(X)=b(X),\qquad
 A(X)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}A_kX^k,
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:linear}
\end{equation}
where $A_k\in\Z^{r\times n}$, $b\in\Z[X]^r$, and the unknown vector
$u$ lies in $\N[X]^n$. Zero leading matrices are allowed. Choose
$M\ge0$ with $\deg b\le M$; the zero right-hand side may use $M=0$.
For a polynomial $p$, write
\[
 \htc(p)=\max\{p_t:t\ge0\},\qquad \htc(0)=0.
\]
The bound $B_0$ is a bound on \emph{all coefficients}, not on polynomial
degrees or on values at selected numerical arguments.

Write $u_t\in\{0,\ldots,B_0\}^n$ for the coefficient vector of $X^t$ in
$u$, put $u_t=0$ for $t<0$, and let $b_t$ be the coefficient vector of
$b$. Comparing coefficients in~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:linear} gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=0}^{d}A_k u_{t-k}=b_t\qquad(t\ge0).
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:window}
\end{equation}
Each test depends only on the next coefficient vector and the previous
$d$ coefficient vectors. There is no unbounded carry: these are identities
of formal polynomials, not equalities after evaluation at an integer base.

\subsection{Decision procedure and a uniform degree bound}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-state bounded-height theorem]
\label{pwh:oc:thm:finite-state}
The bounded-height solvability problem for~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:linear} is decidable.
Set
\[
 a=(B_0+1)^n,\qquad N=a^d=(B_0+1)^{nd}.
\]
If a bounded-height solution exists, one exists with
$\max_i\deg u_i\le M+N$. This includes $d=0$ and $B_0=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient alphabet is the finite set
$\mathcal C=\{0,\ldots,B_0\}^n$, of size $a$. A memory state is the
ordered tuple $(u_{t-1},\ldots,u_{t-d})\in\mathcal C^d$; there are $N$
states. For $d=0$, there is one empty state. The initial memory is all
zero. A candidate symbol $v\in\mathcal C$ is a legal next symbol at time
$t$ precisely when
\[
 A_0v+\sum_{k=1}^{d}A_k u_{t-k}=b_t.
\]
The new memory is obtained by prepending $v$ and dropping the oldest
vector. All tests use finite exact integer arithmetic.

First compute the set of memories reachable after reading positions
$0,\ldots,M$. During this finite prefix, the required right-hand vector
may vary. After position $M$, the right-hand vector is always zero, so
legal transitions form a fixed directed graph on the same $N$ memories.
Call a memory \emph{drainable} when a path in this zero-right-hand graph
reaches the all-zero memory. Reachability in this finite graph is decidable.
The original problem is solvable exactly when one of the reachable prefix
memories is drainable.

For the forward direction, any genuine polynomial solution is eventually
zero. After its last nonzero coefficient, $d$ further zero coefficient
vectors flush the memory to zero. Its coefficient sequence therefore
supplies the required prefix and draining path. Conversely, a valid
prefix followed by a draining path can be followed forever by zeros.
All prefix and homogeneous equations then hold, and the resulting
coefficient sequence has finite support. It is the desired bounded-height
polynomial solution.

If a draining path exists, a shortest one has no repeated state and has
length at most $N-1$. Keeping a prefix that reaches its starting memory,
then following that path and appending zeros, produces a solution with
no nonzero coefficient beyond position $M+N-1$; the stated bound $M+N$
is a harmless uniform relaxation. For $d=0$, an admissible prefix can
already be followed by zeros. For $B_0=0$, the only possible sequence is
zero. Both cases are covered by the same argument.
\end{proof}

This is a finite-state algorithm, not a polynomial-time claim for arbitrary
encodings of the parameters. The state space may be enormous, and an
exponent $M$ given sparsely may itself be large. A direct implementation
examines at most $N$ memory states and $a$ next symbols per state after
the input prefix. The executable solver in the package follows precisely
this construction and returns a witness, not only a Boolean decision.

\begin{proposition}[Regularity refinement for a fixed matrix]
\label{pwh:oc:prop:regular}
For fixed $A$ and $B_0$, the coefficient words of right-hand sides $b$ that
admit a bounded-height solution form a regular language, using
least-degree coefficients first and allowing trailing zero vectors.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Label a transition, specified by a memory and a next candidate coefficient
vector, with the right-hand vector computed by the left side
of~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:window}. Only finitely many labels occur. Start at the zero
memory and make the drainable memories accepting. A finite labelled path
then recognizes exactly a right-hand prefix that can be completed by a
zero-labelled tail and finally by zero coefficient vectors. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:finite-state} gives the equivalence with polynomial
solvability. Right-hand letters outside the finite transition-label
alphabet are rejected.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The impossibility result, with its exact quantifiers}

\begin{corollary}[No computably bounded-height linear universal compiler]
\label{pwh:oc:cor:no-linear}
Let $L$ be an undecidable language. There do not exist total computable
maps $x\mapsto(A_x,b_x)$ and $x\mapsto B_x\in\N$ such that both
\begin{align*}
 x\in L &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
           \exists u\in\N[X]^{n_x}\; A_xu=b_x,\\
 \exists u\;A_xu=b_x &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
           \exists v\;\bigl(A_xv=b_x\ \text{and}\ \htc(v_i)\le B_x
           \text{ for all }i\bigr)
\end{align*}
hold for every input $x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Compute $A_x,b_x,B_x$ and decide the bounded-height problem by
Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:finite-state}. The second hypothesis makes bounded
solvability equivalent to unrestricted solvability for this input family;
the first then decides $L$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

In particular, choosing $B_x=1$ rules out the precursor's proposed
univariate affine-linear compiler with Boolean full witnesses. The proof
does not need a fixed matrix, unique witnesses, Boolean coefficients in
\emph{every} solution, or a uniform number of unknowns. It only needs a
computable bound for \emph{some} solution whenever one exists. Thus a
universal linear compiler over $\N[X]$, of the sort compatible with
Narendran's theorem, must have no total computable input-only upper bound
on the minimum coefficient height of its accepting witnesses.

This establishes Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:obstruction-intro}. It also identifies
exactly what must change in the positive construction: either coefficient
height must become unbounded in a noncomputably controlled way, a second
coordinate must remain, or genuine nonlinearity must be introduced.
We choose the last option.

\section{Cellular semantics and the two-coordinate starting point}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:semantics}

\subsection{A guarded expanding computation region}

Let $w=w_0\cdots w_{m-1}$, with $m\ge1$, and define the initial
configuration on $\Z$ by
\[
 c_0(i)=\begin{cases}w_i,&0\le i<m,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The evolution is
\begin{equation}
 c_{t+1}(i)=f\bigl(c_t(i-1),c_t(i),c_t(i+1)\bigr).
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:ca}
\end{equation}
Quiescence, $f(0,0,0)=0$, implies by induction that
\begin{equation}
 c_t(i)=0\quad\text{outside }[-t,m-1+t].
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:lightcone}
\end{equation}
At transition time $t$, include centres
$i=-t-1,\ldots,m+t$ and encode the centre by
\[
 j=i+t+1,\qquad 0\le j<w_t,\qquad w_t=m+2t+2.
\]
The first and last centre symbols are blank guards. Their outputs may
become nonblank on the next step, while two new blank source guards are
added. A cell at fixed physical position moves from encoded position $j$
to position $j+1$ in the next row.

\subsection{Polynomials and linear forms}

For each triple $(\ell,c,r)\in\A^3$, use a tile polynomial $Z_{\ell cr}$.
For each $a\in\A$, use a terminal polynomial $T_a$. We abbreviate the
following linear forms:
\begin{align}
 H&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}Z_{\ell cr}, & K&=\sum_aT_a,\label{pwh:oc:eq:totals}\\
 L&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}\ell Z_{\ell cr}, &
 C&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}c Z_{\ell cr},\label{pwh:oc:eq:features-a}\\
 R&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}r Z_{\ell cr}, &
 O&=\sum_{\ell,c,r}f(\ell,c,r)Z_{\ell cr},\label{pwh:oc:eq:features-b}\\
 \widehat T&=\sum_a aT_a, &
 I&=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}w_jX^j.\label{pwh:oc:eq:features-c}
\end{align}
The alphabet labels themselves provide an injective numerical feature,
with blank label zero. Also define
\begin{equation}
 \CF=\sum_{c\in\F}Z_{\ell cr},\qquad
 \TF=\sum_{a\in\F}T_a,
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:acceptingforms}
\end{equation}
where unspecified indices range freely. These expressions are
abbreviations, not new unknowns. Write
$\rep{k}=1+X+\cdots+X^{k-1}$ for $k\ge1$, and $\rep0=0$.

The precursor \cite{oc:Precursor} uses five further unknown polynomials
$D,Q,B,V,J$ and $G=V+B+Q+D$. Its weighted two-coordinate compiler is
\begin{align*}
 D+Q&=X^{m+1}+X^2YQ, & B+V&=1+YV,\\
 XH+V&=H+XQ, & H+K&=X\rep m+XYH+G,\\
 XC&=L, & XR&=C,\\
 C+\widehat T&=XI+XYO, & \CF&=0,\\
 \TF+J&=B+XJ.
\end{align*}
Here the unknowns lie in $\N[X,Y]$, and $Y$ is a fixed indeterminate.
The new system replaces $Y$ by an unknown $S\in\N[X]$ and supplies two
additional equations that make this replacement exact and canonical.
We reprove the required reconstruction statements below; the precursor's
conclusions are not treated as a black box.

\subsection{Why a fixed substitution cannot be enough}

For any fixed positive integer $W$, the homomorphism
\[
 \Phi_W:\Z[X,Y]\longrightarrow\Z[X],\qquad
 \Phi_W(X)=X,\quad \Phi_W(Y)=X^W
\]
is not injective: $Y$ and $X^W$ have the same image. It becomes injective
on polynomials whose $X$-degree is strictly less than $W$, since then
$j+Wt$ determines $(j,t)$ by quotient and remainder.

The problem is that the required horizontal width depends on how long
the computation runs. For a universal source, there cannot be a total
computable input-only bound on that running time. Simply choosing a
large computable stride does not solve the problem. Allowing an arbitrary
sufficiently large stride would instead introduce infinitely many
auxiliary witnesses. The new calibration chooses the exact stride from
the certificate's own clocks.

\clearpage
\section{The complete eleven-equation compiler}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:compiler}

\subsection{Unknowns, domain, and every residual}

All unknowns now range over $\N[X]$. In addition to $Z_{\ell cr}$ and
$T_a$, introduce
\[
 D,\ Q,\ B,\ V,\ J,\ S,\ E.
\]
There are $s^3+s+7$ unknown polynomials. Continue to abbreviate
$G=V+B+Q+D$, and retain the linear forms
\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:totals}--\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:acceptingforms}. The complete system is
\begin{align}
 D+Q&=X^{m+1}+X^2SQ, &&\tag{1}\label{pwh:oc:eq:c1}\\
 B+V&=1+SV, &&\tag{2}\label{pwh:oc:eq:c2}\\
 XH+V&=H+XQ, &&\tag{3}\label{pwh:oc:eq:shapeh}\\
 H+K&=X\rep m+XSH+G, &&\tag{4}\label{pwh:oc:eq:shapek}\\
 XC&=L, &&\tag{5}\label{pwh:oc:eq:left}\\
 XR&=C, &&\tag{6}\label{pwh:oc:eq:right}\\
 C+\widehat T&=XI+XSO, &&\tag{7}\label{pwh:oc:eq:vertical}\\
 \CF&=0, &&\tag{8}\label{pwh:oc:eq:early}\\
 \TF+J&=B+XJ, &&\tag{9}\label{pwh:oc:eq:terminal}\\
 S+E&=X+XE, &&\tag{10}\label{pwh:oc:eq:stride}\\
 SB&=XD. &&\tag{11}\label{pwh:oc:eq:calibration}
\end{align}
Equalities are identities at every polynomial coefficient. There are no
implicit inequalities, no Booleanity equations, no support predicates,
and no hidden constraints on the degrees of any unknowns.

The distinction between $E$ and $V$ is important. The former is a
one-coordinate prefix that forces a positive stride. The latter is the
clock prefix with one monomial per transition time. The calibration
links the final points of two differently sloped clocks, not their
numerical values at a chosen base.

\subsection{Literal algebraic ledger}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-template and degree bounds]
\label{pwh:oc:prop:ledger}
System~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:c1}--\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:calibration} has eleven equations of
unknown-degree at most two. Its quadratic dependence is confined to
multiplication of $S$ by the five linear forms
\[
 Q,\quad V,\quad H,\quad O,\quad B.
\]
After all abbreviations are expanded, every nonconstant-in-the-unknowns
coefficient has $X$-degree at most two and integer coefficient magnitude
at most $s-1$. These terms depend only on $f$ and $\F$. Input-dependent
constant terms are exactly those formed from $X^{m+1}$, $X\rep m$, and
$XI$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The only products of unknowns in the displayed system occur in
\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:c1}, \eqref{pwh:oc:eq:c2}, \eqref{pwh:oc:eq:shapek}, \eqref{pwh:oc:eq:vertical},
and~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:calibration}. They have the stated form. Each of
$H,O$ is a linear combination of tile unknowns, so expansion does not
increase unknown-degree beyond two. The largest coordinate shift
multiplying an unknown monomial is $X^2$, in~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:c1}.
All weights in $L,C,R,O,\widehat T,I$ lie between zero and $s-1$;
all other nonzero coefficients have magnitude one. Any two appearances
of the same tile in a feature row have different coordinate powers or
different unknown monomials, so the expansion does not accumulate larger
integer coefficients. The input occurs only in the three supplied
constant polynomials stated above.
\end{proof}

This ledger counts polynomial identities and polynomial unknowns.
The five factored products are not five ordinary-integer arithmetic
operations: evaluating a polynomial product, or forming a dense linear
form, has its own cost. No comparison with the repository's 87-operation
ordinary universal polynomial is implicit.

\subsection{A unit-coefficient variant}

For an injective map
$\chi:\A\to\{0,\ldots,B_0\}^{d_0}$ with $\chi(0)=0$, replace each
numerical symbol weight in the three feature equations by each coordinate
of $\chi$. Retain all other equations unchanged. There are then
\begin{equation}
 8+3d_0\quad\text{equations on the same }s^3+s+7\text{ unknowns}.
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:featurecount}
\end{equation}
The proof in the next section applies coordinatewise after occupancy has
been established. It proves that this variant has exactly the same full
solution tuples as the weighted compiler.

Binary digits give $8+3\lceil\log_2s\rceil$ equations with unit
coefficients. Base-$b$ digits give $8+3\lceil\log_bs\rceil$ equations
with coefficient magnitude at most $b-1$. The eleven-row form takes the
single feature $\chi(a)=a$. No optimality assertion is made for these row
counts; fewer, denser equations can have more terms after squaring.

\section{All-solution soundness and canonical reconstruction}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:proof}

The proof order matters. We first derive monomial clocks, their common
height, and the exact stride. Only then can disjoint blocks and unit
occupancy be inferred. Weighted feature equalities are used only after
there is a single symbol, rather than an arbitrary multiset, on each side.

\subsection{Finite nonnegative flow on a ray}

\begin{lemma}[Univariate ray lemma]
\label{pwh:oc:lem:ray}
\wtag\ [The one-variable case ($S=X^a$, $M=X^b$) of Part~I's
Lemma~\ref{pwh:uh:lem:ray}; source~15's statement and proof are kept
because the lemmas of this Part cite them.]
Let $a\ge0$, $b\ge1$, and let $U,V\in\N[X]$. Then
\begin{equation}
 U+V=X^a+X^bV
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:ray}
\end{equation}
holds if and only if, for a unique $k\in\N$,
\[
 U=X^{a+bk},\qquad V=X^a\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}X^{bt}.
\]
In particular, $\wt(U)=1$ and $\wt(V)=k$, where $\wt(P)=P(1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Evaluation at $X=1$ gives $\wt(U)=1$, so $U$ is a monomial with coefficient
one. Regard $V_e$ as a nonnegative integer flow along the directed edge
$e\to e+b$ in $\N$. Equation~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:ray} is conservation with one
source at $a$ and one sink at the exponent of $U$.

To make the flow argument explicit, partition $\N$ into the residue
chains modulo $b$. On any chain not containing $a$, coefficient induction
from its least exponent shows that no positive outgoing flow can arise;
a sink on such a chain would demand a negative coefficient. On the chain
containing $a$, the same induction gives zero flow before $a$, then unit
flow after the source until the sink is reached. Finite support prevents
this unit flow from continuing forever. After the sink the flow is zero.
The sink therefore lies at $a+bk$ for some $k\ge0$, with exactly the
stated prefix. Conversely, these expressions telescope to~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:ray}.
Their exponents and masses establish uniqueness.
\end{proof}

The lemma uses both nonnegative coefficients and finite support. Neither
is an optional side condition. It is a polynomial-semiring power-forcing
mechanism of the kind already present in earlier work \cite{oc:Narendran}.

\subsection{Positive stride and synchronization}

\begin{lemma}[Forced monomial stride and clock endpoints]
\label{pwh:oc:lem:clocks}
Every solution of the full system has integers $W\ge1$ and $h,k\ge0$
such that
\begin{align}
 S&=X^W,& E&=X+X^2+\cdots+X^{W-1},\label{pwh:oc:eq:SE}\\
 D&=X^{m+1+(W+2)h},&
 Q&=\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}X^{m+1+(W+2)t},\label{pwh:oc:eq:DQ}\\
 B&=X^{Wk},& V&=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}X^{Wt}.\label{pwh:oc:eq:BVtemp}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:shapeh} then forces $h=k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:ray} to~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:stride} with source $X$ and
multiplier $X$. Its endpoint is $X^{1+q}$ for some $q\ge0$; put
$W=q+1$. This gives~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:SE}, including the empty prefix at $W=1$.
In particular, $S$ cannot be zero or the constant monomial one.

Now $X^2S=X^{W+2}$ and $S=X^W$ are positive-degree monomials. Apply
the ray lemma to~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:c1} and~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:c2} to obtain
\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:DQ}--\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:BVtemp}. Finally evaluate
\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:shapeh} at $X=1$. The two $H(1)$ terms cancel, giving
$V(1)=Q(1)$, hence $k=h$. No separate synchronization equation is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact self-sizing]
\label{pwh:oc:lem:calibration}
In every solution,
\begin{equation}
 W=m+2h+2.
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:W}
\end{equation}
Thus the stride equals the width of the guarded terminal row.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the monomial expressions from Lemma~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:clocks}, with
$k=h$, into~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:calibration}. Equality of monomials gives
\[
 W(h+1)=m+2+(W+2)h.
\]
Cancel $Wh$ to obtain~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:W}. Conversely, that equality makes
the calibration identity hold for these clock expressions.
\end{proof}

Notice that the cancellation is performed after $S,B,D$ have been proved
to be monomials. It is not an illicit replacement of arbitrary polynomial
identities by degree identities. The positivity of $W$ was obtained
before either clock was decoded.

\subsection{Forced transition and terminal masks}

\begin{lemma}[Canonical masks and unit occupancy]
\label{pwh:oc:lem:masks}
Every solution satisfies
\begin{align}
 H&=\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}X^{Wt}\rep{w_t},
       & w_t&=m+2t+2,\label{pwh:oc:eq:Hmask}\\
 K&=X^{Wh}\rep W,
       & W&=m+2h+2.\label{pwh:oc:eq:Kmask}
\end{align}
Each coefficient of these masks is zero or one. At each permitted source
site exactly one tile polynomial has coefficient one; at each terminal
site exactly one terminal-symbol polynomial has coefficient one. Every
tile or terminal coefficient outside its respective mask vanishes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Rearrange~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:shapeh} as
\[
 (1-X)H=V-XQ
       =\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}X^{Wt}(1-X^{m+2t+2}).
\]
Since $(1-X)\rep r=1-X^r$, the proposed $H$ satisfies this identity.
It is the unique polynomial solution because $1-X$ is not a zero divisor
in $\Z[X]$. Thus~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:Hmask} holds.

The known clock expressions give
\begin{equation}
 G=\sum_{t=0}^{h}X^{Wt}(1+X^{m+2t+1}).
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:Gmask}
\end{equation}
In~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:shapek}, the row at time zero of
$X\rep m+XSH+G$ is $\rep{m+2}$. For each $1\le t\le h$, its row
is
\[
 X\rep{m+2t}+1+X^{m+2t+1}=\rep{m+2t+2}.
\]
Equivalently, summing these identities directly yields
\[
 X\rep m+XSH+G=\sum_{t=0}^{h}X^{Wt}\rep{w_t}.
\]
Subtract the already determined $H$ to obtain~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:Kmask}.
This algebra also covers $h=0$.

By~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:W}, every $w_t$ with $t\le h$ is at most $W$.
The exponent intervals $[Wt,Wt+w_t-1]$ are therefore disjoint for
distinct $t$. The displayed masks have only zero--one coefficients.
Their definitions as sums of nonnegative tile or terminal polynomials
then force exactly one coefficient-one label at each occupied site and
zero coefficients elsewhere. No Booleanity hypothesis has been used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spatial feature equations recover actual windows}

For a source row $t<h$, let $u=w_t$ and let
$(\ell_j,c_j,r_j)$ be the unique triple occupying the coefficient
$X^{Wt+j}$, $0\le j<u$. Here $c_j$ is a local symbol label; the global
configuration remains denoted $c_t(i)$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact horizontal adjacency]
\label{pwh:oc:lem:adjacency}
The feature equations imply
\[
 \ell_0=c_0=0,\qquad c_{u-1}=r_{u-1}=0,
\]
and for $1\le j<u$,
\[
 \ell_j=c_{j-1},\qquad c_j=r_{j-1}.
\]
Thus every source triple is the genuine consecutive window in its row of
centre symbols, with blank exterior.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For source rows, $u\le W-2$. Multiplication by $X$ moves their support
only to positions at most $W-2$ within a block, so it cannot mix distinct
rows. Compare coefficients in $XC=L$. At position zero it gives
$\ell_0=0$; at internal position $j$ it gives $\ell_j=c_{j-1}$; at the
position immediately following the source row it gives $c_{u-1}=0$.
Likewise $XR=C$ gives $c_0=0$, $c_j=r_{j-1}$ internally, and
$r_{u-1}=0$ at the position after the row. Unit occupancy ensures that
each coefficient is the label of a single symbol, rather than an
ambiguous sum of labels. Since the labels $0,\ldots,s-1$ are distinct,
these numerical equalities are symbol equalities.
\end{proof}

For vector-valued features, the same comparisons give equality of every
coordinate of the two feature vectors. Injectivity and $\chi(0)=0$
then recover exactly the same symbols and blanks. This proves the
spatial part of the same-tuple feature variant stated earlier.

\subsection{Vertical reconstruction, including guards and time zero}

\begin{lemma}[Recovery of the cellular evolution]
\label{pwh:oc:lem:evolution}
For $0\le t<h$, the tile at exponent $Wt+j$ is the actual triple
\[
 \bigl(c_t(j-t-2),\ c_t(j-t-1),\ c_t(j-t)\bigr),
 \qquad 0\le j<w_t.
\]
The symbol in the terminal polynomial at exponent $Wh+j$ is
$c_h(j-h-1)$, for $0\le j<W$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At time zero, the coefficient comparison in~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:vertical} sets
the centre-symbol feature to $w_{j-1}$ at positions $1\le j\le m$
and to zero at the two guard positions. If $h>0$, this is the first
source row; if $h=0$, it is already the terminal row. The mask lemma
ensures that no terminal terms intrude on earlier source rows and no
source terms remain on the terminal row.

Suppose a source row $t<h$ has been recovered. By
Lemma~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:adjacency}, its triple at each site is the genuine
neighbourhood of its centre. The corresponding coefficient of $O$ is
therefore the numerical feature of the actual local output. The factor
$XS=X^{W+1}$ places that output in the next block one horizontal
position to the right, exactly as required by the encoding
$j=i+t+1$. Equation~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:vertical} identifies each next-row
centre or terminal symbol with that output.

The two new outer positions receive no contribution from $XSO$ or $XI$.
Their feature must be zero, and occupancy together with injectivity of the
feature makes them blank. They are genuine blank source guards by the
light-cone bound~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:lightcone}. This proves the next-row statement.
Induction recovers every source row and the terminal row. For $h=0$,
the initial coefficient comparison already proves the whole assertion.

All involved shifts stay within the intended blocks: a source row has
width at most $W-2$, and after the horizontal shift its output lies at
positions $1,\ldots,W-2$ at the last step. Thus no part of the induction
uses a numerical equality produced by a block collision.
\end{proof}

The vector-feature proof is identical except that equality of all feature
coordinates is used to recover a symbol. We have therefore justified the
full same-tuple equivalence of the feature variants: all reconstruct the
same actual history with the same clock and marker polynomials.

\subsection{First acceptance and uniqueness of the position marker}

\begin{lemma}[Canonical acceptance]
\label{pwh:oc:lem:acceptance}
Every solution encodes a first singleton acceptance at time $h$. If its
accepting cell has encoded position $j_*$ in the terminal block, then
\begin{equation}
 \TF=X^{Wh+j_*},\qquad
 J=X^{Wh}\rep{j_*},\qquad 1\le j_*\le W-2.
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:J}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:early} is a sum of nonnegative tile polynomials equal
to zero. Thus no source row before $h$ has an accepting centre. The
reconstruction lemma and blank exterior identify these with the actual
earlier configurations.

Apply Lemma~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:ray} to~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:terminal}, with source
$B=X^{Wh}$ and multiplier $X$. It gives a single monomial $\TF$ and
the unique prefix $J$ in~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:J}. The terminal mask restricts the
monomial to the top row. Its two guards are blank, and $0\notin\F$,
so the accepting position lies strictly between them. There is one
accepting cell, not a selected cell among several.
\end{proof}

If the first accepting configuration contains two or more accepting
cells, the system has no solution. A later singleton cannot repair this,
because the earlier accepting event would violate~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:early}.
This qualification is part of the source language, not an omitted edge
case. The one-head universal-machine instantiation avoids it automatically.

\subsection{Completeness and full-tuple single-foldness}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:main}, except for the degree formula]
Suppose the actual evolution has a first singleton acceptance at time $h$.
Set $W=m+2h+2$ and define $S,E,D,Q,B,V$ by the explicit formulas in
Lemmas~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:clocks}--\ref{pwh:oc:lem:calibration}. At every source site
$(j,t)$, put coefficient one at $X^{Wt+j}$ in the polynomial for the
actual neighbourhood triple. At each terminal site, put coefficient one
in the polynomial for its actual symbol. Define $J$ from the unique
accepting position by~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:J}. Every unspecified coefficient is zero.

The clocks and positive-stride equation telescope, and the calibration
holds by the definition of $W$. The shape equations follow from the
identities in Lemma~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:masks}. The feature equations express actual
neighbours, the loaded input, the local update, and the blank guards.
There is no earlier acceptance, and the terminal accepting set is a
singleton. Thus all eleven equations hold.

Conversely, the preceding lemmas reconstruct a genuine first singleton
acceptance from every solution. Determinism makes its time $h$ unique.
The calibration then makes $W$ unique. All clocks and $E$ are fixed by
the ray lemma; every source tile and terminal symbol is fixed by the
actual evolution; and $J$ is fixed by the unique accepting position.
Thus the entire witness tuple is unique, including all auxiliaries.
Its coefficients are Boolean by the clock, mask, and marker formulas.
The algebraic counts were proved in Proposition~\ref{pwh:oc:prop:ledger};
Section~\ref{pwh:oc:sec:resources} proves the exact degree formula.
\end{proof}

\section{An exact bijection with two-coordinate histories}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:bijection}

The new system does more than reproduce the same accepted language. It
translates the entire canonical witness relation of the precursor without
creating stride multiplicity or losing information about any history
component.

\begin{proposition}[The two-coordinate witness shape]
\label{pwh:oc:prop:bivariate}
\wtag\ [The same as Part~I, Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:main} in the nine-row
weighted form of Corollary~\ref{pwh:uh:cor:feature-counts}; source~15's
proof is kept because the bijection, Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:bijection},
uses it.]
For the nine two-coordinate identities in Section~\ref{pwh:oc:sec:semantics},
every solution in $\N[X,Y]^{s^3+s+5}$ has a first singleton accepting time
$h$ and the forms
\begin{align*}
 D&=X^{m+1+2h}Y^h,&Q&=\sum_{t<h}X^{m+1+2t}Y^t,\\
 B&=Y^h,&V&=\sum_{t<h}Y^t,\\
 H&=\sum_{t<h}Y^t\rep{m+2t+2},&
 K&=Y^h\rep{m+2h+2},\\
 \TF&=X^{j_*}Y^h,&J&=Y^h\rep{j_*}.
\end{align*}
All tile and terminal polynomials encode the actual history. The full
witness is unique whenever it exists, and every component has $X$-degree
at most $m+2h+1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The ray lemma extends verbatim to exponent pairs in $\N^2$ whenever the
multiplier is a monomial of positive total degree. Its directed chains
are rays parallel to that multiplier's exponent vector; coefficient
induction on total degree excludes any source-free nonnegative flow.
Apply it to the two clocks, with multipliers $X^2Y$ and $Y$.
Evaluation at $(1,1)$ in the source-shape equation synchronizes their
heights. Division by $1-X$ gives the displayed $H$, and the terminal
shape equation gives the displayed $K$.

Distinct powers of $Y$ separate all rows, so these masks have unit
coefficients without needing a stride inequality. Nonnegativity forces
unit occupancy. The spatial and vertical feature comparisons now work
exactly as in Lemmas~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:adjacency} and~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:evolution}, with
$Y$ recording time instead of a block offset. The acceptance equations
and the horizontal ray lemma give the displayed $\TF,J$ and first
singleton acceptance. Determinism fixes every coordinate as before.
The right endpoint and terminal guard attain $X$-degree $m+2h+1$;
all remaining components have no larger horizontal degree.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full-witness packing and unpacking]
\label{pwh:oc:thm:bijection}
For each input word, there is a bijection between the full solution set
of the precursor's nine two-coordinate identities and the full solution
set of the eleven univariate identities. On a two-coordinate solution of
height $h$, set $W=m+2h+2$, apply $Y\mapsto X^W$ to every old component,
and add
\[
 S=X^W,\qquad E=X+\cdots+X^{W-1}.
\]
The inverse deletes these two added components and sends every old
component's monomial $X^e$ to
\[
 X^{e\bmod W}Y^{\lfloor e/W\rfloor}.
\]
Both maps are effective on supplied finite witness tuples.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a two-coordinate solution, Proposition~\ref{pwh:oc:prop:bivariate} gives
horizontal degree less than $W$ in every component. Packing is injective
on those components, and specialization preserves the nine inherited
identities. The added ray equation telescopes; the calibration follows
from $W=m+2h+2$. Thus the forward map is well defined.

For a univariate solution, Lemmas~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:clocks}--\ref{pwh:oc:lem:acceptance}
put every old component into the stated canonical blocks. Quotient and
remainder therefore recover its clock, tile, terminal, or marker data
uniquely. One may verify the inherited equations either through the
semantic reconstruction or directly through injectivity. For the latter,
every term in each decoded residual has horizontal degree below $W$.
The potentially largest shift is the term $X^2YQ$, whose final horizontal
exponent is $W-1$. Spatial shifts of source rows reach at most $W-2$;
vertical outputs reach at most $W-2$ in the terminal block; $XJ$ reaches
at most $W-2$; and terminal guards have exponent $W-1$. The input
endpoint has exponent $m+1\le W-1$.

Packing is injective on each such residual polynomial, so the vanishing
univariate residual implies the vanishing two-coordinate residual.
Finally, $S$ and $E$ are forced by the recovered height and cannot vary
independently. The two maps therefore compose to the identity on both
full solution sets.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ At the intake the bijection was checked on the shared worked
example (input $(1,0,0,0,2)$, $h=4$): packing all 73 components of
Part~I's witness by $Y\mapsto X^{15}$ gives exactly this Part's
corresponding components, with $S=X^{15}$.

\subsection{What is minimal about the stride}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal stride for the one-block-per-row convention]
\label{pwh:oc:prop:minimalstride}
Suppose a row-major encoder places each occupied site $(j,t)$ at exponent
$j+Wt$ and requires \emph{every row}, including the entire terminal row,
to have its within-block positions in $\{0,\ldots,W-1\}$. For the guarded
history of height $h$, it must have $W\ge m+2h+2$. The calibration attains
this lower bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The terminal row contains exactly $m+2h+2$ consecutive occupied positions,
starting at zero. Containment in one $W$-position block therefore requires
the inequality. Equation~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:W} gives equality.
\end{proof}

This is deliberately a restricted minimality statement. It is not a lower
bound for all injective encodings of a finite cone, nor even for row-major
encodings that allow the last row to spill beyond its nominal block.
It says that the calibration wastes no extra width under the exact
block-decoding convention used in our soundness proof.

\section{Universality and the sharp representation boundary}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:universality}

\subsection{A transparent one-head source}

Take a deterministic one-tape Turing machine with tape alphabet $\Gamma$,
blank $\square$, state set $Q_0$, and accepting state $q_{\rm acc}$.
Its transition function is total on nonaccepting states; a rejecting halt
can be replaced by a permanent nonaccepting stationary loop. After
acceptance, keep the accepting head in place. Standard universal machines
with a finite program-and-input word provide such a source
\cite{oc:Turing}.

Use the cellular alphabet
\begin{equation}
 \A=\Gamma\ \sqcup\ (Q_0\times\Gamma),\qquad
 s=|\Gamma|(1+|Q_0|),
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:tmca}
\end{equation}
identifying the unmarked blank with label zero. An unmarked symbol is a
cell without the head; a marked pair $(q,a)$ is a cell with the head in
state $q$ reading $a$. The radius-one update is defined by these ordered
cases. A central accepting head stays fixed. A nonaccepting central head
writes its new symbol and either stays marked with the new state or
becomes unmarked when moving. An unmarked centre receives a head from
its left neighbour when that head moves right; otherwise it receives a
head from its right neighbour when that head moves left; otherwise it
keeps its tape symbol. The order resolves invalid multiple-head
neighbourhoods and makes the table total. Such neighbourhoods never
occur from the one-head input encoding.

\begin{lemma}[Step-for-step source simulation]
\label{pwh:oc:lem:tmca}
From a finite word carrying exactly one head, this cellular automaton
simulates one machine transition per cellular step and preserves exactly
one head. Its first acceptance, when present, is a singleton.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The old head writes exactly the prescribed symbol. A stationary move
retains exactly that head in its new state. For a moving instruction,
exactly the indicated adjacent cell receives the new head state, while
the old cell becomes unmarked. Every other cell retains its tape symbol.
Since there was only one head, there are no competing incoming moves.
Induction proves the simulation and the one-head invariant. Taking the
accepting alphabet to be all $(q_{\rm acc},a)$ gives the singleton claim.
\end{proof}

The input encoder places the initial head on the first input symbol and
uses the remaining cells unmarked. An empty machine input is represented
by one blank cell carrying the head, so our condition $m\ge1$ is harmless.
For a fixed universal machine, both $f$ and $\F$ are fixed. Loading a
program and its input requires only forming the finite word and the three
constant polynomials $X^{m+1}$, $X\rep m$, and $XI$; it does not require
running the program.

\subsection{Fixed-template completeness and noncomputable bounds}

\begin{corollary}[A fixed unambiguous hard family]
\label{pwh:oc:cor:hard}
For one fixed cellular rule and accepting set, solvability of the eleven
identities on the effective word-input family is $\Sigma^0_1$-complete.
Every input has either no solution or exactly one full solution, and all
coefficients of any solution are zero or one.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:main} to the cellular simulation of a fixed
universal machine. The computable input map just described reduces the
machine's acceptance problem to solvability, proving
$\Sigma^0_1$-hardness. For membership, enumerate all finite tuples of
nonnegative integer polynomials and check the eleven identities by exact
coefficient arithmetic. The uniqueness and Boolean-coefficient assertions
are those of the main theorem.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ The univariate counterpart of Part~I's
Corollary~\ref{pwh:uh:cor:hard-intro}.

This is an explicit generic compiler theorem. The package does not claim
to supply or formally verify a particular numerical universal-machine
table. Its four-symbol worked rule is an illustrative terminating example,
not a universal rule. The universal instance is obtained by the proved
machine-to-cellular construction once a universal source table is chosen.

\begin{corollary}[No computable stride or degree bound]
\label{pwh:oc:cor:nobound}
On the fixed universal word-input family, there is no total computable
input-only function bounding the stride of the solution on every
solvable input. There is also no such function bounding the maximum
witness degree.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A stride bound $g(w)$ would bound the possible accepting time through
$W=m+2h+2$. Simulating up to that finite bound would decide acceptance.
A degree bound would also bound $W$, because $S=X^W$ is one witness
component. Either would contradict Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:hard}.
Alternatively, a degree bound together with Boolean coefficients makes
the set of candidate full tuples finite and effectively searchable.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ The univariate counterpart of Part~I's
Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:nobound}, and like it the bounded-search argument
of Proposition \texttt{cdc:bd:prop:nobound} in~\cite{cdc}; Part~III's
Corollary~\ref{pwh:bt:cor:bound} is the counter-machine version.

\begin{proposition}[Every fixed-stride fibre is decidable]
\label{pwh:oc:prop:fibre}
If $S=X^W$ is supplied as part of the input, the full system is solvable
precisely when
\[
 h=(W-m-2)/2
\]
is a nonnegative integer and the source evolution has first singleton
acceptance at that time. Thus the fixed-stride problem is decidable.
All remaining identities are affine-linear in the remaining unknowns.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Necessity is Lemma~\ref{pwh:oc:lem:calibration} followed by semantic soundness;
sufficiency is the explicit witness construction. A finite simulation
checks the condition. Substitution of a supplied $S$ turns every listed
quadratic product into multiplication by a fixed coefficient polynomial.
\end{proof}

The undecidable family is therefore an existential union of decidable
linear fibres over an unbounded monomial parameter. The ray equation
makes that parameter Boolean-coefficient, but it does not put a computable
bound on its exponent.

\section{Exact degree, support, and coefficient ledgers}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:resources}

\begin{proposition}[Exact maximum degree]
\label{pwh:oc:prop:degree}
For an accepting history of height $h$ and stride $W=m+2h+2$, the maximum
witness degree is
\[
 D_{\max}=\max\{W,\,Wh+W-1\}
 =\begin{cases}W,&h=0,\\W(h+1)-1,&h\ge1.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular, it grows quadratically in $h$ for fixed input length.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The right-clock endpoint $D=X^{Wh+W-1}$ attains degree $Wh+W-1$.
The terminal row has the same final exponent. Every tile, terminal,
clock-prefix, and position-marker exponent is at most this endpoint.
The new polynomial $E$ has degree $W-1$, while $S$ has degree $W$.
These are all the components. For $h\ge1$, $Wh+W-1\ge W$; for $h=0$,
the endpoint degree is only $W-1$, so $S$ is the largest component.
\end{proof}

This completes the remaining assertion of Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:main}.
The special case $h=0$ should not be hidden in an asymptotic expression:
the stride polynomial then has degree one larger than the terminal row.

\begin{proposition}[Exact full-witness mass]
\label{pwh:oc:prop:mass}
If the unique accepting cell is at encoded horizontal position $j_*$,
then the sum of all coefficients in the entire witness tuple is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M=h^2+(m+7)h+2m+6+j_*.
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:mass}
\end{equation}
Since every coefficient is Boolean, this is also the total number of
nonzero coefficients across all components, counting components separately.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The source tiles contribute
\[
 H(1)=\sum_{t<h}(m+2t+2)=h^2+(m+1)h.
\]
The terminal polynomials contribute $W$. The two clock endpoints
$D,B$ contribute $2$; their prefixes $Q,V$ contribute $2h$.
The accepting-position marker contributes $j_*$. Finally $S$ and $E$
contribute $1+(W-1)=W$. Summing and substituting $W=m+2h+2$ gives
\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:mass}.
\end{proof}

The count includes blank guards, blank interior tiles, and all clock and
stride auxiliaries. It does not silently count only nonblank computation
cells. Compared with the precursor's full witness, packing preserves
every existing coefficient and adds exactly $W$ nonzero coefficients
in $S,E$.

One may recover the acceptance time from the stride by the exact formula
$h=(\deg S-m-2)/2$. Every solution therefore satisfies
$\deg S\ge m+2$ and $\deg S\equiv m\pmod2$. These are consequences of the
system, not separately imposed congruence tests.

\section{A single quartic and an ordinary bounded export}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:quartic}

\subsection{Same-witness reduction to one equation}

Let $P_1,\ldots,P_{11}$ be the full left-minus-right residuals of the
system, after expanding all linear-form abbreviations. Regard them as
polynomials in the $n=s^3+s+7$ unknowns with coefficients in $\Z[X]$.
Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal Q_w(U_1,\ldots,U_n)=\sum_{i=1}^{11}P_i^2.
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:quartic}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[One quartic, identical full solutions]
\label{pwh:oc:thm:quartic}
Over $\N[X]^n$, the equation $\mathcal Q_w=0$ has exactly the same full
solutions as the eleven-row system. Its unknown-degree is exactly four.
Its coefficient polynomials have $X$-degree at most $2(m+1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any supplied tuple, the evaluated residuals are ordinary polynomials
in $\Z[X]$. If some residual is nonzero, let $e$ be the largest residual
degree. The coefficient of $X^{2e}$ in the sum of their squares is the
sum of squares of the nonzero leading coefficients of precisely the
residuals of degree $e$. It is a strictly positive integer. Thus the
sum cannot vanish. Conversely, vanishing of every residual clearly
makes the sum zero. This proves exact equality of the full solution sets.

Every residual has unknown-degree at most two, so the sum has degree at
most four. In the first residual, the term $-X^2SQ$ contributes
$X^4S^2Q^2$ after squaring. No other residual has a quadratic monomial
$SQ$, and no other pair of terms can cancel this degree-four monomial.
The degree is therefore exactly four. The largest coordinate coefficient
degree in a residual is at most $\max\{2,m+1\}=m+1$, since $m\ge1$.
Squaring gives the stated coefficient-degree bound.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ The univariate counterpart of Part~I's
Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:quadratic}, one degree higher because the
identities are quadratic.

This is a quartic equation over a polynomial semiring. Its root variables
are themselves univariate polynomials. It is not an ordinary quartic
with only $n$ natural-number unknowns, and its existence does not settle
an ordinary single-fold or finite-fold Diophantine conjecture.

\subsection{External degree truncation gives ordinary integer equations}

A useful, entirely explicit finite export is available when a cutoff is
supplied externally. This makes the hidden amount of scalar data visible.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded ordinary quartic export]
\label{pwh:oc:thm:boundedexport}
For each supplied integer $L\ge0$, replace every polynomial unknown by
\[
 U_i(X)=\sum_{t=0}^{L}u_{i,t}X^t,\qquad u_{i,t}\in\N.
\]
Coefficient comparison in the eleven residuals yields an ordinary
quadratic system on $n(L+1)$ natural-number unknowns, with at most
\[
 11(K_L+1)\quad\text{rows},\qquad K_L=\max\{2L+2,m+1\}.
\]
The sum of squares of these scalar residuals is an ordinary polynomial
of degree at most four with the same solutions. It has exactly one
natural solution when the source has first singleton acceptance and its
canonical witness has $D_{\max}\le L$, and no solution otherwise.
No Boolean side equations are required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the substitution, the largest degree contributed by an unknown
product and its fixed coordinate shift is at most $2L+2$; input constants
have degree at most $m+1$. Comparing all coefficients through $K_L$
therefore imposes the complete polynomial identities. Each coefficient
is quadratic or lower in the scalar variables $u_{i,t}$. Natural scalar
coefficients produce exactly polynomial tuples in $\N[X]^n$ of degree
at most $L$, with a unique zero-padded coefficient representation.
Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:main} now gives precisely the stated zero-or-one
solution count and forces Boolean coefficients automatically.
A sum of squares of integer residual values vanishes exactly when all
are zero, proving the final scalar-polynomial assertion.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ The univariate counterpart of Part~I's
Proposition~\ref{pwh:uh:prop:bounded}.

The cutoff is not part of the fixed univariate compiler. The scalar arity
grows with it, and Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:nobound} prevents choosing a total
computable adequate cutoff from the input alone for the universal family.
Thus this theorem is a bounded export, not a shortcut from polynomial
single-foldness to ordinary fixed-arity single-foldness.

\section{A parsimonious extension to labelled branching}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:branching}

The clock and geometry proof does not require determinism. Determinism
is used only to make the reconstructed accepting history unique. This
separation allows a precise counting extension.

Let $\mathcal R$ be a finite indexed set of labelled local transitions.
Each $\tau\in\mathcal R$ has a source triple
$(\ell_\tau,c_\tau,r_\tau)$, an output $a_\tau$, and its own identity
label. Different labels may have identical source and output data.
Assume each source triple has at least one allowed transition, and that
there is exactly one transition with source triple $(0,0,0)$, with
output zero. The latter condition makes the infinite blank exterior
both quiescent and free of unrecorded choice multiplicity.

A labelled computation chooses a permitted labelled transition at every
source site. Choices outside the finite guarded region are the unique
all-blank transition. Its next configuration is given by the chosen
outputs. First singleton acceptance is interpreted along that chosen
history.

\begin{theorem}[Labelled-history root correspondence]
\label{pwh:oc:thm:branching}
Replace the $s^3$ deterministic tile unknowns by one $Z_\tau$ per labelled
transition. Define $H,L,C,R,O,\CF$ using the corresponding source,
output, and accepting data. The same eleven identities, now on
$|\mathcal R|+s+7$ unknowns, have solutions in bijection with finite
labelled computation histories ending at their first singleton acceptance.
For each such history, every auxiliary polynomial is uniquely determined,
all coefficients are Boolean, and the degree and mass formulas remain
unchanged.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ray, synchronization, calibration, and mask arguments use only the
sum of the tile unknowns, so they are unchanged. Unit occupancy now
selects one labelled transition at every site. Feature adjacency recovers
its source triple, and the vertical equation matches the next symbol
to that transition's output. This reconstructs a valid labelled history.
The acceptance equations give its first singleton accepting event.

Conversely, any such labelled history determines one coefficient-one
label per source site, its terminal symbols, and all clocks and markers
by the earlier formulas. They satisfy the system. Two different labelled
histories differ in a source label, a configuration, or their stopping
time, hence in a witness component. Conversely, no auxiliary can vary
after the labelled history is fixed. The unique quiescent exterior
prevents an unencoded infinite collection of exterior choices from being
counted as distinct histories. The mask and clock counts establish the
same resource formulas.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ The univariate counterpart of Part~I's
Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:nondet}.

The counted objects are labelled local histories, not merely sequences
of configurations and not equivalence classes under rescheduling of
independent actions. Forgetting duplicate labels changes the counting
problem. A probabilistic interpretation would likewise require explicit
weights and a separate summation semantics; root correspondence alone
is not a theorem about probabilities.

\section{Two essential constraints and literal counterexamples}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:counterexamples}

\subsection{Without calibration, padding creates infinitely many witnesses}

Fix any source evolution with first singleton acceptance at height $h$.
Take its two-coordinate witness and substitute $Y=X^W$ for any
$W\ge m+2h+2$. Add $S=X^W$ and $E=X+\cdots+X^{W-1}$.
Every inherited equation and the positive-stride equation hold. All
components remain Boolean, because the blocks do not collide.

However, the calibration holds only when $W=m+2h+2$. Deleting it
therefore introduces infinitely many full witnesses for the same
computation, distinguished by $S,E$ and the packed exponents.
A mere lower bound on the stride cannot restore full-tuple uniqueness.
This is a failure of canonicalization even among perfectly valid,
nonoverlapping, Boolean histories; no malformed coefficients are needed.

\subsection{Without the positive-stride equation, zero erases time}

Consider alphabet $\{0,1\}$, the all-zero local rule $f\equiv0$,
accepting set $\{1\}$, and input word $w=(0)$. The source never accepts.
Delete only equation~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:stride}. The following natural-polynomial
tuple satisfies every remaining equation:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 S=E=D=B=J=0,\qquad Q=X^2,\qquad V=1,\\
 Z_{000}=1+X+X^2,\qquad Z_{\ell cr}=0\ ((\ell,c,r)\ne(0,0,0)),
 \qquad T_0=T_1=0.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{pwh:oc:eq:ghost}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the two clocks become $Q=X^2$ and $V=1$; the source-shape equation
is $(X-1)(1+X+X^2)=X^3-1$; and the terminal-shape equation is
$1+X+X^2=X+(1+X^2)$. All features and accepting forms vanish.
The terminal marker equation and calibration are $0=0$.
The deleted positive-stride residual, however, is $-X$.

Thus $S=0$ can erase the entire future and leave a false certificate of
acceptance. The equation forcing a positive-degree monomial must be
proved before the clock equations are interpreted as finite runs.
The implementation reproduces this literal counterexample coefficientwise.

\section{Executable validation and trust boundaries}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:validation}

\subsection{Implementation interface}

The implementation uses exact sparse dictionaries with nonnegative
integer exponents. Residual polynomials may have signed integer
coefficients, but the public witness interface validates every supplied
witness coefficient as a natural number. It rejects negative exponents,
negative coefficients, floating-point values, Boolean values masquerading
as integers, and missing or extra witness coordinates. Rule tables are
copied into immutable tuples; compiled residual mappings are deep-copied
and frozen. Mutating a caller's original table does not alter the meaning
of an already created compiler.

The source compiler accepts a rule, an accepting set, and a word. It does
\emph{not} accept a horizon. A separate candidate generator takes an
external horizon so that tests can construct both valid and invalid
proposed certificates. It deliberately generates candidates even at
nonaccepting times or after a previous acceptance; the full residual
checker must reject them. The independent truth check uses a separate
dense cellular evolution, with no stride, clock, or polynomial packing.

The finite-state solver is separate from the cellular compiler. It
accepts integer coefficient matrices, a coefficient-vector right-hand
side, and a natural bound. It enumerates finite memories, solves the
inhomogeneous prefix, and searches the homogeneous graph for a draining
path. This independently exercises the negative theorem's algorithm.

\section{Further research questions and concrete next steps}
\label{pwh:oc:sec:future}

These questions are proposed continuations of the proved construction.
They are not all asserted to be previously published open problems.
The first task in any priority claim is a focused literature comparison.

\subsection{Can the eleven rows be reduced without changing the full fibre?}\label{pwh:oc:q:rows}

The three feature rows already use an injective one-coordinate code.
The remaining rows pay separately for two clocks, two aggregate masks,
first acceptance, a position marker, positive stride, and calibration.
Which of these can be combined while retaining a bijection of all
natural-polynomial witnesses? The counterexamples in
Section~\ref{pwh:oc:sec:counterexamples} give immediate regression tests:
any proposed merger must reject the zero-stride ghost and prevent
arbitrary padding. A reduction should publish the full residual vector
and inverse witness map, not only an implication between accepted inputs.

\wtag\ \emph{Answered in part by the research programme (note of
2~October 2026).} The review of this manuscript~\cite{reviewOC} proves, and
checks by exact expansion on 25 emitted schemas, a projection to ten
quadratic identities on $s^3+s+6$ unknowns over $\N[X]$: put $S=XS_0$,
$E=XE_0$, $D=S_0B$, replace the stride identity by $S_0+E_0=1+XE_0$ and
delete the calibration identity; every product still contains $S_0$, the
largest coordinate shift rises from $X^2$ to $X^3$, and the full
natural-polynomial zero sets correspond bijectively. The review publishes
the residuals and the witness map, as asked above. This was not rechecked at
this write. Whether fewer than ten rows suffice remains open.

\subsection{How much shared-stride nonlinearity is necessary?}

The weighted compiler uses $S$ times five specified linear forms.
Can universality with automatically Boolean witnesses be achieved with
fewer such products, or with only one explicit quadratic monomial after
introducing linear auxiliaries? A useful classification would fix the
coefficient ring, the allowed coordinate shifts, the variable count
convention, and whether products of linear forms count as one gate or
many expanded monomials. The linear obstruction gives an exact zero
nonlinearity lower bound, but does not distinguish intermediate bilinear
normal forms.

\subsection{Can one equation have degree two or three?}

The same-witness sum of squares gives a single quartic. Can one directly
construct a single quadratic or cubic equation over $\N[X]$ with the
same canonical universal behavior? The finite-state theorem rules out
degree one, but it does not rule out two or three. Combining signed
residuals by a simple sum is unsound because cancellation is possible;
a lower-degree finalizer would need a rigorously proved noncancellation
mechanism or a different certificate representation.

\subsection{A fully numerical universal instance}\label{pwh:oc:q:numerical}

Choose and document one actual universal machine table, including its
tape alphabet, halt convention, ordinary program-input format, and empty
input handling. Generate the cellular table by~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:tmca}, then
emit all eleven equations and their single quartic. The meaningful
milestone is a verified complete input-to-certificate bridge with a
numerical value of $s^3+s+7$, not merely a small table advertised as
universal. A proof-assistant audit of the source simulation should
precede any claim about a trusted numerical universal polynomial.

\wtag\ The same target is posed in Part~I (Section~\ref{pwh:uh:q:numerical})
and Part~III (Section~\ref{pwh:bt:q:concrete}).

\subsection{Tighter packing than constant-width blocks}

Our stride is minimal for the stated one-block-per-row decoder, but early
rows leave unused positions. A triangular layout using cumulative row
widths would store the cone more densely. Can a fixed-degree polynomial
system force such a variable-offset layout while keeping every auxiliary
unique? A solution must account for the changing row-to-row displacement
and must not replace it with a freely chosen sequence of offsets. Exact
support and maximum-degree formulas would make the improvement measurable.

\subsection{Ordinary arithmetic with a complete fibre ledger}\label{pwh:oc:q:packing}

The bounded coefficient export is explicit but grows in scalar arity.
An ordinary integer packing would need a base, a carry bound, a finite
support representation, and a proof that every integer solution decodes
correctly. The new stride supplies a canonical polynomial size parameter,
not automatically a canonical ordinary-integer base or an ordinary
Diophantine monomial predicate. A first concrete goal is a bounded
integer packing with a proved zero-set bijection; an unbounded extension
must keep the ordinary single-fold question separate from what has
actually been proved.

\wtag\ The same question is posed in Part~I (Section~\ref{pwh:uh:q:packing})
and Part~III (Section~\ref{pwh:bt:q:arithmetic}).

\subsection{Linear compilers with controlled but unbounded coefficient height}

Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:no-linear} rules out every total computable input-only
bound on the minimum coefficient height for a universal univariate
linear family. It does not forbid bounds in terms of the accepting
running time, the degree of a solution, or another noncomputable
complexity measure. For an explicit linear universal compiler, determine
how coefficient height grows along genuine accepting histories, and
whether that growth can be reduced while preserving full-witness
uniqueness. This would quantify the alternative to our degree-two route.

\subsection{Compositional output and labelled counting interfaces}

The terminal polynomials contain the entire final tape, but the present
interface is acceptance-oriented. A composition theorem should specify
how one machine's canonical output becomes another's canonical input,
including head position, blank padding, and word length. On the branching
side, implement Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:branching} and verify counts of labelled
histories on finite examples. Distinguish duplicate labels, configuration
histories, and schedules from the outset; these produce genuinely
different root-counting questions.

\subsection{Kernel verification of the obstruction and the positive compiler}\label{pwh:oc:q:kernel}

The finite-state theorem is a useful first formalization target: bounded
coefficient vectors, a memory graph, draining paths, and the polynomial
reconstruction theorem are all finite combinatorial objects. For the
positive compiler, begin with finitely supported maps $\N\to\N$,
the ray lemma, and the cancellation identity for $1-X$. Next prove
clock synchronization and exact calibration, then the mask and
unit-occupancy lemmas. Feature reconstruction and the source-machine
bridge come last. This order ensures that no semantic assumption about
well-formed tableaux is smuggled into the proof of their well-formedness.

\wtag\ Parts~I and~III propose the same programme
(Sections~\ref{pwh:uh:q:kernel} and~\ref{pwh:bt:q:analysis}).

\section{Conclusion}

The precursor's requested one-coordinate affine-linear compiler cannot
retain a computable bound on full-witness coefficient heights. This is
not just a failure of a particular encoding: a finite-state algorithm
rules out the whole proposed class, even with nonunique witnesses and
input-dependent matrices.

The obstruction has a precise positive counterpart. Replacing the fixed
time indeterminate by a shared stride unknown makes the system quadratic.
The equations $S+E=X+XE$ and $SB=XD$ force a positive monomial stride
and calibrate it to the terminal width. The resulting eleven identities
encode a whole finite computation, force unit occupancy, reject earlier
acceptance, and uniquely determine every auxiliary. Their full solutions
are Boolean-coefficient and are in bijection with the earlier
space--time witnesses.

The established advance is therefore a sharp linear--quadratic boundary
for this coefficient-bounded representation problem, together with an
explicit canonical construction and exact resource formulas. The
univariate polynomial domain, the uninstantiated numerical universal
source table, and the absence of proof-assistant checking remain clear
boundaries of the present result. They leave specific next steps rather
than an unqualified claim to have solved an ordinary Diophantine
representation conjecture.

\subsection{The question addressed}
The broad existence of an integer Diophantine representation for an
effectively enumerable acceptance relation is supplied by MRDP. Merely
applying that theorem to another universal programming language does not
usually reveal what is special about the language. Here the target is a
different question:
\begin{quote}
Can a Turing-complete transition system have an explicit \emph{linear}
algebraic certificate whose correctness survives arbitrary coefficient
characteristic, whose full witness is unique, and whose failure can be
compared exactly with continuous relaxation?
\end{quote}
The answer is affirmative when the unknowns are finite-support
polynomials in specified subrings. The representation exposes precisely
where the computational power resides: in unbounded finite support and
in two independent boundary restrictions, rather than in multiplication
between unknowns.

The substrate used here is \emph{linear boundary transport}. A monomial
is a configuration. Multiplication by $X$ or $Y$ increments a counter;
extracting the constant coefficient in one coordinate tests whether that
counter is zero; shifting the remaining coefficients down decrements a
positive counter. A finite control table combines these operations. The
linear extension is not itself deterministic on arbitrary superpositions,
but it is deterministic on every individual configuration monomial. The
proofs exploit the latter fact and do not assume that arbitrary solutions
are positive.

\subsection{What is inherited and what is proved here}

external input. Instruction sets matter: Dudenhefner's account explicitly
distinguishes variants and establishes undecidability for its $\mathrm{CM2}$
model~\cite{bt:dudenhefner}. Our instruction set allows arbitrary destinations
for both branches and therefore contains that model. We do not infer
universality from a reversible or differently restricted variant.

Undecidability of constrained linear polynomial equations is not new as
a general phenomenon. Narendran proved undecidability when polynomial
solutions are required to have nonnegative coefficients~\cite{bt:narendran}.
Dong proved a polynomial-time result for a particular single homogeneous
univariate equation with all unknown polynomials nonzero and
nonnegative~\cite{bt:dong2023}. Dong also proved undecidability for linear
Laurent-polynomial systems with designated monomial unknowns~\cite{bt:dong2025}.
Our restrictions are different: selected unknowns must omit a coordinate,
and in the scalar presentation all unknowns must omit a row-tagging

\section{The algebra of a deterministic transition graph}\label{pwh:bt:sec:graph}

\subsection{Finite support and terminal configurations}
Fix a nonzero commutative ring $R$ with identity. Thus $1_R\ne0_R$; no
assumption of characteristic zero, absence of zero divisors, or ordering
is made. Let $S$ be a set of configurations and let
\[
 f:S\longrightarrow S\cup\{\dagger\}
\]
be a deterministic partial transition, where $f(s)=\dagger$ means that
$s$ is terminal. We count a terminal configuration as part of a halting
history. A history that halts at time $h$ is
\[
 c_0=s,\quad c_{t+1}=f(c_t)\ (0\le t<h),\qquad f(c_h)=\dagger.
\]
In particular, an initially terminal source has $h=0$.

Let $\V_R=R^{(S)}$ be the free $R$-module of finitely supported coefficient
functions on $S$, with basis $e_s$. Define the linear transport operator
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:U}
 Ue_s=\begin{cases}e_{f(s)},&f(s)\ne\dagger,\\0,&f(s)=\dagger.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This definition gives a well-defined endomorphism of $\V_R$ even if a
configuration has infinitely many predecessors: the image of a finite
linear combination is still finite. We use bounded indegree only in the
Hilbert-space section.

A \emph{weak component} means a connected component after forgetting the
directions of the edges $s\to f(s)$; terminal vertices have no outgoing
edge. A weak component either contains one terminal vertex, contains one
directed cycle, or contains neither. If a component contains a terminal
vertex, every vertex in it eventually reaches that terminal vertex.
Indeed, across each undirected edge the property of eventually reaching
a specified terminal is equivalent at the two endpoints. The same
argument shows that a component with no terminal contains only
nonhalting configurations.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-support transport and the full fiber]\label{pwh:bt:thm:unclocked}
For every $s\in S$, the equation
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:unclocked}
 (I-U)H=e_s,\qquad H\in\V_R,
\end{equation}
has a solution if and only if the computation from $s$ halts.
When the computation halts at time $h$, define
\[
 H_s=\sum_{t=0}^{h}e_{c_t}.
\]
The complete solution set is
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:fiber}
 H_s+\bigoplus_{C\text{ a directed cycle}}R\one_C,
 \qquad \one_C=\sum_{v\in C}e_v.
\end{equation}
Here a direct sum means that only finitely many cycle coefficients are
nonzero. All cycles are disjoint from the halting component of $s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a halting history the sum telescopes:
\[
 (I-U)\sum_{t=0}^{h}e_{c_t}=e_{c_0}.
\]
No configuration can repeat before a deterministic computation halts;
a repetition would force an infinite periodic future. Hence the
coefficients of $H_s$ are zero or one.

Suppose instead that the computation from $s$ does not halt, and let $D$
be its weak component. Define the linear functional
\[
 \lambda_D(H)=\sum_{v\in D}H(v).
\]
The sum is finite for each $H\in\V_R$. There is no terminal vertex in $D$,
and no edge crosses a weak-component boundary, so
$\lambda_D U=\lambda_D$. Applying $\lambda_D$ to
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:unclocked} would give $0=1_R$, a contradiction.

It remains to calculate the kernel. Let $K=UK$ have finite support, and
let $K(v)\ne0$. The coefficient equation at $v$ implies that some
predecessor $w$ has $K(w)\ne0$: otherwise the finite sum of nonzero-support
predecessor coefficients would be empty. Repeatedly choosing such a
predecessor stays in a finite set, so eventually produces a directed
cycle. The selected predecessor chain gives a forward path from that
cycle to $v$. A deterministic directed cycle cannot have an outgoing
edge leaving the cycle, so $v$ lies on it. Thus the support of $K$ is a
union of directed cycles. On a cycle the coefficient equations say that
the coefficients are constant, because no noncycle predecessor has
nonzero coefficient. Conversely each $\one_C$ belongs to the kernel.
Subtracting $H_s$ from any solution proves \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:fiber}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An exact sequence]\label{pwh:bt:cor:exact-sequence}
Let $\mathcal C$ be the set of directed cycles and $\mathcal D$ the set of
nonhalting weak components. There is an exact sequence of $R$-modules
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:exact-sequence}
 0\longrightarrow R^{(\mathcal C)}\longrightarrow\V_R
 \xrightarrow{\ I-U\ }\V_R\longrightarrow R^{(\mathcal D)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The left injection sends a cycle to $\one_C$. The right map sends $e_s$
to its component basis vector if its component has no terminal, and to
zero otherwise.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The kernel statement is Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:unclocked}. In the cokernel the
relations are $e_v=e_{f(v)}$ for a live vertex and $e_v=0$ for a terminal
vertex. Along any finite undirected path, adjacent basis vectors
therefore represent the same class. Terminal components collapse to
zero; every other component gives one generator. The component-sum
maps show that these remaining generators have no nontrivial relations.
All reductions of a finite combination use only finitely many paths.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{pwh:bt:rem:separation}
Component membership is not being used as an algorithm. It is a
mathematical separation functional proving nonsolvability. The theorem
allows negative coefficients, modular cancellation, and zero divisors;
none of those can turn the component sum $1_R$ into zero.
\end{remark}

\section{The clocked resolvent and uniqueness}\label{pwh:bt:sec:clock}

Adjoin an indeterminate $Z$, and extend $U$ coefficientwise to
$\V_R[Z]$. The clocked equation is
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:clocked}
 (I-ZU)H=e_s,\qquad H\in\V_R[Z].
\end{equation}
An element of $\V_R[Z]$ has both a finite time degree and finitely many
configuration coefficients in each time layer. Equivalently, it has
finite total support in $S\times\N$.

\begin{theorem}[Characteristic-free unique certificate]\label{pwh:bt:thm:clocked}
The operator $I-ZU$ is injective on $\V_R[Z]$. Equation
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:clocked} has a solution precisely when $s$ halts. In that case
its unique solution is
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:unique-history}
 H_s(Z)=\sum_{t=0}^{h}Z^t e_{c_t}.
\end{equation}
Its degree in $Z$ is $h$, its support size is $h+1$, and all its nonzero
coefficients equal $1_R$. These conclusions hold for every nonzero
commutative ring $R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $H=\sum_{t\ge0}Z^tH_t$, with all but finitely many $H_t$ zero.
Comparison of coefficients in \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:clocked} gives
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:recursion}
 H_0=e_s,\qquad H_t=UH_{t-1}\quad(t\ge1).
\end{equation}
The same comparison for a homogeneous solution gives $H_0=0$ and then
$H_t=0$ for every $t$, proving injectivity. For the inhomogeneous equation
we obtain $H_t=U^te_s$. Along a deterministic trajectory this vector is
exactly $e_{c_t}$ until a terminal configuration is passed, and is zero
thereafter. Since $1_R\ne0_R$, a nonhalting trajectory has a nonzero
coefficient in every time layer and cannot be a polynomial. The stated
formula and counts follow. Different times remain distinct even when
the unclocked configuration repeats.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What changes on completion}
In the formal power-series module $\V_R[[Z]]$ there is always a unique
solution,
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:formal}
 H_\infty=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}Z^tU^te_s.
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $ZU$ is coefficientwise well-defined, and
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:recursion} verifies the equation directly. Thus existence in
the power-series completion has no halting content. The relevant
question is whether the formal solution belongs to the much smaller
polynomial module. The clock enforces uniqueness; finite support
enforces halting. These are separate mechanisms.

\begin{corollary}[Exact base change]\label{pwh:bt:cor:basechange}
For a fixed deterministic program and a single configuration source,
solvability of the clocked equation is the same over
$\Z,\Q,\R,\C,\Fbb_p$, and $\Z/m\Z$ for every $m\ge2$.
If a solution exists, it is obtained over each ring from the same
zero--one history.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[The source restriction is essential]\label{pwh:bt:rem:multisource}
For a general linear combination of source configurations,
finite-time cancellation may occur. For example, let two different
states $a,b$ both transition to a nonhalting state $c$. Over $\Fbb_2$,
$U(e_a+e_b)=0$, so $H=e_a+e_b$ solves
$(I-ZU)H=e_a+e_b$ although neither source halts. Over $\Z$ use
$e_a-e_b$ instead. Our characteristic-independent halting statements
always use one configuration monomial with coefficient one.
\end{remark}

\section{A universal substrate made of linear boundary operators}\label{pwh:bt:sec:substrate}

\subsection{The machine convention}
Let the control states be $Q=\{0,\ldots,q-1\}$, with configurations
$(r,a,b)\in Q\times\N^2$. Each state has one of the following instructions:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Instruction & Action\\
\midrule
$\inc(j,p)$ & Increment counter $j$ and go to $p$.\\
$\test(j,p_+,p_0)$ & If counter $j>0$, decrement it and go to $p_+$;\\
 & otherwise leave it at zero and go to $p_0$.\\
$\halt$ & Declare the current configuration terminal.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $j\in\{X,Y\}$. There are no implicit wraparound counters, negative
counter values, or automatic jumps past the end of the table. Every
destination is a valid state. A missing next instruction in a different
machine convention can be translated to an explicit terminal state.

This model contains the undecidable $\mathrm{CM2}$ instruction set
specified in~\cite{bt:dudenhefner}. The standard universality construction
also provides a fixed interpreter machine with computably encoded input
in the counters. One may choose the usual interface with fixed initial
state $r_0$, second counter zero, and first counter containing an encoded
input~\cite{bt:minsky,bt:dudenhefner}. Nothing here requires the physical
counter value to equal an unencoded source-program number. All
completeness statements concern computable many-one reductions; no
polynomial-time size bound for that encoding is asserted.

\subsection{Polynomial configurations}
Identify
\[
 e_{(r,a,b)}\longleftrightarrow e_rX^aY^b,
 \qquad \V_R\cong R[X,Y]^Q.
\]
For $F\in R[X,Y,Z]$ define
\begin{align}
 M_XF&=XF,& P_XF&=F(0,Y,Z),&
 D_XF&=\frac{F-P_XF}{X},\label{pwh:bt:eq:boundaryX}\\
 M_YF&=YF,& P_YF&=F(X,0,Z),&
 D_YF&=\frac{F-P_YF}{Y}.\label{pwh:bt:eq:boundaryY}
\end{align}
The divisions in these definitions are exact polynomial shifts, not
inversions in a field. They are valid over every coefficient ring.
On a monomial,
\[
 P_X(X^aY^bZ^t)=\begin{cases}Y^bZ^t,&a=0,\\0,&a>0,\end{cases}
\]
and $D_X(X^aY^bZ^t)=X^{a-1}Y^bZ^t$ for $a>0$, while it is zero
for $a=0$. The analogous statements hold for $Y$.

\begin{lemma}[Boundary algebra]\label{pwh:bt:lem:boundary}
All six operators are $R[Z]$-linear, and
\[
 D_XM_X=I,\qquad M_XD_X=I-P_X,\qquad
 P_X^2=P_X,\qquad P_XM_X=0,\qquad D_XP_X=0.
\]
The $Y$ identities are analogous. Every $X$-coordinate operator commutes
with every $Y$-coordinate operator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each identity follows by evaluating it on $X^aY^bZ^t$ and distinguishing
$a=0$ from $a>0$ where necessary. The cross-coordinate statements follow
because the two operations change or test different exponents. Linearity
extends the identities to arbitrary polynomials.
\end{proof}

For a vector $H=(H_r)_{r\in Q}$ define $UH$ by adding the following
contribution from each control state $r$:
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:contribution}
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{instruction at }r & \text{contribution to }UH\\ \hline
\inc(X,p) & e_p M_XH_r\\
\inc(Y,p) & e_p M_YH_r\\
\test(X,p_+,p_0) & e_{p_+}D_XH_r+e_{p_0}P_XH_r\\
\test(Y,p_+,p_0) & e_{p_+}D_YH_r+e_{p_0}P_YH_r\\
\halt & 0
\end{array}
\end{equation}
On a single monomial configuration exactly the intended branch is
nonzero. Thus this $U$ is exactly the transport operator of
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:U}, not an approximation to it.

\begin{corollary}[Native boundary-transport representation]\label{pwh:bt:cor:native}
For a source $(r_0,a_0,b_0)$, the linear operator equation
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:native}
 H-ZUH=e_{r_0}X^{a_0}Y^{b_0},\qquad H\in R[X,Y,Z]^Q,
\end{equation}
has a solution if and only if the counter machine halts on that source.
Its solution, when present, is unique and binary. Removing $Z$ preserves
existence but introduces exactly the cycle fibers of
Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:unclocked}.
\end{corollary}

This is already an explicit quasi-Diophantine representation. The next
section removes $P_X,D_X,P_Y,D_Y$ from the displayed language, at the
cost of making the polynomial sorts visible.

\section{Compilation to mixed-subring linear equations}\label{pwh:bt:sec:compiler}

\subsection{Eliminating a boundary operator}
\begin{lemma}[Unique boundary split]\label{pwh:bt:lem:split}
For $F\in R[X,Y,Z]$ there is a unique pair
\[
 D\in R[X,Y,Z],\qquad B\in R[Y,Z]
\]
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:split}
 F=XD+B.
\end{equation}
It is $D=D_XF$ and $B=P_XF$. The analogous result holds with $X$ and $Y$
interchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Group the monomials of $F$ by their exponent of $X$. The zero-exponent
terms give $B$, and shifting the other terms down gives $D$. For
uniqueness, evaluate \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:split} at $X=0$ to recover $B$, then use
the injectivity of multiplication by the indeterminate $X$ to recover
$D$. That injectivity holds even when $R$ has zero divisors.
\end{proof}

Let $T\subseteq Q$ be the set of test states, and write $d=|T|$.
Introduce $H_r\in R[X,Y,Z]$ for every state. For each $r\in T$ introduce
$D_r\in R[X,Y,Z]$ and a boundary unknown $B_r$, whose sort is
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:sorts}
 B_r\in\begin{cases}R[Y,Z],&\text{the instruction tests }X,\\
 R[X,Z],&\text{the instruction tests }Y.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Impose one split equation at each test state:
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:splitrow}
 H_r-X_jD_r-B_r=0,\qquad X_X=X,\ X_Y=Y.
\end{equation}

For each destination state $p$, form the incoming expression $I_p$.
An increment instruction at $r$ contributes $X_jH_r$ to its destination;
a positive test branch contributes $D_r$; a zero branch contributes
$B_r$; a terminal instruction contributes nothing. Impose the balance
rows
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:balancerow}
 H_p-ZI_p=S_p\qquad(p\in Q),
\end{equation}
where $S_{r_0}=X^{a_0}Y^{b_0}$ and all other $S_p$ are zero. If both
branches of a test have the same destination, both contributions are
added there. The split still makes them disjoint on a configuration
monomial.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit sparse linear compiler]\label{pwh:bt:thm:compiler}
Equations \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:sorts}--\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:balancerow} give a compiler with
$q+2d$ polynomial unknowns and $q+d$ linear equations. For every nonzero
commutative coefficient ring, its solution set is in bijection with the
solutions of \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:native}, by forgetting the $D_r,B_r$ unknowns.
Consequently the complete compiled witness is unique when it exists,
exists exactly for halting computations, and has only zero--one
coefficients.

Every matrix-coefficient monomial is a signed member of
\[
 \{1,X,Y,Z,ZX,ZY\}.
\]
Each unknown has at most two signed monomial occurrences in the entire
matrix, with occurrences possibly combined in one entry. Thus matrix
coefficient degree is at most two. If halting occurs at time $h$, the
main $H$-support has size $h+1$. The full witness support has size
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:fullsupport}
 h+1+\#\{t:0\le t\le h,\ \text{the state of }c_t\text{ is a test state}\},
\end{equation}
and is at most $2h+1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{pwh:bt:lem:split} forces $D_r=D_jH_r$ and $B_r=P_jH_r$.
Substitution in the balance rows therefore gives precisely
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:native}. Conversely a solution of that equation has exactly
one extension through these boundary splits. This proves the bijection
and the uniqueness claim.

There are $q$ main unknowns and two new unknowns for each of the $d$
tests. There are $q$ balance rows and $d$ split rows. An increment-state
unknown occurs in its own balance row with coefficient $1$ and in its
destination balance row with coefficient $-ZX_j$. A test-state $H_r$
occurs in its own balance row and its split row, each with coefficient
$1$. A terminal-state unknown occurs only in its own balance row.
Each $D_r$ occurs with $-X_j$ in the split row and $-Z$ in its positive
destination row. Each $B_r$ occurs with $-1$ in the split row and $-Z$
in its zero destination row. Self-destinations combine occurrences but
do not increase their number or degree.

For a halting trajectory, each time layer of the main witness has
exactly one monomial. At a test-state occurrence, that monomial
contributes to exactly one of $D_r$ and $B_r$, again with coefficient
one. Time exponents prevent collisions. Hence \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:fullsupport}
holds. A terminal state is not a test state, so there are at most $h$
such additional monomials.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One scalar equation without sums of squares}
Let $E_0,\ldots,E_{m-1}$ be the residuals of the $m=q+d$ rows, including
the subtraction of the right-hand side. Introduce an additional
indeterminate $W$, and replace the system by
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:tag}
 \sum_{i=0}^{m-1}W^iE_i=0.
\end{equation}
\emph{Every unknown retains its original sort and is independent of
$W$.} Thus the coefficient of $W^i$ is exactly $E_i$, and the tagged
equation is equivalent to the system. This uses no order and no
sum-of-squares argument, so it works in characteristic two as well.
After collecting each unknown, the result is one equation
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:scalar}
 \sum_{j=1}^{q+2d}p_j(W,X,Y,Z)F_j
   =W^{r_0}X^{a_0}Y^{b_0}.
\end{equation}
The $p_j$ are explicitly determined by the program, each has at most two
signed monomials, and their total degree is at most $m+1$. The degree in
the unknowns $F_j$ is one. The dependence restrictions on the $F_j$ are
part of the equation's specification, not informal advice.

\begin{theorem}[A fixed single-equation universality normal form]\label{pwh:bt:thm:fixed}
Choose a fixed universal deterministic two-counter interpreter $\mathcal U$
with an encoded-input interface $(r_0,n,0)$. There are fixed integers
$q,d$, fixed polynomials $p_j\in\Z[W,X,Y,Z]$, and fixed polynomial sorts
chosen from
\[
 R[X,Y,Z],\qquad R[X,Z],\qquad R[Y,Z]
\]
such that the set of $n\in\N$ for which
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:fixed}
 \sum_j p_jF_j=W^{r_0}X^n
\end{equation}
has a solution is computably enumerable complete. Its solution fiber is
empty or a singleton. If it is a singleton, its coefficients are zero
or one. The accepted set is identical over every nonzero commutative
ring $R$, in particular over each fixed finite field.

There is also an unclocked version: one equation in $R[W,X,Y]$ with
unknown sorts $R[X,Y],R[X],R[Y]$ has the same accepted set, but need not
have singleton fibers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compile the fixed interpreter using Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:compiler}, then
apply \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:tag}. The source monomial is computable from $n$.
Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:clocked} identifies its accepted set with the
interpreter's halting set, giving completeness and all fiber statements.
The unclocked variant follows from Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:unclocked} and the
same unique boundary splitting without $Z$.

Over $\Z$, $\Q$, or a finite field, one can also see semidecidability
by enumerating finite polynomial tuples and checking the identities and
sorts. For an arbitrary ring no effective presentation is presumed:
the algebraic equivalence with the same halting set remains true, but
an enumeration of arbitrary ring elements is not asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not numerically instantiated]\label{pwh:bt:rem:instantiated}
The compiler accepts every concrete valid finite program, and the worked
example below is completely instantiated. The existence of a fixed
universal table in Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:fixed} uses counter-machine
universality; this report does not supply a particular numerical
universal interpreter table or optimize its $q,d$. Thus the theorem is
an effective structural construction, not a claimed small numerical
universal equation or an arithmetic-circuit record.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[No computable complete degree bound]\label{pwh:bt:cor:bound}
For the fixed universal source family there is no total computable
function bounding $\deg_Z H$ on all solvable instances. There is also no
total computable bound on the main-witness support size, or on the full
witness support size, on all solvable instances.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $B(n)$ bounded the time degree, simulate the interpreter for $B(n)$
transitions; all halting cases would have been detected. This would
decide its halting set. A support bound gives a time bound because the
main support has size $h+1$ and is contained in the full support.
\end{proof}

\wtag\ The bounded-search argument of Proposition
\texttt{cdc:bd:prop:nobound} in~\cite{cdc}, with $\deg_ZH$ and support in
place of an integer witness height; its counterparts are
Theorem~\ref{pwh:uh:thm:nobound} (Part~I) and
Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:nobound} (Part~II).

\section{A complete five-polynomial certificate}\label{pwh:bt:sec:example}

Consider the program that transfers the first counter to the second:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cl@{}}
\toprule
State & Instruction\\
\midrule
$0$ & $\test(X,1,2)$\\
$1$ & $\inc(Y,0)$\\
$2$ & $\halt$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For input $(0,2,0)$ the exact trajectory is
\[
 (0,2,0)\to(1,1,0)\to(0,1,1)\to(1,0,1)
 \to(0,0,2)\to(2,0,2).
\]
It halts at time $h=5$. The five unknowns have sorts
\[
 H_0,H_1,H_2,D_0\in R[X,Y,Z],\qquad B_0\in R[Y,Z].
\]
The complete system is
\begin{align}
 H_0-ZYH_1&=X^2,\label{pwh:bt:eq:example0}\\
 H_1-ZD_0&=0,\\
 H_2-ZB_0&=0,\\
 H_0-XD_0-B_0&=0.\label{pwh:bt:eq:example3}
\end{align}
Its unique solution is
\begin{align}
 H_0&=X^2+Z^2XY+Z^4Y^2,&
 H_1&=ZX+Z^3Y,\label{pwh:bt:eq:example-sol}\\
 H_2&=Z^5Y^2,&
 D_0&=X+Z^2Y,& B_0&=Z^4Y^2.\nonumber
\end{align}
Direct substitution verifies every row, including the final terminal
state. The main support has six monomials and the full witness has
nine. The three additional monomials correspond exactly to the three
visits to state zero.

Tagging the four rows by $1,W,W^2,W^3$ yields the single equation
\begin{align}\label{pwh:bt:eq:example-scalar}
 &(1+W^3)H_0+(W-ZY)H_1+W^2H_2\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{1.8em}-(WZ+W^3X)D_0-(W^2Z+W^3)B_0=X^2.
\end{align}
All unknowns are independent of $W$, and $B_0$ is additionally
independent of $X$. Formula \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:example-sol} is the unique solution
over the integers, over the rationals, and over every finite field.
In characteristic two the minus signs become plus signs; the uniqueness
and halting equivalence do not change.

\section{A strictly convex energy with an exact integrality gap}\label{pwh:bt:sec:energy}

\subsection{Clocked configurations and the incidence operator}
Let
\[
 \Omega=Q\times\N^2\times\N
\]
be the clocked configuration set. For $v=(r,a,b,t)$ define $F(v)$ by
executing the machine step and increasing $t$ by one; if the instruction
is terminal, leave $F(v)$ undefined. Write $v\text{ live}$ for the first
case. On finite-support arrays define
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:A}
 \Ann e_v=\begin{cases}e_v-e_{F(v)},&v\text{ live},\\
 e_v,&v\text{ terminal}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This is $I-ZU$ in coefficient notation. Every column has one or two
nonzero entries, all equal to $1$ or $-1$. A row has at most $2q+1$
nonzero entries: at most two predecessors can arise from each control
state, in addition to its diagonal entry. The matrix is fixed by the
finite program, although its row and column sets are infinite.

Fix the initial clocked source $v_0=(r_0,a_0,b_0,0)$. For finitely
supported real $h$ define
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:energy}
 E_{v_0}(h)=\|\Ann h-e_{v_0}\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
All sums are finite at such an $h$. The zero set of this energy is
exactly the clocked certificate set.

\begin{theorem}[Exact integer gap and nonattained real infimum]\label{pwh:bt:thm:gap}
The functional $E_{v_0}$ is strictly convex on the real vector space of
finite-support arrays. Its integer minimum is
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:integer-gap}
 \min_{h\in\Z^{(\Omega)}}E_{v_0}(h)
 =\begin{cases}0,&\text{the source halts},\\1,&\text{the source does not halt}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Over finite-support rational or real arrays the infimum is always zero.
That infimum is attained if and only if the source halts; if attained,
its minimizer is the unique binary history.

For a nonhalting trajectory $v_0,v_1,\ldots$, set
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:taper}
 h^{(N)}=\sum_{t=0}^{N}\frac{N+1-t}{N+2}\,e_{v_t}
 \qquad(N\ge0).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 \Ann h^{(N)}-e_{v_0}
   &=-\frac{1}{N+2}\sum_{t=0}^{N+1}e_{v_t},\label{pwh:bt:eq:taper-residual}\\
 E_{v_0}(h^{(N)})&=\frac{1}{N+2},\label{pwh:bt:eq:taper-energy}\\
 \|h^{(N)}\|_2^2&=\frac{(N+1)(2N+3)}{6(N+2)}.\label{pwh:bt:eq:taper-norm}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The clocked operator is injective by Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:clocked}. For
$h\ne k$ and $0<\theta<1$, the parallelogram expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 E_{v_0}(\theta h+(1-\theta)k)
 &=\theta E_{v_0}(h)+(1-\theta)E_{v_0}(k)\\
 &\quad-\theta(1-\theta)\|\Ann(h-k)\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
The last term is strictly negative, proving strict convexity.

If a finite integer array has nonzero residual, the residual contains a
nonzero integer coefficient, so its squared norm is at least one. A
nonhalting source has no zero residual by Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:clocked}, and
$h=0$ has energy one. This proves the nonhalting case of
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:integer-gap}. A halting source has its integer binary history
of energy zero.

For the nonhalting taper, the coefficient at $v_0$ in the residual is
$(N+1)/(N+2)-1=-1/(N+2)$. At each $v_t$ with $1\le t\le N$, the
coefficient is the difference of consecutive taper values, also
$-1/(N+2)$. At $v_{N+1}$ it is the negative of the final taper value.
All clocked vertices are distinct, even for an unclocked periodic run.
This proves \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:taper-residual} and \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:taper-energy}.
Summing $1^2+\cdots+(N+1)^2$ and dividing by $(N+2)^2$ gives
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:taper-norm}. Thus rational arrays already make the energy
arbitrarily small. Attainment of the infimum would be a finite-support
zero residual, which is equivalent to halting and has the unique
witness already described.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The taper is optimal on its trajectory segment}
\begin{proposition}[Exact segment minimum]\label{pwh:bt:prop:segment}
For a nonhalting source, the minimum of $E_{v_0}$ on
$\operatorname{span}_{\R}\{e_{v_0},\ldots,e_{v_N}\}$ is $1/(N+2)$,
attained uniquely at \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:taper}. The same minimum holds over the
rational span.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $h=\sum_{t=0}^N a_te_{v_t}$. Its residual coefficients on
$v_0,\ldots,v_{N+1}$ are
\[
 a_0-1,\quad a_1-a_0,\quad\ldots,\quad a_N-a_{N-1},\quad -a_N.
\]
Their sum is $-1$. By Cauchy--Schwarz the sum of their squares is at
least $1/(N+2)$, with equality if and only if all are $-1/(N+2)$.
Solving those differences gives exactly \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:taper}.
\end{proof}

This is an optimum on the indicated \emph{trajectory-supported}
subspace, not a claim about every finite spatial box. Allowing branches
outside that trajectory can alter finite-box approximation rates. The
global statements of Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:gap} do not require a formula for
those other finite-dimensional minima.

\subsection{The complete quadratic and its locality}
For distinct vertices $v,w$ with a common successor write
$\{v,w\}\in\mathcal S$. Expanding the square gives
\begin{align}\label{pwh:bt:eq:expanded}
 E_{v_0}(h)
 &=1-2h_{v_0}
 +\sum_v\bigl(1+\one_{v\text{ live}}\bigr)h_v^2\nonumber\\
 &\quad-2\sum_{v\text{ live}}h_vh_{F(v)}
 +2\sum_{\{v,w\}\in\mathcal S}h_vh_w.
\end{align}
To verify this, expand $\|h\|^2+\|ZU h\|^2-2\langle h,ZUh\rangle$
and the source terms. No transition enters time zero, so the linear
term is exactly $-2h_{v_0}$. The diagonal contribution of $\|ZU h\|^2$
is one for each live column; its off-diagonal terms are precisely
pairs with a common successor. Clocking prevents a pair from being both
a parent--child pair and a sibling pair.

Thus the scalar coefficients of this infinite quadratic expression lie
in $\{0,\pm1,\pm2\}$. Interactions occur only between a configuration
and its next configuration or between two configurations with a common
successor. In the three coordinates $(a,b,t)$ their $\ell^1$ separation
is at most two; the finite state labels are additional fixed types.
The local rules differ at the coordinate boundaries, which is exactly
where the zero tests act. This is a finite-rule description on
$\N^3$, not a claim of a translation-invariant system on all of $\Z^3$.

For a fixed universal program, deciding whether the integer minimum is
zero or one is undecidable. Deciding whether the real infimum is
attained is also undecidable, even though the infimum value itself is
always zero. This is an \emph{additive} integrality gap of one in the
nonhalting case, not a finite multiplicative gap ratio. There is no
contradiction with solvability of finite-dimensional least squares: no
computable finite support bound is available for all instances.

\section{Dense nonclosed range in a fixed Hilbert space}\label{pwh:bt:sec:hilbert}

The finite-support statements admit a particularly sharp analytic
completion. Let $\ell^2(\Omega)$ be the real or complex Hilbert space
of square-summable arrays on clocked configurations.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded extension]\label{pwh:bt:lem:bounded}
Suppose every unclocked configuration has at most $B$ predecessors.
Then $U$ extends to a bounded operator on $\ell^2(S)$ with
$\|U\|\le\sqrt B$. Its clocked shift $ZU$ is bounded on
$\ell^2(S\times\N)$ with the same bound, and
\[
 \|\Ann\|=\|I-ZU\|\le1+\sqrt B.
\]
For the counter machines above one may take $B=2q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a finite-support vector $x$,
\[
 \|Ux\|_2^2
 =\sum_w\left|\sum_{v:f(v)=w}x_v\right|^2
 \le B\sum_w\sum_{v:f(v)=w}|x_v|^2
 \le B\|x\|_2^2.
\]
Each live predecessor occurs in exactly one inner sum. Density extends
the operator to $\ell^2$. The time shift preserves the norm when
applied separately from $U$, giving the clocked bound. For each source
control state an increment gives at most one predecessor of a specified
configuration, and a test gives at most two, one from each branch.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact range encodes halting]\label{pwh:bt:thm:range}
The bounded operator $\Ann$ on $\ell^2(\Omega)$ is injective and has
dense range. For a clocked basis vector $e_{(s,t_0)}$,
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:range}
 e_{(s,t_0)}\in\ran\Ann
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{the unclocked computation from }s\text{ halts}.
\end{equation}
If at least one source does not halt, the range is not closed. In
particular a fixed universal counter machine yields a fixed computably
specified sparse bounded operator whose range membership on a
computable family of basis vectors is computably enumerable complete.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose $h\in\ell^2(\Omega)$ into time layers $h_t\in\ell^2(S)$.
For a homogeneous equation $\Ann h=0$ the time-zero equation gives
$h_0=0$, and the successive equations give $h_t=Uh_{t-1}=0$.
This proves injectivity without any finite-support assumption.

For $\Ann h=e_{(s,t_0)}$, the same recursion forces zero layers below
$t_0$, followed by
\[
 h_{t_0+t}=U^te_s\qquad(t\ge0).
\]
If the source halts this is the finite binary history and belongs to
$\ell^2$. If it does not halt, every displayed layer has squared norm
one, so no square-summable solution exists. This proves
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:range}.

For density, it is enough to approximate every basis vector. A halting
source basis vector already lies in the finite-support range. For a
nonhalting source the shifted tapers of \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:taper} satisfy
$\|\Ann h^{(N)}-e_{(s,t_0)}\|_2\to0$. Finite combinations of basis
vectors are dense, so the range is dense. A nonhalting source is in its
closure but not in the range, proving nonclosedness. The fixed-machine
computability statement follows from the finite transition table and
its effective monomial input encoding.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Spectral location, without a spectral-gap claim]\label{pwh:bt:cor:spectral}
Over the complex Hilbert space, if a nonhalting source exists then zero
belongs to the continuous spectrum of $\Ann$: the operator is injective
with dense proper range. It is not an eigenvalue of $\Ann$.
\end{corollary}

This conclusion is about a generally non-self-adjoint incidence
operator. It is not an assertion about a finite-dimensional quantum
Hamiltonian or a new many-body spectral-gap undecidability theorem.
The distinctive statement here is the exact range membership of the
specified computable basis-source family.

\wtag\ The opposite regime is treated in batch-79 manuscripts~10 and~14,
placed in the report \emph{stochastic-and-thermal-exactness}~\cite{ste} for
its Part~V (Section~\ref{pwh:sec:relation}): there a fixed sparse operator
built from a counter machine is uniformly coercive, hence boundedly
invertible, and halting is read off its Green data, whereas here zero lies
in the continuous spectrum (Corollary~\ref{pwh:bt:cor:spectral}) and the
range is not closed.

The nonhalting tapers also illustrate why one should not mistake
$\|\Ann h^{(N)}-e_{v_0}\|\to0$ for convergence of the witnesses.
Their norms diverge by \eqref{pwh:bt:eq:taper-norm}. Coefficientwise they
converge to the infinite binary computation history, which lies in the
formal time completion but not in $\ell^2$. The three domains therefore
give different answers: polynomial witnesses detect halting;
formal-power-series witnesses always exist; square-summable witnesses
again detect halting, despite the dense approximate range.

\section{Failure modes and the ordinary Diophantine boundary}\label{pwh:bt:sec:limits}

\subsection{Dropping a boundary sort creates an actual false positive}
Consider the three-state program
\[
 0:\test(X,1,2),\qquad1:\inc(X,0),\qquad2:\halt.
\]
From $(0,1,0)$ it repeats
$(0,1,0)\to(1,0,0)\to(0,1,0)$ and never halts. Its clocked rows are
\begin{align*}
 H_0-ZXH_1&=X,& H_1-ZD_0&=0,\\
 H_2-ZB_0&=0,&H_0-XD_0-B_0&=0,
\end{align*}
with the essential type $B_0\in R[Y,Z]$. If that type is relaxed to
$R[X,Y,Z]$, the tuple
\begin{equation}\label{pwh:bt:eq:fake}
 H_0=X,\qquad H_1=0,\qquad H_2=ZX,\qquad D_0=0,\qquad B_0=X
\end{equation}
satisfies every displayed identity. It illegitimately routes the
positive counter value through the zero branch. The supplied checker
accepts this tuple when sort enforcement is disabled and rejects it
when the declared sorts are enforced. This is an explicit counterexample
to that relaxed compiler, not merely a warning about a hypothetical bug.

\subsection{Discarding a final spill row creates another false positive}
For a nonhalting source, the truncated true history
$H_{\le N}=\sum_{t=0}^NZ^te_{c_t}$ satisfies
\[
 (I-ZU)H_{\le N}-e_s=-Z^{N+1}e_{c_{N+1}}.
\]
If a bounded coefficient checker retains only rows of time degree at
most $N$, it incorrectly accepts every truncated nonhalting run. A
correct finite-box checker must include \emph{all output rows generated
by the allowed columns}, even when those rows lie just outside the
candidate box. The independent finite-field checker in this package
uses precisely that rule. Truncation modulo $Z^{N+1}$ is a different
problem from a polynomial identity.

\subsection{Clocking, signs, and several sources}
The unclocked equation is sound for a single source even with signed
coefficients, but its global witness need not be unique because
unrelated directed cycles may be added. The clock eliminates those
cycles from the kernel, rather than relying on a minimality convention.
Nonnegative coefficients alone do not remove disconnected positive
cycle additions. Conversely, permitting several sources can introduce
finite-time cancellation even in the clocked construction, as in
Remark~\ref{pwh:bt:rem:multisource}. Positivity, clocking, and a one-monomial
source are different assumptions and should not be interchanged.

\subsection{Why this is not fixed-arity integer MRDP}\label{pwh:bt:sec:mrdp}
The sorts $R[X,Y,Z]$ and $R[Y,Z]$ quantify over objects with arbitrarily
large finite support. Calling them $q+2d$ unknowns does not make them a
fixed tuple of $q+2d$ integers in an ordinary Diophantine equation. A
support list contains an unbounded number of exponents and coefficients.
The subring restriction is also indispensable, as
\eqref{pwh:bt:eq:fake} demonstrates.

For $R=\Z$ one can canonically encode each finite polynomial tuple as a
natural number. A checker can decode its lists, reject malformed or
noncanonical encodings, verify the omitted-variable restrictions,
compute every residual coefficient, and test that all vanish. With
standard finite-list and integer encodings this is a primitive
recursive verification procedure. The accepted input relation is then
\[
 n\in K_{\mathcal U}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists w\in\N\;\operatorname{Check}(n,w)=1.
\]
MRDP converts this enumerable relation to an ordinary integer
polynomial with finitely many natural witnesses. ProveIt's
\texttt{rePred\_dioph} provides an appropriate existing endpoint for
such an integration~\cite{bt:repo-bridge}.

But that conversion can introduce new auxiliary witnesses. The
uniqueness of the canonical polynomial tuple gives no proof that those
integer auxiliaries are unique or even finite in number. Thus the
present theorem does not settle single-fold or finite-fold ordinary
Diophantine representation. The distinction is substantial; it is the
subject of dedicated work on Diophantine univocity~\cite{bt:matiyasevich2010,bt:cantone2024}.
Nor is the operation count of polynomial-list verification the
operation count of an expanded universal integer polynomial.

\section{A decidable one-counter boundary}\label{pwh:bt:sec:onecounter}

The second independently testable exponent is not a cosmetic feature.
The corresponding deterministic one-counter machine class has a direct
halting algorithm. We give the proof to delimit the exact scope of this
comparison.

\begin{proposition}[One-counter machine-generated certificates]\label{pwh:bt:prop:onecounter}
For the same instruction convention with only one nonnegative counter,
halting from a specified configuration is decidable. Consequently the
polynomial-certificate existence problem is decidable for the subclass
compiled from such one-counter programs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
While the counter is strictly positive, every instruction has a fixed
successor control state: an increment contributes $+1$, a test takes
its positive branch and contributes $-1$, and a terminal state halts.
Thus a positive excursion follows a deterministic finite control graph
with edge weights $\pm1$, until it reaches zero or halts.

Follow the excursion until one of those events occurs or a control
state repeats. A repeated state identifies a control cycle that has
already been traversed with all intermediate tested counter values
positive. Let $\Delta$ be its net counter change. If $\Delta\ge0$, each
successive traversal starts with at least as large a counter as the
previous one, so the same positive cycle repeats forever. If
$\Delta<0$, repeated traversals decrease the starting counter by the
positive integer $-\Delta$; after finitely many traversals the counter
reaches zero during a traversal. One can compute the number of full
safe traversals from the cycle's partial sums, or simply iterate these
decreasing cycles until zero is reached. Either method terminates in
this case. Hence each positive excursion can be classified as halting,
returning to zero, or infinite.

At counter zero there are only $q$ configurations, one per control
state. Record each zero configuration encountered. Repetition of one
forces an infinite deterministic future. Otherwise after at most $q$
distinct zero configurations the procedure either halts, reaches an
infinite positive excursion, or repeats a zero configuration. A
possible initial positive excursion is handled first by the same
procedure. This is a terminating decision algorithm.
\end{proof}

This proposition concerns the \emph{machine-generated} class. It is
not a theorem that every linear equation with one restricted polynomial
sort is decidable. General linear systems may have branching,
coefficients, several sources, or dependence restrictions not realizable
by this one-counter control discipline. The distinction between a
syntactic subclass and the whole ambient equation problem must be kept
explicit.

\section{Executable compiler and validation}\label{pwh:bt:sec:validation}

\subsection{What the implementation does}
The accompanying Python implementation uses sparse polynomials keyed by
triples of nonnegative exponents $(a,b,t)$. Coefficients are exact
integers or rational numbers, and optional modular arithmetic is
implemented by reduction modulo a supplied integer. No floating-point
arithmetic is used in any certificate check.

\texttt{code/boundary\_transport.py} implements validated finite program
tables; exact transitions; sparse addition and shifts; boundary
splitting; the linear transport action; construction of histories and
residuals; the mixed-subring compiler; sort-aware certificate checking;
and emission of the tagged scalar equation. It also contains an
independent finite-box solver over $\Fbb_2$.

The latter solver constructs a coefficient matrix directly from the
configuration successor relation, rather than from the generated
polynomial rows. For each allowed column $e_v$ it inserts its diagonal
row and, when live, its successor row. It retains every such row,
including spatial and temporal spill rows outside the candidate box.
Bitset Gaussian elimination then checks column rank and consistency.
This tests the compiled semantics against an independent finite
linear-algebra route instead of merely asking the certificate generator
to confirm its own output.

The implementation is a research artifact, not a certified prover or a
high-performance universal-machine simulator. Its input validation
rejects empty programs, invalid destinations, invalid counter indices,
and negative exponent or counter values in the exercised cases. It also
rejects nonintegral indices and moduli, and snapshots a caller-supplied
instruction sequence as an immutable tuple.
Canonical polynomial sorting is used in the mathematical discussion of
integer coding; no claim is made that the current Python API is already
a production-strength MRDP encoding format.

\subsection{A practical verifier contract}
A robust consumer of these certificates should treat the following as
part of the trusted interface: the validated instruction table; the
source configuration and its coefficient-one convention; the declared
polynomial sorts; exact coefficient arithmetic; the complete set of
residual monomials, including spill; and the independent status of the
clock variable and row tag. Omitting any of these changes the theorem
being checked. A certificate need not carry a claimed runtime:
its degree in $Z$ reveals the runtime after the identities have been
verified.

\section{Integration with ProveIt}\label{pwh:bt:sec:integration}

A useful formalization would separate the generic algebra from the
machine compiler. The first layer is a finite-support module over an
arbitrary nontrivial commutative ring, a deterministic partial
successor, and its linear extension. The proofs of
Theorems~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:unclocked} and~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:clocked} can then be stated
without counter-machine syntax. The clocked proof is especially small:
it is coefficient induction, followed by evaluation of the iterates on
one basis vector.

The second layer identifies two-counter configurations with monomials
and proves the boundary split. It should make omitted-coordinate
conditions explicit in the types, for example by storing $B_r$ as an
actual polynomial over the smaller variable index set rather than as a
large-ring polynomial with an unchecked promise. The compiler-correctness
theorem is then a bijection of full solution sets, not just a forward
construction of a witness.

A third layer could specify a canonical natural-number encoding of
finite polynomial tuples and prove that its verifier is primitive
recursive. Composing that result with the existing
\texttt{rePred\_dioph} interface would yield an ordinary Diophantine
existence theorem for the accepted language. The primitive-recursive
verifier and the MRDP-generated polynomial should remain separate
objects with separate complexity ledgers. The repository's existing
source bridge supplies the latter logical endpoint, but none of these
new layers has been implemented in Lean in this package~\cite{bt:repo-bridge}.

Finally, the convex-energy theorem can be formalized over finitely
supported real arrays before bringing in any Hilbert-space completion.
The integer lower bound is just the fact that a nonzero integer residual
has squared norm at least one; the approximation formula is a finite
telescoping sum. This separation would keep the most concrete new
certificate results independent of analytic-library overhead.

\section{Further research questions}\label{pwh:bt:sec:future}

\subsection{Concrete universal instances and smaller interfaces}\label{pwh:bt:q:concrete}
\paragraph{A numerical universal table.}
Choose and validate a published universal two-counter interpreter,
including its ordinary encoded-input loader, then emit the complete
fixed $p_j$ of Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:fixed}. The measurable targets are the
number of states $q$, tests $d$, atomic coefficient terms, input encoding
cost, and total witness support. This would upgrade the structural
fixed-program theorem to a directly inspectable numerical instance;
it would not by itself improve ordinary integer universal bounds.

\wtag\ The same target is posed in Part~I (Section~\ref{pwh:uh:q:numerical})
and Part~II (Section~\ref{pwh:oc:q:numerical}).

\paragraph{Uniqueness without an extra clock coordinate.}
Can a universal counter-machine compilation eliminate all directed
cycles globally while preserving a small two-counter instruction table
and a manageable input interface? Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:unclocked} shows
exactly why this would produce unique unclocked certificates. The
question is not merely whether a selected run has no repetition: every
unrelated cycle contributes a kernel vector.

\paragraph{Minimal polynomial sorts.}
Determine the smallest number of independent indeterminates and
omitted-coordinate sorts needed for a universal \emph{linear}
certificate with this sparsity discipline. The scalar construction
uses a row tag solely to replace a vector equation. Can that coordinate
be removed without hiding vector-valued equality or introducing
nonlinear witness products? A lower bound must specify a precise
syntactic class; Proposition~\ref{pwh:bt:prop:onecounter} gives only one
restricted decidable boundary.

\begin{remark}[Editorial; not stated by source~01 or source~15]\label{pwh:bt:rem:univariate}
\wtag\ Part~II's finite-state theorem bounds this question from below. Its
proof (Theorem~\ref{pwh:oc:thm:finite-state}) uses only that the
coefficient vectors range over a finite alphabet and that the window
identities~\eqref{pwh:oc:eq:window} are checked by exact arithmetic in the
coefficient ring. With the alphabet $\{-B_0,\ldots,B_0\}^n$ it decides
solvability of a univariate linear system over $\Z[X]$ by a solution of
coefficient height at most $B_0$, and over a finite ring $R$ (for example
$\Fbb_p$ or $\Z/6\Z$) with the alphabet $R^n$ it decides solvability of
univariate linear systems outright. As in
Corollary~\ref{pwh:oc:cor:no-linear}, no computable reduction of a
computably enumerable complete set to univariate linear systems over
$\Z[X]$ can guarantee a solution with coefficients in $\{0,1\}$ on every
solvable instance, and none to univariate linear systems over a finite
ring exists at all. So the $0/1$ certificates of
Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:compiler}, and its validity over finite fields,
cannot be kept with a single indeterminate (clock and row tag included):
at least two are necessary in this linear syntax. This is a partial answer
only; the gap between two indeterminates and the four ($W,X,Y,Z$) of
Theorem~\ref{pwh:bt:thm:fixed} remains open. The extension of the proof to
$\Z[X]$ and to finite rings was observed at the intake and checked by
reading the proof; source~15 states it only over $\N[X]$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Arithmetic compilation and quantitative certificates}\label{pwh:bt:q:arithmetic}
\paragraph{An explicit integer bridge with controlled fibers.}
Develop a concrete arithmetization of the sparse polynomial checker,
track every scalar auxiliary, and determine which steps preserve a
bijection of witnesses. The hard objective is not the existence
statement already supplied by MRDP, but an audited operation count and
controlled auxiliary multiplicity. Any claimed single-fold conclusion
must account for all newly introduced scalar witnesses.

\wtag\ The same question is posed in Part~I (Section~\ref{pwh:uh:q:packing})
and Part~II (Section~\ref{pwh:oc:q:packing}).

\paragraph{Sharp support-growth calibration.}
For fixed universal machines, compare maximal halting time at bounded
input description length with maximal minimal certificate support.
The clocked correspondence gives the exact identity
$|\supp H|=h+1$ before arithmetic compression. Which natural changes
of machine or native substrate preserve this identity up to affine,
polynomial, or only computable distortion?

\paragraph{Decidable fragments.}
Extend the one-counter analysis to carefully delimited classes:
restricted zero-test placement, bounded changes in counter direction,
acyclic branch-control patterns, or fixed bounds on one counter.
The goal is a theorem about the \emph{generated equation class}, with
an effective bound or structural algorithm, not an assertion that all
similar-looking mixed-subring equations are decidable.

\subsection{Native computational substrates}
\paragraph{Direct encodings beyond counters.}
For a deterministic evaluator for combinatory logic, interaction nets,
queue rewriting, or another esoteric substrate, construct a native
monomial transition operator and prove the relevant boundary or
rewriting decomposition. The generic clocked theorem then applies
immediately, but the native compiler must still pay for syntax,
matching, wiring, or queue legality. The research target is reducing
that overhead while retaining a full solution-set bijection.

\paragraph{Nondeterminism and cancellation.}
A linear extension that sends a configuration to the sum of all its
successors encodes a branching computation, not existential acceptance.
Over finite characteristic, distinct branches can cancel. Find exact
conditions or additional certificate structure that represent
existence of a halting branch while preserving a useful
characteristic-independent semantics. The one-source deterministic
proof cannot simply be reused.

\subsection{Approximation and functional analysis}\label{pwh:bt:q:analysis}
\paragraph{Optimal finite-box approximation.}
The trajectory-segment optimum is exactly $1/(N+2)$. What is the optimum
when all configurations in a counter/time box are allowed? Incoming
branches may spread the residual and change the rate. An exact
combinatorial characterization could connect least-squares residuals
with finite network potentials while retaining the terminal-column
convention and spill rows.

\paragraph{Effective range criteria.}
The fixed operator has dense nonclosed range, and membership is hard
already on a selected family of basis vectors. Identify natural
restricted source subspaces where membership becomes decidable, or
where an effective bound on the inverse norm is possible. Multiple
source vectors bring genuine cancellation and require a different
classification from the single-basis theorem.

\paragraph{Rational generating functions for nonhalting histories.}
An eventually periodic unclocked run has a rational formal clocked
history. More complicated nonhalting runs need not have such a simple
form. Characterize those machine fragments for which a rational
representation of the formal resolvent is effectively recognizable,
and determine what termination information survives this larger
certificate language. Rationality must not be confused with polynomiality.

\paragraph{A fully verified compiler pipeline.}
Formalize the sparse compiler, sort checker, row-tagging map, spill-aware
finite-field checker, and integer verifier bridge as separate verified
components. The deliverable should include an explicit theorem that the
complete compiled witness is unique, not only soundness of the forward
history construction. This is the most direct route for converting the
present research package into a reusable ProveIt component.

\wtag\ Parts~I and~II propose the same programme
(Sections~\ref{pwh:uh:q:kernel} and~\ref{pwh:oc:q:kernel}).

\section{Conclusion}
A deterministic computation admits an exact linear certificate before
any conversion to an ordinary integer Diophantine equation. Two boundary
projections supply the zero tests; a clock makes the entire certificate
unique; finite support determines whether the computation terminates.
The resulting mixed-subring system is sparse, linear in all its
unknowns, and valid over every nonzero commutative coefficient ring.
A row-tagging variable turns it into one equation without invoking an
order or a sum-of-squares test.

The same construction also gives a precise obstruction to treating
approximate feasibility as exact feasibility. On a nonhalting input,
integer residual energy is at least one, yet rational finite-support
arrays approach zero energy with the explicit rate $1/(N+2)$. In
Hilbert space the operator is injective with dense nonclosed range,
and exact basis-source membership retains the halting problem.

The research value is a transparent normal form linking computation,
polynomial sorts, unique witnesses, and convex relaxation. Its limits
are equally explicit: unrestricted polynomial sorts are unsound,
truncated spill rows are unsound, infinite power-series existence is
uninformative, and scalar MRDP uniqueness does not follow. Those
boundaries identify concrete next steps rather than concealing them
inside a generic universality assertion.

\section{Compiler specification in pseudocode (appendix of source~01)}\label{pwh:bt:app:compiler}

The following specification is independent of the sparse-polynomial
storage format. Each balance row initially contains only its diagonal
main unknown. The source term is subtracted from the selected row.
\begin{lstlisting}
compile(program, source, clocked=True):
    validate(program, source)
    clock = Z if clocked else 1
    create H[r] for each control state r
    balance[r] = H[r] - source_polynomial_in_state(r)
    splits = []
    for r, instruction in program:
        if instruction is INC(j, p):
            balance[p] -= clock * X[j] * H[r]
        elif instruction is TEST(j, positive, zero):
            create D[r] in the full polynomial ring
            create B[r] in the subring omitting X[j]
            splits.append(H[r] - X[j]*D[r] - B[r])
            balance[positive] -= clock * D[r]
            balance[zero]     -= clock * B[r]
        elif instruction is HALT:
            pass
    rows = balance + splits
    scalar_equation = sum(W**i * rows[i] for i in range(len(rows)))
    require every unknown to omit W
    return sorts, rows, scalar_equation
\end{lstlisting}
The compiler itself does not simulate the source and does not need a
halting bound. Simulation is used only to construct candidate witnesses
in tests and examples. Checking a supplied tuple is a terminating
finite algebraic operation regardless of whether the source halts.
