# Canonical outer-history transport: proof summary

## Scope and inherited hypothesis

This summary uses the canonical positive-integer outer-history projection theorem of Report 22, original release dated 2026-10-03. It applies to each fixed accepted external pair of a retained nonempty deterministic first-halting construction, conditional on the inherited complete native AND, residue-history, and clean-target theorems. It is a proof-level transport statement. Report 25 does not rerun, re-emit, simplify, or claim new operation totals for the older outer circuits.

## Why the outer coordinates are fixed

The deterministic first-halting trace fixes its length, terminal raw payload, exact native physical clock, every state, and every residue quotient. These determine S and the unique dyadic h in (S,2S], so the positive slacks eta=h-S and kappa=2S+1-h are fixed, including the dyadic boundary h=2S. The trace then fixes the radix, packed selectors and quotient words, their positive hats, and the paid clock quotient. The global equality fixes its positive slack. Every supplied nonnative coordinate and every padded native port and prescribed scale are therefore fixed.

## Full-fiber projection

At this fixed outer tuple, the five nonnative residuals vanish independently of the native coordinates. The remaining sixteen residuals are the complete native block at the fixed ports, with the same twenty-two-coordinate order. Hence projection to the native coordinates is bijective: injectivity follows from the unique outer tuple, and surjectivity follows because every positive native solution may be adjoined to that same outer tuple. No canonical choice of native Pell index is imposed.

Let C_out be the maximum of the finitely many fixed supplied outer coordinates, and let y_aux be the exact native height proved in this release. The complete supplied-tuple height is max(C_out,y_aux). Therefore its height count is zero below C_out and equals the native count at every threshold H >= C_out. A finite outer-coordinate cutoff does not alter either coefficient in the two-term count or the stated inverse-height asymptotic. This possible outer cutoff is distinct from the native fiber itself, whose height is always exactly y_aux.

## Boundaries

The separate initially halted four-operation, no-witness circuits are excluded. This is fiberwise insertion of an already fixed outer tuple, not an all-input algorithm for finding a halting trace, and not an all-tuple polynomial change of variables. No finite-fold theorem or universal ordinary-input loader is asserted.
