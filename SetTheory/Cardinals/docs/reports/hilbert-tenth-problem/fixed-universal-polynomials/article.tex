\begin{abstract}
We construct one literal Grill program with 397,488 run phases and one complete integer polynomial circuit attached to it. The circuit accepts an ordinary positive integer input, pays for its conversion into the finite input convention of a fixed universal machine, and binds the initial queue to its exact bit width. Its six external coordinates are the ordinary input and five program parameters fixed for each represented language. It has 797,135 positive existential coordinates and 3,600,546 binary arithmetic operations, comprising 803,517 multiplications and 2,797,029 additions or subtractions. Its exact total degree is 69,339,973, established by an all-tuple norm cancellation and an explicit nonzero leading coefficient on a univariate ray.

The language theorem is conditional on precisely pinned full native-history, binary-recoder, and finite-input U15 universality theorems. The exact-degree theorem is unconditional algebra on the unchanged pinned circuit. The new contribution is the explicit composition: corrected right and left tag macros, a unique accepting-head event, phase-preserving normalization, two distinct complete recoders, signed finite-frame arithmetic, exact unary loading, and a unit-safe finalizer. A complete 61,209,290-byte arithmetic DAG and independent whole-source checks accompany the report. The costs describe this actual emitted circuit in a literal-integer arithmetic model; they are not bit complexity or a minimality claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and its mathematical meaning}
The purpose of this report is to close the ordinary-input interface for a concrete Grill route to Diophantine representation. A native queue-history relation by itself is insufficient: it may permit different terminal-zero paddings of the same integer, and a substrate-universality slogan does not identify what language a particular numerical input represents. Here both interfaces are explicit and paid.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional fixed universal polynomial]\label{thm:main}
Assume the pinned theorem imports in Section~\ref{sec:imports}. There exists a single polynomial
\[
 F\in\Z[x,a,p,s,C,L,y_1,\ldots,y_{797135}]
\]
represented by the complete arithmetic DAG in the accompanying archive, such that for every computably enumerable set $S\subseteq\Np$ there is a fixed tuple
\[
 \theta_S=(a_e,p_e,s_e,C_e,L_e)\in\Np^5
\]
with, for every $x\in\Np$,
\[
 x\in S\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists(y_1,\ldots,y_{797135})\in\Np^{797135}:
 F(x,a_e,p_e,s_e,C_e,L_e,\mathbf y)=0.
\]
All five coordinates of $\theta_S$ have the finite program-dependent recipes in Section~\ref{sec:parameters}. The polynomial has 3,600,546 emitted binary arithmetic gates and exact total degree 69,339,973 when all six external coordinates and every witness have degree one. The degree assertion is proved independently of these theorem imports in Section~\ref{sec:exactdegree}.
\end{theorem}

The program parameters are not existentially varied with $x$. An arbitrary positive tuple $(a,p,s,C,L)$ still gives a polynomial slice, but only the explicitly constructed valid tuples have the stated machine interpretation. The theorem covers positive integer inputs and positive integer witnesses. No statement about $x=0$, negative inputs, or real zeros is intended.

The construction establishes a concrete representation through a selected computational substrate. It is not a new general MRDP theorem, and the operation or witness counts are not asserted to be optimal. The finite audits establish exact source facts and implementation identities; the unbounded existential equivalence additionally uses the imported complete theorems. In particular, no astronomical full Pell witness tuple or complete universal accepting execution has been materialized.

\section{A concrete finite U15 input language}\label{sec:u15}
Use $c=0$, $b=1$, and blank zero outside a finite tape. Refine a U15 control state $u$ and already-read symbol $r$ to
\[
 i=2(u-1)+r\in\{0,\ldots,29\}.
\]
The initial refined state is $0=(u_1,0)$; the accepting state is $19=(u_{10},1)$. The unique absent instruction in primary Table~16 is $(u_{10},b)$. A primary prose sentence instead names $c$; the literal construction follows the table, and the discrepancy is recorded rather than silently reconciled.

For a nonhalting instruction $(u,r)\mapsto(v,\epsilon,D)$, the refined machine writes $\epsilon$, moves in direction $D$, reads $r'$, and selects $j_{r'}=2(v-1)+r'$. There are 15 left-moving and 14 right-moving nonhalting instructions.

Let $M,N$ be the left and right binary tape integers, with the square nearest the head as the least significant bit. The scanned symbol is omitted because the refined control remembers it. A right move changes
\[
 (i,M,N)\mapsto(j_{N\bmod2},\,2M+\epsilon,\,\lfloor N/2\rfloor),
\]
and a left move changes
\[
 (i,M,N)\mapsto(j_{M\bmod2},\,\lfloor M/2\rfloor,\,2N+\epsilon).
\]
These formulas include zero tape integers and indefinitely blank tails.

\subsection{The chosen semidecider and physical input blocks}
For each $S\subseteq\Np$ that is computably enumerable, choose a finite semidecider that reads the canonical LSB-first spelling of $u=x+1$, decodes it, subtracts one, and recognizes $S$. This is a fixed program choice. It is not an uncharged external algorithm applied anew to $x$.

Write the physical binary blocks
\[
 \mathsf A_i=(01)^{8i-5}11,\qquad
 \mathsf B_0=\mathsf A_2\mathsf A_1,\quad
 \mathsf B_1=\mathsf A_1\mathsf A_2.
\]
Both $\mathsf B_0,\mathsf B_1$ have 32 bits. The U15 head scans the final $c$ of the start marker $bc$. Its left word is the finite encoded program followed by $b$; define $M_e$ by reversing that word and taking its LSB value. Its right word is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame}
 \mathsf P_e\mathsf B_{w_0}\cdots\mathsf B_{w_{n-1}}\mathsf S_e,

\end{equation}
where
\[

The alphabet size $q_B$ and the two boundary indices depend only on the selected finite program. All squares beyond the displayed words are blank. Put $N_e(x)=\val(\text{word in \eqref{eq:frame}})$. The suffix ends in $11$, hence $N_e(x)>0$.

\section{Corrected tag macros and the accepting head}\label{sec:tag}

\subsection{The right macro}
For an instruction writing $\epsilon$ and moving right, use the complete rules below. On internal symbols suppress the state index $i$.
\begin{align*}
 A_i&\to C\xi_i(c\xi_i)^\epsilon,& a_i&\to c\xi_i c\xi_i,\\
 B_i&\to S,&b_i&\to s,\\
 C&\to D_1D_0,&c&\to d_1d_0,\\
 S&\to T_1T_0,&s&\to t_1t_0,\\
 D_1&\to A_{j_1}\xi_{j_1},&d_1&\to a_{j_1}\xi_{j_1},\\
 D_0&\to\xi_{j_0}A_{j_0}\xi_{j_0},&d_0&\to a_{j_0}\xi_{j_0},\\
 T_1&\to B_{j_1}\xi_{j_1},&t_1&\to b_{j_1}\xi_{j_1},\\
 T_0&\to B_{j_0}\xi_{j_0},&t_0&\to b_{j_0}\xi_{j_0}.
\end{align*}
Put $M'=2M+\epsilon$. Exact intermediate queue cuts are
\begin{align*}
 &B_i\xi_i(b_i\xi_i)^N C\xi_i(c\xi_i)^{M'},\\
 &C\xi_i(c\xi_i)^{M'}S s^N,\\
 &S s^N D_1D_0(d_1d_0)^{M'}.
\end{align*}
If $N=2k+1$, the shrinking traversal leaves
\[
 D_1D_0(d_1d_0)^{M'}T_1T_0(t_1t_0)^k,
\]
which rotates through the $D$ and $T$ blocks to exactly $\mathcal C(j_1,M',k)$.
If $N=2k$, its last deletion consumes $D_1$, leaving
\[
 D_0(d_1d_0)^{M'}T_1T_0(t_1t_0)^k.
\]
The $D_0/d_0$ traversal consumes $T_1$ in its last deletion, including when $M'=0$, and gives
\[
 T_0(t_1t_0)^k\xi_{j_0}A_{j_0}\xi_{j_0}(a_{j_0}\xi_{j_0})^{M'}.
\]
The last $T_0/t_0$ deletion consumes the extra filler and yields precisely $\mathcal C(j_0,M',k)$, including $k=0$.

The final filler in $t_0\to b_{j_0}\xi_{j_0}$ is deliberate. The inspected printed Cocke--Minsky production omits it although the next displayed target retains paired repetitions. With $M=0,N=2,\epsilon=0$, the printed rule produces a five-symbol target after ten steps, missing its sixth filler. The construction here proves its own corrected rule; it does not present an asserted published erratum.

\subsection{The left macro and explicit rotation}
For a left move replace the initial four right rules by
\begin{align*}
 A_i&\to L_i\xi_i,& a_i&\to l_i\xi_i,\\
 B_i&\to C\xi_i(c\xi_i)^\epsilon,&b_i&\to c\xi_i c\xi_i,\\

\end{align*}
One traversal gives
\[
 L_i\xi_i(l_i\xi_i)^M C\xi_i(c\xi_i)^{2N+\epsilon}.

Keep the $C/c/S/s$ preparation. Replace target $A_j,a_j$ in the $D/d$ rules by temporary $B'_j,b'_j$, and replace target $B_j,b_j$ in the $T/t$ rules by actual $A_j,a_j$. Add
\[
 B'_j\to B_j\xi_j,\qquad b'_j\to b_j\xi_j.

The right parity calculation with the tape roles exchanged yields
\[
 B'_j\xi_j(b'_j\xi_j)^{2N+\epsilon}

 \qquad j=j_{M\bmod2}.

Moving the leading prime block to the tail gives the exact canonical left-move target. This finite rotation is the missing content behind an informal appeal to left/right symmetry.

\subsection{Safety before acceptance and uniqueness at acceptance}
Every canonical word has at least four symbols. All preacceptance productions except the shrinking $B_i/b_i$ or $L_i/l_i$ rules have length at least two. During the shrinking phase an untouched $C\xi_i(c\xi_i)^{M'}$ block of length at least two remains, and an $S$ remains at the finish. Therefore every preacceptance intermediate queue has length at least three. Prime rotation preserves length. No valid rejecting run can halt by an accidental short queue.

The exact cuts create one next canonical head. It is read only after all old-macro symbols preceding it are consumed; at that read the whole word is canonical. In particular the first active $A_{19}$ occurs at a genuine accepting boundary, with no delayed old head lineage elsewhere in the queue.

Set $A_{19}\to H$, with a fresh halt symbol $H$. Give the accepting suffix symbols inert productions $ZZ$, and $Z\to ZZ$. No other nonhalting rule emits $H$. Once $A_{19}$ is read, the remaining accepting-state symbols cannot recreate either $A_{19}$ or $H$. Thus exactly one fresh $H$ is emitted. An occurrence in the second deleted position is never mistaken for an active head event.

\section{Normalization with a persistent phase}\label{sec:normalization}
Give each original tag symbol width one. Its unnormalized Genera production is its tag production at phase zero and empty at phase one. Two FIFO reads implement one deletion-2 step. The phase is persistent across generation boundaries, so a generation ending after an odd number of original reads is not reset.

Introduce a width-zero dummy $d$, with $d\to dd$. Pad each production to four symbols using trailing $d$, and replace its two consecutive pairs by fresh width-zero pair symbols. A pair $[u,v]$ expands to $uv$ at either phase. Every normalized production now has exactly two outputs.

\begin{lemma}[Two-layer projection]
Starting in an original-symbol layer, erasing dummies after two normalized generations gives exactly one unnormalized generation, with exactly its final persistent phase.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Only original symbols have width one, so only the first pass changes the phase. Its outputs are pairs and width-zero dummies. The second pass expands each pair, preserves order, and leaves the phase unchanged. Dummy expansion erases to the empty word. Applying these facts successively proves both the word and phase identities, also for odd erased word length.
\end{proof}

A first literal $H$ occurs only when its containing pair expands. The preceding prefix of that output contains original symbols and dummies. Their hypothetical next outputs contain only pairs and dummies, never $H$. This verifies the extra prefix condition in the Genera halt convention, not merely the existence of a halt symbol.

There are 2,024 nonhalting phase rows, each with two outputs; the two ignored halt rows are also total. Its unique halt-containing pair is $(H,d)$, numeric ID 694; the halt has ID 1012. At the output the improved bound and a nonzero coefficient agree, and the coefficient matches \eqref{eq:raycoefficient}.

The second verifier independently propagates exact sparse highest forms using an opaque degree-three base, authenticates the selector sum and all kernel rows, and proves equality with \eqref{eq:fullleading}. Both execute newly written standard-library code, read the frozen source as data, and retain explicit checks under optimized Python. Their receipts record source pins, kernel and port degrees, all residual bounds, factor degrees, and the two coefficient residues.

The modular verifier uses at most four million source nodes, 800,000 coordinates, 256 monomials for its four-indeterminate identity, 512 MiB address space, and a 180-second ceiling. It keeps compact degree/coefficient arrays, not a degree-sized univariate polynomial or a full multivariate expansion. The independent symbolic verifier has its own explicit limits and receipt. These operational bounds constrain the checks; they are not bounds on witness size or computational complexity of the represented languages.

The historical manifest and original reproducibility subtree remain byte-for-byte unchanged, including their valid syntactic degree bound and earlier inconclusive probes. The separate exact-degree extension supplies the stronger invariant for the \emph{same} source. This preserves an auditable distinction between an improved theorem about a circuit and an alteration of that circuit.

The evidence has several layers, with deliberately different purposes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Semantic audit.} Independent checks cover 29,696 sparse-tape instruction contexts, 1,056 signed input-frame cases, 4,132 canonical-length cases, 192 exponent cases, and 192 exact-width E cases with rejected padding variants. A separate normalization audit checks 819,907 two-generation identities and 237,750 unique-H prefix cases. The accompanying proofs, rather than these finite counts, establish the parametric statements.
\item \emph{Literal-table audit.} All 29 instructions and all numeric tag/Genera productions are reconstructed independently. Dynamics include 18,125 macro comparisons and 2,005,675 tag steps, with all 534 operative nonhalt tag symbols exercised, minimum preacceptance length three, and 300 accepting entries. Original-source code is read as data, never executed.
\item \emph{Grill table audit.} Every one of the 397,488 exponents is derived by an independent position formula and compared byte for byte. Exact local E/L/R, shifted-erasure, and complete cleanup tests check the actual source rows, including the unique halt-producing pair.
\item \emph{Complete arithmetic audits.} Independent readers check the binary DAG's typed references, constant-only recipes, six-external signature, witness count, all comparisons, one-unit finalizer, gate/input liveness, ledger, and degree bound. An exact motif audit consumes every row and compares every runtime coefficient by integer equality; all 67 native rows match authenticated complete fixtures. A separate formula evaluator checks the full source modulo independent primes without importing the producer.
\end{enumerate}

The mathematical text, source emitters, complete DAG, literal table, frozen proof imports, audit code, and results are included in the archive. Revision 1 adds a separate exact-degree proof and two independent verifiers using the existing DAG without duplicating it. The original scientific subtree and its historical degree metadata remain byte-for-byte unchanged. The portable replay uses Python 3.10 or newer on Linux and only its standard library; the resource-limited checkers use the Unix resource module and Linux RSS units. It needs no network or upstream Python execution. It works from a freshly extracted archive and is checked both normally and with \fname{python3 -O}. Explicit exceptions, rather than removable assertions, enforce validation. PDF rebuilding additionally requires a conventional LaTeX installation and the packages named in the preamble.

The source-provenance record distinguishes original frozen hashes from documented portability transformations, including path adaptation and replacing local validation assertions by explicit exceptions. The inventory authenticates the delivered file set, including the actual arithmetic DAG, and rejects missing, extra, and altered files. Regeneration is performed in a separate work directory; it does not rewrite the supplied scientific evidence. Timing fields may vary, while mathematical metadata and DAG bytes are deterministic.

The binary format is deliberately simple. Its header is \fname{CDAGv1} followed by two zero bytes. Each subsequent 17-byte record is little-endian \fname{<Bqq}: opcode 0 for addition, 1 for subtraction, or 2 for multiplication, followed by two signed 64-bit handles. A negative handle selects a constant recipe; a handle at least $2^{50}$ selects a supplied coordinate; other nonnegative handles refer to earlier gates. The JSON manifest gives compact coordinate families, exact coefficient recipes, comparisons, and the final output handle. This fully specifies the polynomial without expanding a prohibitively large monomial list.

\section{Further mathematical questions}
\paragraph{Can a different representation reduce the exact degree?}
The exact degree of this frozen polynomial is now settled, with all program parameters still variable. Lowering degree therefore requires a genuinely different polynomial, rather than a sharper analysis of these same gates. One concrete direction is to isolate the largest native comparison and ask whether a paid auxiliary-variable interface can reduce total degree while controlling witness count, coefficient height and operation count. Such a change needs its own complete positive soundness and converse.

\paragraph{Can ordinary input be loaded without unary expansion?}
The second recoder pays for an exponent equal to the finite tape integer plus one. This is finite and arithmetically explicit but encodes an enormous unary tag word. A direct binary-input source compiler could remove that conceptual expansion. Such a proposal needs an actual fixed source-language theorem and an exact-width loader, rather than substituting a familiar universality claim.

\paragraph{Which selectors can genuinely be shared?}
The current emitter preserves all $2m$ chronological selectors. Equal run exponents share justified history classes, not phase identities. Eliminating selectors requires a sound and complete reconstruction of their phase chronology, especially across repeated zero exponents. A smaller numerical table could reduce both selectors and gates, but must retain the complete halt protocol.

\paragraph{Can the five program parameters be tied together?}
The valid tuple recipes have substantial algebraic dependence on a selected finite program. Encoding those dependences with fewer external parameters is a distinct task: it requires paying for a program decoder and proving that its range is the valid slice. Existentially freeing the present five parameters would change the recognized language and is not an acceptable shortcut.

\paragraph{Can the imported theorem boundary be reduced?}
A proof-assistant development could first formalize the all-tuple native identities, typed coefficient language, and exact source compiler, leaving the complete Pell/AND theorem as one explicit interface. A fuller development would certify that interface and the finite U15 initialization. These are separable proof obligations, and neither is supplied by increasing the number of bounded tests.

\paragraph{What should an explicit accepting witness demonstrate?}
Constructing a full positive tuple for a small genuine accepting source would test the most remote native interfaces, especially prescribed scale and restoration. It would still not prove universality. A feasible experiment should first estimate witness bit lengths and distinguish a full native solution from outer port assignments or modular residues.

\paragraph{What is the right cost comparison?}
The emitted source offers a reproducible arithmetic-operation baseline. Comparing substrates also requires coefficient height, source-program size, witness arity, maximum witness growth, exact-degree information, and bit cost. A source with fewer arithmetic gates can be worse on the other measures. Reporting those tradeoffs is more informative than collapsing them into a single unqualified complexity figure.

\section*{Conclusion}
The completed construction has one fixed numerical Grill program, one fully emitted polynomial circuit, explicit ordinary-input and program-parameter semantics, and an exact initial-width interface. The paid composition uses 797,135 positive witnesses and 3,600,546 operations. Its exact total degree is 69,339,973, certified on the unchanged source by the explicit full leading form and nonzero ray coefficient. The central logical safeguards are the accepting-head invariant, phase-preserving normalization, unrestricted second recoder, same-width positive native converse, and one integer-unit finalizer. Under the pinned complete theorem imports, these give the fixed-language equivalence of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for every positive ordinary input.
