\begin{abstract}
We investigate a concrete open problem in formal-language theory: does the
three-component canonical tuple grammar introduced by Yuyama generate every word
with equally many occurrences of \(\aa,\bb,\cc\)?  The problem is the first
unresolved case of a combinatorial reformulation of the Kanazawa--Salvati
conjecture.  We develop a terminating, cut-sensitive membership recurrence and
prove its equivalence to the defining grammar.  We then prove a constructive
three-component inclusion theorem and an infinite two-component obstruction
family.  In particular,
\[
 \aa^{3m}\bb^{3m}\cc^m\bb^{2m}\cc^{4m}\aa^{2m}
 \in L_3\setminus L_2\qquad(m\geq1).
\]
The first member is a previously published example; the report supplies an
analytic family argument rather than claiming that example as a discovery.
A reproducible exhaustive computation accounts for all \(417\,019\,279\)
balanced words of length at most \(21\), using symmetry reduction, and finds
all of them in \(L_3\).  Thus a counterexample, if one exists, has length at
least \(24\), subject to the correctness of the supplied software execution.
Python and C++ implementations, a forward-only validation oracle, and
independently checked positive derivation certificates are included.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question, its scope, and the outcome}
\label{sec:question}

\subsection{A small, explicit target within a larger conjecture}
Let
\[
 \Mix=\{w\in\{\aa,\bb,\cc\}^{*}:
       \len{w}_{\aa}=\len{w}_{\bb}=\len{w}_{\cc}\}.
\]
Here \(\len{w}_x\) counts occurrences of the letter \(x\).  Membership in
\(\Mix\) is easy to decide by counting.  The difficult question concerns
\emph{how a restricted grammar can construct all of its words}, not whether
one can recognize balanced letter counts.

A multiple context-free grammar allows a nonterminal to produce a tuple of
strings rather than a single string. Its arity, also called its fan-out,
measures the number of components. Restrictions on how the components of
several children may interleave lead to the well-nested subclass. Kanazawa
and Salvati proved that \(\Mix\) is not a tree-adjoining language
\cite{KS2012}; Salvati proved that it is a two-component multiple
context-free language without the well-nested restriction \cite{Salvati2015}.
These statements concern different grammar classes and are not contradictory.

Yuyama's September 12, 2026 preprint \cite{Yuyama2026} gives explicit tuple
sets \(\A_r\), defined in \cref{sec:grammar}, and canonical languages
\(L_r=\cat(\A_r)\).  Its Section 6 and Remark 3.19 explicitly identify the
following first unresolved case.

\begin{problem}[The selected problem]
\label{prob:main}
Determine whether \(L_3=\Mix\). Equivalently, either construct and prove a
word in \(\Mix\setminus L_3\), or prove that every balanced word belongs to
\(L_3\).
\end{problem}

The source's Corollary 3.18 identifies the full Kanazawa--Salvati conjecture
with the assertion that \(L_r\subsetneq\Mix\) for \emph{every} \(r\).
Consequently, a counterexample at \(r=3\) would establish one canonical case,
not the full conjecture. Moreover, the cofinality theorem for arbitrary
well-nested grammars can increase arity: proving \(L_3\ne\Mix\) would not, by
itself, exclude \emph{every} well-nested grammar of fan-out three. Conversely,
\(L_3=\Mix\) would refute the full conjecture because the canonical grammar
itself is well-nested. These logical directions are important.

\subsection{What this investigation establishes}
The principal mathematical result is a family with exact canonical rank
three. Put \(N=p+u\). For positive integers satisfying
\[
 p>s,\qquad u>r,\qquad r\ne s,
\]
we prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainfamily}
 \aa^p\bb^{N-s}\cc^r\bb^s\cc^{N-r}\aa^u\in L_3\setminus L_2.
\end{equation}
The inequalities imply that every displayed block is nonempty. The proof
rules out all two-component derivations by analyzing the only three possible
initial useful cuts and all subsequent endpoint deletions. It is not an
extrapolation from a finite search.

A stronger positive statement explains why this family cannot produce the
sought three-component counterexample. Whenever
\(v\in\{\bb,\cc\}^{*}\) has \(N\) occurrences of each letter and
\(0\leq p\leq N\), we construct the \emph{specific tuple}
\[
  (\aa^p,v,\aa^{N-p})\in\A_3.
\]
The position of both cuts is part of the theorem.

The computational result is a completed exhaustive search through length
\(21\), with no word outside \(L_3\). In particular, all
\(111\,564\) length-\(21\) words rejected at level two are accepted at level
three. The enumeration uses symmetry and the inclusion \(L_2\subseteq L_3\);
it does not run an independent level-three query on each of hundreds of
millions of words.

\subsection{Provenance and limits}
The tuple grammar and the open status of \cref{prob:main} are taken from
\cite{Yuyama2026}, version 1. Its Example 1.4 already gives
\(\word{aaabbbcbbccccaa}\in L_3\setminus L_2\), and its Remark 3.19 reports
that this is a lexicographically first shortest level-two obstruction.
That example is a regression test, not a new discovery in this report.
The one-sided binary inclusion used below also appears in the proof of its
Proposition E.3; it is explicitly attributed when restated.

The proofs of the recurrence, the two-ended inclusion, the parameterized
obstruction argument, and the associated consequences are given in full so
that their correctness does not depend on trusting an experimental pattern.
Their bibliographic novelty has not been established by an exhaustive
literature review. The source and focused follow-up searches identify a
published open target; they do not establish a claim that no unindexed
solution exists anywhere.

\section{The canonical tuple grammar}
\label{sec:grammar}

\subsection{Regrouping means merging, not cutting}
Write \(\Sigma=\{\aa,\bb,\cc\}\), and let \(\eps\) be the empty word.
For a list \((v_1,\ldots,v_m)\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regroup}
 \Reg_r(v_1,\ldots,v_m)
 =\left\{\left(\prod_{j=d_0+1}^{d_1}v_j,\ldots,
                   \prod_{j=d_{r-1}+1}^{d_r}v_j\right):
       0=d_0\leq d_1\leq\cdots\leq d_r=m\right\}.
\end{equation}
An empty product denotes \(\eps\). Products here are ordered concatenations,
not commutative products. An output component may merge consecutive input
components; a component may not be split internally. For example,
\((\word{ab},\word{c})\) can be regrouped to
\((\word{abc},\eps)\), but not to
\((\word{a},\word{bc})\).

\begin{definition}[Canonical grammar; Yuyama \cite{Yuyama2026}]
\label{def:grammar}
For an integer \(r\geq1\), \(\A_r\) is the least set of \(r\)-tuples of
words closed under the following rules.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Empty:} \((\eps,\ldots,\eps)\in\A_r\).
\item \textbf{Wrap:} from \((w_1,\ldots,w_r)\in\A_r\), infer
\((x_1w_1y_1,\ldots,x_rw_ry_r)\), where each endpoint word
\(x_i,y_i\) is either empty or a single letter, and their concatenation
contains exactly one \(\aa\), one \(\bb\), and one \(\cc\).
\item \textbf{Insert and regroup:} from \(U=(u_1,\ldots,u_r)\) and
\(V=(v_1,\ldots,v_r)\) in \(\A_r\), choose \(0\leq i\leq r\) and infer
any tuple in
\[
 \Reg_r(u_1,\ldots,u_i,v_1,\ldots,v_r,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_r).
\]
\end{enumerate}
Finally, \(L_r=\{w_1\cdots w_r:(w_1,\ldots,w_r)\in\A_r\}\).
\end{definition}

All components of the second child are inserted at a \emph{single gap}
in the first child's list. They are not arbitrarily shuffled through it.
Likewise, the wrap rule has \(2r\) distinct endpoint slots, each with capacity
one. Treating a slot as capable of receiving a longer word changes the
grammar.

\subsection{Elementary invariants and normalization}
For a tuple \(T\), let \(\norm(T)\) be its list of nonempty components,
with their order unchanged. A normalized tuple may have any number \(k\)
of components with \(0\leq k\leq r\). Write \(T\in\overline\A_r\) if
padding it with empty components gives a tuple in \(\A_r\).

\begin{lemma}[Regrouping and empty positions]\label{lem:normalize}
The set \(\A_r\) is closed under regrouping its own component list.
Furthermore, all placements of empty components around a fixed ordered
nonempty list are equivalent for membership. Thus
\(\overline\A_r\) is well defined, and
\[
 w\in L_r\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (w)\in\overline\A_r
\]
for nonempty \(w\); the empty word corresponds to the empty normalized tuple.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the third rule with the all-empty tuple as the second child.
Inserting that child does not change the nonempty list, and the permitted
regroupings include every merger of adjacent components and every desired
placement of empty components. In particular, one can pass between two
paddings of the same nonempty list without splitting anything. Merging all
nonempty components proves the forward implication in the last equivalence;
the reverse implication is the definition of concatenation.
\end{proof}

Normalization does \emph{not} forget the boundaries between nonempty
components. It only forgets the positions of empty slots.

\begin{proposition}[Balance, monotonicity, and symmetries]
\label{prop:basic}
Every tuple in \(\A_r\) has a concatenation in \(\Mix\). Also
\(L_r\subseteq L_{r+1}\), \(L_1=\{\eps\}\), and
\(\bigcup_{r\geq1}L_r=\Mix\). Membership is preserved by a global permutation
of the alphabet and by full reversal
\[
 (w_1,\ldots,w_k)\longmapsto(w_k^{\mathrm{rev}},\ldots,w_1^{\mathrm{rev}}).
\]
Each \(L_r\) is closed under concatenation of words.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The empty tuple is balanced. A wrap adds one of each letter, and a binary
rule adds the two children's letter counts. This proves balance by induction.

To increase arity, pad every tuple in a derivation with empty components.
The same endpoint insertions remain legal, and the same nonempty component
lists can be inserted and regrouped. This simulates the whole derivation
at the larger arity. At arity one there are only two endpoint slots, so no
wrap can insert three letters; binary combinations of empty tuples remain
empty. Hence \(L_1=\{\eps\}\).

For a balanced word of length \(3N>0\), use \(3N\) components, one for
each position of the word. Starting from empty components, fill one
as-yet-unfilled occurrence of each letter in each of \(N\) wrap steps.
The final tuple lists the individual letters of the word. This proves
membership in \(L_{3N}\), hence the union assertion.

A letter permutation maps each grammar rule to another legal rule. Full
reversal reverses the ordered component list and each component, exchanges
left and right endpoint insertions, and maps an insertion gap to its
reflected gap. Regrouping is also respected. Induction proves both symmetry
claims. Finally, normalize derivations of \(x,y\in L_r\) to one nonempty
component each, insert the second after the first, and merge the result.
This derives \(xy\in L_r\).
\end{proof}

For a balanced word, define its \emph{canonical rank} by
\(\rank(w)=\min\{r:w\in L_r\}\). This is a rank in this particular canonical
family, not a minimal fan-out over all possible grammars generating a
language. In particular, every singleton language is regular, so confusing
these notions would make the definition meaningless.

\section{An exact backward membership recurrence}
\label{sec:parser}

\subsection{The three inverse operations}
Let \(T=(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\) be normalized, with \(k\leq r\), and put
\(w=t_1\cdots t_k\), \(n=\len{w}\). We use half-open position intervals
\([p,q)\), with \(0\leq p<q\leq n\).

\paragraph{Peeling.}
Choose one physical endpoint occurrence of each of \(\aa,\bb,\cc\).
Remove the three chosen letters and discard resulting empty components.
A component can lose at most one letter from each end. A length-one
component has one physical occurrence, not two different occurrences.
Choosing one occurrence of each different letter automatically prevents
choosing the same occurrence twice.

\paragraph{Extracting a balanced interval.}
Choose a proper, nonempty balanced factor \(w[p:q]\). For each original
component, intersect it with that interval. The ordered list of nonempty
intersections is the inside tuple \(B_{p,q}\). The outside tuple
\(A_{p,q}\) consists of the nonempty portions before and after that
intersection, in their original order. Every original nonempty boundary is
retained. If the interval lies strictly inside a single component, its
exposed prefix and suffix become \emph{two different components} of
\(A_{p,q}\). Reject this extraction alternative if either child has more
than \(r\) nonempty components.

For example, extracting the displayed \(\word{abc}\) from the
one-component tuple \((x\,\word{abc}\,y)\), with nonempty words
\(x,y\), produces the outside tuple \((x,y)\), not \((xy)\). This distinction is necessary to reinsert the inside
child at a gap.

\paragraph{Refining a cut.}
When \(k<r\), split one component into two nonempty consecutive parts.
This is a \emph{backward} alternative: a successful derivation of the
finer tuple can be merged to derive the current tuple. It is not a
forward closure property of \(\A_r\).

\subsection{The characterization theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Cut-sensitive recurrence]
\label{thm:recurrence}
The following recursive procedure decides membership in
\(\overline\A_r\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{\arabic*.},leftmargin=2em]
\item Accept the empty tuple. Reject tuples of more than \(r\) nonempty
components, and reject tuples whose total letter counts are not balanced.
\item Accept if some peeling alternative has an accepted child.
\item Accept if some proper balanced interval extraction has both induced
children accepted and each has at most \(r\) components.
\item Accept if some one-cut refinement has an accepted child.
\item Otherwise reject.
\end{enumerate}
The order of alternatives affects running time but not the answer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Termination]
Use the lexicographic measure
\[
 \mu(T)=\bigl(\len{t_1\cdots t_k},\ r-k\bigr)
 \quad\text{in }\mathbb N\times\mathbb N.
\]
A peeling child has three fewer letters. Both extraction children have
strictly fewer letters because the extracted factor is nonempty and
proper. A refinement keeps the letter count unchanged but increases the
number of components by one. Thus every recursive call strictly decreases
\(\mu\). There are finitely many alternatives at each call, so the entire
search terminates, even without memoization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Soundness]
Proceed by well-founded induction on \(\mu\). The empty tuple is legal.
For a successful peel, pad the accepted child so that all surviving
components occupy the positions they had before deletion. Components
removed entirely can be restored as empty slots; there are at most \(r\)
slots because the parent has at most \(r\) components. Inserting the
three removed letters at their original endpoints is a valid wrap.
A two-letter parent component whose letters were both deleted uses the
two distinct endpoints of one empty child component, as required.

For a successful extraction, pad both accepted induced tuples to arity
\(r\). The inside components form a single consecutive block in the
flattened parent. The outside tuple retains a boundary at the place where
that block must be inserted, including the case of a split original
component. Insert the inside list at that gap and regroup to the
original tuple. Every original parent boundary is available in the
combined list, so no internal splitting is needed.

For a successful refinement, the accepted finer tuple merges directly
to the parent by \cref{lem:normalize}. These operations prove that every
accepted tuple belongs to \(\overline\A_r\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Completeness]
We show that every finite grammar derivation yields a successful recursive
alternative. Empty placements may be ignored by
\cref{lem:normalize}; regrouping is allowed as a derived unary rule.

Suppose the last effective operation is a wrap. Its three newly inserted
letters occur at endpoints in the conclusion. Peeling precisely those
occurrences recovers its premise, modulo empty positions. This is an
alternative of the recurrence.

Suppose the last operation is a binary insertion with two nonempty
children \(U,V\), followed by regrouping. All letters from \(V\) form a
contiguous factor \([p,q)\) in the concatenated conclusion. The factor
is nonempty, proper, and balanced. Each component of \(V\) remains whole
through the final regrouping. Consequently, the induced inside tuple
\(B_{p,q}\) is obtained by merging some adjacent components of \(V\),
possibly changing empty positions. Likewise, the induced outside tuple
\(A_{p,q}\) is a regrouping of \(U\). If the conclusion has one component
crossing both sides of the inserted factor, its outside prefix and suffix
come from opposite sides of an actual gap in \(U\); retaining them as
separate components therefore does not create more components than
\(U\) had. Both induced children belong to \(\overline\A_r\) by
regrouping closure, have at most \(r\) components, and have smaller
length. Induction applies to them.

A binary insertion with an empty child is just regrouping of its
nonempty premise. If the normalized conclusion is unchanged, discard
that redundant step. Otherwise its premise is a strict refinement of
its conclusion, obtainable by inserting one missing boundary at a time.
All intermediate tuples are coarsenings of the derivable premise, so
all are derivable. Every inserted boundary is a legal one-cut
refinement, and the measure decreases along this inverse sequence.
There cannot be an infinite chain of discarded redundant rules because
the original derivation is finite. These cases exhaust the defining
rules, proving completeness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A diagnostic example: cuts cannot be forgotten}
\begin{example}\label{ex:cuttrap}
The word \(\word{aaccbb}\) belongs to \(L_2\), but
\[
  (\word{a},\word{accb},\word{b})\notin\A_3.
\]
For the positive assertion, \(\word{ccbb}\) is a balanced binary word,
so the one-sided inclusion of \cref{lem:onesided} applies. For the negative
assertion, no component endpoint is \(\cc\), and there is no spare
component for refinement. No length-three factor of \(\word{aaccbb}\)
is balanced; the only other nonempty balanced factor length is six,
which is the whole word. Therefore every recurrence alternative fails.
\end{example}

In particular, replacing tuple states by their concatenations gives a
wrong parser. Even accepting every cut of an accepted word is already
wrong at total length six. This small example is included in the tests.

\subsection{Complexity and implementation choices}
For a fixed input word of length \(n\), every component encountered by the
uncanonicalized recurrence is an interval of the original word. Deleting
endpoints shortens an interval, while extraction and refinement split
intervals without reordering their surviving positions. With \(k\)
nonempty components, the number of possible ordered disjoint interval
lists is
\[
 \binom{n+k}{2k}.
\]
Indeed, the \(k\) interval lengths are positive and the \(k+1\) gaps
are nonnegative; subtracting one from each interval length gives this
stars-and-bars count. Hence the total number of positional states is
\(O_r(n^{2r})\).

Each state has \(O_r(1)\) endpoint choices, \(O(n^2)\) interval choices,
and \(O_r(n)\) refinement choices. A straightforward implementation that
materializes child strings in \(O_r(n)\) time therefore has the
conservative bounds
\[
  O_r(n^{2r+3})\ \text{time},\qquad
  O_r(n^{2r+1})\ \text{stored string space}
\]
with an unbounded memo table and ordinary constant-time dictionary
operations apart from string processing. A dense positional chart can
avoid relying on hashing for the state lookup bound. These bounds are
not claims of optimal grammar parsing complexity.

The supplied programs additionally identify states under global alphabet
renaming and full reversal. These are genuine symmetries by
\cref{prop:basic}; at most twelve positional variants are involved.
Python uses an optional least-recently-used cache. C++ caches states of
letter length at most \(18\), and the enumeration driver occasionally
clears its level-two cache. Cache eviction may cause recomputation;
it never licenses accepting or rejecting a state without evaluating it.
The production exhaustive driver has no node budget or timeout that
could be mistaken for a negative membership result.

\section{A constructive three-component inclusion theorem}
\label{sec:positive}

\subsection{Balanced binary words and one-sided storage}
A binary word \(v\in\{\bb,\cc\}^{*}\) is called balanced here when
\(\len{v}_{\bb}=\len{v}_{\cc}\). Let this common number be \(N\).
For a nonempty balanced binary word, follow the difference
\(\#\bb-\#\cc\) along its prefixes. Its first return to zero gives
either a proper balanced initial factor or the whole word. In the latter
case the first and last letters differ, and deleting them leaves a
balanced word. Thus induction can always use either a concatenation of
two shorter balanced words or an outer pair of opposite letters.

\begin{lemma}[One-sided binary inclusion]
\label{lem:onesided}
For every balanced binary word \(v\) with \(N\) occurrences of each
letter,
\[
  (v,\aa^N)\in\A_2,\qquad (\aa^N,v)\in\A_2.
\]
This fact is also established in the proof of Yuyama's Proposition E.3
\cite{Yuyama2026}; we give the elementary construction for use below.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on \(N\). The empty case is immediate. If the first return is the
whole word, write \(v=xt y\), where \(\{x,y\}=\{\bb,\cc\}\) and
\(t\) is balanced with \(N-1\) occurrences of each letter. From
\((t,\aa^{N-1})\), insert \(x\) and \(y\) at the two ends of the first
component and \(\aa\) at one end of the second. This is one wrap.

Otherwise write \(v=v_1v_2\) with positive sizes \(N_1,N_2\),
\(N_1+N_2=N\). Induction gives
\((v_1,\aa^{N_1})\) and \((v_2,\aa^{N_2})\). Insert the second tuple
between the two components of the first and regroup:
\[
 (v_1,v_2,\aa^{N_2},\aa^{N_1})
   \longmapsto (v_1v_2,\aa^{N_2+N_1}).
\]
This proves the first assertion. Apply full reversal to the first
assertion for \(v^{\mathrm{rev}}\) to obtain the second.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Splitting the homogeneous block between both ends}
\begin{theorem}[Two-ended binary inclusion]
\label{thm:binary}
Let \(v\in\{\bb,\cc\}^{*}\) contain \(N\) copies of each letter. For
all \(p,u\geq0\) with \(p+u=N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary}
  (\aa^p,v,\aa^u)\in\A_3.
\end{equation}
The proof yields a finite derivation using only the original three rules.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on \(N\), including all values of \(p\) at once. At \(N=0\), all
components are empty. For \(N>0\), use the first-return decomposition
of \(v\).

If the first return is the whole word, write \(v=xt y\), with opposite
binary letters \(x,y\). If \(p>0\), apply the induction hypothesis to
\((\aa^{p-1},t,\aa^u)\), then insert \(\aa\) in the first component and
\(x,y\) at the ends of the middle component. If \(p=0\), then \(u=N>0\);
start from \((\eps,t,\aa^{u-1})\) and put the new \(\aa\) in the last
component instead. The insertions use three distinct legal slots.

Otherwise write \(v=v_1v_2\), with balanced positive sizes
\(N_1,N_2\). There are two cases; choosing the allocation carefully is
what avoids a forbidden crossing of child components.

If \(p\leq N_1\), use the inductively derivable tuples
\[
 X=(\aa^p,v_1,\aa^{N_1-p}),\qquad
 Y=(\eps,v_2,\aa^{N_2}).
\]
Insert \(Y\) after the second component of \(X\). The resulting list is
\[
 (\aa^p,v_1,\eps,v_2,\aa^{N_2},\aa^{N_1-p}).
\]
Group its first entry, its next three entries, and its last two entries.
The result is \((\aa^p,v_1v_2,\aa^{N-p})\).

If \(p>N_1\), take instead
\[
 X=(\aa^{p-N_1},v_2,\aa^{N-p}),\qquad
 Y=(\aa^{N_1},v_1,\eps).
\]
Insert \(Y\) after the first component of \(X\). The combined list is
\[
 (\aa^{p-N_1},\aa^{N_1},v_1,\eps,v_2,\aa^{N-p}).
\]
Group its first two entries, its next three entries, and its last entry.
This again gives the required tuple. All exponents used in the
induction hypotheses are nonnegative, and both binary words are
shorter. This completes the construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ends}
Every balanced word in the pattern \(\aa^{*}\{\bb,\cc\}^{*}\aa^{*}\)
belongs to \(L_3\). The same is true after any global letter permutation.
\end{corollary}

This corollary rules out a large natural source of prospective
three-component counterexamples: arbitrarily complicated binary interiors
with all occurrences of the third letter confined to the two ends.
Complexity of the binary interior alone cannot defeat level three.

\subsection{Derivation certificates rather than search traces}
The construction is implemented in \word{src/family\_certificate.py}.
Its output records actual empty, wrap, and binary-regroup rules, including
insertion gaps and weak cut indices. The independent checker
\word{src/check\_certificate.py} simply verifies these local rules;
it does not invoke the membership parser or the family theorem.

There is one wrap for each pair of binary letters. The recursive
first-return decomposition uses at most \(N-1\) binary nodes and at most
\(N\) empty leaves when \(N>0\). Thus a tree certificate can be chosen
with at most \(3N-1\) nodes. The number of rule nodes is linear, although
explicitly storing the full tuple strings at every node can take
quadratic total space. This distinction is useful when turning the
construction into a proof-producing implementation.

\section{An infinite family that cannot use two components}
\label{sec:negative}

\subsection{A balanced-factor obstruction}
Call a nonempty word \emph{factor-balanced-primitive} if it is balanced
and has no nonempty proper balanced factor. This terminology refers to
letter balance, not to the usual notion of a word that is not a proper
power.

\begin{lemma}[A homogeneous exterior and a positive binary excursion]
\label{lem:primitive}
Suppose \(z\in\{\bb,\cc\}^{*}\) has \(M>0\) copies of each letter,
and every nonempty proper prefix of \(z\) has more \(\bb\)'s than
\(\cc\)'s. If \(P,U\geq0\) and \(P+U=M\), then
\(\aa^Pz\aa^U\) is factor-balanced-primitive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A nonempty balanced factor must contain an \(\aa\), since a word
without \(\aa\)'s cannot have three equal positive letter counts.
If the factor meets both exterior \(\aa\)-blocks, it contains all of
\(z\). It must therefore contain \(M\) occurrences of \(\aa\), hence
all occurrences of every letter, and so is the whole word.

If it meets only the initial \(\aa\)-block, its binary part is a
nonempty prefix of \(z\). That prefix must have equal binary counts.
By the strict prefix assumption it is all of \(z\), so the factor
needs \(M\) initial \(\aa\)'s. This is possible only when \(U=0\),
and then the factor is the whole word. The suffix case is similar:
a proper nonempty suffix of \(z\) cannot have equal binary counts,
because its complementary proper prefix would then have zero balance.
These cases exhaust every factor containing \(\aa\).
\end{proof}

A useful special case is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excursion}
 z=\bb^Q\cc^R\bb^S\cc^T,
 \qquad Q+S=R+T=M,\quad Q>R,\quad T>S,
\end{equation}
where \(Q,T>0\) and \(R,S\geq0\). Its binary height is positive
between its two endpoints: after the first descending block it is
\(Q-R>0\), after the next ascending block it is \(T\), and only the
last descending block returns it to zero. Either \(R\) or \(S\) may
be zero; the argument still holds.

\subsection{The obstruction theorem}
\begin{theorem}[A parameterized level-two obstruction]
\label{thm:cone}
Let \(p,u,r,s\) be positive integers, let \(N=p+u\), and assume
\[
  p>s,\qquad u>r,\qquad r\ne s.
\]
Then the balanced word
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
 W_{p,u,r,s}
   =\aa^p\bb^{N-s}\cc^r\bb^s\cc^{N-r}\aa^u
\end{equation}
satisfies \(W_{p,u,r,s}\notin L_2\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set \(q=N-s\) and \(t=N-r\). The hypotheses imply
\[
  N>r+s,\qquad q>r,\qquad t>s.
\]
Reversing the entire word and interchanging \(\bb\) with \(\cc\)
sends \((p,u,r,s)\) to \((u,p,s,r)\). This preserves all hypotheses
and membership. Since \(r\ne s\), we may therefore assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assume}
 r<s.
\end{equation}
In particular \(p>s>r\), \(u>r\), and both exterior blocks survive
any sequence of at most \(r\) deletions of \(\aa\).

The binary interior \(\bb^q\cc^r\bb^s\cc^t\) has the form
\eqref{eq:excursion}. By \cref{lem:primitive}, the whole word has no
nonempty proper balanced factor. Viewed as a one-component tuple,
its endpoints are both \(\aa\), so it cannot be peeled. The recurrence
of \cref{thm:recurrence} must therefore begin by inserting one cut.

After that cut there are two nonempty components and no spare component
slot. To peel a triple, the cut must expose both a \(\bb\) and a
\(\cc\), since the two outer endpoints are \(\aa\)'s. The only useful
initial cuts are the following three seams:
\begin{align*}
 \mathrm{I}:&\quad
 (\aa^p\bb^q,\ \cc^r\bb^s\cc^t\aa^u),\\
 \mathrm{II}:&\quad
 (\aa^p\bb^q\cc^r,\ \bb^s\cc^t\aa^u),\\
 \mathrm{III}:&\quad
 (\aa^p\bb^q\cc^r\bb^s,\ \cc^t\aa^u).
\end{align*}
Every other cut has an endpoint alphabet missing at least one letter.
It cannot be extracted, peeled, or further refined, so it is immediately
rejected. We now rule out every continuation of the three useful cuts.

\paragraph{Case I.}
Until \(j=r\) triples have been peeled, the \(\bb\) must be removed
from the end of the first component, and the \(\cc\) from the start
of the second. After \(j\) steps, the flattened binary interior is
\[
  \bb^{q-j}\cc^{r-j}\bb^s\cc^t.
\]
The two relevant strict inequalities are
\[
 (q-j)-(r-j)=q-r>0,\qquad t-s>0.
\]
It remains a positive binary excursion; the exterior \(\aa\)-counts
sum to its binary size. Thus \cref{lem:primitive} excludes every
extraction throughout the trajectory. Both exterior \(\aa\)-blocks
remain positive because \(p,u>r\), and neither component becomes
empty. At \(j=r\), the first \(\cc\)-block disappears. The two inner
endpoints are then both \(\bb\); the outer endpoints remain
\(\aa\). No endpoint is \(\cc\), so peeling stops without reaching
the empty tuple.

\paragraph{Case II.}
The forced binary deletions now remove from the first \(\cc\)-block
and the second \(\bb\)-block. After \(0\leq j\leq r\) steps the
binary interior is
\[
  \bb^q\cc^{r-j}\bb^{s-j}\cc^t.
\]
Its strict inequalities are
\[
 q-(r-j)=q-r+j>0,\qquad t-(s-j)=t-s+j>0.
\]
Again every flattened word is factor-balanced-primitive, both exterior
blocks stay positive, and neither component disappears. Because
\(r<s\), the first \(\cc\)-block disappears first, at \(j=r\).
Both inner endpoints are then \(\bb\), while both outer endpoints
are \(\aa\). Peeling again stops with no \(\cc\)-endpoint.

\paragraph{Case III.}
For \(0\leq j\leq s\), the binary deletions remove from the second
\(\bb\)-block and the last \(\cc\)-block. The binary interior is
\[
  \bb^q\cc^r\bb^{s-j}\cc^{t-j},
\]
with
\[
 q-r>0,\qquad (t-j)-(s-j)=t-s>0.
\]
The initial \(\aa\)-block stays nonempty because \(p>s\). The
terminal \(\aa\)-block might vanish before these \(s\) steps have
finished. This causes no omitted alternative: it merely exposes the
other end of the \emph{same} last \(\cc\)-block. Removing a \(\cc\)
from either exposed end gives the same shorter homogeneous block.
The \(\bb\)-deletion is still forced from the second \(\bb\)-block,
and all available \(\aa\)-deletions are already covered by allowing
arbitrary allocations between the two exterior blocks.

The positive-excursion inequalities still exclude every extraction.
At \(j=s\), the second \(\bb\)-block disappears while
\(t-s>0\). Both components are nonempty: the first still contains
\(\aa\), \(\bb^q\), and \(\cc^r\), and the second still contains
\(\cc^{t-s}\). Their exposed endpoints are \(\aa\) or \(\cc\);
no endpoint is \(\bb\). No further peel is possible.

In all three cases, every state before the terminal stall has two
nonempty components and no proper balanced factor. There is no way to
free a component slot or to use a binary extraction. The only possible
peeling trajectories all stall. The complete recurrence therefore
rejects every initial cut, and hence rejects the word itself.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the unequal parameters matter]
The assumption \(r\ne s\) is not cosmetic. If \(r=s\), the middle two
blocks can disappear simultaneously in Case II, exposing different
endpoints and invalidating the stall argument. The theorem asserts a
sufficient obstruction region; it does not assert that its conditions
are a complete classification of all six-block words.
\end{remark}

\section{Consequences: exact rank, counting, and rotation}
\label{sec:consequences}

\begin{corollary}[Exact canonical rank three]
\label{cor:rank}
Every word in \cref{thm:cone} has \(\rank(W_{p,u,r,s})=3\).
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled}
 w_m=\aa^{3m}\bb^{3m}\cc^m\bb^{2m}\cc^{4m}\aa^{2m}
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad \rank(w_m)=3\qquad(m\geq1).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The binary interior of \(W_{p,u,r,s}\) has \(N\) copies of each binary
letter. The two-ended inclusion theorem gives membership in \(L_3\),
while \cref{thm:cone} excludes \(L_2\). Monotonicity excludes every
lower rank. For \(w_m\), take
\((p,u,r,s)=(3m,2m,m,2m)\).
\end{proof}

The word \(w_1\) is the published example described in
\cref{sec:question}. The proof above treats all \(m\), and in fact a
larger multi-parameter region, without relying on the published
computer search for its negative half.

\begin{corollary}[A cubic-size certified obstruction region]
\label{cor:count}
For \(N\geq2\), the parameter region in \cref{thm:cone} gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 C_N=\binom{N-1}{3}-\left\lfloor\frac{(N-2)^2}{4}\right\rfloor
\end{equation}
distinct displayed six-block words of length \(3N\), with the binomial
coefficient interpreted as zero when its upper index is below three.
Thus \(\len{(L_3\setminus L_2)\cap\Sigma^{3N}}\geq C_N\), and
\(C_N\sim N^3/6\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For fixed \(p\) and \(u=N-p\), choose
\(1\leq s\leq p-1\) and \(1\leq r\leq u-1\), excluding \(r=s\).
The number is
\((p-1)(u-1)-\min(p-1,u-1)\). Put \(M=N-2\) and \(x=p-1\).
Summing over \(x=1,\ldots,M-1\) gives
\[
 \sum_{x=1}^{M-1}x(M-x)=\binom{M+1}{3},\qquad
 \sum_{x=1}^{M-1}\min(x,M-x)=\left\lfloor M^2/4\right\rfloor.
\]
The first identity follows from the sums of the first integers and their
squares; the second follows by pairing positions equidistant from the
ends, with a central term when \(M\) is even. All six runs are nonempty
and adjacent runs have different letters, so their run lengths uniquely
recover the parameters. There is no duplication in this count. The
asymptotic follows from \eqref{eq:count}.
\end{proof}

For \(N=5,6,7,8,9,10\), the lower bounds are
\(2,6,14,26,44,68\). These are counts in one oriented block pattern,
not total counts of level-two nonmembers; the exhaustive totals in
\cref{sec:experiments} are much larger.

\begin{corollary}[Failure of cyclic-rotation closure]
\label{cor:rotation}
The canonical language \(L_2\) is not closed under cyclic rotation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write \(w_m=\aa^{3m}v_m\aa^{2m}\). The word
\(\aa^{5m}v_m\) belongs to \(L_2\) by \cref{lem:onesided}, because
\(v_m\) has \(5m\) copies of each binary letter. Moving the first
\(2m\) occurrences of \(\aa\) from the beginning to the end produces
\(w_m\), which is not in \(L_2\) by \cref{cor:rank}.
\end{proof}

This is a property of one canonical language. It is not a claim that the
entire class of well-nested multiple context-free languages lacks any
particular closure property.

\begin{proposition}[What uniform scaling does and does not imply]
\label{prop:dilation}
Let \(h_m\) replace each letter \(x\) by \(x^m\), with \(m\geq1\).
Componentwise application of \(h_m\) preserves \(\A_r\), and therefore
\(h_m(L_r)\subseteq L_r\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induct on a derivation. Empty and binary-regroup rules commute with the
homomorphism. Replace each wrap by \(m\) copies of that same wrap,
using the same endpoint slots. Repeatedly inserting \(x\) before a
component and \(y\) after it yields \(x^m h_m(w)y^m\), exactly the
image of that component's original wrap. Each repeated step is legal.
\end{proof}

This only propagates \emph{positive} membership. It does not establish
\(h_m(w)\notin L_r\) from \(w\notin L_r\). The negative scaling
statement for \eqref{eq:scaled} genuinely uses \cref{thm:cone}; it is
not an automatic closure argument.

\section{Exhaustive computation and validation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Enumeration and symmetry weights}
The number of words with \(N\) occurrences of each of three letters is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multinomial}
 M_N=\frac{(3N)!}{(N!)^3}.
\end{equation}
For \(N>0\), the action of the six alphabet permutations is free:
a permutation fixing a word must fix every letter appearing in it,
and all three appear. Choose the representative in which the first
new letter is \(\aa\), the second is \(\bb\), and the third is
\(\cc\). The Python enumerator uses exactly these representatives,
each with weight six.

The C++ enumerator also uses full reversal. Let \(R(w)\) be the reverse
word relabelled by first occurrence. It visits only representatives
satisfying \(w\leq R(w)\) lexicographically. The corresponding orbit
has size six if \(w=R(w)\), and size twelve otherwise. This is because
reversal either stays in the original six-element letter orbit or
creates a disjoint second letter orbit. The empty word is handled
separately with weight one.

For every length, the program checks that the sum of these weights is
exactly \eqref{eq:multinomial}. At each representative it first queries
\(L_2\). Only a negative level-two result triggers a level-three
query, since \(L_2\subseteq L_3\) is proved independently of the
program. Counts of negative results are multiplied by the full orbit
weight.

\label{prop:frontier}
The supplied exhaustive runs return
\[

Subject to the correctness of the implementation and its recorded

\(24\).
\end{proposition}

The enumeration accounts for every balanced word at the possible
lengths \(0,3,6,\ldots,21\), by the orbit-weight argument and the

three, with level-two positives transferred by monotonicity.
By \cref{thm:recurrence}, the implemented recurrence is the exact
mathematical decision procedure. Every balanced length is a multiple
of three, so the next possible length is \(24\). The remaining trust
assumption is the faithful implementation and execution of that
procedure, not a probabilistic inference from a sample.
\end{proof}

The structural tests verify the published length-\(15\) witness, the

These checks serve different purposes. Forward saturation tests the
recurrence against the original grammar on small cases. The Python
and C++ agreement tests two implementations and two symmetry

theorems. Nor are stored positive certificates supplied for every
orbit in the length-\(21\) run; the finite frontier is independently
reproducible computation, not a compact formal certificate of all
its decisions.

\section{What the attempt says about the open problem}
\label{sec:remaining}

\subsection{Why the natural obstruction strategy stops at level three}
The proof of \cref{thm:cone} works because a two-component derivation,
once cut, has no unused component slot. A positive binary excursion
prevents nontrivial extraction, and endpoint deletion eventually
exposes only two letters. The argument would fail if another useful
cut could be inserted before that stall. At level three, that extra
cut is available, and \cref{thm:binary} does more than suggest a
rescue: it explicitly derives the whole two-ended binary family.

Consequently, simply making the published obstruction longer, using
unequal run lengths, or substituting a more complicated balanced
binary interior cannot solve \cref{prob:main} while retaining the
pattern \(\aa^*\{\bb,\cc\}^*\aa^*\). A successful obstruction must
leave this entire positive family, up to alphabet permutation.

The endpoint argument is nevertheless useful. It demonstrates how to
prove a \emph{negative} grammar-membership statement without relying
on the parser: identify all initial meaningful cuts, establish an
invariant excluding binary extraction, and show that every legal
endpoint trajectory stalls while keeping all component slots
occupied. A level-three version would need to control two initial
cuts and also all ways that one of three components can vanish.
The present proof does not supply that stronger invariant.

\subsection{Valid and invalid reductions in a future search}
The concatenation closure in \cref{prop:basic} gives a sound reduction:
a shortest word outside \(L_r\), if there is one, has no nonempty
proper balanced prefix. Otherwise its balanced prefix and suffix are
shorter and hence both in \(L_r\), forcing their concatenation into
\(L_r\).

The superficially stronger reduction to words having \emph{no proper
balanced factor anywhere} has not been justified. An internal factor
can be extracted only when its two induced tuple children are
accepted; acceptance of the flattened outside word is insufficient.
The cut trap in \cref{ex:cuttrap} shows exactly why. Thus searching
only factor-balanced-primitive candidates is a heuristic restriction,
not an exhaustive reduction of the open problem.

The same warning applies to cyclic rotation: \cref{cor:rotation}
shows that even level two does not respect it. Alphabet renaming and
full reversal are safe symmetries; arbitrary cyclic rotation is not
used in the exhaustive enumeration.

\subsection{A precise next mathematical target}
One route would be to find a family of three-component tuple states
with a finite symbolic description, closed under every possible
backward peeling and admissible extraction, whose states cannot
reach the empty tuple. The family must also account for all
refinements of a one-component candidate. This is stronger than
finding a few difficult strings or observing a long running time.

A complementary route toward equality would be an induction asserting
membership of a suitably rich class of \emph{tuples}, not merely all
balanced words. The theorem \((\aa^p,v,\aa^u)\in\A_3\) is an example
of an induction hypothesis strong enough to survive concatenation
of binary interiors. Extending it to arbitrary distributions of all
three letters would require additional structure; arbitrary cut
placements cannot be included because they are already false at
length six.

The finite frontier offers no statistical reason to prefer equality
or inequality. Many grammar obstructions require a structured word
far longer than every short test case. What it supplies is a checked
starting point and a lower bound on the length of a prospective
witness, not a resolution by numerical evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
The selected open problem has led to three kinds of concrete output.
First, the grammar admits an exact terminating backward recurrence
whose state records the actual component boundaries. Second, the
report gives complete analytic proofs of a broad three-component
positive family and a multi-parameter two-component negative family,
with exact-rank, counting, and rotation consequences. Third, two
implementations and several independent validation mechanisms
support an exhaustive frontier through length \(21\).

The result is a partial research contribution and a reproducible
experimental platform. It is \emph{not} a solution of
\(L_3\stackrel{?}{=}\Mix\), not a resolution of the full
Kanazawa--Salvati conjecture, and not a formal verification in a
proof assistant. The positive family identifies a substantial region
where a counterexample cannot lie; the obstruction proof identifies
the kind of boundary-sensitive invariant that a stronger negative
argument would need.

\section{Reference pseudocode}
\label{app:pseudocode}
The pseudocode below omits symmetry canonicalization and caching,
which preserve the answer but are not needed for correctness.
\begin{lstlisting}
MEMBER(r, T):
    T := delete empty components of T
    if T is empty: return true
    if number_of_components(T) > r: return false
    if concatenation(T) is not balanced: return false

    for each choice of one physical endpoint a, one b, one c:
        U := T with these three occurrences deleted
        if MEMBER(r, U): return true

    w := concatenation(T)
    for each nonempty proper balanced interval [p,q) of w:
        B := nonempty intersections with the components of T
        A := nonempty outside pieces, preserving boundaries
             and keeping an exposed prefix and suffix separate
        if components(A) <= r and components(B) <= r:
            if MEMBER(r, B) and MEMBER(r, A): return true

    if components(T) < r:
        for each split of one component into nonempty x,y:
            U := T with that component replaced by x,y
            if MEMBER(r, U): return true
    return false
\end{lstlisting}

For interval tests, the programs use prefix vectors
\[
 d_j=\bigl(\len{w[0:j]}_{\aa}-\len{w[0:j]}_{\cc},\
           \len{w[0:j]}_{\bb}-\len{w[0:j]}_{\cc}\bigr).
\]
The factor \(w[p:q]\) is balanced exactly when \(d_p=d_q\).
Restricting candidate lengths to multiples of three is therefore a
safe preliminary filter, not an approximation.

Python tools use only the standard library. Commands below are run
from the archive's top-level directory.

\paragraph{Individual membership queries.}
\begin{lstlisting}
python src/mix_parser.py 2 aaabbbcbbccccaa
python src/mix_parser.py 3 aaabbbcbbccccaa
python src/mix_parser.py 3 a accb b
\end{lstlisting}
The expected Boolean answers are respectively false, true, and false.
Each command-line word after the arity is one component, so the last
command is deliberately not a query about a single concatenated word.

\paragraph{Independent checks and exhaustive runs.}
\begin{lstlisting}
python tests/forward_oracle.py
python tests/test_results.py
python src/exhaust_python.py 6
mkdir -p build
c++ -O3 -std=c++17 src/exhaust.cpp -o build/mix_exhaust
build/mix_exhaust 7
\end{lstlisting}
The final argument is the maximum number of occurrences of each letter,
not the maximum total word length. Thus \word{7} means length \(21\).
The C++ driver emits JSON to standard output and progress to standard
error. A run interrupted before its final closing JSON braces is not
a completed result, even though its header describes the intended
complete computation. Increasing the bound can increase time and
memory substantially; the programs make no promise of a uniform
resource bound in an arbitrary environment.

\paragraph{Positive certificates.}
\begin{lstlisting}
python src/check_certificate.py certificates/separation_m1.json
python src/family_certificate.py bbbcbbcccc 3 > new_certificate.json
python src/check_certificate.py new_certificate.json
\end{lstlisting}
The archive includes certificates for \(m=1,2,5\) in
\eqref{eq:scaled}. The checker rejects malformed rule parameters or
claimed tuples that differ from their computed conclusions.

\paragraph{PDF.}
Run \word{make pdf}, or run \word{pdflatex mix\_grammar\_report.tex}
twice. The bibliography is embedded in the source; a separate BibTeX
file is provided for reuse, not required for compilation.

\paragraph{Integrity and verification scope.}
No checksum manifest is bundled, because re-running the checks or
rebuilding the PDF changes file hashes. Hashes would identify the
artifacts; they do not prove the code correct. There are no bundled
font files, third-party article PDFs, machine-specific executables,
or claims of external peer review. The mathematical arguments and
programs were produced for this research request; independent human
review is appropriate before using any claimed extension as a
published result.
