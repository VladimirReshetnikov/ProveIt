1. REPOSITORY INSPIRATION

The project began with the user-supplied OpenAI mathematics repository:
https://github.com/openai/math

The inspected family 271 manuscript is "The first lattice correction to Bloch's
law", dated 2026-10-05. Its repository directory is:
preprints/The-first-lattice-correction-to-Blochs-law-October-5-2026/

The methodological inspiration is its treatment of a lattice-dispersion term
inside a heat-kernel integral before extracting a correction. The present work
similarly retains the exact Gaussian spectral log-MGF kernel before using the
available moments. It does not assume any interacting-spin or thermodynamic-limit
theorem from that repository. Its finite-dimensional probability statements are
proved independently. The repository was inspected as a source; no external
repository was modified or submitted to during this work.

2. ESTABLISHED CONCENTRATION RESULTS AND THE CORRECT BASELINE

For Q = g^T A g - tr(A), L = ||A||_op, and v = tr(A^2), the norm-only Gamma
rate v/(2 L^2) [tL/v - log(1+tL/v)] is established. Its coefficient in the
prefactor-one quadratic/linear form is (1-log(2))/2 = 0.153426409720....
Neither that rate nor that coefficient is claimed as a new result here.

Daniel Dadush, Cristobal Guzman, and Neil Olver, "Fast, deterministic and sparse
dimensionality reduction", SODA 2018, pages 1330-1344, give this functional form
and coefficient for diagonal-free subgaussian quadratic forms; see Theorem 3.2
and Section 6. Their Gaussian comparison also supplies the coordinatewise MGF
replacement used for the manuscript's diagonal-free subgaussian extension.
Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611975031.87
Institutional full text: https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/27624/27624.pdf

Robert E. Gallagher, Louis J. M. Aslett, David Steinsaltz, and Ryan R. Christ,
"Improved concentration bounds for Gaussian quadratic forms", arXiv:1911.05720,
give a trace-based Chernoff framework whose central-Gaussian specialization
contains the same optimizable norm-only Gamma envelope for general symmetric
matrices. Their discussion of positive/negative spectral asymmetry is relevant
to the motivation for retaining a signed third moment.
Official record: https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05720
Full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05720

Beatrice Laurent and Pascal Massart, "Adaptive estimation of a quadratic
functional by model selection", Annals of Statistics 28 (2000), 1302-1338,
provide the classical baseline initially stated for nonnegative weights. The
manuscript acknowledges that setting. Its displayed bound for general symmetric
Gaussian forms is also implied by the norm-only envelope used here.
Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1015957395

An initial research candidate was the unrestricted coefficient above. Inspection
of the older sources ruled out a novelty claim for it. The final comparisons
therefore use that established coefficient, rather than a weaker coefficient
advertised for a particular recent inequality family.

3. CLOSE OVERLAP AND CLASSICAL INGREDIENTS

Zhekai Pang, "A Gamma envelope and sharp moment inequalities for Gaussian
quadratic forms", arXiv:2609.05914v1, dated 2026-09-05, constructs a centered
Gamma difference matching the first three centered moments under Frobenius
normalization. Theorem 2.1 compares a class of functions with convex second
derivative and yields absolute-moment bounds. Its endpoint-chord method and
third-moment-matched Gamma difference overlap with ingredients of this work.
The manuscript credits this overlap. Pang's stated optimization problem and
normalization differ from the operator-norm-conditioned tail coefficient here.
Official version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05914v1
Full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05914v1

Kamyar Moshksar, "Refining concentration bounds for Gaussian quadratic chaos
with applications", arXiv:2412.03774v5, dated 2026-09-13, supplies neighboring
norm and Schatten-moment refinements. The reviewed version did not provide the
complete signed cubic-moment coefficient frontier stated in this manuscript.
Some of its reported coefficients are smaller than the older Dadush-Guzman-
Olver coefficient; those comparisons do not establish a global best baseline.
Official version: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03774v5

Zhaojun Bai and Gene H. Golub, "Computation of large-scale quadratic forms and
transfer functions using the theory of moments, quadrature and Pade
approximation", in Modern Methods in Scientific Computing and Applications
(2002), provide classical background on moment-based spectral quadrature.
Gauss-Radau extremality and Hermite interpolation are established methods.
The four-moment section applies them to the signed, variance-weighted Gaussian
log-MGF kernel; it does not claim to invent quadrature or general moment
extremality.
Author-hosted text: https://web.cs.ucdavis.edu/~bai/publications/baigolub02.pdf

Beta/binomial identities and Stirling expansions used in the exact harmonic
example are classical. The NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions is
the cited reference for Gamma asymptotics and controlled remainders.
Official references: https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11
                     https://dlmf.nist.gov/8.11

Application context also includes Alice Cortinovis and Daniel Kressner,
"On randomized trace estimates for indefinite matrices with an application to
determinants", and Raphael A. Meyer, Cameron Musco, Christopher Musco, and
David P. Woodruff, "Hutch++: Optimal stochastic trace estimation". These sources
locate the trace-estimation application; no new matrix-query complexity exponent
or dominance over more sophisticated estimators is asserted.
Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-021-09525-9
Public full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8553228/

4. PRECISE SCOPE OF THE PROPOSED CONTRIBUTION

The principal proposed-original result is the complete optimal coefficient at
fixed cubic spectral balance s = tr(A^3)/(L v):

    c(s) = min(1/4, J_s(1)) for -1 < s <= 1, and c(-1) = 1/4,

where J_s is the explicitly optimized signed-Gamma rate defined in the paper.
The proof identifies the transition at s = -0.705589538603..., the value
c(0) = 0.188714038110..., and sharpness over actual finite-dimensional matrices
at every exact real s. It also gives optimality for the class with ratio at
most s. The theorem strengthens the exponent within a specified functional
form; it does not change the quadratic/linear asymptotic orders.

The four-moment contribution is an explicit signed Radau envelope, optimizer,
equality description, and nonnegative remainder. Its extremality is over a
specified probability-measure moment relaxation. This is not a blanket claim
of finite-matrix optimality for every fixed pair of third/fourth moment ratios.
An actual matrix family attains the envelope and yields exact rational tail
probabilities and a proved first correction to their asymptotic equivalent.

Targeted searches included the numerical fingerprints of the proposed constants
and combinations of "signed cubic moment", "skewness-dependent concentration",
"Gaussian quadratic form", and "Gauss-Radau moment envelope". No matching
statement of the complete c(s) frontier and transition was located in the
reviewed sources. This is a bounded literature assessment, not a guarantee of
priority or a claim to have resolved a previously named major conjecture.
Broader specialist literature review remains appropriate before publication.

5. WHAT HAS AND HAS NOT BEEN VERIFIED

Mathematical proof: the TeX/PDF contains the exact identities, inequalities,
moment extremality arguments, equality cases, coefficient minimization, and
finite-matrix sharpness construction. Proofs, rather than numerical experiments,
are the justification for their all-dimensions and all-parameter statements.
The diagonal-free subgaussian extension transfers Chernoff bounds derived from
the Gaussian MGF envelopes; it does not transfer Gaussian exact CDF identities
or their finite probability sandwiches.

Computational checks: accompanying scripts reproduce floating-point comparisons
of envelopes and optimized rates, symbolic identities, boundary regressions,
figures, and exact integer/rational probability certificates. The verification
JSON records check counts and discrepancies; the code records comparison
tolerances. Exact arithmetic certifies
the finite cases it computes. Symbolic and floating-point checks are useful
independent checks on formulas, not proofs covering untested parameter ranges.
The floating-point implementation is not an outward-rounded interval-arithmetic
certificate; its documented input tolerances and numerical safeguards matter.

Review status: separate reasoning and code-audit passes during the AI-assisted
preparation cross-checked the principal arguments. This is internal checking,
not external mathematical peer review. The results have not been formally
verified in Lean or another proof assistant. The manuscript does not rely on
unproved physics conjectures, and its harmonic applications concern normalizable
finite-dimensional Gibbs systems.
