# Attribution and verification boundary

The original 219-state construction is the public upstream ProveIt packet at
commit `26d5de6d21fa354d50e0b84fe851c289925b8d72`. Its unchanged ZIP is included
under `vendor/`; its attribution, licenses, proof sources, and recursively
bundled predecessor constructions remain intact. The original K219 machine is
not a discovery of this alternative-construction packet.

The new contribution is the three alternative 219-state source/table pairs,
source transport proofs, exact startup and complete unbounded runtime checks,
independent audits, deterministic reconstruction/export tools, and report.
The work was developed with ChatGPT-assisted research and independent review
for Vladimir Reshetnikov's ProveIt project. New material uses the inherited
MIT No Attribution terms in `LICENSE`. Adapted source, canonicalization and
runtime wrappers derive from the local independent K219 zero-terminal audit;
the underlying symbolic runtime and helper are unchanged upstream files and
are extracted from the recursively included predecessor archive.

The certificates are ordinary executable mathematical proofs/checks, not a
new Rocq or Lean theorem for these exact machine bytes. The inheritance claim
is the conditional equivalence supplied by the pinned construction: in ZFC,
blank nonhalting is equivalent to Con(ZFC). No consistency assumption is
proved, no unconditional nonhalting is asserted, and no minimality or
218-state result is claimed.

Historical development receipts inside the untouched upstream ZIP can refer
to earlier file hashes. In particular, its `verification/calibration_audit.json`
refers to a development parent archive and source builder, not the final
bundled bytes. They are historical evidence only. Current-byte inheritance is
bound through the outer upstream ZIP hash, operative portable manifests and
the recursively replayed construction/calibration chain, not by relabeling
those historical receipts as fresh certificates.
