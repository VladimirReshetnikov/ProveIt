## Exact theory and checked proof obligations

The theory is classical nonempty-domain equality-free first-order logic with one binary relation, primitive logical falsity, and the unrestricted parameterized **infinite** subset-seeded PSC schema. Local output bounds are `y⊆b`; closure has `(x⊆a or x∈C)`; support is the global-output condition `y∈C -> exists x rho(x,y)`, with no requirement that the support input be active. Auxiliary variables are fresh. Arbitrary finite formulas and finite parameter tuples are allowed; “positive” does not restrict formula polarity (`sections/theory.tex:35–78`). No Extensionality, irreflexivity, first-argument profile congruence, equality axiom, or external all-relations axiom is hidden in this statement.

1. **Exact classes before Separation.** Constant-output PSC gives the exact extension of any subset-bounded equality-free predicate. Incoming-profile `D(y,a)` implies `y⊆a`, so saturated singletons and fragments `{z:z∈x and D(z,a)}` are available before raw Separation. The fragment is a subset of the particular raw `x`; it need not include every copy of its profile (`theory.tex:121–154`). This avoids the invalid assumption that a full D-singleton is a subset of an arbitrary nonsaturated object.
2. **Saturated Pairing.** The two-cycle profile relation has at most one output profile at each input, including the coinciding-profile case. Starting at `a`, then closing at actual member `b`, includes both whole profile classes; support excludes everything else (`theory.tex:156–180`). This is pairing modulo D, not literal raw two-element Pairing.
3. **Monochromatic decoder.** `Mono(x,y)` explicitly requires a nonempty input. Its endpoints have a single D-profile, hence one endpoint bounds the entire fiber; otherwise all outputs are subsets of `x`. The second arm is gated by nonexistence of any monochromatic endpoint (`theory.tex:184–216`). For an active nonmonochromatic `x` and `y∈x`, the pre-existing fragment `F(x,y)` is nonempty and a subset of `x`, so decoder closure reaches it and then `y` in two steps. No outgoing-profile congruence is used (`theory.tex:218–253`). This gives `A⊆C` and transitivity, without asserting `A∈C`.
4. **Raw Separation/Union.** A transitive cover of `A` subset-bounds every prospective separated member and every prospective union member. Constant exact PSC then gives the full equality-free Separation schema and exact raw Union (`theory.tex:257–276`). No Collection/Choice is used to select a family of bounds.
5. **Exact locally bounded images.** The Russell root `r={t∈A:t∉t}` is outside `A`. The relation's root arm explicitly contains `x∉A`, which is needed because a D-copy of `r` may lie in `A`. Thus its root and source arms are disjoint, root outputs are bounded by raw `union A`, and the supplied source bounds handle the other arm. Initial closure includes `A`; support limits the hull to `A` plus the desired image; raw Separation extracts the exact image (`theory.tex:278–326`). This is stronger than literal functional Replacement: it collects all outputs of each locally subset-bounded fiber, including fibers unique only modulo D.
6. **CP consequence.** Decoder and exact-power-output fibers can be combined after binary raw Union exists; one exact power output bounds all coextensional power outputs. The initial power object belongs to the hull and contains the seed, putting the seed itself in the transitive hull. This proves the displayed CP container sentence but does **not** derive unrestricted GIC or calibrate the separate emitted C/G theory (`theory.tex:328–353`).
7. **Hereditary saturation.** `H(a)` is saturation plus a transitive cover of its members whose members are saturated. D-invariance and heredity follow directly from its definition. The source08 purity cut `W={u∈V:H(u)}` proves upward H-closure without collecting covers, requiring least covers, or using Replacement (`theory.tex:365–405`). There is no circular appeal to the upward lemma when proving heredity.
8. **Interpreted ZF0.** Equality is D, membership is raw membership, and quantifiers are restricted to H. Congruence holds on H assignments because the membership target is saturated; it is not claimed for arbitrary raw formulas. Every raw member of an H object is H, giving interpreted Extensionality and making interpreted inclusion absolute. The stated Empty, Pair, Union, Power, Separation and Replacement constructions use only fixed formula patterns plus the inserted translated matrices (`theory.tex:409–476`). Interpreted Replacement requires only uniqueness modulo D, precisely enough for the exact-image theorem.
9. **Direct ordinary Infinity.** At H inputs, the union of self-profile and saturated-successor-profile outputs is bounded by one successor. Support guarantees every hull member is H even for an inactive support witness; closure on each hull member supplies all its D-copies, making the hull saturated. A raw empty seed enters through the self-profile arm. Upward H-closure then supplies an interpreted ordinary inductive set (`theory.tex:478–508`). This is a useful direct proof and does not depend on a weak-Infinity equivalence.
10. **Reverse and consistency bridge.** In ZF without Foundation/Choice, each fiber is separated in one local power set, and Extensionality makes that exact fiber unique independently of the local bound. Replacement collects the unique fibers; recursion along omega and the union of positive stages give precisely PSC closure and unrestricted support, even when stages are not increasing (`theory.tex:510–547`). The ZFC metatheoretic completeness/quotient argument uses a set model H/D, then the usual internal WF and L interpretations; it assumes no externally well-founded or transitive model (`theory.tex:549–605`). This is ordinary mathematical ZFC-provable equiconsistency, not an already serialized proof-code translator or Lean theorem.

The urelement example correctly demonstrates that raw incoming-profile equality need not be a global membership congruence even in PSC models (`theory.tex:607–625`). The finite decoder failure examples are explicitly local-lemma witnesses, not finite models of the whole PSC schema (`audit_monochromatic_decoder.py:1–8,181–193`).

## Tableau/reference search audit

The implemented public acceptance boundary matches the mathematical one. Named matrices are parsed in equality-free syntax, inserted into a capture-avoiding fresh PSC wrapper, universally parameter-closed, and converted to closed de Bruijn formulas (`qpsc_syntax.py:34–42,66–79,105–125`; `environment_tableau.py:57–87`). The wrapper chooses variables above *every* matrix label, so no matrix binder or parameter can collide with an auxiliary variable. The simultaneous substitution removes a bound variable from the local map and renames a potentially captured binder using a globally fresh label. Shadowing and free input/output occurrences are handled correctly.

`check_certificate` accepts only `matrices`, `proof`, and optional demonstration `theorem`; it generates each nonlogical root internally and adds a negative target. It rejects equality, schema holes, unknown fields, and open conclusions (`environment_tableau.py:181–197`). The refutation enumerator never generates a theorem field, and acceptance additionally checks the Boolean `refutation` result (`refutation_search.py:102–123`). Thus a valid theorem demonstration cannot be mistaken for a contradiction.

All six proof productions match ordinary signed tableaux:

- T implication forks into F antecedent / T consequent; F implication appends both T antecedent and F consequent (`environment_tableau.py:147–161`). Both fork children must close.
- T universal allows **any** natural parameter, retaining the original formula for repeated use. F universal enforces its own `max(used)+1`, and all root-environment and subsequently introduced names are tracked (`:110–118,162–175`). New arbitrary T-universal parameters can be assigned arbitrary domain elements in the soundness invariant; nonempty semantics supplies one. Fresh F-universal names preserve previous evaluations.
- Atom closure compares evaluated ordered environment pairs, not literal atom indices. Only opposite signs close. Positive bottom alone closes (`:96–100,134–145`). Negative bottom does not close.

The ordinary completeness proof is sound: fair saturation supplies each F-universal witness and instantiates every T-universal at every eventual parameter. Parameter0 provides a nonempty canonical domain. The open-branch atomic relation and formula induction give the countermodel. With no functions or equality, no additional term or congruence productions are missing. Standard finite-tableau completeness and finite fresh-name renaming then give a certificate accepted with the checker's deterministic fresh choices (`sections/implementation.tex:120–149`). This is **not** a claim of formal correctness of the Python interpreter.

The list/matrix/proof/certificate encodings cover every finite primitive certificate. Recursive matrix components, proof children, and list tails strictly decrease; pairing/unpairing is standard Cantor coding (`refutation_search.py:20–112`; `implementation.tex:151–183`). Enumerating all natural certificate codes sequentially is therefore mathematically fair, not a bounded-depth heuristic. Optional named truth is unnecessary for coverage because it can be expanded as bottom→bottom. Side-condition failures reject only the candidate. Resource exhaustion, recursion/interpreter errors and the diagnostic `--limit` stop are not treated as accepted refutations (`refutation_search.py:115–149`; README:53–65).
