# Attribution and scope of this packet

This packet was prepared for Vladimir Reshetnikov and the ProveIt repository.
Its new contribution is the rho6 parameter-permuted 216-state variant, the
two-row construction and exact source delta, fresh source/startup/runtime
verification, and the accompanying formalism and encoding research.

The physical saving to 216 states is inherited from the public partial-selector
candidate accepted at ProveIt commit
`e6083665c6826e3a8aef969a8a4a8bc9031bd32b`. Its original submitted archive arrived
in commit `6ec69081ca2a4abc03c35b573e22eb0d939a6324`. That upstream construction
became public during this investigation. This packet does not claim discovery
or authorship of its partial-selector source, contextual allocation repair,
or 183-to-164 state fold.

The parameter permutation was first constructed and fully checked on the
accepted 217-state parent, then composed with that newly public 216-state
construction. The resulting exact table is different from both parents.

The native-calculus/ZFC calibration, dominated-rho8 recovery certificate,
proof compiler, earlier state folds, and symbolic runtime are inherited.
`code/runtime/symbolic_runtime.py`, `code/runtime/independent_runtime.py`, and
`code/runtime/legacy_mapping.json` preserve the pinned inherited bytes.
The forward dominated-row checker and certificate are also inherited.
The candidate driver, local construction/audit, and research checks accompany
the new work; they are not proof-assistant kernel certificates.

The three-valued U=A obstruction is an independently checked smaller model
for a family already excluded by the repository's fourth-parameter research.
The protected-credit argument extends the inherited Cantor-diagonal seed
argument. The pairing-change theorem concerns all raw natural codes and
does not by itself establish a smaller physical machine.

Preserve the original attribution and notices under
`inputs/upstream216/provenance/`, `inputs/upstream216/NOTICE.md`, and `licenses/`.
Those notices credit the ProveIt project and Vladimir Reshetnikov, Mira,
Elliot Glazer's compact-theory development, Jeff's Rocq reference, and
Andrew James Wade's compiler ancestry. Existing third-party license terms
retain their original scope. This packet does not relicense inherited work.

The public parents are pinned by commit and SHA-256. The delivered evidence
records the newly executed checks separately from inherited mathematical
premises and finite discovery experiments.
