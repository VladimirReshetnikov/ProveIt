\begin{abstract}
We seek a small blank-tape Turing machine $M$ satisfying
\[\PA\vdash\Halt(M)\leftrightarrow\neg\Con(\ZFC).\]
We continue the three reports in the supplied \texttt{zfc-consistency.zip}
and analyze the closure principle in MathOverflow question 380000.
The main addition is a family of \emph{seeded bounded-image closure}
axiomatizations. Each of two transparent equality-bearing theories has
just Extensionality and one parameterized schema, and is deductively
equivalent to ZF with Foundation and Choice omitted. Infinity,
Separation, Power Set, Pairing, Union, and functional Replacement are
all derived rather than separately recognized by the proof-search engine.
A subset-seeded variant admits a direct equality-free calibration using
one congruence sentence, without the raw-bisimulation construction used
by the earlier aggressive container--collection proposal.
We give explicit mathematical proofs, explain the uniform syntactic
reductions required for PA, and compare the theories in a declared
formula-tree metric. The point-seeded theory uses 69 template nodes;
the directly calibrated equality-free variant uses 83, versus 80 for
RCC in the same metric. These are not Turing-machine state counts.
Executable finite checks and a precise certification roadmap accompany
the article. No smaller transition table, global minimality theorem,
or serialized PA proof object is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Assessment and the substantive change from the supplied research}

The right optimization target is not the number of displayed axioms.
It is the size of the complete machine implementing a sound and complete
finite-certificate search, together with a proof in PA that its halting
condition is exactly ZFC inconsistency. A complicated interpretation may
be harmless if it stays in the correctness proof; a short axiom may be
expensive if it requires elaborate parsing or variable manipulation.

The supplied dossier develops three important controls: the
subset-bounded image theory $\Ext+\mathsf{Infinity}+\SBI$;
nonextensional relational container--collection, abbreviated RCC; and
finite predicative class theories. It also emphasizes the distinction
between refutation and designated-target theoremhood, and the need to
calibrate the actual arithmetic endpoint \cite{prior1,prior2,prior3}.
We retain these distinctions rather than reclassifying an auxiliary
positive theory as ZFC-equiconsistent.

The MathOverflow proposal adds a useful ingredient: close a set under a
definable relation whose individual fibers are bounded \cite{mo}.
We combine that ingredient with the earlier exact-image idea. The new
schema requires both closure and a weak global-image condition. That
condition is precisely what makes constant relations produce \emph{exact}
sets and thereby absorbs Separation as well as Power Set.

The recommended experimental comparison is:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{.19\linewidth}p{.34\linewidth}p{.37\linewidth}}
\toprule
Candidate & Reason to implement it & Main remaining cost or risk\\
\midrule
$\Tpt$ & Extensionality plus one closure schema; direct elementary calibration & Logical equality remains on the search side.\\
$\QTsub$ & Equality-free; only one congruence sentence and one schema & Congruence has a measurable syntax cost.\\
RCC & Earlier aggressive equality-free benchmark & More elaborate nonextensional interpretation.\\
Finite predicative classes & No set-theoretic schema recognizer after finite compilation & Larger fixed vocabulary/axiom data or Skolem-term machinery.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The new contribution is a mathematical reduction and a better-controlled
candidate family, not an assertion that any displayed candidate is the
smallest possible machine. Novelty here means an extension of the supplied
dossier; no historical priority claim is made.

\section{The arithmetic contract and the meaning of calibration}
\label{sec:contract}

Fix effective proof systems and ordinary arithmetizations. Set
\[
 \Inc(T):=\exists p\,\Prf_T(p,\ulcorner\bot\urcorner),
 \qquad \Con(T):=\neg\Inc(T).
\]
An axiom schema is represented by its formula parameter, fresh variables,
and any finite syntactic side conditions. A certificate may carry the
schema parameter, so the checker need not solve a reverse pattern-matching
problem on an arbitrary purported axiom.

For a fixed literal Turing-machine table $M$, let $\Halt(M)$ be the
$\Sigma^0_1$ sentence asserting a finite halting computation from its
specified blank-tape initial configuration. The required statement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 \PA\vdash \Halt(M)\leftrightarrow\Inc(\ZFC).
\end{equation}
It is not enough to show that both sides are false under a consistency
assumption, or that some stronger background system verifies an informal
analogue of this equivalence.

\begin{proposition}[Compositional refutation contract]\label{prop:contract}
Suppose $f$ and $g$ are primitive-recursive proof transformers and PA proves
\begin{align*}
 \Prf_T(p,\bot)&\longrightarrow\Prf_{\ZFC}(f(p),\bot),\\
 \Prf_{\ZFC}(p,\bot)&\longrightarrow\Prf_T(g(p),\bot).
\end{align*}
Suppose also that PA proves $\Halt(M)\leftrightarrow\Inc(T)$.
Then \eqref{eq:target} follows in PA.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Existentially quantify the two proof implications to obtain
$\Inc(T)\leftrightarrow\Inc(\ZFC)$, and compose the equivalences.
No semantic soundness assertion for ZFC is used.
\end{proof}

A theory will be called \emph{PA-equiconsistent} with another when their
consistency sentences are provably equivalent in PA; we write $T\eqpa S$.
This is not the same as deductive equivalence $T\eqd S$ in a fixed language.
The new equality-bearing theories satisfy the stronger deductive
comparison with an explicitly specified foundation-free theory.

\begin{definition}[The comparison theory]\label{def:ZF0}
$\ZFz$ consists of Extensionality, Empty Set, Pairing, Union, Power Set,
Infinity, full Separation, and full \emph{functional} Replacement, in
classical first-order membership logic with equality. It has neither
Foundation nor Choice. It does not have unrestricted Collection as an
additional axiom schema.
\end{definition}

The classical effective Foundation and Choice reductions yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:standard}
 \ZFz\eqpa\ZF\eqpa\ZFC.
\end{equation}
The relevant use of this result is syntactic: translate formulas into a
well-founded class and then the constructible class, and expand translated
axiom instances by uniform derivations. Section~\ref{sec:PA} makes this
boundary explicit. Foundation elimination is described in
\cite{avigad}; the constructibility argument and its metatheoretic
formalization issues are discussed in \cite{paulson}. Background on
arithmetization and incompleteness is given in \cite{buss}.

\section{The MathOverflow closure proposal, with the bootstrap made explicit}
\label{sec:MO}

Write $\rho(x,y;\ps)$ with the \emph{input} first: from $x$, the relation
produces $y$. This reverses the display order of the predecessor notation
in the question but not its intended direction. Define
\begin{align}
 \LB(\rho)&:\quad
   \forall x\exists b\forall y\bigl(\rho(x,y)\to y\in b\bigr),\label{eq:LB}\\
 \Cl_\rho(W)&:\quad
   \forall x\forall y\bigl((x\in W\land\rho(x,y))\to y\in W\bigr).
\end{align}
The corrected set-seeded schema in the question is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TCset}
 \TC^{\subseteq}_\rho:\quad
 \LB(\rho)\to\forall A\exists W
       \bigl(A\subseteq W\land\Cl_\rho(W)\bigr).
\end{equation}
A related root-seeded schema is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TCroot}
 \TC^{\in}_\rho:\quad
 \LB(\rho)\to\forall a\exists W
       \bigl(a\in W\land\Cl_\rho(W)\bigr).
\end{equation}
All parameters are universally closed, and witness variables are fresh.
There is no quantification over arbitrary semantic class relations: each
$\rho$ is an ordinary first-order formula.

The original question includes Extensionality, Foundation, Separation,
Power Set, and Choice in addition to \eqref{eq:TCset}. Glazer's answer
establishes the intended equivalence with ZFC and discusses the seed
convention \cite{mo}. For the machine problem, Foundation and Choice can
be omitted. Here is a streamlined derivation that also avoids defining
``natural number'' before Infinity is available.

\begin{theorem}[Calibration of the corrected closure proposal]
\label{thm:MO}
Both
\[
 T_{\mathrm{MO}}=\Ext+\Sep+\mathsf{Power}+\TC^{\subseteq}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 T_{\mathrm{MO}}^{\mathrm{root}}
       =\Ext+\Sep+\mathsf{Power}+\TC^{\in}
\]
are deductively equivalent to $\ZFz$, by uniform axiom-instance proofs.
Consequently both are PA-equiconsistent with ZFC.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First work with the set-seeded presentation. Separation from any object
produces an empty set $e$. For every $a$, the Power Set axiom and
$a\subseteq a$ give $a\in\Pow(a)$; separation in $\Pow(a)$ yields
$\{a\}$. Thus set-seeded closure can also be applied with seed $\{a\}$
to obtain root-seeded closure.

For Pairing, use the constant relation $\rho(x,y)\equiv y=v$ and start
with seed $\{u\}$. The pointwise bound is $\{v\}$. A closure witness
contains $u$ and $v$, and Separation extracts exactly $\{u,v\}$.

For Union, use $\rho(x,y)\equiv y\in x$, whose pointwise bound is $x$
itself. A witness for seed $A$ contains every member of every member of
$A$, so Separation in that witness produces $\bigcup A$.

Pairing and Union now give, for every set $x$, a unique successor
$x^+=x\cup\{x\}$. Apply closure to
$\rho(x,y)\equiv y=x^+$ and seed $\{e\}$. Every fiber has singleton
bound $\{x^+\}$. The resulting $W$ contains $e$ and is closed under
successor, so it is an inductive set. This proves ordinary Infinity
without referring to a preexisting $\omega$.

For a functional Replacement instance on $A$, use
$\rho(x,y)\equiv x\in A\land\varphi(x,y)$ and seed $A$.
Each fiber is empty or a singleton, so it is set-like. Closure bounds all
outputs, and Separation extracts exactly the image of $A$.
This derives all axioms of $\ZFz$.

For the root presentation, apply closure to the empty relation to get a
set containing any prescribed $a$; Separation gives $\{a\}$.
Pairing follows from the same constant relation. Membership closure
rooted at $A$ is transitive and contains $A$, hence bounds $\bigcup A$.
Successor closure rooted at $e$ proves Infinity. To collect a functional
image of $A$, first choose $r\notin A$ by Russell separation and use
\[
 \rho(x,y)\equiv
   (x=r\land y\in A)\lor(x\in A\land\varphi(x,y)).
\]
The cases $x=r$ and $x\in A$ are disjoint, so each fiber is either $A$,
a singleton, or empty. A root witness at $r$ contains all inputs and
outputs; Separation extracts the image.

Conversely, in $\ZFz$ a pointwise membership bound lets Separation form
the exact fiber $d_x=\{y:\rho(x,y)\}$. Extensionality makes $d_x$
unique, independently of the chosen bound. Replacement and Union form
the image of any set under these fibers. Starting from $A$ or $\{a\}$,
iterate this operation along $\omega$ and take the union of the stages.
This supplies both closure schemas. The construction uses unique exact
fibers, not a chosen function of arbitrary bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The seed distinction is real before bootstrapping]
The theory $\Ext+\Sep+\TC^{\subseteq}$ without Power Set has a one-object
model with empty membership. For a nonempty relation the antecedent
$\LB$ fails; for the empty relation the closure conclusion is vacuous.
Thus one cannot replace $A\subseteq W$ by $a\in W$ for free in this weak
base. The Power Set step that yields $\{a\}$ is an actual proof obligation.
\end{remark}

\section{Main construction: seeded bounded-image closure}
\label{sec:main}

The closure proposal by itself still needs a separate Separation schema
and Power Set axiom. We now absorb both. The key is to require that all
members of the output occur somewhere in the defining relation's global
range, without requiring that they come directly from the initial seed.

Define the mixed fiber bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K}
 K(y,b):\quad y\in b\ \lor\ y\subseteq b,
 \qquad
 B_K(\rho):\quad
 \forall x\exists b\forall y\bigl(\rho(x,y)\to K(y,b)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Let $\sigma(x,a)$ be either of the two fixed seed tests
\begin{equation}\label{eq:seeds}
 \sigma_{\mathrm{pt}}(x,a):\ x=a,
 \qquad
 \sigma_{\mathrm{sub}}(x,a):\ x\subseteq a.
\end{equation}
Both satisfy $\sigma(a,a)$ by logic alone. In ordinary set theory their
seed classes are respectively $\{a\}$ and $\Pow(a)$.

\begin{definition}[Seeded bounded-image closure]\label{def:BIC}
For every formula $\rho(x,y;\ps)$, the schema $\BIC_\sigma$ contains the
universal parameter closure of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BIC}
\begin{split}
 B_K(\rho)\ \longrightarrow\ \forall a\exists C\,[{}&
 \forall x\forall y\bigl(
   ((\sigma(x,a)\lor x\in C)\land\rho(x,y))\to y\in C\bigr)\\
 &{}\land\forall y\bigl(y\in C\to\exists x\,\rho(x,y)\bigr)].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The seed variable $a$ and the witness $C$ are fresh for $\rho$.
A relation may nevertheless depend on an intended seed as an ordinary
parameter; instantiate that parameter and $a$ with the same object when
needed. This convention avoids an implicit variable-capture exception.
The last existential input $x$ in \eqref{eq:BIC} is unrestricted.
We are not silently replacing it by a bounded input quantifier.

The two theories are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tptsub}
 \Tpt:=\Ext+\BIC_{\sigma_{\mathrm{pt}}},
 \qquad
 \Tsub:=\Ext+\BIC_{\sigma_{\mathrm{sub}}}.
\end{equation}
Each has \emph{one fixed nonlogical sentence and one infinite schema}.
Neither is a finite axiomatization.

\begin{theorem}[Main deductive calibration]\label{thm:main}
In classical first-order membership logic with equality,
\[
 \Tpt\eqd\ZFz\eqd\Tsub.
\]
The equivalences are given by uniform axiom-instance derivations.
Consequently
\[
 \PA\vdash\Con(\Tpt)\leftrightarrow\Con(\Tsub)
                \leftrightarrow\Con(\ZFC).
\]
\end{theorem}

We prove the forward derivations without assuming any of the set
constructions that the theories are intended to supply.

\subsection{The constant-relation exactness lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Exact bounded classes]\label{lem:constant}
Work in either theory in \eqref{eq:Tptsub}. Suppose $\theta(y;\ps)$ has
some mixed bound:
\[
 \exists b\forall y\bigl(\theta(y)\to K(y,b)\bigr).
\]
Then there is a set $C$ with
$\forall y(y\in C\leftrightarrow\theta(y))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use $\rho(x,y)\equiv\theta(y)$, independent of $x$. The assumed $b$
verifies $B_K(\rho)$ for every input. Choose any object $a$, using the
nonempty-domain convention of first-order logic, and obtain $C$ from
\eqref{eq:BIC}. If $\theta(y)$, take $x=a$. Since $\sigma(a,a)$,
the closure clause gives $y\in C$. Conversely $y\in C$ implies
$\exists x\,\theta(y)$ by the global-image clause, hence $\theta(y)$.
No Separation, Pairing, or infinite set was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Four immediate constructions]\label{cor:basic}
Either theory proves Empty Set, singleton formation, full Separation,
and Power Set.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For Empty Set, take $\theta(y)\equiv\bot$; any object is a bound.
For $\{t\}$, take $\theta(y)\equiv y=t$ and use $b=t$, since
$t\subseteq t$. For separation of $\psi$ from $A$, take
$\theta(y)\equiv y\in A\land\psi(y)$ and $b=A$, using the membership
arm of $K$. For Power Set of $A$, take $\theta(y)\equiv y\subseteq A$
and again $b=A$, this time using the subset arm.
\end{proof}

The two arms of $K$ have different bootstrap jobs. Deleting either arm
requires a new argument; the displayed derivation does not justify such
a deletion. In particular, the earlier subset-only image theorem cannot
be applied here before its separate bootstrap hypotheses are recovered.

\subsection{Pairing from a two-cycle, without a preexisting pair bound}

\begin{lemma}[Two-cycle pairing]\label{lem:pair}
Either theory proves Pairing.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given $u,v$, put
\[
 \rho(x,y)\equiv(x=u\land y=v)\lor(x=v\land y=u).
\]
Every fiber is empty or a singleton. If $u=v$, the two displayed branches
have the same output, so this assertion remains correct. An empty fiber
has any bound; a singleton fiber with output $t$ has bound $b=t$ because
$t\subseteq t$. Thus $B_K(\rho)$ holds without first forming
$u\cup v$ or $\{u,v\}$.

Apply the schema with seed parameter $a=u$. The seed test holds at $x=u$,
so $v\in C$. Closure at $x=v$ then gives $u\in C$. Every member of $C$
belongs to the global range, which is included in $\{u,v\}$ in the
\emph{formula} sense $y=u\lor y=v$. Therefore
\[
 \forall y\,(y\in C\leftrightarrow(y=u\lor y=v)).
\]
This is Pairing, also when $u=v$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Union from membership closure}

\begin{lemma}[A transitive bound for the union]\label{lem:union}
Either theory proves Union.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use $\rho(x,y)\equiv y\in x$. The choice $b=x$ verifies the membership
arm of $B_K(\rho)$. Apply the schema with $a=A$. Because
$\sigma(A,A)$, every $x\in A$ belongs to $C$. Closure says that every
member of every member of $C$ is again in $C$. In particular,
$y\in x\in A$ implies $y\in C$.

Separation, already derived in Corollary~\ref{cor:basic}, forms
\[
 U=\{y\in C:\exists x\,(x\in A\land y\in x)\}.
\]
This is exactly $\bigcup A$. Notice that $A\in C$ was not needed:
$A$ is an allowed \emph{input seed}, not necessarily an output.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Replacement without Collection or Choice}

\begin{lemma}[Partial functional images]\label{lem:replacement}
Either theory proves the exact-image schema for every relation
$\varphi(x,y;\ps)$ which is at most single-valued on $A$.
In particular, it proves ordinary functional Replacement.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Separation form
$r=\{t\in A:t\notin t\}$. Russell's argument proves $r\notin A$:
if $r\in A$, then $r\in r\leftrightarrow r\notin r$.
Use the relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star}
 \rho(x,y)\equiv
 (x=r\land y\in A)\lor(x\in A\land\varphi(x,y)).
\end{equation}
At input $r$, every output is in $A$, so $b=A$ is a mixed bound.
At an input in $A$, the first branch is false and there is at most one
output; a present output $t$ is bounded using $t\subseteq t$.
At every other input the fiber is empty. These are local existence
arguments, not a simultaneous selection of bounds.

Apply the schema with $a=r$. Since $\sigma(r,r)$, all $x\in A$ lie in
$C$. The closure clause then puts every $\varphi$-output of such an $x$
in $C$. Separation in $C$ gives the exact image
\[
 \{y\in C:\exists x\in A\,\varphi(x,y)\}.
\]
Nothing in this proof invokes unrestricted Collection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Infinity from successor closure}

\begin{lemma}[Infinity with no natural-number predicate]\label{lem:infinity}
Either theory proves the ordinary axiom of Infinity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $e$ be the empty set. Pairing and Union supply a unique set
$x^+=x\cup\{x\}$ for each $x$. Use
\[
 \rho(x,y)\equiv\Succ(x,y),\qquad
 \Succ(x,y):\ \forall z\bigl(z\in y\leftrightarrow(z\in x\lor z=x)\bigr).
\]
Each fiber consists of its unique successor, and $b=x^+$ is a subset
bound for that output. Apply the schema with $a=e$.
Then $e^+\in C$ and $x\in C$ implies $x^+\in C$.
Using Pairing and Union, form $I=C\cup\{e\}$. It contains $e$, and every
member has its successor in $I$: for $e$ this is the initial output,
and for a member of $C$ it is the closure clause. Thus $I$ is inductive.
\end{proof}

All axioms of $\ZFz$ have now been obtained. The argument never assumes
Foundation to distinguish an object from one of its members; the only
fresh root needed in the Replacement proof is supplied by Russell
separation.

\subsection{The reverse direction: collect exact fibers, not arbitrary bounds}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform construction in $\ZFz$]\label{lem:reverse}
$\ZFz$ proves every instance of both seeded bounded-image closure schemas.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\rho$ and assume $B_K(\rho)$. For a fixed input $x$, take a bound
$b$. In $\ZFz$ the set
\[
 b\cup\Pow(b)
\]
exists. Separation in this set forms
\[
 d_x=\{y\in b\cup\Pow(b):\rho(x,y)\}.
\]
Because $b$ bounds every output, this is the exact fiber
$\{y:\rho(x,y)\}$. Extensionality shows that it is independent of the
chosen bound. Consequently ``$d$ is the exact fiber at $x$'' is a
definable, everywhere functional relation.

For any set $D$, Replacement forms the family of its exact fibers and
Union forms
\[
 F(D)=\bigcup\{d_x:x\in D\}.
\]
Fix $a$. Let $D_0=\{a\}$ for the point seed and $D_0=\Pow(a)$ for the
subset seed. Define $D_{n+1}=F(D_n)$ by recursion on $\omega$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positiveorbit}
 C=\bigcup_{n<\omega}D_{n+1}.
\end{equation}
The seeds themselves need not be outputs, which is why the union starts
at $D_1$ rather than $D_0$.

If $\sigma(x,a)$, then $x\in D_0$ and all its outputs lie in $D_1$.
If $x\in C$, it lies in some $D_{n+1}$ and all its outputs lie in
$D_{n+2}$. This proves the closure clause. Every $y\in C$ belongs to a
positive stage and therefore is the output of some input, proving the
global-image clause. Only functional Replacement, Separation, and
ordinary $\omega$-recursion have been used. There is no choice function
$x\mapsto b_x$ in the construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Completion of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The preceding lemmas derive every axiom in both directions. Their
schema-dependent parts are fixed proof templates with the matrix as a
syntactic parameter. Capture-avoiding substitution and concatenation of
these templates give primitive-recursive proof transformations, verified
by induction on syntax and proof records in PA. Compose with
\eqref{eq:standard} to obtain the stated consistency equivalences.
Section~\ref{sec:PA} distinguishes this metamathematical argument from an
exported formal PA certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A reusable seed criterion]\label{cor:seed}
The same proof works for any fixed seed formula $\sigma(x,a)$ such that
$\forall a\,\sigma(a,a)$ has a pure logical proof and $\ZFz$ has a fixed
proof that $\{x:\sigma(x,a)\}$ is a set for every $a$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The forward bootstrap uses only $\sigma(a,a)$. The reverse construction
uses only the set of seeds as $D_0$. Both hypotheses are effective when
the displayed logical and set-theoretic proofs are fixed.
\end{proof}

The criterion explains the point/subset tradeoff. Point seeding has the
shortest displayed wrapper but uses equality. Subset seeding costs a few
membership-logic nodes and removes equality from the wrapper itself.

\section{A directly calibrated equality-free competitor}
\label{sec:equalityfree}

The subset-seeded schema contains no equality except any equality placed
inside its arbitrary matrix. This permits a direct equality-free control
whose calibration does not use raw set-coded bisimulation.

In equality-free membership logic define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
 D(x,y):\quad\forall z\,(z\in x\leftrightarrow z\in y),
\end{equation}
and add the single sentence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:EC}
 \EC:\quad\forall x\forall y
 \left[D(x,y)\to\forall w\,(x\in w\leftrightarrow y\in w)\right].
\end{equation}
The first argument of membership must respect equality of member
profiles. Respect for the second argument already follows from the
definition of $D$.

\begin{definition}
$\QTsub$ is the equality-free theory consisting of $\EC$ and all
$\BIC_{\sigma_{\mathrm{sub}}}$ instances with equality-free matrices.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Equality-free seed calibration]\label{thm:Qsub}
There are primitive-recursive refutation translations, correct in PA,
between $\QTsub$ and $\Tsub$. In particular,
\[
 \PA\vdash\Con(\QTsub)\leftrightarrow\Con(\ZFC).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Translate an equality-bearing formula $A$ to $A^D$ by replacing
$x=y$ with $D(x,y)$ and commuting with the other constructors.
Pure logic proves that $D$ is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.
The definition of $D$ supplies membership congruence in its second
argument; $\EC$ supplies it in its first. Induction on formulas therefore
constructs the usual substitution-of-equals derivations for $D$.
This justifies the translated logical equality axioms and rules.

Extensionality translates to
\[
 \forall x\forall y\,(D(x,y)\to D(x,y)),
\]
which is a logical theorem. The subset seed, the mixed bound, and all
other parts of the closure wrapper are equality-free. Hence the
translation of a source closure instance is precisely an allowed
closure instance for the matrix $\rho^D$. This gives a proof compiler
from $\Tsub$ to $\QTsub$.

For the reverse compiler, work in $\Tsub$ with actual logical equality.
Extensionality gives $D(x,y)\to x=y$, and equality substitution proves
$\EC$. Every equality-free closure instance is already among the
$\Tsub$ axioms. Thus a $\QTsub$ derivation can be expanded in $\Tsub$.
Both compilers preserve falsity. Formula and derivation induction prove
their correctness in PA.
\end{proof}

This is more than an informal quotient-model observation: it describes
why the translated logical rules and every translated schema instance
are syntactically available. In a semantic presentation, the same proof
says that the quotient by $D$ is extensional and has well-defined
membership. No choice of representatives is needed in the proof compiler.

\paragraph{What is not being deleted.}
It would be incorrect to drop $\EC$ and continue using this argument.
Equality of predecessor profiles alone need not imply equality of
outgoing membership behavior. The raw nonextensional theory obtained by
deleting $\EC$ is an interesting next problem, but it is not calibrated
by Theorem~\ref{thm:Qsub}. The earlier RCC interpretation relies on
additional constructions that cannot simply be imported without checking
which collection principles they use.

\section{Two complementary refinements of the closure idea}
\label{sec:otherkernels}

The point/subset-seeded mixed-bound theories are the main recommendation.
Two other refinements help isolate where the compression comes from and
provide alternative implementation controls.

\subsection{Subset-bounded closure with a separate Separation schema}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SB}
 \SB(\rho):\quad
 \forall x\exists b\forall y\bigl(\rho(x,y)\to y\subseteq b\bigr),
\end{equation}
and use the root closure schema
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SBC}
 \SBC_\rho:\quad
 \SB(\rho)\to\forall a\exists W
 \bigl(a\in W\land\Cl_\rho(W)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here we strengthen the permitted fibers from membership-bounded to
subset-bounded, while retaining Separation rather than the global-image
condition.

\begin{theorem}[Subset-bounded closure]\label{thm:SBC}
$\Ext+\Sep+\SBC$ is deductively equivalent to $\ZFz$, and hence
PA-equiconsistent with ZFC.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Separation gives an empty set. Closure for the empty relation gives a
set containing any desired root; Separation extracts singletons.
A constant singleton-output relation and Separation give Pairing.
For Power Set of $A$, use $\rho(x,y)\equiv y\subseteq A$,
whose fibers have subset bound $A$, then separate the subsets of $A$
from a root closure witness.

The nontrivial bootstrap is Union. Merely closing under subsets is not
enough to obtain a transitive set: for example, the family
$\{\varnothing,\{\{\varnothing\}\}\}$ is closed under taking subsets of
its members but omits $\{\varnothing\}$, a member of one of them.
Instead use the following switching relation. Define
\[
 \Sing(x,t):\quad\forall z\,(z\in x\leftrightarrow z=t)
\]
and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:switch}
 \rho_*(x,y):\quad
 \Sing(x,y)\ \lor\
 \bigl(\neg\exists t\,\Sing(x,t)\land y\subseteq x\bigr).
\end{equation}
If $x$ is a singleton with unique member $t$, its only output is $t$,
bounded by $b=t$. Otherwise its outputs are the subsets of $x$, bounded
by $b=x$. Thus $\SB(\rho_*)$ holds without already having Union.

Let $W$ contain a root $A$ and be closed under $\rho_*$. We claim $W$
is transitive. Given $x\in W$ and $y\in x$, if $x$ is a singleton its
decoder edge directly places $y$ in $W$. Otherwise $\{y\}\subseteq x$,
so $\{y\}\in W$, and its decoder edge then gives $y\in W$.
Since $A\in W$, two applications of transitivity bound $\bigcup A$;
Separation gives its exact set.

Membership-bounded relations are now subset-bounded: if all outputs lie
in $b$, they are all subsets of $\bigcup b$. Thus every instance of root
set-like closure follows. The earlier successor and functional-image
proofs give Infinity and Replacement.

Conversely, under $\SB(\rho)$, $\ZFz$ forms the unique exact fiber by
Separation in $\Pow(b)$, and iterates those unique fibers along $\omega$.
This proves \eqref{eq:SBC}. All arguments are uniform in $\rho$.
\end{proof}

The switching relation is useful independently of this particular theory:
it converts a subset-closure resource into transitivity without requiring
a simultaneous bound for two different kinds of outputs. The mutually
exclusive cases avoid the circular use of a binary union.

\subsection{Subset-only iterated images with a weak nonempty seed}

The supplied subset-image theory uses a relatively long Infinity axiom.
It can instead use an iterated image schema plus the very short sentence
\[
 \NE:\quad\exists u\exists v\,(u\in v).
\]
For every matrix $\rho$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ISI}
\begin{split}
 \ISI_\rho:\quad \SB(\rho)\to\forall A\exists C\,[{}&
 \forall x\forall y\bigl(((x\in A\lor x\in C)\land\rho(x,y))
                       \to y\in C\bigr)\\
 &{}\land\forall y\bigl(y\in C\to\exists x\,\rho(x,y)\bigr)].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This wrapper is equality-free and uses only subset bounds. Unlike the
main schemas, its seed is the membership extension of $A$; it need not
contain any initial input without $\NE$.

\begin{theorem}[Iterated subset images]\label{thm:ISI}
$T_{\ISI}=\Ext+\NE+\ISI$ is deductively equivalent to $\ZFz$.
Its equality-free congruence version $\EC+\NE+\ISI$ is PA-equiconsistent
with ZFC.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First derive the subset-bounded image schema from the supplied dossier:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SBI}
\begin{split}
 &\forall x\in A\exists b\forall y
   (\varphi(x,y)\to y\subseteq b)\\
 &\hspace{1em}\longrightarrow\exists C\forall y
   \bigl(y\in C\leftrightarrow\exists x\in A\,\varphi(x,y)\bigr).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Use $\rho(x,y)\equiv x\in A\land\varphi(x,y)$ with $A$ as a parameter
of the matrix. For inputs outside $A$ the fiber is empty, so the local
premise becomes global. Instantiate the schema's independently bound
seed variable with $A$. All desired outputs are in $C$, and the
global-image clause excludes every other output. Thus \eqref{eq:SBI}
holds exactly, not merely as a bounding assertion.

A false matrix yields an empty set $e$. A fixed nonempty domain from
$\NE$, with constant output $t$, yields $\{t\}$: a partial functional
fiber is subset-bounded by its unique output. In particular let
$s=\{e\}$, so $e\ne s$. Apply \eqref{eq:ISI} to the two-cycle
$e\mapsto s\mapsto e$, starting with the membership seed $s=\{e\}$.
The resulting output is exactly $\{e,s\}$.
Using these two labels as a domain in \eqref{eq:SBI}, a functional
selector gives $\{u,v\}$ for arbitrary $u,v$.

Partial functional images give Separation. A constant matrix
$y\subseteq a$ on a fixed nonempty domain gives Power Set.
For Union, first form
\[
 D=\{s:\exists x\in A\ (s\subseteq x)\}
\]
using \eqref{eq:SBI} directly. Apply partial functional image formation
to the singleton decoder $\Sing(s,y)$ on $D$. Its image is exactly
$\bigcup A$: a member $y\in x\in A$ is decoded from $\{y\}\subseteq x$,
and every decoded singleton has its unique member in some $x\in A$.
Functional Replacement follows from the partial functional image rule.

Finally apply \eqref{eq:ISI} to the successor relation with membership
seed $\{e\}$. The output $C$ contains $e^+$ and is successor-closed;
$C\cup\{e\}$ is inductive. Conversely, exact fibers in $\Pow(b)$ and
positive $\omega$-iteration from $D_0=A$ prove every $\ISI$ instance in
$\ZFz$. The equality-free calibration uses the proof translation from
Theorem~\ref{thm:Qsub}, since the whole wrapper and $\NE$ are equality-free.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A necessary bootstrap for this particular wrapper]
Deleting $\NE$ from $\Ext+\NE+\ISI$ leaves a one-object model with empty
membership. Both clauses about $C$ are vacuous there for every matrix.
This deletion really does lose the required strength; it is not just an
unfilled step in the proof. By contrast, the reflexive point and subset
seeds in the main construction force outputs of constant nonempty
relations, so no separate $\NE$ axiom is needed.
\end{remark}

\section{Comparison with container--collection and the finite-axiom route}
\label{sec:competitors}

\subsection{The earlier aggressive relational kernel}

For comparison, the inherited RCC presentation uses the container sentence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CP}
\begin{split}
 \CP:\quad\forall p\exists V\,[p\in V\land\forall x\in V\,(
 x\subseteq V\land\exists W\in V\,
          \forall z(z\subseteq x\to z\in W))]
\end{split}
\end{equation}
and the global-image collection schema
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GIC}
\begin{split}
 \GIC_\varphi:\quad\forall B\exists C\,[{}&
 \forall x\in B\forall y\bigl(\varphi(x,y)\to
         \exists v(\varphi(x,v)\land v\in C)\bigr)\\
 &{}\land\forall y\in C\exists x\,\varphi(x,y)].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Both are read in equality-free logic with unrestricted equality-free
matrices and fresh schema witnesses.

The preceding dossier gives a nonextensional calibration via raw set
constructions, a set-coded bisimulation relation, and transfer of the
compact axioms to an extensional completion \cite{prior2,prior3}.
The present work does not independently reconstruct that whole proof.
Its main new theories provide a different control: their extensional
calibration is direct, and $\QTsub$ removes equality by a single explicit
congruence law. That makes it possible to compare machine size without
making the most delicate interpretation the only route to certification.

It is significant that the reverse proofs here collect \emph{unique exact
fibers}. They do not replace functional Replacement by unrestricted
Collection in a foundation-free universe. Importing the full RCC
bisimulation proof into a weakened image theory would require an audit
of precisely that distinction.

\subsection{Finite predicative classes remain a serious alternative}

The finite-axiom route should use a precisely specified predicative
G\"odel--Bernays/NBG theory, with the set/class and choice conventions
frozen. Friedman states finite axiomatizability and equiconsistency with
ZF in a fragment of PA allowing superexponentiation; the corresponding
choice versions calibrate with ZFC \cite[p.~28]{friedman}.
This is the right kind of metatheoretic strength for the requested PA
endpoint.

The attraction is operational: once a finite class theory is compiled,
there is no unbounded set-theoretic schema recognizer. One can Skolemize
it offline and search for finite ground refutations. The price is the
fixed clause data, Skolem-term handling, and any treatment of equality.
A finite presentation is not automatically a smaller complete program.

Do not substitute impredicative class comprehension for predicative
comprehension. That changes the theory and invalidates the cited
calibration. Nor should one assume that the expanded-language finite
class theory is uniformly interpretable back into ZFC merely because it
is conservative over ZFC for set-language sentences. The source explicitly
distinguishes conservativity, equiconsistency, and interpretability.

\subsection{Why a finite list of ordinary ZFC theorems cannot suffice}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-consequence obstruction]\label{prop:finite}
Assume ZFC is consistent. Let $F$ be a finite set of membership-language
sentences, each provable in ZFC. Then
\[
 \PA\not\vdash\Con(F)\leftrightarrow\Con(\ZFC).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Reflection for the finite list of sentences yields, inside ZFC, a set
structure satisfying all members of $F$. Satisfaction for that finite
list and elementary soundness give $\ZFC\vdash\Con(F)$.
If PA proved the displayed equivalence, ZFC would prove its own
consistency, contrary to the second incompleteness theorem under the
consistency assumption.
\end{proof}

Thus bundling all ZFC schemas into one enormous fixed sentence of the
same language cannot work if that sentence is itself a ZFC theorem and
has the requested calibration. A finite class theory changes the
language and the way finite proofs are reduced; the obstruction does
not rule it out. Likewise, writing ``for every formula'' is a schema,
not a finite first-order axiom unless the required syntactic machinery
has genuinely been internalized and justified.

\section{From the mathematical reductions to a proof in PA}
\label{sec:PA}

\subsection{What has been proved at the mathematical level}

For the main theories, the new part of the calibration consists of
explicit uniform derivations. A source axiom certificate contains its
matrix and variable data. The reduction substitutes that matrix into
one of a fixed collection of derivations, renames bound variables, and
concatenates the result with the translated logical proof.

For example, the following proof constructors are sufficient for the
$\ZFz$-to-$\Tpt$ direction:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Constructor & Formula parameter or fixed input\\
\midrule
\texttt{empty}, \texttt{singleton}, \texttt{pair} & fixed templates and variable codes\\
\texttt{separation} & an arbitrary formula $\psi(y,\ps)$\\
\texttt{powerset}, \texttt{union}, \texttt{infinity} & fixed templates\\
\texttt{replacement} & an arbitrary formula $\varphi(x,y,\ps)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The Replacement constructor inserts the two-level relation
\eqref{eq:star}; it does not inspect the semantic meaning of $\varphi$.
The reverse closure constructor inserts $\rho$ into the exact-fiber and
recursion proof in Lemma~\ref{lem:reverse}. Matrix complexity does not
require a stronger mathematical induction principle on the target side;
it affects only the length of the generated finite proof.

With standard syntactic coding these constructors are primitive
recursive. Their correctness is proved by structural induction on
formulas and finite derivations, together with fixed checks of the
underlying proof templates. PA supports those inductions. This is the
ordinary metamathematical content of saying that the displayed
calibrations are provable in PA.

\subsection{The standard Foundation/Choice bridge is off-machine}

The remaining bridge \eqref{eq:standard} is not a claim that PA proves
set-theoretic truth or that $\ZFz$ proves the existence of a set model of
ZFC. In a foundation-free ambient theory, the cumulative well-founded
hierarchy is constructed by recursion along ordinal well-orders. The
class of its ranked sets satisfies Foundation as well as the other ZF
axioms; the constructible class inside it satisfies Choice.

To obtain the arithmetic theorem, use the corresponding formula
relativizations as proof translations. For each source axiom instance,
insert the formal derivation of its relativization. The uniformity in
Separation and Replacement matrices is essential. The arithmetic
verification is an induction on a \emph{finite proof code}, not an
application of transfinite induction in PA.

This distinction matters in formal developments: a proof assistant may
verify mathematical statements about a constructible class while leaving
the uniform metatheorem external. Paulson's discussion explicitly warns
about this gap \cite{paulson}. A Lean or Coq theorem in a strong ambient
logic is valuable evidence, but it is not by itself an exported proof
that PA derives the intended arithmetized equivalence.

\subsection{The exact deliverable boundary}

This archive supplies ordinary mathematical proofs and executable
syntax/finite-combinatorics checks. It does \emph{not} supply a complete
PA proof term, a serialized derivation of every template in a chosen
Hilbert calculus, or a verified TM compiler. The mathematical statements
above do not depend on the finite experiments; the experiments are
regressions for formulas and constructions used in those statements.

The next certification artifact should fix, rather than leave implicit,
the following interfaces:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PAinterfaces}
\begin{aligned}
 \PA&\vdash \Prf_T(p,\bot)\to\Prf_{\ZFC}(f(p),\bot),\\
 \PA&\vdash \Prf_{\ZFC}(p,\bot)\to\Prf_T(g(p),\bot),\\
 \PA&\vdash \operatorname{Accept}_T(c)\to\Inc(T),\\
 \PA&\vdash \Inc(T)\to\exists c\,\operatorname{Accept}_T(c),\\
 \PA&\vdash \Halt(M)\leftrightarrow\exists c\,\operatorname{Accept}_T(c).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The last line must refer to the literal released transition table, the
actual blank-tape convention, and the actual halting convention. None
of those details is determined merely by selecting a set theory.

\section{Co-designing the search language and the halting condition}
\label{sec:search}

\subsection{Generate certified instances rather than recognize arbitrary axioms}

For a closure-schema certificate, store the matrix and a small constructor
tag. The checker builds the axiom using the fixed wrapper, verifies the
freshness convention, and universally closes the remaining parameters.
This often avoids a more complicated recognizer that tries to determine
whether an arbitrary formula is an instance of the schema.

A reference enumerator has the following shape:
\begin{lstlisting}
for n = 0, 1, 2, ...:
    c = decode_finite_certificate(n)
    if well_formed(c) and verify_derivation(c, theory=T):
        if conclusion(c) == designated_target:
            halt
\end{lstlisting}
Every candidate check must terminate. If the certificate is itself a
program, include a finite execution bound or dovetail its execution;
otherwise one invalid or diverging candidate could block the entire
search. For a total syntactic checker, PA proves that every fixed
finite candidate is eventually examined. Completeness then translates
a source proof into one such finite accepted candidate.

A useful implementation sequence is to keep one common parser, one
logical calculus, one proof-record format, and one TM backend, while
substituting only the theory-instance generator. Otherwise the comparison
conflates changes in foundations with changes in compiler engineering.

\subsection{Falsity-free theorem search is compatible with the new kernel}

The dossier's positive-target technique applies to $\QTsub$ as well as
RCC. It should remain a separate implementation layer, not be confused
with the consistency calibration of the foundation.

Let the equality-free primitive language be
\[
 E(v_i,v_j),\qquad A\to B,\qquad\forall v_i A,
\]
and let $H=\forall x\forall y\,E(x,y)$. For every formula $A$ in this
language, pure implicational/universal logic proves $H\to A$ by a
uniform induction: instantiate $H$ for atomic conclusions, use the
consequent for an implication, and generalize for a universal formula.
The closedness of $H$ is essential in the last step.

Start with a bottom-bearing equality-free theory $T$ proving $\neg H$.
Expand its derived connectives in $E,\to,\forall,\bot$ and replace
$\bot$ by $H$. Call the resulting axiom presentation $T^+$.
The uniform explosion lemma translates a $T$ refutation into a
$T^+$ proof of $H$. Conversely, under the fixed $T$ theorem $\neg H$,
structural induction gives equivalence of every source formula with its
translation; a $T^+$ proof of $H$ therefore yields a $T$ refutation.

For $T=\QTsub$, the required $\neg H$ follows from the empty-set instance
of the closure schema. Under $H$, that empty object would be a member
of itself. Hence the target translation is available with no additional
set-theoretic axiom.

Since all theory axioms are sentences, a fixed open theorem
$E(v_0,v_1)$ with \emph{distinct} variables can be universally generalized
to $H$, and $H$ can be instantiated back. Thus an implementation may use
this atomic theoremhood target. It must not substitute that open atom
for falsity inside arbitrary formulas: the substitution macro is the
\emph{closed} $H$.

The positive theory $T^+$ has a one-object full-relation model: every
atom and hence every formula in this grammar is true. It is therefore
not the desired ZFC-equiconsistent foundation. What is calibrated is
\[
 \Prov_{T^+}(H)\ \leftrightarrow\ \Inc(T),
\]
not $\Inc(T^+)\leftrightarrow\Inc(T)$. This distinction is unchanged
from the supplied dossier \cite{prior2}.

\subsection{The diagonal target and irreflexivity must be audited together}

A nonextensional machine may encode falsity by $E(v_0,v_0)$ only when its
theory/calculus supplies the required refutability. Removing an
irreflexivity axiom without changing that encoding can invalidate
soundness even when the underlying theory remains equiconsistent with
ZFC. The inspected 254-state source explicitly identifies this dependency
\cite{upstream}.

The all-relation target $H$ avoids assuming irreflexivity of membership,
but its wrapper and any resulting formula expansion have a cost.
Neither the old diagonal encoding nor the positive-target alternative
can be declared cheaper before passing through the same backend.

\subsection{Costs deliberately not hidden inside a state estimate}

A complete cost ledger must include formula decoding, substitution and
freshness, logical axiom generation, theory-instance generation, proof
validation, enumeration, the target test, bootstrap tape data, the
register/stack interpreter, and the final encoding into states and
symbols. Actual sizes need not add independently: code sharing and
state merging can couple the components.

Equality is especially important. The low score of $\Tpt$ does not
include the extra formula constructor or equality-rule machinery that
an equality-bearing proof engine may need. Conversely, eliminating
logical equality by replacing each equality atom with $D$ can greatly
expand \emph{proofs} while adding only a small fixed generator. Proof
length and machine description size are different objectives.

The subset test also occurs repeatedly in the new schemas. Sharing one
subset-template constructor could be more valuable than saving one
unshared formula occurrence. A backend-aware experiment should compare
both expanded trees and constructor-program descriptions.

\subsection{First milestone: compile the transparent candidates}

Implement $\Tpt$, $\QTsub$, and RCC as theory generators within one
certificate checker and one backend. Keep the formula coding, inference
rules, enumeration order, and target convention fixed whenever possible.
Record the complete tables and code-size ledger, not only the number of
source instructions. The purpose is to learn whether equality elimination,
shorter bootstrapping, or a smaller container generator dominates the
actual cost.

The directly calibrated $\QTsub$ deserves early attention: it keeps the
machine language equality-free while remaining close to RCC in the
declared fixed-template metric. The $\Tpt$ version is the natural
readable correctness control and may still win if equality support is
cheap or already shared with other proof machinery.

\subsection{Second milestone: serialize the new proof reductions}

Choose a concrete first-order calculus and implement the finite proof
constructors in Section~\ref{sec:PA}. Begin with the constant-relation
lemma, which simultaneously covers Empty Set, Separation, and Power
Set. Next certify two-cycle Pairing and the Replacement star. Only then
add the recursion proof used for the reverse direction.

This ordering isolates short, checkable obligations before introducing
the standard Foundation/Choice bridge. It also makes errors such as
using an unavailable singleton or silently choosing a family of bounds
visible in a small derivation rather than in the final end-to-end proof.

\subsection{Third milestone: test a finite class-theory ground engine}

Fix one finite predicative class presentation. Compile to clauses
outside the runtime machine and search for finite ground certificates.
The machine should enumerate terms and validate a propositional
refutation certificate; the proof of Skolemization and completeness
belongs in the PA audit. If equality is retained, ground congruence or
an equality elimination must be part of the soundness/completeness proof.

The stopping criterion is a \emph{complete} state count for the same
blank-tape and symbol conventions. A smaller axiom database but a larger
term interpreter is not a win.

\subsection{Focused mathematical questions}

Several possible deletions now have precise boundaries.

\paragraph{Can the membership arm be deleted from point-seeded BIC?}
Replace $K(y,b)$ by $y\subseteq b$ but retain the point seed and the
global-image clause. The constant false relation, constant singleton
relations, two-cycle Pairing, and Power Set still have immediate
proofs. The direct Separation argument no longer applies: members of
$A$ need not all be subsets of an available common bound before Union
has been obtained. The subset-image argument from the earlier dossier
has extra bootstrap assumptions, so it cannot simply be cited here.
The calibration of this smaller point-seeded theory is left open in
this report; neither inadequacy nor equiconsistency is claimed.

\paragraph{Can $\EC$ be removed from the subset-seeded theory?}
This asks for a new nonextensional interpretation or a rigorous
countermodel/strength analysis. The direct $D$-translation fails at the
missing first-argument congruence step. The closure operation may provide
useful transitive bounds, but whether it supplies the representative
collection needed for a raw quotient requires a separate proof.

\paragraph{Can the seed and fiber kernels share more code?}
The general seed criterion allows $\sigma(x,a)=K(x,a)$ as well:
its seed set is $a\cup\Pow(a)$ and $K(a,a)$ is logically valid.
This is not the shortest expanded tree, but sharing the same kernel in
the local-bound test and the seed test may improve an actual generator.
It is a backend experiment supported by Corollary~\ref{cor:seed}, not
an unproved consistency conjecture.

\paragraph{Can the minimum useful matrix grammar be reduced safely?}
The calibration uses arbitrary formulas, including the translated
matrices of arbitrary ZFC Separation and Replacement instances. A bounded
quantifier-rank restriction or a fixed finite list cannot simply be
assumed to retain ZFC strength. A useful restricted grammar would need
an explicit uniform coding of all required instances and a PA proof of
that translation.

\paragraph{Can proof certificates absorb more operational complexity?}
Hints for substitutions, branch selections, and term instantiations may
make validation simpler even when certificates become much longer.
Such a design is appropriate for Busy Beaver applications, where
runtime can be enormous. Every hint still needs a finite syntactic
check; no semantic oracle may be hidden in the certificate format.

\section{Busy Beaver consequences and final recommendation}

If a particular $n$-state table is certified to satisfy
\eqref{eq:target}, consistency of ZFC implies that it does not halt.
The same equivalence gives
$\PA\vdash\neg\Halt(M)\leftrightarrow\Con(\ZFC)$.
Thus, under the usual consistency hypothesis, ZFC cannot prove its
nonhalting, by the second incompleteness theorem. Ruling out every
possible false proof of the $\Sigma^0_1$ halting claim would require an
appropriate soundness hypothesis, not merely consistency.

For the usual step-count Busy Beaver function, a proved finite value
at that state budget would bound the machine's halting time. If ZFC is
consistent, any particular finite initial run is nonhalting and PA can
verify it. Combining the asserted bound with that finite verification
would prove nonhalting, giving the familiar incompleteness obstruction.
This consequence becomes quantitative only after a literal table and
its state budget have been certified. The present theory proposals do
not establish a new numerical Busy Beaver threshold.

The immediate recommendation is therefore to preserve the earlier RCC
route while adding the seeded closure family as a serious competitor.
The main equality-bearing theory is particularly compact:
\[
 \boxed{\Tpt=\text{Extensionality}+\text{one seeded bounded-image closure schema}.}
\]
The subset seed then gives the direct equality-free control
\[
 \boxed{\QTsub=\text{member-profile congruence}+\text{one equality-free closure schema}.}
\]
Both have explicit calibration arguments; the second avoids the raw
bisimulation step. Finite predicative class theory remains the genuinely
finite-axiom alternative. The decisive next result should be an
arithmetically audited compiler comparison, not another uncompiled
claim that a short displayed axiom must give the smallest machine.
