# An exact-theory reparameterization of the rho6 emitter

## Definitions and source

Use the unchanged native constructors

\[
P(a,b)=(a+b)(a+b+1)/2+b,\quad
I(a,b)=4P(a,b),\quad R(a,b)=4P(a,b)+1,
\]
\[
Q(a,b)=4P(a,b)+2,\quad E(a,b)=4P(a,b)+3,
\quad F=1,\quad N(A)=I(A,1),\quad
\operatorname{Ex}_i A=N(Q(i,N(A))).
\]

The ordinary rho6 family is

\[
D(i,A)=I(\operatorname{Ex}_i Q(i,A),A).
\]

The original AX row takes a numerical triple (a,b,c) and emits D(a,c).
The changed AX row takes the same triple and emits D(a,b). Every other
row, the gated K bundle, the fourth-field reader, the two proof rules,
the target formula, and the source equality optimization are unchanged.

The complete source is
`../machines/rho6_219/sites.json`, generated by
`calculus_experiments/row_permutations.py` from the pinned K220 source and the exact four
K219 equality replacements. It has 4,744 primitive occurrences,
206 distinct source DAG nodes, maximum source depth 38 and maximum
zero run 6, equal to K219's corresponding measurements.

## Exact axiom-set theorem

Let T be the original native calculus and T' the changed calculus.
Their axiom sets are literally equal sets of natural numbers, hence
their theorem sets and their refutability of code 1 are identical.

Indeed, as a,b,c independently range over the natural numbers,

\[
\{D(a,c):a,b,c\in\mathbb N\}
=\{D(i,A):i,A\in\mathbb N\}
=\{D(a,b):a,b,c\in\mathbb N\}.
\]

The equality concerns the range of this individual ungated row. A whole
AX batch with unchanged input fields need not emit the same ordered
list of nine axiom codes. No claim of pointwise batch equivalence is
needed or made.

For a concrete finite proof translation, replace an original use of
rho6 with parameters (a,b,c) by the changed row with parameters (a,c,0).
Its emitted formula is D(a,c), exactly the original code. In the reverse
direction, replace a changed use at (a,b,c) by the original row at
(a,0,b). Its emitted formula is D(a,b). The unused fourth input field
can be retained or set to zero; rho6 does not inspect it. Leave every
other axiom occurrence and every inference occurrence unchanged.

These are primitive recursive translations of finite proofs. An AX
parameterization within the inherited exhaustive proof enumerator can
be re-encoded with the same row selector and the substituted numerical
fields. Exhaustive coverage guarantees that this new finite command
sequence occurs; no preservation of the original enumeration index or
running time is asserted. Accordingly the inherited ZFC proof of
\(\operatorname{Con}(T)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})\)
also gives the same equivalence for T'.

The bundle's gate does not interfere: rho6 is one of the ungated
logical rows, and the K bundle itself is unchanged. In particular the
chosen replacement c=0 is not a side condition on rho6.

## Source macro correctness

In the source postfix representation, rho6 occupies the primitive
ordinal interval [895,1178). Its word is

\[
a\;a\;c\;Q\;1\;I\;Q\;1\;I\;c\;I.
\]

The changed word replaces both c entries by b and leaves every
constructor and every other operand unchanged. Here a,b,c are input
parameter pushes and 1 is a literal-one push; they are not new
logical symbols. The original pushes have identical stack and scratch
contracts: each preserves the input register, pushes its numerical
value, and restores its scratch cells. The replacement therefore emits
exactly D(a,b) and preserves the surrounding source's frame conditions.

The generator replaces the closed atoms of the two corresponding
push macros inside the original parse tree, preserving that tree's
associations outside those atoms. Both push macros have 26 primitive
occurrences and six minimal closed atoms. Consequently the total
primitive count and positions of subsequent source instructions do
not change. The four independent K219 equality-macro replacements are
then applied at their original paths. The closed-atom construction
preserves guarded control-flow semantics; the emitted flat word can
also be checked against the displayed postfix word independently.

This theorem concerns the source program and calculus. A particular
compiled native transition table additionally requires its own decoder
binding and runtime simulation checks. An equal source DAG size by
itself does not establish a state count or correctness of a native
table.
