The reusable theorem is CofinalScheduler.Dynamics.reach_or_halt.
It assumes only:
  successful boundary step -> candidate index increases by one;
  successful active step -> pending rank decreases, index unchanged.
There is no upper bound on the refilled pending rank. Its termination measure
is the lexicographic pair (target - index, pending). It works from an arbitrary
state whose index does not exceed the target. The target boundary is either
reached or the process has already halted.

complete_at composes that theorem with a stopping witness at ONE selected
target. Its witness is needed only for boundary states actually reachable
from the given starting state. This preserves the required order of
quantifiers and avoids assuming that all payloads reach the same target.

The concrete run functions stay literally unchanged. The Dynamics record
stores each concrete step/run and proves its zero/successor equations; no
conversion of executable semantics is needed. All four original clients
retain run_inv, run_add, drain, reach, and their old endpoint types. The
EnumPerm client additionally exports complete_any and halts_iff_any: the
proof-list pointer need not be zero initially. Completeness itself does not
require the soundness invariant; the equivalence does.

Attribution: this is a generic proof-engineering factorization of the existing
enumerator scheduling proofs. Arbitrary-state completeness, tail universality,
and lexicographic scheduling are not claimed as novel mathematics. Earlier
repository packets, particularly the checked-transport/further-interface
work, already propose those directions. The contribution here is the checked
weak scheduler contract, concrete production overlays, preserved interfaces,
and successful regeneration of both maintained forked enumerators.
