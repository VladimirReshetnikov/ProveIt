\subsection{The reduction that crosses the 246-state boundary}
\label{sec:zero6-breakthrough}
The successful construction combines a shorter marker scanner with a
context-aware reassociation of the fixed decoder program.  Its final grammar
has $128$ pure sequence rules after the old lookup at state~177 is represented
as a grammar router.  The corresponding non-grammar framework has $118$
working states before shortening the scanner, and $117$ afterwards.  Thus the
realized table has
\[
 128+117=245
\]
working states.  These accounting categories are semantic roles; a freed
numeric state label may be reused by a decoder rule in the final table.
The exact expanded source has maximum zero run~$6$, maximum leaf depth~$51$,
and the same ordered list of all $6\,460$ instructions as the reference.

\paragraph{Why a previously rejected rotation now works.}
Write $[q]$ for the closed semantic word of old sequence state~$q$.  The
well-known proposed reduction
\[
 [178]=([23],([24],[206]))
       \quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
       (([23],[24]),[206])=([16],[206])
\]
removes rule~208.  It previously failed because the folded call $218/0$
entered state~154 from the left, and required its two projections
$00\mapsto[23]$ and $10\mapsto[105]$.
The new construction sends this call to the already useful word~86:
\[
 [86]=([98],[99]),\qquad
 [98]=([23],([132],[16])),\qquad
 [99]=([105],[114]).
\]
It follows immediately that word~86 has exactly the two required
projections.  No change to the closed semantics of word~86 is needed,
because the new rule for~98 is simply
\[
 ([113],[16])=(([23],[132]),[16])
       \quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
       ([23],([132],[16])).
\]
The helper $([132],[16])$ has the semantic word
\texttt{KCeijkfg}.  Its shorter left spine also helps satisfy the scanner's
zero-run bound.  This sharing of a reassociation between two purposes is the
key: it reduces a dangerous decoder depth and supplies a different legal
folded-entry target, which in turn permits the old~178 rotation.

In the successful old-label table, the mechanism appears directly as
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{state}&\text{read }0&\text{read }1\\\hline
98 &0\,R\,23&1\,R\,74\\
74 &0\,R\,132&1\,R\,16\\
178&0\,R\,16&1\,R\,206\\
218&0\,L\,86&1\,R\,70
\end{array}
\]
State label~74 is now the helper decoder, not the old scanner state.
The scanner's transition $58/0$ instead goes directly to $84$.
Other cost-neutral associations rearrange larger unique decoder regions so
that no source path contains seven consecutive zeros.

\paragraph{How the graph was found and checked.}
The search releases a fixed folded-entry root only when each external call
can be routed through another active grammar word with the same required
projections.  It also synthesizes router-only words, rather than limiting the
candidate pool to substrings of the original source.  All~22 folded
projections and the~54 sufficient bootstrap row constraints are checked.
A graded objective first reduces the number of seven-zero nodes from~18 to~1
without increasing the~128-rule grammar budget.  A small explicit
reassociation then gives a~129-rule, zero-six intermediate graph.
A final search keeps the zero-six bound as a hard condition and removes one
rule.  With seed~$129177$, it finds the~128-rule solution after
$7\,040\,505$ proposals.  These proposal counts describe a heuristic run;
they are not an exhaustive search or a state lower bound.

The frozen successful grammar has SHA-256
\begin{center}\ttfamily\footnotesize

\end{center}
Independent Python checks verify its exact words, orientations, all~22
projections, all~54 bootstrap constraints, reachability of every rule, and
expanded source bounds.  The separate literal-machine audit realizes it as a
$245$-state table, checks the register-machine bisimulation, and verifies all
$8\,391$ affine instruction cases.  In this lane, a second exact check
repaints the~$8\,385$ retained interval representatives using the new source
paths, header distance~$181$, and~$167$ spare blocks of value~$1$.
Every case passes a literal binary-tape execution; the maximum is~$2\,686$
moves and all~$490$ transition rows are exercised.  This sample-based replay
corroborates, but does not replace, the parametric instruction proof.

Fresh independent sparse and dense interpreters both reach the original
bootstrap frame after~$407\,915$ moves, with state~96, head~$-5$, header~176,
and exactly~351 one-cells.  The logical seed is
$(3,0,0,0,3,0,0,0,0)$ and the~167 spare blocks all have value~1.
The logical and parametric proof components are separate obligations in the
complete consistency-equivalence argument; a small state count and a long
non-halting simulation alone would establish neither.
