\section{An exact obstruction in a finite grammar neighborhood}
\label{sec:restricted-grammar-bound}

The routed machine separates into 117 fixed framework roles and 128 binary
grammar roles. We investigated whether a smaller grammar could be obtained
while retaining the present words at the externally constrained entries.
The result below is a lower bound for an explicit finite grammar family.
It is not a lower bound for unrestricted Turing machines, all source
reassociations, all routing choices, or all encodings of the theory.

Let $G_0$ be the supplied 128-rule grammar and let $w_{\rm AX}$ be its
702-symbol AX word. Begin with the nonterminal words of $G_0$, the 44
semantic terminal symbols, and every substring of $w_{\rm AX}$ of length
between 2 and 40 having at least two occurrences. For each nonterminal
$w$, admit every production $w\to uv$ with $w=uv$ and with both children in
this vocabulary, subject to the original incoming-bit restrictions on
terminal children. Freeze the original productions at the 43 words
traversed by the fixed routes and the 54 bootstrap projection constraints.
Delete unsupported words and productions repeatedly. The resulting
explicit universe has 797 nonterminal words and 4,511 permitted
productions. The retained entry words and frozen paths force 47 of the
presence variables to be one.

\begin{theorem}[Finite grammar neighborhood]
Among the straight-line grammars using this exact production universe,
the frozen productions, and the prescribed entry words, the minimum
number of live nonterminal words is 128. This statement uses no bound on
the maximum zero run of source addresses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Introduce a binary presence variable $x_w$ for each nonterminal and a
production variable $y_p$ for each permitted production. Every grammar
in the stated family satisfies
\[
  \sum_{p:\,\operatorname{parent}(p)=w}y_p=x_w,
  \qquad y_{(w,u,v)}\leq x_u,\quad
  y_{(w,u,v)}\leq x_v,
\]
where child inequalities are omitted for terminals. Required presence
variables have lower bound one. All other variables lie between zero and
one. The grammar cost is $\sum_w x_w$. We may relax the production
variables to real numbers when deriving a lower bound.

A branch tree on four presence variables has five leaves. The branches
below are read from left to right, starting with the variable for
\texttt{llYW}; the subsequent variables, where reached, are
\texttt{eijkbIXllYW}, \texttt{lYW}, and \texttt{jGeijkbIXll}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cc}
Branch path & Exact rational lower bound \\
\hline
$0000$ & $1398/11$ \\
$0001$ & $1409/11$ \\
$001$  & $4205/33$ \\
$01$   & $1398/11$ \\
$1$    & $1779/14$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For completeness, each leaf is verified directly as follows. Write its
linear constraints as $Az=b$, $Bz\leq d$, and $\ell\leq z\leq u$.
For the cost vector $c$, the certificate provides exact rational vectors
$\lambda$ and $\mu$ with $\mu\leq0$. Define
$r=c-A^{\mathsf T}\lambda-B^{\mathsf T}\mu$. Every feasible vector obeys
\[
 c^{\mathsf T}z\ \geq\
 \lambda^{\mathsf T}b+\mu^{\mathsf T}d
 +\sum_j\begin{cases}r_j\ell_j,&r_j\geq0,\\r_ju_j,&r_j<0.\end{cases}
\]
This inequality is checked with rational arithmetic, including every
residual coefficient; no floating-point tolerance is a premise. Its
right-hand side equals the rational number displayed for that leaf.
Each bound is strictly greater than 127. The five leaves cover all
assignments of the branched binary presence variables, so every grammar
has integer cost at least 128. The original grammar is checked to belong
to the stated family and has exactly 128 rules, proving sharpness.
\end{proof}

The supplied standard-library verifier reconstructs the full vocabulary,
terminal restrictions, 43 production freezes, fixed-point pruning,
production ordering, and forced presence variables from the pinned input
texts. It then reconstructs all model equations and child dependencies,
checks the model and certificate hashes, checks the positive witness,
and verifies all five leaf inequalities. It imports no numerical solver.
The integer-programming solver was used only to discover the certificate.
The model permits every selected nonterminal at most one chosen
production; context-dependent multiple meanings are outside this family.

The finite restriction is material. Longer new substrings, changed route
words, altered frozen productions, semantic respellings, different theory
templates, and changes to the 117-role framework may all escape the
bound. An expanded search allowing additional prefix and suffix words
had 6,596 words and 85,865 productions; its solver terminated on the time
limit without an incumbent. No lower bound is inferred from that run.
