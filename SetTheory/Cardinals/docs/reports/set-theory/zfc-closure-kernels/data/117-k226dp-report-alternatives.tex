\section{Alternative formalisms and encoding routes}
\label{sec:alternatives}

The most useful distinction is between a shorter foundational theory,
a shorter serialized query, and a smaller complete binary machine.
Each can improve independently.  The alternatives below have concrete
mechanisms, but none supplies an additional certified state saving
beyond the K-based construction presented here.

\paragraph{Native closure theories.}
The K230 theory uses subset-indexed witness closure and a support
condition, with unrestricted formula instances and finite parameter
tuples.  Its ordinary and irreflexive versions are both calibrated
to StandardZFC, while the emitted numerical calculus uses the
irreflexive version for its falsity alias.  That calibration already
reaches the published binary table.  It makes K the best verified
machine starting point among the sources actually measured here.
The older raw EPSC route instead activates every empty input and
thereby removes an explicit seed parameter.  Its EPSC/PSC equivalence
has uniform finite schema-instance arguments, but a smaller displayed
schema does not remove the proof enumerator or its substitution and
freshness obligations.  The retained Source~14 review explicitly
distinguishes this ordinary equivalence from rejected numerical
shortcuts and from the missing new-source formal endpoint.\footnote{
K230: \url{https://github.com/elliotglazer/k230-rocq/blob/3142e4dd980f0be12f4abb636c4ae2528afc17eb/docs/proof-overview.md}.
EPSC: \url{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/SetTheory/Cardinals/docs/reports/set-theory/zfc-closure-kernels/intake-146-source-14.md}.}

\paragraph{Finite class and assignment kernels.}
GB is finitely axiomatizable and conservative over ZF.  Adding the
ordinary set-theoretic Choice sentence therefore gives a finite
conservative extension of ZFC: conservativity with this extra set
sentence follows by the deduction theorem.  This specifies the
Choice convention without relying on an ambiguous use of the name
NBG.  The class-comprehension matrices remain restricted to formulas
without bound class variables.  Van Wesep's Theorem~3.1 gives a more
general finite conservative class extension for any recursively
enumerable extension of the underlying set theory.\footnote{
Robert A. Van Wesep, \emph{Satisfaction relations for proper classes},
arXiv:1201.5509v2, pp.~3--4 and Theorem~3.1:
\url{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.5509}.}
For machine design, finiteness replaces schema recognition by a
literal axiom payload; class operations, sorts, and their first-order
proof apparatus still contribute to the implementation.

The repository's FC15 route makes the mechanism concrete: assignment
evaluation and coordinate update, NAND and existential class
constructors, and six closure-or-counterexample clauses represent
the raw EPSC schema by fifteen universal clauses.  Its recommended
form fuses guarded witnesses and derives a member selector.
The one common domain and genuine equality are essential; a compiler
must implement congruence and handle coincident values.  The source
supplies an ordinary model interpretation, not a complete binary
proof search.\footnote{
\url{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/aaf47dea43ec6cc3c1f42b6ffd56a92fb6e7fdab/SetTheory/Cardinals/docs/reports/set-theory/zfc-closure-kernels/44-nine-notes-finite_class_closure.md}.}

\paragraph{Pure equational search.}
Ground contextual rewriting removes variable binding from the
enumerated certificates.  Completeness follows from the ground-term
algebra modulo the generated congruence, with a constant ensuring
nonemptiness.  Arbitrary ground substitutions, both rewrite
directions, repeated-variable matching, contextual replacement, and
fair certificate coverage remain necessary.  The inherited E663
packet already carries seven equations and its complete query;
Section~\ref{sec:e662} gives the separate one-bit conservative
fresh-constant improvement to E662.  This is a measured payload
change, not a K improvement or a machine-state result.
The repository's different seven-equation generator experiment
produced a 299-state complete control source before the missing
ZFC-specific target/writer and full calibration.  That measurement
is neither a compilation nor a lower bound for E662.\footnote{
\url{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/13ec39ed43148b1807f51ed6e6528e1c84659562/Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/equational_generator_cost.md}.}

The cyclic Df3 route compresses three commuting modal closures into
one closure and an order-three rotation.  Its E9 basis has nine
equations; the reverse validity reduction uses a threefold product
and coordinate projection.  This transports empty-premise
equational validity, not arbitrary extra premises.  The ZFC-specific
target must contain the full first-order and pairing translation.
The retained record includes a finite class source and its
10,182-node model wrapper, but expressly lacks the final expanded
modal/equational target.  Counting only the nine kernel equations
would omit the main unresolved payload.\footnote{
\url{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/aaf47dea43ec6cc3c1f42b6ffd56a92fb6e7fdab/SetTheory/Cardinals/docs/reports/set-theory/zfc-closure-kernels/41-cyclic-notes-algebraic_development.md}.}

\paragraph{Combinators and complete-description accounting.}
The repository also supplies an explicit closed SK term $P_M$
simulating a literal machine, with a literal normal-form halt
handler $K$.  The two SK equations plus $P_M\ne K$ are consistent
exactly when $M$ does not halt: confluence relates conversion to
reduction to $K$, and the ground-term quotient gives the separating
model.  Equations alone would always have a singleton model.
The measured 253-state carrier requires 192,272 BCL bits, with
the table accounting for about 98.3 percent; its one-constant version
grows to 302,419 bits.  Those are complete term measurements but
exclude a binary evaluator and blank loader.  This is a concrete
way to transfer a known calibration, and an example of why a smaller
alphabet need not yield a shorter complete description.\footnote{
\url{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/aaf47dea43ec6cc3c1f42b6ffd56a92fb6e7fdab/SetTheory/Cardinals/docs/reports/set-theory/zfc-closure-kernels/24-proxy-experiments-sk_carrier-README.md}.}

The promising next encoding experiment is a shared ground-term
walker used for decoding, substitution, contextual replacement,
and query comparison, measured together with its literal writer.
Frequency-based opcode tags or arity codes can shorten the data,
but the decoder may grow and change state sharing.  A complete
comparison must count the resulting combined transition table;
no state budget is inferred here from symbol counts or bit counts.
