\section{A supplementary equational result: three structural witness identifications}
\label{sec:stack937}

The binary construction and the equational construction in this section
have separate correctness arguments.  The former gives the stated
238-state machine.  Here we specialize the complete 946-bit native-stack
packet of the preceding coordinated-reuse report~\cite{coordinated239}.
Three of its closure-witness functions can share existing structural
constructors:
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{Target}(b)=Q(b),\qquad
 \mathsf{Next}(b,z)=P(b,z),\qquad
 \mathsf{Mem}(b,z)=A(b,z).
 \label{eq:937-identifications}
\end{equation}
The resulting complete packet has \textbf{937 bits}, 14 equations,
215 term nodes, 10 operation symbols and three local variables.
The reduction is nine bits in this particular encoding.  No binary
implementation of its equational proof search is supplied, and no
minimum over other encodings is asserted.

The substantive point is the compatibility proof.  A stack constructor,
a class constructor and a Skolem witness need not have compatible
interpretations merely because their arities agree.  We construct a
countable model whose elements carry both a finite structural tag and a
raw set value.  A weighted recursion assigns the latter in the order
required by all three witness obligations.  The argument applies to the
complete packet, including malformed selectors, arbitrary descriptors,
both closure branches and its native equality equations.

\subsection{The inherited interface and the exact finite packet}

Write \(\mathcal K_{946}\) for the inspected parent packet.  It already
identifies its class-NAND constructor \(J\) with its stack constructor
\(P\), implements a member selector as \(W(x,d)\), and uses the same
element \(d\) as its Boolean truth constant.  Let \(\mathcal K_{937}\)
be its literal transformation by~\eqref{eq:937-identifications}.
The remaining signature is
\[
 d^{(0)},\quad N^{(2)},\quad V^{(2)},\quad P^{(2)},\quad
 A^{(2)},\quad Q^{(1)},\quad W^{(2)},\quad
 p_E^{(2)},\quad p_\varepsilon^{(2)},\quad p_G^{(1)}.
\]
Its ground query is \(N(d,d)=d\).  Bare equational consistency would
not be the appropriate query: every equational theory has a one-element
model.  We require a model separating these two ground terms,
equivalently their nonderivability.

The first eight equations, with all displayed variables universally
quantified, are
\begin{align}
 N\bigl(N(x,N(N(y,x),x)),N(y,N(z,x))\bigr)&=y,
       \label{eq:937-sh1}\\
 d&=N(d,N(d,d)),\label{eq:937-top}\\
 V(P(x,s),d)&=x,\label{eq:937-head}\\
 V(P(x,s),A(j,d))&=V(s,j),\label{eq:937-tail}\\
 p_\varepsilon(s,P(a,b))
       &=N(p_\varepsilon(s,a),p_\varepsilon(s,b)),\\
 p_\varepsilon(s,A(i,j))
       &=p_E(V(s,i),V(s,j)),\\
 p_\varepsilon(s,Q(a))
       &=p_\varepsilon(P(W(s,a),s),a),\label{eq:937-witness}\\
 d&=N\bigl(p_\varepsilon(P(z,s),a),
                N(d,p_\varepsilon(s,Q(a)))\bigr).
                \label{eq:937-arbitrary}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:937-sh1} is the inherited Sh1 Boolean basis;
the parent report supplies its attribution and the same-carrier
Boolean conversion~\cite{coordinated239}.

For the remaining six equations it is clearer first to display their
ordinary two-valued content.  Put
\[
 R_b(x,y)=
 \varepsilon\bigl(P(y,P(x,b)),V(b,d)\bigr),\qquad h=Q(b).
\]
The six clauses are
\begin{align}
 G(b)&\ \lor\ R_b(h,P(b,z)), \label{eq:937-bad1}\\
 G(b)&\ \lor\ E(A(b,z),P(b,z)), \label{eq:937-bad2}\\
 G(b)&\ \lor\ \neg E(A(b,z),z), \label{eq:937-bad3}\\
 \neg G(b)&\ \lor\ E(W(x,d),x)\ \lor\
                    \neg R_b(x,y)\ \lor\ E(y,h), \label{eq:937-empty}\\
 \neg G(b)&\ \lor\ \neg E(x,h)\ \lor\
                    \neg R_b(x,y)\ \lor\ E(y,h), \label{eq:937-close}\\
 \neg G(b)&\ \lor\ \neg E(y,h)\ \lor\
                    R_b(P(b,y),y). \label{eq:937-support}
\end{align}
Each clause has the parent's explicit Boolean equation in \(N,d\).
For example~\eqref{eq:937-bad1} is
\[
 d=N\!\left(N(d,p_G(b)),
 N\!\left(d,p_\varepsilon
       \bigl(P(P(b,z),P(Q(b),b)),V(b,d)\bigr)\right)\right).
\]
The file \texttt{stack937.equations} contains all 14 equations fully
expanded, and \texttt{stack937.bits} encodes exactly those equations and
the two ground endpoints.  Thus the abbreviations in this exposition
are not omitted parts of the measured payload.

\subsection{The raw closure theory used by the upper construction}

In an ordinary nonempty structure with membership relation \(E\), define
\[
 \operatorname{Empty}(x)\ \equiv\ \forall u\,\neg E(u,x),
 \qquad
 y\subseteq_E z\ \equiv\
       \forall u\,(E(u,y)\rightarrow E(u,z)).
\]
For every \emph{equality-free pure-membership} formula
\(R(x,y;\bar p)\), with any finite tuple of parameters, put
\[
 \operatorname{LB}(R)\ \equiv\
    \forall x\,\exists z\,\forall y\,
                (R(x,y;\bar p)\rightarrow y\subseteq_E z).
\]
The raw empty-seed positive subset-closure scheme, abbreviated EPSC,
asserts that \(\operatorname{LB}(R)\) implies the existence of \(h\)
such that
\begin{align}
 \forall x\,\forall y\,
 \bigl((\operatorname{Empty}(x)\lor E(x,h))\land R(x,y;\bar p)
                    \rightarrow E(y,h)\bigr),\label{eq:937-epsc-close}\\
 \forall y\,\bigl(E(y,h)\rightarrow
                          \exists x\,R(x,y;\bar p)\bigr).
                          \label{eq:937-epsc-support}
\end{align}
The parent packet's ordinary consistency calibration uses precisely
this formula-indexed scheme~\cite{coordinated239}.  The proof below
does not assume closure for arbitrary externally specified relations.
It also does not replace the scheme by a single fixed first-order sentence.

\begin{lemma}[Literal reverse interpretation]
\label{lem:937-reverse}
Every \(\mathcal K_{937}\)-model expands to a
\(\mathcal K_{946}\)-model by~\eqref{eq:937-identifications}.
The expansion preserves the values of \(N(d,d)\) and \(d\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Give each deleted operation its displayed defining term.
Every parent equation becomes exactly the transformed equation.
The ground query uses only unchanged symbols.
\end{proof}

The upper direction requires a different argument.  We prove it for
every countable EPSC structure, which isolates the new construction
from the inherited set-theoretic calibration.

\subsection{Finite tags, total selectors and the dependency order}

Let \(M=(M,E_M)\) be a countable nonempty EPSC structure.
Take a countably infinite carrier \(D\) of freely generated finite trees.
Its leaves are one distinguished element \(d\) and a separate element
\(e_m\) for every \(m\in M\); its constructors are \(P,A,Q\).
The leaves \(e_m\) are elements of a chosen model carrier, not additional
constant symbols in the finite equational signature.  Interpret
\(P,A,Q\) by their disjoint injective tree constructors.

Assign weight one to every leaf and define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 w(Q(b))&=w(b)+1,\\
 w(P(b,z))&=w(b)+w(z)+2,\\
 w(A(b,z))&=w(b)+w(z)+3.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:937-weights}
\end{equation}
Every proper subterm has smaller weight.  In addition, for every
\(b,z\in D\),
\begin{equation}
              w(Q(b))<w(P(b,z))<w(A(b,z)).
              \label{eq:937-cross-order}
\end{equation}
These cross-constructor inequalities will allow a hull or bad input
to be selected first, then a relation witness, and then a member
separating that witness from a proposed bound.

For any selector \(i\), let \(\operatorname{idx}(i)\) be the number of
successive outer layers of the form \(A(j,d)\).  A selector of every
other shape has index zero.  Regard \(P(x,s)\) as a cons cell and
every non-\(P\) tail as the infinite constant-\(d\) stream.
Define \(V(s,i)\) to be coordinate \(\operatorname{idx}(i)\) of that
stream.  This defines \(V\) on all of \(D^2\), and
\eqref{eq:937-head}--\eqref{eq:937-tail} follow directly.
In particular, their universal ranges have not been restricted to
canonical selectors.

Every class tree \(c\) also decodes syntactically to a finite
pure-membership formula in finitely many stream coordinates.
A leaf denotes falsity; \(P(a,b)\) denotes NAND; \(A(i,j)\) denotes
membership between coordinates \(\operatorname{idx}(i)\) and
\(\operatorname{idx}(j)\); \(Q(a)\) existentially prepends a coordinate.
This is recursion on the finite class tree.  It does not inspect any
membership relation or raw set value.

\begin{lemma}[Descriptor descent]
\label{lem:937-descent}
For each descriptor \(b\), the expression
\[
       \varepsilon(P(y,P(x,b)),V(b,d))
\]
decodes to one finite equality-free formula
\(\widehat R_b(x,y;\bar u)\).  Each fixed parameter in \(\bar u\)
is a proper subterm of \(b\) or the default leaf \(d\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The class \(V(b,d)\) is the head of \(b\) if \(b\) is a \(P\)-tree,
and is \(d\) otherwise.  Decode that finite class first.
Its finitely many referenced stream coordinates are the two newly
prepended variables, coordinates of the tail \(b\), or coordinates
introduced by its own existential binders.  Every coordinate of \(b\)
is a head of a proper tail subtree, hence a proper subterm of \(b\),
or is the default \(d\).  Bound coordinates become quantified
variables.  Only the stated fixed parameters remain.
\end{proof}

The lemma also covers classes that inspect their code slot or an
unexpectedly deep coordinate.  The relevant property is finite
structural descent, not a promise that arbitrary descriptors have
the intended syntactic format.

\subsection{Assigning raw values without a circular definition}

Fix \(m_0\in M\).  We define a projection \(\mu:D\to M\) by recursion
on~\eqref{eq:937-weights}, starting with
\[
                   \mu(d)=m_0,\qquad \mu(e_m)=m.
\]
These leaves guarantee surjectivity independently of every later
constructor choice.  Countability permits all subsequent witnesses
to be selected using a fixed external enumeration of \(M\).

Once the parameters in Lemma~\ref{lem:937-descent} have received
their raw values, its decoded formula defines a particular
pure-membership relation \(R_b^M\) in \(M\).  It is one of the
permitted EPSC instances.  Make the following fixed choice for
each descriptor \(b\).

If \(\operatorname{LB}(R_b^M)\) holds, set \(g_b=\mathrm{true}\)
and choose an EPSC hull \(h_b\).  If it fails, set
\(g_b=\mathrm{false}\) and choose a bad input \(h_b\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \forall z\in M\,\exists n,m\in M\,
 \bigl(R_b^M(h_b,n)\land E_M(m,n)\land\neg E_M(m,z)\bigr).
 \label{eq:937-bad-input}
\end{equation}
The latter choice is exactly classical negation of the displayed
local-boundedness statement, including the definition of subset.
Assign
\begin{equation}
                         \mu(Q(b))=h_b.
                         \label{eq:937-projection-q}
\end{equation}

For \(P(b,z)\), distinguish the same two branches.
If \(g_b=\mathrm{false}\), choose \(n\) with
\[
 R_b^M(h_b,n),\qquad n\not\subseteq_{E_M}\mu(z),
\]
as supplied by~\eqref{eq:937-bad-input}.
If \(g_b=\mathrm{true}\) and \(E_M(\mu(z),h_b)\), choose \(n\)
with \(R_b^M(n,\mu(z))\), using hull support.
If \(g_b=\mathrm{true}\) and \(\mu(z)\) is not a member of \(h_b\),
take \(n=m_0\).  In each case set
\begin{equation}
                         \mu(P(b,z))=n.
                         \label{eq:937-projection-p}
\end{equation}
Finally, if \(g_b=\mathrm{false}\), choose a member of
\(\mu(P(b,z))\) that is not a member of \(\mu(z)\), and assign
that value to \(\mu(A(b,z))\).  If \(g_b=\mathrm{true}\),
assign \(m_0\) to \(\mu(A(b,z))\).

\begin{lemma}[Well-founded raw interpretation]
\label{lem:937-projection}
These prescriptions define a total surjection \(\mu:D\to M\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
To choose \(R_b^M,g_b,h_b\), only the proper-subterm parameter
values of \(b\), and the already fixed \(\mu(d)\), are needed.
Thus~\eqref{eq:937-projection-q} is legitimate at weight \(w(Q(b))\).
When assigning \(\mu(P(b,z))\), both \(\mu(z)\) and the needed
\(\mu(Q(b))=h_b\) have smaller weight by~\eqref{eq:937-cross-order}.
When assigning \(\mu(A(b,z))\), the value \(\mu(P(b,z))\) is
available for the same reason.  Each requested witness exists by
the selected branch's defining property.  Recursion on positive
integer weights therefore supplies all values.  The equations
\(\mu(e_m)=m\) prove surjectivity.
\end{proof}

Define raw membership on \(D\) by
\[
                      E_D(u,v)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                      E_M(\mu(u),\mu(v)).
\]
Induction on formulas, with surjectivity used at quantifiers, gives
\begin{equation}
 (D,E_D)\models\phi(\bar u)
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (M,E_M)\models\phi(\mu(\bar u))
 \label{eq:937-formula-transfer}
\end{equation}
for every equality-free pure-membership formula \(\phi\).
Native equality is deliberately excluded from this transfer:
two distinct tags may have the same raw value.
Native equality in the equational kernel remains literal equality
of trees and satisfies the stack equations already proved.

\subsection{Class semantics, the free witness slice and both branches}

Interpret \(\varepsilon(s,c)\) by the finite decoded class \(c\),
its stream coordinates and \(E_D\).  This realizes class NAND and
atomic membership.  The definition of the quantifier constructor
gives
\[
 \varepsilon(s,Q(a))
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \exists z\in D\,\varepsilon(P(z,s),a).
\]
For \(a\ne d\), choose \(W(s,a)\) to witness this existential
whenever it is true, and choose an arbitrary element otherwise.
At \(a=d\), the class is always false.  Both sides of the chosen
witness equivalence are therefore false whatever \(W(s,d)\) is,
and the arbitrary-candidate implication has a false antecedent.
Use this unconstrained slice to set
\[
 W(x,d)=
 \begin{cases}
   e_m \text{ for some }m\text{ with }E_M(m,\mu(x)),
                      &\text{if such }m\text{ exists},\\
   d,                  &\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently \(E_D(W(x,d),x)\) holds whenever \(x\) is nonempty.
The selector still has exactly the member-choice behavior required
by the parent packet.

Set \(G(b)=g_b\).  Formula transfer~\eqref{eq:937-formula-transfer}
and the definitions of \(\varepsilon,V\) identify \(R_b(x,y)\)
with \(R_b^M(\mu(x),\mu(y))\).
If \(g_b=\mathrm{false}\), the chosen raw values of \(Q(b)\),
\(P(b,z)\) and \(A(b,z)\) satisfy
\eqref{eq:937-bad1}--\eqref{eq:937-bad3}; the other clauses
hold because their first literal is true.

If \(g_b=\mathrm{true}\), the first three clauses hold immediately.
For~\eqref{eq:937-empty}, a nonempty \(x\) satisfies its member-selector
literal.  For an empty \(x\), the hull's empty-seed closure
property~\eqref{eq:937-epsc-close} supplies the last literal whenever
\(R_b(x,y)\) holds.  The same hull property directly gives
\eqref{eq:937-close}.  If \(E_D(y,Q(b))\), the definition of
\(\mu(P(b,y))\) supplies a supporting input for \(R_b(-,y)\),
which proves~\eqref{eq:937-support}.
This verifies all six clauses for every descriptor and every tuple
of carrier elements.

\begin{theorem}[Upper model and ordinary consistency calibration]
\label{thm:937-calibration}
Every countable nonempty EPSC structure has a countable tagged cover
supporting a \(\mathcal K_{937}\)-model in which \(N(d,d)\ne d\).
Using the inherited raw-EPSC/ZFC calibration of
\(\mathcal K_{946}\), ordinary ZFC proves
\[
       \mathcal K_{937}\nvdash N(d,d)=d
                \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The construction above gives the ordinary relational and native-function
interpretation.  Transport a countably infinite Boolean algebra onto
the same carrier \(D\), mapping its top to the distinguished \(d\).
Interpret \(N\) as Boolean NAND and \(p_E,p_\varepsilon,p_G\)
as its top or bottom according to the ordinary predicate truth values.
Keep \(P,A,Q,V,W\) unchanged.  The verified native equations and
Boolean clauses give exactly \(\mathcal K_{937}\), and its
Boolean bottom \(N(d,d)\) differs from \(d\).

For the final equivalence, the inherited calibration supplies a
countable EPSC model from \(\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})\),
in the ordinary ZFC metatheory.  The upper construction gives a
separating \(\mathcal K_{937}\)-model.  Conversely, a separating
model expands to a separating parent model by
Lemma~\ref{lem:937-reverse}, and the parent calibration yields
\(\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})\).
Equational completeness converts existence of a separating model
to nonderivability of the ground query.  The ground-term congruence
quotient suffices for this last step.
\end{proof}

The Boolean role of \(d\) and its class role differ:
\(d\) is the Boolean top, while \(\varepsilon(s,d)\) is always
false in the upper model.  No equation identifies an object with
its class-incidence value.  This proof is ordinary mathematics,
formalizable in ZFC; it supplies no new Lean/Rocq endpoint or
serialized PA derivation.

\subsection{Exact accounting and the remaining implementation boundary}

The unchanged codec includes an eight-bit symbol count, three arity
bits per symbol, an eight-bit variable capacity, one marker per
equation followed by a terminating marker, every term node, and both
ground endpoints.  Ten symbols plus three variables still require
four-bit opcodes.  Thus the measured length is
\[
 \underbrace{8+3\cdot10+8}_{46\text{ header bits}}
 +\underbrace{14+1+4\cdot215}_{875\text{ equation bits}}
 +\underbrace{4\cdot4}_{16\text{ endpoint bits}}
 =\boxed{937}.
\]
The three identifications preserve arity and the number of term
nodes; their nine-bit saving comes entirely from the signature header.
The serializer reconstructs the exact parent packet before applying
them.  Two independent parsers agree on the transformed packet;
all 937 strict prefixes are rejected, and the finite Boolean audit
checks 72 clause-group valuations.  Four malformed or trailing-input
controls also pass.  The builder explicitly rejects Python
\texttt{-O} and \texttt{-OO}, which would otherwise disable assertions.
These are exact serialization and Boolean-transcription checks.
They do not replace the unbounded upper-model proof.

The bit-character SHA-256, excluding the terminating file newline, is
\begin{center}\small\ttfamily
a35c535276aa32cefc6c019824124a6841\\
d9aef91b6bf679fae0af3116da2f52
\end{center}
The complete machine-readable equations, bits, parsed data and
verification receipt accompany the packet as
\texttt{stack937.equations}, \texttt{stack937.bits},
\texttt{stack937.json} and \texttt{verification.json}.

This alternative keeps genuine equational substitution.  That is
materially different from deleting equality rows in the current
numerical proof engine: those rows also implement cross-variable
instantiation, whose absence admits the documented finite-sort
countermodel~\cite{typedcountermodel}.  Likewise, the familiar
dual-\(\rho_6\) rewrite requires additional care with the runtime's
relative falsity; ordinary Boolean resemblance alone does not
establish the required refutation equivalence~\cite{dualgap}.
The main binary result retains its calibrated complete frontend.

Finally, the present sharing argument does not permit arbitrary
operation identifications.  For example, \(N=P\) would make
the native cons constructor commutative, since Boolean NAND is
commutative.  Then for arbitrary \(x,s\),
\[
 x=V(P(x,s),d)=V(P(s,x),d)=s,
\]
and the carrier would collapse to one element.  We keep Boolean
\(N\) separate from structural \(P\).  We also keep the free
\(W(-,d)\) slice available for member selection: additionally
forcing it to equal \(Q\) would impose a further compatibility
condition not proved here.

A complete binary implementation of this packet must still account
for substitutions with repeated variables, arbitrary one-hole
contexts, both equation orientations, fair unbounded certificate
enumeration, and the blank-tape initializer.  The 937-bit result
supplies a smaller explicit input in the inspected stack family.
Its bit count cannot be subtracted from the 238-state machine's
state count.
