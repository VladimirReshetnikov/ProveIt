\section{What changing the logical formalism can and cannot buy}
\label{sec:routes}

The goal concerns the cost of a complete computation.  A shorter
axiom, smaller constructor alphabet, or smaller encoded packet
is useful only when its search engine, initialization, and
correctness interface can be charged in the same TM convention.
The inspected alternatives divide into the following precise
cases.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.23\textwidth}XX@{}}

\end{center}

The current SIR theory has no separate extensionality,
foundation, pairing, union, infinity, separation, or replacement
axiom.  A bisimulation quotient reconstructs the required set
theory~\cite{m254sets}.  Irreflexivity is unnecessary in that
quotient direction, but essential to the current numerical
falsity alias $1=\operatorname{Rel}(0,0)$, logical-row soundness,
and the permitted $a=b$ collision cases in the bundled schema.
The gate $c\ne a,b$ is essential too.  Removing equality or IR
therefore cannot simply inherit the original binary theorem.

The raw equality-free C/G compiler illustrates the problem.
The repository records a 252-state measurement with fewer
logical rows, base-three formula tags, and a dual quantifier
row, but it also records the finite-sort obstruction to the
intended emitted calculus~\cite{eqfree}.  The set-theoretic
consistency strength of a full schema does not repair missing
logical substitution or quantifier rules in a numerical engine.
This route is excluded as a newly calibrated smaller candidate.

The finite equational route makes a different tradeoff.  Native
stack equations handle selector access and binder environments;
ordinary ground contextual rewriting supplies the proof system.
There is no uncharged claim that its syntax is already executed
by the 237-state register engine.  The complete new packet below
combines~\cite{e713,e937}, retaining the native-stack and raw-EPSC
dependencies~\cite{source79,epsc}.  Its first Boolean equation is
the published Sh1 axiom~\cite{sh1}.  The novel work here is the
compatibility proof and exact combined encoding, followed by a
small Boolean term optimization and a new selector representation;
no global shortest-packet or
historical-priority claim is made.
