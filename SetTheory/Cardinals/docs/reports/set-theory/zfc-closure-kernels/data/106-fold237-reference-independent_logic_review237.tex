\section{Why the final machine has the requested ZFC calibration}
\label{sec:logic-review-endpoint}

Let $M_{237}$ denote the final canonical binary table in this report,
whose SHA-256 is
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
7eda1da1fcda629cae86d22b01d3faf4\\
19552cf04ed4179f0c045ad0adf2c1a6.
\end{quote}
The displayed line break is typographic.  There are 237 working states,
including the complete initializer, and a separate halt state.  Write
$\operatorname{Halt}(M_{237})$ for the existence of a finite execution from
state 0, head 0, on the all-zero two-way tape that reaches that halt state.
Fix a customary effective finite-proof predicate
$\operatorname{Proof}_{\rm ZFC}(p,\bot)$.  The mathematical endpoint is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{ZFC}\ \vdash\quad
 \operatorname{Halt}(M_{237})
 \ \longleftrightarrow\
 \exists p\;\operatorname{Proof}_{\rm ZFC}(p,\bot).
 \label{eq:review-halting}
\end{equation}
Negating both sides gives the requested equivalence
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\ \vdash\quad
 \neg\operatorname{Halt}(M_{237})
 \ \longleftrightarrow\
 \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
This section specifies the composition establishing that conclusion and
the formalization boundary.  No premise asserting ZFC's consistency is
used to prove the equivalence.

\subsection{A reusable criterion for composing source and tape changes}

The most useful composition criterion refers directly to the final
machine.  Let $P_0$ be a calibrated finite register program, let $P_1$ be
a proposed replacement, and let $R_*$ be the fixed initial register file.
Suppose the following facts have been proved.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The two register programs have equivalent halting from their root
      paths and $R_*$.
\item A finite blank prefix of the final binary machine reaches a complete
      tape frame representing $(P_1,\epsilon,R_*)$, without halting.
\item Every nonhalting instruction of $P_1$, for every register file and
      every allowed tape-frame parameter, is simulated by a finite
      positive-length binary segment.  The segment does not halt, and
      ends at a complete frame for the precise next source configuration.
\item Every source halt instruction produces a finite binary halt.
      The instruction cases are exhaustive, and the allowed frame family
      is preserved by the outgoing segments.
\end{enumerate}
Then the final binary machine halts exactly when $P_0$ halts from $R_*$.

\begin{proof}
Remove the finite initialized prefix and use its complete final frame as
the first checkpoint.  If the source executes finitely many instructions
and reaches its halt instruction, concatenate their finite segment
witnesses and then the halt witness.  This gives a binary halting trace.

For the converse, suppose the source does not halt.  Its finite full
control tree and total natural-number register operations give a next
instruction at every source stage.  Recursively concatenate the certified
segments.  If their lengths are $d_0,d_1,\ldots$, each $d_i\geq1$; hence
the time of the $n$th checkpoint is at least $n$.  Every finite binary time
therefore lies before some checkpoint, inside one of the verified finite
segments.  No such segment halts.  Determinism identifies this concatenation
with the actual binary execution, so the actual machine never halts.
The source equivalence in the first premise supplies the claimed endpoint.
All the arguments use recursion and induction over natural numbers and
finite sequences.
\end{proof}

This criterion explains how the two incoming 239-state reductions and the
further $235\mapsto98$ fold can be combined without assuming that their
stated savings simply add.  Disjoint
edited rows are insufficient by themselves: one edit may change the tape
context in which another edited row is entered.  For the present combined
machine, the complete instruction simulation is checked afresh against
the final 474 rows.  The result supplies a single frame invariant for the
combined implementation.  It also avoids treating a guard proved for an
older source tree as automatically valid for a reassociated tree.

The positive-segment criterion is a standard simulation argument; no claim
of priority is made for it.  The new engineering result is that the
specific reassociated decoder, context split, and both coordinated helper
reuses meet its premises simultaneously at 237 states.

These source data and their reassociation are inherited.  The independent

The exact semantics matters.  A finite program-counter word $p$ is padded
on the right by zeros, and the decoder stops at the first encountered
leaf.  If that leaf has depth $d$, the instruction computes with the
consumed $d$-bit leaf path, discarding the unused tail of the old word.
Increment, break, and continue apply to this consumed path.  These are the
literal conventions in the retained
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Lean/BusyBeaver/ZFC/RegisterPath.lean}
{RegisterPath definition}, rather than an informal interpretation of
binary-tree associativity.

Number the leaves from left to right.  For each increment record the
register number and successor leaf; for each decrement record the
register and both successors; for each break or continue record its jump
target; and for a halt record a halt descriptor.  The two exact sources
have identical complete descriptor arrays.  Configurations having the
same register file and the same encountered leaf index are therefore
strongly bisimilar.  The decrement outcomes agree because the tested
register values agree.  Jump descriptors preserve the register file,
and the unique halt descriptor is matched exactly.  Induction proves
halting equivalence for every initial register file, including all unused
registers beyond the first nine.

The independent checker \texttt{check\_source\_graph.py} uses integer
path arithmetic and a separate prefix lookup.  It freshly checks all
6,455 leaves, 8,385 successor or guarded edges, and the sole halt
descriptor.  Both empty paths locate leaf 0.  The maximum leaf depth
changes from 51 to 48.  Every generated successor has only zeros after
its encountered leaf, giving the stronger physical-canonicality identity
\[
 p0^\omega=p_{\operatorname{loc}(p)}0^\omega.
\]
That identity handles both a path shorter than the next leaf and a path
with redundant trailing zeros.  It ensures that discarding the unused
source-path tail cannot erase a physical one in the subsequent decoder
frame.  Changed-register, removed-leaf, and duplicate-leaf negative
controls all reject.  These are finite exhaustive control checks, with
an ordinary induction proving their unbounded meaning; no register bound
or proof-search horizon is imposed.

\subsection{The final tape contracts}

The final table reaches its complete root frame after 409,835 blank
steps.  The canonical dispatcher is 84, the head is at $-5$, the marker
at $-6$, and the header at 176.  The logical registers are
\[
 R_*=(3,0,0,0,3,0,0,0,0),
\]
with the remaining logical registers zero.  The physical suffix contains
167 spare register blocks of value 1.  The tape has exactly 351 one-cells
and every other cell is zero.  The startup evidence records the whole
finite support and agrees with an independent dense run of the canonical
table, so agreement of a few selected registers is not being used in
place of whole-frame identity.

The direct universal runtime audit then passes all 8,386 instruction
guards for $P_1$, using the exact final 474 transition rows.  It exercises
every row and has minimum certified instruction duration 13.  Its
2,261,369 symbolic rule applications are the size of the proof computation,
not the length of the eventual consistency search.

The symbolic domains retain arbitrary natural primary-register values,
nonnegative extra spacing, and the arbitrary finite spare suffix allowed
by the stated runtime contract.  The rules are literal table transitions,
induction along a unary run, and induction over finite register-block
lists after checking the one-block identities.  They identify the complete
outgoing tape, control state, and head displacement.  The verified spare
and gap updates keep the frame family closed.  Thus the direct audit
provides the last three premises of the positive-segment criterion, and
establishes
\[
 \operatorname{Halt}(M_{237})
 \longleftrightarrow
 \operatorname{RegisterHalts}(P_1,\epsilon,R_*)
 \longleftrightarrow
 \operatorname{RegisterHalts}(P_0,\epsilon,R_*).
\]

\subsection{What the inherited logical calibration actually says}

The retained source theorem is
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Lean/BusyBeaver/ZFC/CalibratedRotation240.lean}
{CalibratedRotation240.halts\_iff\_zfc\_refutation}:
\[
 \operatorname{RegisterHalts}(P_0,\epsilon,R_*)
 \longleftrightarrow
 \operatorname{BProv}(\mathrm{ZFCax\_s},[],\bot).
\]
The native compact theory has a power-container axiom CP, the complete
global-image collection schema GIC, and irreflexivity, in ordinary
first-order logic with genuine equality.  It does not contain a separate
emitter for each familiar ZFC axiom.  In particular, deleting a familiar
axiom name from a conventional list is not an available local state
optimization of this source.

The mathematical calibration has four relevant pieces.  The program
enumerates a complete proof language for the numerical calculus.  That
calculus represents refutations of the native CP/GIC/irreflexivity theory,
including its special truth and falsity aliases.  The native theory is
compared with ZF through its profile/bisimulation quotient and internally
well-founded interpreted substructure.  Finally, Choice is removed from
refutations by the constructible submodel.  The actual comparison sources
are
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Lean/BusyBeaver/ZFC/WFTheory.lean}
{WFTheory},
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Lean/BusyBeaver/ZFC/ZFNative.lean}
{ZFNative}, and
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Lean/BusyBeaver/ZFC/ConstructibleChoice.lean}
{ConstructibleChoice}.

These are arguments about finite syntax, countable set-coded models,
quotients of sets, definable substructures, and first-order soundness and
completeness.  They can be carried out within ordinary ZFC.  A consistency
assumption is introduced only inside a relative-consistency implication,
where completeness supplies a set-coded model of the assumed-consistent
theory.  The argument never assumes that ZFC already proves existence of
a model of all of ZFC.

There is also no hidden change in the meaning of consistency.
The actual definition in
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/Logic/FirstOrder/Lean/FirstOrder/Completeness.lean}
{FirstOrder.Completeness} is
\[
 \operatorname{BProv}(B,G,\varphi)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists G_B\;[\,G_B\subseteq B\ \land\
                   \operatorname{Prov}(G_B\mathbin{+\!+}G,\varphi)\,],
\]
where $G_B$ is a finite list and $\operatorname{Prov}$ is finite derivation.
The actual
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/SetTheory/ZF/Lean/ZF/Zf.lean}
{ZF axiom predicate} includes full Separation and functional Replacement
with all parameters universally closed; the
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/SetTheory/BoundedConsistency/Lean/BoundedZFCConsistency/Choice.lean}
{ZFC predicate} adds the specified choice-set axiom.  Coding the finite
lists, axiom-instance witnesses, and proof trees identifies this statement
with existence of an ordinary finite ZFC refutation.  Effective translations
between customary first-order proof calculi give the same consistency
statement.  Composition proves \eqref{eq:review-halting}.

\subsection{Formalization status and independence consequences}

The final 237-state conclusion is an ordinary mathematical proof with
finite executable certificates and explicitly inherited logical
calibration.  A new serialized Lean theorem for the entire 237-state
binary endpoint is not supplied here.  The separate task of representing
every unbounded syntax and simulation step in PA is also not claimed to
be complete.  These distinctions matter, but they are not an additional
set-theoretic axiom needed by the ordinary ZFC proof just given.

In particular, an external Lean theorem is not converted into a ZFC
theorem merely by citing the Lean kernel.  The reason ZFC suffices here
is the concrete mathematical content of the finite simulation and the
set-coded logical comparison, rather than a soundness assertion for all
of Lean's type theory.

If ZFC is consistent, the equivalence and the second incompleteness
theorem show that ZFC cannot prove this machine nonhalting.  Consistency
alone should not be reported as excluding a ZFC proof of halting: that
direction is a false $\Sigma^0_1$ assertion when ZFC is consistent and
requires an additional suitable soundness assumption to exclude.
Neither absolute consistency nor unconditional nonhalting is asserted.

\section{Alternative formalisms: conclusions that affect the next reduction}
\label{sec:logic-review-alternatives}

The present improvement preserves the calibrated theory.  The alternatives
below were examined to identify where a more substantial future saving
would have to come from.  Their mathematical promise is distinct from
the cost of a complete binary implementation.

\subsection{Raw closure schemas and a finite equational packet}

The repository already studies equality-free empty-seed positive subset
closure (EPSC), with a complete ordinary first-order logic and arbitrary
finite parameters.  Its adequacy is a statement about the entire schema
and its quotient interpretation.  It does not justify deleting equality
and substitution machinery from the existing numerical proof calculus.
The retained
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/typed_countermodel.md}
{finite-sort counterinterpretation} pinpoints that distinction.

A more concrete independent architecture is the 946-bit packet in the
incoming
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/docs/incoming/BusyBeaver239_Research_2026-10-19.zip}
{coordinated-reuse archive}.  It has 14 equations, 215 term nodes,
13 operation symbols, and three local variables.  Its native stack
constructor also acts as a class NAND constructor; an unconstrained
false-class witness slice supplies member selection; and its truth
constant is shared with the existing distinguished object.  The earlier
report supplies the countable upper model needed to justify those
identifications.  These packet and model results are inherited, not new
claims of the present construction.

The refutation target is the particular ground equation $N(d,d)=d$.
The equations alone always have a one-element model, so their bare
consistency is the wrong endpoint.  What corresponds to
$\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})$ is nonderivability of that ground
equation, equivalently existence of an algebra separating its two sides.
A complete ground contextual rewrite search is adequate: the quotient of
the ground term algebra by the congruence generated by all ground axiom
instances is a separating model whenever the ground equation is not
derivable.  Every equality in the generated congruence has a finite
contextual rewrite witness.  Both directions of equations and all
substitution variables, including variables introduced only by reversing
an equation, must be included.

The open engineering cost is exact and substantial: a binary machine
must represent and compare unbounded terms, check repeated variables,
enumerate finite substitutions and one-hole contexts fairly, manage its
working tape, and write the entire fixed packet from the blank tape.
The number 946 measures serialized theory data, not working states.
It becomes a competing construction only after the interpreter, packet
loader, and their interaction have all been charged and verified.

\subsection{Logical basis changes}

The current single implicational axiom is the four-variable
\L ukasiewicz axiom.  The three-schema Tarski--Bernays basis and alternative
single axioms give valid competing logical interfaces; a primary reference
is Pfenning's
\href{https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~fp/papers/cade88.pdf}
{Single Axioms in the Implicational Propositional Calculus}, Section 2.
Formula length alone does not decide their compiled cost.  A different
basis changes variable reads, stack output width, shared subprograms, and
the encoding of proof instructions.

The incoming
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/ce192939372ac7944fe2a0208c68a96d49fb5e3b/docs/incoming/ZFC239_Companion_2026-10-19.zip}
{companion report} already proves a useful obstruction: replacing the
fourth input by any implication-only term in the other three inputs and
truth fails to recover all instances of Peirce's law in its specified
retained logical fragment.  Its three-valued counterinterpretation covers
arbitrarily large such terms by closure of a two-element subset.  Its
scope deliberately excludes CP and GIC, so it does not settle the
consistency strength of every full altered set theory.  It does exclude
claiming logical completeness from that specialization alone.

Consequently, a successful basis change needs an explicit complete
proof translation and a new source-encoding transport.  The already
calibrated proof language is retained in the exhibited 237-state machine.

\subsection{Two finite-presentation tests}

There is no general prohibition on using a finite theory equiconsistent
with ZFC.  Two more precise tests are useful.

\paragraph{Finite fragments of the actual set universe.}
Let $\Gamma$ be a fixed finite collection of membership-language theorems
of ZFC.  Reflection, applied to their finite conjunction and its
subformulas, gives a rank segment satisfying $\Gamma$.  Hence
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash\operatorname{Con}(\Gamma).
\]
Assuming ZFC is consistent, it follows that ZFC cannot also prove
$\operatorname{Con}(\Gamma)\leftrightarrow
\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})$.  Otherwise ZFC would prove its own
consistency.  This excludes replacing the infinite schema by finitely
many of its ordinary set-language theorem instances.  It does not exclude
finite class theories, finite theories in an expanded interpretation,
or fixed arithmetic theories containing a consistency assertion.
The same precisely scoped observation is recorded in the incoming
239-state construction's alternative-formalisms appendix.

\paragraph{Universal relational kernels without functions.}
Consider a finite purely relational language with $c$ constants and no
positive-arity function symbols, and a finite universal theory in it.
Any nonempty model has an induced substructure on its constant values,
or on one chosen element when $c=0$.  Universal sentences are preserved
by induced substructures, so satisfiability has a model of size at most
$\max(1,c)$.  Exhausting these finitely many structures decides it.
Ground disequalities can be retained in the same argument.  Thus a
generic universal refutation architecture of this restricted form
cannot represent the required unbounded ground structure.  The native
stack's positive-arity functions are an essential resource of its
particular finite presentation, rather than dispensable notation.

These tests are proof obligations and rejection filters, not lower
bounds on arbitrary Turing machines.  They motivate comparing complete
implementations of adequate theories while keeping the final binary
runtime certificate as the deciding state-count criterion.
