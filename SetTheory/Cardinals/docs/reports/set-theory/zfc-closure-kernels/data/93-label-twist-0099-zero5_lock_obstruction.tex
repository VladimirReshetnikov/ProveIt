\subsection{A finite obstruction to a five-zero search with the old bootstrap locks}

Let the semantic atoms and their literal implementations be those of the
245-state routing construction.  Suppose a candidate retains its required
entry word \texttt{eijkKCqkjGeU} (the old state 172) and the following five
left-child equations:
\begin{align*}
\texttt{eijkKCqkjGeU}&\xrightarrow{0}\texttt{eijkKCqkjGe},\\
\texttt{eijkKCqkjGe}&\xrightarrow{0}\texttt{eijkKCqkjG},\\
\texttt{eijkKCqkjG}&\xrightarrow{0}\texttt{eijkKC},\\
\texttt{eijkKC}&\xrightarrow{0}\texttt{eijk},\\
\texttt{eijk}&\xrightarrow{0}\texttt{e}.
\end{align*}
All five equations occur in the inherited 54-equation bootstrap lock.
The fixed implementation of atom \texttt{e} has AST index 40; its left
child is the instruction \(\operatorname{dec}(T)\).  Consequently the
required entry has a leaf at relative address \texttt{000000}.  Its
syntactic maximum zero run is therefore at least six.

This is a finite proof: concatenate the five specified grammar edges and
the one specified atomic edge.  It rules out a global five-zero bound in
this constrained family independently of the time spent searching.  It
does not rule out a smaller scanner justified by stronger reachable-frame
invariants, nor a grammar with different initialization, different atoms,
or released bootstrap equations.

The obstruction also explains a dependency in releasing constraints.  In
an ordered-word binary grammar, fixing either child fixes the split point
and hence the other child.  Removing only the top left-child equation
while retaining its right child \texttt{U} cannot change that split.  The
rotation
\[
 ((\texttt{eijkKCqkjG}\;\texttt{e})\;\texttt{U})
 \quad\longmapsto\quad
 (\texttt{eijkKCqkjG}\;(\texttt{e}\;\texttt{U}))
\]
can keep the grammar-state count unchanged if the obsolete prefix word
\texttt{eijkKCqkjGe} is also allowed to disappear.  Our associated
discovery experiment therefore removes the four lock rows belonging to
these two words, retains the other 50, and records a new exact blank-start
proof as an outstanding obligation for every generated candidate.
