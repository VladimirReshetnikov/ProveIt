\section{Certified rewrites that preserve the register computation}
\label{sec:neutral-macros}

The following result does not reduce the working-state count.  It provides
five independently checked runtime variants of the 246-state table and a
useful exact reduction of its tape overhead.  No blank initializer is
claimed for these variants.  In particular, a recurrent simulation theorem
must not be mistaken for a blank-tape consistency machine.

All state labels in this section are the original labels.  Write
\[
 B(v)=01^{v+1},\qquad B(A)=\prod_{v\in A}B(v),\qquad
 C(v)=101^v,\qquad C(A)=\prod_{v\in A}C(v).
\]
Let $A$ be the prefix before the selected primary register, let $x$ be its
value, and let $S$ be its nonempty suffix of ordinary register blocks.
The input to an arithmetic macro is
\[
 B(A)\,00\,1^x B(S)0^\omega.
\]
The left context is arbitrary.  Put $p=|B(A)|$ and
$w=|B(A\mathbin{+\!+}[x]\mathbin{+\!+}S)|$, so $w$ is the
width of the entire unmarked file.  Arbitrary finite lists and arbitrary
nonnegative register values are allowed.

\subsection{Removing the increment's accumulating spare blocks}

Make the single transition replacement
\[
 (131,0)\mapsto (1,R,103).
 \tag{A}
\]
The replaced transition was $(131,0)\mapsto(0,L,48)$.
All other transition rows remain unchanged.

\begin{lemma}[A terminating erasure gadget]
On a local word $011$ with state 131 at its first cell, replacement
\textup{(A)} changes that word to $000$ and reaches state 48 one cell
left of the word in exactly five steps.  It does not inspect either
exterior context.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The literal rows give
\[
 131\xrightarrow{0/1,R}103
 \xrightarrow{1/1,R}120
 \xrightarrow{1/0,L}61
 \xrightarrow{1/0,L}33
 \xrightarrow{1/0,L}48.
\]
Every read symbol in this calculation is supplied either by the input
$011$ or by an earlier displayed write.  The final word is $000$ and
the net head movement is one cell left.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Increment with no added spare]
For the modified table, the increment macro starting in state 73 at the
file's origin reaches state 48 one cell left of the origin, after
$2w+11$ transitions, with output
\[
 B(A\mathbin{+\!+}[x+1]\mathbin{+\!+}S)0^\omega.
\]
The entire suffix $S$, including all spare blocks, is preserved exactly.
The positive- and zero-decrement macros retain the original contracts,
with durations $2p+4$ and $2p+7$, respectively.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The seeker and forward-carry block inductions are unchanged.  At the
right exterior, the old increment creates $1011$, reaches state 131 at
its middle zero, and subsequently returns left.  The new five-step
gadget replaces that middle $011$ by $000$, leaving only the leading
one.  The word identity $C(S)1=1B(S)$ then gives the stated incremented
register with no extra block.  Replacing one old step by five new steps
adds exactly four transitions to the old $2w+7$ formula.  The return
scan crosses the same expanded old portion as before.

The elementary carry and rollback block proofs use only the read-one
row of state 131.  The zero-decrement local proof also uses only that
row.  The original positive-decrement route uses states 63 and 48.
Thus neither decrement macro changes.  Each statement is checked by
symbolic unary induction and structural induction over the arbitrary
block lists, rather than by bounded numerical samples.
\end{proof}

The complete decoder must still be checked, because state 131 also
occurs outside the arithmetic box.  The unchanged affine symbolic
executor accepts all 8,391 guarded instruction cases on this modified
literal table, including all 1,931 zero and 1,931 positive decrement
branches.  The new macro contracts above are the arithmetic premises.
The executor verifies the entire successor program counter, header
translation, register effect, and overflow behavior.  Its interpretation
of a register box quantifies over a nonempty arbitrary spare suffix;
the new increment preserves that premise exactly.  This is a
mathematical symbolic proof check, not a proof-assistant kernel theorem.

\begin{corollary}[Exact saving in tape overhead]
Start the original runtime and variant \textup{(A)} from corresponding
valid dispatch frames with identical logical registers, program counter,
header gap, and initial spare suffix.  After any common finite sequence
of instructions containing $I$ increments, variant \textup{(A)} has
exactly $3I$ fewer cells in its encoded register file and $2I$ fewer
one-cells contributed by spare blocks.  Its number of spare blocks
remains equal to the initial number.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The original increment appends the three-cell word $B(1)=011$; the
new increment appends nothing.  Both tables perform the same primary
register update.  All other instructions preserve the spare suffix
in both runtimes.  Induction over the common instruction sequence
proves the formulas.  Both tables have the same header movement at
instruction boundaries, so the difference also applies to the right
endpoint of their finite encodings relative to the same header.
\end{proof}

This is a storage reduction for the runtime from admitted frames.
A bounded search of all 246 existing states as blank starts, with a
three-million-step limit and finite tape bounds, found no admitted
root for variant \textup{(A)}.  That failure is not an impossibility
result, and it is not replaced here by an uncounted initializer.

\subsection{Routing positive decrement through the seeker}

Two further replacements, used separately, are
\[
 (75,1)\mapsto(1,R,73)\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 (75,1)\mapsto(1,L,73).
 \tag{B$_R$, B$_L$}
\]
The original row was $(75,1)\mapsto(1,L,63)$.  For a positive
selected value, these replacements send the machine through the
rightward seeker and its terminating carry before returning left.
They preserve the primary decrement and the one-cell boundary shift.
They append two value-one spare blocks, or one such block if combined
with \textup{(A)}.  The left-moving choice takes two extra steps over
the right-moving choice.

For clarity, the complete local contracts are summarized below.
The listed append counts concern value-one spare blocks; zero
append count means that the whole existing suffix is preserved.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\hline
Variant & Increment time & Positive decrement time
 & Inc. append & Pos. dec. append\\
\hline
Original & $2w+7$ & $2p+4$ & 1 & 0\\
A & $2w+11$ & $2p+4$ & 0 & 0\\
B$_R$ & $2w+7$ & $2w+10$ & 1 & 2\\
B$_L$ & $2w+7$ & $2w+12$ & 1 & 2\\
A+B$_R$ & $2w+11$ & $2w+14$ & 0 & 1\\
A+B$_L$ & $2w+11$ & $2w+16$ & 0 & 1\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Zero decrement has duration $2p+7$ and preserves the whole file for
all six rows.  Marking is also unchanged.  The macro induction checker
and the complete 8,391-case affine instruction checker succeed for
all five modifications listed after the original.

\subsection{Scope of the resulting optimization search}

Each neutral variant was used as a fresh base for state reduction.
The finite discovery tests permitted translated dispatch frames,
header gaps at least 40, the primary values of the specified finite
sample suite, and arbitrary valid nonempty spare suffixes.  Visits
were recomputed on each new base, so an old trace was not used to
exclude newly reachable transitions.

Across the five bases, 597,334 mixed-row state-identification recipes
were tested: one state is removed, incoming edges are redirected to
a retained state, and one row of the removed state supplies a row of
the retained state.  All five searches returned zero survivors.
Uniform state identifications likewise had zero survivors.  Searches
of every single incoming edge to a state, together with selected
multiple-incoming edges, also failed to produce a smaller candidate.
These bounded searches do not establish minimality.  Their purpose
is to distinguish the certified neutral transformations from a
state-count improvement, which has not been obtained by this route.
