CONSTRUCTOR-ONLY PRESENTATION OF THE EXACT C254 CALCULUS
======================================================

Status: an ordinary mathematical specification and proof sketch, accompanied
by a Python reference checker and finite controls. This is not a new Lean
theorem, PA internalization, binary codec, compiled machine or state bound.

1. Normal terms
---------------
Let NTerm consist of Z and B_t(a,b), for t in {0,1,2,3} and a,b in NTerm,
excluding B_0(Z,Z). Define normalized constructors b_t by the single reduction
b_0(Z,Z)=Z; in every other case b_t(a,b)=B_t(a,b). No logical equivalences are
used as rewrite rules. In particular, Z and B_0(B_1(Z,Z),B_1(Z,Z)) are distinct
terms, even though their associated C254 formulas can both express truths.

For the Cantor pairing P(a,b)=(a+b)(a+b+1)/2+b, define denotations

  val(Z) = 0,
  val(B_t(a,b)) = 4 P(val(a),val(b)) + t.

This is an effective bijection from NTerm to Nat. First, the only normal term
of value zero is Z: value zero forces t=0 and both children to have value zero,
and B_0(Z,Z) is excluded. Equality of positive denotations determines the same
tag modulo four, then the same two child denotations by injectivity of P.
Induction gives equality of normal terms. For surjectivity, given n>0 write
n=4P(a,b)+t. Both a and b are less than n, so induction constructs their normal
terms. The resulting B_t is allowed because the excluded case would mean n=0.
The zero case is Z. The inverse uses pairing only in this mathematical argument
or in an optional producer, never in the ground-term verifier.

Normalized construction therefore satisfies, for every a,b,t,

  val(b_t(a,b)) = 4 P(val(a),val(b)) + t.

The distinguished falsity term is F=B_1(Z,Z), with val(F)=1.

2. Exact transported rules
--------------------------
Interpret wim, wel, wal and weq as b_0, b_1, b_2 and b_3 respectively. Define
wn(a)=wim(a,F), wa(a,b)=wn(wim(a,wn(b))) and
wex(a,b)=wn(wal(a,wn(b))). The eight rho templates and the bundle template are
the exact ASTs extracted from the pinned Code254.lean; they are not shortened,
replaced or interpreted semantically.

The twelve rules are truth Z; modus ponens from a and wim(a,b) to b;
generalization from a to wal(v,a); eight rho template families; and the bundle
family with its two side conditions c != a and c != b. All metavariables range
over normal ground terms. Since val is injective, these two term inequalities
are exactly the original inequalities on natural-number codes. Thus the rules
commute with val, including their side conditions.

Induction on derivations in each direction yields the ordinary equivalence

  CT254(t)  iff  C254(val(t)).

In particular, CT254(F) iff C254(1). This transports the same fixed calculus;
it does not supply a separate proof of its existing links to ZF or ZFC. Those
links and the distinction between external reasoning and formal repository
endpoints remain as described in the report.

3. Finite certificates
-----------------------
A certificate has a topological arena and a topological list of proof rows.
Arena node zero is Z. Every later node contains a constructor tag and two
earlier node IDs. Duplicate constructor keys and B_0(0,0) are forbidden. By
induction on nodes and injectivity above, two arena IDs denote the same natural
number iff their IDs agree. All proof arguments are arena IDs, not natural
formula codes. Premise references are to earlier proof rows.

The verifier freezes the arena before evaluating the fixed rule templates:
every required constructor must already be present. It checks exact rule
arity, the two bundle gates, the modus-ponens constructor identity, and each
claimed conclusion ID. Failing to include a needed term causes rejection.
The final root must be the fixed F term unless the caller explicitly requests
a different target for a control. A goal field in the certificate is ignored.

Soundness follows by induction on proof rows, unfolding any shared proof DAG
into a finite derivation. Completeness follows by interning all subterms of a
finite CT254 derivation, including intermediate fixed-template constructions,
and then listing its proof steps in postorder. This produces the required
unique arena and backward premise references. Thus, at the specified calculus
level, existence of a default-target certificate is equivalent to C254(1).
The Python implementation is supported by ordinary inspection and the finite
controls in check_term254.py; these arguments have not been mechanized for it.

4. What is and is not removed
-----------------------------
The ground verifier uses constructor keys, bounded reference comparisons and
fixed template expansion. It does not evaluate a term's natural denotation,
decode numeric literals, pair or unpair denotations, parse old raw proof codes,
reconstruct proof quotes, or call either previous certificate verifier. Python
still uses integers for finite indices and rule tags; 'constructor-only' does
not claim the implementation has no counters or index manipulation.

The adapter from accepted canonical certificates replaces each numeric argument
with its certified normal-term root, then drops the literal and quote records.
The adapter and certificate generator are deliberately outside the ground
verifier. The finite audit covers all twelve rule tags, both bundle gates,
corruption rejection and deep generalization while disabling the arithmetic
and producer helpers. Positive test goals are ordinary derived formulas, not F;
all of them are also checked to reject at the fixed falsity target.

The abstract finite-certificate relation is effective. To turn it into a small
blank-tape consistency machine, the next work is a binary term/proof codec,
an exhaustive enumerator, concrete routines for canonical sharing or structural
comparison, a compiler/state budget and a formal equivalence proof. The Python
dictionary is not free finite control. A representation that minimizes arena
size can require more lookup states than a duplicated tree representation.
No numerical improvement over an existing Turing-machine state bound is
claimed for this front end.
