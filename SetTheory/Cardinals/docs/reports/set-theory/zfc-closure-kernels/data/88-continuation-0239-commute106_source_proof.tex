\section{Independent certificate for the color-106 source commutation}
\label{sec:commute106-independent}

The transformation in this section changes the register program while
preserving its behavior from the root.  It produces another 246-state
binary candidate; it does not by itself remove a working state.
Its source proof is a finite-region argument, rather than equality of
the old and new instruction graphs indexed by depth-first occurrence.

Write $N(A,B)$ for a cons node in the source decoder.  Let
\[
 A=\mathrm{CI}(7),\qquad
 B=N\bigl(\mathrm{CD}(6),N(\mathrm{CI}(1),\mathrm{CC}(2))\bigr).
\]
The proposed source transformation replaces $N(A,B)$ by $N(B,A)$.
In the original shared decoder this is node 48, whose children are
terminal 18 and node 47; its physical decoder color is old state 106.

\begin{lemma}[The transfer region is total]
For every vector $R\in\mathbb N^9$, execution of $B$ from its entry
exits normally after exactly $3R_6+1$ primitive instructions.  Its final
register vector $S$ satisfies
\[
 S_1=R_1+R_6,\qquad S_6=0,\qquad
 S_j=R_j\quad(j\notin\{1,6\}).
\]
It cannot halt or escape by a break or continue.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The positive branch of $\mathrm{CD}(6)$ decreases $R_6$ and reaches
$\mathrm{CI}(1)$, followed by $\mathrm{CC}(2)$ and a return to the
same decrement.  The zero branch exits the region normally.  Induction
on the initial value of $R_6$ proves the claim: the zero case takes one
instruction; a positive case takes three instructions before invoking
the induction hypothesis with $R_6-1$ and $R_1+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact local commutation]
Both $N(A,B)$ and $N(B,A)$ terminate normally on every natural register
vector after exactly $3R_6+2$ primitive instructions.  In either order
the resulting vector is
\[
 R'_7=R_7+1,\qquad R'_1=R_1+R_6,\qquad R'_6=0,
 \qquad R'_j=R_j\ (j\notin\{1,6,7\}).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The increment of register 7 does not affect either register read or
written by $B$.  Conversely, $B$ preserves register 7.  Apply the lemma
and count the additional increment instruction.
\end{proof}

The general closed-component rule also applies: the escape bounds of
both components are $(0,0)$, their register footprints are respectively
$\{7\}$ and $\{1,6\}$, and neither contains a halt instruction.  Here
the explicit totality proof gives a stronger, shorter argument.  Merely
requiring closedness and disjoint footprints would not justify swapping
arbitrary halt-containing components: a halt and a diverging component
can change halting behavior when exchanged.

\subsection{Complete occurrence and control-flow audit}

The independent verifier parses the literal \texttt{Prog254.v} source
and the emitted candidate DAG directly.  It imports neither the optimizer
nor its transformation or source-equivalence checker.  Its path operations
are an independent implementation of consumed-path binary increment,
break, and continue, including the implicit zero suffix on decoding.

There are 6,459 literal internal nodes and 6,460 instruction occurrences.
The verifier establishes all of the following finite facts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every structural difference between the literal source and the
candidate is exactly the displayed swap.  It occurs in 242 pairwise
disjoint unfolded regions.  All other instructions and operands are
matched occurrence by occurrence.
\item Each old region has the control edges
$A\to D$, $D_+\to I$, $I\to C$, $C\to D$, with $D_0$ reaching the
normal exit.  Each new region has
$D_+\to I$, $I\to C$, $C\to D$, $D_0\to A$, with $A$ reaching its
normal exit.  Here $D=\mathrm{CD}(6)$, $I=\mathrm{CI}(1)$, and
$C=\mathrm{CC}(2)$.
\item No edge from outside a region enters its interior.  The only
external entry is $A$ in the old program and $D$ in the new program.
Thus the local theorem is never incorrectly applied to a computation
that starts halfway through the exchanged sequence.
\item The normal exits correspond under the boundary map.  All 7,180
guarded edges outside the regions correspond exactly under that map,
and the initial root controls correspond.  Both root escape bounds are
zero.  The unique halt instruction lies outside every region.
\end{enumerate}
The candidate has 203 reachable shared pair nodes and 23 reachable
terminal entries.  These are source counts, not a binary state claim.
The complete check and the 242 occurrence records are emitted by
\texttt{check\_commutation.py} into
\texttt{commute106\_source\_audit.json}.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-source equivalence]
For every natural initial register vector, the literal source and the
color-106 source have the same halting behavior from the root.  Whenever
they halt, their final register vectors and total primitive-instruction
counts agree.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Contract each audited four-instruction region to one boundary transition
with the total register transformation in the corollary and cost
$3R_6+2$.  Unique entry and matching exit justify contraction in each
program.  The checked boundary correspondence identifies every
contracted transition, every remaining guarded primitive transition,
and the halt instruction.  Induction on a finite contracted computation
therefore transfers its full sequence of boundary register vectors and
its cost in either direction.  A finite halting computation cannot end
inside a region, because the region contains no halt.  Conversely, each
contracted region step expands to a finite concrete computation by the
totality lemma.  Thus contraction and expansion preserve and reflect
finite halting computations, including their final vectors and costs.
Negating finite halting gives nonhalting equivalence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Separate binary obligations}

The two binary row edits are
\[
 (106,0)\mapsto(0,\mathrm R,122),\qquad
 (106,1)\mapsto(1,\mathrm R,93).
\]
The table still has 246 working states, 492 defined rows, and one edge
to halting label 254.  The independently implemented dense blank-tape
replay reaches, after 410,735 steps, working state 96 and head position
$-5$, with exactly the full reference dispatch frame: marker $-6$,
header 176, physical primary values $(0,0,0,3,0,0,3,0,0)$, and 167 spare
blocks of value one.  The header distance is 181 and the tape has
exactly 351 ones.  Both edited rows occur during that prefix; the result
is established by literal replay, not by an assertion that its transition
trace was unchanged.

The separate symbolic binary audit reports all 8,391 guarded cases for
arbitrary natural primaries, arbitrary nonempty valid spare suffixes,
and header distance at least 64.  Together with its inductive register
macros and the exact bootstrap, that supplies the binary simulation
obligations for this 246-state derivative.  The checks are ordinary
finite and induction certificates, not a serialized proof-assistant
endpoint.  No sub-246 result follows from the source commutation alone.

\subsection{Composition with later source reassociations}

After the commutation, subsequent accepted rotations can be checked by a
stronger and simpler finite certificate.  Number instruction occurrences
in depth-first order in both the commuted seed and the rotated candidate.
For each occurrence, compare its operation and operand and every guarded
successor occurrence computed by the consumed-path interpreter.  Check
also the initial occurrence.  If all comparisons succeed, equality of the
occurrence index and register vector is an exact one-instruction
bisimulation between those two source programs.  Its halting equivalence
composes with the region-contraction theorem above; no assumption that
every intermediate rotation was semantically admissible is needed.

The independent checker \texttt{check\_reassociated\_graph.py} validates
all 6,460 primitive labels and operands and all 8,390 guarded successor
edges in this way.  It confirms the following two retained candidates
against the commuted seed.
