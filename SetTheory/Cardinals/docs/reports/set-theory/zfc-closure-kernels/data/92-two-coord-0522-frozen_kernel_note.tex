\begin{abstract}
We give an ordinary mathematical consistency calibration for a finite
universal theory with one constant, six proper functions, two non-equality
binary predicates, thirteen clauses and twenty-six literals.  A fourteenth
clause makes its membership reduct extensional.  The construction uses a
class operator that freezes all matrix parameters except two selected
coordinates.  The existing freeze object also supplies the universal
quantifier's counterexample witness through an assignment coordinate.
This second sharing step is justified by a rich coding construction in a
countable set-coded ZFC model; it is not justified by an arbitrary function
identification.  The thirteen-clause theory has a 1515-bit equality-completed
template in the same flat format in which the previous nine-function FC15*
payload has 1616 bits.  Its native template is larger, 1040 rather than
881 bits.  These are explicit finite-theory and data results.  No binary
machine, state reduction, proof-assistant theorem, or internal PA endpoint
is asserted for this frontend.
\end{abstract}

\section{Inherited interface and the exact new signature}

The assignment/class method is attributed to Friedman's \emph{Impossible
Counting}, Section 4.1.  In particular his axiom K[1,5] already has a unary
projection operation freezing parameters; $F$ below is its two-coordinate
assignment-pullback variant.  His Theorem 4.1.3 gives the countable
definable-class expansion.  ProveIt's source44 supplied the FC15*/FC16* finite
closure kernels and their ordinary ZFC consistency calibration.  We retain
their exact empty-seeded, subset-bounded, globally supported closure
interface.  The contribution here is their particular six-function
combination, including shared witness coordinates, and its measured
encoding.  It does not weaken that interface or claim priority for
projection/class coding in general.

All variables range over one nonempty domain with native equality.  There
is one constant $d$, six total functions
\[
 V:2,\quad C:3,\quad I:2,\quad A:2,\quad U:2,\quad F:2,
\]
and two binary predicates $E,\Inc$.  The symbol $q$ is always the literal
term $I(d,d)$; it is not a second constant.  Define the following term
abbreviations:
\begin{align*}
 P(a,b)&=C(C(d,d,a),q,b),\\
 J(s,j,a)&=V(F(P(s,j),a),q),\\
 B(b,s)&=C(C(b,d,V(s,d)),q,V(s,q)).
\end{align*}
In particular $J$ is not an additional function symbol.

We also use finite class-term abbreviations, expanded in the supplied JSON
and bit streams:
\begin{align*}
 \Neg{a}&=I(a,d),\\
 \ClassAnd{a}{b}&=I(I(a,I(b,d)),d),\\
 \Or{a}{b}&=I(I(a,d),b),\\
 \Ex{j}{a}&=I(U(j,I(a,d)),d).
\end{align*}
For an auxiliary selector $r$, put
\begin{align*}
 S_r(t)&=U\bigl(d,\Ex{r}{U(q,I(t,U(d,I(A(d,q),A(d,r)))))}\bigr),\\
 E_0&=U(q,I(A(q,d),d)),\\
 K_r(t)&=U\bigl(d,U(q,I(\Or{E_0}{A(d,r)},I(t,A(q,r))))\bigr),\\
 L_r(t)&=U(q,I(A(q,r),\Ex{d}{t})),\\
 T_r(t)&=I\bigl(S_r(t),\Ex{r}{\ClassAnd{K_r(t)}{L_r(t)}}\bigr).
\end{align*}
The scopes deliberately reuse $d$ inside the subset test and $q$ inside
the emptiness test.  Those inner scopes do not include the matrix $t$.
Only one auxiliary selector, distinct from $d,q$, is needed.

\section{The thirteen universal clauses}

Every displayed row is a universally quantified clause.  Repeated variable
substitutions are allowed.  The finite theory $\FC$ consists of exactly:
\begin{align}
 &V(C(s,j,z),j)=z,\\
 &k=j\ \lor\ V(C(s,j,z),k)=V(s,k),\\
 &\neg\Inc(s,I(a,b))\ \lor\ \neg\Inc(s,a)\ \lor\ \Inc(s,b),\\
 &\Inc(s,I(a,b))\ \lor\ \Inc(s,a),\\
 &\Inc(s,I(a,b))\ \lor\ \neg\Inc(s,b),\\
 &\neg\Inc(s,d),\\
 &\neg\Inc(s,A(j,k))\ \lor\ E(V(s,j),V(s,k)),\\
 &\Inc(s,A(j,k))\ \lor\ \neg E(V(s,j),V(s,k)),\\
 &\neg\Inc(s,U(j,a))\ \lor\ \Inc(C(s,j,z),a),\\
 &\Inc(s,U(j,a))\ \lor\ \neg\Inc(C(s,j,J(s,j,a)),a),\\
 &\neg\Inc(s,F(b,t))\ \lor\ \Inc(B(b,s),t),\\
 &\Inc(s,F(b,t))\ \lor\ \neg\Inc(B(b,s),t),\\
 &r=d\ \lor\ r=q\ \lor\ \Inc(b,T_r(F(b,t))).
\end{align}
Their literal counts are
$1,2,3,2,2,1,2,2,2,2,2,2,3$, totaling $26$.
At most four local universal variables occur in a row.  The maximum
expanded term depth is $15$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact core and selector supply]
The first ten clauses imply the exact implication, falsity, membership
atom and universal-quantifier laws.  Moreover $d\ne q$, $P$ is injective
in both arguments, and there exist arbitrarily many pairwise distinct
selector values avoiding $d,q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The three implication clauses give
$\Inc(s,I(a,b))\leftrightarrow(\Inc(s,a)\to\Inc(s,b))$.
Clause (6) makes $d$ false at every assignment, while $q=I(d,d)$ is true
at every assignment.  Hence $d\ne q$.  The two atom clauses are exactly
their stated biconditional.  Clauses (9)--(10) give
\[
 \Inc(s,U(j,a))\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \forall z\,\Inc(C(s,j,z),a).
\]
For the right-to-left implication, a false left side would supply the
counterexample $J(s,j,a)$ by clause (10).

The two assignment laws and $d\ne q$ give
$V(P(a,b),d)=a$ and $V(P(a,b),q)=b$, proving injectivity.
Set $a_0=P(q,d)$ and $a_{n+1}=P(d,a_n)$ for each external finite $n$.
The map $P(d,-)$ is injective and $a_0$ is outside its range by the first
projection.  Thus the $a_n$ are pairwise distinct.  Among any $m+2$ such
values at most two equal $d,q$, giving the required finite selector supply.
This does not assert that a predetermined $a_n$ always avoids $d,q$.
\end{proof}

\section{Why freezing removes the capture problem}

For fixed $b,t$, write
\[
 R_{b,t}(x,y)=\Inc(C(C(b,d,x),q,y),t).
\]
Clauses (11)--(12) give the exact identity
\[
 \Inc(s,F(b,t))\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 R_{b,t}(V(s,d),V(s,q)).
\]
Thus this class depends on only two assignment coordinates, even if the
original class $t$ depends on other selectors, including $r$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact wrapper]
If $r\notin\{d,q\}$, the final incidence in clause (13) holds precisely
when the following implication holds for $R=R_{b,t}$:
\begin{align*}
 &[\forall x\,\exists u\,\forall y\,
       (R(x,y)\to\forall z(E(z,y)\to E(z,u)))]\\
 &\qquad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists c\,\bigl[\forall x,y\,
       (((\forall z\,\neg E(z,x))\lor E(x,c))\land R(x,y)
         \to E(y,c))\\
 &\hspace{53mm}\land\ \forall y(E(y,c)\to\exists x\,R(x,y))\bigr].
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the exact core laws and the freeze identity to expand the five
class-term abbreviations.  In $S_r$ the inner $U(d,-)$ is confined to the
subset test, so the antecedent matrix keeps the outer input value.
In $E_0$ the local $U(q,-)$ is confined to the emptiness test, so the matrix
in $K_r$ retains its outer output value.  Both scopes preserve the hull or
bound coordinate $r$.  The two occurrences binding $r$ are in disjoint
antecedent/consequent scopes.  Every displayed quantified clause is
therefore exactly the indicated closure clause; no freshness assumption
about the original class $t$ is needed.
\end{proof}

Deleting $F$ and directly using $t$ in the wrapper is unsound.  For example,
if the original class depends only on coordinate $r$, quantifying over $r$
changes that class.  In a two-value assignment diagnostic, a class true
precisely when the $r$ coordinate is $1$ has false universal $r$-closure,
whereas its frozen version at a base with $r=1$ has true universal closure.
This is a capture counterexample, not a finite model of the whole theory.

\begin{proposition}[Full lower interface]
The $E$-reduct of every $\FC$ model satisfies every raw equality-free EPSC
instance, with unrestricted finite matrix parameters and quantifiers.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any fixed finite equality-free $E$-formula, use the selector supply to
choose distinct labels for all its free and bound names, assigning $d,q$
to its input and output.  The exact core laws represent its atoms,
implications, falsity and universal quantifiers by a class term.
Successive $C$ updates set each of its finitely many parameter coordinates
to the desired values while preserving the others.  This gives an
assignment $b$ and class $t$ representing the entire matrix as $R_{b,t}$.
Choose any $r$ avoiding $d,q$; clause (13) and the wrapper lemma give its
exact EPSC instance.  The original matrix may use this same label $r$:
its coordinate is frozen in $b$, so there is no capture.
\end{proof}

\section{Rich class coding justifies the shared witness}

The less compressed intermediate theory has an independent ternary $W$
in place of $J$ in clause (10).  A $\FC$ model expands to such a model by
$W(s,j,a)=J(s,j,a)$ immediately.  The converse is not obtained by changing
$W$ in an arbitrary model: the required incidence classes might lack
representatives with the needed assignment coordinate.  We now construct
a model with precisely this richness.

\begin{lemma}[Class codes in every coordinate fiber]
Work in ZFC.  Let $M$ be a countably infinite set-coded model of ZFC.
Choose distinct domain elements $d,q$, and interpret $V,C$ by the common
normalization to set-coded partial function graphs specified below, with
default value $d$.  There exists a class coding $c\mapsto D_c$ onto all externally
pure-membership-definable subsets of $M$ such that
\[
 D_d=\varnothing,\qquad D_q=M,\qquad
 \forall D\,\forall w\,\exists c\;(D_c=D\ \land\ V(c,q)=w).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The set $\mathcal D$ of externally definable classes with finite
$M$-parameter tuples is countable.  Each fiber
$B_w=\{c:V(c,q)=w\}$ is countably infinite: the objects $P(v,w)$, as
$v$ ranges over $M$, lie in it and are distinct by their $d$ coordinates.
Removing the at most two designated objects $d,q$ leaves each fiber
countably infinite.  On that remainder choose a surjection onto
$\mathcal D$, independently for each fiber.  Add the two prescribed class
codes and assign arbitrary definable classes to any unused objects.
The fibers partition the domain, so these choices are compatible.
All choices occur in the ambient ZFC construction; no internal truth
predicate for $M$ is asserted.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Explicit membership-language normalization}

Here is a fixed first-order definition of the two operations.  The class
family permits logical equality; ``pure membership'' excludes the added
function and predicate symbols, not logical equality.  If equality-free
definitions are preferred, Extensionality in $M$ replaces $x=y$ by
$\forall z(E(z,x)\leftrightarrow E(z,y))$, with fresh binders.
To specify the graph definitions, write
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{Sing}(u,x)&\equiv\forall v(v\in u\leftrightarrow v=x),\\
 \operatorname{Dbl}(u,x,y)&\equiv\forall v(v\in u\leftrightarrow(v=x\lor v=y)),\\
 \operatorname{KP}(p,x,y)&\equiv
 \exists u\,\exists v\,
 (\operatorname{Sing}(u,x)\land\operatorname{Dbl}(v,x,y)
 \land\operatorname{Dbl}(p,u,v)).
\end{align*}
The graph-entry predicate is
$\operatorname{Entry}(g,x,y)\equiv\exists p(p\in g\land\operatorname{KP}(p,x,y))$.
Let $\operatorname{PF}(g)$ assert that every member of $g$ is a Kuratowski
pair, and
\[
 \forall x,y,z\;
  (\operatorname{Entry}(g,x,y)\land\operatorname{Entry}(g,x,z)\to y=z).
\]
Put
\[
 \Gamma(s,g)\equiv
 (\operatorname{PF}(s)\land g=s)\lor
 (\neg\operatorname{PF}(s)\land\forall p\,p\notin g).
\]
For fixed default $d$, the graph of $V$ is the formula
\[
 \exists g\;\Gamma(s,g)\land
 \bigl[\operatorname{Entry}(g,j,z)\lor
  (z=d\land\neg\exists y\,\operatorname{Entry}(g,j,y))\bigr].
\]
The graph of $C(s,j,z)=c$ is
\begin{align*}
 \exists g\;\Gamma(s,g)\land\forall p\;[p\in c\leftrightarrow
  (\operatorname{KP}(p,j,z)\lor
   (p\in g\land\neg\exists y\,\operatorname{KP}(p,j,y)))].
\end{align*}
Extensionality gives the uniqueness of the empty normal form and updated
graph; Pairing, Separation and Union give their existence.  The graph
functionality condition gives the unique $V$ value.  Updating preserves
$\operatorname{PF}$ and directly proves both assignment equations,
including the case of an invalid input $s$.  Finiteness of the graph is
unnecessary.  Externally finite patterns are represented by finitely many
updates starting from the empty graph.

In an ill-founded or $\omega$-nonstandard model $M$ these formulas mean
$M$-satisfaction, not the external assertion that the members of an
$M$-object form a genuine well-founded graph.  Since ZFC proves existence
and uniqueness for these fixed formulas, $M$ satisfies those theorems.
Native equality then makes each formula select one actual domain element
for each actual input tuple.  This defines external total operations on
the set-sized carrier and supplies all identities used above.  No
absoluteness of $M$'s finiteness or well-foundedness is assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Upper model construction]
ZFC proves $\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})\to\Con(\FC)$.  The same holds for the
extensional fourteen-clause variant defined below.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Completeness and the countable-model theorem provide such an $M$ from
$\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})$.  Interpret $\Inc(s,c)$ as $s\in D_c$ using rich
coding.  Implication of two definable classes, membership of two $V$
values, and the universal preimage under $C$ are again pure-$E$-definable.
Choose $I,A,U$ to name those classes, stipulating $I(d,d)=q$; the latter
is possible since $D_q=M$.  This order of choices removes any apparent
circularity in the syntactic definition of $q$.

For each $(b,t)$, the frozen class
\[
 D^{\rm fr}_{b,t}=\{s:C(C(b,d,V(s,d)),q,V(s,q))\in D_t\}
\]
is pure-$E$-definable.  If $b=P(s,j)$, the pair $(s,j)$ is unique.  When
some $z$ falsifies $\Inc(C(s,j,z),t)$ choose one such $z$ as $w$;
otherwise choose any $w$.  If $b$ has no such pair representation also
choose any $w$.  Richness provides a class code $c$ for $D^{\rm fr}_{b,t}$
with $V(c,q)=w$.  Set $F(b,t)=c$.

This satisfies both freeze clauses and the counterexample-witness clause
simultaneously.  There is no incompatible collision: the common key is
exactly $(b,t)$, and injectivity of $P$ makes its potential witness request
unique.  The incidence class and the coordinate value are independent
requirements precisely because of the rich coding lemma.

For each fixed $b,t$, the relation $R_{b,t}$ is represented in $M$ by an
ordinary finite pure-membership formula.  ZFC satisfies its EPSC instance:
under the local subset bound each fiber is separated inside a power set,
and its exact value is unique; Replacement and iteration over internal
$\omega$ then produce the positive supported closure from the empty set.
This is instancewise formula reasoning inside $M$, not expanded-language
comprehension involving $F$ or $\Inc$.  The wrapper lemma now proves
clause (13).  Since $M$ is extensional, the additional row below also holds.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Which syntax universe is used}

All uses of $\operatorname{Def}(M)$ refer to finite formulas in the
\emph{ambient} ZFC proof's natural-number syntax, evaluated by its
set-coded satisfaction relation for $M$.  They do not quantify over
formula codes in $M$'s own possibly nonstandard $\omega$.  In an external
description these are ordinary finite formulas.  When the entire argument
is read in another ambient model of ZFC, ``finite formula'' and
``satisfaction'' have that ambient model's ordinary internal meanings.
The proof is uniform in that syntax and does not assume the ambient
model is externally $\omega$-standard either.

The required uniformity is elementary template substitution.  Given a
formula code $\rho(x,y,\bar p)$, capture-safe renaming and substitution
produce the subset bound and supported-closure wrapper.  A single finite
ZF proof template, with $\rho$ as a relation placeholder, derives its EPSC
instance.  For every input $x$, its exact output fiber is separated inside
$\mathcal P(b)$ for any local subset bound $b$.  Extensionality makes that
fiber unique independently of the chosen bound.  Replacement collects
these unique fibers on any set.  The definable step
\[
 c\longmapsto c\cup\bigcup\{\{y:\rho(x,y,\bar p)\}:x\in c\}
\]
is functional.  The ordinary recursion proof on internal $\omega$, another
fixed formula-indexed template, starts with the exact empty-input fiber
and forms the union of its stages.  Induction gives recurrent closure
and global output support.  Every occurrence of Separation and
Replacement in this template becomes an actual ZF schema instance after
substitution of $\rho$; no schema for the expanded $\Inc,F$ language is used.

Consequently ambient ZFC proves uniformly that every set-coded ZF model
satisfies every one of these ambient-formula EPSC instances.  This is why
choosing a defining formula for each $D_t$ is sufficient even when $M$
is nonstandard.  There is no unrestricted internal truth predicate for
the ambient universe, and no reflection from a prover's metatheory.

\subsection*{Finite proof transport and formalization scope}

For the lower direction, a finite source proof uses finitely many EPSC
matrices.  Each is compiled recursively to a class term, with a fresh
finite selector list; the assignment identities and the wrapper lemma
give a finite derivation of its instance.  Native-equality elimination
in the extensional control similarly replaces each equality atom by its
incoming-profile formula with a fresh label.  The existing EPSC-to-ZF
and Foundation/Choice derivation transports then apply to a finite
refutation.  This supplies ordinary effective finite proof transport,
as well as the semantic consistency argument.

For the upper direction the set-coded model construction, countability,
rich coding, and satisfaction arguments are all carried out inside
ordinary ZFC.  First-order soundness converts the constructed model into
the statement that no finite proof of contradiction exists.  Thus the
displayed consistency equivalences have the ambient ZFC meaning requested
for the machine project.  This note does not give a serialized proof-code
compiler, prove its represented correctness in PA, or claim that an
external proof-assistant theorem already yields that arithmetic endpoint.

\section{An extensional control with no extra function}

For class terms put
\[
 \operatorname{Xor}(p,q')=I(I(p,q'),I(I(q',p),d)).
\]
Its incidence is exclusive disjunction by the implication/falsity laws.
Add to $\FC$ the single clause
\[
 j=d\ \lor\ j=q\ \lor\ a=b\ \lor\
 \Inc\left(P(a,b),\Ex{j}{
 \operatorname{Xor}(A(j,d),A(j,q))}\right).
\]
Call the result $\FCE$.  If $j$ avoids $d,q$, the final literal says
exactly that some object belongs to precisely one of $a,b$.  The selector
supply provides such a $j$, so the row implies ordinary Extensionality.
Conversely Extensionality implies the row, including all colliding-selector
cases covered by its guards.  It is therefore an exact core-relative
Extensionality formulation.

\begin{theorem}[Consistency calibration and its dependencies]
Using the existing ProveIt ordinary calibration of full raw EPSC and of
Extensionality plus full EPSC, ZFC proves
\[
 \Con(\FC)\ \longleftrightarrow\ \Con(\mathrm{ZFC}),\qquad
 \Con(\FCE)\ \longleftrightarrow\ \Con(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper implications are proved above.  The lower interface proposition
and source44's inherited raw-EPSC calibration give the first lower
implication.  For the second, every $\FCE$ model has an extensional
$E$-reduct satisfying full EPSC, hence ZF without Foundation or Choice by
the accepted direct EPSC bootstrap.  Its existing Foundation and
constructibility consistency transports give $\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})$.
The extensional route does not use the hereditary-saturation interpretation
needed for the raw route.  These named prior results are dependencies, not
new proof-assistant results of this note.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact costs, tests, and an executable compiler target}

The supplied standard-library Python file emits literal expanded clauses,
encodes and independently parses the same flat template convention as
source44, and reports exact metrics.  In that convention a clause length
uses three bits, each literal uses three control bits, and each term node
uses four bits; three zero bits terminate the stream.  Equality completion
adds reflexivity, left-Euclidean equality, one congruence clause per retained
function and one transport clause per non-equality predicate.  Padded
function opcodes receive no congruence after passage to the retained reduct.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\toprule
Theory & Func. & Clauses & Literals & Term nodes & Bits & Bytes\\
\midrule
FC15* native (source44) &9&15&35&182&881&111\\
FC15* completed &9&29&82&320&1616&202\\
$\FC$ native &6&13&26&230&1040&130\\
$\FC$ completed &6&23&57&318&1515&190\\
FC16* native (source44) &9&16&39&230&1088&136\\
FC16* completed &9&30&86&368&1823&228\\
$\FCE$ native &6&14&30&274&1231&154\\
$\FCE$ completed &6&24&61&362&1706&214\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The raw completed stream saves 101 bits and six clauses; the extensional
completed stream saves 117 bits and six clauses.  A frontend that already
handles equality by quotient labels instead of explicit congruence clauses
must price the \emph{native} rows, which grow by 159 and 143 bits respectively.
Thus the encoding benefit depends on the checker architecture.  The term
depth increases from the old shallow kernel to 15; tree-grounding stages can
become much more expensive.  Neither improvement nor regression in these
metrics determines the final binary-state count.

Six proper functions fit a radix-eight ground term codec, with two padded
unary constructor tags.  The old nine-function code needed radix sixteen.
For a tuple pairing $\pi_k$ with each child no larger than its tuple code,
use $0$ for $d$ and
\[
 \operatorname{code}(f_i(t_1,\ldots,t_k))
 =1+i+8\pi_k(\operatorname{code}(t_1),\ldots,\operatorname{code}(t_k)).
\]
This is a total padded ground-term enumeration with children strictly
smaller than parents.  A concrete compiler target is the existing
finite-quotient frontend with these six retained tags, two predicate tables,
the literal native rows above, and congruence checks only on retained tags.
Every colliding substitution and every finite quotient labeling must remain
eligible.  Compactness and the retained term-algebra quotient give the same
ideal inconsistency semidecision theorem as the existing frontend.

The included finite checks compare the class wrapper with an independently
written direct EPSC predicate for all 260 pairs of arbitrary binary $E,R$
tables on carriers of size one or two, both before and after the safe binder
reuse.  They also check 4096 freezing-support diagnostics and an explicit
unfrozen capture counterexample.  These carriers are not models of the
infinite assignment/class core.  Every generated payload round-trips through
its parser; malformed truncation controls are rejected.  These finite checks
support the transcription and codec; the unbounded consistency statement
rests on the ordinary proofs above.

The separate executable \texttt{frozen\_frontend.py} realizes a complete
ground-clause reference using explicit equality completion, rather than
the suggested quotient-label implementation.  At finite stage $n$ it
instantiates every local variable by every term code $0,\ldots,n$, retaining
endpoint terms beyond that frontier, and runs exact iterative DPLL.  It
imposes no artificial total-function interpretation on the bounded
frontier.  Its \texttt{--whole-search} loop halts only at an exhausted UNSAT
stage and catches no resource exception as a logical answer.  Fairness
covers every ground instance; compactness followed by the retained-language
equality quotient gives its ideal inconsistency equivalence.  A finite-stage
SAT valuation is a propositional certificate, not a model of the whole kernel.
The default $n=1$ checks return SAT for both targets, with 300/308 clauses,
259/267 atoms, and maximum endpoint bit length 63197.  The latter is the
bit length of a deeply nested ground term, despite the tiny substitution
frontier; it is not a 63197-bit template or a TM state count.  The reference
also passes 130 exhaustive small-CNF comparisons and 4096 radix-eight
encode/decode round trips.

No complete RM or TM compilation is supplied.  Template emission, ground
term traversal, equality/partition checking, Boolean valuation, outer-stage
enumeration, and blank initialization remain to be charged.  A small
signature and a 190-byte completed stream do not establish a machine with
fewer than 245 states.
