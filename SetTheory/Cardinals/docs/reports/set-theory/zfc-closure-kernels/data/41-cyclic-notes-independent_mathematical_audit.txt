1. Nine-equation cyclic presentation: accepted
--------------------------------------------
Assume the cited three-equation Boolean-algebra basis over join and
complement. Add the following equations, with all displayed variables
universally quantified:

  x + c(x) = c(x)
  c(x+y) = c(x)+c(y)
  c(-c(x)) = -c(x)
  r(x+y) = r(x)+r(y)
  r(r(r(x))) = x
  c(r(c(r(r(x))))) = r(c(r(r(c(x))))) .

The last equation is [c, r c r^2]=0, in composition notation. Let

  c0=c,  c1=r c r^2,  c2=r^2 c r.

The implementation uses the equivalent shorter row

  c(r(c(x))) = r(c(r(r(c(r(x)))))).

Composing its argument with r^2 and applying r^3=id gives the displayed
commutator; conversely substitute r(x) into the commutator. The implemented
row has 11 tree nodes over both sides, versus 12 for the direct form.

Because r^3=id, r is bijective with inverse r^2. Join preservation makes it
an order isomorphism: monotonicity is immediate, and order reflection follows
by applying r^2. Every order isomorphism of Boolean algebras preserves zero,
one, meets, and complements. Thus r is a Boolean automorphism; complement
preservation does not need to be an additional axiom.

Idempotence of c already follows from the third closure equation and Boolean
double negation. Apply that equation at -c(x):

  c(-c(-c(x))) = -c(-c(x)).

Replacing c(-c(x)) by -c(x) gives c(c(x))=c(x). Conjugation by a Boolean
automorphism preserves all four usual complemented-closure equations.
Conjugating [c0,c1]=0 by r gives [c1,c2]=0; conjugating again gives
[c2,c0]=0. Hence every cyclic algebra has a Df3 reduct.

For the converse preservation of EQUATIONAL VALIDITY, take any Df3 algebra
(A,+,-,f,g,h) and use A^3, with Boolean operations coordinatewise,

  c(a,b,d)=(f(a),g(b),h(d)),  r(a,b,d)=(b,d,a).

Then c1 acts as (g,h,f), and c2 acts as (h,f,g). The final cyclic equation
holds because f,g,h commute pairwise. Every other defining equation follows
coordinatewise or from rotation. Translate source f,g,h by c0,c1,c2. For a
source term t and diagonal assignments to its generators, the coordinates of
the translated value are evaluations of t under the three cyclic orderings
(f,g,h), (g,h,f), (h,f,g). In particular coordinate zero is exactly the
original value. Every failure of a source equation therefore survives.

Conclusion: source Df3 validity of an equation is equivalent to validity of
its cyclic translation. The diagonal is NOT asserted to be a subalgebra;
typically c sends a diagonal element to a non-diagonal element. Coordinate
projection at translated terms, rather than a diagonal homomorphism, is the
correct argument.

Arbitrary source assumptions do not automatically transfer in the same way:
the other two coordinates see rotated versions of those assumptions. The
proposal avoids that issue by encoding the desired consistency question as
one theorem target without adding arbitrary source assumptions.

There is also a direct syntactic reverse proof transport. Interpret every
cyclic term as a triple of source terms; Boolean operators act coordinatewise,
c uses f/g/h, and r rotates the tuple. Cyclic axioms become source axiom
instances or reflexivities, and each equational inference transports
coordinatewise. This yields effective reverse proof transport. Forward
transport requires finitely many fixed equational derivations of the
automorphism/conjugation facts above; these are ordinary algebraic arguments
here, not exported checked derivation certificates.

2. A fixed generator may be r-invariant: accepted, with a critical boundary
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add a distinguished CONSTANT p and the single GROUND equation r(p)=p.
The validity/consequence comparison for source one-generator equations
continues to hold: in the converse product construction interpret p as
(a,a,a), so the extra ground equation holds and the first-coordinate
counterexample survives.

The equation r(p)=p must not be treated as a universal rewrite schema whose
p can match arbitrary terms. That would force r=id everywhere, identify all
three conjugate operators, and invalidate the intended reduction. Fixed p
must be distinguished from axiom metavariables in the checker.

This ground equation does not force c0(p)=c1(p). For example, take the
four-element Boolean algebra, f=h=id, and g the closure mapping every nonzero
element to top. With diagonal p=(1,1,1), r(p)=p while c0(p)=(1,3,1) and
c1(p)=(3,1,1), using bit-mask values 0,1,2,3.

A pure nine-equation alternative replaces p by p+r(p)+r^2(p), which is
r-invariant. On diagonal product assignments this replacement equals p,
so counterexamples still transfer. Whether either form saves actual machine
states depends on the serialized target and evaluator.

3. Phase optimization: accepted only for the explicit grammar
------------------------------------------------------------
Let D_t[i] represent r^(-i) tau(t), where tau is the cyclic translation and
i is modulo three. A direct representative B_t[k] is available as follows:

  p:        cost 1 only for k=0 (all k for the ground-invariant variant);
  -u:       1 + D_u[k];
  u+v:      1 + D_u[k] + D_v[k];
  c_j(u):   1 + D_u[j], available only for k=j.

Then

  D_t[i] = min_k ((k-i mod 3) + B_t[k]).

The sign is correct: prefixing a representative of r^(-k) tau(t) with
r^(k-i) gives r^(-i) tau(t). Boolean cases use automorphism preservation;
the modal case uses r^(-j)c_j = c r^(-j). Each node has a fixed 3-by-3
transition computation, so runtime is linear in source tree size.

The resulting exact optimality is ONLY among representations generated by
these local phase rules, with reduced outer rotation chains. It is not
optimality among all algebraically equivalent terms, all skeleton-preserving
equivalent terms, all DAGs, or all Turing machines. For example p+-p is
r-invariant because it is top; recognizing that semantic invariant can remove
rotations even when the local pure-E9 leaf rule does not. Boolean identities,
closure distribution/idempotence, operator commutation, incidental invariants,
and DAG sharing are outside the stated dynamic-programming grammar.

4. A finite-model wrapper avoids the full generic translation theorem
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Let U be a fixed finite first-order relational theory with equality and one
binary relation R. Fix its single conjunction A_U. Let KP(z,x,y) be a pure
membership formula for z={{x},{x,y}} in ordinary set structures. Define
psi_U to assert the existence of d,e such that d has a member and A_U holds
when every quantifier is relativized to x in d and every R(x,y) is replaced
by exists z (z in e and KP(z,x,y)). Rename binders throughout to avoid capture.

If an arbitrary membership structure satisfies psi_U, let D be its members
of d and define R_D using the displayed formula. D is nonempty. Structural
induction on every U-formula proves satisfaction transfer, with native
equality inherited on D. Therefore this produces a U-model. KP need not be
unique, total, or correctly set-theoretic in this direction: it is simply a
formula defining some relation on D.

Conversely, within ZFC, Con(U) yields a set-coded nonempty model (A,R) by
first-order completeness. Choose a sufficiently large limit ordinal lambda,
for example one larger than rank(A union R)+omega, and use the ordinary
membership structure V_lambda. Set d=A,e=R. Transitivity and closure under
finite Kuratowski pairing ensure that the graph interpretation agrees exactly
with R. Hence V_lambda satisfies psi_U. The concrete ordered-pair predicates
in Andréka–Németi Definition 5.2 are genuine Kuratowski projections there,
so their nontrivial strong pairing sentence pi+ holds as well. There is no
claim that V_lambda satisfies ZFC, and no inaccessible cardinal is required.

For a final report, the exact source pi (referenced there to earlier work)
must be fixed alongside pi+. It cannot be replaced by an unspecified generic
pairing sentence merely because the notation is similar.

Thus ZFC proves Con(U) iff pi+ and psi_U have a set-coded model. It follows
that pi+ entails not-psi_U iff not-Con(U). The cited effective variable
compression f satisfies pi entails (f(chi) iff chi) for sentences. Since
pi+ implies pi, the fully proved Andréka–Németi Theorem 5.4 with empty T
then gives theoremhood of h(f(not-psi_U)) iff not-Con(U).

This use of a coded model avoids requiring the domain of a direct NBG model
to satisfy pairing for all its classes. A finite NBG presentation in one
binary language can be used for U, but its exact finite axioms and the
ordinary ZFC-proof of Con(U) iff Con(ZFC) must be separately specified.

Primary source: H. Andréka and I. Németi, Formalizing set theory in weak
logics, arXiv:1111.0995v1, especially Definition 5.2, Theorem 5.4, and equation
(8) in the proof of Theorem 2.1. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.0995v1
The paper's introduction says its full generic Theorem 2.2 is only partially
proved there; the above wrapper deliberately uses Theorem 5.4 instead.

The finite target exists through effective syntax transformations, but no
claim that its literal output has been generated is justified by this audit.
Full target emission, size measurement, equational checker compilation,
blank initialization and binary simulation remain separate deliverables.

6. Eight-equation presentation with three proper operators: accepted
------------------------------------------------------------------
Let N be Boolean complement in a cyclic algebra and define s=N r^2.
N and r commute, N^2=id, and r^3=id, so s^2=r and s^3=N. Replace every
complement in the nine-equation basis by s^3, every r by s^2, and omit
the transformed r^3=id row. This leaves eight equations in (+,c,s).

Conversely, in any model of those eight equations, N=s^3 obeys the three
Boolean axioms, so N is an actual Boolean complement. Its involutivity gives
s^6=id. Setting r=s^2 therefore recovers r^3=id, and all other cyclic rows
are present by substitution. Finally N r^2=s^3 s^4=s^7=s, so the constructions
are inverse term definitions. This is a term-equivalence of the two varieties,
not merely a one-way consistency implication.

The optional ground equation r(p)=p becomes s^2(p)=p and retains the same
literal-constant boundary. The three proper term operators permit a ternary
constructor tag, but complement expansion grows from one unary operator to
three and rotation from one to two. Neither the smaller signature nor eight
axioms is evidence of a binary-machine improvement; full emitted terms and
compiler cost must be measured.

An analogous sound six-phase grammar can combine these unary expansions.
Let D_t[i] represent s^(-i) tau8(t), modulo six. A source complement merely
shifts the requested phase: D_not(u)[i]=D_u[(i-3) mod 6]. For a source join,
the direct rule 1+D_left[k]+D_right[k] is allowed at even k, because even
powers of s are Boolean automorphisms. For source modal c_j, the direct rule
is 1+D_child[2j] at k=2j. The leaf is direct at k=0, or all even k with the
ground-invariant generator. Remaining phases use the same outer-rotation
minimum, now with (k-i mod 6) copies of s. This is linear-time with a fixed
six-by-six state table, and has the same local-grammar-only optimality scope.
