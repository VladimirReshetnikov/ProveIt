\section{A smaller relational axiom presentation}
\label{sec:formalisms}

The successful binary construction retains the calibrated logical
engine. Exploring alternative formalisms nevertheless gives a
useful concrete theorem: in complete first-order logic, one
membership-congruence axiom follows from the other through
global-image collection. This section gives the derivation,
its consistency calibration, and the exact implementation
obligation that remains.

\subsection{The native theory and its collection schema}

The inherited native theory $\SIR$ uses ordinary logical equality,
membership, a power-container axiom $C_P$, full global-image
collection, and irreflexivity \cite{upstream,native}. It does not
add Extensionality, Foundation, or Choice as axioms.
For clarity, write
\[
 x\subseteq y\quad\text{for}\quad
 \forall u\,(u\in x\to u\in y).
\]
The container principle has the form
\[
 C_P:\quad
 \forall a\,\exists V\left[
 a\in V\ \land\
 \forall x\in V\left(
 x\subseteq V\ \land\
 \exists y\in V\,\forall z\,(z\subseteq x\to z\in y)
 \right)\right].
\]
It supplies a transitive host and bounds for subsets inside
that host. The full calibration of this nonextensional theory
uses the inherited interpretation machinery; the displayed
formula alone is not a new consistency proof.

For a matrix $\theta(x,y;\bar p)$, global-image collection is
\begin{align}
 \GIC(\theta):\quad
 \forall A\,\exists B\bigl[
 &\forall y\bigl(y\in B\to\exists x\,\theta(x,y;\bar p)\bigr)
 \notag\\
 {}\land{}&
 \forall x\bigl(x\in A\to
 ((\exists y\,\theta(x,y;\bar p))
 \to\exists y\,(y\in B\land\theta(x,y;\bar p)))\bigr)
 \bigr].
 \label{eq:gic}
\end{align}
Every parameter is universally closed, and the displayed
variables $A,B$ are fresh for the matrix. The first conjunct
is \emph{global range purity}; the second chooses a witness
for each nonempty fiber over the seed set.
All finite native formulas, including formulas with equality,
are permitted as matrices. Restricting this to equality-free
matrices without justification would change the theory.

\subsection{Replacing logical equality by a relation}

Work in complete first-order logic without built-in equality,
over two binary relations $E$ and $R$. Write $x\in y$ for
$R(x,y)$. Quantifiers range over a single common nonempty
domain, and ordinary capture-avoiding first-order
instantiation is available. Initially $E$ is merely a relation.

Assume the universal closures of
\begin{align*}
 H_1 &: \quad \forall b\,\exists a\,E(a,b),\\
 H_7 &: \quad E(c,a)\to(E(c,b)\to E(a,b)),\\
 H_5 &: \quad E(c,a)\to(R(c,b)\to R(a,b)).
\end{align*}
These are the relational versions of the upstream numerical
rows $\rho_1,\rho_7,\rho_5$. The fourth row of interest is
\[
 H_3:\quad E(a,b)\to(R(c,a)\to R(c,b)).
 \tag{right congruence}
\]
The row numbering and formulas agree with the actual upstream
definitions \cite{codes,tarski}.

\begin{lemma}[Equivalence from predecessor existence and Euclideanness]
\label{lem:equiv}
$H_1$ and $H_7$ prove that $E$ is an equivalence relation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For arbitrary $b$, take $d$ with $E(d,b)$ by $H_1$.
Apply $H_7$ with common left endpoint $d$ and both premises
$E(d,b)$ to obtain $E(b,b)$; the needed instance is
$E(d,b)\to(E(d,b)\to E(b,b))$.
From $E(a,b)$ and reflexivity $E(a,a)$, the common-left-endpoint
instance at $a$ gives $E(b,a)$.
Finally, if $E(a,b)$ and $E(b,c)$, symmetry gives $E(b,a)$,
and $H_7$ with common left endpoint $b$ gives $E(a,c)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One collection instance derives right congruence]
\label{thm:collectioncong}
The axioms $H_1,H_7,H_5$ together with the single universally
closed GIC instance whose matrix is
\[
 \theta(x,y;a,c)=E(y,a)\land R(c,y)
\]
prove $H_3$. No container, Extensionality, irreflexivity,
Infinity, or separate nonempty-member-set assumption is
required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:equiv}, $E$ is an equivalence relation.
Assume $E(a,b)$ and $R(c,a)$. Apply the displayed collection
instance with seed $A=a$, and let $B$ be the resulting
collection set.

The matrix is independent of its input variable $x$.
Reflexivity gives $E(a,a)$, so $y=a$ witnesses
$\theta(c,y;a,c)$. Since $R(c,a)$, coverage at input $c$
gives an object $w$ with
\[
 R(w,B),\qquad E(w,a),\qquad R(c,w).
\]
Transitivity applied to $E(w,a)$ and $E(a,b)$ gives $E(w,b)$.
Now $H_5$, transporting the \emph{first} membership argument,
gives $R(b,B)$. Range purity, instantiated at the member $b$,
gives $\exists x\,\theta(x,b;a,c)$, whose second conjunct
is precisely $R(c,b)$.

Discharge the two assumptions and universally generalize.
The premise $R(c,a)$ itself supplied the nonempty seed.
No step transported an object through the second membership
argument by assumption; that transport was obtained from
collection purity.
\end{proof}

The argument uses positive first-order reasoning and is
intuitionistically valid at this local level. The later
consistency comparison is stated in ordinary classical
first-order logic.

\begin{remark}[Collection is a substantive premise]
The other axioms and irreflexivity alone do not imply $H_3$.
Take a three-element domain $\{a,b,c\}$, let the $E$-classes
be $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c\}$, and let the only membership pair
be $R(c,a)$. Then $E$ is an equivalence relation, $H_1,H_7,H_5$
and irreflexivity hold, but $E(a,b)$ and $R(c,a)$ hold while
$R(c,b)$ fails. The GIC instance used above fails in this
structure, exactly as the theorem requires.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A stronger saturation statement}

\begin{proposition}[Definable predicates are saturated]
\label{prop:saturation}
Suppose $E$ is an equivalence relation, $H_5$ holds, and
$R(u,A)$ holds for some $u,A$. For any formula
$\varphi(y;\bar p)$, assume GIC for
\[
 \theta_\varphi(x,y;a,\bar p)
 =E(y,a)\land\varphi(y;\bar p).
\]
Then
\[
 E(a,b)\to
 \bigl(\varphi(a;\bar p)\leftrightarrow\varphi(b;\bar p)\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume $E(a,b)$ and $\varphi(a;\bar p)$ and collect over the
fixed nonempty seed $A$. The output $a$ witnesses the fiber
over $u$. Coverage gives $w\in B$ with $E(w,a)$ and
$\varphi(w;\bar p)$. Transitivity and $H_5$ place $b$ in $B$;
purity yields $\varphi(b;\bar p)$. Interchanging $a,b$
and using symmetry proves the converse.
\end{proof}

This statement also works in an expanded relational language
when the corresponding matrices are present. For the language
$\{E,R\}$ alone, Theorem~\ref{thm:collectioncong} first makes
both atomic relations congruent, after which ordinary formula
induction proves all formula congruence without a separate
nonempty-member assumption.

\subsection{An equiconsistent alternative theory}

Define $T_-$ in the relational language $\{E,R\}$ to consist of
$H_1,H_7,H_5$, the displayed $C_P$ written with $R$,
irreflexivity $\forall x\,\neg R(x,x)$, and GIC for every
finite $\{E,R\}$ matrix with arbitrary finite parameters.
Its background logic is the complete equality-free
first-order calculus just specified.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:tminus}
Using conventional effective proof predicates,
\[
 \ZFC\vdash
 \Con(T_-)\longleftrightarrow\Con(\SIR)
 \longleftrightarrow\Con(\ZFC).
\]
The final equivalence is the inherited SIR calibration.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every SIR model expands to a $T_-$ model by interpreting
$E$ as actual identity. All three $H$ axioms hold, and
each translated GIC instance is a native SIR instance.

Conversely, let a $T_-$ model have domain $D$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:equiv} and Theorem~\ref{thm:collectioncong}
give an equivalence relation and congruence of $R$ in both
coordinates. On the quotient $D/E$ define
\[
 [a]\in^*[b]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R(a,b).
\]
The relation is well-defined, and equality of quotient classes
is exactly $E$ on representatives. Induction on formulas gives
the truth lemma for replacing native equality by $E$.
The quantifier steps use surjectivity of the quotient projection;
no global choice of representatives is necessary.
The quotient therefore satisfies $C_P$, irreflexivity, and
every native GIC instance, so it is an SIR model.

The two model transports and first-order completeness give
the two consistency implications inside ZFC. The languages,
formula translations, and fixed right-congruence derivation
are all effective. The established SIR/ZFC comparison supplies
the last equivalence \cite{upstream,native,calibration}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why this does not delete a machine row}

The actual numerical calculus has modus ponens,
generalization, its truth-code-0 axiom, its eight logical rows, and its gated
bundle as primitive operations. Ordinary arbitrary
first-order instantiation is \emph{derived} in that engine.
The established derivation uses the equality rows,
including $\rho_3$ \cite{tarski}.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:collectioncong} instantiates
collection at $A=a$, uses the witness $y=a$, and specializes
purity at $y=b$. To remove $\rho_3$ from the numerical
machine, these exact inferences would need a numerical
proof scheme using only the remaining rows and permitted
gated instances. The full-logic argument does not supply
that scheme automatically.

At the ordinary formula level, the deleted row has three
atomic occurrences and two implication nodes. The equality
constructor, its coding tag, and other equality rows are
still needed. A replacement instantiation engine has its
own cost. Accordingly, this report proves a simpler
equiconsistent presentation and identifies a concrete
future compiler target; it assigns no additional state
saving to that theorem. The accepted 238-state table keeps
every logical row of its calibrated source.

\section{Other formalisms and complete encoding costs}
\label{sec:costs}

The repository already contains useful matched controls.
These are \emph{reported prior measurements}, identified
in the included reference notes, rather than new compiler
executions in this work. They use a public Wade baseline of
281 states, which is different from the separately optimized
240-state machine. They cannot be subtracted directly from
238 or 240.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.34\textwidth}rL{0.43\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Alternative & Count & Meaning of the measurement\\
\midrule
Public original reconstruction & 281 & Matched nine-register baseline\\
Full logical engine with raw subset-seeded PSC & 279 & Fixed shared control; partial named table and new proof obligations\\
GEN elimination by prefix closure & 285 & Best grouped control in the reported search\\
Eight-register alternate pairing & 306 & Best shared full-source control\\
Extensional subset PSC & 320 & Particular fixed emitter/control\\
Functional subset closure & 347 & Corresponding equality-bound control\\
Ground-equational generic control & 299 & Before a literal ZFC query and its writer\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The full-logic PSC control has a meaningful theory direction,
but its new schema translation, emitter discipline, source
proof, and binary simulation remain separate obligations.
The notes explicitly identify its missing named row;
it is not a complete new 279-state consistency machine
\cite{controls}.

GEN elimination has a correct proof-theoretic motivation,
yet grouping universally prefixed axiom families cost more
in the reported backend. Likewise the eight-register pairing
$2^a(2b+1)-1$ reduces the number of scratch registers while
making arithmetic and formula handling more expensive
\cite{genfree,registers}.

The subset and functional closure presentations offer
legitimate ordinary mathematical comparisons, but their
measured emitters cost 320 and 347 states respectively
\cite{functional}. The ground-equational generator moves
complexity from inference checking into generation of
valid equations, yet its 299-state control still lacks
the literal ZFC query and writer \cite{equational}.

The tempting smaller equality-deletion experiments have
a different problem. Removing the machinery for
cross-variable instantiation admits the repository's
finite-sort counterinterpretations. Their small table
counts do not certify an equiconsistent search engine.
A primitive falsity symbol does not repair that
completeness failure \cite{countermodel}.

Clause engines, finite-quotient proof certificates, and
short equational packets remain plausible directions.
For a blank-tape bound, however, every packet bit, query,
decoder, inference mechanism, and initializer must be
charged. A short input for a universal machine is not
already a short blank machine, and an alphabet reduction
can introduce additional states. Standard complete
first-order engines make substitutions and equality
handling explicit \cite{avigad}; that explicitness is
precisely what a competitive alternative must implement
compactly.

The experimental choice in the present construction is
therefore supported by concrete costs and proof
obligations: retain the established logical calibration,
then compress its source and binary control together.
The alternate theory $T_-$ remains a separate, precisely
stated candidate for a future complete frontend.
