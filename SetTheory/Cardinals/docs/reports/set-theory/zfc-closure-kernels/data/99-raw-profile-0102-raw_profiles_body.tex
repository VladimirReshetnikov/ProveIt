\section{What changes in the mathematical argument}

ProveIt's source44 supplies FC15$^*$, its FC16$^*$ extensional control,
and their ordinary ZFC consistency calibrations~\cite{source44}. The recent incoming
\texttt{zfc-sub245-research-20261018.zip} replaces their full congruence
completion by a quotient construction that preserves $V,C$ and
reconstructs the remaining functions. Its final raw target has twenty
clauses and 1134 bits; the extensional target has twenty-one and 1341 bits~\cite{incoming20261018}.

The construction here does not attempt to turn every raw function into a
well-defined quotient operation. It constructs an ordinary binary
membership structure and proves that every required first-order closure
instance holds in it. Raw assignment and class terms implement each fixed
formula separately. This is why neither $C$ nor the incidence predicate
has to descend to the quotient. It also explains why the proof cannot be
replaced by the ordinary all-functions congruence theorem.

The retained source dependency for the lower calibration is the following
specific, full schema. For every equality-free membership formula
$\varphi(x,y,\bar p)$, with unrestricted finite parameters, put
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{SB}_\varphi
 &:=\forall x\,\exists u\,\forall y\,
  \bigl(\varphi(x,y,\bar p)\to
         \forall z(E(z,y)\to E(z,u))\bigr),\\
\operatorname{Hull}_\varphi(c)
 &:=\forall x,y\,
  \bigl(((\forall z\,\neg E(z,x))\lor E(x,c))\land
          \varphi(x,y,\bar p)\to E(y,c)\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{9mm}\land\forall y\,
       \bigl(E(y,c)\to\exists x\,\varphi(x,y,\bar p)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Raw EPSC consists of all instances
$\operatorname{SB}_\varphi\to\exists c\,
\operatorname{Hull}_\varphi(c)$ in a nonempty domain.
Source44 and its cited raw-EPSC interpretation establish in ordinary ZFC
\[
\Con(\mathrm{raw\ EPSC})\longrightarrow\Con(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
Its separate extensional route says that Extensionality plus this full
schema has the intended ZF-without-Foundation-or-Choice comparison. Those
previous interpretations are named dependencies; this note proves the
new finite-theory-to-schema interpretation in detail.

\section{Exact eighteen-clause target}

The language has two constants $d,q$, ten total functions
\[
 V^2,C^3,N^2,A^2,Q^2,W^3,Y^3,Z^3,H^2,D^1,
\]
and four binary predicates $E,\Inc,G,\Eq$. Superscripts are arities.
$\Eq$ is an ordinary predicate, not built-in logical equality. In
particular no congruence rule is supplied by the logic merely because a
predicate is named $\Eq$. All eighteen clauses below are universally
quantified in an equality-free first-order language. The constants $d,q$
are independent; there is no axiom $q=N(d,d)$.

Abbreviate the literal term pattern
\[
 R(b,t,x,y):=\Inc(C(C(b,d,x),q,y),t).
\]
The first fifteen rows are source44's guard-fused presentation with a
primitive member selector, except that $q$ is a constant:
\begin{align}
&\Eq(V(C(s,j,z),j),z),\tag{01}\label{rp01}\\
&\Eq(k,j)\lor\Eq(V(C(s,j,z),k),V(s,k)),\tag{02}\label{rp02}\\
&\Inc(s,N(a,b))\lor\Inc(s,a),\tag{03}\label{rp03}\\
&\Inc(s,N(a,b))\lor\Inc(s,b),\tag{04}\label{rp04}\\
&\neg\Inc(s,N(a,b))\lor\neg\Inc(s,a)\lor\neg\Inc(s,b),\tag{05}\label{rp05}\\
&\neg\Inc(s,A(j,k))\lor E(V(s,j),V(s,k)),\tag{06}\label{rp06}\\
&\Inc(s,A(j,k))\lor\neg E(V(s,j),V(s,k)),\tag{07}\label{rp07}\\
&\neg\Inc(C(s,j,z),a)\lor\Inc(s,Q(j,a)),\tag{08}\label{rp08}\\
&\neg\Inc(s,Q(j,a))\lor\Inc(C(s,j,W(s,j,a)),a),\tag{09}\label{rp09}\\
&G(b,t)\lor R(b,t,H(b,t),Y(b,t,u)),\tag{10}\label{rp10}\\
&G(b,t)\lor E(Z(b,t,u),Y(b,t,u)),\tag{11}\label{rp11}\\
&G(b,t)\lor\neg E(Z(b,t,u),u),\tag{12}\label{rp12}\\
&\neg G(b,t)\lor\neg E(x,H(b,t))\lor\neg R(b,t,x,y)
                  \lor E(y,H(b,t)),\tag{13}\label{rp13}\\
&\neg G(b,t)\lor E(D(x),x)\lor\neg R(b,t,x,y)
                  \lor E(y,H(b,t)),\tag{14}\label{rp14}\\
&\neg G(b,t)\lor\neg E(y,H(b,t))\lor R(b,t,Y(b,t,y),y).\tag{15}\label{rp15}
\end{align}
There are exactly three further rows:
\begin{align}
&\neg\Eq(x,y)\lor\neg\Eq(x,z)\lor\Eq(y,z),\tag{16}\label{rp16}\\
&\neg\Eq(x_0,y_0)\lor\neg\Eq(x_1,y_1)
            \lor\neg E(x_0,x_1)\lor E(y_0,y_1),\tag{17}\label{rp17}\\
&\neg\Eq(d,q).\tag{18}\label{rp18}
\end{align}
Call the resulting theory $\RP$. There is no congruence for any function and no
congruence for $\Inc$ or $G$. Every local variable receives every ground
term in the refutation semantics; no colliding substitution is discarded.

\section{The quotient of data}

Let $\mathcal T$ be the set of all ground terms of this finite signature.
Let $v$ be a Boolean valuation of all ground atoms satisfying every
ground instance of rows~\ref{rp01}--\ref{rp18}. Consistency yields such
a valuation by first-order completeness and evaluation of ground terms;
equivalently, simultaneous finite ground satisfiability yields it by
propositional compactness. The added padding constructors of the numerical
codec can be retained in $\mathcal T$ as well. No congruence is assumed
for them, and every variable may receive a padded term.

Put $s\sim t$ when $v(\Eq(s,t))=1$. This is an equivalence relation.
For any ground $z$, row~\ref{rp01} with $s=j=d$ gives
$\Eq(p_z,z)$ for $p_z=V(C(d,d,z),d)$. Row~\ref{rp16} with
$(x,y,z)=(p_z,z,z)$ then gives $\Eq(z,z)$. Reflexivity plus the left
Euclidean law yields symmetry and transitivity. Thus no separate
reflexivity template is needed.

Let $\mathcal A=\mathcal T/{\sim}$ and write $\cl t$ for the class
of $t$. Define
\[
 E^{\mathcal A}(\cl s,\cl t)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v(E(s,t))=1.
\]
Row~\ref{rp17} and symmetry make this independent of representatives.
This is the only binary predicate that needs to descend. We do not define
$\Inc^{\mathcal A}$, $G^{\mathcal A}$, or $C^{\mathcal A}$.
Even $\cl s\mapsto\cl{V(s,j)}$ for a fixed raw selector need not be
well-defined: evaluation has no congruence at all. The next two sections
prove profile and closure lemmas for any finite list of distinct selector
classes already available. Section~\ref{rp-infinity} first uses only
one coordinate, then three coordinates, to prove that arbitrarily many
such selectors exist. This order avoids assuming the selector supply
that those closure arguments are used to establish.

\section{Exact formula interpretation without a quotient change function}

Fix an equality-free formula $\varphi$ in the language of $E$. Rename
bound variables so that its finite list of free and bound names is
pairwise distinct as syntactic names. For this lemma, assume a raw ground
selector $j_w$ has been chosen for each name $w$, with pairwise
inequivalent selectors. No claim of an arbitrarily large supply is yet
needed. The data values
assigned to different names may coincide; only their selector classes
are chosen distinct. For any raw assignment term $s$, let
\[
 \alpha_s(w):=\cl{V(s,j_w)}\in\mathcal A.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Raw assignment update]\label{rp-update}
For every raw value $z$ and every name $w$, the valuation associated with
$C(s,j_w,z)$ agrees with $\alpha_s$ at all other names and gives
$\cl z$ to $w$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the updated coordinate use row~\ref{rp01}. For a different name
$u$, the first literal of row~\ref{rp02}, instantiated at
$(j,k)=(j_w,j_u)$, is false by the selector choice; its second literal
is the desired equality of value classes. This holds even when the raw
assignment, a data value, or several parameter values happen to equal or
be equivalent to a selector. No such diagonal case is excluded.
\end{proof}

Use NAND and existential quantification as the Boolean and quantifier
basis of finite first-order syntax. They express the usual connectives
and universal quantification by fixed finite translations. A constantly
true class is available as $N(B,N(B,B))$ for any fixed atomic class $B$;
its NAND with itself is constantly false. Thus the arbitrary incidence
profile of the constant $d$ is never treated as logical falsity.

Construct a raw class term $t_\varphi$ by structural recursion:
\[
 t_{E(u,w)}=A(j_u,j_w),\quad
 t_{\operatorname{Nand}(\psi,\theta)}=N(t_\psi,t_\theta),\quad
 t_{\exists w\,\psi}=Q(j_w,t_\psi).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Raw class-profile truth lemma]\label{rp-truth}
For every raw ground assignment $s$,
\[
 v(\Inc(s,t_\varphi))=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (\mathcal A,E^{\mathcal A})\models\varphi[\alpha_s].
\]
The class term remains raw; it is not replaced by a representative of
its $\sim$-class.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For atoms, rows~\ref{rp06}--\ref{rp07} give the exact equivalence
between incidence and the raw $E$ atom on $V$ outputs. The definition
of quotient membership turns this into the required semantic atom.
Rows~\ref{rp03}--\ref{rp05} give precisely the NAND truth table at
every raw assignment.

For the existential step, assume first that
$v(\Inc(s,Q(j_w,t_\psi)))=1$. The raw instance of row~\ref{rp09}
supplies the raw witness
$z=W(s,j_w,t_\psi)$ with
$v(\Inc(C(s,j_w,z),t_\psi))=1$. The induction hypothesis and
Lemma~\ref{rp-update} give a satisfying data value $\cl z$ for $w$.
Conversely, if a value $a\in\mathcal A$ satisfies the existential
matrix, choose any raw representative $z$ of $a$. The update lemma
and induction hypothesis give the raw incidence at $C(s,j_w,z)$.
Row~\ref{rp08} yields incidence at $Q(j_w,t_\psi)$.

This argument preserves the exact witness term supplied by the raw
row. It never replaces it by a canonical representative before applying
$C$. Such a replacement would incorrectly require the deleted
congruence of $C$ in its value argument. Likewise, the raw assignment
$s$ and the raw class term $t_\psi$ stay literally fixed in both
quantifier clauses. All quantified data values are covered because
$\mathcal T\to\mathcal A$ is surjective.
\end{proof}

\section{Transfer of any available raw profile}

Fix any binary relation $\varphi(x,y,\bar a)$ on $\mathcal A$, with
the displayed data parameters held fixed. At this stage, assume that
fixed raw terms $b,t$ have the following property for every pair of raw
ground inputs $x_0,y_0$:
\begin{equation}\label{rp-Rtruth}
 v(R(b,t,x_0,y_0))=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal A\models\varphi(\cl{x_0},\cl{y_0},\bar a).
\end{equation}
This is a semantic identity for these fixed raw $b,t$ and all raw
inputs. It supplies the particular invariance needed for this matrix;
no general congruence of $R$, $C$, or $\Inc$ is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Raw-profile closure transfer]\label{rp-transfer}
Under \eqref{rp-Rtruth}, the EPSC instance for this particular relation
holds in the quotient membership structure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume $\operatorname{SB}_\varphi$ in $\mathcal A$. Fix the raw terms
$b,t$ above and put $h=H(b,t)$. Suppose $v(G(b,t))=0$. The subset
bound for the data value $\cl h$ has some data value $u$; take a raw
representative $u_0$ of it. The instances of rows~\ref{rp10}--\ref{rp12}
at the same raw key $(b,t,u_0)$ give
\[
 R(b,t,h,Y(b,t,u_0)),\quad
 E(Z(b,t,u_0),Y(b,t,u_0)),\quad
 \neg E(Z(b,t,u_0),u_0).
\]
By \eqref{rp-Rtruth} and quotient membership, these are an actual
$\varphi$ output at $\cl h$ with a member outside its asserted bound.
This contradicts $\operatorname{SB}_\varphi$. Hence $v(G(b,t))=1$.

Take $c=\cl h$. If $E^{\mathcal A}(a,c)$ and
$\varphi(a,a',\bar a)$, choose any raw representatives $x_0,y_0$ of
$a,a'$. Row~\ref{rp13}, with the fixed raw $b,t$, and
\eqref{rp-Rtruth} give $E^{\mathcal A}(a',c)$.

If instead $a$ has no incoming member in the quotient, the raw atom
$E(D(x_0),x_0)$ is false: $D(x_0)$ is some ground term, so its class
would otherwise be an incoming member of $a$. Row~\ref{rp14} and
\eqref{rp-Rtruth} again give closure for every output of $a$.
There is no need to prove that $D$ itself is a well-defined quotient
function or a member selector in arbitrary raw models.

Finally, if $E^{\mathcal A}(a',c)$, choose a raw representative
$y_0$. Row~\ref{rp15} gives
$R(b,t,Y(b,t,y_0),y_0)$. By \eqref{rp-Rtruth}, the data value
$\cl{Y(b,t,y_0)}$ is an actual supporting input for $a'$.
The same single $h$ is used throughout; $H,Y,Z$ and the guard are
never reconstructed separately at inconsistent representative choices.
This proves both clauses of $\operatorname{Hull}_\varphi(c)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The closure rows force arbitrarily many selectors}\label{rp-infinity}

For $a\in\mathcal A$ write
$\operatorname{pred}(a)=\{z\in\mathcal A:E^{\mathcal A}(z,a)\}$.
These are sets of the ambient ZFC proof; no extensionality of the
membership structure is assumed.

\begin{lemma}[At least three data classes]\label{rp-three}
The quotient $\mathcal A$ has at least three elements.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Only one assignment coordinate is needed for each relation in this
paragraph. Let $T=N(d,N(d,d))$ and $F=N(T,T)$. The NAND rows make
$\Inc(s,T)$ always true and $\Inc(s,F)$ always false, for every raw
assignment $s$. Thus the profiles $R(b,F,x,y)$ and $R(b,T,x,y)$
represent the constantly false and constantly true binary relations,
independently of $b,x,y$.

For the false relation, local subset boundedness is immediate, and the
support part of Lemma~\ref{rp-transfer} gives an object $e$ with
$\operatorname{pred}(e)=\varnothing$. Row~\ref{rp18} already gives
two distinct classes. Suppose these are the only two elements, written
$e,a$. Every predecessor set is contained in
$\operatorname{pred}(a)$, because the only other column is empty.
Consequently the true relation is locally subset bounded, with the
same bound $a$ for every input. Its supported hull, using the empty
input $e$, is a universal object $u$ with
$\operatorname{pred}(u)=\mathcal A$.

The class term $N(A(q,q),A(q,q))$ has profile
$R(b,t,x,y)\leftrightarrow\neg E^{\mathcal A}(\cl y,\cl y)$:
the last update at $q$ supplies exactly the value $y$. The universal
object $u$ bounds this relation. Its hull $h$, again using the empty
input and then global support, satisfies
\[
 E^{\mathcal A}(y,h)\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \neg E^{\mathcal A}(y,y)
 \qquad(y\in\mathcal A).
\]
Taking $y=h$ is a contradiction. Thus there cannot be only two classes.
No extra selector, evaluation congruence, or quantified class formula
was used to establish this lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Relative power objects with three coordinates]\label{rp-power}
For every $a\in\mathcal A$ there is an object $a^+$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{pred}(a^+)
   =\{y\in\mathcal A:\operatorname{pred}(y)
                         \subseteq\operatorname{pred}(a)\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a fixed raw selector $r$ inequivalent to both $d,q$, using
Lemma~\ref{rp-three}. Choose a raw representative $p$ of $a$ and put
$b_p=C(d,r,p)$. The desired profile is
\[
 \varphi_p(x,y)=\forall z\,(E(z,y)\longrightarrow E(z,p)),
\]
which does not use its first free input $x$. Use selector $q$ for $y$,
$r$ for the parameter, and $d$ for the bound variable $z$.
For full explicitness, put
\[
 I=N(A(d,q),N(A(d,r),A(d,r))),\qquad
 B=Q(d,N(I,I)),\qquad t=N(B,B).
\]
Here $I$ is the implication inside the universal quantifier, $B$ is
its existential counterexample class, and $t$ is its negation.

In $s=C(C(b_p,d,x),q,y)$, the $r$ coordinate still has value $a$
and the $q$ coordinate has value $\cl y$. In $C(s,d,z)$ those two
coordinates are preserved while the $d$ coordinate becomes $\cl z$.
The truth lemma, or directly rows 01--09, therefore proves
\eqref{rp-Rtruth} for this $\varphi_p$, for every raw $x,y$.
Reusing $d$ for the bound variable is legitimate because the matrix
is independent of the first input: the quantifier replaces that
coordinate completely. This three-coordinate argument does not assume
a fourth selector for a syntactically unused free name.

Local subset boundedness holds with bound $a$ for every input.
Lemma~\ref{rp-transfer} supplies $a^+=\cl{H(b_p,t)}$. The previously
obtained empty input makes every $\varphi_p$ output a member of
$a^+$; global support gives the reverse containment. This is exactly
the displayed predecessor identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Infinite data quotient without function congruence]\label{rp-many}
The quotient $\mathcal A$ has arbitrarily many distinct classes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $a_0$ with empty predecessor set and recursively choose
$a_{n+1}=a_n^+$. The explicit raw term $H(C(d,r,p),t)$ in the previous
proof can make each choice; it is unnecessary to make this operation
descend as a quotient function. Since $a_0$ is a subset of itself in
the predecessor sense, $a_0\in\operatorname{pred}(a_1)$, and
\[
 \operatorname{pred}(a_0)\subsetneq\operatorname{pred}(a_1).
\]
If $\operatorname{pred}(a)\subsetneq\operatorname{pred}(b)$, then
every predecessor set contained in $\operatorname{pred}(a)$ is
contained in $\operatorname{pred}(b)$. Hence
$\operatorname{pred}(a^+)\subseteq\operatorname{pred}(b^+)$.
This containment is strict: $b\in\operatorname{pred}(b^+)$ but
$b\notin\operatorname{pred}(a^+)$, because
$\operatorname{pred}(b)\nsubseteq\operatorname{pred}(a)$.
Thus the predecessor sets of $a_0,a_1,\ldots$ form a strictly
increasing chain. Distinct predecessor sets imply distinct quotient
objects. Among any sufficiently long finite initial segment, arbitrarily
many classes avoid the two distinguished classes of $d,q$.
\end{proof}

This replaces the earlier nineteen-clause variant's pairing argument.
That variant retained
$\neg\Eq(s,t)\lor\Eq(V(s,j),V(t,j))$ to show that raw pairs
separate data classes. The new power-object proof removes that row
entirely. The finite $n=2,3$ graph probes retained with the artifacts
were exploratory diagnostics; this general proof, rather than those
finite checks, is the justification for removing it.

\section{Every full EPSC instance holds in the quotient}

\begin{theorem}[Direct lower interpretation]\label{rp-lower}
The quotient membership structure of every all-ground model of $\RP$
satisfies every instance of full raw EPSC.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take any equality-free matrix $\varphi(x,y,p_1,\ldots,p_m)$ and any
actual data parameters $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in\mathcal A$. Rectify its
bound names. Set the selector for $x$ to the literal constant $d$ and
the selector for $y$ to $q$. By Theorem~\ref{rp-many}, choose distinct
additional selectors for every parameter and bound name, independently
of all data values.

Choose raw representatives of the parameters. Starting at raw $d$,
perform finitely many $C$ updates at their selectors. The resulting
raw term $b$ has the specified parameter values by
Lemma~\ref{rp-update}; values at unused coordinates do not matter.
With $t=t_\varphi$, Lemma~\ref{rp-truth} gives
\eqref{rp-Rtruth} for all raw inputs. Lemma~\ref{rp-transfer} proves
the required EPSC instance.
\end{proof}

The structure $\mathcal A$ may have many nonextensional or non-well-founded
objects. The theorem establishes exactly the raw EPSC interface. The
inherited hereditary interpretation is what supplies the FC15 route to
ZF consistency; no assertion that $\mathcal A$ itself is ZF is made.

\section{A direct upper expansion from a set-coded ZFC model}

For clarity the upper model is described independently of the inherited
FC15 completion. Work inside ordinary ZFC and assume $\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})$.
Completeness and downward L\"owenheim--Skolem give a countably infinite
set-coded model $M$ of ZFC. It need not be transitive, externally
well-founded, or externally $\omega$-standard. Let the raw theory's
carrier be the domain $|M|$, and interpret $E$ as $M$'s membership.
Choose $d$ to be $M$'s empty set and $q$ to be a different object,
for example its singleton. Interpret $\Eq$ as actual identity of domain
elements.

The operations $V,C$ are fixed first-order membership-definable
operations in $M$. Normalize a code $s$ to the partial function graph
it denotes if it is such a graph, and to the empty graph otherwise.
Take $V(s,j)$ to be its value at $j$ when present and $d$ otherwise.
Take $C(s,j,z)$ to be the graph obtained after that same normalization
by deleting any old pair at $j$ and inserting $(j,z)$. Extensionality,
Pairing, Separation and Union prove these definitions total and unique
in ZFC. They satisfy the two assignment identities for every input,
including invalid graph codes. The definition does not need external
finiteness of an $M$-object.

In the ambient ZFC proof, let $\operatorname{Def}(M)$ be the family of
subsets of $|M|$ definable by finite pure-membership formulas with finite
tuples of $M$ parameters. This family is countable. Choose a surjection
$t\mapsto D_t$ from $|M|$ onto it, allowing repeated class names, and put
$\Inc(s,t)$ iff $s\in D_t$. Choose:
\begin{itemize}
\item $N(a,b)$ to name the complement of $D_a\cap D_b$;
\item $A(j,k)$ to name $\{s:E(V(s,j),V(s,k))\}$;
\item $Q(j,a)$ to name $\{s:\exists z\ C(s,j,z)\in D_a\}$;
\item $W(s,j,a)$ to be an actual witness of that last existential
whenever it is true, and an arbitrary value otherwise;
\item $D(x)$ to be some $E$-member when $x$ is nonempty and an
arbitrary value otherwise.
\end{itemize}
All three class operations give pure-membership-definable classes,
because $V,C$ are definable and each particular $D_t$ has such a
definition. Their class names and witnesses can be chosen in the ambient
ZFC model; they need not be definable in $M$. This gives rows 01--09.
There is no prescribed relationship between $q$ and the chosen name
$N(d,d)$, and none is required.

For each fixed pair $(b,t)$, the relation $R(b,t,-,-)$ has an ordinary
pure-membership definition over $M$, with finitely many parameters.
The standard ZF argument proves its EPSC instance uniformly in that
definition. Under local subset boundedness, each fiber is a set:
separate the outputs inside the power set of any local subset bound.
The fiber's exact extension is unique independently of the chosen bound.
Replacement collects these exact fibers over a set of inputs. Starting
with the fiber of the empty set, iterate
\[
 c\longmapsto c\cup\bigcup\{\{y:R(x,y)\}:x\in c\}
\]
over $M$'s natural numbers and take the union of the stages. Induction
gives recurrent closure; the initial fiber gives closure from the unique
empty object; every inserted element belongs to an actual fiber, giving
global support. This is an ordinary formula-indexed ZF proof. Each
Separation and Replacement use is an instance for the selected finite
pure-membership formula, not a scheme for the expanded class language.

Set $G(b,t)$ true exactly when the local subset-bound antecedent for
this relation holds in $M$. If true, choose a supported hull as $H(b,t)$
and a supporting input as $Y(b,t,y)$ for each of its members $y$.
If false, classical negation of the antecedent gives
\[
 \exists x\,\forall u\,\exists y,z\,
       [R(b,t,x,y)\land E(z,y)\land\neg E(z,u)].
\]
Choose $H(b,t)$ as this bad input, and $Y(b,t,u),Z(b,t,u)$ as the
corresponding output and escaping member. Inactive choices are arbitrary.
All shared witness requests have the identical key $(b,t)$ and opposite
guards, so these definitions are compatible. Rows 10--15 follow directly.
Rows 16--18 hold because equality is actual identity and $d\ne q$.

All the objects in this construction are sets of the ambient ZFC proof.
The syntax used to define $\operatorname{Def}(M)$ is that ambient
theory's finite syntax, and its satisfaction relation is the set-coded
satisfaction relation for $M$. No expanded-language truth predicate is
asserted in $M$, and no external standardness assumption is made.

\begin{theorem}[Ordinary ZFC consistency calibration]\label{rp-main}
Using the explicitly named full raw-EPSC calibration, ZFC proves
\[
 \Con(\RP)\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\Con(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper model construction and soundness give the right-to-left
implication. From consistency of $\RP$, completeness supplies an
all-ground valuation; Theorem~\ref{rp-lower} constructs a model of
full raw EPSC. Soundness and the inherited consistency interpretation
give the other implication. Ground terms, valuations, equivalence
classes and the finite structural recursion are set-coded inside ZFC.
\end{proof}

\section{Two nine-function alternatives and extensional controls}

In row~\ref{rp14}, replace the member literal $E(D(x),x)$ by
$E(Z(b,t,x),x)$, and delete the function $D$ from the signature.
Call this target $\RPZ$. Its lower proof is unchanged: if $\cl x$
is empty, the first endpoint of the new literal is still some raw
ground term and cannot be a member. For the upper model, the existing
$Z$ constraints occur only when $G(b,t)$ is false. When it is true,
choose $Z(b,t,x)$ as a member of $x$ whenever one exists. The opposite
branches have the identical key $(b,t)$ and never conflict. Thus
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash\Con(\RPZ)\longleftrightarrow\Con(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
This changes the raw term from unary $D(x)$ to ternary $Z(b,t,x)$,
adding two term nodes but removing one function. The extra nodes cost
eight flat template bits. There is no claim that the representation
with fewer functions necessarily compiles to fewer states.

An additional nine-function target, $\RPV$, instead replaces
$E(D(x),x)$ by $E(V(x,d),x)$ and deletes $D$. The lower proof is
unchanged: for an empty quotient object the first endpoint of that
literal, whatever raw term it is, cannot be a member. For its upper
proof, use the standard constructible-universe relative-consistency
theorem to choose a countable set-coded model
$M\models\mathrm{ZFC}+V=L$ from $\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})$~\cite{godel1938}.
Let $g(s)$ be the first member of a nonempty $s$ in the definable global
well-order of $L$, and put $g(\varnothing)=d=\varnothing$. This is a
pure-membership-definable total operation in $M$.

For a set-coded partial function $F$ with $d$ in its domain, let
$a=F(d)$ and define, using Kuratowski ordered pairs,
\[
 \operatorname{Enc}(F)=\{a,\langle F,a\rangle\}.
\]
The ordered pair has rank strictly greater than the rank of $a$.
Thus an encoded record has exactly two members, its lower-rank member
is uniquely $a$, and its higher-rank member uniquely recovers $F$.
In particular $\operatorname{Enc}$ is injective and its image and
decoding function are definable in $M$. For any raw object $s$, define
its normalized graph $F_s$ to be its decoded graph when it is an encoded
record, and otherwise the singleton function $\{(d,g(s))\}$.
Interpret
\[
 V(s,j)=\begin{cases}F_s(j)&j\in\operatorname{dom}(F_s),\\d&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \qquad
 C(s,j,z)=\operatorname{Enc}(F_s[j\mapsto z]).
\]
Updating preserves $d$ in the domain, and injective decoding recovers
the updated graph exactly. Hence both assignment identities hold for
every input. In addition, $V(s,d)\in s$ for every nonempty $s$:
for an encoded record it is the literal member $F_s(d)$, and for an
uncoded object it is $g(s)$.

These $V,C$ are pure-membership-definable, so the earlier external
class-code construction and formula-indexed ZFC hull argument apply
without change. Row 14 is satisfied using the additional member
property just proved. No symbol for $g$, $F_s$ or $\operatorname{Enc}$
is added to the finite theory. The argument is valid inside an arbitrary
set-coded model, since its uniqueness and rank comparisons are theorems
of $M$; no external well-foundedness is assumed. Therefore
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash\Con(\RPV)\leftrightarrow\Con(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
Its flat stream is 906 bits. The generic plain Huffman codec makes it
one bit smaller than $\RPZ$, whereas the measured selective dictionary
makes it one bit larger. Both are retained as compilation targets.

Put $P(a,b)=C(C(d,d,a),q,b)$ and define the fully expanded NAND class term
\[
 \operatorname{Xor}(u,v)=N(N(u,N(u,v)),N(v,N(u,v))).
\]
Add the one clause
\begin{equation}\label{rp-ext}
 \Eq(j,d)\lor\Eq(j,q)\lor\Eq(a,b)
 \lor\Inc\bigl(P(a,b),Q(j,\operatorname{Xor}(A(j,d),A(j,q)))\bigr).
\end{equation}
These extensional targets are $\RPE$ and $\RPEZ$.

For unequal data classes $\cl a,\cl b$, choose a raw selector $j$
inequivalent to $d,q$. The first three literals of
\eqref{rp-ext} are false. Its incidence literal, expanded by the raw
truth lemma and the update identities, says exactly that there is a
data value belonging to exactly one of $\cl a,\cl b$. Thus the
quotient membership is extensional. All colliding selector substitutions
are covered by the explicit guards in the clause. The upper model is
already extensional and satisfies the added row by the same profile
identity. The inherited extensional EPSC calibration therefore gives
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash\Con(\RPE)\leftrightarrow\Con(\mathrm{ZFC}),
 \qquad
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash\Con(\RPEZ)\leftrightarrow\Con(\mathrm{ZFC}).
\]
These controls avoid the raw hereditary interpretation at the lower
boundary, while retaining the previously established extensional
EPSC-to-ZF and Foundation/Choice consistency transports.

\section{Why the distinct-constants clause remains necessary}

The successful deletion of evaluation congruence does not justify
also deleting $\neg\Eq(d,q)$. For the eighteen-clause raw target,
that further deletion has a concrete model whose existence ZFC proves.
Let the carrier be the hereditarily finite sets $\mathrm{HF}=V_\omega$,
with actual membership and equality, and interpret both $d,q$ as the
empty set. Interpret $V,C$ by the normalized finite-graph operations
in the preceding upper construction. Every finite graph and update
is still hereditarily finite, so those operations are total on HF.

Externally in ZFC, enumerate the parameter-definable classes of HF and
choose their class codes, Boolean operations, quantification operations,
witnesses and member selectors as before. The decisive identity is now
\[
 C(C(b,d,x),q,y)=C(C(b,d,x),d,y)=C(b,d,y).
\]
Consequently every $R(b,t,x,y)$ is a unary profile $\psi_{b,t}(y)$,
independent of $x$. Its subset-bound antecedent says precisely that
there is $u\in\mathrm{HF}$ such that each selected $y$ is an actual
subset of $u$. Any such profile is a subset of the finite set
$\mathcal P(u)$, hence is itself a hereditarily finite set $h$.
Choose that exact class extension as $H(b,t)$ whenever the guard is
true. Closure from empty inputs, recurrent closure, and global support
then all hold because membership in $h$ is exactly $\psi_{b,t}$.
When the guard is false, choose the counterexamples $H,Y,Z$ by the
same classical-negation argument as in the upper construction.

All seventeen retained rows are satisfied. This is a set-coded model
constructed in ZFC, so ZFC proves consistency of the theory obtained
by that further deletion. Subject to consistency of ZFC, it therefore
cannot also have the required ZFC-provable consistency equivalence,
by the second incompleteness theorem. The raw binary closure interface
needs the distinct update coordinates $d,q$; an infinite supply of other
selector classes does not repair their collision. This countermodel
also works for the member-fused raw variant.

\section{Exact syntax cost and an executable finite refuter}

The template format has a three-bit clause length, followed for each
literal by one sign bit and a two-bit predicate index, followed by two
prefix terms. Every term opcode has four bits. A zero clause length
terminates the stream. In each clause variables are renamed in alphabetical
order to local codes $0,1,2,3$. The remaining codes are $d,q$ and the
listed functions. For $\RP$,
\[
 4\text{ variable codes}+2\text{ constant codes}+10\text{ function codes}=16.
\]
The removed six-variable $C$ congruence row is what permits this dictionary.
Restoring primitive $D$ would have forced five-bit opcodes in the old
six-variable interface. This exact threshold, rather than a proportional
state assumption, accounts for the encoding choice.

\[
 L=3c+3\ell+4n+3,
\]
where $c,\ell,n$ are clause, literal and term-node counts. For $\RP$
this is $54+129+716+3=902$. The payload parser is a separate cursor

The companion \texttt{raw\_profiles.py} also gives a total mathematical
ground-term representation. Codes zero and one denote $d,q$. For a
retained function with tag $i$ and argument tuple $\bar a$, use
\[
 \operatorname{code}(F_i(\bar a))=2+i+16\operatorname{tuple}(\bar a),
\]
where tuples use the source44 Cantor pairing and the unused tags are
unary padding constructors. Every child code is smaller than its
parent. At stage $b$, instantiate every local variable by every code
$0,\ldots,b$. Encode the four predicates as ordinary propositional
atoms on endpoint codes and decide the resulting finite CNF exactly.
$\Eq$ is handled as the fourth predicate with the three retained rows;
the solver does not silently restore the deleted congruences.

The stage CNFs are nested and every ground instance eventually appears.
If one is unsatisfiable, the theory is inconsistent. Conversely, if
all are satisfiable, propositional compactness gives an all-ground
valuation and its ordinary term structure is a model of the universal
theory. The latter structure interprets all function symbols as raw
term constructors and $\Eq$ as the given predicate; it does not identify
terms by native logical equality. Therefore the ideal loop that halts
on the first unsatisfiable stage has, in ZFC, nonhalting equivalent to
$\Con(\RP)$ and hence to $\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})$.

The implementation uses the retained exact source44 DPLL routine as a
reference finite decision procedure. Its finite checks include independent
template round trips, 2048 total ground-code round trips for each of the
five variants, and checked satisfying valuations for the actual stage
$b=0$ of each theory. These are representation and implementation checks,
not evidence proving consistency or the unbounded theorem. Resource
exceptions propagate; no timeout, memory limit, or incomplete run is an
UNSAT verdict. No unbounded physical guarantee about CPython is asserted.

\section{A charged prefix-code and selective term-dictionary experiment}

The fixed four-bit opcode format is deliberately simple. Its repeated
local-variable codes make it an avoidable source of static data cost.
The companion \texttt{encoding\_experiments.py} supplies a second,
precisely defined generic codec. The signature and function arities are
part of the decoder protocol, as they are in the preceding fixed-width
format. For each base opcode, in the same fixed order, the stream starts
with a four-bit field giving its canonical Huffman code length. A zero
length means unused. Codes are assigned in order of length and then
opcode. Three-bit clause lengths, literal sign and predicate fields,
two prefix terms per literal, and the final zero clause header are all
retained. Thus this is a complete static theory stream for its fixed
decoder protocol; the lengths used to parse it are included in the count.
No decoder program or signature-to-Turing-machine compilation is included.

For $\RP$ the 64-bit length header and 734-bit body total 798 bits.
For $\RPZ$ one fewer base opcode saves four header bits, giving
$60+736=796$ bits. The same format gives the following comparison with
the retained earlier clause sets. Their six variable slots, single
constant and nine proper functions also give sixteen base opcodes.

The selective dictionary format starts with an Elias gamma code for
$K+1$, where $K$ is its entry count. It extends the opcode alphabet by
$K$ zero-arity references, supplies the four-bit canonical-Huffman length
field for every base or reference opcode, serializes the $K$ definitions
in increasing term-size order, then writes the full clause body.
References inside definitions may point only to earlier entries.
Variables in a definition are the already-normalized local variable
slots of the enclosing clause when that definition is used. This is a
shared syntax template, not a new first-order symbol or a new clause.
The independently implemented decoder expands every reference to recover
the exact original clause trees.

For all three raw variants the best selected dictionary is
\[
 r_0=H(v_0,v_1),\qquad
 r_1=C(C(v_0,d,v_2),q,v_3).
\]
For $\RPZ$, the header consists of three gamma bits for $K+1=3$
and $4(15+2)=68$ code-length bits. The definitions cost 41 bits,
and the entire clause body including its terminator costs 675 bits:
\[
 \boxed{71+41+675=787\text{ bits}.}
\]
For $\RP$ the corresponding sum is $75+41+674=790$.
For $\RPV$ it is $71+41+676=788$.
The reversed ranking of the two raw variants is a concrete reason to
measure each actual representation instead of predicting cost from the
number of functions or flat term nodes alone.

The search enumerates every subset of the syntactically repeated
compound subterms after the specified per-clause alphabetical variable
normalization. There are ten candidates and 1024 subsets in each raw
variant, thirteen candidates and 8192 subsets in each extensional
variant. It verifies a complete encode/decode round trip for every
candidate tested. This is exhaustive within those stated finite candidate
sets; other variable permutations, other macro forms, other compression
algorithms, and different decoder designs were not optimized. The fully
materialized DAG column instead charges its node count, fixed-width
function tags, every backward-reference edge, every literal root
reference, and all clause framing. That particular generic format loses
even though it eliminates every repeated compound subtree.

\section{Why a small UTM does not yet provide the state result}

Neary and Woods supply a standard single-tape fifteen-state two-symbol
UTM, with a finite input encoding of a bi-tag system and its dataword~\cite{nearywoods2009}.
Its exterior tape is uniformly blank $c$; it does not require a weakly
universal periodic background. However, their Equation (3) requires a
specific finite frame, with the encoded production table to the left of
the separator $G=bc$, optional $S=(cc)^2$ blocks, and encoded bi-tag
data to the right. Its prescribed start is state $u_1$ with the head on
the rightmost symbol of $G$. The blank-start initializer must establish
this entire finite frame and that entry position.
Their Table 1 encodes a data symbol $a_i$ as $(cb)^{8i-5}$, followed by
the separator $bb$ in a dataword. Thus encoding a literal bit as $a_1$
or $a_2$ takes eight or twenty-four tape cells. The 902-bit stream here
contains 611 zeros and 291 ones. Under that particular bit-to-symbol
mapping, its dataword alone occupies
\[
 8\cdot611+24\cdot291=11872
\]
cells, before the encoded production table, control symbol, or markers.
This is a measurement of that literal input encoding, not a lower bound
on all ways of programming or initializing that UTM.

The UTM's fifteen states contain no parser for these eighteen clauses.
A real input must additionally specify a bi-tag program that enumerates
terms, ground substitutions and valuations, evaluates clauses, exhausts
each finite stage, and detects the refutation condition. An introspective
printer could compress the repeated unary runs in that input, but its
data mechanism, decompressor, return path and final UTM entry frame must
all be compiled and counted. No such binary backend is supplied here.
Consequently neither $15+113$ nor any ratio of 902 bits to a chosen
bits-per-state rate is an established state bound.

The useful next target is concrete: compile this four-variable,
four-predicate ground-CNF refuter, including a measured data emitter,
and measure the complete binary table from blank. The mathematical
compression removes semantic congruence work as well as static rows,
but only that compilation can establish whether it beats 245 states.

\section{Provenance and proof status}

The general finite assignment/class method is attributed to Harvey
Friedman's \emph{Impossible Counting}, Section 4.1~\cite{friedman2017}.
Its Theorem 4.1.3 constructs the expansion by externally enumerating
parameter-definable classes over a countable model; the internal
assignment operations and external class-code interpretation are distinct.
The fifteen native
closure rows and the raw/full-parameter EPSC consistency calibration are
from ProveIt source44 and its cited earlier sources. The direct
raw-profile interpretation, three-coordinate power-object proof of an
infinite data quotient without any function congruence,
independent-selector constants, and the resulting four-variable
opcode reuse are the additions in this note. No wider historical priority
claim is made.

The source44 reference modules are copied with their repository license
from the same directory as its note, under
\path{code/44-nine-code-ground_kernel.py} and
\path{code/44-nine-code-coded_extensional.py}. The current repository
