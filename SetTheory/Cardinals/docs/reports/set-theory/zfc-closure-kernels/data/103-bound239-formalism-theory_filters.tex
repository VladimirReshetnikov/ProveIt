\section{Two precise filters on proposed smaller set-theory kernels}
\label{sec:theory-filters}

The smallest displayed formula is not necessarily the cheapest complete
consistency search.  In particular, a proposed deletion from the present
container axiom must retain both its coverage of arbitrary objects and its
requirement that a set bounding all subsets be an element of the same
container.  The two statements below isolate these obligations.  They
supplement the existing deletion examples rather than claiming a new general
countermodel method or a new Turing-machine bound.

\subsection{Exact language and the retained schema}

Use classical first-order logic with native equality, a nonempty domain, and
one binary relation $\in$.  Put
\[
 x\subseteq y\quad:\!\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \forall z\,(z\in x\longrightarrow z\in y),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}(V)\quad:\!\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       \forall x\in V\;(x\subseteq V).
\]
The current container axiom is
\begin{equation}
\forall a\;\exists V\left[
 a\in V\ \land\ \operatorname{Tr}(V)\ \land\
 \forall x\in V\;\exists W\in V\;\forall z
       (z\subseteq x\longrightarrow z\in W)\right].
\tag{CP}\label{eq:filter-cp}
\end{equation}
For every actual finite formula $\theta(x,y;\bar p)$, including formulas
with equality and arbitrarily many parameters, retain
\begin{equation}
\forall\bar p\;\forall A\;\exists B\left[
 \forall x\in A\bigl(\exists y\,\theta(x,y;\bar p)
             \longrightarrow\exists y\in B\,\theta(x,y;\bar p)\bigr)
 \ \land\ \forall y\in B\;\exists x\,\theta(x,y;\bar p)
 \right].
\tag{GIC}\label{eq:filter-gic}
\end{equation}
All parameters are universally closed; all auxiliary bound variables are
fresh.  The supporting input in the last clause is unrestricted.  This is
an effective infinite schema, not an axiom for arbitrary semantic relations.
Write $\mathrm{IR}$ for $\forall x\,(x\notin x)$.  These are the native
theory interfaces documented in ProveIt's
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/13ec39ed43148b1807f51ed6e6528e1c84659562/Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/native_theory254.md}{\texttt{native\_theory254.md}}
and
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/13ec39ed43148b1807f51ed6e6528e1c84659562/Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/equality_free_bisimulation.md}{\texttt{equality\_free\_bisimulation.md}};
the latter displays the same formulas before restricting its matrix language.

\subsection{One good container does not replace coverage of every root}

Replace \eqref{eq:filter-cp} by the shorter fixed assertion
\begin{equation}
\exists V\left[
 (\exists a\;a\in V)\ \land\ \operatorname{Tr}(V)\ \land\
 \forall x\in V\;\exists W\in V\;\forall z
       (z\subseteq x\longrightarrow z\in W)\right].
\tag{CP$_{\exists}$}\label{eq:filter-rootless}
\end{equation}
The assertion still requests a nonempty transitive container and genuine
internal power bounds.  Its only missing feature is coverage of an arbitrary
external root $a$.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
Let $T_{\exists}$ contain $\mathrm{CP}_{\exists}$, all instances of GIC,
and all the axioms of ZFC except Power Set, with full Separation and
Replacement retained.  Then ZFC proves that $T_{\exists}$ has a set model,
and consequently proves $\operatorname{Con}(T_{\exists})$.  In particular,
adding Extensionality, Empty Set, Pairing, Union, Infinity, Foundation,
Choice, or IR to the shortened packet does not repair it.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Work in ZFC and take
\[
 M=H_{\omega_1}
   =\{x: \operatorname{TC}(\{x\})\text{ is countable}\},
\]
with actual membership and equality.  This is a nonempty transitive set.
Every element of $M$ is countable, and a countable collection of elements
of $M$ is again an element of $M$: its transitive closure is contained in
a countable union of countable transitive closures.  The use of countable
choice in this sentence occurs in the ambient ZFC proof.

Fix an internally interpreted GIC matrix, its finite parameter tuple in
$M$, and $A\in M$.  In the ambient universe form
\[
 D=\{x\in A:M\models\exists y\,\theta(x,y;\bar p)\}.
\]
The domain $D$ is countable.  Choose, in the ambient universe, one
$f(x)\in M$ satisfying the matrix for each $x\in D$, and put
$B=\{f(x):x\in D\}$.  If $D$ is empty take $B=\varnothing$.
The closure fact above gives $B\in M$.  It supplies a witness for every
nonempty fiber over $A$, and each of its members has a supporting input
in $D\subseteq A$.  Thus it satisfies even the domain-guarded support
condition, which is stronger than the support required by GIC.

The same closure fact gives Pairing, Union, and every Replacement instance.
For Separation, any externally defined subset of $A\in M$ belongs to $M$,
in particular a trace defined using the set-model satisfaction relation.
Actual transitivity gives Extensionality and Foundation; $\omega\in M$
gives Infinity.  An ambient choice function for a member $A$ of $M$ has
countable graph and hereditarily countable values, hence belongs to $M$,
so the internal Choice axiom also holds.

Finally $V_\omega\in M$ is a witness to $\mathrm{CP}_{\exists}$.
It is countable, transitive, and nonempty.  For $x\in V_\omega$, every
subset of $x$ is hereditarily finite and the finite set
$W=\mathcal P(x)$ also belongs to $V_\omega$.  Because $M$ is transitive,
its subset relation between its elements is the actual subset relation,
so these are exactly the required internal power bounds.

For clarity, $M$ does not satisfy Power Set at $\omega$.  All actual
subsets of $\omega$ belong to $M$, while every member of $M$ is
countable.  A putative internal power set of $\omega$ would therefore
be countable and contain all actual reals, contrary to Cantor's theorem.
The original CP fails at the root $a=\omega$ for the same reason: its
power bound for that member would have to contain all reals.

This is a uniform set-model argument.  ZFC has a satisfaction relation
for the set $M$ and can quantify over finite formula codes and their
finite parameter tuples in the preceding witness construction.  It
therefore proves that every axiom of the recursively presented packet
holds in $M$.  Formalized first-order soundness gives
$\operatorname{Con}(T_{\exists})$.  We are not merely asserting that
each externally fixed axiom has some unrelated model.\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Containing each subset is weaker than containing its bound}

A second plausible simplification removes the existential $W$ from the
power clause and puts its elements directly in $V$:
\begin{equation}
\forall a\;\exists V\left[
 a\in V\ \land\ \operatorname{Tr}(V)\ \land\
 \forall x\in V\;\forall z
       (z\subseteq x\longrightarrow z\in V)\right].
\tag{CP$_{\mathrm{flat}}$}\label{eq:filter-flat}
\end{equation}
CP implies this sentence, because the old bound $W$ lies in a transitive
$V$.  The converse is false even when all native GIC instances, full
Extensionality, Foundation, Separation, Replacement, and ordinary Power
Set are available.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
Let $T_{\mathrm{flat}}$ contain $\mathrm{CP}_{\mathrm{flat}}$, all GIC
instances, and all axioms of ZFC except Infinity.  ZFC proves that
$V_\omega$ is a set model of $T_{\mathrm{flat}}+\neg\mathrm{Infinity}$;
in particular it proves $\operatorname{Con}(T_{\mathrm{flat}})$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Every member of $V_\omega$ is finite.  For any matrix interpreted in this
set structure and any domain $A\in V_\omega$, choose one witness from
each nonempty fiber over the finite set $A$.  The finite set of chosen
witnesses belongs to $V_\omega$ and satisfies both GIC clauses.  The
argument works uniformly for all finite formula codes via satisfaction
for the set $V_\omega$.  The other listed set axioms hold because finite
sets are closed under their relevant finite constructions; Separation
and Replacement again use the internally interpreted matrix.

Given $a\in V_\omega$, choose a natural $n>\operatorname{rank}(a)$.
Then $V_n\in V_\omega$, $a\in V_n$, and $V_n$ is transitive.
If $x\in V_n$ and $z\subseteq x$, then
$\operatorname{rank}(z)\leq\operatorname{rank}(x)<n$, so $z\in V_n$.
Thus $V_n$ is a witness to $\mathrm{CP}_{\mathrm{flat}}$.
There is no inductive set in $V_\omega$: such a finite set would have
to contain every finite von Neumann ordinal.  The set-model soundness
argument yields the consistency conclusion as above.\hfill$\square$

There is also a compact arithmetic witness that makes the lost resource
visible.  Interpret membership on natural numbers by Ackermann coding:
$i\mathrel{E}x$ iff bit $i$ of $x$ is one.  For the root $a$, put
\[
 v=2^{a+1}-1.
\]
Its members are precisely $0,\ldots,a$, so $aEv$.  If $xEv$ and $uEx$,
then $u<x\leq a$, giving transitivity.  If $z\subseteq_E x$, bit
inclusion implies $z\leq x\leq a$, so $zEv$.  Therefore the same single
arithmetic term witnesses all of $\mathrm{CP}_{\mathrm{flat}}$.
Nevertheless this particular $v$ never supplies the original internal
power bound for its member $a$.  Such a bound $W\mathrel{E}v$ would
have to contain $a$, since $a\subseteq_E a$.  But $W\leq a<2^a$,
whereas $aEW$ requires $W\geq 2^a$.

The companion diagnostic checks these exact bit identities and witness
failures for a finite range of roots.  It is a regression check of the
displayed constructions, not a finite proof of the infinite model
argument.  The arithmetic calculation is compatible with the existing
PA interpretation of the transitive-container/GIC theory in
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/13ec39ed43148b1807f51ed6e6528e1c84659562/Computability/BusyBeaver/Research/ZFC/mira_containers.md}{\texttt{mira\_containers.md}}.
No serialized PA interpretation or arithmetic consistency certificate is
exported here.

\subsection{What the consistency filter actually excludes}

Fix the usual effective finite-proof consistency predicates.  For either
of the two weakened packets $T$, the preceding argument gives
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash\operatorname{Con}(T).
\]
Consequently, if ZFC is consistent, ZFC cannot also prove
$\operatorname{Con}(T)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})$:
that would give a ZFC proof of its own consistency, contradicting the
second incompleteness theorem.  If a literal machine is correctly
compiled so that ZFC proves its nonhalting equivalent to
$\operatorname{Con}(T)$, ZFC will prove that machine nonhalting.  It
therefore cannot simultaneously have the requested ZFC-provable
equivalence with $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC})$, assuming consistency.

This conclusion excludes the stated presentations as targets for the
requested calibration.  It does not say that every unrelated
interpretation into those theories is impossible, assign a lower bound
on binary states, or replace a required source-to-machine proof by a
semantic slogan.  The argument is an ordinary proof formalizable in
ZFC; this appendix supplies no Lean, Rocq, or serialized first-order proof.

\subsection{Prior overlap and the surviving engineering question}

The repository already uses hereditarily finite and hereditarily
countable models to reject other deletions from positive-closure
schemas; see the source 01--03 reconciliation in
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/13ec39ed43148b1807f51ed6e6528e1c84659562/SetTheory/Cardinals/docs/reports/set-theory/zfc-closure-kernels/README.md}{the closure-kernel host}.
The two applications here concern the exact CP interfaces
\eqref{eq:filter-rootless} and \eqref{eq:filter-flat}.  No historical
priority is claimed for the models or the general consistency argument.
The existing
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/13ec39ed43148b1807f51ed6e6528e1c84659562/SetTheory/Cardinals/docs/reports/set-theory/zfc-closure-kernels/intake-144-source-09-theory.md}{source 09 review}
instead removes CP's transitivity and uses a pseudo-hereditary model at
a singular strong limit; the primary construction is discussed by
Greg Oman, \emph{On the axiom of union}, Arch. Math. Logic 49 (2010),
283--289, \href{https://doi.org/10.1007/s00153-009-0163-1}{doi:10.1007/s00153-009-0163-1}.
The current
\href{https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/blob/13ec39ed43148b1807f51ed6e6528e1c84659562/SetTheory/Cardinals/docs/reports/set-theory/zfc-closure-kernels/intake-170.md}{source 80 reconciliation}
uses rational cuts to reject deletion of GIC support and proves that
full native GIC is equivalent to weak Collection plus full Separation.
These are distinct weakened packets.

The successful construction in Section~\ref{sec:fold239} follows the source
route: it keeps the already calibrated full theory and complete calculus,
while changing which binary decoder contexts can be shared.  The raw PSC, finite
assignment kernels, and alternative complete proof languages are useful
longer-term comparison specifications.  Their formula counts and finite
certificate sizes do not establish a smaller complete binary machine.
Even a source reassociation saving one decoder state must charge any
extra decoder depth or change in the blank initialization contract.
