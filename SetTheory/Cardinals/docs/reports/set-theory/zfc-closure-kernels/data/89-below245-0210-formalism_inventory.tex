\section{Which alternative formalisms preserve the target?}
\label{sec:formalism-inventory}

The objective requires an effective refutation equivalence, not merely
that a theory is strong enough to prove some set-theoretic theorems.
For a recursively axiomatized theory $T$, the usable interface is
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash
  (\exists p\,\operatorname{Prf}_{T}(p,\bot))
  \longleftrightarrow
  (\exists q\,\operatorname{Prf}_{\mathrm{ZFC}}(q,\bot)).
\]
A proof enumerator for $T$ then has the required nonhalting target
once its literal simulation is established.  A finite formula wrapper,
a finite list of axiom names, and a finite first-order axiomatization
are different objects.  In particular, quantifying over all matrices
in a schema retains an infinite recursive family.

\subsection{ZF, SIR, and the existing relative-falsity encoding}

Omitting Choice from ZFC is sound for this target: the usual
constructibility interpretation supplies the reverse consistency
direction, while inclusion supplies the other.  Omitting Foundation
also has a separate effective consistency comparison.  The repository
carefully distinguishes its foundation-free intermediate $\mathrm{ZF}_0$
from standard ZF.  These reductions already underlie the retained
machine and do not represent an additional state saving.  Their
source interfaces are \path{WFTheory.native_refutation_iff_zf}
and \path{ChoiceCalibration254.zf_refutation_iff_zfc}.

The native SIR presentation has ordinary first-order equality, the
container sentence CP, one instance of GIC for every finite formula
with parameters, and irreflexivity IR.  With
$x\subseteq V$ abbreviating $\forall z(z\in x\to z\in V)$,
\[
\begin{split}
\mathrm{CP}:\quad \forall p\exists V\,[p\in V\land
 \forall x\,(x\in V\to (x\subseteq V\land
  \exists W\,(W\in V\land
    \forall z(z\subseteq x\to z\in W))))],
\end{split}
\]
and, for each matrix $\varphi(x,y;\bar p)$,
\[
\begin{split}
\mathrm{GIC}[\varphi]:\quad\forall B\exists C\,[{}&
 \forall a\,(a\in B\to
  \forall y(\varphi(a,y)\to
    \exists y'(\varphi(a,y')\land y'\in C)))\\
 &{}\land\forall y(y\in C\to\exists x\,\varphi(x,y))].
\end{split}
\]
All displayed parameters are universally closed and auxiliary names
are fresh.  The source uses an equivalent compact variable-sharing
version with its stated gates.  SIR is nonextensional at the raw
membership level; the consistency proof uses a represented quotient
and the separate Foundation/Choice comparisons.  It does not obtain
Extensionality simply by declaring duplicate elements identical.

The machine already avoids an emitted IR axiom.  Its relative falsity
is $F=R(0,0)$, and replacing primitive $\bot$ by $F$ sends
the comparison sentence $\forall_0(R(0,0)\to\bot)$ to
\[
 \forall_0(F\to F),
\]
which follows by generalization of propositional identity.
Every binder of label zero also binds occurrences inside $F$;
global irreflexivity makes that capture harmless in the semantic
comparison.  This is the explicit alias translation in
\path{TarskiFalsity.lean}, through the theorems
\path{Tarski.thm_tau} and \path{Tarski.alias_complete};
it is not an argument
that one may delete IR from an ordinary classical theory and ask
for an arbitrary atomic theorem instead of a contradiction.
The original numerical bridge handles the truth and falsity aliases.
No fixed proof-length bound is permitted: the enumerator must search
all finite candidates.  Bounding each local validation computation
is compatible with an unbounded enumeration of those candidates.

\subsection{A rigorous equality-free SIR control}

There is an actual equality-free formalism at the same consistency
strength.  Use ordinary equality-free first-order logic, one binary
predicate $R$, and the equality-free CP, IR and full equality-free
GIC schemas.  Call this $\mathrm{SIR}^{-}$.  Define
\[
\begin{split}
D(x,y):\quad&\forall z\,(R(z,x)\leftrightarrow R(z,y))\\
 &{}\land\forall z\,(R(x,z)\leftrightarrow R(y,z)).
\end{split}
\]
Equality-free logic proves that $D$ is an equivalence relation and
that replacing either argument of $R$ by a $D$-equivalent argument
preserves its truth.  An induction on formulas gives substitution
for every translated formula.  Consequently replacing every
equality atom by $D$ translates every equality axiom into a theorem
of equality-free logic.  CP and IR are fixed, and a GIC instance
becomes the GIC instance of the translated matrix, which is in
the full equality-free schema.  This gives an effective
falsity-preserving translation
\[
  \mathrm{SIR}\vdash\bot
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathrm{SIR}^{-}\vdash\bot.
\]
The reverse implication follows by viewing an equality-free proof
as an equality-bearing proof.  Thus $\mathrm{SIR}^{-}$ is
equiconsistent with SIR and ZFC by explicit proof translations.

The two-sided definition of $D$ is essential to this short proof.
Equality of predecessor profiles alone need not imply congruence
in the other argument of a nonextensional relation.  More importantly,
the formalism uses a \emph{complete} equality-free proof calculus.
Deleting equality rows from the present small Tarski engine does
not implement that calculus: its equality rows carry the variable
transport needed to derive full first-order instantiation.  The
repository's \path{logical_engine_completeness.md} and
\path{typed_countermodel.md} already document this obstruction.
This is a sound alternative theory, with no newly established
complete-machine state count.

\subsection{Positive subset-seeded closure}

A different existing compact presentation uses Extensionality and
one positive closure schema.  For a seed formula $\sigma(x,a)$,
let
\[
\operatorname{SB}(\rho):=
 \forall x\exists b\forall y(\rho(x,y)\to y\subseteq b).
\]
The schema is
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{SB}(\rho)\to\forall a\exists C[{}&
\forall x\forall y(((\sigma(x,a)\lor x\in C)\land\rho(x,y))
                     \to y\in C)\\
&{}\land\forall y(y\in C\to\exists x\,\rho(x,y))].
\end{split}
\]
Both point seeding $\sigma(x,a)\equiv x=a$ and subset seeding
$\sigma(x,a)\equiv x\subseteq a$ are deductively equivalent,
with Extensionality, to the repository's foundation-free,
choice-free $\mathrm{ZF}_0$.  All matrices and finite parameter
tuples are retained.

The forward reduction has concrete content.  Constant matrices
give exact bounded classes, including Empty Set, singletons and
Power Set.  A gated singleton decoder gives a transitive cover of
each root's members before Union has been established.  Those
covers yield Separation and Union.  A two-cycle functional relation
gives Pairing; a Russell root outside the input set yields functional
Replacement without assuming Collection; a successor relation
then gives Infinity.  In the reverse direction, each locally bounded
fiber is a unique exact subset of a power set.  Replacement collects
these exact fibers, and their finite-stage iteration gives the
required positive hull.  This avoids choosing arbitrary local bounds.
These are the existing proofs in \path{subset_only.md}; the
subset-seeded equality-bearing result has the repository's Lean
\path{PSCCalibration} implementation.  They are prior work,
not new claims of this experiment.

An equality-free version replaces equality by predecessor-profile
equality and adds the explicit sentence asserting congruence in
the other membership argument.  Its full calibration depends on
that sentence.  Under the repository's formula-tree metric, the
point-seeded equality-bearing theory has 65 nodes, the subset-seeded
one 68, and the equality-free congruence-plus-closure theory 79.
These numbers exclude variable payloads, proof-checking and the
binary backend.  They therefore cannot be compared directly with
245 states or with the 493-call orientation word.

\subsection{Finite class and algebraic presentations}

Finite class-theory presentations offer a different tradeoff: a
finite axiom test replaces an arbitrary-matrix schema generator,
but the fixed class axioms and their full first-order engine must
be stored and interpreted.  A conventional finite presentation of
NBG, with its set-theory consistency comparison and the same
constructibility reduction, has the required consistency
calibration to ZFC.  The library of prior incoming
research already includes a 14-sentence transcription of Friedman's
class presentation (752 formula nodes in its stated metric),
plus its class/set scopes and a repaired local Capture argument.
Here the unary set predicate has been eliminated as
$M(x)\leftrightarrow\exists y\,R(x,y)$, removing the corresponding
definitional axiom from the author's 15-axiom presentation.\footnote{
Harvey M. Friedman, \emph{Simplified axioms for class theory},
15 September 2020, sections 1--3:
\url{https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/1/1952/files/2020/09/NBGAxioms091520.pdf}.}
The set-theory theorem does not make those 14 sentences 14 machine
instructions.  We did not replace that data with a claimed smaller
calibrated transition table.

The same accounting applies to equational, relation-algebraic,
combinatory and lambda-calculus encodings.  A complete finite
algebraic presentation can reduce the syntax of proofs, while
moving first-order substitution, relation pairing, class theory
or satisfaction into fixed terms.  The prior inventory
\path{38-equation-experiments-equational-literature_notes.md}
and the concrete development
\path{41-cyclic-notes-algebraic_development.md} retain these
calibration obligations.  Merely compiling the existing proof
recognizer to a universal combinator does not erase the recognizer
or its blank-tape loader.  No new state count is claimed for these
routes.

The new work in this packet therefore concentrates on a presentation
change that can be costed without replacing the established
first-order engine: the 32 directly equivalent equality orientations,
with 32 additional converse-membership controls.  All their emitted
source words are provided.  Their logical preservation theorem is
separate from, and considerably easier than, establishing a smaller
complete binary machine.
