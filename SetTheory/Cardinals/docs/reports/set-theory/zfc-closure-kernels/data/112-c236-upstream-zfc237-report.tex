\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit binary, one-tape Turing machine with
\textbf{\FinalStates\ working states, including its blank-tape initializer},
and an ordinary mathematical proof formalizable in ZFC that its nonhalting
is equivalent to $\Con(\ZFC)$.  The tape is two-way infinite, every move is
left or right, and the separate halting outcome is not counted as a working
state.  Thus the construction meets the requested threshold of fewer than
239 states without a free input, prewritten tape, or extra alphabet symbol.

The starting point is the maintained 240-state source-rotation machine.
Two incoming reports contain distinct 239-state improvements: a source
reassociation with decoder sharing, and a coordinated runtime-helper fold.
We prove that these improvements compose to 238 states.  The substantive
compatibility check is a universal tape guard on the changed source;
disjoint edits alone would not justify composition.
\ifFurtherReduction
A further newly discovered coordinated decoder fold then removes one more
state, giving the primary 237-state machine.  Its simulation uses an
inductive local tape invariant and finite positive blocks.
\fi

The literal final machine is independently checked on all 8,386 guarded
source-instruction interfaces, with arbitrary natural register values,
arbitrary nonnegative padding, and an arbitrary finite spare tail.  We
include complete transition tables, deterministic constructors, finite
source and local certificates, full runtime records, and independent
source, tape, and startup checkers.  The exact final startup takes
$\FinalBoot$ steps.  Existing source/calculus/ZFC calibration is explicitly
inherited.  This report does not supply a newly kernel-checked Lean or
Rocq endpoint or a serialized object-theory derivation for the final table.
\end{abstract}

\section{The result and its exact scope}

\begin{theorem}[Concrete consistency machine]\label{thm:main}
Let $M_{\FinalStates}$ be the complete table printed in
Appendix~\ref{app:table}, equivalently the canonical table identified below.
With start state $0$, head position $0$, and the all-zero tape,
\[
 \ZFC\ \vdash\
 \bigl(\Halt(M_{\FinalStates},\blank)
       \longleftrightarrow
       \exists p\,\ProofRef_{\ZFC}(p)\bigr).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{\quad\ZFC\ \vdash\
  \bigl(\neg\Halt(M_{\FinalStates},\blank)
         \longleftrightarrow\Con(\ZFC)\bigr).\quad}
\]
The machine has exactly $\FinalStates$ working states and $\FinalRows$
transition rows.  Initialization is included in those states.
\end{theorem}

Here $\ProofRef_{\ZFC}$ is the usual effective predicate for a finite
first-order ZFC refutation, identified with the finite proof-tree
presentation used by the inherited calibration.  Structural recursion
encodes and decodes the proof trees; customary alternative effective
proof codings give the same consistency statement by finite syntactic
translations.  We do not redefine $\Con(\ZFC)$ using the machine itself.

\begin{proposition}[A sufficient composition interface]\label{prop:interface}
Suppose a source-calibrated machine $A$ has an exact blank prefix entering
a family $\mathcal F$ of source frames.  Suppose every source instruction
has a positive finite realization on $A$ with no interior halt:
nonhalting instructions preserve $\mathcal F$, and the source halt reaches
the designated halt.  Suppose all these realizations satisfy a guard $G$
at every relevant intermediate control.  Let a finite state map send the
initial control to the initial control and map a control to halt exactly
when that control is halt.  Suppose it sends each guarded step of $A$ to
a positive block of a second machine $B$, with the same
entire tape, head, and mapped endpoint, and with no interior halt.
If the same guard holds during the blank prefix, then $A$ and $B$ have
equivalent blank-tape halting.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Frame closure and induction over source instructions establish $G$ on
every step after initialization.  The finite prefix supplies the missing
initial segment.  Replacing each old step by its verified positive block
relates the two deterministic executions at all checkpoints.  A finite
old halt gives a finite new halt.  An infinite old execution supplies
infinitely many positive new blocks and so cannot correspond to a finite
new halt.  The argument uses ordinary natural-number and finite-list
induction.  The frame theorem is needed only to establish the guard;
the block identities themselves may use arbitrarily large tape exteriors.
\end{proof}

This proposition gives a reusable obligation for future combinations:
carry the guard as part of the optimization's interface and re-establish
it after a source or encoding change.  The present construction provides
the exact finite and unbounded instances of that obligation.

\section{The source transformation and its calibration}
\label{sec:source}

Let $P_0$ be the maintained source-rotation240 program and $P_1$ the
boundary program.  Both have 6,455 ordered instruction occurrences.  Their
source representation is a finite full binary tree with increment,
conditional decrement, break, continue, and halt leaves.  Repeated
subtrees are stored once in a directed acyclic graph.

\bottomrule

These source counts are distinct from binary machine state counts.  The

It leaves every ordered leaf instruction and operand unchanged.  The last

from the complete control graph, rather than a restrictive rule requiring
all rotated chunks to be closed.

\subsection{A finite certificate for every register valuation}

For a path word $u$, let $\operatorname{loc}_P(u)$ follow $u0^\omega$
from the source root and stop at the first leaf.  Any input suffix beyond
the reached leaf is not used by the source step.  Increment is fixed-width
binary increment, with all-ones rollover to all zeros.  Break strips a
suffix through the indicated zero, then increments the remaining word;
continue strips through the indicated one.  Exhaustion gives the empty
word.  These are the consumed-leaf path conventions in the inherited
\file{RegisterPath.lean} semantics~\cite{registerpath}.

Each source leaf has a complete finite descriptor: its operation and
register, its successor leaf, and for decrement both the positive and
zero successors.  A halt leaf has a halt descriptor.  Break and continue
are represented by their actual destination leaf.  Descriptor equality
therefore compares the entire finite control relation, rather than a
sample of reachable branches.

\begin{lemma}[Source control isomorphism]\label{lem:source}
If two finite full source trees have the same ordered instruction word
and identical descriptors at every leaf, their source executions from
the empty path are strongly bisimilar for every initial natural register
file.  In particular their halting predicates agree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Relate two source configurations when their register files are equal and
their located leaf indices agree.  An increment changes the same register
and selects the same successor.  A decrement tests equal register values,
chooses the same branch, and makes the same update.  Each jump preserves
the registers and has the same target.  The halt descriptor halts on both
sides.  These cases preserve the relation one source instruction at a
time.  Full finite source trees ensure every decoder walk reaches a leaf.
\end{proof}

The inherited 13-stage checker verifies 8,385 successor/guard edges and
one halt descriptor after \emph{every} stage.  A fresh independently
written checker uses integer path arithmetic and verifies the same final
control graph.  It also rejects changed decrement operands, missing
leaves, and duplicate leaves.  Neither checker samples register values.

Both checks enforce an additional physical-interface fact: every unused
successor suffix consists entirely of zeros.  Thus replacing a path word
by its reached leaf does not discard a nonzero tape cell.  Empty-path
initialization and the path to the first all-zero leaf have the same
zero-padded physical representation.  This is required when concatenating
the binary instruction contracts; source bisimulation alone would not
justify discarding arbitrary unused tape bits.

\subsection{The inherited ZFC consistency endpoint}

Let
\[
 R_*=(3,0,0,0,3,0,0,0,0),
\]
with all subsequent logical registers zero.  The pinned theorem
\file{CalibratedRotation240.halts_iff_zfc_refutation} identifies
\[
 \operatorname{RegisterHalts}(P_0,(\epsilon,R_*))
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{BProv}(\mathrm{ZFCax\_s},[],\bot).
 \tag{C0}
\]
It is not a theorem inferred from a README label: the package retains its
actual source, the explicit seed, and the source-tree definitions.
The source-interface checker parses every one of the 6,455 ordered source
triples directly from the pinned Lean row definitions, lookup tree, and
overrides, and identifies the logical/physical register encoding.
It is a static data check, not a Lean elaborator~\cite{calibration}.

The mathematical ancestor of (C0) connects the enumerator to the native
container/global-image-collection/irreflexivity calculus, then to ZF and
ZFC.  The retained \file{WFTheory.lean} and
\file{ConstructibleChoice.lean} sources identify the relevant comparison
arguments: a quotient with an internally well-founded part, first-order

\section{Unbounded binary runtime and exact initialization}
\label{sec:runtime}

Runtime control labels in this section are inherited labels; canonical
state numbers are explicitly identified where used.

\subsection{What a valid frame represents}

For a natural number $v$, set $B(v)=01^{v+1}$.  A dispatch frame has a
single marker $1$ immediately left of the head, a zero-padded source path,
a header $1$, nine physical register blocks, and a nonempty finite spare
list of the same block shape.  Everything outside the finite support is
zero.  In coordinates where the dispatcher head is at zero, its right
word is
\[
 p\,0^{64+g-|p|}\,1\,
 B(r_{\rm phy,0})\cdots B(r_{\rm phy,8})\,
 B(s_0)\cdots B(s_k)\,0^\omega,
 \qquad g,s_i,r_j\in\mathbb N.
\]
The additional gap $g$ is arbitrary, $k\geq0$, and all primary registers
vary independently.  The longest path of $P_1$ has length 48, so the
displayed padding is nonnegative.  The logical-to-physical map is fixed
by the source-interface check, rather than inferred from a numerical run.

The expected successor changes the requested register and source control
exactly as the abstract instruction specifies.  Decrement and source
rollover can enlarge the gap.  Increment appends a value-$1$ spare; every
instruction preserves existing spare values and keeps the spare suffix
nonempty.  The head displacement is checked exactly, including rollover.

\subsection{The mathematical rules used by the runtime checker}

Run lengths and durations are affine expressions in thirteen slots:
a constant, a gap, nine independent primary values, a first-spare value,
and remaining spare-list width.  A symbolic run is only consumed by a
literal nonloop transition when its constant term guarantees a cell is
present.  Coefficient checks prevent a silent negative or unknown-length
case split.  Zero and positive decrement guards are proved separately;
in the positive branch the value is represented as $1+x$.

The proof uses three types of rule:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A literal transition from the supplied complete binary table.
\item Natural-number induction over a unary run when the current row is
a self-loop moving in the scan direction.  This permits an unbounded
scan, including a run whose instantiated length is zero.
\item Structural induction over an arbitrary finite list, after the
corresponding one-block theorem has been rederived from the exact table.
\end{enumerate}

For the third rule, put $C(v)=101^v$.  The four block schemas are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rcllc}
\toprule
Control & Direction & Input family & Output family & Actual steps\\\midrule
73 & right & $B(v)$ & $B(v)$ & $v+2$\\
95 & right & $B(v)$ & $C(v)$ & $v+2$\\
48 & left & $B(v)$ & $B(v)$ & $v+2$\\
112 & left & $C(v)$ & $B(v)$ & $v+2$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each schema quantifies over arbitrary tape exteriors.  The word identity
$C(A)1=1B(A)$ for a finite list $A$, and its reversal, are proved by
empty-list/cons induction and used in normalization.  For one element,
$(101^v)1=1(01^{v+1})$; concatenating this identity proves the list case.

Every nonhalting interface must reach the exact target state, complete
tape expression, and head displacement.  Every halt interface must reach
the designated halt.  All accepted durations have positive constant
terms.  The checker cannot certify a nonhalting instruction by silently
passing through halt: the complete table has no halt row to execute.

The 4,000-rule limit inside the engine bounds construction of a finite
proof for one symbolic case.  It places no bound on the represented
natural numbers, spare-list length, or actual Turing-machine running time.
The complete 238-state proof checks 8,386 cases, executes 2,255,513 symbolic
rules, and covers all 476 rows.  The minimum constant instruction duration
is 13.  Its parameter domain is not a finite collection of small-register
test fixtures.

\subsection{Composing the mathematical endpoint}

Frame closure and positive instruction lengths imply
\[
 \Halt(M_{238},\blank)
 \longleftrightarrow
 \operatorname{RegisterHalts}(P_1,(\epsilon,R_*)).
 \tag{R238}
\]
A finite source halt gives a finite binary halt.  Conversely an infinite
source execution supplies arbitrarily many positive binary segments,
each excluding an interior halt.  Thus it cannot hide a finite binary
halt.  The argument is valid for the exact infinite blank tape, because
both initialization and every interface equate the entire tape, not a
bounded window around the head.

Compose (R238), the source isomorphism, and (C0).  This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} for 238 states.

The independent checkers reduce accidental implementation risk but do
not make the trust base disappear.  The literal simulator, symbolic
engine, finite source checker, and cited calibration arguments must be
reviewed as mathematics and code.  In particular, a JSON label saying
\texttt{PASS} is not itself a mathematical inference.  The report explains
why the checked predicates imply the theorem, and the portable replay
refuses a failed, partial, mismatched, or missing full-runtime record.

The search logs distinguish discovery from proof.  Finite profiles are
used to propose helpers and folds; a survivor is accepted only after its
universal local or instruction proof and startup checks.  A failed search
within a fixed helper grammar is not a lower bound on general machines.
The preserved 238-state construction remains separately identified even
when the final machine is smaller.

\section{Consequences and the next useful formalization step}

Under $\Con(\ZFC)$ the final machine does not halt.  Under the ordinary
hypotheses of the second incompleteness theorem, ZFC cannot prove that
nonhalting statement: the proved biconditional would then let it prove
its own consistency.  Bare consistency alone does not justify the
additional statement that ZFC cannot prove the machine halts; the latter
needs an appropriate soundness assumption.  This report makes no
two-sided independence claim from consistency alone.

With the usual binary blank-input Busy Beaver running-time convention,
the construction places a consistency obstruction at
$\mathrm{BB}(\FinalStates)$.  If ZFC is consistent, it cannot prove a
correct finite value for that number: a proved upper bound together with
the finite check that this particular machine has not halted by that
bound would yield its nonhalting and hence $\Con(\ZFC)$.  This is a
conditional consequence of the explicit machine, not a computation of a
Busy Beaver value.

The most useful integration step is to formalize the small new local
simulation theorem(s), rather than re-derive the proof-search logic from
scratch.  The 238-state step has four exceptional rows, a finite
predecessor invariant, and the explicitly isolated (G169) runtime guard.
The full final source/runtime certificates offer a second formalization
route.  Their finite affine/list inductions can be translated into the
existing ProveIt runtime framework.  The exact source, seed, tables,
numberings, and hashes are all provided so that a formal endpoint can be
tied to this artifact without a provenance gap.
