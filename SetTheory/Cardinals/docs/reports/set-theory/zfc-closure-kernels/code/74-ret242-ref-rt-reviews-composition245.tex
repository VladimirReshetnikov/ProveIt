\section{Uniform padding and the spare invariant}

Write $B(v)=0\,1^{v+1}$. A dispatched frame consists of the left marker,
a finite instruction-pointer word $p$, zero padding, the header one, nine
primary blocks $B(r_i)$, and a nonempty finite list of spare blocks. The
unrepresented tape exterior is blank. Put
\[
 d=n+64,\qquad n\in\mathbb N.
\]
The padding immediately after $p$ therefore has length $n+64-|p|$.
Every leaf of the new complete decoder has depth at most 51, so this
quantity is nonnegative and is at least $n+13$ at any leaf. The initial
frame has $n=181-64=117$.

The complete source decoder has 6459 internal nodes, 6460 instruction
leaves, and 8391 guarded cases. Its maximum run of zero bits on a leaf
path is six. The actual shortened binary scanner, including its exceptional
zero-guard cases, is included in the full symbolic execution checks.
The six-zero bound is not used as a replacement for those checks.

For a source case, let $e=1$ for a positive decrement and $e=0$ otherwise.
Let $o=1$ when the complete fixed-width pointer increment overflows, and
$o=0$ otherwise. Break/zero-decrement pointer increments are included in
this definition. The checked target frame has
\[
 d'=d+e+o,\qquad n'=n+e+o.                 \tag{2}
\]
Thus $n\geq0$ is inductive. The three overflow cases are numbered 8267,
8356 and 8387 in the depth-first guarded-case order. Among the 8390
nonterminal cases, 6456 have $d'=d$ and 1934 have $d'=d+1$.

An increment appends one new spare block of value~1. Every other
nonterminal source operation preserves the spare list. Consequently
\[
 K'=K+1\ \text{for increment},\qquad K'=K\ \text{otherwise}.       \tag{3}
\]
In particular, $K\geq167$ is inductive. This discharges the actual premise
of the zero-decrement macro: even when the selected primary is the ninth
register, a following block is available. The induction lemmas hold for
arbitrary finite spare lists and arbitrary natural block values; they do
not require a bounded or a fixed-size register representation.

\begin{lemma}[Residual-pointer normalization]
For every nonterminal guarded case, let $w$ be its complete successor
pointer, retaining all trailing zero bits. Decode $w0^\omega$ to the next
leaf pointer $q$. Then $w$ and $q$ differ only by a terminal string of
zeroes, and, for every $n\geq0$,
\[
 w\,0^{n+64+e+o-|w|}
   =q\,0^{n+64+e+o-|q|}.                  \tag{4}
\]
The empty initial pointer is equivalent in the same sense to leaf $000$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The clean-pointer argument is generic for a complete finite binary decoder.
Increment flips its final zero and turns the following ones into zeroes,
entering a sibling subtree followed by its all-left branch. A break first
truncates at a zero and then performs that operation. Continue truncates at
a one to an ancestor prefix. In every case, a stored suffix extending beyond
the next leaf consists only of zeroes; an underflowed pointer is the empty
root pointer. This argument uses the new tree's prefix-free complete leaf
set, not a bound copied from the original decoder.

The new decoder is independently reconstructed as a complete binary trie
from its 6460 leaf paths. Decode each full successor pointer, extending it
by zeroes when necessary. All 8390 successors have only zeroes after the
decoded leaf, if any stored bits remain. Neither side of (4) passes the
header: the smallest constant padding in the check is 13. The checker
compares (4) at its constant part; appending the same $n$ zeroes before the
header proves it for every $n$. The initial empty-word check is
$0^{64}=000\,0^{61}$. These are finite word equalities, not bounded
simulation claims.
\end{proof}

Equation (4) is essential for composing instruction simulations: the source
semantics may erase an irrelevant trailing-zero suffix, whereas the literal
tape retains cells and, at overflow, moves its marker. Their pointed frames
agree after precisely the adjustment in (2).

\section{From local macro proofs to halting equivalence}

The actual-table register-operation proofs use four kinds of sound step:
a literal transition; induction on the length of a homogeneous unary run;
induction on a finite list of register blocks; and finite word identities.
For example, with $C(v)=1\,0\,1^v$, the identity
$C(A)1=1B(A)$ follows by induction on the finite list $A$. The positive,
zero and increment contracts are compositions of these steps. The exact
table was substituted into the induction checker before the corresponding
symbolic oracles were admitted.

The affine replay then verifies every one of the 8391 guarded instructions
for all $n\geq0$, all nine natural register values satisfying its named
guard, and every nonempty finite spare list. The guard names the same
physical register as the decrement oracle. Oracles with a mismatched guard
or a pre-existing pending register effect are rejected. Every successful
instruction simulation has at least 13 literal steps; symbolic scans and
macro invocations denote additional finite computations. In particular,
every nonterminal source step has a finite, strictly positive literal
binary duration and finishes in the corresponding nonterminal dispatched
frame. The source halt instruction reaches the unique terminal state.

As a separate verification, the complete-frame symbolic checker was run on
this exact table with its all-values option. It checks all 8391 cases using
2312645 literal, unary-induction and finite-list-induction rules, with all
nine primary values independently quantified and every finite nonempty
spare suffix admitted. It covers all 490 binary transition rows and again
obtains minimum positive progress of 13 steps. This second proof expands
the register file rather than using opaque affine register-operation
oracles. The baseline interval invariant is read and independently checked
by that program, but its lower bounds are then replaced by universal
natural-number ranges before the all-values obligations are generated.

\begin{lemma}[Clocked frame simulation]
At any related source state and binary frame satisfying (2)--(4), the
source computation eventually halts if and only if the binary computation
eventually halts.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The source-to-binary direction is induction on the finite source halting
computation, concatenating the checked finite instruction segments. The
frame premises persist by (2), (3) and (4).

For the converse, use strong induction on the remaining literal binary
halting time $t$. If the current source instruction is halt, the conclusion
already holds. Otherwise it has a deterministic successor, and its checked
simulation has some finite duration $k>0$ and a nonterminal endpoint.
That segment contains no terminal state. Determinism and the assumed
binary halt imply $k\leq t$: a halt before the end of a verified executable
segment is impossible. The successor frame has a binary halting computation
of length $t-k<t$, so the induction hypothesis supplies a source halt.
This proof requires no choice of an infinite sequence of simulations and
no uniform upper bound on their durations.
\end{proof}

The new source program is related to the original literal 6459-node
program by a separately checked segment bisimulation with 3013 pairs,
6024 successor edges, and one terminal pair. Related test sites decrement
the same logical register. Between tests, the two sides execute the same
number of increments on each register; break/continue steps inspect no
register value. Counts can therefore be compared without assuming a bound
on register values. The certificate also relates the segments from the
empty pointers. This is the ordinary mathematical argument underlying
\path{RMBisim.check_halts_iff}.

\section{Exact source and consistency calibration}

The existing theorem \path{Bisim254.prog254_halts_iff} relates the original
literal decoder to \path{RMEquiv254.pathDag254}, the 219-node structured
program, for every register file. At (1), the exact dirty-root
specialization is
\begin{center}\small
\path{DirtyProgram254.six_nonformula_path_iff 3 0 3 0 0 0}.
\end{center}
Its six arguments are $(N,A,D,X,Y,L)$ in that order. The top and retained
formula are both the truth code~0, and the stack is empty. Their safety
witnesses are \path{Code254.C254.truth}, twice, and
\path{Enum254.good_zero}. Arbitrary numerical fields are allowed by the
existing scratch-absorption and padded-proof-enumeration theorems. Starting
at candidate counter 3 therefore does not omit a refutation.

The source file \path{L2.enc} sets logical registers with index at least
nine to zero. The physical spare blocks are auxiliary storage and are
not identified with those logical registers. Every source register operand
is in $0,\ldots,8$, as independently checked on all instruction leaves.

Write $\Refute_T$ for a finite derivation of falsity from the indicated
first-order theory. The source and interpretation endpoints give
\[
 \Halt(M,\mathrm{blank})
 \ \longleftrightarrow\ \Refute_{\mathrm{NativeAx}}
 \ \longleftrightarrow\ \Refute_{\mathrm{ZF}}
 \ \longleftrightarrow\ \Refute_{\mathrm{ZFC}}.              \tag{5}
\]
The exact native theory is the original CP/all-formula-GIC/irreflexivity
theory. No logical-axiom restriction, theory substitution or row permutation
is needed for this candidate. The named inherited comparison theorems are
\path{NativeTheory254.c254_refutation_iff},
\path{WFTheory.native_refutation_iff_zf}, and
\path{ChoiceCalibration254.zf_refutation_iff_zfc}.

To calibrate proof strength, interpret these arguments inside ZFC using
finite syntax and set-coded first-order structures. Soundness, Henkin
completeness, the inherited native/ZF model constructions and the internal
constructible-model construction are available there. Models need not be
externally well-founded or transitive. The finite machine certificates,
word identities, natural-number inductions and strong halting-time induction
likewise concern sets and finite codes. Thus (5) is an ordinary ZFC proof;
it is not an inference that an arbitrary universe-polymorphic Lean theorem
automatically internalizes into ZFC.

\begin{theorem}
For the fixed 245-working-state, two-symbol blank-tape machine above,
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\ \vdash\
 \bigl(\neg\Halt(M,\mathrm{blank})\leftrightarrow\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})\bigr),
\]
where $\Con(\mathrm{ZFC})$ asserts the absence of a finite refutation in
the fixed first-order presentation used by the source comparison.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compose the exact blank prefix, clocked frame simulation, new source
bisimulation, dirty-root theorem and interpretation comparisons, giving (5).
Negate both sides. No consistency or soundness assumption about ZFC is a
premise of this equivalence. Actual nonhalting follows if ZFC is consistent.
\end{proof}

The same argument gives the concise comparison
\[
 \mathrm{ZFC}\vdash
 \bigl(\Halt(M,\mathrm{blank})\leftrightarrow
       \Halt(M_{246},\mathrm{blank})\bigr)
\]
with the reviewed Prefix246 machine. Both exact bootstraps reach the same
complete register-frame seed; both runtimes simulate their source program
on unbounded register frames; and the new source is segment-bisimilar to the
original source. Thus the original ordinary ZFC consistency calibration may
also be inherited through this direct halting-equivalence comparison.
