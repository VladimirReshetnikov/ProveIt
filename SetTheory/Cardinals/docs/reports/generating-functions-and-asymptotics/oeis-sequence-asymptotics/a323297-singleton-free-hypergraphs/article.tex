{\LARGE\bfseries All Order Asymptotics for Hypergraphs\\[5pt] without Singleton Intersections}\par\vspace{9pt}
{\large Exceptional components, two fluctuation scales, and inversion}\par\vspace{7pt}
5 October 2026
\end{center}
\begin{abstract}
Let $A_n$ count simple $3$-uniform hypergraphs on the labelled set $[n]$ in which distinct edges never meet in one vertex, and let $B_n$ count those with no isolated vertices. These are OEIS A323297 and A323296. Starting from Howroyd's component decomposition, we give an explicit finite algorithm for every fixed saddle correction, with relative remainder $O_J((r/n)^{J+1})$ at the exact saddle. We reverse the counting scale through three inverse-logarithmic corrections and give an arbitrary-order recursion, then distinguish this coarse expansion from a much finer exact-saddle inverse and rounding-safe two-ceiling bounds. For the vector of isolated vertices and the two nonsunflower tetrahedral types, we prove a globally summable conditional-likelihood expansion and the sharp product-Poisson total-variation equivalent
\[
 \TV\sim\frac{10}{3}\frac{e^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{r^3}{n}.
\]
Separate saddle-shift estimates give relative probabilities of rare component-deletion events. Projecting away total vertex count resolves the nearly singular edge/component covariance: the component count has scale $\sqrt n/r$, while its edge-defect residual has the independent scale $r^2/\sqrt3$. A quantitative mixed Gaussian--Poisson test bound retains the entire exceptional vector without normalizing it. All results transfer to $B_n$ at its own saddle. Complete coefficient expansions and conditioned Poisson representations are classical frameworks; no priority claim, effective numerical onset, or discrete-to-continuous total-variation claim is made. Exact bounded checks, noncertified numerical diagnostics, and a reproducible source archive accompany the proofs.
\end{abstract}
