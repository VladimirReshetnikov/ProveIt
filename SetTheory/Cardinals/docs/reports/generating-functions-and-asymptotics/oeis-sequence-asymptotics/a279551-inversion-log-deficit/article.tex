\begin{abstract}
An inversion sequence of length $n$ is an integer word $e_1\cdots e_n$ with $0\le e_i<i$. Britt and Beaton's classes 247 and 759, the inversion sequences avoiding the patterns $(000,010,110,120)$ and $(010,110,120)$, are counted by OEIS A279551 and A279556; their exponential growth constants are $\mu=8$ and $\mu=9$. This report prints three manuscripts on the logarithmic deficit $\Dfc_j(n)=n\log\mu_j-\log a_j(n)$ as Parts~I--III, in the order in which each depends on the one before. Part~I proves that $\Dfc_j(n)$ has order $n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}$, that $a_j(n)\le\mu_j^n$ for every $n$ and $a_j$ is strictly increasing for $n\ge1$, that the inverse threshold carries a positive correction of order $(\log T)^{1/3}(\log\log T)^{2/3}$, that the boundary function has sharp Gevrey order~$3$, that $1/\mu_j$ is a genuine singularity, and that neither generating function is D-finite. Part~II proves $\Dfc_j(n)=\sigma_DS_n+o(S_n)$ with $S_n=n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}$ and $\sigma_D=(3\pi^2/(2D))^{1/3}$, where $D=1$ for A279556 and $D=1/2$ for A279551, together with the matching inverse and derivative-growth constants. Part~III proves $\Dfc_j(n)=\sigma_DS+\frac{7\sigma_D}{3}S\log\log n/\log n+O(S/\log n)$, with refined inverse and derivative consequences. Britt and Beaton's numerical forms with a stretched term $n^{3/8}$, which they call non-rigorous, are therefore not asymptotic equivalents. The combinatorial input is their exact commitment-tree construction. The coefficient of $S/\log n$, a coefficient equivalent, ratio asymptotics and effective constants remain open. The report is unrefereed, and nothing in it is formalized.
\end{abstract}

\begin{partabstract}
For the inversion-sequence classes numbered 247 and 759, we prove that the
exponential growth constants are respectively $8$ and $9$, and that the
logarithmic deficit from exponential growth has order

\section{Definitions and the main theorem}\label{ivd:sc:sec:main}
An inversion sequence of length $n$ is an integer sequence
$e=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ with $0\le e_i<i$. Let $a_{247}(n)$ count such
sequences with no $i<j<k$ satisfying $e_i\le e_j$ and $e_i\ge e_k$.
Let $a_{759}(n)$ count those with no $i<j<k$ satisfying these two
inequalities and $e_j\ne e_k$. In the usual pattern notation, these are
avoidance of $(000,010,110,120)$ and $(010,110,120)$, respectively.
Thus
\[
 a_{247}(n)=\mathrm{A279551}(n),\quad
 a_{759}(n)=\mathrm{A279556}(n),\quad a_j(0)=1.
\]
All logarithms are natural. Put $\mu_{247}=8$, $\mu_{759}=9$, and
$\psi(t)=t^{1/3}(\log t)^{2/3}$ for $t>1$.
\begin{theorem}\label{ivd:sc:thm:main}
For each $j\in\{247,759\}$ there are constants $0<c_j\le C_j<\infty$
and $n_j$ such that, for every integer $n\ge n_j$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:main}
 \mu_j^n e^{-C_j\psi(n)}\le a_j(n)\le
 \mu_j^n e^{-c_j\psi(n)}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $D_j(n):=n\log\mu_j-\log a_j(n)$ satisfies
\[
 c_j\psi(n)\le D_j(n)\le C_j\psi(n).
\]
Moreover $a_j(n)\le\mu_j^n$ for every $n\ge0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:growth}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}a_{247}(n)^{1/n}=8,\qquad
 \lim_{n\to\infty}a_{759}(n)^{1/n}=9.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The statement is an order-of-magnitude theorem for the logarithm. It does
not imply that $D_j(n)/\psi(n)$ has a limit. In particular, writing
$a_j(n)=\mu_j^n\exp\{-\Theta(\psi(n))\}$ is shorthand for the two
inequalities in \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:main}, not for an equivalent with an unspecified
constant. Fixed prefactors in intermediate bounds will be absorbed into
$c_j,C_j$ only after $n$ is sufficiently large.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]

Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}, Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:main}, proves that $D_j(n)/\psi(n)$ does converge, to $\sigma_{D_j}=(3\pi^2/(2D_j))^{1/3}$ with $D_{759}=1$, $D_{247}=\frac12$ (Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}'s diffusion coefficients, not this Part's deficit), and Part~\ref{ivd:ll:part}, Theorem~\ref{ivd:ll:thm:main}, adds the term $\frac{7\sigma_D}{3}\psi(n)\log\log n/\log n$ with remainder $O(\psi(n)/\log n)$. Both imply \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:main} for every $c_j<\sigma_{D_j}<C_j$. The proof of this Part (Sections~\ref{ivd:sc:sec:staircase}--\ref{ivd:sc:sec:timetilt}) is a different route to the order statement and is kept in full as a second route. In the write's own text the deficit $D_j(n)$ is written $\Dfc_j(n)$.
\end{writenote}

\subsection{Published inputs and comparison}
The combinatorial input is Britt and Beaton's exact succession rules
\cite[Sections 3.8--3.9, Lemmas 5--6 and Appendix A]{BB}, in version 3
dated 29 September 2026. Their Section 4.2 explicitly labels its
asymptotic estimates as nonrigorous numerical analysis. Equations
(4.12)--(4.13) propose forms of the type
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:oldform}
 A n^g\mu_j^n\exp\{-b n^{3/8}\},\qquad A,b>0.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:main} disproves \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:oldform} as a literal full
asymptotic equivalent. It does not dispute the finite enumerations or the
exact trees that make the present proof possible. No global literature
priority claim is made here. The estimates below are derived from those
trees and do not use fitted numerical constants.
\begin{remark}[\wtag\ Britt--Beaton's numerical forms, on record; 5 October 2026]\label{ivd:sc:rem:bb}
Section~4.2 of \cite{BB}, which its authors describe as non-rigorous and based only on numerical analysis, gives as best estimates, with thanks to A.~J.~Guttmann for assistance,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:bbforms}
 \begin{aligned}
 \text{A279551 (their (4.12))}:&\quad I_n\sim C\,n^g\,8^n\mu_1^{n^{3/8}},\quad g\approx4.25,\ \log\mu_1\approx-13,\ \log C\approx8;\\
 \text{A279556 (their (4.13))}:&\quad I_n\sim C\,n^g\,9^n\mu_1^{n^{3/8}},\quad g\approx3.25,\ \log\mu_1\approx-10.4,
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
that is, \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:oldform} with $b=-\log\mu_1>0$. As asymptotic equivalents both forms are false, and so is every form
\[
 a_j(n)\sim C\,n^g\mu_j^n\exp\{-bn^\beta\}\qquad(C>0;\ g,b,\beta\in\mathbb R).
\]
\emph{Proof.} Such an equivalent gives, on taking logarithms, $\Dfc_j(n)=bn^\beta-g\log n-\log C+o(1)$. By Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:main}, $c_j\psi(n)\le\Dfc_j(n)\le C_j\psi(n)$ for large $n$, with $\psi(n)=n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}$; note $\log n=o(\psi(n))$. If $b\le0$ or $\beta\le0$, then $bn^\beta$ is bounded above, so $\Dfc_j(n)\le|g|\log n+O(1)=o(\psi(n))$, contradicting the lower bound. If $b>0$ and $0<\beta\le1/3$, then $n^\beta/\psi(n)=n^{\beta-1/3}(\log n)^{-2/3}\to0$, so again $\Dfc_j(n)=o(\psi(n))$, a contradiction. If $b>0$ and $\beta>1/3$, then $n^\beta/\psi(n)\to\infty$, so $\Dfc_j(n)/\psi(n)\to\infty$, contradicting the upper bound. Britt and Beaton's case is $\beta=3/8$, $b>0$: there $\Dfc_j(n)/n^{3/8}$ would tend to $b>0$, while $\Dfc_j(n)/n^{3/8}\le C_jn^{-1/24}(\log n)^{2/3}\to0$. $\square$

Only the upper half of Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:main}'s deficit bound, that is the lower counting bound \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:lower}, is needed for Britt and Beaton's case $\beta=3/8$, $b>0$ (precision added 5~October 2026, after the independent check recorded at the end of Section~\ref{ivd:ll:sec:further}: the sentence first read ``is needed for $\beta=3/8$'', but for $\beta=3/8$ with $b\le0$ the proof uses the lower half), and the simpler ladder of Section~\ref{ivd:sc:sub:simpler} already suffices for it, since $n^{1/3}\log n=o(n^{3/8})$. Section~\ref{ivd:sc:sub:exponent} gives the same argument for $\beta=3/8$ and for a pure $n^{1/3}$ deficit; Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}, Section~\ref{ivd:sh:sub:scope}, repeats it. Nothing here disputes Britt and Beaton's exact trees or finite enumerations, on which every Part relies, and nothing is said about how well \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:bbforms} fits finite data (Section~\ref{ivd:ll:sec:further}, question~5).

\emph{Added 5~October 2026, after the independent check.} The logarithmic factor does not repair the form either. If $a_j(n)\sim C\,n^g\mu_j^n\exp\{-bn^\beta(\log n)^\gamma\}$ with $C>0$ and real $g,b,\beta,\gamma$, then $\Dfc_j(n)=bn^\beta(\log n)^\gamma-g\log n-\log C+o(1)$. Since $\log n=o(\psi(n))$, Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:main}, $\Dfc_j(n)=\sigma_{D_j}\psi(n)+o(\psi(n))$, forces $(\beta,\gamma,b)=(\frac13,\frac23,\sigma_{D_j})$, so that $\Dfc_j(n)-\sigma_{D_j}\psi(n)=O(\log n)$. But by Theorem~\ref{ivd:ll:thm:main} this difference is at least $\frac{\psi(n)}{\log n}\bigl(\frac{7\sigma_{D_j}}3\log\log n-C'\bigr)$ for some constant $C'$ and all large $n$, and $\psi(n)\log\log n/(\log n)^2\to\infty$; so it is not $O(\log n)$.
\end{remark}

\section{Decorated commitments and exact counting}\label{ivd:sc:sec:counting}
\subsection{Auxiliary objects and terminal extraction}
The growth operation increments every positive entry, may increment
certain zeros, and prepends a new zero. For class 759, the auxiliary
class permits zero-valued instances of $010$ while excluding the other
forbidden patterns. For class 247, the auxiliary class additionally
permits zero-valued instances of $000$. Let $p$ be the initial-zero-run
length. Zeros lying after that initial run may have to be incremented in
later stages before growth within the initial run is again allowed.
A commitment word records their future nonempty batches of increments.
Its letters $1,\ldots,b$ must all occur. The number $c$ in the label
$(p,c)$ counts remaining batches, rather than the number of pending zeros.

For 759 the onto commitment words avoid $212,112,213$; for 247 they
also avoid $111$. The exact word counts supplied by the source are
$C_b\binom{k-1}{b-1}$ and $C_b\binom b{k-b}$ for length $k$, where
$C_b=\binom{2b}b/(b+1)$ is the Catalan number. We use these published
word-count identities as part of the combinatorial input.

Both label trees start at $(0,0)$ and have edges
\begin{align}
 (p,c)&\longrightarrow(p+1,c),\\
 (p,c)&\longrightarrow(p+1,c-1)\quad(c>0),\\
 (p,0)&\longrightarrow(p-\ell,b)\quad(0\le\ell\le p-1),
 \label{ivd:sc:eq:edges}
\end{align}
where the third edge has multiplicity
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:multiplicities}
 M_{759}(\ell,b)=C_b\binom\ell b,\qquad
 M_{247}(\ell,b)=C_b\binom{b+1}{\ell-b}.
\end{equation}
Out-of-range binomial coefficients mean zero. Thus the 247 support is
$b\le\ell\le2b+1$. The admissible terminal labels are
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:terminal}
 \mathcal T_{759}=\{(p,0):p\ge0\},\qquad
 \mathcal T_{247}=\{(p,0):0\le p\le2\}.
\end{equation}
Counting every auxiliary node would give the wrong sequences. In
particular, a long all-zero initial access path is legal in the auxiliary
247 tree, although it is not itself a target avoider. Only the final
extraction uses \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:terminal}.

Here is the multiplicity interpretation. Without a new commitment,
increment a nonempty suffix of $r$ initial zeros. The new prefix has
length $p-r+1$, so $\ell=r-1$. For 759 every $r\ge1$ is allowed; for
247 only $r=1,2$ is allowed. With a new commitment, increment a suffix
of $r\ge0$ zeros and one additional zero to its left, leaving $k\ge1$
zeros between. Then $\ell=r+k$ and the new prefix length is $p-\ell$.
Summing the word counts gives
\[
 \sum_{k=b}^{\ell}C_b\binom{k-1}{b-1}=C_b\binom\ell b
\]
for 759. For 247, $r$ is zero or one, and Pascal's identity gives
\[
 C_b\left[\binom b{\ell-b}+\binom b{\ell-b-1}\right]
 =C_b\binom{b+1}{\ell-b}.
\]
For $b=0$, use the suffix rules directly. They give
$M_{759}(\ell,0)=1$ for every $\ell\ge0$, and
$M_{247}(\ell,0)=1$ for $\ell=0,1$, with zero otherwise,
as in \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:multiplicities}.
At $p\ge1$, both classes have a unit boundary self-loop. Class 247
also has a unit boundary edge from $p$ to $p-1$ for $p\ge2$.

\subsection{Why completed histories do not overcount}
Given a physical growth step $S\to T$, recover $S$ uniquely by removing
the first zero of $T$ and replacing each remaining entry $t$ by
$\max(t-1,0)$. The formerly zero positions incremented in that step are
exactly those whose corresponding entries in $T$ equal one. Iterating
this operation recovers the entire physical history from a final sequence.

For a completed commitment, its word is also recoverable: order the
subsequent nonempty batches in which the pending zeros are incremented,
and give each pending position the index of its batch. Onto-ness ensures
that no empty, hence invisible, commitment can be inserted. Idle steps
are already determined by the physical history. It follows that two
different fully completed decorated histories cannot have the same
physical endpoint. At an unfinished endpoint, future commitment words
need not yet be distinguishable physically; this is another reason to
use only \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:terminal}.

Conversely, the source construction gives every target avoider its
completed decorated history. Its reverse-growth chain can encounter the
allowed zero-valued exceptions in the auxiliary class, and the later
increment order supplies the commitment words. Every legal completed
history with the relevant terminal label is a target avoider. Thus the
multiplicity-weighted paths used below count distinct objects exactly.
When restricting paths to a fixed sequence of cycles, their deterministic
cycle lengths fix all cut positions; no further ambiguity is introduced.

\section{Critical tilts and completed cycles}\label{ivd:sc:sec:tilts}
Choose parameters
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:parameters}
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 j&\mu&x&y&\alpha&r\\ \hline
 759&9&3&1/2&3/2&2/3\\
 247&8&2&1/3&4/3&5/8
\end{array}
\end{equation}
and let $h(p,c)=x^p y^c$. Give an edge of multiplicity $m$ the normalized
weight $q(s,t)=m h(t)/(\mu h(s))$. A length-$n$ path of multiplicity $m$
then has weight
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:telescope}
 m\mu^{-n}h(\mathrm{endpoint}),
\end{equation}
since the root has weight one.

For $c>0$, the outgoing row mass is $x/\mu+x/(\mu y)=1$.
For boundary rows, let $\mathcal C(v)=\sum_{b\ge0}C_bv^b$ and $u=1/x$.
The nonnegative commitment sums extended to every $\ell\ge0$ are
\begin{align}
 G_{759}(u,y)&=\frac1{1-u}\mathcal C\!\left(\frac{yu}{1-u}\right),\\
 G_{247}(u,y)&=(1+u)\mathcal C\big(yu(1+u)\big).
\end{align}
Both Catalan arguments are $1/4$, where the series converges and
$\mathcal C(1/4)=2$. Both $G$ values equal three. Adding the ordinary
edge gives boundary row masses at most $2/3$ and $5/8$, respectively.
The root has just the ordinary edge, of mass $x/\mu$. Thus $q$ is
substochastic. Its total mass at any original integer time is at most
one. At valid terminals $h=x^p\ge1$; \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:telescope} gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:crudeupper}
 a_j(n)\le\mu_j^n\qquad(n\ge0).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact completed cycle weights}
From $(p,0)$, take a commitment edge with $b\ge1$ and $\ell\le p-1$.
Follow it by $w\ge b$ bulk edges, exactly $b$ of which fulfill a
commitment, the last fulfillment occurring on the final edge. Choose the
first $b-1$ fulfillment positions among the first $w-1$ edges. There
are $\binom{w-1}{b-1}$ choices, and every proper bulk prefix has $c>0$.
The cycle duration is $w+1$, displacement $w-\ell$, and total weight
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:Q}
 Q_j(b,\ell,w)=\mu_j^{-w-1}x_j^{w-\ell}
 M_j(\ell,b)\binom{w-1}{b-1}.
\end{equation}
Here ``cycle'' means completed commitment excursion, not necessarily a
return to the same height. It is legal if the multiplicity is nonzero,
$w\ge b$, and $\ell\le p-1$. During the bulk portion, $p$ only increases.

Define the negative-binomial and binomial masses by
\[
 \NB_{b,q}(r)=\binom{r-1}{b-1}q^b(1-q)^{r-b},\qquad
 \Bin_{m,q}(r)=\binom mr q^r(1-q)^{m-r}.
\]
Direct cancellation in \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:Q} yields the exact identities
\begin{align}
 Q_{759}(b,\ell,w)&=\frac{C_b}{4^b}\frac{\ell}{9b}
  \NB_{b,2/3}(\ell)\NB_{b,2/3}(w),\label{ivd:sc:eq:factor759}\\
 Q_{247}(b,\ell,w)&=\frac{C_b}{4^b}\frac3{16}
  \Bin_{b+1,1/3}(\ell-b)\NB_{b,3/4}(w).\label{ivd:sc:eq:factor247}
\end{align}
For 247 the exact center of $\ell$ is $(4b+1)/3$, so its bounded
shift from $\alpha b=4b/3$ must be retained in local estimates.

\section{A uniform moderate local lower bound}\label{ivd:sc:sec:local}
\begin{lemma}\label{ivd:sc:lem:local}
There exist constants $c,C>0$, $b_0$, and $\delta>0$ such that, for
both classes and integer $b\ge b_0,\ell,w$ satisfying
$|\ell-\alpha b|\le\delta b$ and $|w-\alpha b|\le\delta b$,
the probabilistic supports hold and
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:local}
 Q_j(b,\ell,w)\ge c b^{-5/2}
 \exp\left\{-\frac Cb\big[(\ell-\alpha b)^2+(w-\alpha b)^2\big]\right\}.
\end{equation}
The geometric constraint $\ell\le p-1$ is separate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two-sided Stirling inequalities
\[
 \sqrt{2\pi m}(m/e)^m e^{1/(12m+1)}\le m!
 \le\sqrt{2\pi m}(m/e)^m e^{1/(12m)}\qquad(m\ge1)
\]
imply, uniformly for $r/N$ in a fixed compact interval $I\subset(0,1)$,
\[
 \Bin_{N,q}(r)\ge c_I N^{-1/2}e^{-ND(r/N\Vert q)},
\quad D(t\Vert q)=t\log(t/q)+(1-t)\log\frac{1-t}{1-q}.
\]
Since $D(q\Vert q)=D'(q\Vert q)=0$ and
$D''(t\Vert q)=1/[t(1-t)]$, Taylor's theorem on $I$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:binlocal}
 \Bin_{N,q}(r)\ge c N^{-1/2}
 \exp\{-C(r-Nq)^2/N\}
\end{equation}
when $|r-Nq|\le\eta N$ for some fixed $\eta>0$.
The exact relation $\NB_{b,q}(r)=(b/r)\Bin_{r,q}(b)$ gives
\[
 \NB_{b,q}(r)\ge c b^{-1/2}
 \exp\{-C(r-b/q)^2/b\},\qquad |r-b/q|\le\delta b,
\]
because $r\asymp b$ and $b/r$ stays away from zero and one.
For the 247 binomial factor,
\[
 (\ell-b)-(b+1)/3=(\ell-4b/3)-1/3,
\]
and $(u-1/3)^2\le2u^2+2/9$ absorbs the bounded displacement.
Also $C_b/4^b\ge c b^{-3/2}$ by Stirling, and $\ell/b$ in
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:factor759} is bounded below. Multiplication proves the result.

For explicit support margins one may take $\delta=1/12$ and enlarge
$b_0$. The 759 ratios $b/\ell,b/w$ lie in $[12/19,12/17]$;
the 247 ratio $b/w$ lies in $[12/17,4/5]$ and
$(\ell-b)/(b+1)$ lies in $[1/8,5/12]$. All are inside
$[1/8,7/8]$, which also contains the relevant success probabilities.
Thus the compact-set constants are uniform. In particular this proof
allows deviations of size $\sqrt{b\log b}$; it is not an application
of a fixed-width central-limit window outside its range.
\end{proof}

\section{The logarithmic staircase counting lower bound}\label{ivd:sc:sec:staircase}
\subsection{Rounded heights and legal upward and downward stages}
For integers $k\ge k_0$, set
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:stairs}
 p_k=\lfloor k^2\log(k+1)\rfloor,\quad
 b_k=\lfloor p_k/4\rfloor,\quad
 \ell_k=\lfloor\alpha b_k+1/2\rfloor,\quad d_k=p_{k+1}-p_k.
\end{equation}
Fix $k_0$ sufficiently large, independently of $n$. An upward stage
from $p_k$ uses $(b_k,\ell_k,\ell_k+d_k)$ and ends at $p_{k+1}$.
A downward stage from $p_{k+1}$ uses
$(b_k,\ell_k,\ell_k-d_k)$ and ends at $p_k$.
The mean-value theorem applied to $t^2\log(t+1)$, with the floor
error bounded by one, gives
\[
 p_k\asymp b_k\asymp k^2\log k,\quad
 d_k=O(k\log k)>0,\quad
 d_k/b_k=O(1/k),\quad d_k^2/b_k=O(\log k).
\]
The limiting margins are strictly positive:
$\ell_k/p_k\to\alpha/4<1$, $\ell_k/b_k\to\alpha>1$,
and, for 247, $2-\alpha>0$. Therefore a single fixed $k_0$ makes
$\ell_k\le p_k-1$, $\ell_k-d_k\ge b_k$, and every commitment
support condition hold for all subsequent $k$. The downward departure
height is larger and imposes no stronger $\ell$ constraint.
Nearest-integer rounding gives $|\ell_k-\alpha b_k|\le1/2$;
the waiting-time deviations are at most $d_k+1/2=o(b_k)$.
Lemma~\ref{ivd:sc:lem:local} consequently gives a stage weight at least $k^{-A}$
for a fixed $A>0$. Indeed its negative logarithm is bounded by
\[
 O(1)+\tfrac52\log b_k+O((d_k+1)^2/b_k)=O(\log k).
\]
Ascending from $p_{k_0}$ to $p_K$ and descending in reverse order has
exact length
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:TK}
 T_K=\sum_{k=k_0}^{K-1}\big[(\ell_k+d_k+1)+(\ell_k-d_k+1)\big]
 =\sum_{k=k_0}^{K-1}(2\ell_k+2)=O(K^3\log K)
\end{equation}
and normalized weight at least $\exp\{-C K\log K\}$. The signed
increments cancel exactly in the duration, not just asymptotically.

\subsection{Every integer length at the plateau}
At height $p_K$ put $b=b_K$, $L=\ell_K+1$, and
$R=\lfloor K\log(K+1)\rfloor$. For every integer $s\in[-R,R]$,
choose $\ell=w=L-1+s$. This is a same-height return cycle of length
$L+s$. Since $R/b=O(1/K)$ and $(R+1)^2/b=O(\log K)$, the same
strict support margins and Lemma~\ref{ivd:sc:lem:local} give a uniform
weight at least $K^{-A'}$ for every such cycle. In particular, the
available durations contain the entire integer interval $[L-R,L+R]$,
where $0<R<L$, $L\asymp K^2\log K$, and $R\asymp K\log K$.

\begin{lemma}[Integer filling]\label{ivd:sc:lem:fill}
If $L,R$ are positive integers with $R<L$, every integer
$N\ge (L^2-R^2)/(2R)$ is a sum of
$m=\lceil N/(L+R)\rceil$ integers in $[L-R,L+R]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The upper endpoint satisfies $N\le m(L+R)$. The lower endpoint obeys
\[
 m(L-R)\le\left(\frac N{L+R}+1\right)(L-R)\le N,
\]
where the last inequality is precisely the assumed bound on $N$.
Start all summands at $L-R$ and distribute the integer excess with
capacity $2R$ per summand. This realizes every integer between the two
extreme sums; no divisibility or parity assumption is needed.
\end{proof}
Here the sufficient threshold is $O(K^3\log K)$, and
$m=O(N/(K^2\log K)+1)$. We fix one composition of $N$ once and
for all. Counting different compositions is unnecessary and is not used.

\subsection{Assembly at every sufficiently large integer time}
Let $p_0=p_{k_0}$ be fixed. Reach $(p_0,0)$ by $p_0$ ordinary edges.
For 759, return to this valid endpoint after the descent. For 247,
append $p_0-2$ unit downward boundary edges and end at $(2,0)$.
The total fixed access and termination lengths are
$F_{759}=p_0$, $F_{247}=2p_0-2$, and their normalized weights are
fixed positive constants.

Choose a sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and set
\[
 K=\left\lfloor\varepsilon(n/\log n)^{1/3}\right\rfloor.
\]
There are fixed constants $M_1,M_2$ bounding $T_K$ and the filling
threshold by $M_iK^3\log K$. Choose
$\max(M_1,M_2)\varepsilon^3\le1/4$. Eventually $K\ge k_0$,
$\log K\le\log n$, and $F_j\le n/4$, so
\[
 N:=n-F_j-T_K\ge n/2
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad (L^2-R^2)/(2R)\le n/4.
\]
Insert the exact $m$-cycle plateau filling between ascent and descent.
The total original length is exactly $n$. Its normalized weight is at
least
\begin{align*}
 \exp\left\{-C\left[K\log K+
   \left(\frac n{K^2\log K}+1\right)\log K\right]\right\}
 &\ge \exp\{-C'(K\log K+n/K^2)\}\\
 &\ge \exp\{-C''\psi(n)\}.
\end{align*}
The floor causes no denominator problem: eventually
$K\ge(\varepsilon/2)(n/\log n)^{1/3}$.
The final tilt is $3^{p_0}$ for 759 and $4$ for 247. These are fixed
constants, so division in \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:telescope} only changes the constant
in the bound. We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:lower}
 a_j(n)\ge\mu_j^n e^{-C_j\psi(n)}
\end{equation}
for every sufficiently large integer $n$. Ending at $p_K$ instead would
incur the much larger endpoint factor $x^{p_K}$; the descent is essential.

\subsection{A simpler bound already excludes the old exponent}\label{ivd:sc:sub:simpler}
For perspective, one can use $p_k=k^2$, $b_k=\lfloor k^2/4\rfloor$,
and the same nearest-integer $\ell_k$. Then $d_k=2k+1$ is
$O(\sqrt{b_k})$, and the fixed-window consequence of
Lemma~\ref{ivd:sc:lem:local} gives stage weights at least $c k^{-5}$.
The total ladder length is $O(K^3)$. At the top, choose return-cycle
lengths in $[L-K,L+K]$, with $L\asymp K^2$; the filling threshold
is $O(K^3)$ and the number of plateau cycles is $O(n/K^2+1)$.
Taking $K=\lfloor\varepsilon n^{1/3}\rfloor$ and using the same fixed
endpoints proves
\[
 a_j(n)\ge\mu_j^n\exp\{-C n^{1/3}\log n\}.
\]
This weaker independent construction alone already rules out a negative
nonzero $n^{3/8}$ term. The logarithmic staircase is needed for the
matching order in Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:main}.

\section{The boundary renewal kernel and its joint transforms}\label{ivd:sc:sec:kernel}
For the matching upper counting bound, add a cemetery state to $q$ to
obtain a killed Markov chain. Killing occurs only on departure from
$c=0$, because bulk rows have mass one. Put $a=x/\mu$; the two bulk
probabilities are $a$ and $1-a=x/(\mu y)$. An excursion with $b$
commitments therefore completes almost surely, with a negative-binomial
duration. A terminal path satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:relaxterminal}
 \frac{a_j(n)}{\mu_j^n}\le
 \text{total $q$-mass of length-$n$ paths ending at $c=0$},
\end{equation}
because its contribution to the count divided by $\mu^n$ is its
$q$-weight times $x^{-p}\le1$. Allowing all boundary terminal heights
in 247 enlarges the sum and is valid for this upper bound.

Embed the chain at successive visits to $c=0$, counting a one-edge
boundary-to-boundary move as its own renewal step. Such a step is an
ordinary edge (duration $1$, displacement $1$, weight $a$), a $b=0$
commitment edge (duration $1$, displacement $-\ell$), or a completed
cycle \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:Q} with duration $L=w+1$ and displacement $D=w-\ell$.
Let $K(p,p')$ be the total weight of all legal such steps, with durations
summed. For a fixed $b\ge1$, extend the $\ell$-sum to all nonnegative
integers. Set $d_b=C_b/4^b$. Direct summation gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:joint}
 \sum_{\ell,w}Q_j(b,\ell,w)e^{\lambda(w-\ell)+\eta(w+1)}
 =A_j(\lambda,\eta)d_b R_j(\lambda,\eta)^b,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 A_{759}(\lambda,\eta)&=\frac{e^\eta}{9(1-e^{-\lambda}/3)},&
 R_{759}(\lambda,\eta)&=
 \frac{4e^\eta}{9(1-e^{-\lambda}/3)(1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/3)},
 \label{ivd:sc:eq:trans759}\\
 A_{247}(\lambda,\eta)&=\frac{e^\eta(1+e^{-\lambda}/2)}8,&
 R_{247}(\lambda,\eta)&=
 \frac{e^\eta(1+e^{-\lambda}/2)}{2(1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/4)}.
 \label{ivd:sc:eq:trans247}
\end{align}
The same $A_jd_bR_j^b$ formula at $b=0$ is the sum of all
zero-commitment edges, with $L=1,D=-\ell$. It inserts no fictitious
bulk steps. To verify the calculation, use
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{\ell\ge b}\binom\ell b z^\ell&=\frac{z^b}{(1-z)^{b+1}},&
 \sum_\ell\binom{b+1}{\ell-b}z^\ell&=z^b(1+z)^{b+1},\\
 \sum_{w\ge b}\binom{w-1}{b-1}t^w&=\left(\frac t{1-t}\right)^b
 &&(b\ge1).
\end{align*}
For each fixed $b$, the sums over $\ell,w$ converge in a fixed
neighborhood of $(0,0)$, which can be chosen independently of $b$.
\emph{The unrestricted sum over all $b$ is not asserted to converge
at positive tilt.} Every positive-tilt use below has the finite legal
cutoff $b\le p-1$ or the finite cutoff $b\le H$.

The critical values and derivatives are
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:derivatives}
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 j&A_j(0,0)&R_j(0,0)&\partial_\lambda\log R_j(0,0)&
 \partial_\lambda^2\log R_j(0,0)&\partial_\eta\log R_j(0,0)\\\hline
 759&1/6&1&0&3/2&3/2\\
 247&3/16&1&0&2/3&4/3
\end{array}
\end{equation}
In particular there are fixed $B,\epsilon_0>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:taylor}
 \log R_j(\lambda,0)\le B\lambda^2,\qquad
 \log R_j(0,\eta)\le B\eta\qquad(0\le\lambda,\eta\le\epsilon_0).
\end{equation}
The prefactors are bounded and Lipschitz there. These are analytic Taylor
bounds for explicit functions, not assumptions about the counting
sequences. At zero tilt, $\sum_{b\ge0}d_b=2$ gives the unrestricted
boundary row mass $a+2A_j(0,0)=r_j<1$, in agreement with
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:parameters}.

\section{A uniform Catalan estimate and a global spatial barrier}\label{ivd:sc:sec:barrier}
\begin{lemma}\label{ivd:sc:lem:catalan}
There is an absolute $B_0<\infty$ such that for every integer $m\ge0$
and $0\le\gamma\le1/4$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:catalan}
 \sum_{b=0}^m d_b\exp\left\{\gamma b\frac{\log(m+2)}{m+2}\right\}
 \le2+B_0\gamma.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The central-binomial bound gives $d_b\le B_1(b+1)^{-3/2}$ and hence
$\sum_{b=1}^m b d_b\le B_2\sqrt m$. Put
$\theta=\gamma\log(m+2)/(m+2)$. For $m\ge1$, use
$e^u-1\le ue^u$ to obtain
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{b=0}^m d_b(e^{\theta b}-1)
 &\le\theta e^{\theta m}\sum_{b=1}^m b d_b\\
 &\le B_2\gamma\log(m+2)(m+2)^{-1/2+\gamma}
 \le B_3\gamma.
\end{align*}
The last bound is uniform since $\gamma\le1/4$ and
$\sup_{t\ge2}(\log t)t^{-1/4}<\infty$. Add
$\sum_{b=0}^m d_b\le2$. The case $m=0$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

Define for real $p\ge0$
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:barrier}
 f(p)=\sqrt{(p+2)\log(p+2)},\quad
 \phi(p)=\kappa[f(p)-f(0)],\quad V(p)=e^{\phi(p)}.
\end{equation}
The constant $\kappa>0$ will be fixed sufficiently small. Writing
$t=p+2$, direct differentiation gives
\[
 f'(p)=\frac{\log t+1}{2\sqrt{t\log t}},\qquad
 f''(p)=-\frac{(\log t)^2+1}{4t^{3/2}(\log t)^{3/2}}<0.
\]
Thus $f$ is globally increasing and concave, with no patch at small
heights. If $\lambda_p=\phi'(p)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:lambdabound}
 0\le\lambda_p\le B_4\kappa,\qquad
 \lambda_p^2\le B_4\kappa^2\frac{\log(p+2)}{p+2},\qquad
 \frac{V(p')}{V(p)}\le e^{\lambda_p(p'-p)}.
\end{equation}
For $p\ge1$, apply this last inequality to legal transitions, then
extend the $\ell$-sum while retaining $b\le p-1$. Equations
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:joint} and \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:taylor} give
\[
 \frac{(KV)(p)}{V(p)}\le a e^{\lambda_p}
 +A_j(\lambda_p,0)\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}d_b R_j(\lambda_p,0)^b.
\]
Choose $\kappa$ small enough that every $\lambda_p$ is in the fixed
Taylor neighborhood. Increasing the cutoff to $p$ if convenient,
Lemma~\ref{ivd:sc:lem:catalan} with $\gamma=O(\kappa^2)$ shows that this
sum is at most $2+O(\kappa^2)$ uniformly in $p$. The Lipschitz
prefactor then yields $(KV)(p)/V(p)\le r_j+O(\kappa)$.
At the root the only edge gives $aV(1)\le a e^{\lambda_0}$, so the
same uniform bound applies. Fix $\kappa$ so small that a common
$\rho<1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:superharmonic}
 KV\le\rho V\le V\quad\hbox{at every boundary state},\qquad V(0)=1.
\end{equation}

Let $P_m$ be the embedded killed chain, with $V(\text{cemetery})=0$,
and let $\tau_H$ be the first renewal index with live height $p\ge H$.
The integrability needed at each step follows from the preceding row
bounds. Bounded optional stopping for the nonnegative supermartingale
gives, for every $m$,
\[
 V(H)\Prob_0(\tau_H\le m)
 \le\E_0[V(P_{\tau_H})\mathbf1_{\{\tau_H\le m\}}]
 \le\E_0[V(P_{\tau_H\wedge m})]\le1.
\]
Taking the increasing limit of the hit probabilities proves
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:hit}
 \Prob_0(\tau_H<\infty)\le V(H)^{-1}
 \le C_1 e^{-\kappa\sqrt{(H+2)\log(H+2)}}.
\end{equation}
There is no unbounded-stopping-time equality, nor any assertion that the
stopped $V$-values themselves increase. Overshoots are harmless because
$V$ is increasing. Equivalently, one can sum the superharmonic inequality
over the first-hit stopping line.

\section{A time tilt and exact original time extraction}\label{ivd:sc:sec:timetilt}
Fix an integer $H\ge2$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:eta}
 \eta_H=\delta\frac{\log(H+2)}{H+2},
\end{equation}
where $\delta>0$ is a sufficiently small fixed constant. Let $K_H(z)$
be the boundary renewal kernel restricted to starting and ending heights
in $\{0,\ldots,H-1\}$, weighting a step of duration $L$ by $z^L$.
The restriction is on boundary heights only; interior bulk heights are
not artificially constrained.

A legal departure below $H$ has $b\le H$. Drop the endpoint restriction
and extend $\ell$, retaining that finite cutoff. The exact transforms,
Taylor bounds and Lemma~\ref{ivd:sc:lem:catalan} imply
\begin{align}
 \|K_H(e^{\eta_H})\|_\infty
 &\le a e^{\eta_H}
  +A_j(0,\eta_H)\sum_{b=0}^H d_b R_j(0,\eta_H)^b\notag\\
 &\le r_j+O(\delta),\label{ivd:sc:eq:timemass}
\end{align}
uniformly in $H$. Indeed use $\gamma=B\delta\le1/4$ in the
Catalan lemma and $\eta_H=O(\delta)$. Choose $\delta$ so small that
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:timemass} is bounded by a fixed $\rho_1<1$ for both
classes. The root row is smaller as well.

The nonnegative generating function for all finite paths from the root
ending at a boundary height below $H$ and having every boundary visit
below $H$ is
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:FH}
 F_H(z)=\sum_{m\ge0}[K_H(z)^m\mathbf1](0).
\end{equation}
Every renewal step has strictly positive original duration. Every
original path ending at $c=0$ decomposes uniquely into these steps.
Consequently $[z^n]F_H(z)=B_H(n)$ is exactly the confined
length-$n$ $q$-mass. In particular this coefficient uses original time,
not renewal index. At the positive evaluation point,
\[
 F_H(e^{\eta_H})\le\sum_{m\ge0}\rho_1^m=\frac1{1-\rho_1}.
\]
Positivity of coefficients now gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:timebound}
 B_H(n)\le\frac1{1-\rho_1}e^{-\eta_H n}.
\end{equation}
The full random excursion durations, and their dependence on spatial
displacements, are retained throughout. Replacing a duration by its mean
would not justify this coefficient bound.

\subsection{Splitting at the first high boundary visit}
Partition length-$n$ paths ending at $c=0$ by whether any boundary visit
has height at least $H$. The confined paths contribute at most
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:timebound}. For each remaining path, split at its first such
boundary visit, at original time $s\le n$. Given its prefix, all
suffixes of length exactly $n-s$ have total $q$-mass at most one, by
substochasticity of the original step chain. Therefore the mass of these
paths is at most the sum of their first-hit prefix weights. Those
prefixes belong to the first-hit stopping line of the embedded chain.
Including prefixes whose hit occurs after time $n$ only enlarges the
bound, so \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:hit} controls this sum. In combination with
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:relaxterminal},
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:split}
 \frac{a_j(n)}{\mu_j^n}\le
 C_1e^{-\kappa\sqrt{(H+2)\log(H+2)}}
 +C_2e^{-\delta n\log(H+2)/(H+2)}.
\end{equation}
Take
\[
 H=\left\lfloor n^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\right\rfloor.
\]
Then
\[
 \sqrt{(H+2)\log(H+2)}\sim\sqrt{2/3}\,\psi(n),\qquad
 \frac{n\log(H+2)}{H+2}\sim\frac23\psi(n).
\]
Both contributions in \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:split} are bounded by
$C e^{-c\psi(n)}$ for suitable fixed positive constants. Absorbing the
prefactor by reducing $c$ for sufficiently large $n$ proves the upper
inequality in \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:main}. The lower inequality was established in
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:lower}. Since $\psi(n)=o(n)$, the two growth limits in
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:growth} follow. This proves Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:main}.

\section{Consequences and the inverse counting threshold}\label{ivd:sc:sec:consequences}
\subsection{The exponent and the literal numerical proposal}\label{ivd:sc:sub:exponent}
For every fixed $\beta>1/3$, Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:main} gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:beta}
 \frac{\log a_j(n)-n\log\mu_j}{n^\beta}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
In particular $\psi(n)/n^{3/8}=n^{-1/24}(\log n)^{2/3}\to0$.
If \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:oldform} were a full equivalent, taking logarithms would
instead give the limit $-b<0$ at $\beta=3/8$, a contradiction.
The issue is the fixed negative nonzero coefficient of $n^{3/8}$ in
the logarithm, not the numerical accuracy of an approximation over a
finite range. A polynomial factor $n^g$ or fixed amplitude cannot repair
the conflict since their logarithms are $O(\log n)$.

The positive lower deficit bound also gives
$D_j(n)/n^{1/3}\to\infty$. Hence a pure $n^{1/3}$ deficit with a
fixed finite positive coefficient is ruled out as well. One precise
sense of the stretched exponent is
\[
 \frac{\log D_j(n)}{\log n}\longrightarrow\frac13,
\]
while the matching logarithmic multiplier is $(\log n)^{2/3}$ in the
two-sided order estimate. None of this supplies a limit for
$D_j(n)/\psi(n)$, nor does it justify differentiating an unspecified
$\Theta$ term to obtain ratio asymptotics.

\subsection{A positive inverse correction with the correct ceilings}\label{ivd:sc:sub:inverse}
For $T>1$ define
\[
 N_j(T)=\min\{n\ge0:a_j(n)\ge T\},\qquad
 s=\frac{\log T}{\log\mu_j}.
\]
The set is nonempty by exponential growth. The sequence is in fact
strictly increasing for $n\ge1$: appending the new largest entry $n$
to a valid sequence of length $n$ is an injection. A forbidden triple
using that last entry would require an earlier entry at least $n$,
which is impossible. The valid sequence
$(0,1,\ldots,n-1,n-1)$ lies outside this injection's image, proving
strictness. The only initial tie is $a_j(0)=a_j(1)=1$.
The following threshold proof does not need that monotonicity.

\end{writenote}

\begin{corollary}\label{ivd:sc:cor:inverse}
For each class there are constants $0<d_j<d_j^+<\infty$ such that, for
all sufficiently large real $T$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:inverse}
 \left\lceil s+d_j\psi(s)\right\rceil\le N_j(T)
 \le\left\lceil s+d_j^+\psi(s)\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
 N_j(T)-\frac{\log T}{\log\mu_j}
 =\Theta\big((\log T)^{1/3}(\log\log T)^{2/3}\big),
\]
with a positive correction.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $L=\log\mu_j$ and use eventual constants $c,C$ in
$c\psi(n)\le nL-\log a_j(n)\le C\psi(n)$.
Choose $0<d<c/L$ and $D>C/L$. For large $T$, no bounded $n$
reaches $T$. If $n<s$, the global bound $a_j(n)\le\mu_j^n<T$
rules it out. If $s\le n<s+d\psi(s)$, monotonicity of the real
function $\psi$ gives
\[
 \log a_j(n)\le nL-c\psi(n)
 <\log T+(dL-c)\psi(s)<\log T.
\]
Thus every integer below $\lceil s+d\psi(s)\rceil$ is excluded,
proving the lower ceiling direction, including when its argument is an
integer. For the upper bound take $n=\lceil s+D\psi(s)\rceil$.
Since $\psi(s)=o(s)$, we have $n/s\to1$ and
$\psi(n)/\psi(s)\to1$. Therefore
\[
 \log a_j(n)\ge nL-C\psi(n)
 \ge\log T+DL\psi(s)-C\psi(n)>\log T
\]
eventually. Finally
$\psi(s)\sim L^{-1/3}(\log T)^{1/3}(\log\log T)^{2/3}$,
and the bounded ceiling errors are negligible on this diverging scale.
\end{proof}
The constants here are existential. The report supplies neither calibrated
finite thresholds nor a rounding-sharp one-term inverse equivalent.
For all $T>1$, the simpler global lower bound
$\lceil\log T/\log\mu_j\rceil\le N_j(T)$ remains valid.

\section{Boundary regularity and differential equations}\label{ivd:sc:sec:analytic}
For either class write $a_n=a_j(n)$, $\mu=\mu_j$, $\rho=1/\mu$,
$b_n=a_n\mu^{-n}$, and
\[
 F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n,\qquad
 f(\theta)=F(\rho e^{i\theta})=\sum_{n\ge0}b_ne^{in\theta}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:main} implies $e^{-C\psi(n)}\le b_n\le
e^{-c\psi(n)}$ eventually. The radius of $F$ is exactly $\rho$.

\begin{theorem}\label{ivd:sc:thm:analytic}
Every derivative of $F$ extends continuously from its open convergence
disk to the closed disk $|z|\le\rho$. The boundary function $f$ is
smooth and periodic. As the integer $k\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \log\|f^{(k)}\|_\infty
 &=3k\log k-2k\log\log(k+2)+O(k),\label{ivd:sc:eq:gevrey}\\
 \log F^{(k)}(\rho)
 &=3k\log k-2k\log\log(k+2)+O(k).\label{ivd:sc:eq:radialgevrey}
\end{align}
The boundary function has sharp Gevrey order $3$. The point $\rho$ is
a genuine singularity of $F$, and $F$ is not D-finite over
$\mathbb C(z)$; in particular it is not algebraic.
\end{theorem}
Here Gevrey order $s$ means that
$\|f^{(k)}\|_\infty\le A B^k(k!)^s$ for some $A,B>0$ and all $k$.
``Sharp order $3$'' means the estimate holds for $s=3$ and fails for
every $s<3$. It is not a claim of a convergent boundary Taylor series.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
The $O(k)$ in \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:gevrey}--\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:radialgevrey} is identified as $[\log(2D/\pi^2)-3]k+o(k)$, plus $k\log\mu$ for the radial derivatives, by Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}, Corollary~\ref{ivd:sh:cor:derivatives}, and refined to $[\log(2D/\pi^2)-3]k-k\log\log k/\log k+O(k/\log k)$ by Part~\ref{ivd:ll:part}, Corollary~\ref{ivd:ll:cor:moments}; those Parts write $\log\log k$, which differs from $\log\log(k+2)$ here by $O(1/(k\log k))$, so by $O(1/\log k)$ after multiplication by $2k$. The genuine singularity at $\rho$, the non-D-finiteness over $\mathbb C(z)$ and the non-algebraicity are proved only in this Part.
\end{writenote}

\subsection{All boundary derivatives and their growth}
For every fixed $k$, the sum $\sum n^k b_n$ is finite because
$\psi(n)/\log n\to\infty$. Thus each derivative series for $F$
converges absolutely and uniformly on $|z|\le\rho$. The same argument
for the Fourier series of $f$ gives smoothness and, for $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:momentnorm}
 f^{(k)}(0)=i^k\sum_{n\ge1}n^k b_n,\qquad
 \|f^{(k)}\|_\infty=\sum_{n\ge1}n^k b_n=:M_k.
\end{equation}
The upper norm bound follows from the triangle inequality, and equality
holds at $\theta=0$ by nonnegativity of $b_n$.

Put $N_k=k^3/[\log(k+2)]^2$. Then $N_k/k\to\infty$ and
$\psi(N_k)/k\to3^{2/3}$. In particular, there exist fixed constants $d,D>0$ such that
\[
 dk\le\psi(N_k)\le Dk
\]
for all large $k$. The single term $n_k=\lfloor N_k\rfloor$ and the
lower coefficient bound give
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:momentlower}
 M_k\ge\exp\{k\log N_k-O(k)\}.
\end{equation}
For a matching upper bound, for any fixed $a>0$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:supmoment}
 \sup_{n\ge2}n^k e^{-a\psi(n)}
 \le\exp\{k\log N_k+O_a(k)\}.
\end{equation}
For $n\le N_k$ this is immediate. For $n\ge N_k$, monotonicity of
$\log$ yields
$\psi(n)\ge\psi(N_k)(n/N_k)^{1/3}\ge dk(n/N_k)^{1/3}$.
Writing $u=n/N_k$, the remaining maximization is of
$\log u-ad u^{1/3}$ over $u\ge1$, whose supremum is a finite
constant independent of $k$. This proves \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:supmoment}.

Absorb finitely many exceptional coefficients into a fixed multiplicative
factor. Apply \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:supmoment} with $a=c/2$, leaving the summable
factor $e^{-c\psi(n)/2}$ outside the supremum. The $n=1$ term is
harmless. Consequently $M_k\le\exp\{k\log N_k+O(k)\}$.
Together with \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:momentlower} and
$\log N_k=3\log k-2\log\log(k+2)$ this proves \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:gevrey}.

For the radial derivatives, with $(n)_k=n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)$,
\[
 F^{(k)}(\rho)=\mu^k\sum_{n\ge k}(n)_k b_n.
\]
The upper bound follows from $(n)_k\le n^k$. For the lower bound use
$n=n_k\ge2k$, so $(n_k)_k\ge(n_k/2)^k$. The extra factors
$\mu^k$ and $2^{-k}$ change the logarithm only by $O(k)$.
This proves \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:radialgevrey}. Stirling's formula now proves
Gevrey order $3$ and excludes every smaller order.

Moreover,
\[
 \left(\frac{F^{(k)}(\rho)}{k!}\right)^{1/k}
 =\frac{k^2}{[\log(k+2)]^2}\exp\{O(1)\}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus the Taylor series formed from the radial derivatives at $\rho$
has radius zero. A holomorphic continuation through $\rho$ would have
a positive-radius Taylor series with those derivatives, which is
impossible. This proves genuine singularity despite all finite
boundary derivatives. It says nothing about analytic continuation
through other points of the convergence circle.

\subsection{The arithmetic obstruction to a differential equation}
Suppose first that $F$ were D-finite over $\mathbb Q(z)$, meaning it
satisfies a nonzero linear differential equation with rational-function
coefficients. Its coefficients are integers, hence have denominator one
and only trivial algebraic conjugates; $a_n\le\mu^n$ gives the required
exponential bound. Therefore it is a G-function.

We use the Andr\'e--Chudnovsky--Katz theorem in the form stated by
Fischler and Rivoal \cite[Theorem 6, pp.\ 328--329]{FR}: a minimal
differential operator of a G-function is Fuchsian, with rational local
exponents. Choose a small sector about $\rho$ containing the interval immediately
to its left and avoiding other operator singularities; this selects the
original branch of $F$. If $\rho$ is an ordinary point of the operator,
$F$ is already holomorphic there. Otherwise, writing $w=\rho-z$,
its solutions on that sector are finite sums
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sc:eq:powerlog}
 \sum_{q\in E}\sum_{j=0}^{J_q} w^q(\log w)^j h_{qj}(w),
\end{equation}
where $E\subset\mathbb Q$ is finite and the $h_{qj}$ are holomorphic
at zero. This is the only arithmetic regular-singularity theorem
imported into the argument.

A convergent representation \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:powerlog} that is smooth to every
order along $w>0$ must actually be holomorphic at $w=0$. To see this,
combine exponent classes modulo integers and expand the finitely many
holomorphic factors. The supports form a finite union of discrete sets
$q+\NN$, bounded below. Separate the nonnegative integral powers
without logarithms; they form a convergent holomorphic function. If any
nonholomorphic term remains, choose its least exponent $r$ and at that
exponent its largest nonzero logarithmic degree $j$. Its coefficient
is a nonzero polynomial $P(\log w)$ of degree $j$. For some
$\epsilon>0$ and finite $J$, the remaining analytic tails satisfy
\[
 R^{(m)}(w)=O\big(w^{r+\epsilon-m}(1+|\log w|)^J\big)
 \qquad(w\downarrow0)
\]
after every fixed number $m$ of derivatives. This follows by factoring
the first surviving power from each of the finitely many convergent
tails and bounding its analytic factor and derivatives on a smaller
disk. For the leading term, the exact identity
\[
 \left(\frac d{dw}\right)^m[w^rP(\log w)]
 =w^{r-m}\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\left(r-\ell+\frac d{dt}\right)P(t)
 \bigg|_{t=\log w}
\]
controls all possible logarithmic cancellation.
\begin{itemize}
\item If $r<0$, this residual function is unbounded as $w\downarrow0$.
\item If $r\ge0$ is nonintegral, differentiate $\lfloor r\rfloor+1$
times. Its falling-factorial multiplier is nonzero, and the resulting
negative power makes that derivative unbounded.
\item If $r\ge0$ is integral, then $j\ge1$. Differentiating $r+1$
times gives a nonzero leading multiple of
$w^{-1}(\log w)^{j-1}$, again unbounded.
\end{itemize}
Higher exponents and lower logarithmic degrees cannot cancel the stated
leading terms. Termwise differentiation is justified by the convergent
local representation, so no uncontrolled asymptotic remainder is being
differentiated. Each case contradicts the finite derivatives established
above. The solution must therefore be holomorphic at $\rho$, contradicting
its genuine singularity. Thus $F$ is not D-finite over $\mathbb Q(z)$.

Finally, D-finiteness over $\mathbb C(z)$ descends to $\mathbb Q(z)$
for a rational-coefficient series. Clear denominators in a proposed
complex differential equation and fix its finite order and coefficient
degree bounds. Its finitely many unknown coefficients satisfy a
homogeneous linear system with rational entries, one equation for each
series coefficient. The rational row space has a finite basis and the
same rank over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb C$. A nonzero complex null
vector would therefore give a nonzero rational one and a rational
differential equation, already excluded. This completes the proof of
Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:analytic}. The familiar implication algebraic
$\Rightarrow$ D-finite gives nonalgebraicity.

These conclusions do not identify a unique dominant singularity or a
complex slit-domain expansion, and do not supply a leading stretched
constant or further terms of a transseries.

\section{What remains to be proved}\label{ivd:sc:sec:remains}
The two bounds identify a scale, leaving several distinct problems open.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A leading logarithmic constant.} Does
$D_j(n)/\psi(n)$ converge to a positive constant? If so, one needs a
sharper principle for the optimal height profile or the killed renewal
kernel. Optimizing the present nonsharp inequalities cannot identify it.
\item \textbf{An equivalent for the sequence.} Even a leading
logarithmic constant would leave lower-order exponent terms, polynomial
factors, and an amplitude unresolved. Their existence and normalization
would require separate arguments.
\item \textbf{Ratios and effective error rates.} A two-sided deficit
bound does not establish $a_j(n+1)/a_j(n)\to\mu_j$ or a correction
rate for this ratio. Effective constants and usable onset bounds are
also not supplied here.
\item \textbf{All orders and transseries.} No complete asymptotic
expansion, all-orders remainder theorem, or transseries follows from
the matching order estimate. Such claims would need an identified
analytic structure and uniform error control.
\item \textbf{Numerical crossover.} The logarithmic multiplier changes
slowly, so finite-range pure-power fits merit fresh investigation against
the proven scale. Determining a practical crossover or an effective
fitting window is a separate numerical project; no new digits or
crossover prediction are claimed in this report.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Definitions and the sharp theorem}\label{ivd:sh:sec:main}
An inversion sequence of length $n$ is an integer sequence
$e=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ with $0\le e_i<i$. Let $a_{247}(n)$ count those
with no $i<j<k$ satisfying $e_i\le e_j$ and $e_i\ge e_k$.
Let $a_{759}(n)$ count those with no $i<j<k$ satisfying these two
inequalities and $e_j\ne e_k$. In pattern notation the forbidden
patterns are $(000,010,110,120)$ and $(010,110,120)$, respectively.
Thus
\[
 a_{247}(n)=\mathrm{A279551}(n),\qquad
 a_{759}(n)=\mathrm{A279556}(n),\qquad a_j(0)=1.
\]
All logarithms are natural. Throughout the proof, $D$ denotes the
positive diffusion coefficient in the following table, not a deficit:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 j&\mu_j&D_j&\sigma_{D_j}=(3\pi^2/(2D_j))^{1/3}\\\hline
 759&9&1&(3\pi^2/2)^{1/3}\\
 247&8&1/2&(3\pi^2)^{1/3}
\end{array}
\]
For $n>1$ define
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:scales}
 H=H_n=n^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3},\quad
 S=S_n=n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3},\quad
 \theta_n=H/n=S/H.
\end{equation}
In particular $H^2=nS$, $H^3=n^2\log n$, $HS=n\log n$,
and $\theta_n^2=S/n$.
\begin{theorem}\label{ivd:sh:thm:main}
For $j\in\{759,247\}$, as $n\to\infty$ through all integers,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:main}
 \log a_j(n)=n\log\mu_j-\sigma_{D_j}S_n+o(S_n).
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
 a_j(n)=\mu_j^n\exp\{-(\sigma_{D_j}+o(1))S_n\}.
\]
Moreover $a_j(n)\le\mu_j^n$ for every $n\ge0$ and
$\lim_{n\to\infty}a_j(n)^{1/n}=\mu_j$.
\end{theorem}
The constants are approximately $2.455445701568578$ for A279556 and
$3.093667726280136$ for A279551. These are evaluations of the exact
formula, not fits to sequence data. The error in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:main} is
only $o(S_n)$; exponentiating it does not give a multiplicative
$1+o(1)$ error. In particular the theorem identifies neither an exact
multiplier nor lower-order exponent terms.

Part~\ref{ivd:ll:part}, Theorem~\ref{ivd:ll:thm:main}, identifies the next exponent term, $\frac{7\sigma_D}3S_n\log\log n/\log n$, with remainder $O(S_n/\log n)$; the multiplier and the polynomial factor are still unknown.

\section{Exact legal renewal transforms}\label{ivd:sh:sec:transforms}
A boundary path has a unique decomposition into ordinary boundary
edges, $b=0$ commitment edges, and completed commitment excursions
\eqref{ivd:sc:eq:Q}. In a completed excursion the last fulfillment is the
first return to zero outstanding commitments. Write $\Delta=w-\ell$
and $L=w+1$ for its displacement and original duration. For $b=0$
use $\Delta=-\ell,L=1$; the ordinary edge has $\Delta=L=1$ and
weight $a=x/\mu$.

Set $d_b=C_b/4^b$. Extending only the drop sum for a fixed $b$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:joint}
 \sum_{\ell,w}Q_j(b,\ell,w)e^{\lambda\Delta+\eta L}
 =A_j(\lambda,\eta)d_b R_j(\lambda,\eta)^b,
\end{equation}
with the duration-one interpretation at $b=0$, where
\begin{align}
 A_{759}&=\frac{e^\eta}{9(1-e^{-\lambda}/3)},&
 R_{759}&=\frac{4e^\eta}{9(1-e^{-\lambda}/3)(1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/3)},
 \label{ivd:sh:eq:trans759}\\
 A_{247}&=\frac{e^\eta(1+e^{-\lambda}/2)}8,&
 R_{247}&=\frac{e^\eta(1+e^{-\lambda}/2)}{2(1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/4)}.
 \label{ivd:sh:eq:trans247}
\end{align}
Indeed, the elementary identities used in this summation are
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{\ell\ge b}\binom\ell b z^\ell&=\frac{z^b}{(1-z)^{b+1}},&
 \sum_\ell\binom{b+1}{\ell-b}z^\ell&=z^b(1+z)^{b+1},\\
 \sum_{w\ge b}\binom{w-1}{b-1}t^w&=\left(\frac t{1-t}\right)^b
 &&(b\ge1).
\end{align*}
At fixed $b$ the sums converge in a fixed neighborhood of $(0,0)$,
independent of $b$. No convergence of the unrestricted positive-tilt
sum over all $b$ is asserted.

Under the fixed-$b$ exponential tilt, drop and duration are independent.
Using $\NB^{\mathrm{fail}}(m,q)$ for failures before $m$ successes
of probability $q$, the tilted drop laws are
\begin{align}
 759:\quad &\ell=b+\NB^{\mathrm{fail}}(b+1,1-e^{-\lambda}/3),\\
 247:\quad &\ell=b+\Bin(b+1,e^{-\lambda}/(2+e^{-\lambda})).
 \label{ivd:sh:eq:droplaws}
\end{align}
They also hold at $b=0$. For $b\ge1$, independently,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:durationlaws}
 w\sim\begin{cases}
 \NB^{\mathrm{trials}}(b,1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/3),&759,\\
 \NB^{\mathrm{trials}}(b,1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/4),&247.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here the superscript ``trials'' means that successes are counted too.
Independence at fixed $b$ survives the legal-drop restriction because
that restriction concerns $\ell$ alone. Displacement and duration
are not independent, since both involve $w$.

Let $F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)$ be the drop CDF in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:droplaws}.
The exact legal row is
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:legalrow}
 T_p(\lambda,\eta)=a e^{\lambda+\eta}
  +A_j(\lambda,\eta)\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}
      d_b R_j(\lambda,\eta)^b F_{b,\lambda}(p-1).
\end{equation}
At $p=0$ the sum is empty. The cutoff is truly $\ell\le p-1$;
using merely $b\le p-1$ would change the sharp result.
The constants and derivatives at the origin are
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:constants}
\begin{array}{c|ccccccc}
 j&a&A_0&\alpha&v&D=v/\alpha&r=a+2A_0&1-r\\\hline
 759&1/3&1/6&3/2&3/2&1&2/3&1/3\\
 247&1/4&3/16&4/3&2/3&1/2&5/8&3/8
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Specifically $R(0,0)=1$, $(\log R)_\lambda(0,0)=0$,
$(\log R)_\eta(0,0)=\alpha$, and
$(\log R)_{\lambda\lambda}(0,0)=v$. Analyticity gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:jointtaylor}
 \log R(\lambda,\eta)=\alpha\eta+\tfrac v2\lambda^2
  +O(|\lambda|^3+|\lambda\eta|+\eta^2).
\end{equation}
The identity $\sum_{b\ge0}d_b=2$ shows that the unrestricted
zero-tilt boundary row is $r<1$, consistently with original-time
substochasticity established earlier.

\subsection{Uniform concentration estimates}
For fixed $b$, normalize the unrestricted zero-tilt commitment mass
by $A_0d_b$. There are fixed $c,C,\theta_0>0$ such that
\begin{align}
 \Prob(|\Delta|>z)&\le C\exp\{-c\min(z^2/(b+1),z)\},
 \label{ivd:sh:eq:deltatail}\\
 \Prob(L>u)&\le C\exp\{-cu+C(b+1)\}.
 \label{ivd:sh:eq:durationtail}
\end{align}
Both include $b=0$. To prove the first, \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:joint} and
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:jointtaylor} give, for $|\vartheta|\le\theta_0$,
\[
 \E e^{\vartheta\Delta}\le
 \exp\{C|\vartheta|+Cb\vartheta^2\}.
\]
Use Chernoff's bound with either sign and magnitude proportional to
$\min(z/(b+1),1)$; bounded $z$ is absorbed in $C$.
A fixed small positive $\eta$ in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:joint} proves
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:durationtail}.

Uniformly for $\lambda$ near zero, the tilted drop law satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:droptail}
 \Prob(|\ell-\E\ell|\ge z)
 \le 2\exp\{-c\min(z^2/(b+1),z)\}.
\end{equation}
Each law in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:droplaws} is a sum of $b+1$ independent
geometric or Bernoulli variables, plus $b$. Their centered log MGFs
are bounded by $C\vartheta^2$ on a common neighborhood of zero.
The same Chernoff argument applies. The means are
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:dropmean}
 \E\ell=\alpha(\lambda)b+O(1),\quad
 \alpha(\lambda)=\begin{cases}
 (1-e^{-\lambda}/3)^{-1},&759,\\
 1+e^{-\lambda}/(2+e^{-\lambda}),&247,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
so $\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha+O(\lambda)$ uniformly.

\section{The uniform sharp local root}\label{ivd:sh:sec:localroot}
\begin{theorem}\label{ivd:sh:thm:localroot}
Fix $B<\infty$. Uniformly for
$|\lambda|\le B\sqrt{\log p/p}$ and $c$ in a fixed compact interval,
put
\[
 s=\tfrac12\log p+\log\log p+c,\qquad
 \eta=s/p-D\lambda^2/2.
\]
Then, as integer $p\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:rowlimit}
 T_p(\lambda,\eta)=r+2A_0\sqrt{\alpha/\pi}\,e^c+o(1).
\end{equation}
The unique root $T_p(\lambda,\eta_p(\lambda))=1$ therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:root}
 \eta_p(\lambda)=
 \frac{\frac12\log p+\log\log p+c_*+o(1)}p
  -\frac D2\lambda^2,
 \qquad c_*=\tfrac12\log(\pi/\alpha),
\end{equation}
uniformly on the same $\lambda$ range.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $v=\alpha D$, \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:jointtaylor} yields
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:betaroot}
 \beta:=\log R(\lambda,\eta)=\alpha s/p+o(1/p).
\end{equation}
Indeed, $p$ times the remainder is
$O((\log p)^{3/2}/\sqrt p+(\log p)^2/p)=o(1)$ uniformly.
Also $A=A_0+o(1)$ and $ae^{\lambda+\eta}=a+o(1)$.

Set $h=\sqrt p\log p$. The tilt moves the mean drop cutoff by
$O(p|\lambda|+1)=O(\sqrt{p\log p})=o(h)$.
Equations \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:droptail}--\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:dropmean} imply
\begin{align}
 b\le p/\alpha-h&\implies F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)
       \ge1-e^{-c_1(\log p)^2},\label{ivd:sh:eq:cutlower}\\
 b\ge p/\alpha+h&\implies F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)
       \le e^{-c_1(\log p)^2}.
 \label{ivd:sh:eq:cutupper}
\end{align}
At either endpoint the distance between threshold and mean is a
positive fixed multiple of $h$; use $b+1\le p$ in the concentration
denominator. Rounding changes the margin by $O(1)$ only.
For $m_-=\lfloor p/\alpha-h\rfloor$ and
$m_+=\lceil p/\alpha+h\rceil$, the legal sum is squeezed between
the untruncated sums through $m_-$ and $m_+$, with a multiplicative
$1-o(1)$ below and an additive error above bounded by
\[
 e^{-c_1(\log p)^2}\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}d_bR^b=o(1).
\]
The last sum grows only polynomially in $p$ by \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:betaroot}.
This is the essential use of the exact drop cutoff.

For either comparison endpoint $m$, we have $m\sim p/\alpha$ and
$m\beta=s+o(1)$, since $h\log p/p=o(1)$. Stirling's formula gives
$d_b=\pi^{-1/2}b^{-3/2}(1+O(1/b))$ for $b\ge1$.
We claim
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:laplacesum}
 \sum_{b=0}^m d_b e^{\beta b}
   =2+\frac{e^s}{\sqrt{\pi m}\,s}(1+o(1)).
\end{equation}
For $b\le m/2$, the excess over the unweighted sum is at most
\[
 C\beta\sqrt m\,e^{\beta m/2}
 =O(p^{-1/4}(\log p)^{3/2})=o(1),
\]
using $\sum_{b\le m}b d_b=O(\sqrt m)$.
The omitted unweighted tail is $O(m^{-1/2})$.
For $b>m/2$, write $k=m-b$. After extracting
$e^{\beta m}/(\sqrt\pi m^{3/2})$, the remaining sum is
\[
 \sum_{0\le k<m/2}e^{-\beta k}(1-k/m)^{-3/2}(1+O(1/m))
 \sim\beta^{-1}.
\]
For a direct proof, first restrict to $k\le K/\beta$; there
$k/m=O(K/s)\to0$. The complementary geometric tail is at most
$C e^{-K}/\beta$. Let $p\to\infty$ and then $K\to\infty$.
This proves \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:laplacesum}, uniformly in the stated ranges.
Now
\[
 \frac{e^s}{\sqrt{\pi m}\,s}\longrightarrow
  2\sqrt{\alpha/\pi}\,e^c,
\]
which proves \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:rowlimit}.

For $p\ge2$ the legal $b=1,\ell=1$ term is positive; its bulk MGF
diverges at the upper duration-MGF edge. The row is continuous and
strictly increasing in $\eta$, tends to zero as $\eta\to-\infty$,
and therefore has exactly one root. At $p=0,1$ the explicit rows are
\[
 T_0=ae^{\lambda+\eta},\qquad
 T_1=e^\eta(ae^\lambda+1/\mu),
\]
and also have unique roots. In \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:rowlimit} bracket the root
with $c=c_*\pm\varepsilon$. Since $1-r=2A_0$ in both classes,
$c_*=(1/2)\log(\pi/\alpha)$, and uniformity plus monotonicity give
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:root}.
\end{proof}
The local $\log\log p$ term in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:root} is not a
next-order expansion of the coefficient logarithm. On the global
scales \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:scales}, this theorem implies
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:scaledroot}
 \frac nS\eta_{\lfloor Hx\rfloor}(\theta_n q)
   \longrightarrow\frac1{3x}-\frac D2q^2
\end{equation}
locally uniformly for $x>0$ and bounded $q$. The factor $1/3$
comes from $HS/n=\log n$ and $\log H\sim(2/3)\log n$.

\section{The singular variational minimum}\label{ivd:sh:sec:variational}
For nonnegative $f\in H_0^1(0,1)$ with $\int_0^1 1/f<\infty$, set
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:action}
 I_D(f)=\int_0^1\left(\frac{f'(t)^2}{2D}+\frac1{3f(t)}\right)\dd t.
\end{equation}
Finite potential means $f>0$ almost everywhere; endpoint values are
understood as Sobolev traces.
\begin{theorem}\label{ivd:sh:thm:variational}
The unique minimizer of \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:action} is the interior positive
symmetric function $f_*$ given parametrically by
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:minimizer}
 f_*(t)=M\sin^2\vartheta,\qquad
 t=\frac{\vartheta-\sin\vartheta\cos\vartheta}{\pi},\quad
 0\le\vartheta\le\pi,\qquad M=\left(\frac{2D}{3\pi^2}\right)^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
Its action is $I_D(f_*)=1/M=\sigma_D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Direct differentiation, using $M^3\pi^2=2D/3$, gives
\begin{align}
 f_*'&=M\pi\cot\vartheta,&
 f_*''&=-D/(3f_*^2),\label{ivd:sh:eq:minode}\\
 \frac{f_*'^2}{2D}&=\frac13(1/f_*-1/M),&
 \int_0^1\frac{\dd t}{f_*(t)}&=2/M.
 \label{ivd:sh:eq:minintegrals}
\end{align}
Near zero, $f_*(t)\sim(3D/2)^{1/3}t^{2/3}$, with the reflected
behavior at one. Thus $f_*\in H_0^1$, both terms of its action
are integrable, and \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:minintegrals} gives $I_D(f_*)=1/M$.

For any admissible $g$, the first variation vanishes:
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:firstvariation}
 \int_0^1\left[\frac{f_*'(g'-f_*')}{D}
                   -\frac{g-f_*}{3f_*^2}\right]\dd t=0.
\end{equation}
To justify integration by parts, the Sobolev trace estimate is
$|g(t)|\le\sqrt t\,\|g'\|_{L^2(0,t)}=o(\sqrt t)$.
Since $f_*'=O(t^{-1/3})$, the boundary product vanishes.
Also $|g|/f_*^2=O(t^{-5/6})$ and $1/f_*=O(t^{-2/3})$
are integrable, and the kinetic cross term is integrable by
Cauchy--Schwarz. The other endpoint is identical. Apply
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:minode} on $[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]$ and pass to the
limit to obtain \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:firstvariation}.
The scalar identity
\[
 \frac1g-\frac1{f_*}+\frac{g-f_*}{f_*^2}
   =\frac{(g-f_*)^2}{g f_*^2}
\]
holds almost everywhere. Subtracting the two actions and using
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:firstvariation} yields the nonnegative remainder
\[
 I_D(g)-I_D(f_*)=
 \frac1{2D}\int_0^1(g'-f_*')^2\dd t
 +\frac13\int_0^1\frac{(g-f_*)^2}{g f_*^2}\dd t\ge0.
\]
Equality forces $g=f_*$, proving uniqueness.
\end{proof}
The variational calculation alone is not a coefficient theorem.
The remaining proof sections establish the required global bounds for the
actual legal kernel, including both singular endpoint layers.

\section{Exact tilted laws and their legal moments}\label{ivd:sh:sec:moments}
At height $p$, define the row-one probability law on legal cycles by
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:probabilitylaw}
 \asymplaw_{p,\lambda}(\text{cycle})
  =Q_p(\text{cycle})e^{\lambda\Delta+\eta_p(\lambda)L}.
\end{equation}
The notation $Q_p$ includes ordinary and $b=0$ boundary edges.
All expectations in this section use \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:probabilitylaw} unless
a fixed-$b$ unrestricted law is explicitly named.

\begin{lemma}\label{ivd:sh:lem:moments}
For each finite $B$, uniformly as $p\to\infty$ and
$|\lambda|\le B\sqrt{\log p/p}$, there are constants $c,C>0$ with
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:moments}
 cp\le\E L\le Cp,\qquad
 \E L^2\le Cp^2,\qquad \E\Delta^2\le Cp\log p.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At the root tilt, let
$s_p=(1/2)\log p+\log\log p+c_*+o(1)$.
Then \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:root} and \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:jointtaylor} give
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:rootparameters}
 \log R=\alpha s_p/p+o(1/p),\qquad A=A_0+o(1).
\end{equation}
The negative-binomial parameters in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:durationlaws} stay in a
compact subset of $(0,1)$. Thus for $1\le b\le p-1$,
$\E[(w+1)^2\mid b]\le C(b+1)^2\le Cp^2$.
Multiply by the legal masses
$A d_b R^b F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)$, which together with the ordinary edge
sum exactly to one. The $b=0$ and ordinary durations equal one.
This proves $\E L^2\le Cp^2$ and, by Cauchy--Schwarz, $\E L\le Cp$.

For the lower mean bound, restrict $b$ to the integer interval
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:activeinterval}
 J_p=[p/\alpha-2p/\log p,\ p/\alpha-p/\log p].
\end{equation}
It is contained in $[1,p-1]$ for large $p$. On $J_p$, the Catalan
estimate, \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:rootparameters}, and
$e^{s_p}\asymp\sqrt p\log p$ show that
$A d_b R^b\ge c\log p/p$. There are order $p/\log p$ such integers.
The tilted mean drop lies below $p$ by order $p/\log p$; the
$O(p|\lambda|+1)$ shift is smaller, uniformly. Equation
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:droptail} implies $F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)=1-o(1)$ throughout
$J_p$. Hence this interval has probability at least a positive
constant. There $L\ge b+1\ge cp$, proving $\E L\ge cp$.

For the displacement bound, put $h=\sqrt p\log p$ and
$m_+=\lceil p/\alpha+h\rceil$. The local-root proof gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:momentcut}
 \sum_{b\le m_+}d_bR^b=O(1),\qquad
 F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)\le e^{-c(\log p)^2}\quad(b>m_+).
\end{equation}
At fixed $b$, before applying legality, drop and duration are independent,
have variances $O(b+1)$, and their mean coefficients agree at zero tilt.
Analyticity therefore gives
\[
 \E(w-\ell)=O(1+(b+1)(|\lambda|+|\eta|)).
\]
Since $\eta=O(\log p/p)$, for $b\le p-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:fixedbsecond}
 \E[(w-\ell)^2\mid b]
 \le C[(b+1)+(b+1)^2(\lambda^2+\eta^2)]\le Cp\log p.
\end{equation}
For $b\le m_+$, drop the legality indicator only in this numerator;
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:momentcut} makes the summed contribution $O(p\log p)$.
For $b>m_+$ use legality itself: $0\le\ell\le p-1$, and $w$ is
independent of that event. Consequently
\[
 \E[(w-\ell)^2\mathbf1_{\{\ell\le p-1\}}\mid b]
 \le2\E[w^2\mid b]F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)+2p^2F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)
 \le Cp^2F_{b,\lambda}(p-1).
\]
The unrestricted sum through $p-1$ is at most polynomial in $p$ by
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:rootparameters}; the exponentially small factor in
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:momentcut} makes this far tail $o(1)$.
The $b=0$ drop second moment is uniformly bounded. The ordinary edge
contributes at most one. This proves \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:moments}.
Extending the unrestricted mixture all the way to $b=p$ in the
central bound would not be justified; its mass need not be $O(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{ivd:sh:lem:drift}
For fixed $0<l<r<\infty$ and $Q<\infty$, uniformly for
$p/H\in[l,r]$ and $|q|\le Q$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:drift}
 \frac{\E_{p,\theta_n q}\Delta}{\E_{p,\theta_n q}L}
  =\theta_n[Dq+o(1)].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The transform is analytic near its root and its $\eta$ derivative is
positive. Implicit differentiation gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:rootderivative}
 \eta_p'(\lambda)=-\E\Delta/\E L.
\end{equation}
The function $\log T_p$ is convex in $(\lambda,\eta)$ and strictly
increasing in $\eta$. Its sublevel set is the hypograph of the root
function, so $\eta_p$ is concave. Put
$\psi_n(x,q)=(n/S)\eta_{\lfloor Hx\rfloor}(\theta_n q)$.
Equation \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:scaledroot} gives locally uniform convergence to
$1/(3x)-Dq^2/2$. For differentiable concave functions, the derivative
at $q$ lies between the forward and backward difference quotients
with step $u>0$. Uniform convergence bounds these quotients uniformly
in $x$ and on compact $q$ intervals. Let first $n\to\infty$, then
$u\downarrow0$. The limiting quadratic has a uniform modulus of
continuity of its derivative, hence
$\partial_q\psi_n(x,q)\to-Dq$ uniformly.
Because $(n/S)\theta_n=1/\theta_n$, this says
$\eta_p'(\theta_n q)=\theta_n[-Dq+o(1)]$.
Use \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:rootderivative} to conclude.
\end{proof}
No derivative of an unspecified asymptotic remainder has been taken.
Concavity supplies the derivative control that is actually needed.

\section{A positive strip path with its genuine random clock}\label{ivd:sh:sec:tube}
\begin{theorem}\label{ivd:sh:thm:tube}
Let $0\le a<b\le1$ and let $f$ be $C^2$ and positive on a neighborhood
of $[a,b]$. Start at boundary height $p_n$ and integer original time
$s_0$ with $p_n/H\to f(a)$ and $s_0/n\to a$. At departure time $s$
and height $p$ use \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:probabilitylaw} with
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:control}
 \lambda_s=\theta_n q(s/n),\qquad q=f'/D.
\end{equation}
Stop at the first boundary time $s_N\ge nb$. With probability tending
to one under this tilted law,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:tube}
 s_N/n\to b,\qquad
 \max_{i\le N}|p_i/H-f(s_i/n)|\to0.
\end{equation}
For every fixed positive action tolerance, the original normalized mass
of a set of these paths is at least
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:tubemass}
 \exp\left\{-S\left[
  \int_a^b\left(\frac{f'^2}{2D}+\frac1{3f}\right)\dd t+o(1)
 \right]\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:tube} is convergence in probability; equivalently in
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:tubemass} any fixed positive tolerance may replace $o(1)$
for all sufficiently large $n$. The estimates are uniform for bounded
$C^2$ families on common neighborhoods with a common positive strip
margin and converging starting heights and times.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a strip $[lH,rH]$ whose rescaled interior contains the values
of $f$ with a fixed positive margin, and extend $f$ slightly past $b$.
Stop additionally on leaving that strip and after
\[
 m_n=\lceil2n/(cH)\rceil+1
\]
cycles, where $c$ is a uniform lower-mean constant from
Lemma~\ref{ivd:sh:lem:moments} on the strip. All martingale increments below
are set to zero after this auxiliary stopping time.

Write $\mu_i=\E[L_i\mid\text{past}]$. On active steps,
$\mu_i\ge cH$ and $\E[L_i^2\mid\text{past}]\le CH^2$.
The clock martingale
\[
 Z_k=\sum_{i<k}(L_i-\mu_i)
\]
has expected quadratic variation at most $Cm_nH^2=O(nH)$.
Doob's $L^2$ inequality yields
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:clock}
 \max_{k\le m_n}|Z_k|/n\longrightarrow0
 \quad\hbox{in probability}.
\end{equation}
Also
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:mesh}
 \E\sum_{i<m_n,\,\mathrm{active}}(L_i/n)^2=O(H/n)=o(1).
\end{equation}
Thus $\max L_i/n\to0$ in probability. If neither time $nb$ nor
the strip boundary has been reached by the cap, accumulated conditional
mean duration exceeds $2n$ whereas actual elapsed time is at most $n$.
Equation \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:clock} excludes this with probability $1-o(1)$.

At each active step let
\[
 v_i=\frac{\E[\Delta_i\mid\text{past}]}{\E[L_i\mid\text{past}]}
     =\theta_n[f'(s_i/n)+\varepsilon_{n,i}],
 \qquad \sup_i|\varepsilon_{n,i}|\to0,
\]
by Lemma~\ref{ivd:sh:lem:drift}. The separate reward martingale is
\[
 M_k=\sum_{i<k}(\Delta_i-v_iL_i).
\]
Its conditional second moments are at most
$2\E\Delta_i^2+2v_i^2\E L_i^2\le CH\log H$,
since $\theta_n^2H^2=O(H\log H)$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:reward}
 \E M_{m_n}^2=O(n\log n),\qquad
 \max_{k\le m_n}|M_k|/H\longrightarrow0
 \quad\hbox{in probability},
\end{equation}
because $n\log n/H^2\to0$.
Up to the stop, elapsed time is at most $n+\max L_i=O(n)$ with
high probability. Therefore, uniformly at stopped boundary times,
\[
 p_k/H=p_0/H+\sum_{i<k}f'(s_i/n)L_i/n+o_{\Prob}(1).
\]
The difference between the sum and
$f(s_k/n)-f(s_0/n)$ is at most a fixed multiple of
$\sum_i(L_i/n)^2$, by the Lipschitz bound for $f'$.
This tends to zero by \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:mesh}.
The resulting tube convergence precludes strip exit because $f$ has
a positive strip margin. The cap was already excluded. Thus the first
stop is the crossing of $nb$, its overshoot is $o(n)$, and
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:tube} follows. Boundary-to-boundary linear interpolation
has the same limit because the rescaled time mesh tends to zero.

For each finite realized path the inverse change of measure is exact:
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:likelihood}
 \prod Q_{p_i}=\prod\asymplaw_{p_i,\lambda_{s_i}}\,e^{-\mathcal A_n},
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_n=\sum_i[\lambda_{s_i}\Delta_i+
                 \eta_{p_i}(\lambda_{s_i})L_i].
\end{equation}
It remains exact for stopped paths because stopping is a deterministic
function of their observed finite history. No individual duration has
been conditioned on a global exact-time event. On the strip,
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:scaledroot} gives
\begin{align}\label{ivd:sh:eq:likelihoodscaled}
 \frac{\mathcal A_n}{S}
  ={}&\sum_i q(s_i/n)\frac{p_{i+1}-p_i}{H}\\
    &+\sum_i\left[\frac1{3(p_i/H)}-\frac D2q(s_i/n)^2+o(1)\right]
                          \frac{L_i}{n}.\nonumber
\end{align}
Discrete summation by parts treats the first sum: the endpoint terms
converge, while its remaining sum converges to $-\int f\dd q$.
Replacing the random boundary path by $f$ incurs at most its uniform
error times the bounded total variation of $q$, plus endpoint errors.
No total-variation estimate on the random path is required. Its limit
is $\int qf'\dd t$. The second sum converges by the tube estimate,
the vanishing time mesh, and the $o(n)$ terminal overshoot to
$\int[1/(3f)-Dq^2/2]\dd t$. Consequently
\[
 \mathcal A_n/S\longrightarrow
  \int_a^b[f'^2/(2D)+1/(3f)]\dd t
\]
in probability. Restrict to a high-probability event with a prescribed
small action tolerance and integrate \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:likelihood} over it.
The logarithm of its probability is $o(S)$, proving
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:tubemass}. All constants used above are uniform for the
families stated in the theorem.
\end{proof}
This theorem ends at its actual random boundary time and height.
It does not assert an exact-time bridge or a zero endpoint. Those
requirements are supplied next by a completely discrete construction.

\section{Uniform entrance and exact integer time repair}\label{ivd:sh:sec:entrance}
\begin{lemma}\label{ivd:sh:lem:entrance}
There are constants $C,p_*$ such that for every integer $p\ge p_*$
there is a legal normalized path family from the root to $(p,0)$,
of a deterministic integer length $E(p)$, with
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:entrance}
 E(p)\le C p^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log p},\qquad
 \text{mass}\ge e^{-C\sqrt{p\log p}}.
\end{equation}
There are constants $C_0,C_1,p_*$ such that for every $p\ge p_*$
and every integer $N\ge C_0p^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log p}$, there is a legal
path family from $(p,0)$ to $(2,0)$ of length exactly $N$ and mass
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:repair}
 \text{mass}\ge
 \exp\{-C_1[\sqrt{p\log p}+N\log p/p]\}.
\end{equation}
Both constructions retain all elementary multiplicity and internal
bulk-path realizations of each selected macrocycle.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a sufficiently large integer $k_0$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:stairs}
 p_k=\lfloor k^2\log(k+1)\rfloor,\quad
 b_k=\lfloor p_k/4\rfloor,\quad
 \ell_k=\lfloor\alpha b_k+1/2\rfloor,\quad
 d_k=p_{k+1}-p_k.
\end{equation}
The mean-value theorem and floor errors bounded by one give
\[
 p_k\asymp b_k\asymp k^2\log k,\quad
 d_k=O(k\log k)>0,\quad d_k^2/b_k=O(\log k).
\]
The upward stage $(b_k,\ell_k,\ell_k+d_k)$ takes $p_k$ to
$p_{k+1}$; the downward stage $(b_k,\ell_k,\ell_k-d_k)$ takes
$p_{k+1}$ to $p_k$. For all $k\ge k_0$ both are supported and legal:
$\ell_k/p_k\to\alpha/4<1$, while $\ell_k-b_k$ is a positive
fixed fraction of $b_k$ and dominates $d_k$. In class 247,
$\ell_k$ lies strictly between $b_k$ and $2b_k+1$.
Lemma~\ref{ivd:sc:lem:local} gives each stage mass at least
$\exp(-C\log k)$ and length $O(p_k)$.
Thus ascent or descent between $p_{k_0}$ and $p_K$ has
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:staircost}
 \text{length }O(K^3\log K),\qquad
 \text{mass at least }e^{-CK\log K}.
\end{equation}

Let $K$ be largest with $p_K\le p$, and put $g=p-p_K$.
Then $0\le g<d_K=O(K\log K)$ and $p\asymp p_K$.
A bridge from $p_K$ to $p$ uses
$(b_K,\ell_K,\ell_K+g)$; the reverse bridge uses
$(b_K,\ell_K,\ell_K-g)$.
The same strict support margins prove legality for all large $K$,
and the deviations from $\alpha b_K$ are at most $g+1$.
Lemma~\ref{ivd:sc:lem:local} gives mass $e^{-C\log K}$ and duration
$O(p_K)$. This includes $g=0$, when the bridge is a return cycle.
Fixed ordinary access from the root to $p_{k_0}$, followed by ascent
and the upward bridge, proves \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:entrance}, since
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:Kscale}
 K\log K\asymp\sqrt{p\log p},\qquad
 K^3\log K\asymp p^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log p}.
\end{equation}

For repair, take the downward bridge to $p_K$, later descend to the
base, and finally use a fixed sequence of $b=0$ unit-drop boundary
edges to $(2,0)$. Such edges have positive weight in both classes
and are legal from every height at least three.
Let $F(p)$ be the length of these pieces without top returns. Then
\[
 F(p)\le CK^3\log K,\qquad
 \text{their mass}\ge e^{-CK\log K}.
\]
At $p_K$, before descending, allow the return cycles
$(b_K,\ell_K+s,\ell_K+s)$ for integers $|s|\le R$, where
\[
 L=\ell_K+1,\qquad R=\lfloor K\log(K+1)\rfloor.
\]
Their lengths are every integer in $[L-R,L+R]$. They are supported
and legal for large $K$, since $R/b_K=O(1/K)$, and each has mass
at least $e^{-C\log K}$ by Lemma~\ref{ivd:sc:lem:local}.

Every integer
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:fillingthreshold}
 U\ge (L^2-R^2)/(2R)=O(K^3\log K)
\end{equation}
is a sum of $m=\lceil U/(L+R)\rceil=O(U/p_K+1)$ such lengths.
Indeed $m(L-R)\le U\le m(L+R)$ follows from
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:fillingthreshold}. Starting with $m$ copies of $L-R$,
distribute the integer excess into increments from zero to $2R$.
This covers every residue; no divisibility condition remains.
Taking $C_0$ sufficiently large makes $U=N-F(p)$ satisfy
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:fillingthreshold}, uniformly in $p$. Fill precisely $U$
at $p_K$. The total time is exactly $N$ and its logarithmic cost
is at most
\[
 CK\log K+C(U/p_K+1)\log K
 \le C_1[\sqrt{p\log p}+N\log p/p],
\]
which proves \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:repair}.

For example, distribute the excess greedily to make the selected
macrocycle pattern a deterministic function of $(p,N)$.
Entrance likewise has a fixed pattern and exact length once $p$ is
chosen. A macrocycle weight $Q$ still counts every elementary edge
multiplicity and each of the $\binom{w-1}{b-1}$ bulk realizations.
Choosing a pattern does not choose only one such realization.
\end{proof}

\section{The sharp coefficient lower bound}\label{ivd:sh:sec:lower}
We now show
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:lowergoal}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{n\log\mu-\log a_j(n)}S\le\sigma_D.
\end{equation}
Take the minimizer $f_*$ in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:minimizer}. Fix a small
height $\varepsilon\in(0,M)$ and let $t_\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)$
satisfy $f_*(t_\varepsilon)=\varepsilon$.
Then $t_\varepsilon=O(\varepsilon^{3/2})$.
Choose a large constant $A$, independent of $\varepsilon,n$, as
specified below, and put
\[
 \delta=A\varepsilon^{3/2},\qquad b=1-\delta,\qquad
 P_n=\lfloor\varepsilon H\rfloor.
\]
Use Lemma~\ref{ivd:sh:lem:entrance} to enter $P_n$ at deterministic time
$E_n$, and write $a_n^*=E_n/n$ (not to be confused with the counting
sequence). For fixed $\varepsilon$ and large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:entrancescale}
 a_n^*\le C\varepsilon^{3/2},\qquad
 \text{entrance mass}\ge e^{-C\sqrt\varepsilon S}.
\end{equation}
Increase $A$ to ensure $a_n^*\le\delta/2$, and take
$\varepsilon$ small enough that $\delta<1/4$.
On $[a_n^*,b]$ define the reparameterized central path
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:centralpath}
 f_{n,\varepsilon}(t)
   =f_*(t_\varepsilon+c_{n,\varepsilon}(t-a_n^*)),\qquad
 c_{n,\varepsilon}=\frac{1-2t_\varepsilon}{b-a_n^*}.
\end{equation}
Both endpoint heights equal $\varepsilon$.
For each fixed $\varepsilon$, these functions form a bounded $C^2$
family on a common neighborhood with a positive strip margin.
Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:tube} applies, uniformly, to paths starting from
$P_n/H=\varepsilon+o(1)$ at time $E_n$ and stopping at their first
boundary time $s_N\ge bn$. With probability tending to one,
\[
 p_N/H=\varepsilon+o(1),\qquad s_N/n=b+o(1).
\]
On a high-probability subevent, for large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:endwindow}
 \tfrac12\varepsilon H\le p_N\le2\varepsilon H,\qquad
 \tfrac12\delta n\le n-s_N\le\delta n.
\end{equation}
The upper time bound follows from $s_N\ge bn$; the lower from the
$o(n)$ overshoot.

Increase $A$ once more so that every height in
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:endwindow} satisfies
\[
 \delta n/2\ge C_0p_N^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log p_N}
\]
for sufficiently large $n$. This is possible because
$H^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log H}\asymp n$, with constants uniform when a
fixed $\varepsilon$ is followed by $n\to\infty$.
The repair of Lemma~\ref{ivd:sh:lem:entrance} then has exactly the residual
time $n-s_N$, ends at $(2,0)$, and has logarithmic cost at most
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:repairscale}
 C[\sqrt\varepsilon S+(\delta/\varepsilon)S]
 \le C_A\sqrt\varepsilon S.
\end{equation}
The constant $A$ is fixed before $\varepsilon$ tends to zero.

For the central action, set
\[
 K_\varepsilon=\int_{t_\varepsilon}^{1-t_\varepsilon}
                  \frac{f_*'^2}{2D}\dd t,\qquad
 V_\varepsilon=\int_{t_\varepsilon}^{1-t_\varepsilon}
                  \frac1{3f_*}\dd t.
\]
The exact reparameterization formula is
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:rescaledaction}
 I_D(f_{n,\varepsilon};[a_n^*,b])
  =c_{n,\varepsilon}K_\varepsilon
     +c_{n,\varepsilon}^{-1}V_\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, uniformly for sufficiently large $n$
at fixed $\varepsilon$, $c_{n,\varepsilon}\to1$ and
$K_\varepsilon+V_\varepsilon\to\sigma_D$. Endpoint integrability
was proved in Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:variational}.
Thus \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:rescaledaction} is at most
$\sigma_D+o_\varepsilon(1)$ uniformly.

We check multiplicities before multiplying the three masses.
The entrance macrocycle pattern and its length $E_n$ are fixed,
while all elementary realizations counted by its weights are retained.
From any completed decorated path, the entrance cut is recovered at
the deterministic original time $E_n$. The interior endpoint is the
first subsequent boundary visit at or after $bn$.
Canonical boundary-cycle decomposition makes this rule unambiguous.
Thus distinct interior prefixes, entrance realizations, or elementary
multiplicity choices remain distinct full paths. The repair pattern
is deterministic in $(p_N,n-s_N)$ and still retains every internal
realization. Shared repair suffixes never identify different prefixes.
The completed-history injectivity proved earlier then ensures that
these are distinct enumerated objects, rather than only labels.

Multiplying \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:entrancescale}, the mass in
Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:tube} with \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:rescaledaction}, and the worst
repair mass \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:repairscale} gives total normalized mass at
exact original time $n$ and endpoint $(2,0)$ at least
\[
 \exp\{-S[\sigma_D+o_\varepsilon(1)+C_A\sqrt\varepsilon+o_n(1)]\}.
\]
This endpoint is valid for both classes. Its $h$-weight is the fixed
$x_j^2$, so \eqref{ivd:sc:eq:telescope} yields
\[
 a_j(n)\ge\mu_j^n x_j^{-2}
  \exp\{-S[\sigma_D+o_\varepsilon(1)+C_A\sqrt\varepsilon+o_n(1)]\}.
\]
First let $n\to\infty$ at fixed $\varepsilon$, then
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This proves \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:lowergoal}.
\section{Uniform subcritical rows and a global spatial tail}\label{ivd:sh:sec:tails}
For the upper coefficient bound, write
\[
 k=S/n=\log n/H,\qquad\sqrt k=S/H=\theta_n.
\]
The local-root theorem is not needed in the subcritical argument below;
the exact transforms and concentration estimates suffice.

\begin{lemma}\label{ivd:sh:lem:subcritical}
Fix bounds on real $q,h$, and fix $R,e>0$. For integer
$0\le p\le RH$, write $x=p/H$ when $p>0$. If
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:subcriticalcondition}
 h+Dq^2/2\le1/(3x)-e,
\end{equation}
then uniformly over these choices,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:subcriticalrow}
 T_p(\sqrt k\,q,kh)\le r+o(1).
\end{equation}
At $p=0$ the conclusion holds without \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:subcriticalcondition}.
The fixed bounds may depend on $R$ and on a chosen smooth function,
but never on $n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\lambda=\sqrt k\,q$, $\eta=kh$, and $\beta=\log R(\lambda,\eta)$.
Analyticity gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:subcriticalbeta}
 \beta=\alpha k(h+Dq^2/2)+O(k^{3/2}),\qquad \beta\le Ck.
\end{equation}
Also $A=A_0+o(1)$ and $ae^{\lambda+\eta}=a+o(1)$ uniformly.
If $\beta\le0$, the sum in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:legalrow} is at most two.
Otherwise use, for $m\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:catalanexcess}
 \sum_{b=0}^m b d_b\le C\sqrt{m+1},\qquad
 \sum_{b=0}^m d_b(e^{\beta b}-1)
       \le C\beta\sqrt{m+1}e^{\beta m}.
\end{equation}
Choose a sufficiently small fixed $l>0$. For $p\le lH$, discard
$F$ and use $b\le p$. The excess over two is at most
\[
 Ck\sqrt H\,e^{CklH}
 =O(n^{-1/3+Cl}(\log n)^{5/6})=o(1)
\]
if $Cl<1/3$. This case needs no condition involving $1/x$.
At $p=0$ there is only the ordinary edge.

Now let $lH\le p\le RH$ and set $z_p=\sqrt p\log p$.
The mean-cutoff shift $O(p|\lambda|+1)=o(z_p)$ uniformly.
By \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:droptail}--\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:dropmean}, for
$b\ge p/\alpha+z_p$ the CDF is at most $Ce^{-c(\log p)^2}$.
Thus the legal Catalan sum is bounded by the untruncated sum through
$m=\lceil p/\alpha+z_p\rceil$ plus $o(1)$. This tail error is
$o(1)$ because the full sum through $p$ is at most
$2e^{Ckp}=n^{O(1)}$. An endpoint $m>p-1$, if it occurs, only enlarges
the upper bound.
Using \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:subcriticalcondition}--\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:subcriticalbeta},
$kp=x\log n$, $pk^{3/2}=o(1)$, and $kz_p=o(1)$, we obtain
\[
 \beta m\le(1/3-ex)\log n+o(1)
             \le(1/3-el)\log n+o(1).
\]
Equation \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:catalanexcess} now bounds the excess by
\[
 Ck\sqrt H\,n^{1/3-el}e^{o(1)}
 =O((\log n)^{5/6}n^{-el})=o(1).
\]
Hence the legal sum is at most $2+o(1)$ throughout the strip.
This proves \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:subcriticalrow}.
\end{proof}
The coefficient $1/3$ in this lemma uses both the true cutoff
$p/\alpha$ and the factor $H^{-1/2}$ in the Catalan tail.

\begin{lemma}\label{ivd:sh:lem:spatialtail}
There are fixed $\kappa>0,C<\infty$ such that the total $q$-mass
of all first-hit boundary prefixes reaching height at least $P$ is at
most
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:spatialtail}
 C\exp\{-\kappa\sqrt{(P+2)\log(P+2)}\}.
\end{equation}
No restriction on their original duration is imposed. The same bound
holds for the normalized coefficient contribution of length-$n$ paths
whose boundary heights reach $P$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For real $p\ge0$, put
\[
 G(p)=\sqrt{(p+2)\log(p+2)},\qquad
 V(p)=e^{\kappa(G(p)-G(0))}.
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
 G'(p)=\frac{\log(p+2)+1}{2\sqrt{(p+2)\log(p+2)}},\qquad
 G''(p)=-\frac{(\log(p+2))^2+1}
 {4(p+2)^{3/2}(\log(p+2))^{3/2}}<0.
\]
Thus $G$ is increasing and concave. With
$\lambda_p=\kappa G'(p)$,
\[
 0\le\lambda_p\le C\kappa,\qquad
 \lambda_p^2\le C\kappa^2\frac{\log(p+2)}{p+2},\qquad
 V(p+\Delta)/V(p)\le e^{\lambda_p\Delta}
\]
for actual legal endpoints. Extend the drop sum in this exponential
majorant, retaining $b\le p-1$. For the legal renewal kernel $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:barrierrow}
 \frac{KV(p)}{V(p)}\le ae^{\lambda_p}+A(\lambda_p,0)
 \sum_{b=0}^{p-1}d_b
 \exp\left\{C\kappa^2b\frac{\log(p+2)}{p+2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Here $\log R(\lambda,0)\le C\lambda^2$ near zero.
For every $m\ge0$ and $0\le\gamma\le1/4$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:barriercatalan}
 \sum_{b=0}^m d_b e^{\gamma b\log(m+2)/(m+2)}\le2+C\gamma.
\end{equation}
Indeed \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:catalanexcess} bounds the excess by
$C\gamma\log(m+2)(m+2)^{-1/2+\gamma}$, uniformly $O(\gamma)$.
In \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:barrierrow} increase the cutoff to $m=p$ and choose
$\gamma=C\kappa^2$ small enough. Analyticity of $A$ then gives
$KV/V\le r+O(\kappa)$ uniformly. At $p=0$ only the ordinary edge
occurs and satisfies the same bound. Fix sufficiently small $\kappa$
so that for some $\rho<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:barriersuper}
 KV\le\rho V\le V.
\end{equation}
After adjoining a cemetery state with value zero, this is a nonnegative
supermartingale. Sum over its first-hit stopping line, or first use
bounded optional stopping and pass by monotone convergence.
Because $V$ is increasing, overshoots only strengthen the bound.
The total first-hit prefix mass is at most $V(0)/V(P)$, proving
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:spatialtail}.

For a first-hit prefix of original length $s\le n$, original-time
substochasticity makes the total $q$-mass of all suffixes of exactly
$n-s$ steps at most one. The true terminal multiplier $x_j^{-p}$
is at most one too. First-hit prefixes partition the relevant paths.
Summing their bounds proves the coefficient assertion. Prefixes whose
first hit is after $n$ may be included harmlessly in the upper bound.
\end{proof}
In particular, for each fixed $R>0$, the contribution of paths whose
boundary heights exceed $RH$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:Rtail}
 \exp\{-(\kappa\sqrt{2R/3}+o(1))S\},
\end{equation}
since $\sqrt{RH\log(RH)}/S\to\sqrt{2R/3}$.

\end{writenote}

\section{A positive space time supersolution}\label{ivd:sh:sec:upper}
Fix $u,v\in C^2([0,1])$ with $v(0)=0$. Suppose that for some $e>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:HJ}
 u'(\tau)x+v'(\tau)+Du(\tau)^2/2
       \le1/(3x)-e
 \quad(0\le\tau\le1,\ x>0).
\end{equation}
Fix $R>0$ and define on integer original times and boundary heights
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:Phi}
 \Phi_n(t,p)=S[u(t/n)p/H+v(t/n)],\qquad
 0\le t\le n,\quad0\le p\le RH.
\end{equation}
Keep only legal complete cycles with $t+L\le n$ and
$0\le p+\Delta\le RH$, and multiply their weights by
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:Phitransform}
 \exp\{\Phi_n(t+L,p+\Delta)-\Phi_n(t,p)\}.
\end{equation}
We claim that every row of this finite space--time kernel is at most a
fixed $\rho<1$ for sufficiently large $n$.

Put $\delta_n=n^{-1/12}$. Call a cycle good if
$L\le\delta_n n$ and $|\Delta|\le\delta_n H$.
For admitted good cycles Taylor's theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:taylorspacetime}
 \Phi_n(t+L,p+\Delta)-\Phi_n(t,p)
 \le\lambda_t\Delta+\eta_{t,p}L+C_R\delta_n(S/n)L,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \lambda_t=(S/H)u(t/n),\qquad
 \eta_{t,p}=(S/n)[u'(t/n)p/H+v'(t/n)].
\]
For clarity, write $\tau=t/n$, $z=p/H$, $l=L/n$, $d=\Delta/H$.
After division by $S$, the exact remainder is
\begin{align*}
 &[u(\tau+l)-u(\tau)]d\;+
 z[u(\tau+l)-u(\tau)-u'(\tau)l]\\
 &\hspace{38mm}+[v(\tau+l)-v(\tau)-v'(\tau)l].
\end{align*}
Its absolute value is at most $Cl|d|+C_Rl^2$, proving
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:taylorspacetime}. In particular the mixed space--time term
has not been dropped. Every Taylor argument lies in $[0,1]$ because
crossing cycles were excluded.
By \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:HJ}, the effective time tilt on the right of
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:taylorspacetime} satisfies Lemma~\ref{ivd:sh:lem:subcritical}
with $q=u(t/n)$ and slack at least $e/2$ eventually. The required
bounds on $q,h$ are fixed on $p/H\le R$. Summing good cycles is
therefore at most $r+o(1)$, uniformly in $t,p$.

For bad cycles, $|\Phi_n|\le C_RS$ everywhere in the confined region.
Discard their legal drop restriction but retain $b\le p-1\le RH$.
Equations \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:deltatail}--\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:durationtail}, followed
by $\sum d_b\le2$, bound their transformed row mass by
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:badcycles}
 Ce^{C_RS}\left\{
  e^{-c\min(\delta_n^2H^2/(RH+1),\,\delta_n H)}
   +e^{-c\delta_n n+C(RH+1)}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The ordinary edge is good for large $n$. The bound in
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:badcycles} is $\exp\{-\omega(S)\}$ because
\[
 \delta_n^2H/S\to\infty,\qquad
 \delta_n n/H\to\infty,\qquad\delta_n n/S\to\infty.
\]
A union bound on the two bad events suffices; independence between
$L$ and $\Delta$ is neither asserted nor needed. Combining good and
bad cycles proves the claimed row bound, for example eventually with
$\rho=(1+r)/2$. Rows at $t=n$ are zero.

\subsection{Extraction at exact original time}
Starting at $(0,0)$ in space--time, total transformed mass of all
finite paths is at most
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:geometricmass}
 \sum_{m\ge0}\rho^m=(1-\rho)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Every original length-$n$ path ending at $c=0$ and with all boundary
heights at most $RH$ occurs at exactly $t=n$ in this kernel.
Its canonical complete cycles retain every original integer duration.
A cycle with $t+L>n$ cannot be present in such a boundary-terminal
path, so deleting it is exact, not a clock approximation.
The factors \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:Phitransform} telescope and $\Phi_n(0,0)=0$.
The genuine coefficient terminal multiplier gives
\begin{align}\label{ivd:sh:eq:endpointfactor}
 e^{-\Phi_n(n,p)}x_j^{-p}
  &=e^{-Sv(1)}\exp\{-p[\log x_j+(S/H)u(1)]\}\\
  &\le e^{-Sv(1)}\nonumber
\end{align}
for all $p\ge0$ and sufficiently large $n$, since $u(1)$ is fixed
and $S/H\to0$. For class 247, keeping its actual terminal set or
enlarging it to all boundary heights gives the same upper bound.
Hence the confined contribution to $a_j(n)/\mu_j^n$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:confined}
 (1-\rho)^{-1}e^{-Sv(1)}.
\end{equation}
All low heights, including the root, were covered by the subcritical
lemma; no unproved entrance approximation is needed for this direction.

\subsection{Smooth dual functions approaching the sharp constant}
For the minimizer of \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:minimizer}, equations
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:minintegrals} imply
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:dualvalue}
 \int_0^1\left[\frac2{3f_*}-\frac{f_*'^2}{2D}\right]\dd t
 =1/M=\sigma_D.
\end{equation}
Fix $0<\zeta<1/2$, set $r_\zeta=1-2\zeta$, and put
\[
 u_\zeta(t)=f_*'(\zeta+r_\zeta t)/D.
\]
This is smooth with bounded derivatives on $[0,1]$, and
$u_\zeta'(t)=-r_\zeta/[3f_*(\zeta+r_\zeta t)^2]<0$.
For a further fixed $e>0$, define $v(0)=0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:vdual}
 v'(t)=2\sqrt{-u_\zeta'(t)/3}-Du_\zeta(t)^2/2-e.
\end{equation}
The negative derivative is bounded away from zero on this compact
interval, so $v$ has all the regularity used above. For all $x>0$,
\[
 u_\zeta'(t)x+2\sqrt{-u_\zeta'(t)/3}\le1/(3x),
\]
by expanding
$(\sqrt{(-u_\zeta')x}-\sqrt{1/(3x)})^2\ge0$.
Thus \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:HJ} holds with slack $e$.
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:dualapprox}
 v(1)=\frac1{r_\zeta}
 \int_\zeta^{1-\zeta}
 \left[\frac{2\sqrt{r_\zeta}}{3f_*(s)}
                  -\frac{f_*'(s)^2}{2D}\right]\dd s-e
 \longrightarrow\sigma_D-e
\end{equation}
as $\zeta\downarrow0$, by endpoint integrability and
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:dualvalue}. Choosing first small $\zeta$ and then small
$e$ therefore produces fixed, bounded smooth functions with $v(1)$
exceeding any prescribed number below $\sigma_D$.
We never insert the singular limiting derivative itself in a kernel.

\subsection{Completion of the upper coefficient bound}
Given $\varepsilon>0$, fix such $u,v$ with
$v(1)>\sigma_D-\varepsilon/2$. Then choose $R$ so large that
$\kappa\sqrt{2R/3}>\sigma_D+1$.
These choices are fixed before $n$ tends to infinity.
Split paths according to whether their boundary heights stay at most
$RH$. Equations \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:Rtail} and \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:confined} give
\[
 \frac{a_j(n)}{\mu_j^n}\le
 (1-\rho)^{-1}e^{-Sv(1)}
  +\exp\{-(\kappa\sqrt{2R/3}+o(1))S\}.
\]
The fixed prefactor disappears on the $S$ scale. Thus eventually
$a_j(n)\le\mu_j^n e^{-(\sigma_D-\varepsilon)S}$, proving
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:uppergoal}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n\log\mu-\log a_j(n)}S\ge\sigma_D.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:lowergoal} and \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:uppergoal} proves
Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:main}. Original-time substochasticity already gave
the all-$n$ bound $a_j(n)\le\mu_j^n$, and $S_n=o(n)$ gives the
exponential growth constants.
\section{Precise inverse thresholds and derivative growth}\label{ivd:sh:sec:inverse}
Write $\lambda=\log\mu>0$ and $\psi(t)=t^{1/3}(\log t)^{2/3}$.
In this section $\sigma=\sigma_D$ and $a(n)$ denotes either sequence.

\begin{corollary}\label{ivd:sh:cor:inverse}
For real $T\to\infty$, let $N(T)=\min\{n:a(n)\ge T\}$ and
$s=(\log T)/\lambda$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:inverse}
 N(T)=s+\frac{\sigma}{\lambda}\psi(s)+o(\psi(s)).
\end{equation}
Equivalently the correction coefficient multiplying
$(\log T)^{1/3}(\log\log T)^{2/3}$ is
$\sigma/\lambda^{4/3}$. The correction is positive.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$ and constants
$0<d<(\sigma-\varepsilon)/\lambda$ and
$D_+>(\sigma+\varepsilon)/\lambda$.
The global upper bound $a(n)\le\mu^n$ excludes every integer $n<s$.
For $n\ge s$ and sufficiently large $T$, the sharp coefficient upper
bound and the increasing function $\psi$ give
\[
 \log a(n)\le\lambda n-(\sigma-\varepsilon)\psi(n)
 <\lambda s\quad\hbox{if }n<s+d\psi(s).
\]
At $n=\lceil s+D_+\psi(s)\rceil$, regular variation gives
$\psi(n)/\psi(s)\to1$, and the coefficient lower bound implies
$\log a(n)\ge\lambda s$ eventually. This proves the corresponding
two bounds on $N(T)$ without coefficient monotonicity or a ratio
limit. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and let $d,D_+$ approach
$\sigma/\lambda$. Integer ceilings contribute only
$O(1)=o(\psi(s))$. Finally
$\psi(s)\sim\lambda^{-1/3}(\log T)^{1/3}(\log\log T)^{2/3}$.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
This refines Part~\ref{ivd:sc:part}, Corollary~\ref{ivd:sc:cor:inverse}, and is refined by Part~\ref{ivd:ll:part}, Corollary~\ref{ivd:ll:cor:inverse}, which adds $\frac{7\sigma}{3\lambda}\psi(s)\log\log s/\log s$ and replaces $o(\psi(s))$ by $O(\psi(s)/\log s)$.
\end{writenote}

Let
\[
 b_n=a(n)\mu^{-n},\qquad F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a(n)z^n,
 \qquad\rho=1/\mu,\qquad f(t)=F(\rho e^{it}).
\]
The radius of convergence is $\rho$ by Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:main}.
For every $k\ge0$, $\sum n^k b_n<\infty$. Termwise differentiation
therefore gives continuous boundary derivatives of all orders,
uniformly in $t$. Since all $b_n\ge0$, their norm is attained at
$t=0$:
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:momentnorm}
 \|f^{(k)}\|_\infty=M_k:=\sum_{n\ge0}n^k b_n\quad(k\ge1).
\end{equation}
Radial derivatives at $\rho$ are also finite, with
$F^{(k)}(\rho)=\mu^k\sum_{n\ge k}(n)_k b_n$.

\begin{corollary}\label{ivd:sh:cor:derivatives}
As integer $k\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \log M_k
  &=3k\log k-2k\log\log k
      +[\log(2D/\pi^2)-3]k+o(k),\label{ivd:sh:eq:boundaryderivatives}\\
 \log F^{(k)}(\rho)
  &=3k\log k-2k\log\log k
      +[\log\mu+\log(2D/\pi^2)-3]k+o(k).
 \label{ivd:sh:eq:radialderivatives}
\end{align}
These are derivative-growth statements, not further terms in the
coefficient asymptotics.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $c>0$, consider the continuous objective
$k\log x-c\psi(x)$ for $x\ge2$.
Its maximum occurs at $x\to\infty$, and the derivative equation is
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:saddle}
 c\psi(x)(1+2/\log x)=3k.
\end{equation}
The left side is increasing eventually. Taking logarithms in
\eqref{ivd:sh:eq:saddle} first gives $\log x\sim3\log k$, and then
\[
 x=(3/c^3+o(1))\frac{k^3}{(\log k)^2},\qquad
 c\psi(x)=3k+o(k).
\]
Substitution yields
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:sh:eq:saddlesup}
 \log\sup_{n\ge2}n^k e^{-c\psi(n)}
 =3k\log k-2k\log\log k+[\log(3/c^3)-3]k+o(k).
\end{equation}
The same expression holds for integer $n$: rounding the continuous
maximizer changes its objective by $o(k)$.

For each sufficiently small fixed $\varepsilon>0$, eventually
\[
 e^{-(\sigma+\varepsilon)\psi(n)}\le b_n
      \le e^{-(\sigma-\varepsilon)\psi(n)}.
\]
A single saddle integer with $c=\sigma+\varepsilon$ supplies the
lower bound on $M_k$. For the upper bound reserve a summable factor
$e^{-\varepsilon\psi(n)}$, obtaining a constant times the supremum
in \eqref{ivd:sh:eq:saddlesup} with $c=\sigma-2\varepsilon$.
The finitely many earlier coefficients contribute $e^{O(k)}$.
Their addition changes the logarithm of the rapidly growing saddle
bound by $o(1)$, so in particular it does not alter its linear term.
Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This proves
\[
 \log M_k=3k\log k-2k\log\log k+[\log(3/\sigma^3)-3]k+o(k).
\]
For the radial upper bound, $(n)_k\le n^k$. For the lower bound
use the same saddle integer $n\asymp k^3/(\log k)^2$, for which
\[
 \log\frac{(n)_k}{n^k}
  =\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\log(1-j/n)=O(k^2/n)=o(k).
\]
Hence the moment linear term is unchanged, while the external factor
$\mu^k$ adds $k\log\mu$. Finally $3/\sigma_D^3=2D/\pi^2$.
No differentiation of the coefficient's $o(S_n)$ remainder is used.
\end{proof}
Under the convention
$\|f^{(k)}\|_\infty\le C A^k(k!)^s$, these estimates in particular
show that the boundary function has sharp Gevrey order $3$:
the bound holds for $s=3$ and fails for every $s<3$.
Indeed $\log(k!)=k\log k-k+O(\log k)$, so the leading coefficient
$3$ dominates every smaller choice of $s$ and the negative
$2k\log\log k$ permits $s=3$.

Corollary~\ref{ivd:sh:cor:derivatives} identifies the $O(k)$ of Part~\ref{ivd:sc:part}, Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:analytic}; Part~\ref{ivd:ll:part}, Corollary~\ref{ivd:ll:cor:moments}, adds the term $-k\log\log k/\log k$ with error $O(k/\log k)$. The sharp Gevrey order~$3$ was already proved in Part~\ref{ivd:sc:part}, which also proves the genuine singularity at $\rho$ and non-D-finiteness; this Part does not restate them.

\section{What is established and what remains open}\label{ivd:sh:sec:open}
The sharp constant closes the leading logarithmic problem:
\[
 \frac{n\log\mu_j-\log a_j(n)}{n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}}
 \longrightarrow\left(\frac{3\pi^2}{2D_j}\right)^{1/3}.
\]
The proof retains the full legal cycle mixture, correlated random
duration, exact original time, and true terminal extraction.
The lower argument controls the clock and reward separately, and its
repair works at every sufficiently large residual integer duration.
The upper argument controls all small heights, long cycles, large
jumps, and escaping boundary heights before exact coefficient
extraction. The singular minimizing profile is approached only through
fixed smooth or positive-strip approximants.

Several stronger conclusions are not supplied:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An asymptotic equivalent for $a_j(n)$, including its exact
multiplicative constant, any polynomial factor, and all lower-order
exponent terms, remains open here. A subleading logarithm can still
produce an unbounded multiplicative error.
\item The local $\log\log p$ term does not by itself give a global
next-order coefficient term. Accumulating it through a varying kernel
and both endpoint layers requires additional estimates.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Answered by Part~\ref{ivd:ll:part}, Theorem~\ref{ivd:ll:thm:main}: the global next term is $\frac{7\sigma_D}3S_n\log\log n/\log n$, obtained by accumulating the local terms through the varying kernel with integrable height errors and treating both endpoint layers (Sections~\ref{ivd:ll:sec:potential}--\ref{ivd:ll:sec:lower}). Item~2 is kept as the scope statement of this Part.
\end{writenote}
\item A limit or expansion for $a_j(n+1)/a_j(n)$ is not proved by
this report. The unspecified logarithmic remainder cannot simply be
differenced to infer a ratio theorem.
\item No quantitative coefficient remainder, effective onset, usable
finite-$n$ calibration, all-orders expansion, or transseries is asserted.
The theorem supplies neither a practical numerical crossover nor a
new fit to finite data.
\item Smooth boundary values and their derivative growth do not identify
a unique dominant singularity or an exterior analytic continuation
region. No exterior $\Delta$-domain expansion is claimed.
\end{enumerate}
The inverse and derivative corollaries follow from the sharp leading
logarithm using comparison and optimization. Their extra precision
should not be mistaken for a stronger coefficient equivalent.

\section{The theorem and the reader bundle}\label{ivd:ll:sec:theorem}
An inversion sequence of length $n$ is a sequence $e=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$
of integers with $0\le e_i<i$. Write $a_{247}(n)$ for the number with
no $i<j<k$ satisfying $e_i\le e_j$ and $e_i\ge e_k$, and $a_{759}(n)$
for the number with no such triple also satisfying $e_j\ne e_k$.
Their forbidden pattern sets are $(000,010,110,120)$ and
$(010,110,120)$, respectively. In the usual indexing,
\[
 a_{247}(n)=\mathrm{A279551}(n),\qquad
 a_{759}(n)=\mathrm{A279556}(n),\qquad a_j(0)=1.
\]
All logarithms are natural. The diffusion parameter $D$ and the
critical exponential growth constant $\mu$ are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 j&\mu&D&\sigma_D=(3\pi^2/(2D))^{1/3}\\\hline
 759&9&1&(3\pi^2/2)^{1/3}\\
 247&8&1/2&(3\pi^2)^{1/3}
\end{array}
\]
Set, for sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:scales}
 \begin{gathered}
 N=\log n,\quad J=\log\log n,\quad
 H=n^{2/3}N^{1/3},\quad S=n^{1/3}N^{2/3},\\
 \theta=H/n=S/H,\qquad k_0=S/n=N/H.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Thus $H^2=nS$, $HS=nN$, and $\theta^2=k_0$.
The symbol $P(n)$ below always denotes a fixed finite power of
$\log n$, with its exponent allowed to increase between estimates.
It never hides a positive power of $n$.

\begin{theorem}\label{ivd:ll:thm:main}
For either class, as $n$ tends to infinity through all integers,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:main}
 n\log\mu-\log a_j(n)
 =\sigma_D S+\frac{7\sigma_D}{3}\frac{SJ}{N}
                  +O\!\left(\frac SN\right).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, there are constants $C,n_0$ such that for all $n\ge n_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:envelopes}
 e^{-G(n)-C R(n)}\le a_j(n)\mu^{-n}\le e^{-G(n)+C R(n)},
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \psi(t)=t^{1/3}(\log t)^{2/3},\quad
 R(t)=\frac{\psi(t)}{\log t},\quad
 G(t)=\sigma_D\psi(t)+\frac{7\sigma_D}{3}
                  \frac{\psi(t)\log\log t}{\log t}.
\]
The constants are not asserted to be effective or numerically calibrated.
\end{theorem}
The coefficient $7/3$ comes from adding the $J/6$ contributed by
$\tfrac12\log H$ to the $J$ contributed by $\log\log H$, and
then evaluating the resulting $7J/(6N)$ potential correction along
the singular minimizing curve. Retaining an integrable height error
is necessary to distinguish this coefficient from the remainder.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Since $SJ/N=o(S)$, this theorem implies Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}'s Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:main} and Part~\ref{ivd:sc:part}'s Theorem~\ref{ivd:sc:thm:main}, and it answers item~2 of Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}'s Section~\ref{ivd:sh:sec:open}. The coefficient of $S/N$ is not determined (Section~\ref{ivd:ll:sec:ledger}); it is question~1 of Section~\ref{ivd:ll:sec:further}.
\end{writenote}

\section{Exact model and earlier proved inputs}\label{ivd:ll:sec:inputs}
\subsection{Counting and normalization}
The two auxiliary label trees start at $(0,0)$ and have edges
\begin{align*}
 (p,c)&\longrightarrow(p+1,c),\\
 (p,c)&\longrightarrow(p+1,c-1)\quad(c>0),\\
 (p,0)&\longrightarrow(p-\ell,b)\quad(0\le\ell\le p-1).
\end{align*}
The last edge has multiplicity
\[
 M_{759}(\ell,b)=C_b\binom\ell b,\qquad
 M_{247}(\ell,b)=C_b\binom{b+1}{\ell-b},\qquad
 C_b=\frac1{b+1}\binom{2b}b.
\]
Out-of-range binomial coefficients are zero. The exact terminal sets are
\[
 \mathcal T_{759}=\{(p,0):p\ge0\},\qquad
 \mathcal T_{247}=\{(p,0):0\le p\le2\}.
\]
Multiplicity-weighted paths ending in these sets count the respective
sequences exactly. In particular the extra auxiliary nodes are not
all valid terminal objects. The completed decorated-history
injectivity proof is in Report 127, Section 2.

Use $(\mu,x_j,y_j)=(9,3,1/2)$ for 759 and $(8,2,1/3)$ for 247,
and normalize an edge of multiplicity $m$ by
\[
 q(s,t)=\frac{m h(t)}{\mu h(s)},\qquad h(p,c)=x_j^p y_j^c.
\]
Every length-$n$ path of multiplicity $m$ has normalized mass
$m\mu^{-n}h(\text{endpoint})$. Thus at a valid terminal its
coefficient multiplier is $x_j^{-p}$. These elementary rows are
substochastic, so the mass at every exact original integer time is
at most one, and $a_j(n)\le\mu^n$ for every $n$.

At a boundary $(p,0)$, a completed commitment with $b\ge1$ has drop
$\ell\le p-1$, followed by $w\ge b$ bulk steps, with its last
fulfillment at the final bulk step. Its total mass, duration, and
displacement are
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:cycle}
 Q_j(b,\ell,w)=\mu^{-w-1}x_j^{w-\ell}M_j(\ell,b)
                     \binom{w-1}{b-1},\qquad
 L=w+1,\quad\Delta=w-\ell.
\end{equation}
For $b=0$ use $L=1$, $\Delta=-\ell$, and mass
$\mu^{-1}x_j^{-\ell}M_j(\ell,0)$. The ordinary edge has
$L=\Delta=1$ and mass $a=x_j/\mu$. Every boundary path decomposes
uniquely into these cycles, including the duration-one edges. A
``cycle'' here need not return to the same height.

\subsection{Exact transforms and fixed batch laws}
Put $d_b=C_b/4^b$. Summing unrestricted drops at a fixed $b$ gives
$A_j(\lambda,\eta)d_bR_j(\lambda,\eta)^b$, where
\begin{align}
 A_{759}&=\frac{e^\eta}{9(1-e^{-\lambda}/3)},&
 R_{759}&=\frac{4e^\eta}{9(1-e^{-\lambda}/3)(1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/3)},
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:transform759}\\
 A_{247}&=\frac{e^\eta(1+e^{-\lambda}/2)}8,&
 R_{247}&=\frac{e^\eta(1+e^{-\lambda}/2)}{2(1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/4)}.
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:transform247}
\end{align}
These follow from geometric and binomial summations in
Report 127, Section 5. They are analytic on a fixed neighborhood of
$(0,0)$, independently of $b$; no unrestricted infinite positive-tilt
sum over $b$ is being asserted.

At a fixed $b$ under the exponential tilt, the drop laws are
\begin{align}
 759:\quad&\ell=b+\NB^{\mathrm{fail}}(b+1,1-e^{-\lambda}/3),\\
 247:\quad&\ell=b+\Bin(b+1,e^{-\lambda}/(2+e^{-\lambda})),
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:drop}
\end{align}
where the negative binomial counts failures before the stated number
of successes. Independently, for $b\ge1$, $w$ is a negative-binomial
trials variable with $b$ successes and success probability
$1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/3$ or $1-e^{\lambda+\eta}/4$, respectively.
At $b=0$ duration is one. The legal cutoff conditions only the drop,
so fixed-$b$ independence is preserved; $\Delta$ and $L$ themselves
are not independent.

Let $F_{b,\lambda}$ be the appropriate drop CDF. The exact legal row is
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:row}
 T_p(\lambda,\eta)=a e^{\lambda+\eta}
  +A_j(\lambda,\eta)\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}
    d_bR_j(\lambda,\eta)^b F_{b,\lambda}(p-1).
\end{equation}
The sum is empty at $p=0$. The constants are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 j&a&A_0&\alpha&D&r=a+2A_0\\\hline
 759&1/3&1/6&3/2&1&2/3\\
 247&1/4&3/16&4/3&1/2&5/8
\end{array}
\]
In both cases $1-r=2A_0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:logR}
 \log R(\lambda,\eta)=\alpha\eta+\frac{\alpha D}{2}\lambda^2
           +O(|\lambda|^3+|\lambda\eta|+\eta^2).
\end{equation}
For $b\le p$, the tilted drop mean is
$\alpha b+O(p|\lambda|+1)$. Uniformly for $\lambda$ near zero,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:drop-tail}
 \Prob(|\ell-\E\ell|\ge u)
 \le C\exp\{-c\min(u^2/(b+1),u)\}.
\end{equation}
This follows because the centered log moment generating functions of
the geometric or Bernoulli summands are bounded by $C\vartheta^2$
on a fixed interval. At zero tilt, the fixed-$b$ normalized laws also obey
\begin{align}
 \Prob(|\Delta|>z)&\le C e^{-c\min(z^2/(b+1),z)},\label{ivd:ll:eq:jump-tail}\\
 \Prob(L>u)&\le C e^{-cu+C(b+1)}.\label{ivd:ll:eq:clock-tail}
\end{align}
A Chernoff bound applied to \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:logR} proves the first;
a fixed positive duration tilt proves the second. Both include $b=0$.

\subsection{Uniform endpoint and spatial estimates}
We state the discrete inputs with their uniformity explicitly.
Their proofs, including integer-time filling, are in Report 127,
Sections 4, 10, and 12.

\begin{proposition}[Earlier proved inputs]\label{ivd:ll:prop:inputs}
There are fixed positive constants with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every sufficiently large integer $p$ there is a legal
normalized path family from the root to $(p,0)$, of deterministic
integer length $E(p)$, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:entrance-input}
 E(p)\le C p^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log p},\qquad
 \text{mass}\ge e^{-C\sqrt{p\log p}}.
\end{equation}
\item For every sufficiently large integer $p$ and every integer
$m\ge C_0p^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log p}$ there is a legal normalized family
from $(p,0)$ to $(2,0)$ of length exactly $m$, with
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:repair-input}
 \text{mass}\ge
 \exp\{-C_1[\sqrt{p\log p}+m\log p/p]\}.
\end{equation}
All elementary multiplicity and bulk-path realizations of its selected
macrocycles are retained.
\item The total normalized mass of all first-hit boundary prefixes
reaching height at least $P$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:barrier-input}
 C e^{-\kappa\sqrt{(P+2)\log(P+2)}}.
\end{equation}
No restriction on their original duration is needed. The same estimate
bounds the normalized coefficient contribution of length-$n$ paths
whose boundary heights reach $P$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
For precision about these references, the entrance and repair proof
uses staircase heights $p_k=\lfloor k^2\log(k+1)\rfloor$ and legal
moderate-deviation cycles of mass at least $e^{-C\log k}$.
Their accumulated time is $O(K^3\log K)$ and logarithmic cost
$O(K\log K)$. Return-cycle blocks and bounded terminal edges fill
every remaining integer duration. Thus the constants do not depend
on a fixed positive lower bound for $p/H$.
The spatial proof uses the increasing concave function
$\sqrt{(p+2)\log(p+2)}$ and a sufficiently small exponential multiple
as a positive supermartingale. First-hit prefix extraction followed
by original-time substochasticity gives the coefficient assertion.
These proof summaries clarify the hypotheses; their full arguments
remain in the supplied unchanged companion.

\section{A quantitative local critical root}\label{ivd:ll:sec:root}
For sufficiently large $p$ and the tilt ranges below, write
$\eta_p(\lambda)$ for the unique solution of
$T_p(\lambda,\eta_p(\lambda))=1$ and $\kappa_p=\eta_p(0)$.
The row is continuous and strictly increasing in $\eta$ near the
brackets constructed below, so those brackets prove existence and
uniqueness of the relevant root.

\begin{lemma}\label{ivd:ll:lem:root}
For each fixed $B\ge1$, uniformly for
$|\lambda|\le p^{-1/2}(\log p)^B$, as $p\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \kappa_p&=\frac{\tfrac12\log p+\log\log p+c_*
                 +O(\log\log p/\log p)}p,
 &c_*&=\tfrac12\log(\pi/\alpha),\label{ivd:ll:eq:scalar}\\
 \eta_p(\lambda)&=\kappa_p-D\lambda^2/2
                         +O(p^{-3/2}(\log p)^{C_B}),\label{ivd:ll:eq:anchor}\\
 \eta_p'(\lambda)&=-D\lambda+O(p^{-3/4}(\log p)^{C_B}).
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:root-derivative}
\end{align}
For each fixed bounded real $z$, uniformly in this same range,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:shift-row}
 T_p(\lambda,\eta_p(\lambda)-z/p)
              =r+(1-r)e^{-z}+o(1).
\end{equation}
The finite exponent $C_B$ may be enlarged in each appearance.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First take $\lambda=0$ and set
\[
 s=\tfrac12\log p+\log\log p+c,\quad
 \beta=\log R(0,s/p),\quad
 m=p/\alpha+O(\sqrt p(\log p)^2),
\]
with bounded $c$. Then $\beta=\alpha s/p+O(s^2/p^2)$ and
$\beta m=s+O(p^{-1/2}(\log p)^3)$. By \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:drop-tail},
the legal CDF is squeezed between hard cutoffs
$p/\alpha\pm\sqrt p(\log p)^2$, with exponentially small tails.
The endpoint Laplace sum has the quantitative estimate
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:laplace}
 \sum_{b=0}^m d_b e^{\beta b}
 =2+\frac{e^{\beta m}}{\sqrt\pi m^{3/2}\beta}
          \left[1+O\!\left(\frac1{\beta m}\right)\right]
            +O(p^{-1/4}(\log p)^C).
\end{equation}
Indeed $d_b\le C(b+1)^{-3/2}$, so the excess from $b\le m/2$
is at most $C\beta\sqrt m e^{\beta m/2}$, of the displayed
polynomially small order. For $b>m/2$, put $b=m-h$ and use
$d_b=(\sqrt\pi b^{3/2})^{-1}(1+O(1/b))$.
The geometric zeroth moment gives the displayed main term, while
its first-moment correction divided by $m$ is
\[
 \frac{C\sum_{h\ge0}h e^{-\beta h}}
      {m\sum_{h\ge0}e^{-\beta h}}=O((m\beta)^{-1}).
\]
The geometric tail, Stirling error, unweighted missing tail
$O(m^{-1/2})$, and integer rounding are smaller.

Near $p/\alpha$, the individual weighted Catalan coefficients are
$O(\log p/p)$. The CDF transition window therefore costs
$O(p^{-1/2}(\log p)^C)$. Substitution in \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:row} yields
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:scalar-row}
 T_p(0,s/p)=r+2A_0\sqrt{\alpha/\pi}\,e^c
           [1+O(\log\log p/\log p)].
\end{equation}
Since $1-r=2A_0$, monotonicity brackets the root at
$c=c_*+O(\log\log p/\log p)$. A bounded subtraction of $z$ from
$s$ similarly proves \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:shift-row} at zero tilt.

For the anchored estimate put $\eta_c=\kappa_p-D\lambda^2/2$.
Analyticity and \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:logR} give
\begin{align}
 \log R(\lambda,\eta_c)-\log R(0,\kappa_p)
                         &=O(p^{-3/2}(\log p)^C),\label{ivd:ll:eq:Rcomparison}\\
 A(\lambda,\eta_c)-A(0,\kappa_p)
                         &=O(p^{-1/2}(\log p)^C).\nonumber
\end{align}
For example, the unsimplified remainder is bounded by a constant
times $|\lambda|^3+|\lambda|\kappa_p+\kappa_p^2+\lambda^4$.
The cancellation of $D\lambda^2/2$ inside $\log R$ is retained
even when $\eta_c$ is negative.

Choose $h=\sqrt p(\log p)^{B+2}$ and $m_\pm=p/\alpha\pm h$.
The actual mean shift $O(p|\lambda|+1)$ is smaller than $h$.
The two CDFs therefore differ from the hard cutoff only inside this
window, apart from tails bounded by $e^{-c(\log p)^{2B+4}}$.
The entire unrestricted coefficient sum through $p$ is at most a
fixed power of $p$: \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:scalar} and \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:Rcomparison}
give $\log R=O(\log p/p)$. Thus these tails remain negligible after
multiplication by that sum. The truncated unrestricted sum through
$m_+$ is $O(1)$ by \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:laplace}, and each coefficient in the
window is $O(\log p/p)$. The exponent comparison changes any
coefficient with $b\le p$ by relative
$O(p^{-1/2}(\log p)^C)$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:rowcomparison}
 |T_p(\lambda,\eta_c)-1|\le Cp^{-1/2}(\log p)^C.
\end{equation}

In an $\eta$ neighborhood of radius $p^{-3/2}(\log p)^{C+1}$,
we have $\partial_\eta T_p\ge cp$. To see this, restrict $b$ to
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:active-b}
 [p/\alpha-2p/\log p,\ p/\alpha-p/\log p].
\end{equation}
There are order $p/\log p$ terms with individual legal mass at
least $c\log p/p$. The margin $p/\log p$ dominates both the tilt
mean shift and its fluctuations; changes of the exponent in the
stated neighborhood are $o(1)$. Each of these cycles has
$L\ge b+1\ge cp$. Bracket \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:rowcomparison} using this
derivative lower bound to prove \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:anchor}. The same comparison
after a bounded shift $-z/p$ proves \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:shift-row}.

Finally, $\log T_p$ is convex and strictly increasing in $\eta$.
Its zero sublevel set is the hypograph of the root, so
$\eta_p$ is concave; analyticity and $\partial_\eta T_p>0$ also
make it differentiable. A differentiable concave function has its
derivative between its adjacent secant slopes. Apply these inequalities
to \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:anchor} with increment
$h_p=p^{-3/4}(\log p)^C$, taking $C$ sufficiently large and first
enlarging the tilt exponent to $B+1$. Both endpoints then lie in
that enlarged range. The secant error is
\[
 O(h_p)+O(p^{-3/2}(\log p)^C/h_p),
\]
which proves \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:root-derivative}. The scalar $\kappa_p$
cancels exactly. Concavity applied only to an unanchored $o(1/p)$
remainder would not supply the needed derivative rate.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
This lemma is the quantitative and anchored form of Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}'s Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:localroot}, which has $o(1)$ in place of $O(\log\log p/\log p)$ in \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:scalar} and no rate for the tilt dependence; Lemma~\ref{ivd:ll:lem:moments} below sharpens Lemmas~\ref{ivd:sh:lem:moments} and~\ref{ivd:sh:lem:drift} in the same way.
\end{writenote}

\begin{lemma}[Genuine duration moments]\label{ivd:ll:lem:moments}
For the exact row-one probability law
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:law}
 \Prob_{p,\lambda}(\text{cycle})
       =Q_p(\text{cycle})e^{\lambda\Delta+\eta_p(\lambda)L},
\end{equation}
in the tilt range of Lemma~\ref{ivd:ll:lem:root},
\begin{align}
 cp\le\E L\le Cp,&\qquad \E L^2\le Cp^2,
       &\E\Delta^2&\le Cp(\log p)^C,\label{ivd:ll:eq:moments}\\
 \frac{\E\Delta}{\E L}&=D\lambda+O(p^{-3/4}(\log p)^C).
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:drift}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At fixed $b$, the duration law remains negative binomial with success
parameter in a fixed compact subset of $(0,1)$. Its second moment
is $O((b+1)^2)$. Sum against the actual legal $b$-masses, which total
at most one, and use \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:active-b} for the lower mean.
For displacement, below $m_+$ the unrestricted fixed-$b$ second
moment is
\[
 O\big((b+1)+(b+1)^2(\lambda^2+\eta_p(\lambda)^2)\big)
 \le Cp(\log p)^C.
\]
Only the unrestricted sum through $m_+$ is used here, and it is
$O(1)$. Above $m_+$, retain legality: $\ell\le p-1$, and $w$ is
independent of that event. The second-moment numerator is bounded
by $Cp^2F_{b,\lambda}(p-1)$, whose exponentially small CDF beats
the polynomial unrestricted mass. The $b=0$ and ordinary terms
have bounded moments. Implicit differentiation of the exact row gives
$\eta_p'(\lambda)=-\E\Delta/\E L$; use
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:root-derivative}. No individual duration is replaced by
its conditional mean and no displacement-duration covariance is dropped.
\end{proof}

\section{The corrected potential and integrable height error}\label{ivd:ll:sec:potential}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:An}
 A_n=\frac13+\frac76\frac JN,\qquad e=e_n=N^{-6}.
\end{equation}
On a shrinking strip $ce\le x=p/H\le R$, with fixed $c>0$ and
$R<\infty$, and for $|q|\le P(n)$, Lemma~\ref{ivd:ll:lem:root} gives
\begin{align}
 \frac nS\eta_p(\theta q)&=\frac{A_n}{x}-\frac D2q^2+E_n(x,q),
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:potential}\\
 |E_n(x,q)|&\le\frac{C(1+|\log x|)}{Nx}+n^{-1/3}P(n).
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:potential-error}
\end{align}
Indeed $\lambda\sqrt p=\sqrt{Nx}\,q$, so some fixed $B$ covers
all such tilts. The scalar algebra is
\begin{align*}
 \tfrac12\log p&=\tfrac13N+\tfrac16J+\tfrac12\log x,\\
 \log\log p&=J+\log(2/3)+O((J+|\log x|)/N).
\end{align*}
Divide their sum by $Nx$. The constants $c_*$ and $\log(2/3)$
enter the $1/N$ remainder only; the anchored error in
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:anchor} becomes $n^{-1/3}P(n)$ after multiplication by
$n/S$ on this strip.

\begin{lemma}[Variational profile and height integrability]\label{ivd:ll:lem:profile}
For $A$ in a compact neighborhood of $1/3$, the minimum of
\[
 I_A(f)=\int_0^1\left(\frac{f'^2}{2D}+\frac A f\right)\dd t,
 \qquad f(0)=f(1)=0,
\]
over positive absolutely continuous paths of finite action is
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:min-value}
 \min I_A=\frac{3A}{M_A}=\sigma_D(3A)^{2/3},\qquad
 M_A=(2DA/\pi^2)^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
The minimizer is parameterized by
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:profile}
 f_A(t)=M_A\sin^2\varphi,\qquad
 t=\frac{\varphi-\sin\varphi\cos\varphi}{\pi},
 \quad0\le\varphi\le\pi.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $f_A''=-DA/f_A^2$ and
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:profile-integrals}
 \int_0^1\frac{\dd t}{f_A}=\frac2{M_A},\qquad
 \int_0^1\frac{1+|\log f_A|}{f_A}\dd t
 =\frac2{M_A}(1-\log M_A+2\log2)
\end{equation}
when $M_A<1$, as holds here for all sufficiently large $n$.
All these endpoint estimates are uniform for the indicated $A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\dd t=(2/\pi)\sin^2\varphi\dd\varphi$, direct
differentiation proves the ODE and the first integral in
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:profile-integrals}. The ODE first integral is
$f_A'^2/(2D)=A(1/f_A-1/M_A)$; integrating it proves
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:min-value}. The second integral follows from
$\int_0^\pi\log(\sin\varphi)\dd\varphi=-\pi\log2$.
For completeness the latter identity follows by symmetry and the
double-angle formula: if $I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\log\sin u\dd u$, then
$2I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\log(\sin u\cos u)\dd u
=I-(\pi/2)\log2$.

To verify minimality, convexity of $v\mapsto v^2/(2D)$ and of
$x\mapsto A/x$ gives, on $[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]$, the
pointwise lower tangent inequality around $f_A$.
The linear terms integrate by parts to a boundary term because
$f_A''=-DA/f_A^2$. For a competing finite-action path $f$, its
zero endpoint and Cauchy--Schwarz imply $f(t)=o(t^{1/2})$ as
$t\downarrow0$ and similarly at one. Since
$f_A(t)\asymp t^{2/3}$ and $f_A'(t)=O(t^{-1/3})$, the boundary
products $f_A'(t)(f(t)-f_A(t))$ vanish. Letting
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves $I_A(f)\ge I_A(f_A)$.
Strict convexity gives uniqueness. These endpoint powers also
show integrability directly and uniformity when $A$ stays in a
compact positive interval.
\end{proof}
A strip supremum bound $|\log x|=O(J)$ would turn
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:potential-error} into an artificial bulk term of size
$J/N$. We instead use the finite integral
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:profile-integrals} in both proof directions.

At $A=\frac13$, Lemma~\ref{ivd:ll:lem:profile} is Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}'s Theorem~\ref{ivd:sh:thm:variational}, whose proof also gives the exact quadratic remainder. The second integral in \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:profile-integrals} was rechecked by hand at the placement from $\int_0^\pi\log\sin\varphi\dd\varphi=-\pi\log2$.
\end{writenote}

\section{The coefficient upper bound}\label{ivd:ll:sec:upper}
\subsection{A quantitative dual with an integrated penalty}
Take $A=A_n$, $\zeta=N^{-9}$, and $r_n=1-2\zeta$. Set
\[
 u(t)=f_A'(\zeta+r_nt)/D,\qquad
 g(t)=f_A(\zeta+r_nt)/\sqrt{r_n}.
\]
Then $u'=-A/g^2$, $\min g\asymp N^{-6}=e$, and
$\max g<1$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Every fixed derivative
needed below is bounded by $P(n)$. Choose a sufficiently large fixed
$K$ and define $v(0)=0$ by
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:dual-v}
 v'(t)=\frac{2A}{g(t)}-\frac D2u(t)^2
       -\frac{K(1+|\log g(t)|)}{N g(t)}.
\end{equation}
The absolute value is smooth because $g<1$. From
Lemma~\ref{ivd:ll:lem:profile},
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:dual-value}
 v(1)\ge\sigma_D(3A_n)^{2/3}-C/N-C\sqrt e.
\end{equation}
Indeed the unpenalized integral is
\[
 \frac1{r_n}\int_\zeta^{1-\zeta}
 \left(\frac{2A\sqrt{r_n}}{f_A}-\frac{f_A'^2}{2D}\right)\dd t.
\]
Its omitted endpoint pieces are $O(\zeta^{1/3})=O(\sqrt e)$;
the time-rescaling factors cost $O(\zeta)$.
The penalty integrates to $O(1/N)$ by
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:profile-integrals}.

For each $t$ let $F_t(x)=-Ax/g(t)^2+2A/g(t)$. The exact gap is
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:dual-gap}
 \frac A x-F_t(x)=\frac A x(1-x/g(t))^2.
\end{equation}
For all $ce\le x\le R$, sufficiently large fixed $K$ makes
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:dual-penalty}
 F_t(x)-\frac{K(1+|\log g(t)|)}{N g(t)}
 \le\frac A x-\frac{C(1+|\log x|)}{Nx}-\frac3{Nx},
\end{equation}
where $C$ is from \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:potential-error}. If
$1/2\le x/g\le2$, both $1/x$ and $1+|\log x|$ are bounded
by fixed multiples of their counterparts at $g$, so the penalty
alone suffices. Outside that range \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:dual-gap} is at
least $A/(4x)$, which absorbs the displayed error since
$|\log x|=O(J)$ and $J/N\to0$. The polynomially small part of
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:potential-error} is also less than $1/(Nx)$ uniformly.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:dual-root}
 u'(t)x+v'(t)\le\frac nS\eta_p(\theta u(t))-\frac2{Nx}.
\end{equation}
After multiplying by $k_0=S/n$, the slack is exactly $2/p$.

\subsection{Uniform rows including very small heights}
For integer original time $0\le t\le n$ and $0\le p\le RH$, put
\[
 \Phi(t,p)=S[u(t/n)p/H+v(t/n)].
\]
Retain only legal cycles with $t+L\le n$ and endpoints in this
height region, multiplying their masses by
$e^{\Phi(t+L,p+\Delta)-\Phi(t,p)}$. Call a cycle good if
$L\le d_n n$ and $|\Delta|\le d_n H$, where $d_n=n^{-1/12}$.
Taylor's formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:taylor}
 \Phi(t+L,p+\Delta)-\Phi(t,p)
 \le\theta u(t/n)\Delta+
 k_0[u'(t/n)p/H+v'(t/n)+d_nP(n)]L.
\end{equation}
To include the mixed term explicitly, after dividing by $S$ and
writing $l=L/n$, $d=\Delta/H$, $x=p/H$, its remainder is
\begin{align*}
 &[u(t/n+l)-u(t/n)]d\\
 &\quad+x[u(t/n+l)-u(t/n)-u'(t/n)l]\\
 &\quad+[v(t/n+l)-v(t/n)-v'(t/n)l],
\end{align*}
bounded by $P(n)(l|d|+l^2)$. This proves \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:taylor}.

At $p\ge ceH$, its extra $\eta$ shift times $p$ is at most
$RN d_nP(n)=o(1)$. Equation~\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:dual-root} leaves at least
$1/p$ root slack. The shifted-row limit \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:shift-row} with
$z=1$ bounds every such good-cycle row by a fixed number below one.

Choose the fixed constant $c>0$ sufficiently small for the remaining
heights. At $p\le ceH$, the effective tilts in \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:taylor}
obey
\[
 \lambda^2\le Ck_0/e,\qquad |\eta|\le Ck_0/e,\qquad
 \beta=\log R(\lambda,\eta)\le Ck_0/e,
\]
with constants independent of small $c\le1$. Indeed
$|u'|p/H\le C/e$ there, and the terms of $v'$ satisfy the
same bound. If $\beta\ge0$, discarding only the CDF gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:low-excess}
 \sum_{b=0}^{p}d_b(e^{\beta b}-1)
 \le C\beta\sqrt{p+1}e^{\beta p}
 \le n^{-1/3+Cc}P(n).
\end{equation}
This uses $e^z-1\le ze^z$ and
$\sum_{b\le p}bd_b\le C\sqrt{p+1}$. For negative $\beta$
there is no excess. Take $c$ so that $Cc<1/6$.
The prefactor and ordinary-edge perturbations vanish uniformly,
so these rows are bounded above by $r+o(1)$. At $p=0$ there is
only the ordinary edge. Equality with $r+o(1)$ is not claimed at
fixed finite height, and no local-root approximation is used there.

For bad cycles, $|\Phi|\le SP(n)$ on the retained region.
The zero-tilt fixed-$b$ estimates
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:jump-tail}--\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:clock-tail}, retaining $b\le RH$
and using $\sum d_b\le2$, give transformed mass at most
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:bad}
 Ce^{SP(n)}\left\{
 e^{-c\min(d_n^2H^2/(RH+1),d_nH)}
 +e^{-cd_nn+C(RH+1)}\right\}=o(1).
\end{equation}
The first negative exponent has order $n^{1/2}$ times a fixed
logarithmic power, and the second has order $n^{11/12}$ minus
$O(H)$. Both dominate $SP(n)$. Thus all transformed rows are
bounded by a single fixed $\rho<1$ eventually.

\subsection{Exact original time and large heights}
The transformed mass over every possible number of cycles is at
most $1/(1-\rho)$. Keeping only paths with actual final original
time $n$ can only decrease it. Telescoping the transform and then
applying the genuine terminal multiplier gives
\[
 e^{-\Phi(n,p)}x_j^{-p}
 =e^{-Sv(1)}e^{-p(\log x_j+\theta u(1))}\le e^{-Sv(1)},
\]
since $\theta P(n)\to0$. Hence the confined normalized
coefficient is at most $Ce^{-Sv(1)}$.

For paths with a boundary height beyond $RH$, the spatial input
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:barrier-input} gives contribution
\[
 \exp\{-(\kappa\sqrt{2R/3}+o(1))S\}.
\]
Fix $R$ sufficiently large that the coefficient exceeds
$\sigma_D+1$. This is negligible even at the new logarithmic
scale. Finally,
\[
 \sigma_D(3A_n)^{2/3}
 =\sigma_D+\frac{7\sigma_D}{3}\frac JN+O(J^2/N^2),
 \qquad J^2/N^2=O(1/N).
\]
Together with \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:dual-value}, $\sqrt e=N^{-3}$, and the
fixed geometric prefactor, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:upper-coeff}
 n\log\mu-\log a_j(n)
 \ge\sigma_D S+\frac{7\sigma_D}{3}\frac{SJ}{N}-O(S/N).
\end{equation}

\section{The coefficient lower bound with a shrinking strip}\label{ivd:ll:sec:lower}
\subsection{Entrance and the proposed central path}
Take $P_n=\lfloor eH\rfloor$ and apply
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:entrance-input}. Write $a_n=E(P_n)/n$. Since
$\log(eH)/N\to2/3$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:entrance-scale}
 a_n\le Ce^{3/2},\qquad
 \text{entrance logarithmic cost}\le C\sqrt e\,S.
\end{equation}
Let $f=f_{1/3}$ and choose the left endpoint time $t_e$ with
$f(t_e)=e$. For a sufficiently large fixed $A_{\rm rep}$, put
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:path}
 \delta=A_{\rm rep}e^{3/2},\quad b_n=1-\delta,\quad
 c_n=\frac{1-2t_e}{b_n-a_n},\quad
 f_n(t)=f(t_e+c_n(t-a_n)).
\end{equation}
Increase $A_{\rm rep}$ so that $a_n\le\delta/2$ and the
uniform repair threshold below holds. Because $t_e=O(e^{3/2})$,
$c_n=1+O(e^{3/2})$. On $[a_n,b_n]$ the path has both endpoints
$e$, lies in $[e,M_{1/3}]$, and has every required fixed derivative
bounded by $P(n)$. Extend it beyond $b_n$ by the same formula for
time $n^{-1/12}$; this is valid eventually because the distance
to its singular endpoint has only a negative logarithmic order.

\subsection{Controlled law and the clock}
At an active departure time $s$, use \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:law} with
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:control}
 \lambda_s=\theta q(s/n),\qquad q=f_n'/D.
\end{equation}
Stop at the first boundary time $s_K\ge nb_n$, on leaving
$[eH/2,RH]$, or after
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:cap}
 m_n=\left\lceil\frac{4n}{c eH}\right\rceil+1
\end{equation}
cycles, where $c$ is the lower-mean constant in
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:moments}. All increments below are set to zero after
stopping. The chosen $R$ is larger than the profile's maximum.
All active departure states satisfy the quantitative local lemmas.

The clock martingale $Z_k=\sum_{i<k}(L_i-\E[L_i\mid\text{past}])$
satisfies
\[
 \E Z_{m_n}^2\le m_n C H^2\le nH P(n).
\]
Doob's inequality therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:clock-control}
 \max_k|Z_k|/n\le n^{-1/12}
\end{equation}
with failure probability at most $n^{-1/6}P(n)$. Also
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:mesh}
 \E\sum_i(L_i/n)^2\le n^{-1/3}P(n),\qquad
 \sum_i(L_i/n)^2\le n^{-1/6}
\end{equation}
with failure probability at most $n^{-1/6}P(n)$. On this event
the maximum time mesh is at most $n^{-1/12}$.
The cycle cap cannot be the first stop without the desired time
crossing or spatial exit: absent a prior stop,
$\E[L_i\mid\text{past}]\ge ceH/2$, and the cap in
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:cap} gives accumulated conditional mean above $2n$.
Its actual elapsed time would still be below $n$, contradicting
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:clock-control}. The factor $4$ accounts for the
lower strip height $eH/2$.

Put $v_i=\E[\Delta_i\mid\text{past}]/\E[L_i\mid\text{past}]$.
The anchored derivative estimate gives, uniformly,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:physical-drift}
 v_i=\theta f_n'(s_i/n)+O(n^{-1/2}P(n)),\qquad
 v_i/\theta=f_n'(s_i/n)+O(n^{-1/6}P(n)).
\end{equation}
The reward martingale $M_k=\sum_{i<k}(\Delta_i-v_iL_i)$ obeys
$\E M_{m_n}^2\le nP(n)$. To check the rate without independence,
use $(\Delta-vL)^2\le2\Delta^2+2v^2L^2$ in
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:moments}; each active increment has second moment at
most $HP(n)$ and $m_n\le(n/H)P(n)$.
Doob's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:reward-control}
 \max_k|M_k|/H\le n^{-1/12}
\end{equation}
with failure probability at most $n^{-1/6}P(n)$.

Since the total time is at most $n+\max_iL_i$, these estimates
imply at every visited boundary
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:path-sum}
 p_k/H=p_0/H+\sum_{i<k} f_n'(s_i/n)L_i/n
                     +O(n^{-1/12}P(n)).
\end{equation}
The Riemann error in this sum is at most
$P(n)\sum_i(L_i/n)^2$. The entrance rounding is $O(1/H)$.
It follows, on an event whose probability tends to one, that
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:tube}
 \sup_k|p_k/H-f_n(s_k/n)|\le\tau_n:=n^{-1/14},\qquad
 0\le s_K/n-b_n\le n^{-1/12}.
\end{equation}
Here $n^{-1/12}P(n)=o(n^{-1/14})$. Since $\tau_n=o(e)$,
the tube excludes a spatial exit: the proposed curve has margin
at least $e/2$ from the lower boundary and fixed upper margin.
The stop is therefore the intended original-time crossing.
This proves a quantitative shrinking-strip result rather than
appealing to a fixed-strip convergence theorem.

\subsection{Exact likelihood and an integrated error}
For each realized stopped prefix its inverse change of measure is
exact:
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:likelihood}
 \prod_i Q_{p_i}(\text{cycle}_i)
 =\prod_i\Prob_{p_i,\lambda_{s_i}}(\text{cycle}_i)e^{-B_n},
 \qquad
 B_n=\sum_i[\lambda_{s_i}\Delta_i+
                    \eta_{p_i}(\lambda_{s_i})L_i].
\end{equation}
No duration is rounded or frozen. Equations
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:potential}--\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:potential-error}, evaluated at the
actual random heights on \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:tube}, give
\begin{align}\label{ivd:ll:eq:likelihood-expand}
 \frac{B_n}S={}&\sum_iq(s_i/n)\frac{p_{i+1}-p_i}{H}\nonumber\\
 &+\sum_i\left(\frac{A_n}{p_i/H}-\frac D2q(s_i/n)^2\right)
                   \frac{L_i}n+O(1/N)+n^{-c_*'}P(n)
\end{align}
for some positive $c_*'$. The $O(1/N)$ is obtained by integration,
not by a strip supremum. Specifically,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:integrated-remainder}
 \sum_i\frac{1+|\log(p_i/H)|}{p_i/H}\frac{L_i}n=O(1).
\end{equation}
On $[e/2,R]$ this height function is Lipschitz, with constant
$P(n)$ (also across $x=1$). Its path-tube and mesh errors are
polynomially small; its integral along $f_n$ is uniformly bounded
by \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:profile-integrals} and the time change in
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:path}. The short overshoot contributes only
$n^{-1/12}P(n)$. This proves \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:integrated-remainder}.
The anchored-root remainder contributes $n^{-1/3}P(n)$.

For the first sum in \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:likelihood-expand}, discrete
summation by parts bounds the replacement error by
\[
 \tau_n\big(|q(\text{start})|+|q(\text{end})|+\TV(q)\big)
 \le\tau_nP(n).
\]
Thus no bounded-total-variation assertion for the random boundary
path is needed. Riemann errors for the deterministic smooth path
are polynomially small. The second sum is treated by its tube,
mesh, and lower-height bounds. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:likelihood-action}
 \frac{B_n}S=\int_{a_n}^{b_n}
      \left(\frac{f_n'^2}{2D}+\frac{A_n}{f_n}\right)\dd t
                  +O(1/N)+n^{-c_*'}P(n).
\end{equation}
The overshoot again has time at most $n^{-1/12}$ and every relevant
integrand there is at most $P(n)$.
The controlled event has probability tending to one, and is eventually
at least $1/2$. Integrating \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:likelihood} over it costs
only an additional factor $1/2$ in the lower mass bound.

\subsection{Every integer time and evaluation of the action}
On the tube, for sufficiently large $n$,
\[
 eH/2\le p_K\le2eH,\qquad
 \delta n/2\le n-s_K\le\delta n.
\]
Choose the fixed $A_{\rm rep}$ large enough that the repair input
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:repair-input} applies throughout this state and time range.
It appends a legal family of exactly $n-s_K$ steps ending at $(2,0)$,
with logarithmic cost at most
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:repair-cost}
 C\big(\sqrt e\,S+(\delta/e)S\big)\le C\sqrt e\,S.
\end{equation}
The entrance cost has the same order. The terminal multiplier is
exactly the constant $x_j^{-2}$. Concatenation is injective by the
completed-history argument in Report 127: the entrance duration is
deterministic, the interior stop is the first boundary visit at or
after $nb_n$, and the repair macrocycle pattern is a deterministic
function of its start state and residual time. Distinct decorated
prefixes remain distinct completed histories. Every internal
multiplicity realization counted by $Q$ is retained.

Set
\[
 K_e=\int_{t_e}^{1-t_e}\frac{f'^2}{2D}\dd t,
 \qquad W_e=\int_{t_e}^{1-t_e}\frac{\dd t}{f}.
\]
The central action in \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:likelihood-action} is exactly
$c_nK_e+A_nc_n^{-1}W_e$. The endpoint estimates give
\[
 K_e+\tfrac13W_e=\sigma_D+O(\sqrt e),\quad
 W_e=2\sigma_D+O(\sqrt e),\quad c_n=1+O(e^{3/2}).
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:lower-action}
 \int_{a_n}^{b_n}\left(\frac{f_n'^2}{2D}+\frac{A_n}{f_n}\right)\dd t
 =\sigma_D+\frac{7\sigma_D}{3}\frac JN+O(\sqrt e).
\end{equation}
The unperturbed profile suffices because the action is linear in
$A_n$ at that path. Combine \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:entrance-scale},
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:likelihood-action}, \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:repair-cost}, and
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:lower-action}. Since $\sqrt e=N^{-3}=O(1/N)$ and
every $n^{-c}P(n)=o(1/N)$, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:lower-coeff}
 n\log\mu-\log a_j(n)
 \le\sigma_D S+\frac{7\sigma_D}{3}\frac{SJ}{N}+O(S/N).
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:upper-coeff}, this proves
Theorem~\ref{ivd:ll:thm:main} at every sufficiently large integer $n$.

\section{Refined inverse thresholds}\label{ivd:ll:sec:inverse}
In this and the next section write $\sigma=\sigma_D$ and
$\lambda=\log\mu$. Let $\psi$, $R$, and $G$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{ivd:ll:thm:main}.
\begin{corollary}\label{ivd:ll:cor:inverse}
For real $T\to\infty$, define
$N(T)=\min\{n\ge0:a_j(n)\ge T\}$ and $s=(\log T)/\lambda$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:inverse}
 N(T)=s+\frac\sigma\lambda\psi(s)
       +\frac{7\sigma}{3\lambda}
          \frac{\psi(s)\log\log s}{\log s}
       +O\!\left(\frac{\psi(s)}{\log s}\right).
\end{equation}
This conclusion requires neither monotonicity of the coefficients
nor a ratio asymptotic.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $m(s)=s+G(s)/\lambda$ and consider integers within
$O(\psi(s))$ of $s$. Direct differentiation of the explicit smooth
functions gives $G'(t)=O(\psi(t)/t)$ and
$R'(t)=O(R(t)/t)$. Uniformly in this range,
\[
 G(n)=G(s)+O(\psi(s)^2/s)=G(s)+o(R(s)),\qquad
 R(n)=R(s)(1+o(1)).
\]
The small-error assertion follows from
$\psi(s)\log s/s\to0$. For a fixed sufficiently large $K$, let
$n_+=\lceil m(s)+KR(s)\rceil$. The coefficient lower envelope
in \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:envelopes} implies
$\log a_j(n_+)\ge\lambda s$ eventually. Integer rounding costs
$O(1)=o(R(s))$.

For the other direction, $a_j(n)\le\mu^n$ excludes every
integer $n<s$. The explicit function
$t\mapsto\lambda t-G(t)+CR(t)$ is strictly increasing eventually,
since its derivative tends to $\lambda>0$. At
$m(s)-KR(s)$ it is less than $\lambda s$ for large enough
fixed $K$. The upper envelope therefore excludes every integer
between $s$ and this threshold. These two brackets prove
$N(T)=m(s)+O(R(s))$, which is \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:inverse}.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
This refines Part~\ref{ivd:sc:part}, Corollary~\ref{ivd:sc:cor:inverse}, and Part~\ref{ivd:sh:part}, Corollary~\ref{ivd:sh:cor:inverse}. Part~\ref{ivd:sc:part} also proves, in Section~\ref{ivd:sc:sub:inverse}, that $a_j(n)$ is strictly increasing for $n\ge1$, which this corollary does not need.
\end{writenote}

\section{Refined boundary derivative growth}\label{ivd:ll:sec:derivatives}
Let $b_n=a_j(n)\mu^{-n}$, $F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_j(n)z^n$,
$\rho=1/\mu$, and $f(t)=F(\rho e^{it})$. Theorem~\ref{ivd:ll:thm:main}
gives radius $\rho$ and $\sum n^k b_n<\infty$ for every fixed
integer $k$. Thus $f$ is smooth, all its derivatives are represented
by uniformly convergent series, and positivity gives
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:moment-norm}
 \|f^{(k)}\|_\infty=M_k:=\sum_{n\ge0}n^k b_n\quad(k\ge1).
\end{equation}
Its maximum is attained at $t=0$. Radial boundary derivatives are
$F^{(k)}(\rho)=\mu^k\sum_{n\ge k}(n)_k b_n$, with the falling
factorial $(n)_k=n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)$.

\begin{corollary}\label{ivd:ll:cor:moments}
As $k\to\infty$ through integers,
\begin{align}
 \log\|f^{(k)}\|_\infty
 ={}&3k\log k-2k\log\log k
       +[\log(2D/\pi^2)-3]k\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{8mm}-\frac{k\log\log k}{\log k}
                     +O\!\left(\frac{k}{\log k}\right),
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:moment-refinement}\\
 \log F^{(k)}(\rho)
 ={}&3k\log k-2k\log\log k
       +[\log\mu+\log(2D/\pi^2)-3]k\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{8mm}-\frac{k\log\log k}{\log k}
                     +O\!\left(\frac{k}{\log k}\right).
 \label{ivd:ll:eq:radial-refinement}
\end{align}
These are consequences of the explicit envelopes
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:envelopes}; they assert no extra coefficient term.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
We first optimize explicit smooth bounds. For any fixed real $c$,
put $V_c(x)=G(x)+cR(x)$ for sufficiently large real $x$. With
$y=\log x$, $a=7/3$, and $b=c/\sigma$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:explicit-potential}
 V_c(e^y)=\sigma e^{y/3}y^{2/3}
             \left(1+\frac{a\log y+b}{y}\right).
\end{equation}
Its first and second $y$ derivatives are eventually positive,
with second derivative asymptotic to
$(\sigma/9)e^{y/3}y^{2/3}$. Thus $ky-V_c(e^y)$ is strictly
concave eventually, and its unique eventual maximum tends to
infinity. Finitely many smaller $x$ have objective only $O(k)$.
We differentiate only this explicit potential.

Write $B(y)=a\log y+b$. The stationary equation is
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:stationarity}
 k=\frac\sigma3e^{y/3}y^{2/3}
       \left(1+\frac{B(y)+2}{y}
                    +O\!\left(\frac{\log y}{y^2}\right)\right).
\end{equation}
Taking logarithms and multiplying by three gives, with
$L=\log k$,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:saddle-equation}
 y+2\log y+\frac{7\log y+3b+6}{y}
 =3L+3\log(3/\sigma)
                +O\!\left(\frac{(\log y)^2}{y^2}\right).
\end{equation}
First this shows $y\sim3L$; then it shows
$y=3L-2\log L+O(1)$. Substituting this back and expanding yields
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:saddle-location}
 y=3L-2\ell+c_0-\frac\ell L+O(1/L),\qquad
 \ell=\log L,\quad c_0=\log(3/\sigma^3).
\end{equation}
Here is the coefficient calculation explicitly. If
$y=3L-2\ell+c_0+d\ell/L+O(1/L)$, then $2\log y$ contributes
$-4\ell/(3L)$ at this order, and $7\log y/y$ contributes
$7\ell/(3L)$. Their combined $\ell/L$ coefficient in
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:saddle-equation} is $d+1$, so $d=-1$.
The remaining residual is $O(1/L)$ and the left derivative tends
to one, justifying the stated remainder by the mean-value theorem.

Divide \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:explicit-potential} by
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:stationarity}. At the saddle,
\begin{equation}\label{ivd:ll:eq:saddle-value-potential}
 V_c(e^y)=3k\left(1-\frac2y
                    +O\!\left(\frac{\log y}{y^2}\right)\right)
          =3k-\frac{2k}{L}+O(k\ell/L^2).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every fixed $c$,
\begin{align}\label{ivd:ll:eq:optimized}
 \sup_{x\ge x_0}\{k\log x-V_c(x)\}
 ={}&3kL-2k\ell+(c_0-3)k-\frac{k\ell}{L}+O(k/L).
\end{align}
The saddle has
$x\asymp k^3/(\log k)^2$. Rounding to a nearest integer changes
the objective by $o(1)$: in a fixed neighborhood of that saddle,
the absolute $x$ derivative is $O(k/x)$, and $k/x\to0$.
Thus \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:optimized} also holds for the integer supremum.

Now use \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:envelopes}. A single saddle integer for
$V_C$ supplies the lower bound for $M_k$.
For the upper bound reserve the summable positive factor $e^{-R(n)}$:
\[
 n^k b_n\le e^{-R(n)}
       \exp\{k\log n-G(n)+(C+1)R(n)\}
       \qquad(n\ge n_0).
\]
The series $\sum_{n\ge n_0}e^{-R(n)}$ is finite, and the remaining
supremum is \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:optimized} with $c=-(C+1)$.
The earlier coefficients contribute $e^{O(k)}$, negligible beside
the saddle on the logarithmic scale $k/L$. Therefore
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:optimized} proves \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:moment-refinement}, since
$3/\sigma_D^3=2D/\pi^2$.

For radial derivatives, $(n)_k\le n^k$ gives the upper bound.
At the same lower-bound saddle $n\asymp k^3/(\log k)^2$,
\[
 \log\frac{(n)_k}{n^k}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\log(1-j/n)=O(k^2/n)=o(k/\log k).
\]
The falling factorial therefore preserves the displayed refinement;
the factor $\mu^k$ adds $k\log\mu$.
\end{proof}
The unknown coefficient of $R(n)$ affects the moment only at
$O(k/\log k)$, which explains why the additional term in
\eqref{ivd:ll:eq:moment-refinement} is nevertheless determined. In the
convention $\|f^{(k)}\|_\infty\le C A^k(k!)^s$, the sharp
Gevrey order remains $3$, as already established in Report 127.

The dossier of the placement checked \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:optimized} numerically: for $k=10^4,\ldots,10^{14}$, $c\in\{0,\pm3\}$ and both values of $D$, the difference between $\sup_y\{ky-V_c(e^y)\}$ and the right side of \eqref{ivd:ll:eq:optimized} without its $O(k/L)$, divided by $k/L$, stays in $[-4.2,2.5]$, while omitting the term $-k\ell/L$ leaves about $-4.8\,k/L$ at $k=10^{14}$.
