\section{The counting model and main results}\label{sec:results}
For partitions $\lambda,\mu\vdash n$, let $K_{\lambda\mu}$ be the ordinary Kostka number: the number of semistandard Young tableaux of shape $\lambda$ and content $\mu$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defa}
a_n=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\sum_{\mu\vdash n}K_{\lambda\mu},\qquad a_0=1.
\end{equation}
This is OEIS A104779 \cite{A104779}. The former entry A178718 is officially a duplicate, with offset $1$ instead of $0$; its positive indices agree with those of A104779, without an index shift \cite{A178718}. The initial values, at $n=0,1,\ldots$, are
\[
1,1,3,7,21,57,182,565,1931,6670,24537,92337,364602,\ldots.
\]
An equivalent model counts symmetric nonnegative integer matrices, of variable dimension, whose total entry sum is $n$, whose rows are nonzero, and whose row sums are weakly decreasing. Positions remain distinct when margins coincide. In particular, simultaneous permutations among equal-margin rows and columns are \emph{not} identified. Symmetry makes the column conditions automatic. The empty matrix gives $a_0=1$.

All logarithms are natural. Write $I_n$ for the number of involutions in the symmetric group $S_n$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:involutionrec}
I_0=I_1=1,\qquad I_n=I_{n-1}+(n-1)I_{n-2}\quad(n\geq2),
\end{equation}
and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Cintro}
C=\prod_{j\geq2}(1-1/j!)^{-1}
  =2.5294774720791526481801161542539542411787\ldots.
\end{equation}
The product converges because $\sum_{j\geq2}1/j!<\infty$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There are nonnegative, effectively computable constants $H_s$, $s\geq0$, with $H_0=1$ and $H_1=H_2=0$, such that for every fixed integer $S\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
a_n=C\sum_{s=0}^{S}H_s I_{n-s}
       +O_S\bigl(I_n n^{-(S+1)/2}\bigr)\qquad(n\to\infty).
\end{equation}
The shifted terms are used only for $n\geq S$. Each constant is specified by the convergent coefficient product in Section~\ref{sec:identity}, and has a finite rational approximation algorithm with a proved error bound. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstrelative}
\frac{a_n}{CI_n}=1+H_3n^{-3/2}
 +(H_4-\tfrac32H_3)n^{-2}+O(n^{-5/2}),
\end{equation}
where $H_3=0.9887340176\ldots$ and $H_4=6.0121093539\ldots$.
\end{theorem}

Combining this result with the classical involution expansion gives a Poincar\'e expansion at every fixed half-integer order. Its leading equivalent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leading}
a_n\sim \frac{C}{\sqrt2}\left(\frac ne\right)^{n/2}
                   \exp(\sqrt n-1/4).
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:expansions} supplies all terms through $n^{-3}$ in the multiplicative and logarithmic normalizations. The absence of relative orders $n^{-1/2}$ and $n^{-1}$ in \eqref{eq:firstrelative} is specific to normalization by the \emph{exact} involution number. Those orders are present if the leading equivalent in \eqref{eq:leading} is used instead.

There are two further consequences. Under the uniform distribution on the matrices counted by $a_n$, the multiplicities of margins $j\geq2$ converge jointly, in total variation on the entire profile space, to independent geometric variables with parameter $1/j!$. The sharp leading error is $(2H_3/C)n^{-3/2}$. For the nonnegative and binary rectangular sorted-margin models, A321652 and A068313, the analogous expansion has scale $C^2n!$, integer inverse powers, and opposite first relative corrections at order $n^{-2}$.

\subsection{Scope and attribution}
Schwob's exact enumeration of self-inverse double cosets supplies both the Kostka-sum root-count identity and the cycle-index product used here \cite[\S4.3, (4.5)--(4.8)]{Schwob}. The companion identities are also in that source. These are prior results. The involution saddle method and all-order expansion have classical antecedents in Moser--Wyman \cite{MW1955,MW1956}; Bornemann \cite[Appendix C]{Bornemann} provides a modern display of the initial coefficients. We give the analytic details needed for a self-contained application, without claiming the underlying method as new.

The contribution developed here is the global support estimate and stabilization argument for this aggregate, its computable coefficient family, and the consequences for inversion, full margin profiles, and factorial-scale companions. Source comparison is bounded to the actual public versions inspected. In particular, Schwob's arXiv version 1 was inspected, while the journal full text was inaccessible; no claim about an unexamined journal revision is made. Neither a missing formula in an OEIS entry nor a bounded literature search proves global priority.

The phrase ``all fixed orders'' means that the truncation order is fixed before $n\to\infty$. Appendix~\ref{sec:sectors} strengthens the algebraic result with an exact half-axis decomposition and separate fixed-order expansions for its dominant and alternating recessive sectors. It transfers a classical Gaussian-moment and parabolic-cylinder structure through the proved support estimates. We do not prove convergence of the infinite asymptotic series, uniformity for growing truncation order, optimal truncation, or a complete description of additional exponential effects, Stokes data, or resurgence. Coefficient certificates are effective; the constants and onsets in the asymptotic remainders are not numerically certified. This distinction remains essential when rounding an inverse.
