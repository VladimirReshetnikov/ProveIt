\large Fixed depth shifts and proportional depth}
\author{A merged research report built from two manuscripts\\

\date{Manuscripts dated 2 October 2026; merged 2 October 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Let $H(n,m)=n![z^n](e^z-1)^{\circ m}$ be the iterated (higher-order) Bell numbers, so that OEIS A139383 and A261280 are $H(n,n)$ and $H(n,n+1)$. This report prints two manuscripts as two Parts. Part~I proves, for each fixed integer $k$, an expansion $H(n,n+k)=e^kC\,n^{2n-5/6}(2^{n-1}e^n)^{-1}\bigl(1+\sum_{j\le M}P_{j,k}(\log n)n^{-j}+O((1+\log n)^{D_M}n^{-M-1})\bigr)$ to every fixed order, with an outward-rounded interval certificate that $2<C<4$, an exact rational coefficient generator, and inverses of the smooth models with a safe integer threshold bracket. Part~II extends the expansion to proportional depth $m=\lambda n+\delta$, uniformly for $\lambda$ in compact subsets of $(0,\infty)$ and bounded $\delta$; its amplitude $D(\lambda)=\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda^{-1/(3\lambda)}I(1/\lambda)$, with $D(1)=2C$, is proved strictly positive for every $\lambda>0$ by an exact quadratic composition inequality and the single certified seed $I(1)>1.64$ of Part~I. Part~II also gives the floor-depth phase $e^{-\{\lambda n\}/\lambda}$, eventual monotonicity, the first correction and smooth inverses. Part~II is conditional on Part~I. Prellberg's 2002 bivariate formula and parabolic-coordinate method are direct antecedents of both leading equivalents; no priority is claimed for them, for the method, or for the displayed coefficients. The digits $C\approx2.86540798$ are not certified, the certificate relies on the interval arithmetic of \texttt{mpmath.iv}, and the report is unrefereed and not formalized.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{What is not claimed}
\phantomsection\label{ibd:guide:nonclaims}
Collected from both sources; each is also stated where it arises.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Priority.} No priority for the leading formula, the parabolic-coordinate method, or the displayed correction coefficients (source 53), nor for the leading equivalent, the parabolic method, the displayed formal coefficients, or the existence of earlier higher-term calculations (source 28): Prellberg's FPSAC 2002 slides and Mishna's 2005 seminar summary already state the pre-sum bivariate formula. Source 53's contribution is ``the fully specified proof and reproducibility package, without a claim that every component is historically new''. OEIS conjecture labels (as retrieved on 2 October 2026) are not evidence of novelty.
\item \textbf{Verification.} Both sources report independent mathematical review and replay of their checks; neither review is proof-assistant verification, and source 28's review is conditional on Part~I and its certificate. The interval certificate relies on the outward-rounded operations of \texttt{mpmath.iv} (tested with mpmath 1.3.0 and Python 3.12.14). The digits $C\approx2.86540798$ are exploratory and not certified; only $2<C<4$ is.
\item \textbf{Scope of the expansions.} Finite-order Poincar\'e expansions, not exponentially improved transseries; no sharp value of the logarithmic remainder degree $D_M$; the rational Abel constants are conservative bounds, not final instantiated remainder constants. Part~II claims uniformity only on compact positive slope ranges, not as $\lambda\to0$ or $\infty$ with $n$; general-$\beta$ amplitude values, final numerical remainder constants and target-specific threshold validity ranges are uncertified.
\item \textbf{Inverses.} The inverses are inverses of chosen smooth models; no canonical interpolation of the integer sequence or of non-integer depth, and no unique rounding rule for integer thresholds, is claimed; the $O$ statements are not numerically instantiated threshold certificates.
\item \textbf{Literature.} The literature and repository searches are bounded; they exclude neither unpublished results nor other formulations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Result and exact model}\label{ibd:sec:result}
Put $f(z)=e^z-1$ and, for integers $n\geq1$, $m\geq0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:H}
 H(n,m)=n![z^n]f^{\circ m}(z).
\end{equation}
The two sequences of interest are
\[
 A139383(n)=H(n,n),\qquad A261280(n)=H(n,n+1).
\]
The conventional value at $n=0$ is $1$. Equivalently, $H(n,m)$ counts the corresponding leaf-labeled rooted trees with $m$ levels; the second sequence counts $n$ successive refinements of a partition of an $n$-element set. Specifying \eqref{ibd:eq:H} avoids ambiguity about level conventions.

Let $S(n,j)$ be a Stirling number of the second kind. Composition of exponential generating functions gives the exact positive recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:rec}
 H(n,m+1)=\sum_{j=1}^n S(n,j)H(j,m),\qquad H(n,0)=\mathbf1_{\{n=1\}}.
\end{equation}
A useful normalization is $Q(n,m)=2^{n-1}H(n,m)/n!$, for which
\[
 Q(n,m+1)=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{S(n,j)j!2^{n-j}}{n!}Q(j,m).
\]
For fixed $n$, $Q(n,m)$ is a polynomial in $m$ of degree $n-1$ with leading coefficient $1$. This fixed-$n$ fact does not justify taking $m=n$ in its leading term.

\begin{theorem}\label{ibd:thm:main}
There is a real constant $C>0$, defined by the fixed contour in Section~\ref{ibd:sec:contour}, with the certified coarse enclosure $2<C<4$. For each fixed integer $k$ and each $M\geq0$, there are explicit real polynomials $P_{j,k}$, of degree at most $2j$, such that
\begin{align}\label{ibd:eq:main}
 H(n,n+k)&=e^k C\frac{n^{2n-5/6}}{2^{n-1}e^n}
 \left(1+\sum_{j=1}^M\frac{P_{j,k}(\log n)}{n^j}
 +\Oh_{M,k}\!\left(\frac{(1+\log n)^{D_M}}{n^{M+1}}\right)\right).
\end{align}
Here $D_M$ is a finite effectively obtainable logarithmic remainder degree, and $n+k\geq0$, as holds for all sufficiently large $n$. In particular, the ratio of A261280 to A139383 tends to $e$. The expansion is a finite-order Poincar\'e expansion, not an exponentially improved transseries.
\end{theorem}
The theorem's proof combines the analytic argument below with the independently executable amplitude certificate. The leading exponent $-5/6$ arises from a logarithmic drift in inverse iteration. It does not mean that the correction scale is $n^{-1/3}$.

There is also an exact probabilistic interpretation. If $Z_m$ is generation $m$ in a critical Poisson$(1)$ Galton--Watson process starting from one ancestor, then its generating function is $G(s)=e^{s-1}$ and
\[
 G^{\circ m}(1+z)-1=f^{\circ m}(z),\qquad
 H(n,m)=\mathbb E\bigl[(Z_m)_n\bigr].
\]
Thus the diagonal describes factorial moments whose order grows with time.

\section{Inverse logarithmic iteration}\label{ibd:sec:inviter}
Let $h(u)=\log(1+u)$ be the principal logarithm and set
\[
 t_m(u)=\frac2{h^{\circ m}(u)},\qquad
 g(w)=\frac2{\log(1+2/w)},\qquad t_0(u)=\frac2u.
\]
Then $t_{m+1}=g(t_m)$. Lagrange inversion yields the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:residue}
 H(n,m)=(n-1)!\,2^{-n}\Res_{u=0}t_m(u)^n\dd u.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the local inverse of $f^{\circ m}$ is $h^{\circ m}$, and
$[z^n]f^{\circ m}(z)=n^{-1}[u^{n-1}](u/h^{\circ m}(u))^n$.

\begin{lemma}\label{ibd:lem:stieltjes}
Off $[-2,0]$, with consistent half-plane branches,
\begin{align}\label{ibd:eq:stieltjes}
 g(w)&=w+1-\int_0^2\frac{\rho(s)}{w+s}\dd s,
 &\rho(s)&=\frac2{\log^2((2-s)/s)+\pi^2},\\
 g(w)&=\int_0^1 w^{1-v}(w+2)^v\dd v.\label{ibd:eq:mean}
\end{align}
The density has mass and first moment $1/3$, and second moment $19/45$. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:gexp}
 g(w)=w+1-\frac1{3w}+\frac1{3w^2}-\frac{19}{45w^3}+\frac3{5w^4}+\cdots.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $|g(w)|\leq g(|w|)\leq |w|+1$, and $g$ is increasing on positive reals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $w=-s+i0$, $0<s<2$, the inner logarithm is
$\log((2-s)/s)-i\pi$, so the imaginary part of $g$ is $\pi\rho(s)$. Subtracting $w+1$, a keyhole Cauchy integral around $[-2,0]$ gives \eqref{ibd:eq:stieltjes}; the endpoint logarithmic singularities are integrable and the remainder at infinity is $\Oh(1/w)$. Expansion at infinity identifies the mass and moments. Alternatively, the first moment follows from symmetry about $1$.

For \eqref{ibd:eq:mean}, integrate the exponential interpolation between $w$ and $w+2$. Their logarithm difference is the principal logarithm of $1+2/w$ in either half-plane. Taking absolute values and using $|w+2|\leq |w|+2$ proves $|g(w)|\leq g(|w|)$. The arithmetic-mean bound gives $g(r)\leq r+1$. Finally $g'(r)=1+\int\rho(s)/(r+s)^2\dd s>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An effective Fatou coordinate}
For $\Rea w=x\geq4$, put
\[
 V(w)=w+\frac13\log w+\frac1{18w}.
\]
\begin{lemma}\label{ibd:lem:tail}
For $x\geq4$,
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:defect}
 |V(g(w))-V(w)-1|\leq\frac2{x^3}.
\end{equation}
The limit $\Phi(w)=\lim_{j\to\infty}(V(g^{\circ j}(w))-j)$ exists locally uniformly, is analytic, and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:tail}
 |\Phi(w)-V(w)|\leq\frac2{x^2},\qquad
 \Phi(g(w))=\Phi(w)+1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $a=g(w)-w$. The Stieltjes formula gives
$|a-1|\leq1/(3x)$, $|a|\leq13/12$, $\Rea a\geq11/12$, and
\[
 a=1-\frac1{3w}+\frac1{3w^2}+R,\qquad |R|\leq\frac2{3x^3}.
\]
Here the bound on the second moment can be weakened to $2/3$. With $\delta=a/w$, $|\delta|<1/3$. Direct Taylor bounds give
\[
 \log(1+\delta)=\frac1w-\frac5{6w^2}+E,\quad |E|\leq\frac2{x^3},
\]
\[
 \frac1{w+a}-\frac1w=-\frac1{w^2}+E_2,\quad |E_2|\leq\frac9{4x^3}.
\]
For the logarithm, errors from $a/w$ and $a^2/(2w^2)$ are each at most $x^{-3}/2$ and the cubic remainder is less than $x^{-3}$. For the reciprocal, use
$|1-a+a/w|\leq3/(2x)$ and $|1+a/w|\geq2/3$.
The coefficients of $w^{-1}$ and $w^{-2}$ in the Abel defect cancel, leaving at most $35/(24x^3)<2/x^3$.

Along iteration $w_j=g^{\circ j}(w)$, $\Rea w_j\geq x+11j/12$. Summing \eqref{ibd:eq:defect} gives
\[
 \sum_{j\geq0}\frac2{(x+11j/12)^3}
 \leq\frac2{x^3}+\frac{12}{11x^2}<\frac2{x^2}.
\]
Uniform convergence of analytic functions proves the assertions. Also $w_j=w+j+\Oh(\log j)$ by the Stieltjes increment, so
$\Phi(w)=\lim_j(w_j-j+(\log j)/3)$.
\end{proof}
Whenever $t_M(u)$ has entered $\Rea w\geq4$, define
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:psi}
 \Psi(u)=\Phi(t_M(u))-M.
\end{equation}
The functional identity makes this independent of the chosen entry time. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:psitail}
 \left|\Psi(u)-\left(V(t_M(u))-M\right)\right|
 \leq\frac2{(\Rea t_M(u))^2}.
\end{equation}
Only finite continuation near the specific contours used below is needed. No global dynamics theorem or unrestricted circle expansion is assumed.

\section{The finite contour and uniform cut estimates}\label{ibd:sec:contour}
Fix $r=1/2$. The interval certificate in Section~\ref{ibd:sec:certificate} verifies a common finite entry into $\Rea w\geq4$ on every upper semicircle subarc; conjugation supplies the lower semicircle. Strict interval bounds also give entry in small neighborhoods of these arcs.

For fixed $m$, $h^{\circ m}$ is analytic off a negative-real cut beginning at $f^{\circ(m-1)}(-1)$. Nonreal points retain the sign of their imaginary part under principal logarithms, so no off-axis preimage of the cut occurs. Its only zero is $0$. Deforming the small residue contour to the fixed circle and the two lips gives
\begin{align}\label{ibd:eq:contourfinite}
 \Res t_m(u)^n\dd u={}&\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi
 \Rea\!\left[r e^{i\theta}t_m(r e^{i\theta})^n\right]\dd\theta\\
 &+\frac1\pi\int_0^r\Ima\!\left[t_m(-s+i0)^n\right]\dd s.\nonumber
\end{align}
At the finitely many logarithmic preimages met in a fixed iteration, the reciprocal tends to zero, so the small connecting circles vanish. Before its first cut crossing the boundary orbit is real; its contribution to the imaginary part is exactly zero. Values at the exceptional points themselves can be assigned arbitrarily within the limiting bounded enclosure.

\subsection{A compact entrance set}
Consider a nonexceptional upper-cut point $u=-s+i0$. At its first crossing, $h=a+i\pi$ and hence $|t|\leq2/\pi$. One further logarithm gives $h=U+iV$ with
\[
 U=\log|1+a+i\pi|\geq\log\pi>1,\qquad0<V<\pi.
\]
The reciprocal therefore lies in the right-half-plane cone
\[
 |\arg t|\leq\theta_0:=\arctan(\pi/\log\pi)<\pi/2.
\]
Equation~\eqref{ibd:eq:mean} preserves this cone: each integrand argument lies between $\arg w$ and $\arg(w+2)$, and the cone is convex. Stop at the first subsequent iterate with modulus at least $16$. It enters
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:K}
 K=\{w:16\leq|w|\leq17,\ \Rea w\geq16\cos\theta_0>4,\ \Ima w\leq0\}.
\end{equation}
The upper bound is from $|g(w)|\leq|w|+1$. The stopping time is finite: for $\Rea w=x>0$,
$\Rea g(w)\geq g(x)>x$; a finite positive limit of the real parts would contradict this strict continuous increment.

Write $\tau(s)$ for the total entrance time and $w(s)=t_{\tau(s)}(-s+i0)\in K$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:entrance}
 t_n(-s+i0)=g^{\circ(n-\tau)}(w(s)),\qquad
 \Psi(-s+i0)=\Phi(w(s))-\tau
\end{equation}
when $n\geq\tau$. Thus $\Rea\Psi\leq C_K-\tau$ and $\Ima\Psi$ is bounded uniformly. Long logarithmic excursions are absorbed into $\tau$; a uniform bound on $\tau$ near branch preimages is neither asserted nor needed. Between crossing and entrance the reciprocal modulus is less than $16$.

\subsection{Suppression of large delays}
For $R_0>0$ let $R_j=g^{\circ j}(R_0)$. Since
\[
 g(r)\leq r+1-\frac1{3(r+2)},\qquad R_j\leq j+R_0,
\]
harmonic summation gives
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:realbound}
 R_j\leq j-\frac13\log(j+1)+C_{R_0}.
\end{equation}
For $n\geq\tau$ put $j=n-\tau$ and compare with $R_0=17$. If $j\geq n/2$, the inequality $\log(1+v)\leq v$ gives
\[
 n^{1/3}|t_n/n|^n\leq C e^{-\tau}\left(\frac n{j+1}\right)^{1/3}\leq C' e^{-\tau}.
\]
If $j<n/2$, then $|t_n|\leq j+17$ gives $C e^{-c n}$. If crossing has occurred but $\tau>n$, use $|t_n|<16$; before crossing the imaginary part is zero. Altogether,
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:delaybound}
 n^{1/3}\left|\Ima\!\left[(t_n/n)^n\right]\right|
 \leq C e^{-c\min(\tau,n)}.
\end{equation}
Also $e^{\Psi}$ times any fixed polynomial in $\Psi$ is bounded by
$C_d e^{-\tau}(1+\tau)^d$.

Near the cut endpoint, write the pre-crossing reciprocal as $-R$, $R>2$. Its next magnitude satisfies
\[
 R-2\leq\frac2{-\log(1-2/R)}\leq R-1.
\]
The second inequality is the logarithmic-mean arithmetic-mean inequality. Starting at $R=2/s$, the crossing delay is at least $1/s-1$. Hence the limiting cut integrands have an integrable bound of the form $C\exp(-c/s)$ near zero.

\section{All finite orders and a coefficient generator}\label{ibd:sec:orders}
For every $J$, choose rational $d_\ell$ successively so that
\[
 V_J(w)=w+\tfrac13\log w+\sum_{\ell=1}^J d_\ell w^{-\ell},\qquad
 V_J(g(w))-V_J(w)-1=\Oh_J(|w|^{-J-2}).
\]
The multiplier of the new $d_\ell$ at order $w^{-\ell-1}$ is $-\ell$, so the recursion is uniquely solvable; $d_1=1/18$. This is an effective analytic expansion. The Laurent series of $g$ converges for $|w|>2$, and its Stieltjes moments satisfy $\mu_j\leq2^j/3$. Finite Taylor remainders therefore bound every defect. An explicit rational majorant is available. With $z=1/w$ and $q(z)=zg(1/z)$, on $|z|\leq1/3$ the Stieltjes formula gives $|q-1|\leq4/9$ and $|q|\geq5/9$. The analytic defect
\[
 D_J(z)=\frac{q-1-z}{z}+\frac13\log q+
       \sum_{\ell=1}^Jd_\ell z^\ell(q^{-\ell}-1)
\]
has a zero of order $J+2$. On that circle its modulus is bounded by
\[
 B_J=\frac8{15}+\sum_{\ell=1}^J|d_\ell|
                   \left((3/5)^\ell+3^{-\ell}\right),
\]
using $\log(9/5)<3/5$. The maximum-modulus principle applied to $D_J(z)/z^{J+2}$ gives the explicit defect constant $C_J=3^{J+2}B_J$. Consequently, for $\Rea w\geq4$,
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:generalremainder}
 |\Phi(w)-V_J(w)|\leq
 C_J\left(\frac14+\frac{12}{11(J+1)}\right)(\Rea w)^{-J-1}.
\end{equation}
The generator records these exact rational constants. They are conservative bounds for the Abel approximation; no claim is made that they are the final numerically instantiated remainder constants for a concrete sequence threshold.

Summing along the linearly growing real part gives
\[
 \Phi(w)=V_J(w)+\Oh_J((\Rea w)^{-J-1}).
\]
On orbits starting in the compact set $K$, imaginary parts stay bounded: the Stieltjes formula bounds their multiplicative increments by
$1+1/[3(\Rea w)^2]$, whose product converges. Thus the preceding estimates are uniform on these orbits. Reversion, using $V_J'(w)=1+\Oh(1/w)$, supplies uniform finite expansions for $t_n$.

\subsection{Polynomial recursion}
The following equivalent recursion is convenient for actual coefficients. Put
\[
 y=b-\tfrac13\log n,\qquad
 W(n,y)=n+y+\sum_{j\geq1}v_j(y)n^{-j},\qquad b=\Psi(u).
\]
Enforce, as a formal expansion,
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:vrec}
 W(n+1,y-\tfrac13\log(1+1/n))=g(W(n,y)).
\end{equation}
At order $n^{-j-1}$ the new polynomial enters as
$-jv_j-v_j'/3$. This operator is invertible on polynomials of degree at most $j$; induction gives a unique $v_j$ of degree at most $j$. In particular
\[
 v_1(y)=-\frac y3-\frac1{18}.
\]
Define $R_j(y)$ by
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:Rgen}
 \exp\left(n\log\frac{W(n,y)}n-y+
 \sum_{\ell\geq1}\frac{B_{2\ell}}{2\ell(2\ell-1)n^{2\ell-1}}\right)
 =\sum_{j\geq0}\frac{R_j(y)}{n^j}.
\end{equation}
The Bernoulli sum is the logarithmic Stirling expansion. At any requested order both sums are truncated finitely. One has
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:R1}
 R_0=1,\qquad R_1(y)=-\frac{y^2}2-\frac y3+\frac1{36},\qquad \deg R_j\leq2j.
\end{equation}
The second integrand polynomial, useful for checking conventions, is
\[
 R_2(y)=\frac{y^4}8+\frac{y^3}2+\frac{13y^2}{24}
          +\frac{23y}{108}+\frac{341}{12960}.
\]
The included exact-rational program implements \eqref{ibd:eq:vrec}--\eqref{ibd:eq:Rgen}, checks the recurrence cancellations, and verifies the degree bounds through the selected order. The analytic proof, rather than the program's finite tests, justifies arbitrary fixed depth.

\subsection{Why the cut expansion is uniform}
For a requested order $M$, split the cut into $\tau\leq A\log n$ and its complement. In the first region use the compact expansion at time $n-\tau$ with initial coordinate $\Phi(w)$ and reexpand in $n$. The functional relation identifies the parameter as $b=\Phi(w)-\tau=\Psi(u)$. Here $|b|=\Oh(\log n)$, so finite Taylor remainders are uniform. Finite Taylor expansion gives a bound of order
$n^{-M-1}(1+\log n)^{D_M}$ after normalized exponentiation and Stirling multiplication, for some finite effectively obtainable $D_M$. The theorem does not claim a sharp value of this remainder degree.

In the complementary region \eqref{ibd:eq:delaybound} bounds the true integrand by $\Oh(n^{-cA})$. The tails of all finitely many proposed coefficient functions are bounded by $e^{-\tau}$ times a polynomial in $\tau$, and hence by $\Oh(n^{-A}\operatorname{polylog}n)$. Choose $A$ sufficiently large in terms of $M$. The cases $n<\tau$ have already been included in \eqref{ibd:eq:delaybound}. This proves uniform integrability of every finite expansion without excluding neighborhoods of the countably many limiting log-preimages.

On the outer arcs the verified compact entry gives the expansion directly. Finite-length integration in \eqref{ibd:eq:contourfinite} therefore proves Theorem~\ref{ibd:thm:main}, except for positivity, which is supplied in Section~\ref{ibd:sec:certificate}.

\section{Inversion and discrete thresholds}\label{ibd:sec:inverse}
Taking logarithms of \eqref{ibd:eq:main} gives explicit polynomials $A_{j,k}$ and smooth finite models
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:Gmodel}
 G_{N,k}(t)=2t\log t-(1+\log2)t-\frac56\log t+\log(2C)+k
       +\sum_{j=1}^N\frac{A_{j,k}(\log t)}{t^j}.
\end{equation}
At integer points the logarithmic error is
$\Oh_{N,k}(t^{-N-1}(1+\log t)^D)$ for a finite effective $D$. Each chosen model $\exp(G_{N,k})$ is eventually strictly increasing, because
\[
 G_{N,k}'(t)=2\log t+1-\log2+\Oh_k((1+\log t)^D/t).
\]
The leading logarithmic term alone in fact contributes $-5/(6t)$ to the derivative, and all finite correction derivatives are smaller than the main term.

Let $Y$ be the target, $y=\log Y$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:inversecore}
 x=\frac{y}{2W_0(y/(2\sqrt{2e}))},\quad L=\log x,\quad S=2L+1-\log2.
\end{equation}
Then $2x\log x-(1+\log2)x=y$. Seek the inverse in the form
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:inverseansatz}
 t=x+\sum_{j=0}^M x^{-j}U_{j,k}(L,S^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Each $U_{j,k}$ is polynomial in $L$ and $S^{-1}$ with constants formed from $\log(2C)$ and the forward contour moments. Taylor-expand \eqref{ibd:eq:Gmodel} at $x$ and cancel residual powers successively. At order $x^{-j}$ the new unknown occurs only as $S U_{j,k}$; its other occurrences have at least one extra inverse power of $x$. Division by $S$ gives a unique constructive recursion. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:inversefirst}
 U_{0,k}=\frac{(5/6)L-\log(2C)-k}{S}.
\end{equation}
For illustration, if $a=U_{0,k}$ and $A_{1,k}$ is the first logarithmic forward coefficient, then
\[
 U_{1,k}=\frac{(5/6)a-a^2-A_{1,k}(L)}{S}.
\]
The same residual-cancellation rule constructs any prescribed finite order.

This reversion has a remainder proof. The first correction is bounded, and every subsequent term has at most polynomial-logarithmic growth. For sufficiently large $x$ all Taylor arguments lie in $[x/2,2x]$. Finite Taylor remainders give a residual
$\Oh(x^{-M-1}(1+\log x)^E)$ for some finite $E$, taking $N\geq M$. Since $G_{N,k}'\geq S/2$ in this interval, the mean-value theorem gives an inverse-index error
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:inverseerror}
 \Oh\!\left(\frac{x^{-M-1}(1+\log x)^E}{S}\right).
\end{equation}
The first correction alone has the sharper bound
$\Oh(\log^2 x/(xS))$. This proves inverses to every finite order for the specified smooth models; it does not choose a canonical interpolation of the integer sequence.

\subsection{Safe treatment of the integer threshold}
For fixed $k$ define the eventual discrete threshold
$\nu_k(Y)=\min\{n\geq n_0:H(n,n+k)\geq Y\}$, where $n_0$ is large enough for the chosen remainder bounds. Monotonicity has a direct exact proof: \eqref{ibd:eq:rec} implies
\[
 H(n+1,n+1+k)\geq S(n+1,n)H(n,n+k)
      =\binom{n+1}{2}H(n,n+k).
\]
Thus the sequence is strictly increasing once the positive terms and $n\geq2$ are reached.

With an explicit uniform logarithmic remainder bound and an explicit inverse residual bound, enlarge the resulting index uncertainty to $\eta>0$ so that integers below $t-\eta$ have count below $Y$, while integers above $t+\eta$ have count above $Y$. A safe integer enclosure is
\[
 \left\lceil t-\eta\right\rceil\leq\nu_k(Y)
       \leq\left\lfloor t+\eta\right\rfloor+1.
\]
A single rounding rule, such as $\nu_k(Y)=\lceil t\rceil$, is justified only after verifying separation from the relevant integer boundaries or checking the neighboring exact counts. The asymptotic $\Oh$ statements establish eventual enclosures but are not themselves numerically instantiated threshold certificates: effective constants and a validity range must be supplied for a concrete target.

\section{Literature boundary and further questions}\label{ibd:sec:literature}
\subsection{Historical attribution}\label{ibd:sec:attribution}
Prellberg's FPSAC 2002 slides~\cite{prellberg2002}, numbered slide 17 (PDF pages 55--59, final build on page 59), give an asymptotic for the individual coefficient $X_{n,m}$ before summing over depth. Mishna's 2005 summary of the 2002 seminar~\cite{mishna2005}, Section 2.2, page 49, prints the same formula. This is substantive prior overlap with the present diagonal result and method, rather than merely an asymptotic for the depth-summed sequence.

For an explicit comparison, use their setup with $f(z)=e^z-1$, $c=1/2$, $d=1/6$, $a(z)=a_0\in(0,1)$ and $b(z)=z$. Then
\[
 X_{n,m}=a_0^m H(n,m)/n!,\qquad Y(\mu(s))=a_0^s.
\]
Writing $\beta=n/m$, cancellation of $a_0^m$ in their pre-sum formula yields the announced form
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:eq:historical}
 \frac{H(n,m)}{n!}\sim 2^{-n}m^{n-1-\beta/3}\JP(\beta),
 \qquad \JP(\beta)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal C}\mu(s)e^{\beta s}\dd s.
\end{equation}
Thus $m=n$ already gives the power $n^{2n-5/6}$ after Stirling's formula. Their $\mu$ is an inverse parabolic coordinate; the contour and additive-coordinate conventions must be matched before identifying its amplitude with ours. Equation~\eqref{ibd:eq:historical} records the historical announcement, not an additional theorem invoked in our proof.

The brief sources do not supply the quantitative contour and uniform remainder estimates used here. Moreover, the broad printed theorem needs additional hypotheses or normalization in general: for $f(z)=z/(1-z)$, $a(z)=z$, $b(z)=1$, its solution is $1+zB(z)$ with $B$ the ordinary Bell-number series, giving $B_{n-1}$ rather than a nonzero constant multiple of the printed $B_n$ scale. This limitation does not negate the specific pre-sum announcement. The slides also record earlier non-rigorous computation of further terms. Accordingly, we claim no priority for the leading formula, parabolic-coordinate method, or displayed correction coefficients. The present contribution is the fully specified proof and reproducibility package, without a claim that every component is historically new.

\section{Statement and normalization}\label{ibd:pd:sec:statement}
Let $f(z)=e^z-1$, let $f^{\circ0}(z)=z$, and let $H(n,m)=n![z^n]f^{\circ m}(z)$ ($n\geq1$, $m\geq0$) be as in \eqref{ibd:eq:H}. Composition gives the nonnegative integer recurrence \eqref{ibd:eq:rec}, where $S(n,j)$ are Stirling numbers of the second kind. The coordinate $\Psi$ and fixed-radius functional $\I$ will always have the normalization of Section~\ref{ibd:pd:sec:foundation}. For real $\beta>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:amplitude}
 I(\beta)=\I[e^{\beta\Psi}],\qquad
 D(\lambda)=\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda^{-1/(3\lambda)}I(1/\lambda),\qquad
 \alpha=\frac12+\frac1{3\lambda}.
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}\label{ibd:pd:thm:main}
The function $I$ is real analytic and strictly positive on $(0,\infty)$. Fix a compact interval $\Lambda\subset(0,\infty)$, a finite $\Delta$, and an integer $M\geq0$. For $\lambda\in\Lambda$, $|\delta|\leq\Delta$, and integer $m=\lambda n+\delta$, as $n\to\infty$,
\begin{align}\label{ibd:pd:eq:main}
 H(n,m)={}&D(\lambda)e^{\delta/\lambda}
 \left(\frac{\lambda}{2e}\right)^n n^{2n-\alpha}
 \left\{1+\sum_{j=1}^M\frac{P_j(\log n;\lambda,\delta)}{n^j}
 +\Oh\!\left(\frac{(1+\log n)^{D_M}}{n^{M+1}}\right)\right\}.
\end{align}
The error is uniform on these parameter ranges. Each $P_j$ has degree at most $2j$ in $\log n$, is polynomial in $\delta$, and has coefficients analytic in $\lambda>0$. Its exact construction is given in Section~\ref{ibd:pd:sec:coefficients}. The exponent $D_M$ is finite and effectively obtainable; it is not asserted to be optimal.
\end{theorem}
An equivalent leading form, useful before Stirling expansion, is
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:factorial}
 H(n,m)\sim(n-1)!\,2^{-n}(\lambda n)^n
 (\lambda n)^{-1/(3\lambda)}e^{\delta/\lambda}I(1/\lambda).
\end{equation}
At $\lambda=1$, the foundation's convention is
$C=\sqrt{2\pi}I(1)/2$, so $D(1)=2C$ and
\[
 H(n,n)\sim C\frac{n^{2n-5/6}}{2^{n-1}e^n}.
\]
This factor of two is important when comparing OEIS constants. Uniformity is claimed only on compact positive slope ranges, not when $\lambda$ tends to zero or infinity with $n$.

\section{Uniform absolute transfer before positivity}\label{ibd:pd:sec:transfer}
To avoid division by a possibly vanishing amplitude, first put $\eta=n/m$ and fix $0<b\leq\eta\leq B<\infty$. Define the positive normalization
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:Cnm}
 \mathcal C(n,m)=(n-1)!\,2^{-n}m^n m^{-n/(3m)}.
\end{equation}
We first prove the absolute estimate
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:absolute}
 \frac{H(n,m)}{\mathcal C(n,m)}
 =I(\eta)+\Oh\bigl(m^{-1}(1+\log m)^D\bigr),
\end{equation}
and its finite-order analogues uniformly for $\eta\in[b,B]$.

When $j=m-\tau\geq m/2$, \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:realbound} and $\log(1+v)\leq v$ give
\begin{align}\label{ibd:pd:eq:delay}
 m^{\eta/3}|t_m/m|^n
 &\leq e^{\eta C_K-\eta\tau}
 \left(\frac m{j+1}\right)^{\eta/3}
 \leq C e^{-b\tau}.
\end{align}
If $0\leq j<m/2$, use $|t_m|\leq m/2+17$ for an exponentially small bound in $m$, uniformly for $\eta\in[b,B]$. If crossing has occurred but entrance has not, $|t_m|<16$ gives the same conclusion. Before crossing the imaginary part vanishes exactly. Hence, with uniform positive constants,
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:jumpbound}
 m^{\eta/3}\left|\Ima\bigl[(t_m/m)^n\bigr]\right|
 \leq C e^{-c\min(\tau,m)}.
\end{equation}
It is essential to combine the two finite-$m$ lips before estimating: the individual true lip integrals need not be absolutely integrable at zero. Their pre-crossing cancellation cannot be discarded.

For each fixed polynomial $Q$, the limiting coefficient integrands obey
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:coeffbound}
 |e^{\eta\Psi}Q(\Psi)|\leq C_Qe^{-b\tau}(1+\tau)^{\deg Q}.
\end{equation}
Their individual lips are integrable; \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:seed} also supplies direct exponential decay near $s=0$. For any requested finite order, split the cut at $\tau\leq A\log m$. On this part, reversion from the compact entrance orbit and finite Taylor expansion are uniform because $|\Psi|=\Oh(\log m)$ and $\eta$ remains in a compact interval. On the complement, \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:jumpbound} and \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:coeffbound} make both the true integrand and all retained coefficient tails smaller than the requested power by taking $A$ sufficiently large. The case $\tau>m$ is already included in \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:jumpbound}.

On the outer arcs, the certified common finite entry directly gives the uniform expansion. The contour has finite length. Termwise integration of every finite expansion is therefore justified, yielding \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:absolute} and every higher absolute order with a finite effective logarithmic remainder degree. The constants may depend on $b,B$ and the order; no endpoint uniformity is implied.

\section{Analyticity and strict positivity at every slope}\label{ibd:pd:sec:positivity}
For conjugation-symmetric functions, the real form of the contour functional is
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:realI}
 \I[F]=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\Rea\bigl[r e^{i\theta}F(re^{i\theta})\bigr]\dd\theta
 +\frac1\pi\int_0^r\Ima F(-s+i0)\dd s.
\end{equation}
For complex parameters use instead the complex-linear functional
\begin{align}\label{ibd:pd:eq:complexJ}
 \J[F]={}&\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}r e^{i\theta}F(re^{i\theta})\dd\theta\\
 &+\frac1{2\pi i}\int_0^r\bigl(F(-s+i0)-F(-s-i0)\bigr)\dd s.\nonumber
\end{align}
For real $\beta$, $\J[e^{\beta\Psi}]=I(\beta)$. The real-part form is not a complex-linear definition for complex $\beta$.

\begin{proposition}\label{ibd:pd:prop:positive}
The function $I$ extends holomorphically to $\Rea\beta>0$, is real on $(0,\infty)$, and satisfies $I(\beta)>0$ there.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On a compact subset of $\Rea\beta>0$, let $b=\min\Rea\beta>0$ and $T=\max|\Ima\beta|$. Bounded $\Ima\Psi$, \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:entry}, and bounded circle values imply, for every fixed $k$,
\[
 |e^{\beta\Psi}\Psi^k|\leq C_k e^{-b\tau}(1+\tau)^k
\]
on both lips. This is a local integrable majorant for holomorphy and differentiation under \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:complexJ}. Conjugation proves reality for real $\beta$.

Fix any real $\beta>0$ and take $n=\lfloor\beta m\rfloor$ as $m\to\infty$. Since $H(n,m)\geq0$, the absolute estimate \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:absolute} and continuity imply $I(\beta)\geq0$. The certified seed $I(1)>1.64$ shows $I$ is not identically zero. Thus its real zeros are isolated and finite in every compact interior interval. Nonnegativity and isolated zeros alone do not yet prove strict positivity.

Since iterates commute,
$f^{\circ(m+1)}=\exp(f^{\circ m})-1$. Keeping its positive quadratic term gives
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:quadratic}
 H(n,m+1)\geq\frac12\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom nk H(k,m)H(n-k,m).
\end{equation}
The exact normalization cancellation is
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:cancel}
 \frac{\binom nk\mathcal C(k,m)\mathcal C(n-k,m)}{\mathcal C(n,m)}
 =\frac n{k(n-k)}.
\end{equation}
Take $n/m\to\beta$ and restrict the sum to $a\leq k/n\leq c$, where $0<a<c<1$. Both $k/m$ and $(n-k)/m$ remain in compact positive intervals. If each uniform absolute error is at most $\varepsilon_m\to0$, and the corresponding $I$ values are bounded by $M_0$, the product error is at most $2M_0\varepsilon_m+\varepsilon_m^2$. The weights in \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:cancel} have bounded sum on this interval, so their accumulated error tends to zero. The sum tends to the ordinary Riemann integral
\[
 \frac12\int_a^c\frac{I(\beta\theta)I(\beta(1-\theta))}
 {\theta(1-\theta)}\dd\theta.
\]
On the left, $n/(m+1)\to\beta$ and
\begin{align*}
 \log\frac{\mathcal C(n,m+1)}{\mathcal C(n,m)}
 &=n\log(1+1/m)+\frac n3\left(\frac{\log m}{m}
 -\frac{\log(m+1)}{m+1}\right)\longrightarrow\beta.
\end{align*}
The second summand is $\Oh(\log m/m)$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:positiveintegral}
 e^\beta I(\beta)\geq\frac12\int_a^c
 \frac{I(\beta\theta)I(\beta(1-\theta))}{\theta(1-\theta)}\dd\theta.
\end{equation}
Each factor can vanish at only finitely many points in $[a,c]$. Both are nonnegative everywhere and positive elsewhere, so the integral is strictly positive. Therefore $I(\beta)>0$ for every $\beta>0$.
\end{proof}
This proof uses no numerical positivity samples away from $\beta=1$. After positivity is known, monotone convergence permits $a\downarrow0$ and $c\uparrow1$ in \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:positiveintegral}, giving a finite full-interval integral inequality. Continuity and compactness give a positive lower bound for $I$ on each compact positive interval, which now licenses uniform division in the absolute expansions.

\section{All orders and the explicit first correction}\label{ibd:pd:sec:coefficients}
The orbit polynomials are defined by the formal identity
\begin{gather}\label{ibd:pd:eq:W}
 W(q,y)=q+y+\sum_{j\geq1}v_j(y)q^{-j},\\
 W\left(q+1,y-\tfrac13\log(1+1/q)\right)=g(W(q,y)).\label{ibd:pd:eq:vrec}
\end{gather}
At order $q^{-j-1}$ the new polynomial enters through
$-jv_j-v_j'/3$, an invertible operator on polynomials of degree at most $j$. Induction determines $v_j$ uniquely with $\deg v_j\leq j$; in particular
\[
 v_1(y)=-\frac y3-\frac1{18}.
\]
The foundation's Abel defects and reversion give analytic remainders for these finite formal expansions.
\begin{writenote}The identity \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:W}--\eqref{ibd:pd:eq:vrec} is Part~\ref{ibd:part:diag}'s \eqref{ibd:eq:vrec} with $n$ written $q$, and $v_1$ is the same polynomial.\end{writenote}

For $q=\lambda n$, set
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:shift}
 \beta=1/\lambda,\qquad L=\log n,\qquad
 y=\Psi+\delta-\frac{L+\log\lambda}{3}.
\end{equation}
The bounded depth shift is precisely $\Psi\mapsto\Psi+\delta$. This follows from the Abel identity and reexpansion of $W(m,\Psi-\log m/3)$ at $m=q+\delta$. It is an asymptotic inverse-coordinate identity and requires no fractional iterate of the original generating function.

Define the integrand polynomials $R_j(y;\beta)$ by the finite formal generator, with $z=1/n$,
\begin{align}\label{ibd:pd:eq:Rgen}
 \sum_{j\geq0}R_j(y;\beta)z^j
 =\exp\Bigg\{&\frac1z\log\left[1+\beta yz+
 \sum_{j\geq1}v_j(y)\beta^{j+1}z^{j+1}\right]-\beta y\\
 &+\sum_{\ell\geq1}\frac{B_{2\ell}z^{2\ell-1}}{2\ell(2\ell-1)}\Bigg\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
At any requested order, all sums are truncated finitely. The Bernoulli sum is the logarithmic Stirling expansion for $(n-1)!$ in powers of $1/n$, not $1/m$. An order-$j$ logarithmic term has degree at most $j+1\leq2j$ for $j\geq1$, and exponentiation preserves the total degree bound. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:R1}
 R_0=1,\qquad
 R_1(y;\beta)=\frac1{12}+\beta^2\left(-\frac{y^2}{2}-\frac y3-\frac1{18}\right),
 \qquad\deg_yR_j\leq2j.
\end{equation}
Combining the absolute transfer, Stirling's expansion, and positivity proves Theorem~\ref{ibd:pd:thm:main}, with the exact coefficient formula
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:Pgen}
 P_j(L;\lambda,\delta)=\frac1{I(\beta)}\I\!\left[
 e^{\beta\Psi}R_j\!\left(\Psi+\delta-\frac{L+\log\lambda}{3};\beta\right)\right].
\end{equation}
This also proves the asserted analytic parameter dependence and the polynomial dependence on $\delta$ and $L$.

For a fully explicit first correction, put
\[
 \mu_j(\beta)=\frac{I^{(j)}(\beta)}{I(\beta)},\qquad \mu_0=1,\qquad
 A=\delta-\frac{L+\log\lambda}{3}.
\]
These are signed contour moments, not moments of an asserted positive measure. Then
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:P1}
 P_1=\frac1{12}-\beta^2\left[
 \frac{\mu_2+2A\mu_1+A^2}{2}+
 \frac{\mu_1+A}{3}+\frac1{18}\right].
\end{equation}
At $\beta=\lambda=1$, \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:R1} becomes
$-y^2/2-y/3+1/36$, exactly the diagonal convention. The accompanying exact-rational checks verify the first correction, degree bounds, and agreement with the foundation through order three; an independent check verifies bounded-shift translation through order two. Finite checks supplement the analytic proof.

\section{Floor depth and bounded shifts}\label{ibd:pd:sec:floors}
Taking $m=\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor$ gives $\delta_n=-\{\lambda n\}$, so
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:floor}
 H(n,\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor)\sim
 D(\lambda)e^{-\{\lambda n\}/\lambda}
 \left(\frac{\lambda}{2e}\right)^n n^{2n-1/2-1/(3\lambda)}.
\end{equation}
The fractional-part multiplier is indispensable. It is identically one for integer $\lambda$, periodic for noninteger rational $\lambda$, and exhibits the usual fractional-part oscillation for irrational $\lambda$. Thus the unmodulated normalization generally has no single limiting amplitude. The full expansion follows by substituting $\delta_n$ into every $P_j$; bounded-shift uniformity justifies that substitution without any smoothness of the sequence.

For ceilings use $\delta_n=\lceil\lambda n\rceil-\lambda n$, including zero at integral $\lambda n$. General bounded shifts are equally valid in the forward theorem but need not produce a monotone sequence. For a fixed integer increment $r$ and $m=\lambda n+\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:shiftratio}
 \frac{H(n,m+r)}{H(n,m)}\longrightarrow e^{r/\lambda}
\end{equation}
uniformly on the same compact slope and bounded-shift ranges. The leading shift dependence is $e^{\delta/\lambda}$, not $e^\delta$ except at $\lambda=1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{ibd:pd:prop:monotone}
For each fixed $\lambda>0$, the floor-depth sequence $H(n,\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor)$ is eventually strictly increasing.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From \eqref{ibd:eq:rec} and $S(n,n)=1$, $H(n,m+1)\geq H(n,m)$. For fixed $m\geq2$ and $n\geq2$, compare the recurrence for $H(n+1,m)$ with that for $H(n,m)$. The inequalities $S(n+1,j)\geq S(n,j)$ hold, with strict inequality at $j=2$ and $H(2,m-1)>0$. Therefore $H(n+1,m)>H(n,m)$. Eventually $\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor\geq2$ and $\lfloor\lambda(n+1)\rfloor\geq\lfloor\lambda n\rfloor$, proving the claim.
\end{proof}
This supplies a well-defined eventual integer threshold. It does not remove the phase from its inversion.

\section{Smooth inverse construction and discrete thresholds}\label{ibd:pd:sec:inverse}
Fix $\lambda>0$ and a constant real $\delta$. Choose a smooth finite asymptotic model; this is not a claim of canonical interpolation in noninteger depth. Write
\begin{gather}\label{ibd:pd:eq:logmodel}
 c=\log\frac{\lambda}{2e},\qquad
 d=\log D(\lambda)+\frac\delta\lambda,\qquad
 G_N(t)=2t\log t+ct-\alpha\log t+d+
 \sum_{j=1}^N A_j(\log t;\lambda,\delta)t^{-j},\\
 \log\left(1+\sum_{j\geq1}P_j(L;\lambda,\delta)z^j\right)
 =\sum_{j\geq1}A_j(L;\lambda,\delta)z^j.\nonumber
\end{gather}
In particular $A_1=P_1$ and $A_2=P_2-P_1^2/2$. The model is eventually strictly increasing uniformly on compact slope and bounded-shift ranges.

For a large target $Y$, put $y=\log Y$ and use the positive real branch of Lambert's function:
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:Lambert}
 x=\frac{y}{2W_0\!\left(y\sqrt\lambda/(2\sqrt{2e})\right)},\qquad
 L=\log x,\qquad S=2L+1+\log(\lambda/2).
\end{equation}
Then $2x\log x+cx=y$ and $S$ is the derivative of that leading function at $x$. Seek
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:inverse}
 t=x+\sum_{j=0}^M x^{-j}U_j(L,S^{-1};\lambda,\delta).
\end{equation}
Cancel residual powers in $G_N(t)-y$ successively. At its first order, the new $U_j$ appears only as $S U_j$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:U01}
 U_0=\frac{\alpha L-d}{S},\qquad
 U_1=\frac{\alpha U_0-U_0^2-A_1(L;\lambda,\delta)}{S}.
\end{equation}
For an explicit all-orders algorithm, given $U_0,\dots,U_{j-1}$, form
$t_{<j}=x+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}x^{-k}U_k$ and let $E_j$ be the coefficient of $x^{-j}$ in the formal expansion of $G_N(t_{<j})-y$, with $L=\log x$ retained as a formal parameter. Then
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:Urec}
 U_j=-E_j/S\qquad(j\geq1),\qquad N\geq j.
\end{equation}
Here $\log t_{<j}=L+\log(t_{<j}/x)$ is expanded finitely. This makes each $U_j$ a polynomial expression in $L$ and $S^{-1}$ with the stated analytic parameter coefficients, and supplies every finite order.

Taylor remainders and the eventual bound $G_N'\geq S/2$ in a neighborhood of $x$ yield inverse error
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:inverseerror}
 \Oh\!\left(\frac{x^{-M-1}(1+\log x)^E}{S}\right),\qquad N\geq M,
\end{equation}
for a finite effective $E$, uniformly on the compact parameter ranges. In particular, using only $x+U_0$ gives the valid conservative error $\Oh(\log^2x/(xS))$. These are inverses of the chosen smooth model; applying them to integer thresholds still requires the phase and forward error to be controlled.

For $\lambda=p/q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$, each residue class of $n$ modulo $q$ has fixed $\delta_n=-\{pn/q\}$. Apply the corresponding fixed-$\delta$ smooth inverse on each arithmetic progression, then bracket on its lattice. For irrational $\lambda$ there are no finitely many such branches.

One must not hold $\delta_n$ constant while inverting a general floor-depth sequence. Its logarithm contains the bounded sawtooth term $-\{\lambda n\}/\lambda$. Omitting that term produces $\Oh(1)$ logarithmic uncertainty, hence $\Oh(1/\log n)$ uncertainty in the index, larger than higher smooth corrections. Safe alternatives are to bracket by the $\delta=0$ and $\delta=-1$ models with explicitly instantiated forward errors, or to evaluate the neighboring candidate exact counts. A unique rounding rule requires separation from integer boundaries or exact bracketing. No such numerical threshold certificate is supplied here.
\begin{writenote}As in Part~\ref{ibd:part:diag}, the Lambert core \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:Lambert} and the recursion \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:Urec} are instances of the transseries volume's \texttt{p0:thm:lambert-core} and \texttt{p0:thm:perturbed-inversion}, and the lattice bracketing of \texttt{p0:thm:staircase}; at $\lambda=1$, $\delta=k$, \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:Lambert} and \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:U01} reduce to \eqref{ibd:eq:inversecore} and \eqref{ibd:eq:inversefirst}.\end{writenote}

\section{Literature comparison and boundaries}\label{ibd:pd:sec:literature}
\subsection{Prellberg's direct bivariate antecedent}\label{ibd:pd:sec:prellberg}
Prellberg's FPSAC 2002 slides \cite[slide 17 of 26, physical PDF p.~59]{prellberg2002} display a leading two-index coefficient equivalent before summing over the depth. A September 2002 seminar account summarized by Mishna \cite[especially p.~49]{mishna2005} discusses the same parabolic-coordinate method. This is a direct antecedent, not merely a shared exponential scale.

To spell out the algebraic specialization, Prellberg treats
\[
 X(z)=a(z)X(f(z))+b(z),\qquad f(z)=z+cz^2+dz^3+\cdots.
\]
Take $a(z)=a_0\in(0,1)$, $b(z)=z$, and $f=e^z-1$. Then $c=1/2$, $d=1/6$, $1-d/c^2=1/3$, and
\[
 X(z)=\sum_{m\geq0}a_0^mf^{\circ m}(z),\qquad
 X_{n,m}=a_0^mH(n,m)/n!.
\]
If $f^{-1}(\mu(s))=\mu(s+1)$, the homogeneous solution satisfies $Y(\mu(s))=a_0^s$, up to an irrelevant constant factor. Cancelling those factors in the displayed source formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{ibd:pd:eq:prellberg}
 \frac{H(n,m)}{n!}\sim(m/2)^n m^{-1-\beta/3}\JP(\beta),\qquad
 \JP(\beta)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal C}\mu(s)e^{\beta s}\dd s,
 \quad\beta=n/m.
\end{equation}
This is our algebraic specialization of the prior formula, not a newly located verbatim theorem about $H$.
\begin{writenote}This is the specialization \eqref{ibd:eq:historical} of Part~\ref{ibd:part:diag}, written with $(m/2)^nm^{-1-\beta/3}$ in place of $2^{-n}m^{n-1-\beta/3}$. Both sources wrote $J(\beta)$; it is written $\JP(\beta)$ here (see the Guide).\end{writenote}

Formally, if contour correspondence, orientation, normalization, and vanishing endpoint terms are established, integration by parts gives
\[
 -\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal C}\mu'(s)e^{\beta s}\dd s
 =\beta \JP(\beta),
\]
which is the factor $I(\beta)=\beta \JP(\beta)$ needed to match \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:factorial}. This addendum does not infer that analytic contour identification solely from matching formulas. Translating a Fatou coordinate multiplies its amplitude by an exponential, so numerical constants also require matched normalization. For $m=\lambda n$, Stirling converts \eqref{ibd:pd:eq:prellberg} to the same formal leading shape, with prefactor
$\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda^{-1-1/(3\lambda)}\JP(1/\lambda)$.

The located sources describe inverse-coordinate expansions to all orders, but do not state an explicit compact $\beta$ uniformity region, quantified bivariate remainder, all-parameter strict positivity result, or all-orders coefficient expansion for $X_{n,m}$. Their final numbered theorem concerns the sum over $m$. They are short proof outlines; contour and tail estimates are not developed there to the depth required for an independent quantitative audit. The last slide labels earlier higher correction computations as non-rigorous. Visible ancillary typographical inconsistencies further counsel using the original slide formula with checked normalization. None of this disproves that formula, and no exhaustive claim about the later literature is made.

The purpose here is a self-contained compact-uniform transfer, conditional on the explicit audited contour foundation, together with positivity, coefficient construction, floor phase, and safe inverse use. We claim no priority for the leading equivalent, parabolic method, displayed formal coefficients, or the existence of earlier higher-term calculations.

\subsection{Other overlap and scope restrictions}
Skau and Kristensen \cite{skau} use $B_n^{(r)}=H(n,r+1)$ and obtain
$B_n^{(r)}\sim n!r^{n-1}/2^{n-1}$ for fixed $n$ as $r\to\infty$. Their theorem supplies no uniformity in growing $n$, so substituting $r\asymp n$ is not justified. Carlson, Makarychev, and Mosenzon \cite[Theorem 5.7]{cmm} use $B_k(p)=H(p,k+1)$ and obtain root-order bounds; at $k\asymp p$ these give $B_k(p)^{1/p}=\Theta(p^2)$, without the exponential-base constant, power correction, or multiplicative amplitude.

For critical Poisson$(1)$ branching from one ancestor and $m\geq1$,
\[
 H(n,m)=\mathbb E[(Z_m)_n]=\mathbb E[Z_{m-1}^{n}].
\]
Nagaev and Vakhtel's large-deviation theorem \cite{nv} assumes $k/t\to\infty$ and $k=o(t^2)$. Its logarithmic coefficient $1-2f'''(1)/(3f''(1)^2)$ equals $1/3$ for Poisson reproduction. The heuristic moment saddle $k\approx nt/2$ is of order $t^2$ when $t\asymp n$, outside that theorem's range. Its formal extrapolation suggests the logarithmic power but cannot prove the amplitude here.

OEIS A144150 indexes $H(n,k+1)$; A139383, A261280, and A346802 index $H(n,n)$, $H(n,n+1)$, and $H(n,n+2)$ respectively \cite{oeis}. The inspected entries explicitly label the corresponding leading equivalents as conjectures. Their constants are consistent with $D(1)$, $eD(1)$, and $e^2D(1)$ after respecting the denominator convention. Searches for several nonunit-slope initial segments found no dedicated entry, a bounded search result rather than evidence of absence.

The related partition-lattice chain observable is a depth sum:
$Z_n=\sum_{m\geq0}2^{-m-1}H(n,m)$, since its generating function obeys $2Z(z)=z+Z(f(z))$. Its known aggregate asymptotics motivate Prellberg's setting, but a depth-summed equivalent alone does not give a pointwise proportional-depth equivalent. The direct source overlap above was checked at the bivariate formula itself.

\section{Reproducibility and precise limitations}\label{ibd:pd:sec:repro}
The accompanying archive includes the editable \LaTeX\ source and build script; the unchanged attribution-corrected foundation, certificate, and reviews; the proportional research and independent audit snapshots; and exact coefficient-generation scripts with recorded outputs and hashes. All executable replay entry points use package-relative paths. The independent audit's original files and hash manifest are preserved verbatim; the portable replay uses a separately identified path-adjusted copy of its check.

The default replay regenerates the foundation's order-three orbit polynomials, generates the general-$\beta$ integrand polynomials, compares both JSON outputs with the saved exact outputs, and checks the independent low-order identities. An optional diagnostic computes exact recurrence values and exploratory long-double contour quadratures. At $n=160$, the recorded count divided by the first-corrected prediction is approximately $0.999845$, $1.000009$, and $1.000013$ for $\lambda=1/2,1,2$. The only numerical input to the all-$\beta$ positivity argument is the foundation's outward-rounded certificate at $\beta=1$. That certificate relies on the documented interval semantics of its runtime; it is not an independently verified arithmetic kernel. General-$\beta$ amplitude values, final numerical remainder constants, and target-specific threshold validity ranges remain uncertified. Symbolic generation is finite at every requested order and does not assert convergence of an infinite series. No exponentially improved expansion or endpoint slope regime is claimed.
