{\small\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
We study the level-seven Ap\'ery-like sequence A279619, defined by a
second-order polynomial recurrence. Its OEIS entry displays a conjectural
asymptotic with a numerical constant. We prove that asymptotic and identify
the constant as a reciprocal product of three gamma values. Three independent
proofs of the constant are printed. The first: a rational parameter
removes the square roots in the known hypergeometric generating function,
making its singular connection coefficient explicit, and classical
elliptic-period and Ramanujan identities reduce this coefficient to its
gamma-product form. The second: the boundary value of the generating
function is a Dedekind eta product at the Fricke fixed point $\ii/\sqrt7$,
evaluated by the Chowla--Selberg formula at discriminant $-7$, and the
singular coefficient is then read off from Hirschhorn's constant for the
square sequence A183204. The third, from a manuscript added later,
differentiates the Fricke involution of the weight-one theta series at its
fixed point to obtain the singular coefficient directly, and evaluates the
same eta value from an Epstein zeta function with an explicit conductor-two
factor. A functional equation gives a second exact sum,
at $z=1/125$. Frobenius expansion and singularity analysis give all
algebraic correction terms, with rational coefficients and fixed-order
error bounds, together with ratio, root and critical-tail expansions. We
also construct a companion continued fraction, prove exact rational
bracketing, and determine the amplitude and every correction term of its
recessive solution. Applications include convolution powers, A183204,
eventual log-convexity, and Lambert-$W$ inversion with a separate
treatment of integer rounding. Exact symbolic certificates and rational
enclosures accompany numerical experiments. Classical inputs, previously
calculated terms, sequence-specific deductions, and unsettled questions
are explicitly distinguished.\par}

\section{The target and the scope of the contribution}\label{l7g:sec:target}
The sequence begins
\[
1,\ 2,\ 22,\ 336,\ 6006,\ 117348,\ 2428272,\ 52303680,\ 1160427510,\ldots
\]
and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:rec}
(n+1)^2a_{n+1}=(26n^2+13n+2)a_n+3(3n-1)(3n-2)a_{n-1},\qquad n\ge1,
\end{equation}
with $a_0=1$, $a_1=2$. (Manuscript~56 writes the last coefficient as
$27n^2-27n+6$.) Throughout, set
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:defs}
 A(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n,\qquad \rho=1/27,\qquad
 \G=\Gamma(1/7)\Gamma(2/7)\Gamma(4/7).
\end{equation}
This is A279619; $A(z)^2$ is the generating function of A183204
\cite{l7g:oeis279619,l7g:oeis183204}. Its connections with modular forms,
hypergeometric functions, and another Ap\'ery-like sequence make it a
structured target rather than an isolated numerical curiosity.

\subsection{What is already known}
The OEIS entry consulted on October 1, 2026 labels its leading asymptotic
as conjectural and supplies a numerical constant. O'Brien's thesis
\cite[\S8.4, pp.~63--67]{l7g:obrien} obtained the recurrence and the
level-seven modular parametrization, estimates that constant and formally
calculates the first three correction terms. Those three terms are
\emph{not new here}. The hypergeometric representation is also recorded
in OEIS in code attributed to V\'aclav Kote\v{s}ovec in 2018
\cite{l7g:oeis279619}. We prove and rationalize that representation before
using it analytically. The leading asymptotic of the square sequence
A183204, with a complete expansion, is Hirschhorn's theorem
\cite{l7g:hirschhorn}; it is used, and its leading constant re-derived, in
the second proof (Section~\ref{l7g:sec:second}).

The contribution is not a new general method of asymptotic analysis.
It is an exact connection-constant evaluation, a rigorous all-orders
fixed-order expansion, and an explicit recessive companion with a proved
error amplitude. Standard machinery is used to close the displayed
asymptotic question.

\section{Principal results}\label{l7g:sec:principal}
\begin{theorem}[Dominant asymptotics and the exact constant; proved in all three sources]\label{l7g:th:main}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:C}
 C=\frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{\G},\qquad \Ast=\frac{3\G}{8\pi^2}.
\end{equation}
Then $A(\rho)=\Ast$. For every fixed $M\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:dominant}
 a_n=C\frac{27^n}{n^{3/2}}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^M\frac{c_j}{n^j}+O_M(n^{-M-1})\right),
\end{equation}
where the $c_j$ are uniquely determined rational numbers with $c_0=1$.
In particular,
\begin{align}\label{l7g:eq:cvalues}
 c_1&=-\frac{215}{1008},&c_2&=-\frac{1265}{290304},&
 c_3&=\frac{4683055}{877879296},\notag\\
 c_4&=\frac{924908071}{3539609321472},&
 c_5&=-\frac{6088275239005}{3567926196043776}.
\end{align}
An exact triangular algorithm computes every coefficient.
\end{theorem}
The theorem also evaluates a convergent boundary sum:
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:boundarysum}
 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{27^n}
 =\frac{3\Gamma(1/7)\Gamma(2/7)\Gamma(4/7)}{8\pi^2},
 \qquad C\Ast=\frac{3\sqrt3}{8\pi^{3/2}}.
\end{equation}
The gamma product in the boundary value cancels against its reciprocal
in the coefficient amplitude. Numerically
$C=0.0955223052681267146513079107870296256727946666510071798669\ldots$ and
$\Ast=1.221131097467566669284224585685993462375827056379\ldots$; the two
manuscripts' decimal values agree in every printed digit.

\begin{theorem}[Exact critical and subcritical sums; manuscript~56]\label{l7g:cm:thm:sums}
The generating function has the exact values
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:subcritical-sum}
 A(1/27)=\frac{3\G}{8\pi^2},\qquad
 A(1/125)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{125^n}
 =\frac{5\G}{16\pi^2}.
\end{equation}
Numerically,
$A(1/125)=1.017609247889638891070187154738327885313189213649\ldots$.
\end{theorem}
The first value is \eqref{l7g:eq:boundarysum}. The second is proved in
Section~\ref{l7g:cm:sec:functional} from the first and a functional
equation; it appears only in manuscript~56.

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{What is resolved} (manuscript~56).
The decimal constant in the OEIS asymptotic conjecture is exactly the
gamma quotient in \eqref{l7g:eq:C}. More is true: the complete expansion
exists to arbitrary algebraic order, all its normalized coefficients are
rational, the singular connection coefficient is explicit, and the
critical value of the generating function is a closed gamma product.
\end{resultbox}

\subsection{Immediate corollaries}
The all-orders form gives several useful consequences without further
modular input. The three corollaries below are manuscript~56's.

\begin{corollary}[Ratio asymptotics and eventual log-convexity]\label{l7g:cm:cor:ratio}
As $n\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:ratio}
 \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}
 =27\left(
 1-\frac{3}{2n}
 +\frac{2105}{1008n^2}
 -\frac{18815}{7056n^3}
 +\frac{3425255}{1053696n^4}
 +\bigO(n^{-5})\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently $a_{n+1}/a_n\to27$, and $(a_n)$ is strictly log-convex for
all sufficiently large $n$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{l7g:eq:dominant} into the quotient. The difference of
successive ratios is
\[
 \frac{a_{n+2}}{a_{n+1}}-\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}
 =\frac{81}{2n^2}+\bigO(n^{-3})>0
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$. Increasing ratios are equivalent to
strict log-convexity.
\end{proof}
Manuscript~58 proves the first three terms of \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:ratio} and
eventual log-convexity through second differences of $\log a_n$
(Section~\ref{l7g:sec:logratio}); the two increments are consistent,
$81/(2n^2)\div27=3/(2n^2)$.

\begin{corollary}[Root and logarithmic scales]\label{l7g:cm:cor:root}
With $\lambda=\log 27$,
\begin{align}
 \log a_n
 &=n\lambda-\frac32\log n+\log C
   -\frac{215}{1008n}+\bigO(n^{-2}),\label{l7g:cm:eq:log-asymp}\\
 a_n^{1/n}
 &=27\exp\!\left(
   \frac{\log C-\frac32\log n}{n}+\bigO(n^{-2})\right).\label{l7g:cm:eq:root}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}
The first line is the case $M=1$ of manuscript~58's logarithmic expansion
\eqref{l7g:eq:logexp}.

\begin{corollary}[Critical-tail asymptotics]\label{l7g:cm:cor:tail}
Let $\Sigma_N=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_n/27^n$ and $\nu=N+1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:tail}
 A(1/27)-\Sigma_N
 =C\left(
 2\nu^{-1/2}+\frac{541}{1512}\nu^{-3/2}
 +\frac{2411}{145152}\nu^{-5/2}
 +\bigO(\nu^{-7/2})\right).
\end{equation}
In particular, convergence at the critical point is only of order
$N^{-1/2}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Sum \eqref{l7g:eq:dominant} termwise and use the standard Hurwitz-zeta
expansion for $\zeta(s,N+1)$.
\end{proof}
\mergenote{The intake re-derived $541/1512$ and $2411/145152$ by the
Euler--Maclaurin formula, and checked that the partial sum through
$N=5000$ plus the three-term tail matches $3\G/(8\pi^2)$ to
$4.9\times10^{-16}$, consistent with the stated $\bigO(\nu^{-7/2})$.}

\begin{theorem}[An exact recessive companion; manuscript~58]\label{l7g:th:companion}
Let $b_0=0$, $b_1=1$, and impose \eqref{l7g:eq:rec} on $b_n$ for $n\ge1$.
Then the limit
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:L}
 L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b_n}{a_n}
 =0.46721176888437312394211005141309126172410854995971\ldots
\end{equation}
exists and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:bracket}
 \frac{b_{2m}}{a_{2m}}<L<\frac{b_{2m+1}}{a_{2m+1}},\qquad m\ge0.
\end{equation}
For $e_n=b_n-La_n$ and every fixed $M\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:minimal}
 e_n=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{\sqrt3}{56\pi C}\,n^{-3/2}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^M\frac{d_j}{n^j}+O_M(n^{-M-1})\right),
\end{equation}
where $d_0=1$ and
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:dvalues}
 d_1=-\frac{57}{112},\quad d_2=\frac{655}{3584},\quad
 d_3=-\frac{13365}{401408},\quad d_4=-\frac{3627123}{179830784}.
\end{equation}
The $d_j$ are rational and computable to arbitrary order. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:CFerror}
 L-\frac{b_n}{a_n}\sim(-1)^n\frac{\sqrt3}{56\pi C^2}\,27^{-n}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The two scales are $27^nn^{-3/2}$ and $(-1)^nn^{-3/2}$. The second is
exponentially small \emph{relative} to the first, not in absolute
magnitude. We do not assign an unproved canonical subdominant coefficient
to the asymptotic expansion of $a_n$ itself.

\section{From the recurrence to a controlled singularity}\label{l7g:sec:singularity}
\subsection{Positivity, growth, and the differential equation}
The recurrence immediately gives $a_n>0$. Induction also gives
$a_n\le27^n$, since
\[
26n^2+13n+2+\frac{3(3n-1)(3n-2)}{27}
=27n^2+12n+\frac{20}{9}<27(n+1)^2.
\]
Thus $A$ is analytic in $|z|<\rho$. Multiplying the recurrence by $z^n$
and summing, including $a_1=2a_0$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:ODE}
 zD(z)A''+(1-39z-54z^2)A'-(2+6z)A=0,
 \qquad D(z)=(1-27z)(1+z).
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $z^n$ on the left is the recurrence residual, so the
analytic solution normalized by $A(0)=1$ is unique. Outside
$0,\rho,-1$, the equation is ordinary; analytic continuation along paths
avoiding these points follows from linear differential-equation theory.
(Manuscript~56 derives the same equation, written with
$zD(z)=z(1-26z-27z^2)$, in Appendix~\ref{l7g:app:derivations}. Its
positive singularity $\rho$ is separated from the other nonzero finite
singularity $-1$ by a factor of $27$ in modulus.)

\subsection{The local exponents}
In $s=1-27z$, the equation becomes
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:sODE}
 s(1-s)(28-s)y''+(14-43s+2s^2)y'-\frac{20-2s}{9}y=0.
\end{equation}
The indicial equation is $14\alpha(2\alpha-1)=0$. Its exponents $0,1/2$
have nonintegral difference, hence Frobenius theory gives a convergent
local expansion
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:puiseux}
 A(z)=U(s)+B\sqrt{s}\,V(s),\qquad V(0)=1,
\end{equation}
where $U,V$ are analytic at $0$. Branches are positive for $0<z<\rho$.
Below we show $B<0$, so the singularity is not removable.

The self-adjoint form provides a useful sign check:
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:flux}
 \bigl(z\sqrt{D(z)}A'(z)\bigr)'
   =\frac{2+6z}{\sqrt{D(z)}}A(z),\qquad 0<z<\rho.
\end{equation}
Frobenius theory makes $A(\rho)$ finite. Positivity of the right side
implies that the limiting flux is positive, whereas \eqref{l7g:eq:puiseux}
identifies it as $-\tfrac12\sqrt{28/27}\,B$.

There is no other singularity on $|z|=\rho$. The chosen solution extends
to a dented disk of radius greater than $\rho$, cut to the right from
$\rho$. This supplies the global analytic condition for singularity
analysis, not just a formal local expansion \cite[Chapter VI]{l7g:flajolet}.

\section{A rational certificate for the generating function}\label{l7g:sec:certificate}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:w}
 w=\sqrt{\frac{1-27z}{1+z}},\qquad z=\frac{1-w^2}{27+w^2}.
\end{equation}
The points $0,\rho$ correspond to $w=1,0$. Define
\begin{align}\label{l7g:eq:PTJ}
 T(w)&=15w^2+96w+85,\notag\\
 P(w)&=\left(\frac{7(w^2+27)}{T(w)}\right)^{1/4},\notag\\
 J(w)&=\frac{1728(1-w)^7(1+w)}{(w^2+27)T(w)^3},\qquad
 F(x)=\hyp{1/12}{5/12}{1}{x}.
\end{align}
Fourth roots are positive on $[0,1]$.
\begin{proposition}\label{l7g:prop:pullback}
As analytic germs near $w=1$, and throughout $0\le w\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:GF}
 A\!\left(\frac{1-w^2}{27+w^2}\right)=P(w)F(J(w)).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The equation for $y(w)=A(z(w))$ is $y''+p y'+q y=0$, where
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:wpq}
 p=\frac{2w}{(w-1)(w+1)},\qquad
 q=-\frac{16(w^2-15)}{(w-1)(w+1)(w^2+27)^2}.
\end{equation}
Let $h=P'/P=\tfrac14(2w/(w^2+27)-T'/T)$. The hypergeometric equation
\cite[\S15.10]{l7g:dlmf} is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:hypODE}
 x(1-x)F''+(1-\tfrac32x)F'-\tfrac5{144}F=0.
\end{equation}
Substitution of $y=PF(J)$, followed by eliminating $F''$, reduces the
claim to two rational identities:
\begin{align}\label{l7g:eq:certs}
 J''+(2h+p)J'+(J')^2\frac{3J/2-1}{J(1-J)}&=0,\notag\\
 h'+h^2+ph+q+\frac5{144}\frac{(J')^2}{J(1-J)}&=0.
\end{align}
Clearing denominators and expanding proves both; the attached code
checks them identically in $\Q(w)$. This is an exact certificate rather
than a fit to finitely many coefficients.
At $w=1$, $P=1$, $J=0$, and $z'(1)\ne0$. The right side is analytic
there and normalized to $1$. Uniqueness at $z=0$ proves the germ identity.
For $0\le w<1$,
\[
\frac{J'}J=-\frac7{1-w}+\frac1{1+w}-\frac{2w}{w^2+27}
                       -3\frac{30w+96}{T(w)}<0.
\]
Thus $0\le J(w)\le J(0)=64/85^3<1$, and every expression continues
regularly along the interval, proving the full claim.
\end{proof}
Differentiation at a singular $z$-value has now become ordinary
differentiation at the regular parameter value $w=0$.

\section{Evaluating the connection constant (first proof)}\label{l7g:sec:evaluation}
\subsection{An explicit hypergeometric expression}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:q0}
 q_0=\frac{64}{85^3},\qquad P_0=\left(\frac{189}{85}\right)^{1/4}.
\end{equation}
As $s\to0$,
\[
 w=\sqrt{\frac{27s}{28-s}}
   =\sqrt{\frac{27}{28}}\sqrt s\,(1+O(s)).
\]
At $w=0$ we have $P'/P_0=-24/85$ and $J'/q_0=-798/85$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:B}
 B=-\sqrt{\frac{27}{28}}\frac{P_0}{85}
           \bigl(24F(q_0)+798q_0F'(q_0)\bigr)<0.
\end{equation}
Positivity follows from the positive hypergeometric coefficients.
Coefficient transfer for $\sqrt{1-27z}$ and
$\Gamma(-1/2)=-2\sqrt\pi$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:Chyper}
 C=-\frac{B}{2\sqrt\pi}
 =\sqrt{\frac{27}{28}}\frac{P_0}{170\sqrt\pi}
      \bigl(24F(q_0)+798q_0F'(q_0)\bigr).
\end{equation}
This already gives an explicit positive constant. The following
special-value reductions explain its unexpectedly simple form.

\subsection{Ramanujan's identity removes the derivative}
The relevant Clausen identity is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:Clausen}
 F(q)^2=H(q):=\sum_{n\ge0}
      \frac{(1/6)_n(1/2)_n(5/6)_n}{(n!)^3}q^n.
\end{equation}
It follows directly from differential equations: the symmetric square
of \eqref{l7g:eq:hypODE} is
\[
 [\tht^3-q(\tht+1/6)(\tht+1/2)(\tht+5/6)]H=0,
 \qquad\tht=q\frac{d}{dq},
\]
whose analytic solution with $H(0)=1$ is unique. The rational
coefficients of this symmetric-square identity are checked in the code.

The classical level-seven Ramanujan identity, reproduced and obtained
by the construction in \cite[Eq.~(98)]{l7g:hemmecke}, is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:Ramanujan}
 \sum_{n\ge0}(133n+8)
   \frac{(1/6)_n(1/2)_n(5/6)_n}{(n!)^3}q_0^n
      =\frac{85\sqrt{255}}{54\pi}.
\end{equation}
The source uses the coefficient $(6n)!/((3n)!(n!)^3)$, which is $1728^n$
times the coefficient here, and $1728/255^3=64/85^3$. This normalization
check is essential. Equation \eqref{l7g:eq:Ramanujan} is a cited established
identity, not an equality inferred from numerical recognition.
Using \eqref{l7g:eq:Clausen}, it becomes
\[
 8F(q_0)^2+266q_0F(q_0)F'(q_0)=\frac{85\sqrt{255}}{54\pi}.
\]
Multiply by $3/F(q_0)$ and substitute into \eqref{l7g:eq:Chyper}. Since
$\Ast=P_0F(q_0)$ by Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:pullback}, this yields
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:Creciprocal}
 C=\frac{3\sqrt3}{8\pi^{3/2}P_0F(q_0)}
   =\frac{3\sqrt3}{8\pi^{3/2}\Ast}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{An elliptic period supplies the gamma product}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:m7}
 m_7=\frac{8-3\sqrt7}{16},\qquad k_7=\sqrt{m_7},\qquad
 K(k)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\theta}}.
\end{equation}
Here $K$ takes the \emph{modulus} $k$, not the parameter $m=k^2$.
The classical singular-period evaluation is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:Kgamma}
 K(k_7)=\frac{\G}{4\pi7^{1/4}}.
\end{equation}
This is an established complex-multiplication special value
\cite{l7g:borweinzucker,l7g:beta}, not a new identity claimed here.
Appendix~\ref{l7g:app:beta} explicitly reduces the beta-function form in
Zucker's table to this normalization.

The required hypergeometric transformation is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:elliptictransform}
 F\!\left(\frac{27m^2(1-m)^2}{4(1-m+m^2)^3}\right)
 =(1-m+m^2)^{1/4}\hyp{1/2}{1/2}{1}{m}.
\end{equation}
For a direct verification put $Q(m)=1-m+m^2$ and
$j(m)=27m^2(1-m)^2/(4Q(m)^3)$. Then $Q^{-1/4}F(j)$ satisfies
\[
 m(1-m)y''+(1-2m)y'-\tfrac14y=0.
\]
Substituting and clearing denominators proves two rational identities,
exactly as in \eqref{l7g:eq:certs}. Both solutions are analytic at $0$ and
equal to $1$, proving the transformation near $0$. Continuation on
$[0,m_7]$ proves the value used here. The program verifies both identities.

At $m=m_7$,
\[
 m_7(1-m_7)=1/256,\qquad Q(m_7)=255/256,\qquad j(m_7)=q_0.
\]
Euler's integral gives
${}_2F_1(1/2,1/2;1;m_7)=2K(k_7)/\pi$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
 \Ast=P_0F(q_0)
 &=\left(\frac{189}{85}\frac{255}{256}\right)^{1/4}
                       \frac{2K(k_7)}\pi\\
 &=\frac{3\,7^{1/4}}4\frac2\pi
                       \frac{\G}{4\pi7^{1/4}}
 =\frac{3\G}{8\pi^2}.
\end{align*}
Combining this with \eqref{l7g:eq:Creciprocal} proves $C=\sqrt{3\pi}/\G$.
Since the coefficients are positive, the finite limit at $\rho$ also
gives the boundary sum \eqref{l7g:eq:boundarysum}, by monotone convergence.

\begin{remark}[Dependencies of the exact evaluation]
The two non-elementary special values are \eqref{l7g:eq:Ramanujan} and
\eqref{l7g:eq:Kgamma}; both are classical inputs. The recurrence
manipulations, rational pullbacks, connection-coefficient extraction,
and asymptotic deductions are proved here. We do not purport to give a
new derivation of Chowla--Selberg theory or of Ramanujan's general
$1/\pi$ formulas.
\end{remark}

\section{Second proof: the square sequence and a CM evaluation (manuscript 56)}\label{l7g:sec:second}
This section is manuscript~56's proof of $\Ast=3\G/(8\pi^2)$ and
$C=\sqrt{3\pi}/\G$, rewritten in this report's notation
(Section~\ref{l7g:sec:notation}). It is independent of
Sections~\ref{l7g:sec:certificate} and \ref{l7g:sec:evaluation}: it uses
neither the ${}_2F_1$ pullback, nor Ramanujan's series, nor the elliptic
period. Its classical inputs are O'Brien's modular parametrization, the
Chowla--Selberg formula at discriminant $-7$, and Hirschhorn's asymptotic
for A183204, whose leading constant it re-derives.

The proof combines four structures already present around this
level-seven sequence: the square identity $A(z)^2=\sum t_nz^n$ with the
Ap\'ery-like sequence A183204, a one-dimensional Laplace analysis of the
binomial sum for $t_n$, O'Brien's modular parametrization, and a
Chowla--Selberg evaluation of a Dedekind eta value of discriminant $-7$.
They come from two apparently different descriptions of the same object:
a square root of an Ap\'ery-like generating function and a modular
parametrization on $X_0(7)$.

\subsection{The square identity}\label{l7g:cm:sec:square}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:Gdef}
 A(z)^2=\sum_{n\ge0}t_nz^n,\qquad t_n=a_n^{\langle2\rangle}.
\end{equation}
O'Brien's level-seven construction, equivalently the relation recorded in
OEIS, identifies $(t_n)$ with A183204, one of the sporadic Ap\'ery-like
sequences \cite{l7g:cooper}. (Manuscript~58 checks the identification
through the symmetric-square equation \eqref{l7g:eq:squareODE}.) Its
coefficients have the finite binomial sum
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:t-sum}
 t_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}
 \binom{n}{k}^{\!2}\binom{2k}{n}\binom{n+k}{k},
\end{equation}
where terms with $2k<n$ vanish. The initial values are
\[
 1,4,48,760,13840,273504,\ldots.
\]
\mergenote{Manuscript~58 checks the convolution against the different
binomial form
$\sum_k\binom nk^2\binom{n+k}{n}\binom{2n-k}{n}$; the intake checked that
both forms equal $[z^n]A(z)^2$ for $n\le40$.}
Hirschhorn proved a complete asymptotic expansion for this sequence
\cite{l7g:hirschhorn}. We rederive its leading constant in
Section~\ref{l7g:cm:sec:laplace}, because that constant is the bridge from
the known square to the previously unknown square root.

\subsection{The modular parametrization}
Let $\eta$ denote Dedekind's eta function \cite[Ch.~23]{l7g:dlmf},
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:eta}
 \eta(\tau)=\ee^{\pi\ii\tau/12}
 \prod_{m\ge1}\bigl(1-\ee^{2\pi\ii m\tau}\bigr),
 \qquad \Im\tau>0.
\end{equation}
O'Brien's parametrization may be written
\begin{align}
 w_7(\tau)&=\left(\frac{\eta(7\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^4,
 \label{l7g:cm:eq:w7}\\
 X_7(\tau)&=\frac{w_7(\tau)}{1+13w_7(\tau)+49w_7(\tau)^2},
 \label{l7g:cm:eq:X7}\\
 X_7(\tau)&=
 \left(\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(7\tau)}{z_7(\tau)}\right)^3,
 \qquad A(X_7(\tau))=z_7(\tau).
 \label{l7g:cm:eq:z7}
\end{align}
The last identity is initially a $q$-series identity near the cusp and
then extends by analytic continuation. It is the crucial reason the
critical value of $A$ can be reduced to a CM eta product.

\subsection{The square sequence by Laplace's method}\label{l7g:cm:sec:laplace}
We now obtain the leading asymptotic of $t_n$ directly from
\eqref{l7g:cm:eq:t-sum}. This gives a self-contained derivation of the one
published constant from which the new constant will be extracted.

For $\xi\in(1/2,1)$ and $k=\xi n$, Stirling's formula gives, uniformly on
closed subintervals of $(1/2,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:laplace-term}
 \binom{n}{k}^{\!2}\binom{2k}{n}\binom{n+k}{k}
 =n^{-2}\kappa(\xi)\ee^{n\Phi(\xi)}\bigl(1+\bigO(n^{-1})\bigr),
\end{equation}
where, with $\Ent(u)=-u\log u-(1-u)\log(1-u)$,
\begin{align}
 \Phi(\xi)
 &=2\Ent(\xi)+2\xi\Ent\!\left(\frac1{2\xi}\right)
 +(1+\xi)\Ent\!\left(\frac{\xi}{1+\xi}\right),\label{l7g:cm:eq:Phi}\\
 \kappa(\xi)
 &=\frac{1}{2\pi^2\xi(1-\xi)}
 \sqrt{\frac{1+\xi}{2(2\xi-1)}}.\label{l7g:cm:eq:g}
\end{align}
A convenient derivative is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:Phi-prime}
 \Phi'(\xi)=2\log(1-\xi)+\log\!\left(4+\frac4\xi\right)
 -2\log(2\xi-1).
\end{equation}
Furthermore,
\[
 \Phi''(\xi)=-\frac2{1-\xi}-\frac1{\xi(1+\xi)}-\frac4{2\xi-1}<0,
\]
so the critical point is the unique global maximum. Direct substitution
gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:saddle-data}
 \xi_0=\frac45,
 \qquad \ee^{\Phi(\xi_0)}=27,
 \qquad \Phi''(\xi_0)=-\frac{625}{36},
 \qquad \kappa(\xi_0)=\frac{25\sqrt6}{16\pi^2}.
\end{equation}
The endpoint ranges are exponentially smaller than the saddle range.
Replacing the sum by a Riemann sum and applying the Gaussian form of
Laplace's method therefore yields
\begin{align}
 t_n
 &\sim 27^n n^{-3/2}\kappa(\xi_0)
 \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{-\Phi''(\xi_0)}}\notag\\
 &=\Ct\,27^n n^{-3/2},
 \qquad
 \Ct=\frac{3\sqrt3}{4\pi^{3/2}}
 =\frac14\left(\frac3\pi\right)^{3/2}.
 \label{l7g:cm:eq:t-leading}
\end{align}
This agrees with Hirschhorn's theorem, whose further terms begin
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:t-full-known}
 t_n\sim\Ct 27^n n^{-3/2}
 \left(1-\frac{65}{144n}
 +\frac{3865}{41472n^2}
 +\frac{111727}{17915904n^3}+\cdots\right).
\end{equation}
The first correction $-65/144$ is also the case $m=2$ of manuscript~58's
convolution theorem, \eqref{l7g:eq:183204}; the two manuscripts agree.

\begin{remark}[Why the square alone does not determine the answer]
From $A^2=\sum t_nz^n$, the asymptotic constant of $t_n$ is not simply the
square of the constant of $a_n$. Both series converge at $z=\rho$, and
their dominant singularity is additive rather than multiplicative:
$A=A(\rho)+B\sqrt{1-z/\rho}+\cdots$. Thus the singular coefficient of
$A^2$ is $2A(\rho)B$. The missing datum is the exact boundary value
$A(\rho)$, which is evaluated next.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Fricke fixed point}\label{l7g:cm:sec:CM}
Take
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:taustar}
 \tau_*=\frac{\ii}{\sqrt7}.
\end{equation}
Dedekind's inversion law \cite[Ch.~3]{l7g:apostol}
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:eta-inversion}
 \eta(-1/\tau)=\sqrt{-\ii\tau}\,\eta(\tau)
\end{equation}
shows that
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:eta-inv-special}
 \eta(\ii\sqrt7)=7^{-1/4}\eta(\ii/\sqrt7).
\end{equation}
It follows from \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:w7} that $w_7(\tau_*)=1/7$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:X-critical}
 X_7(\tau_*)=
 \frac{1/7}{1+13/7+1}=\frac1{27}=\rho.
\end{equation}
All relevant quantities are positive on the imaginary axis. Therefore
\eqref{l7g:cm:eq:z7} gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:F-eta-product}
 A(\rho)=z_7(\tau_*)
 =3\eta(\ii/\sqrt7)\eta(\ii\sqrt7).
\end{equation}
The remaining task is an exact evaluation of this product.

\subsection{Chowla--Selberg at discriminant \texorpdfstring{$-7$}{-7}}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:tau0}
 \tau_0=\frac{1+\ii\sqrt7}{2}.
\end{equation}
The class number of discriminant $-7$ is one \cite{l7g:cox}. Specializing
the Chowla--Selberg formula in the form proved by van der Poorten and
Williams \cite{l7g:vdpw} gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:CS-special}
 |\eta(\tau_0)|
 =(14\pi)^{-1/4}
 \left(
 \frac{\Gamma(1/7)\Gamma(2/7)\Gamma(4/7)}
 {\Gamma(3/7)\Gamma(5/7)\Gamma(6/7)}
 \right)^{1/4}.
\end{equation}
Reflection \cite[Ch.~5]{l7g:dlmf} and the classical trigonometric product
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:sin-product}
 \sin\frac\pi7\sin\frac{2\pi}7\sin\frac{3\pi}7
 =\frac{\sqrt7}{8}
\end{equation}
imply
\[
 \G\,\Gamma(3/7)\Gamma(5/7)\Gamma(6/7)
 =\frac{8\pi^3}{\sqrt7}.
\]
Consequently \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:CS-special} simplifies to
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:eta-tau0}
 |\eta(\tau_0)|=\frac{\sqrt{\G}}{2\,7^{1/8}\pi}.
\end{equation}

We still need to move from $\tau_0$ to $\ii\sqrt7$. Introduce the Weber
function
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:f2}
 \mathfrak f_2(\tau)=\sqrt2\,\frac{\eta(2\tau)}{\eta(\tau)},
 \qquad
 j(\tau)=\frac{(\mathfrak f_2(\tau)^{24}+16)^3}
 {\mathfrak f_2(\tau)^{24}}.
\end{equation}
The Hilbert class polynomial of discriminant $-7$ is $X+3375$
\cite{l7g:cox}, so $j(\tau_0)=-3375$. Setting
$\mathfrak u=\mathfrak f_2(\tau_0)^{24}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:u-factor}
 (\mathfrak u+16)^3+3375\mathfrak u
 =(\mathfrak u+1)(\mathfrak u^2+47\mathfrak u+4096)=0.
\end{equation}
Here the nome is $\ee^{2\pi\ii\tau_0}=-\ee^{-\pi\sqrt7}$, and the product
definition of $\eta$ shows that $\mathfrak u$ is a negative real number.
The two roots of the quadratic factor in \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:u-factor} are
nonreal. Hence $\mathfrak u=-1$ and $|\mathfrak f_2(\tau_0)|=1$. Since
$2\tau_0=1+\ii\sqrt7$ and $|\eta(\tau+1)|=|\eta(\tau)|$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:eta-isqrt7}
 \eta(\ii\sqrt7)=\frac{|\eta(\tau_0)|}{\sqrt2}
 =\frac{\sqrt{\G}}
 {2\sqrt2\,7^{1/8}\pi}.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:eta-inv-special} and \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:eta-isqrt7},
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:eta-product-exact}
 \eta(\ii/\sqrt7)\eta(\ii\sqrt7)
 =7^{1/4}\eta(\ii\sqrt7)^2
 =\frac{\G}{8\pi^2}.
\end{equation}
Finally, \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:F-eta-product} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:F0-final}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 A(1/27)=\frac{3\G}{8\pi^2}.}
\end{equation}
This is the second proof of $A(\rho)=\Ast$ in Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:main}.

\subsection{A second exact series from the functional equation}\label{l7g:cm:sec:functional}
The square-root generating function satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:functional}
 \frac1{1+4y}A\!\left(\frac{y}{(1+4y)^3}\right)
 =\frac1{1+2y}A\!\left(\frac{y^2}{(1+2y)^3}\right).
\end{equation}
This follows either from O'Brien's modular equation or by taking the
branch equal to $1$ at the origin in the corresponding identity for
$A^2$. At $y=1/8$, the two arguments are $1/27$ and $1/125$, and
\eqref{l7g:cm:eq:functional} reads
\[
 \frac23A(1/27)=\frac45A(1/125).
\]
Together with \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:F0-final}, this proves the second identity
of Theorem~\ref{l7g:cm:thm:sums}.

\mergenote{Provenance of \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:functional}. Manuscript~56
derives it from O'Brien's modular equation (or from the identity for
$A^2$) and uses it only here. Manuscript~58 mentions, without using it, a
functional equation added to the OEIS entry in 2026 (August 2026, in its

increase within $[0,\rho]$ and reach $1/27$ and $1/125$ at $y=1/8$.}

\subsection{The singular connection coefficient}\label{l7g:cm:sec:connection}
Because the exponents at $s=0$ are $0$ and $1/2$, the local form of $A$ is
\eqref{l7g:eq:puiseux}, $A(z)=U(s)+B\sqrt{s}\,V(s)$, with $U(0)=A(\rho)$ and
$V(0)=1$. The coefficient $B$ is nonzero: otherwise $A$, and hence $A^2$,
would be analytic at $1/27$, contradicting \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:t-leading}.

Squaring \eqref{l7g:eq:puiseux},
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:G-local}
 A(z)^2=A(\rho)^2+2A(\rho)Bs^{1/2}+\bigO(s).
\end{equation}
The transfer formula \eqref{l7g:eq:transferexact} at exponent $1/2$
\cite[Ch.~VI]{l7g:flajolet},
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:transfer-basic}
 [z^n](1-27z)^{1/2}
 \sim-\frac{27^n}{2\sqrt\pi\,n^{3/2}},
\end{equation}
then shows that the constant in \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:t-leading} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:Ct-relation}
 \Ct=-\frac{2A(\rho)B}{2\sqrt\pi}
 =-\frac{A(\rho)B}{\sqrt\pi}.
\end{equation}
(The transfer from the local expansion of $A^2$ to $t_n$ uses the same
dented-disk continuation as in Section~\ref{l7g:sec:singularity}; see the
merge note in Section~\ref{l7g:sec:transfer}.)

\section{All orders: existence, remainders, and coefficients}\label{l7g:sec:allorders}
\subsection{The convergent local expansion}
Write $V(s)=\sum_{j\ge0}v_js^j$, $v_0=1$, $v_{-1}=0$.
Substitution of $s^{1/2}V(s)$ into \eqref{l7g:eq:sODE} gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:vrec}
\begin{split}
28j(j+\tfrac12)v_j={}&
 \left(29(j-\tfrac12)^2+14(j-\tfrac12)+\frac{20}{9}\right)v_{j-1}\\
 &-\left((j-\tfrac32)^2+(j-\tfrac32)+\frac29\right)v_{j-2},\qquad j\ge1.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Thus all $v_j$ are rational, with $v_1=593/1512$. Frobenius theory makes
this a \emph{convergent local} power series, not merely a formal solution
at infinity.

\end{equation}
and lists

 +\frac{7493}{31104}(1-27z)^2+\cdots\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Transfer to coefficients}\label{l7g:sec:transfer}
For nonintegral $\alpha$, the exact coefficient formula is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:transferexact}
 [z^n](1-27z)^\alpha
 =27^n\frac{\Gamma(n-\alpha)}{\Gamma(-\alpha)\Gamma(n+1)}.
\end{equation}
Apply it to $B\sum_{j=0}^M v_j(1-27z)^{j+1/2}$ and expand each gamma
ratio \cite[\S5.11]{l7g:dlmf}. Singularity analysis in the dented disk from
Section~\ref{l7g:sec:singularity} transfers the remainder. The locally
analytic part contributes no algebraic terms from this singularity.
Equivalently one may subtract arbitrarily many Taylor terms of $U$ before
applying the transfer theorem. The first omitted nonintegral power is
$(1-27z)^{M+3/2}$, and its coefficient is $O(27^nn^{-M-5/2})$. This proves
\eqref{l7g:eq:dominant} for every fixed $M$.

\subsection{A triangular recurrence at infinity}
For efficient computation insert $u_n=r^nn^{-3/2}S(1/n)$ in the
recurrence, with $r\in\{27,-1\}$. The corresponding formal operator is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:formalE}
\begin{split}
 \mathcal E_r[S](t)={}&r(1+t)^{1/2}S\!\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right)
 -(26+13t+2t^2)S(t)\\
 &-\frac{27-27t+6t^2}{r}(1-t)^{-3/2}
                  S\!\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The characteristic equation is $r^2-26r-27=0$, with roots $27,-1$.
\begin{proposition}[Exact coefficient algorithm]\label{l7g:prop:coeffalgo}
For either root there is a unique formal series
$S_r(t)=1+\sum_{j\ge1}\gamma_jt^j$ with $\mathcal E_r[S_r]=0$.
Writing $S_{m-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\gamma_jt^j$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:triangular}
 \gamma_m=\frac{[t^{m+1}]\mathcal E_r[S_{m-1}](t)}{m(r+27/r)},\qquad m\ge1.
\end{equation}
All coefficients are rational; the denominators in the recursion are
$28m$ and $-28m$, respectively.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A newly inserted $\gamma_mt^m$ contributes zero in order $m$, by the
characteristic equation. Its coefficient in order $m+1$ is
\[
 r(\tfrac12-m)-13-\frac{27}{r}(\tfrac12+m)=-m(r+27/r).
\]
This is nonzero, giving the assertion inductively. At the first two
orders the normalization is compatible with both characteristic roots.
\end{proof}
For $r=27$, the analytic existence proof above identifies this formal
series with the asymptotic series of $a_n/C$. For $r=-1$,
Section~\ref{l7g:sec:recessive} will construct a genuine solution
realizing the series. Formal coefficient generation alone is not used to
assert existence of an asymptotic expansion.

Manuscript~56 derives the case $r=27$ independently: with
$S_{27}=\mathcal A$ in its notation, $\mathcal E_{27}[S]=0$ is its formal

 {175403678081120611363454976},\notag\\
 c_9={}&
 -\frac{541114776511779121907204365}
 {227323166793132312327037648896},\notag\\
 c_{10}={}&

\subsection{Logarithms, ratios, and convexity}\label{l7g:sec:logratio}
Let $\log S_{27}(t)=\sum_{j\ge1}\ell_jt^j$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:logexp}
 \log a_n=n\log27-\frac32\log n+\log C
             +\sum_{j=1}^M\frac{\ell_j}{n^j}+O_M(n^{-M-1}),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:ellvalues}
 \ell_1=-\frac{215}{1008},\quad\ell_2=-\frac{85}{3136},\quad
 \ell_3=\frac{99905}{85349376},\quad\ell_4=\frac{1657}{2458624}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:ratio}
 \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}
 =27\left(1-\frac{3}{2n}+\frac{2105}{1008n^2}+O(n^{-3})\right).
\end{equation}
(Corollary~\ref{l7g:cm:cor:ratio}, from manuscript~56, adds the terms in
$n^{-3}$ and $n^{-4}$.)
The sequence is strictly increasing, since the coefficient of $a_n$ in
\eqref{l7g:eq:rec} already exceeds $1$ for $n\ge1$, and $a_1>a_0$.
Taking sufficiently many terms before forming second differences gives
\[
 \log a_{n+1}-2\log a_n+\log a_{n-1}
 =\frac{3}{2n^2}+O(n^{-3})>0
\]
for sufficiently large $n$. Thus it is eventually strictly log-convex.
No explicit smallest threshold is asserted.

\section{A continued fraction and exact rational bracketing}\label{l7g:sec:continued}
\subsection{The discrete Wronskian}
Let $b_n$ be as in Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:companion} and set
$W_n=a_nb_{n+1}-a_{n+1}b_n$. Eliminating the two forward terms gives
\[
 W_n=-\frac{3(3n-1)(3n-2)}{(n+1)^2}W_{n-1},\qquad W_0=1.
\]
Grouping factors by residue modulo $3$ yields the exact formula
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:Wexact}
 W_n=(-1)^n\frac{(3n)!}{n!(n+1)!^2}.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:delta}
 \delta_n:=\frac{b_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}-\frac{b_n}{a_n}
 =(-1)^n\frac{(3n)!}{n!(n+1)!^2a_na_{n+1}}.
\end{equation}
Its magnitude decreases strictly because
\[
 \left|\frac{\delta_{n+1}}{\delta_n}\right|
 =\frac{3(3n+2)(3n+1)a_n}{(n+2)^2a_{n+2}}<1.
\]
The numerator is one positive summand in the recurrence for the
denominator. The ratio tends to $1/27$ by Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:main}.
Thus $\sum\delta_n$ converges absolutely, and its alternating tails
prove the existence of $L$ and the brackets \eqref{l7g:eq:bracket}.
Even approximants increase and odd approximants decrease; consecutive
ones enclose $L$ with the exact width
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:width}
 \left|\frac{b_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}-\frac{b_n}{a_n}\right|
 =\frac{(3n)!}{n!(n+1)!^2a_na_{n+1}}.
\end{equation}
No unknown constants enter this stopping criterion.

\subsection{Polynomial continued-fraction coefficients}
The rescaled sequences $\mathcal H_n=(n!)^2a_n$ and
$\mathcal K_n=(n!)^2b_n$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:integerrec}
 u_{n+1}=P_nu_n+Q_nu_{n-1},\qquad
 P_n=26n^2+13n+2,\quad Q_n=3n^2(3n-1)(3n-2),
\end{equation}
with initial pairs $(1,2)$ and $(0,1)$. Their integrality follows directly
from this recurrence, independently of integrality of the unscaled
sequence. The continuant recurrence gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:CF}
 L=\cfrac1{2+\cfrac6{41+\cfrac{240}{132+\cfrac{1512}{275+\ddots}}}}.
\end{equation}
The convergent with $n$ denominator levels is
$\mathcal K_n/\mathcal H_n=b_n/a_n$.

\begin{remark}[The initial relation matters]
The companion satisfies the recurrence only for $n\ge1$;
$b_1=2b_0$ is false. Its generating function
$\mathcal B(z)=\sum b_nz^n$ therefore satisfies \eqref{l7g:eq:ODE} with right
side $1$, not $0$. The same holds for $\mathcal B-LA$.
These must not be called second analytic solutions of the homogeneous
equation at $0$. The two-dimensional solution space here consists of
sequences satisfying the recurrence for $n\ge1$.
\end{remark}

\section{The recessive scale and its full expansion}\label{l7g:sec:recessive}
Stirling's formula in \eqref{l7g:eq:Wexact} gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:Wasym}
 W_n=(-1)^n\frac{\sqrt3}{2\pi}\frac{27^n}{n^3}(1+O(n^{-1})).
\end{equation}
Together with the dominant asymptotic this yields
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:deltaasym}
 \delta_n=(-1)^n\frac{\sqrt3}{54\pi C^2}\,27^{-n}(1+O(n^{-1})).
\end{equation}
The exact tail identities are
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:etail}
 L-\frac{b_n}{a_n}=\sum_{j\ge0}\delta_{n+j},\qquad
 e_n=-a_n\sum_{j\ge0}\delta_{n+j}.
\end{equation}
Since $\sum_{j\ge0}(-1/27)^j=27/28$, the leading terms of
\eqref{l7g:eq:CFerror} and \eqref{l7g:eq:minimal} follow. The recessive amplitude
can also be written
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:Dgamma}
 D_{\min}=\frac{\sqrt3}{56\pi C}=\frac{\G}{56\pi^{3/2}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{A remainder proof for the geometric tail}
The full Stirling expansion and Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:main} imply, for each
fixed $M$,
\[
 \delta_n=(-1)^nK_0\,27^{-n}
 \left(\sum_{k=0}^M\eta_kn^{-k}+O_M(n^{-M-1})\right),
 \qquad K_0=\frac{\sqrt3}{54\pi C^2},\quad\eta_0=1,
\]
where all $\eta_k$ are rational. The error bound is valid for all
sufficiently large integer arguments, hence also at $n+j$ uniformly in
$j\ge0$, with a bound by a constant times $27^{-j}n^{-M-1}$ after
factoring out $27^{-n}$.
Expand $(n+j)^{-k}$ for $0\le j\le n/2$ by Taylor's formula, retaining
its bounded remainder. The range $j>n/2$ is exponentially small, even
with any fixed polynomial weight. Every coefficient involves moments
\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}j^h(-1/27)^j,
\]
which are rational: apply $(q\,d/dq)^h$ to $1/(1-q)$ and set $q=-1/27$.
Thus the tail in \eqref{l7g:eq:etail} has an expansion to every fixed order
with a controlled error and rational normalized coefficients.
Multiplication by the expansion of $a_n$ proves existence of
\eqref{l7g:eq:minimal}. Since $e_n$ satisfies the recurrence,
Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:coeffalgo} with $r=-1$ proves that its coefficients
are precisely the $d_j$ already given. This completes the proof of
Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:companion}.

\subsection{What the two-scale expansion does and does not say}
Because $W_n\ne0$, $a_n$ and $e_n$ form an exact basis of the recurrence
solutions for $n\ge1$. Every solution has a unique form
$u_n=\alpha a_n+\beta e_n$. In this specified basis its two asymptotic
scales are
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:twoscale}
\begin{split}
 u_n\sim{}&\alpha C\,27^nn^{-3/2}\sum_{j\ge0}c_jn^{-j}\\
          &+\beta(-1)^{n+1}D_{\min}n^{-3/2}\sum_{j\ge0}d_jn^{-j}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Each exact basis sequence has its own fixed-order expansion. A finite
truncation of the dominant expansion generally has an error far larger
than the entire recessive term. Equation \eqref{l7g:eq:twoscale} is therefore
not a claim that two finite sums resolve exponentially small effects in
a generic solution. A canonical exponentially improved expansion of
$a_n$ alone would require additional summation or Stokes data; that
question is left open.

\section{Convolution powers and A183204}\label{l7g:sec:convolution}

For a fixed positive integer $m$, let
\[
 a_n^{\langle m\rangle}=[z^n]A(z)^m
 =\sum_{n_1+\cdots+n_m=n}a_{n_1}\cdots a_{n_m}.
\]
The exact boundary value controls this family as well as the original
sequence.

\begin{theorem}[Convolution asymptotics; manuscript~58]\label{l7g:th:convolution}
For every fixed $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:convmain}
 a_n^{\langle m\rangle}
 =m\Ast^{m-1}C\frac{27^n}{n^{3/2}}
 \left(1+\frac{c_1^{\langle m\rangle}}n+O_m(n^{-2})\right),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:convc1}
 c_1^{\langle m\rangle}
 =-\frac{215}{1008}-\frac5{21}(m-1)
   -\pi\frac{C^2}{\Ast^2}(m-1)(m-2).
\end{equation}
There is a computable expansion to every fixed order, and all its
normalized coefficients lie in $\Q(\pi C^2/\Ast^2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The analytic part of \eqref{l7g:eq:puiseux} satisfies
\[
 U(s)=\Ast\left(1+\frac{10}{63}s+O(s^2)\right),
\]
as the constant coefficient of \eqref{l7g:eq:sODE} shows. Its subsequent
normalized coefficients are rational. The singular part is
$B\sqrt{s}(1+v_1s+\cdots)$, with $B=-2\sqrt\pi C$ and
$v_1=593/1512$. In the $m$th power the coefficient of $\sqrt{s}$ is
$m\Ast^{m-1}B$. Relative to that coefficient, the coefficient of
$s^{3/2}$ is
\[
 v_1+(m-1)\frac{10}{63}
       +\frac{(m-1)(m-2)}6\frac{B^2}{\Ast^2}.
\]
Transfer as in Section~\ref{l7g:sec:allorders} gives \eqref{l7g:eq:convc1}.
At higher orders only odd powers of $B$ multiply half-integral powers of
$s$. After division by the leading singular coefficient, the remaining
expressions are polynomials in $B^2/\Ast^2=4\pi C^2/\Ast^2$ with rational
coefficients. The convergent Puiseux expansion and the same dented domain
provide the fixed-order remainder estimates.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[An explicit generator for every order; manuscript~32]\label{l7g:tr:rem:generator}
\writenote{Manuscript~32 proves Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:convolution} again
(its Theorem~7.1, with weight $k=m$) and makes the ``computable expansion
to every fixed order'' explicit. Notation is that of
Table~\ref{l7g:tr:tab:notation}.} Put
\[
 \qq=\frac{B^2}{\Ast^2}=\frac{4\pi C^2}{\Ast^2}=\frac{256\pi^6}{3\G^4}
 =0.0768944281311685853765\ldots,
\]
the quantity $B^2/\Ast^2$ of the proof above (it is \emph{not} the
hypergeometric argument $q_0=64/85^3$ of Section~\ref{l7g:sec:evaluation};
manuscript~32's replay output calls it \texttt{q0}). With
$U(s)=\Ast\widehat U(s)$, $\widehat U(0)=V(0)=1$, the coefficient of
$s^{j+1/2}$ in $A(z)^m=(\Ast\widehat U(s)+B\sqrt s\,V(s))^m$ is
$B_j^{\langle m\rangle}=m\Ast^{m-1}B\,\mathcal R_{m,j}$, where the binomial
theorem gives the finite sum
\[
 \mathcal R_{m,j}=\sum_{h=0}^{\min(j,\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor)}
 \frac1m\binom{m}{2h+1}\qq^{h}\,
 [s^{j-h}]\,\widehat U(s)^{m-2h-1}V(s)^{2h+1},
 \qquad \mathcal R_{m,0}=1.
\]
Transfer as in Section~\ref{l7g:sec:transfer} then gives, for every fixed
$M$,
\[
 a_n^{\langle m\rangle}=27^n\sum_{j=0}^{M}B_j^{\langle m\rangle}
 \frac{\Gamma(n-j-\frac12)}{\Gamma(-j-\frac12)\Gamma(n+1)}
 +O_{m,M}(27^nn^{-M-5/2}),
\]
and, with the gamma-ratio coefficients $\mathsf h_i$ of
\eqref{l7g:tr:eq:gammaratio},
\[
 c_i^{\langle m\rangle}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathcal R_{m,j}(-1)^j(\tfrac32)_j\,
 \mathsf h_{i-j}(-j-\tfrac12,1).
\]
All operations are finite and all exponents nonnegative integers, so
$c_i^{\langle m\rangle}$ lies in the polynomial ring $\Q[\qq]$, a sharper
form of the field statement of Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:convolution}. For
$i=1$, $\mathcal R_{m,1}=\frac{593}{1512}+\frac{10}{63}(m-1)
+\frac{(m-1)(m-2)}6\qq$ and $\mathsf h_1(-\frac12,1)=\frac38$, so
$c_1^{\langle m\rangle}=\frac38-\frac32\mathcal R_{m,1}$, which is

for $m\ge3$ the first correction is a polynomial of degree one in $\qq$.
For $m=2$ the leading constant and $c_1^{\langle2\rangle}=-65/144$ are

Manuscript~32 also records that each $a_n^{\langle m\rangle}$ is strictly
increasing in $n$: raising the index of the first factor maps the terms of

\end{remark}

For $m=2$ this is A183204, and the first two terms simplify to
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:183204}
 [z^n]A(z)^2=

The leading formula is already recorded in its OEIS entry

For an algebraic check of the generating-function identification, the

\begin{split}

$a=\lambda=\log27$, $\beta_{\mathrm{CTI}}=-3/2$, $q_j^{\mathrm{CTI}}=\ell_j$ of
Theorem \texttt{t2:thm:balanced-inverse} of the repository's volume
\emph{Combinatorial Transseries and Their Inverses} \cite{l7g:cti}: the
coefficients $q_1,q_2$ of \eqref{l7g:eq:qvalues} below are that theorem's
$d_1,d_2$ (checked by hand at intake). Neither manuscript cites it, and
this report claims no novelty for the inverse expansion. The rounding
proposition, Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:rounding}, is the sequence-specific
part; its conclusion has the form of the separation condition (part~2) of
Theorem \texttt{p0:thm:staircase} in the repository's canonical volume
\emph{Transseries and Inversion} \cite{l7g:tai}.}

Put $\lambda=\log27$ and $\beta=3/2$. The leading smooth model is
$Y=C e^{\lambda x}x^{-\beta}$. Its large positive solution is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:x0}
 x_0(Y)=-\frac{\beta}{\lambda}
 W_{-1}\!\left(-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}(C/Y)^{1/\beta}\right),
\end{equation}
where $W_{-1}$ is the real negative branch of Lambert's function.
The principal branch selects the small solution and is inappropriate
for inversion of the large-$n$ sequence \cite[\S4.13]{l7g:dlmf}.
(Manuscript~56 derives the same formula, raising the envelope to the
power $-2/3$.)

\subsection{A triangular inverse expansion}

Seek
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:inverse}
 x_M(Y)=x_0+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{q_j}{x_0^j}.
\end{equation}
Writing $\delta=x-x_0$, the formal equation determining the coefficients
is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:inverseeq}
 \lambda\delta-\beta\log(1+\delta/x_0)
       +\sum_{k\ge1}\ell_k(x_0+\delta)^{-k}=0.
\end{equation}
The newly introduced coefficient $q_j$ has multiplier $\lambda$, so
this is triangular at every order. The first coefficients are
\begin{align}\label{l7g:eq:qvalues}
 q_1&=-\frac{\ell_1}{\lambda}
       =\frac{215}{1008\lambda},\notag\\
 q_2&=\frac{\beta q_1-\ell_2}{\lambda}
       =\frac{215}{672\lambda^2}+\frac{85}{3136\lambda},\notag\\
 q_3&=\frac{\beta q_2+\ell_1q_1-\ell_3}{\lambda}.
\end{align}
All $q_j$ belong to $\Q(\lambda)$.

An expansion involving only logarithms follows by putting
$X=\log(Y/C)$:
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:pureloginverse}
\begin{split}
 x(Y)={}&\frac1\lambda\left\{
 X+\beta\log\frac X\lambda
 +\frac{\beta^2\log(X/\lambda)-\lambda\ell_1}{X}\right\}\\
 &\hspace{2em}+O\!\left(\frac{(\log X)^2}{X^2}\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $x(Y)$ denotes the asymptotic inverse scale, not a previously
specified analytic interpolation of the integer sequence. Formula
\eqref{l7g:eq:x0} is often more accurate numerically because it retains the
entire leading model instead of truncating its logarithmic inversion.

\begin{remark}[Manuscript~56's form of the inverse]\label{l7g:cm:rem:inverse}
Expanding $W_{-1}$ at $0^-$, manuscript~56 records
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:cm:eq:inverse-expanded}
 x_0(Y)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(
 \log Y+\frac32\log\log Y
 -\frac32\log \lambda-\log C\right)
 +\bigO\!\left(\frac{\log\log Y}{\log Y}\right),
\end{equation}
consistent with the first two terms of \eqref{l7g:eq:pureloginverse}.
It notes that the rational correction series can be inserted and reverted
recursively to produce an arbitrarily long expansion in $1/\log Y$ with
nested powers of $\log\log Y$, and that for the discrete inverse
$N(Y)$ below, formula \eqref{l7g:eq:x0}, ``rounded and corrected by one or
two exact recurrence evaluations, gives an efficient certified locator''.
\mergenote{The last statement is a practical recipe, not a theorem: a
\emph{certified} locator needs explicit constants $K_M$, $Y_M$ in
Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:rounding}, which neither manuscript supplies
(manuscript~58 lists them as a research direction,
Section~\ref{l7g:sec:explicit}). Checking a candidate $n$ by exact
evaluation of $a_{n-1}<Y\le a_n$ is of course rigorous.}
\end{remark}

\subsection{A rigorous discrete statement}

Define
\[
 N(Y)=\min\{n\ge0:a_n\ge Y\}.
\]
The sequence is strictly increasing and unbounded, so this is well-defined
for $Y>1$. An inverse approximation cannot uniformly remove the
ambiguity when its value is exceptionally close to an integer.

\begin{proposition}[Rounding brackets]\label{l7g:prop:rounding}
For every fixed $M\ge0$ there exist $K_M>0$ and $Y_M$ such that, for
$Y\ge Y_M$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:rounding}
 \left\lceil x_M-K_Mx_M^{-M-1}\right\rceil
 \le N(Y)\le
 \left\lceil x_M+K_Mx_M^{-M-1}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
When the two ceilings coincide, they determine the discrete inverse
exactly. For $M=0$, $x_M$ means $x_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the smooth finite model
\[
 g_M(x)=\log C+\lambda x-\beta\log x+
                      \sum_{k=1}^{M}\ell_kx^{-k}.
\]
The all-orders theorem gives
$\log a_n=g_M(n)+O_M(n^{-M-1})$. Also
$g_M'(x)=\lambda+O(x^{-1})$, so it is bounded away from zero for large
$x$. Let $t_M$ be the large solution of $g_M(t_M)=\log Y$.
Formal substitution of \eqref{l7g:eq:inverse} into $g_M$, with ordinary
finite Taylor remainders, gives
$g_M(x_M)-\log Y=O_M(x_M^{-M-1})$. The mean value theorem therefore
implies $t_M-x_M=O_M(x_M^{-M-1})$.

For integers near $t_M$, comparison of $g_M(n)$ with $g_M(t_M)$,
using its derivative lower bound and the error in $\log a_n$, shows
that integers sufficiently below $t_M$ cannot reach $Y$, whereas
integers sufficiently above it do reach $Y$. Enlarge the constant to
absorb $t_M-x_M$, and use monotonicity of $a_n$ for all remaining
integers. This gives the two ceilings in \eqref{l7g:eq:rounding}.
\end{proof}

The proposition asserts the existence of fixed-order constants, not
explicit numerical values of $K_M$ or $Y_M$. Computing certified,
practical values is one of the research directions below. No claim
that $N(Y)=\lceil x_M(Y)\rceil$ holds uniformly is made.

\begin{remark}[Every fixed weight, and an implicit-function proof; manuscript~32]\label{l7g:tr:rem:ceiling}
\writenote{Manuscript~32 states the rounding brackets for every fixed
convolution weight $m$ (its Theorems~8.1 and~8.2, weight $k=m$), with
notation as in Table~\ref{l7g:tr:tab:notation}. For $m=1$ they are
Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:rounding}. The Lambert-$W_{-1}$ core and its
triangular corrections are, as for $m=1$, instances of the repository's
inversion apparatus: \texttt{t2:thm:balanced-inverse} of \cite{l7g:cti}, and
\texttt{p0:thm:lambert-core}, \texttt{p0:thm:perturbed-inversion} and
\texttt{p0:thm:staircase} of \cite{l7g:tai}. Neither manuscript~32 nor this
report claims novelty for them.}
Let $N^{\langle m\rangle}(Y)=\min\{n\ge0:a_n^{\langle m\rangle}\ge Y\}$,
well defined for every real $Y>0$ because $a_n^{\langle m\rangle}$ is
strictly increasing and unbounded (Remark~\ref{l7g:tr:rem:generator}). With
$C^{\langle m\rangle}=m\Ast^{m-1}C$ and
$S_M^{\langle m\rangle}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{M}c_j^{\langle m\rangle}u^j$, define
the smooth model
\[
 \mathcal F_M^{\langle m\rangle}(v)=C^{\langle m\rangle}\ee^{\lambda v}
 v^{-\beta}S_M^{\langle m\rangle}(1/v),\qquad
 \frac{d}{dv}\log\mathcal F_M^{\langle m\rangle}(v)=\lambda+O_{m,M}(1/v),
\]
and let $\tilde x_M^{\langle m\rangle}(Y)$ be its unique large solution of
$\mathcal F_M^{\langle m\rangle}(v)=Y$. Then there are $K_M^{\langle m\rangle}>0$
and $Y_M^{\langle m\rangle}$ such that, for $Y\ge Y_M^{\langle m\rangle}$ and
$\tilde x=\tilde x_M^{\langle m\rangle}(Y)$,
\[
 \bigl\lceil\tilde x-K_M^{\langle m\rangle}\tilde x^{-M-1}\bigr\rceil
 \le N^{\langle m\rangle}(Y)\le
 \bigl\lceil\tilde x+K_M^{\langle m\rangle}\tilde x^{-M-1}\bigr\rceil.
\]
The proof is that of Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:rounding}: the forward
expansion gives $|\log a_n^{\langle m\rangle}-\log\mathcal
F_M^{\langle m\rangle}(n)|\le Dn^{-M-1}$; the logarithmic derivative is at
least $\lambda/2$ beyond a fixed point; for $\epsilon=K\tilde x^{-M-1}$ the
mean value theorem gives $a^{\langle m\rangle}_{n_+}\ge Y$ at
$n_+=\lceil\tilde x+\epsilon\rceil$ and $a^{\langle m\rangle}_{n_-}<Y$ at
$n_-=\lceil\tilde x-\epsilon\rceil-1$, and monotonicity gives
$n_-+1\le N^{\langle m\rangle}(Y)\le n_+$.

For the centre, let $x_0^{\langle m\rangle}(Y)$ be \eqref{l7g:eq:x0} with $C$
replaced by $C^{\langle m\rangle}$, let
$\ell_j^{\langle m\rangle}=[u^j]\log\sum_{i\ge0}c_i^{\langle m\rangle}u^i$
(a formal logarithm; convergence of the coefficient series is not assumed),
and define $q_j^{\langle m\rangle}$ by
$\delta(v)=\sum_{j\ge1}q_j^{\langle m\rangle}v^j$ and
\[
 \lambda\delta-\beta\log(1+v\delta)
 +\sum_{j\ge1}\ell_j^{\langle m\rangle}\Bigl(\frac{v}{1+v\delta}\Bigr)^j=0,
\]
triangular because the new $q_j^{\langle m\rangle}$ enters with
multiplier $\lambda$. Then
$\tilde x_M^{\langle m\rangle}(Y)=x_0^{\langle m\rangle}
+\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_j^{\langle m\rangle}(x_0^{\langle m\rangle})^{-j}
+O_{m,M}((x_0^{\langle m\rangle})^{-M-1})$. Manuscript~32 proves this by the
\emph{analytic} implicit-function theorem rather than by formal
substitution: with $\tilde x=x_0^{\langle m\rangle}+\delta$ and
$v=1/x_0^{\langle m\rangle}$, the exact equation
$\lambda\delta-\beta\log(1+v\delta)+\log S_M^{\langle m\rangle}(v/(1+v\delta))=0$
is analytic near $(0,0)$ with $\delta$-derivative $\lambda\ne0$, so it has a
convergent solution $\delta(v)$, which is the large inverse for small $v>0$;
its first $M$ Taylor coefficients are the $q_j^{\langle m\rangle}$, and
raising $M$ leaves the determined coefficients unchanged. Explicitly
$q_1^{\langle m\rangle}=-\ell_1^{\langle m\rangle}/\lambda$,
$q_2^{\langle m\rangle}=-\beta\ell_1^{\langle m\rangle}/\lambda^2
-\ell_2^{\langle m\rangle}/\lambda$ and
$q_3^{\langle m\rangle}=-\beta^2\ell_1^{\langle m\rangle}/\lambda^3
-(\beta\ell_2^{\langle m\rangle}+(\ell_1^{\langle m\rangle})^2)/\lambda^2
-\ell_3^{\langle m\rangle}/\lambda$; for $m=1$ these are $q_1,q_2,q_3$ of
\eqref{l7g:eq:qvalues} (checked by hand at this write). The
replay records the universal coefficients through order~$6$. Combining the
two statements, the centre $\tilde x$ of the brackets may be replaced by the
truncated expansion after enlarging the constant; for $m=1$ this is
Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:rounding} with centre $x_M$.

As in Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:rounding}, $K_M^{\langle m\rangle}$ and
$Y_M^{\langle m\rangle}$ are existential. Once evaluated effectively, the
brackets decide $N^{\langle m\rangle}(Y)$ whenever the two ceilings agree;
without such values they supply no practical rounding certificate, and no
unqualified exact ceiling at or near a threshold is asserted. The replay's
inverse residuals are taken at $Y=a_n^{\langle m\rangle}$, exactly at
discrete thresholds, and are not a ceiling rule.
\end{remark}

\section{Further research questions and topics}\label{l7g:sec:further}

The questions below are concrete extensions of the proved results.
They are not assertions that every item is globally open: before a
priority claim, the relevant modular-form and continued-fraction
literature should be checked in greater depth.
Sections~\ref{l7g:sec:explicit}--\ref{l7g:sec:formal} are manuscript~58's
twelve; Section~\ref{l7g:cm:sec:further} lists manuscript~56's ten and
records where they meet manuscript~58's.

\subsection{Explicit remainder constants}\label{l7g:sec:explicit}
Theorems~\ref{l7g:th:main} and \ref{l7g:th:companion} give remainders at every
fixed order but not practical values of the constants. Construct
computable $K_M,N_M$ such that
\[
 \left|\frac{a_n n^{3/2}}{C27^n}-\sum_{j=0}^{M}c_jn^{-j}\right|
 \le K_M n^{-M-1}\qquad(n\ge N_M).
\]
One route is to bound the analytic Frobenius functions on a specified
complex neighborhood and make the transfer contour explicit. A second
is to construct upper and lower recurrence solutions from truncated
series plus a controlled correction. The latter may give smaller
thresholds and could turn the inverse brackets into a practical
certified algorithm.

\subsection{Late-order growth and optimal truncation}
Determine the large-$j$ behavior of $c_j$ and $d_j$. The proof of
fixed-order asymptotics does not imply convergence of the series in
$1/n$. A useful target is a sharp factorial-over-power law, with its
constant and phase, or a proof of a specified Gevrey bound. This would
identify an optimal truncation index and quantify what accuracy is
available before the recessive scale becomes relevant.

\subsection{Canonical exponentially improved asymptotics}
The exact basis $a_n,e_n$ removes ambiguity from
\eqref{l7g:eq:twoscale}, but it does not specify a canonical summation of the
dominant formal series. Develop a Borel or contour normalization and
compute the associated connection data between the singularities
$\rho$ and $-1$. The desired result is an error theorem resolving the
alternating contribution after optimal truncation, not merely a formal
addition of a second exponential scale.

\subsection{Recognizing the continued-fraction limit}
Find an exact special-value expression for $L$. The inhomogeneous
operator in the remark following \eqref{l7g:eq:CF} provides a starting point:
variation of parameters expresses its solutions through integrals
involving $A$ and algebraic functions. The condition that the
$\sqrt{1-27z}$ coefficient vanish selects $\mathcal B-LA$ and hence $L$.
A successful reduction might involve an elliptic integral, an iterated
modular integral, or a special $L$-value. No such identification is
proved in this report.

\subsection{Arithmetic strength of the approximants}
Does the continued fraction prove irrationality of $L$, or a useful
irrationality measure? The exponential error $27^{-n}$ alone is not
enough: the natural continuant denominators contain factorial growth.
The first step is to analyze the reduced denominators and common
factors of $\mathcal H_n$ and $\mathcal K_n$. Any arithmetic conclusion
must compare the error with the actual denominator after cancellation.
The general results in \cite{l7g:cohenzudilin} are relevant context, but
do not automatically apply to the specific limit constructed here.

\subsection{The arithmetic conjecture in the entry}\label{l7g:sec:lucas}
The OEIS entry also displays Lucas congruences for primes in the
specified residue classes modulo $7$ \cite{l7g:oeis279619}. The present
asymptotic argument neither proves nor disproves them. A separate
project should first audit subsequent work on the Chan--Cooper--Sica
conjecture and then exploit the modular parametrization or the
functional equation added to OEIS in 2026 (printed as
\eqref{l7g:cm:eq:functional}). This is a different target
from the asymptotic conjecture resolved here; analytic evidence should
not be substituted for an arithmetic proof.

\subsection{Coefficient fields for convolution powers}\label{l7g:tr:sec:fields}
Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:convolution} bounds the coefficient field by
$\Q(\pi C^2/\Ast^2)$. Determine exactly which convolution powers and
which correction orders eliminate the period ratio. The square is
special at its first correction, and its third-order differential
equation may expose cancellations more systematically. A symbolic
classification would clarify when a convolution inherits purely
rational normalized corrections and when new period combinations
necessarily appear.
\writenote{Partly answered by manuscript~32
(Remark~\ref{l7g:tr:rem:generator}): every normalized coefficient
$c_i^{\langle m\rangle}$ is an explicit polynomial in
$\qq=B^2/\Ast^2=4\pi C^2/\Ast^2$ with rational coefficients; for $m=1$ and
$m=2$ every correction is rational, at every order; for $m\ge3$ the first
correction is a polynomial of degree one in $\qq$, so it is rational only if
$\qq$ is, and the rationality of $\qq=256\pi^6/(3\G^4)$ is not known. Still
open: for $m\ge3$, whether any higher correction loses its dependence on
$\qq$, and the arithmetic nature of $\qq$.}

\subsection{Noninteger powers and growing convolution order}
Extend the convolution theorem to a specified branch of $A(z)^u$ for
noninteger real or complex $u$. Zero avoidance and possible additional
singularities must be addressed rather than assumed. A separate
uniform problem lets the integer $m=m(n)$ grow. The fixed-$m$ proof
is then insufficient because powers of the analytic boundary part
and the singular part can compete. Determine the applicable uniform
ranges and the transition scales from the actual local expansion.

\subsection{Positive integral representations}
Find a direct integral or moment representation for $a_n$ that makes
positivity and possible stronger inequalities transparent. A compact
real support reflecting both characteristic roots would be especially
informative, but such a representation and positivity of its density
are not established here. Potential consequences include explicit
log-convexity thresholds, Hankel determinant signs, and alternative
error bounds for coefficient asymptotics.

\subsection{Automated connection-constant recognition}
For families of quadratic-coefficient recurrences with two distinct
characteristic roots, combine three exact procedures: derive the
Fuchsian equation, certify a rational hypergeometric pullback, and test
whether a singular endpoint is a known complex-multiplication value.
The intended output should include a symbolic certificate and a branch
check, not just a high-precision guess. A279619 is a compact test case
because the rational parameter turns the dominant endpoint into $w=0$.

\subsection{A direct modular proof of the product identity}\label{l7g:tr:sec:product}
Can the product $C\Ast=3\sqrt3/(8\pi^{3/2})$ be obtained directly from a
single level-seven modular relation? The proof here combines a
Ramanujan identity with a period evaluation. A direct connection or
Wronskian argument on the modular side may unify the two normalizations
and generalize to other Ap\'ery-like equations. Such a proof could also
explain which sequences exhibit reciprocal boundary and singular
periods.
\mergenote{Manuscript~56's proof (Section~\ref{l7g:sec:second}) obtains
$C\Ast$ as half of Hirschhorn's constant $\Ct$ of the square, and $\Ast$ from
a single CM eta product. It is modular on the boundary value but still
takes the singular coefficient from the separately proved asymptotic of
A183204, so the question as posed, a single modular relation giving the
product, remains open.}
\writenote{Answered in substance for A279619 by manuscript~32
(Proposition~\ref{l7g:tr:prop:local}). Differentiating the Fricke
involution $z_7(-1/(7\tau))=-\ii\sqrt7\,\tau z_7(\tau)$ at its fixed point
$\tau_*$ gives $B\Ast=-3\sqrt3/(4\pi)$, that is
$C\Ast=3\sqrt3/(8\pi^{3/2})$; the only other input is O'Brien's identity
$dw_7/d\tau=2\pi\ii\,w_7z_7^2$, which converts the $\tau$-derivative into a
derivative in $\sqrt{1-27z}$. No hypergeometric identity, no period
evaluation and no input from A183204 is used. The mechanism is not new: it
is the level-seven case of Guillera and Zudilin's modular limit formula
\cite[eq.~(27)]{l7g:tr:gz}, which is not specific to level seven. What
remains open is the last part of the question: which sequences exhibit
reciprocal boundary and singular periods, and why.}

\subsection{Machine-checked certificates and analytic infrastructure}\label{l7g:sec:formal}
A staged Lean development can begin with the recurrence, positivity,
integer continuants, and the exact Wronskian. These statements need only
algebra and induction. The rational identities in
\eqref{l7g:eq:certs} and the triangular coefficient algorithm form a second
self-contained layer. A later analytic layer must connect convergent
local solutions to coefficient asymptotics, including branch choices
and remainder estimates. The complex-multiplication identities should
be stated as explicit dependencies until they too are formalized.
This separation prevents an exact symbolic certificate from being
mistaken for a proof of analytic continuation or of a special value.

\subsection{Manuscript 56's questions}\label{l7g:cm:sec:further}
Manuscript~56 wrote: the exact constant closes one visible gap, but it

can be used. Each item is followed, where it applies, by its relation to
the items above.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=\textbf{R\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Prove the Lucas-congruence conjecture for A279619.}
The OEIS entry conjectures Lucas congruences for primes
$p\equiv0,1,2,4\pmod7$. A promising route is to combine a diagonal or

unit-root arguments. The exceptional residue classes should be explained
geometrically rather than checked case by case.
(Same target as Section~\ref{l7g:sec:lucas}; different suggested route.)

The square coefficients $t_n$ have the compact sum

most natural through their recurrence or modular expansion. A positive
multisum would likely clarify integrality, congruences, and combinatorial
meaning simultaneously.

A183204 belongs to the sporadic Ap\'ery-like family \cite{l7g:cooper}.
The square-root sequence may encode a lower-weight or half-integral

test whether $a_{mp^r}$ satisfies congruences stronger than Lucas
congruence.

\item \textbf{Classify all CM evaluations of $A(X_7(\tau))$.}
For imaginary quadratic $\tau$, Chowla--Selberg expresses eta values in
gamma products. Determine which CM points give algebraic $X_7(\tau)$,
then tabulate exact series evaluations
$\sum a_nX_7(\tau)^n$. The values at $1/27$ and $1/125$ should be the
first entries of a larger level-seven atlas.

\item \textbf{Compute exact connection matrices.}
Equation \eqref{l7g:eq:ODE} has singular points $0$, $1/27$, $-1$, and
$\infty$. The present article computes one connection coefficient
between $0$ and $1/27$. Determine the full $2\times2$ connection
matrices between Frobenius bases at all singular points, preferably in
closed gamma form.
(Manuscript~58's companion adds the exact recessive amplitude
$D_{\min}$ of \eqref{l7g:eq:Dgamma}, a connection datum of the recurrence
at the root $-1$; the full matrices remain open.)

We proved eventual log-convexity. Exact computation suggests stronger
positivity phenomena. Seek a Stieltjes-moment representation, a
continued fraction, or a production matrix proving log-convexity for all
$n$ and perhaps total positivity of the Hankel matrix $(a_{i+j})$.
(Overlaps ``Positive integral representations'' above.)

terms.}
The algebraic expansion is controlled by the singularity at $1/27$.
After optimal truncation, the next singularity at $-1$ should generate an
exponentially small, alternating contribution. Determine the associated
transseries and Stokes constants.
(Same target as manuscript~58's late-order and exponentially improved
items above. Manuscript~58's Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:companion} constructs the
alternating recessive solution of the recurrence exactly; what remains
open is the canonical summation and the Stokes data of $a_n$ itself.)

\item \textbf{Invert the full expansion.}
Extend \eqref{l7g:eq:x0} to a systematic inverse transseries for the
integer threshold $N(Y)$, including rational corrections and a proof of a
uniform rounding rule.
(Re-scoped: the systematic inverse with rational corrections is
\eqref{l7g:eq:inverse}--\eqref{l7g:eq:qvalues}, an instance of
\texttt{t2:thm:balanced-inverse}, and the rounding brackets are

not available: Proposition~\ref{l7g:prop:rounding} decides $N(Y)$ only

\item \textbf{Formalize the algebraic core.}
A realistic Lean project would formalize the recurrence-to-ODE step, the
Frobenius coefficient recurrence, the formal identity
$\mathcal E_{27}[S]=0$, and finite-order transfer algebra. The Dedekind
eta and Chowla--Selberg inputs would initially be isolated as analytic
theorems, making the boundary between formalized algebra and classical
complex analysis explicit.
(Same programme as Section~\ref{l7g:sec:formal}.)

\item \textbf{Audit neighboring level-seven OEIS entries.}
The same thesis introduced the quartic-recurrence sequence A279613 and
related modular functions. Its oscillatory asymptotic is still listed
conjecturally. The exact-CM/connection strategy used here suggests a
systematic attack on its amplitude and phase constants, although the
multiple dominant singularities make the problem substantially harder.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope, audits, and conclusion}\label{l7g:sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Manuscript 56's scope and novelty audit}\label{l7g:sec:audit}
The mathematical proof of Section~\ref{l7g:sec:second} uses published
structural identities, but the closed form \eqref{l7g:eq:C} is obtained
there by combining them in a way not stated in the inspected sources.
The audit performed for manuscript~56 covered:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the current OEIS pages for A279619 and A183204;
\item O'Brien's 2016 thesis, including its recurrence, modular
      parametrization, square relation, and numerical asymptotic work;
\item Hirschhorn's proof of the A183204 asymptotic;
\item van der Poorten and Williams' explicit eta-value formula;
\item targeted web and repository searches for A279619 together with
      the gamma product or the displayed decimal constant;
\item the existing OEIS-asymptotics reports in the ProveIt repository.
\end{itemize}
No prior exact identification of the A279619 constant was found in that
search: the constant appears not to have been recorded in the OEIS entry,
O'Brien's thesis, Hirschhorn's paper, or the targeted searches. This is
evidence of novelty, not a substitute for a full bibliographic review or
peer review, and not a claim that no equivalent formula can exist
elsewhere. All theorem statements are supported by the derivations given
and by the reproducible checks shipped with the source. Manuscript~58's
audit (Section~\ref{l7g:sec:target} and
\file{58-gamma-SOURCE_AUDIT.md}) reached the same conclusion
independently.

\subsection{Integration roadmap and conclusion}

A natural repository layout separates four components: sequence
recurrences and exact certificates; local analytic representation;
asymptotic coefficient calculus; and inverse expansions. The first and
third components can be tested independently of modular-form machinery.
The second supplies the analytic existence and connection constants
that formal manipulations alone cannot establish. The fourth should
retain an explicit distinction between a continuous inverse scale and
an integer threshold function.

No existing Lean declaration is assumed to certify the special-function
steps of this report. A useful first integration milestone is a
machine-checked discrete Wronskian with rational bracketing of $L$.
The next is a generic formal-series lemma giving
\eqref{l7g:eq:triangular}; both are substantially smaller than a full
formalization of the gamma constant. The supplied Python verification
programs are a reproducible algebraic audit and a source of test data,
not a replacement for that formalization.

The principal outcome is a precise resolution of the asymptotic
statement displayed for A279619:
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 a_n\sim\frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{\Gamma(1/7)\Gamma(2/7)\Gamma(4/7)}
                    \frac{27^n}{n^{3/2}}.}
\]
The same argument supplies the boundary sum, every algebraic correction
with a fixed-order error bound, and a useful convolution calculus.
The independent companion construction reveals an alternating
recessive solution with an exact gamma-product amplitude. Inversion
then yields a Lambert-$W$ scale and rigorous rounding brackets. The
remaining questions concern sharper effective bounds, canonical
exponentially improved expansions, arithmetic properties, and broader
families, rather than the existence or value of the leading asymptotic
constant proved here.

\subsection{The Fricke fixed point and the local coefficient}\label{l7g:tr:sec:fricke}
Write $Q_7(j)=j_1^2+j_1j_2+2j_2^2$ for $j=(j_1,j_2)\in\mathbb Z^2$, and
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:theta}
 z_7(\tau)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z^2}\ee^{2\pi\ii\tau Q_7(j)},\qquad \Im\tau>0.
\end{equation}
It has weight one and the quadratic character of discriminant $-7$. With
$w_7$ and $X_7$ as in \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:w7}--\eqref{l7g:cm:eq:z7}, the
modular inputs are O'Brien's published identities \cite[Theorems 3.7, 3.8,
3.10, 4.6 and 6.1]{l7g:obrien}:
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:inputs}
 A(X_7(\tau))=z_7(\tau),\qquad
 \frac{dw_7}{d\tau}=2\pi\ii\,w_7(\tau)z_7(\tau)^2,\qquad
 X_7(\tau)=\Bigl(\frac{\eta(\tau)\eta(7\tau)}{z_7(\tau)}\Bigr)^3.
\end{equation}
(The first and third are \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:z7}, which identifies $z_7$ with
this theta series.) They identify germs at the cusp, where
$X_7=\ee^{2\pi\ii\tau}+O(\ee^{4\pi\ii\tau})$; the germs are then continued
along an explicit path. A global inverse of $X_7$ is neither needed nor
asserted.

\emph{The branch.} By \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:eta-inv-special},
$\eta(\tau_*)=7^{1/4}\eta(\ii\sqrt7)$, $w_7(\tau_*)=1/7$ and
$X_7(\tau_*)=\rho$; all eta and theta values below are positive. For
$\tau=\ii y$ with $y\ge1/\sqrt7$, \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:inputs} gives
$\frac{d}{dy}w_7(\ii y)=-2\pi w_7(\ii y)z_7(\ii y)^2<0$, so $w_7$ increases
from $0$ to $1/7$ as $y$ decreases from $\infty$ to $1/\sqrt7$; and
\[
 \frac{dX_7}{dw_7}=\frac{1-49w_7^2}{(1+13w_7+49w_7^2)^2}>0
 \qquad(0<w_7<1/7).
\]
So $X_7$ increases from $0$ to $\rho$ along the path, its inverse is
regular in the interior, and \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:inputs} identifies the branch
reached from the generating function at zero:
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:leftlimit}
 \lim_{z\uparrow\rho}A(z)=z_7(\tau_*)=\Ast .
\end{equation}
This makes explicit the branch step behind \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:F-eta-product}.

\begin{proposition}[Fricke involution]\label{l7g:tr:prop:fricke}
$z_7(-1/(7\tau))=-\ii\sqrt7\,\tau\,z_7(\tau)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Gram matrix of $2Q_7$ is $\mathbf M=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&4\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
with $\det\mathbf M=7$ and
$7\mathbf M^{-1}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}4&-1\\-1&2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, which
is integrally equivalent to $\mathbf M$ (exchange the coordinates and change
the sign of one). The two-dimensional Poisson formula for
$\sum_{v\in\mathbb Z^2}\exp(\pi\ii\zeta\,v^{\mathsf T}\mathbf Mv)$ transforms it,
at argument $-1/\zeta$, into the sum with matrix $\mathbf M^{-1}$ times the
factor $(-\ii\zeta)/\sqrt{\det\mathbf M}$. Taking $\zeta=7\tau$ and using the
integral equivalence gives the identity exactly; no unspecified multiplier
remains.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The local coefficient at the Fricke point]\label{l7g:tr:prop:local}
Let $\sqrt s=\sqrt{1-27X_7}$, positive on the real path. Then
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:sqrts}
 \sqrt s=\frac{1-7w_7}{\sqrt{1+13w_7+49w_7^2}},\qquad
 \frac{d\sqrt s}{d\tau}\Big|_{\tau_*}=-2\pi\ii\,\Ast^2\sqrt{\frac7{27}}\ne0,
\end{equation}
and the singular coefficient of \eqref{l7g:eq:puiseux} is
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:Blocal}
 B=\frac{dz_7}{d\sqrt s}\Big|_{\sqrt s=0}=-\frac{3\sqrt3}{4\pi\Ast}<0 .
\end{equation}
Equivalently $C\Ast=3\sqrt3/(8\pi^{3/2})$, which is \eqref{l7g:eq:Creciprocal}
of the first proof and \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:Ct-relation} of the second.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The algebraic identity for $\sqrt s$ follows on squaring, since
$1+13w+49w^2-27w=(1-7w)^2$; both square roots are positive for
$0<z<\rho$. Differentiating Proposition~\ref{l7g:tr:prop:fricke} at
$\tau_*$, where $d(-1/(7\tau))/d\tau=-1$ and $-\ii\sqrt7\,\tau_*=1$, gives
$-z_7'(\tau_*)=-\ii\sqrt7\,\Ast+z_7'(\tau_*)$, so
$z_7'(\tau_*)=\tfrac{\ii\sqrt7}2\Ast$. By \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:inputs},
$w_7'(\tau_*)=\tfrac{2\pi\ii}7\Ast^2$; since $1-7w_7$ vanishes at $\tau_*$,
$d\sqrt s/dw_7=-7\sqrt{7/27}$ there, which gives \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:sqrts}. The
inverse-function theorem lets $z_7$ be expanded analytically in $\sqrt s$,
and $B=z_7'(\tau_*)/(d\sqrt s/d\tau)(\tau_*)$ is \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:Blocal}.
Thus the branch of \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:leftlimit} satisfies the convergent
local relation $A(z)=\Ast+B(1-27z)^{1/2}+O(1-27z)$. This fixes the sign and
the magnitude of the singular term without an asymptotic formula for $A^2$
and without a numerical fit. The product form follows from
$C=-B/(2\sqrt\pi)$, \eqref{l7g:eq:Chyper}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The CM period from an Epstein zeta function}\label{l7g:tr:sec:epstein}
It remains to evaluate $\Ast$. The result is classical in CM theory and is
not claimed as new; the purpose is a self-contained derivation in a fixed
normalization, because a normalized instead of an unnormalized Epstein
zeta function would introduce a spurious power of $7$.

\begin{proposition}[Kronecker limit, derivative form]\label{l7g:tr:prop:kronecker}
For $y>0$ and $\Re\sigma>1$ let
$E_y(\sigma)=\sum_{(j_1,j_2)\ne(0,0)}(j_1^2+y^2j_2^2)^{-\sigma}$. Then $E_y$
continues meromorphically to a neighbourhood of $\sigma=0$, and
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:Eprime}
 E_y'(0)=-2\log(2\pi)-4\log\eta(\ii y).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Separate $j_2=0$, apply the Mellin identity to the remaining terms, and use
$\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\ee^{-j^2t}=\sqrt{\pi/t}\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z}\ee^{-\pi^2r^2/t}$;
absolute convergence for $\Re\sigma>1$ permits the interchanges. The zero
frequency gives a gamma integral, and the nonzero frequencies are evaluated
by $\int_0^\infty t^{\nu-1}\ee^{-at-b/t}\,dt=2(b/a)^{\nu/2}K_\nu(2\sqrt{ab})$
($a,b>0$). The result,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:bessel}
\begin{split}
 E_y(\sigma)={}&2\zeta(2\sigma)
 +\frac{2\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\sigma-\frac12)}{\Gamma(\sigma)}
   y^{1-2\sigma}\zeta(2\sigma-1)\\
 &+\frac{8\pi^\sigma}{\Gamma(\sigma)}y^{1/2-\sigma}
 \sum_{n,r\ge1}n^{1/2-\sigma}r^{\sigma-1/2}K_{\sigma-1/2}(2\pi ynr),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
gives the continuation; the double Bessel series and its derivatives in the
order converge locally uniformly by exponential decay, so termwise
differentiation is legitimate. At $\sigma=0$ the first term contributes
$4\zeta'(0)=-2\log(2\pi)$; since $1/\Gamma(\sigma)=\sigma+O(\sigma^2)$,
$\Gamma(-\frac12)=-2\sqrt\pi$ and $\zeta(-1)=-\frac1{12}$, the second
contributes $\pi y/3$; and $K_{-1/2}(u)=\sqrt{\pi/(2u)}\,\ee^{-u}$ makes the
third contribute
$4\sum_{n,r\ge1}\ee^{-2\pi ynr}/r=-4\sum_{n\ge1}\log(1-\ee^{-2\pi yn})$.
With the product for $\eta$ the three combine to \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:Eprime}.
\end{proof}
In the determinant-one normalization $\widetilde E_y(\sigma)=y^\sigma
E_y(\sigma)$ one has instead
$\widetilde E_y'(0)=-2\log(2\pi)-\log y-4\log\eta(\ii y)$, because
$E_y(0)=-1$; the extra $-\log y$ disappears when the normalization is
undone. This agrees with the classical limit-formula literature
\cite{l7g:tr:chamizo}; the derivation makes the convention independent of
a citation.

\begin{proposition}[The conductor-two factor]\label{l7g:tr:prop:conductor}
Let $\chi_{-7}(a)=(a/7)$ and $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\tau_0]$,
$\tau_0=(1+\ii\sqrt7)/2$. For $\Re\sigma>1$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:suborder}
 \sum_{j\ne0}Q_7(j)^{-\sigma}=2\zeta(\sigma)L(\sigma,\chi_{-7}),\qquad
 E_{\sqrt7}(\sigma)=2\varphi_2(\sigma)\zeta(\sigma)L(\sigma,\chi_{-7}),
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_2(\sigma)=1-2^{1-\sigma}+2^{1-2\sigma}$, and both identities
continue meromorphically.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathcal O$ is the maximal order of discriminant $-7$. Minkowski's bound
$2\sqrt7/\pi<2$ puts an integral ideal of norm one in every class, so the
class number is one, and the only units are $\pm1$. Counting a generator
and its negative for each nonzero principal ideal gives the first identity
(equivalently, compare Euler factors at split, inert and ramified primes).
The form $j_1^2+7j_2^2$ belongs to the suborder
$\mathbb Z[\ii\sqrt7]=\mathbb Z+2\mathbb Z\tau_0$. Modulo $2$ the minimal
polynomial $T^2-T+2$ becomes $T(T-1)$, so
$\mathcal O/2\mathcal O\simeq\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2$, and the suborder
consists of the elements with equal residues in the two factors. A nonzero
such element has residues $(1,1)$, hence odd norm, or $(0,0)$, hence lies in
$2\mathcal O$. The prime $2$ splits into two primes of norm $2$; excluding
both multiplies the ideal sum by $(1-2^{-\sigma})^2$ and counts exactly the
odd-norm elements, while multiplication by $2$ multiplies norms by $4$ and
counts the other class. The two sets are disjoint, which gives
$\varphi_2=(1-2^{-\sigma})^2+4^{-\sigma}$. The identity holds first for
$\Re\sigma>1$ and then by continuation. No CM formula for a nonfundamental
discriminant is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The eta value; classical]\label{l7g:tr:thm:eta}
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:etaCM}
 \eta(\ii\sqrt7)^2=\frac{\G}{8\cdot7^{1/4}\pi^2},\qquad
 \Ast=3\eta(\tau_*)\eta(\ii\sqrt7)=3\cdot7^{1/4}\eta(\ii\sqrt7)^2
 =\frac{3\G}{8\pi^2},\qquad B=-\frac{2\pi\sqrt3}{\G}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From $L(\sigma,\chi_{-7})=7^{-\sigma}\sum_{a=1}^6\chi_{-7}(a)\zeta(\sigma,a/7)$,
$\zeta(0,u)=\frac12-u$ and $\zeta'(0,u)=\log\Gamma(u)-\frac12\log(2\pi)$
(the character is $+1$ at $1,2,4$ and $-1$ at $3,5,6$),
\[
 L(0,\chi_{-7})=1,\qquad L'(0,\chi_{-7})=-\log7+\log(\G/\Gm).
\]
With $\varphi_2(0)=1$, $\varphi_2'(0)=-2\log2$, $\zeta(0)=-\frac12$ and
$\zeta'(0)=-\frac12\log(2\pi)$, differentiating \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:suborder}
gives $E_{\sqrt7}'(0)=\log(14/\pi)-\log(\G/\Gm)$. Comparing with
\eqref{l7g:tr:eq:Eprime} at $y=\sqrt7$ and using Gauss's multiplication
formula $\G\,\Gm=\prod_{a=1}^6\Gamma(a/7)=(2\pi)^3/\sqrt7$ gives
$\eta(\ii\sqrt7)^4=\G^2/(64\sqrt7\,\pi^4)$, and positivity fixes the square
root. At $\tau_*$ the third identity of \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:inputs} and
positivity give $\Ast=3\eta(\tau_*)\eta(\ii\sqrt7)$, and
$\eta(\tau_*)=7^{1/4}\eta(\ii\sqrt7)$. Finally
Proposition~\ref{l7g:tr:prop:local} gives $B$.
\end{proof}
The first value is \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:eta-isqrt7} squared, the second is
\eqref{l7g:cm:eq:F0-final}, and the third is \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:B0}.

\begin{remark}[A classical cross-check, and its relation to the second proof]\label{l7g:tr:rem:classical}
Manuscript~32 compares the value with the fundamental-discriminant
formula. Zagier gives \cite[p.~95]{l7g:tr:zagier}
$7^{1/4}|\eta(\tau_0)|^2=\G/(4\pi^2)$. In Reb\'ak's notation the class
invariant is $\mathsf G_n=2^{-1/4}r^{-1/24}\prod_{j\ge0}(1+r^{2j+1})$ with
$r=\ee^{-\pi\sqrt n}$, and the classical evaluation $\mathsf G_7=2^{1/4}$ is
recalled in the proof of his Theorem~4.1 \cite{l7g:tr:rebak}. Dividing the
eta products with $r=\ee^{-\pi\sqrt7}$ gives
$|\eta(\tau_0)|/\eta(\ii\sqrt7)=r^{-1/24}\prod_{j\ge0}(1+r^{2j+1})=\sqrt2$,
which again gives \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:etaCM}. In substance this is the route
of Section~\ref{l7g:sec:second}: Zagier's value is \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:eta-tau0}
(there from van der Poorten and Williams's form of Chowla--Selberg), and the
ratio $\sqrt2$ is \eqref{l7g:cm:eq:eta-isqrt7} (there from Weber's function
with $\mathfrak u=-1$). Neither classical evaluation is claimed as new. The
cross-check separates the discriminant $-7$ period at $\tau_0$ from the
conductor-two point $\ii\sqrt7$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Continuation, transfer and the third proof}\label{l7g:tr:sec:transfer}
Manuscript~32 justifies the continuation without the dented disk of
Section~\ref{l7g:sec:singularity}. The equation \eqref{l7g:eq:ODE} has finite
singular points $0$, $\rho$ and $-1$ only. The germ at zero is analytic,
and every other point of $|z|<\rho$ is ordinary, so $A$ is single-valued
and analytic there; this also justifies continuing \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:inputs}
along the path above. For $\rho<R<1$ the slit disk
$\{|z|<R\}\setminus[\rho,R)$ contains no singular point other than $0$;
loops in it minus $\{0\}$ have monodromy generated by a loop about zero,
which fixes the analytic germ. So the continued solution is single-valued,
its singularity at zero is removable, and it supplies the complex
$\Delta$-domain at $\rho$ that singularity analysis needs, not merely a
real-axis limit. In $s=1-27z$ the equation is \eqref{l7g:eq:sODE}, with
exponents $0$ and $\frac12$, so $A=\Ast\widehat U(s)+B\sqrt s\,V(s)$ with no
logarithmic term; the constants are fixed by \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:leftlimit} and
Theorem~\ref{l7g:tr:thm:eta}, and $B\ne0$ makes $\rho$ a genuine
singularity, the only one on $|z|=\rho$.

For the transfer, expand $\widehat U$ through degree $M+1$ and $V$ through
degree $M$. The subtracted integral-power part is a polynomial in $z$; it is
not necessary to assert that the full analytic branch $U(1-27z)$ extends to
$z=0$. The remainder is analytic in the slit domain and of local size
$O(s^{M+3/2})$, so its coefficients are $O_M(27^nn^{-M-5/2})$ by a
Hankel-type contour near $\rho$ and an outer contour beyond $\rho$
\cite[Chapter~VI]{l7g:flajolet}. With \eqref{l7g:eq:transferexact} this gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:gammaexp}
 a_n=27^nB\sum_{j=0}^{M}v_j
 \frac{\Gamma(n-j-\frac12)}{\Gamma(-j-\frac12)\Gamma(n+1)}
 +O_M(27^nn^{-M-5/2}),
\end{equation}
whose leading term is $-B/(2\sqrt\pi)=\sqrt{3\pi}/\G=C$. The leading bound
makes $\sum a_n\rho^n$ convergent, and Abel's theorem with
\eqref{l7g:tr:eq:leftlimit} identifies its sum as $\Ast$. This completes the
third proof of Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:main}.

\subsection{Generators for every fixed order}\label{l7g:tr:sec:generators}
Both Frobenius series come from one recurrence. For $r\in\{0,\frac12\}$ put
$f_{r,-1}=0$, $f_{r,0}=1$; then for $j\ge1$ and $v=r+j$,
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:frob}
 f_{r,j}=\frac{\bigl(29(v-1)^2+14(v-1)+\frac{20}9\bigr)f_{r,j-1}
 -(v-\frac43)(v-\frac53)f_{r,j-2}}{14v(2v-1)},
\end{equation}
with no vanishing denominator. For $r=\frac12$ this is \eqref{l7g:eq:vrec}
($f_{1/2,j}=v_j$). For $r=0$ it gives the normalized analytic part, which
the proof of Theorem~\ref{l7g:th:convolution} uses only through its first
coefficient:
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:Uhat}
 \widehat U(s)=\frac{U(s)}{\Ast}=1+\frac{10}{63}s+\frac{986}{11907}s^2
 +\frac{89984}{1607445}s^3+O(s^4).
\end{equation}
Both converge near zero by Frobenius theory, not merely as formal series.

Let $\mathsf B_k$ be the Bernoulli polynomials and define rational
polynomials $\mathsf h_i(a,b)$ by
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:gammaratio}
 \sum_{i\ge0}\mathsf h_i(a,b)u^i=\exp\Bigl(\sum_{r\ge1}
 \frac{(-1)^{r+1}(\mathsf B_{r+1}(a)-\mathsf B_{r+1}(b))}{r(r+1)}u^r\Bigr),
\end{equation}
which is the fixed-order expansion
$\Gamma(n+a)/\Gamma(n+b)\sim n^{a-b}\sum_i\mathsf h_i(a,b)n^{-i}$ obtained by
subtracting two Stirling series for $\log\Gamma$
\cite[\S5.11]{l7g:dlmf}. Since $\Gamma(-\frac12)/\Gamma(-j-\frac12)
=(-1)^j(\frac32)_j$, \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:gammaexp} gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:tr:eq:cgamma}
 c_i=\sum_{j=0}^{i}v_j(-1)^j(\tfrac32)_j\,\mathsf h_{i-j}(-j-\tfrac12,1),
\end{equation}
each coefficient by finitely many rational operations. This is the
``Bernoulli-polynomial gamma-ratio'' route by which the batch-73O2 intake
checked $c_0,\dots,c_8$. Manuscript~32's second, independent generator is
the formal equation $\mathcal E_{27}[S_{27}]=0$ of \eqref{l7g:eq:formalE}
with the triangular step \eqref{l7g:eq:triangular} (denominator $28m$),
proved in both earlier manuscripts and printed once there; it gives
uniqueness and rationality and checks \eqref{l7g:tr:eq:cgamma}. The formal
equation identifies the coefficients of an actual expansion only because
the expansion has already been established; a formal ansatz alone
determines neither the connection constant nor the remainder. Manuscript~32
prints $c_1,\dots,c_6$, equal to \eqref{l7g:eq:cvalues} and
\eqref{l7g:cm:eq:alpha-more}.

\section{Checking the classical period normalization}\label{l7g:app:beta}

The table in Zucker's singular-value notes \cite[Table~1]{l7g:beta} uses
$K[N]=K(k_N)$ with $K(k_N')/K(k_N)=\sqrt N$. At $N=7$ it gives
\begin{equation}\label{l7g:eq:betatable}
 K[7]=\frac{2^{5/7}7^{3/4}}{14}
 \frac{\beta(1/7)\beta(2/7)}{\beta(1/14)},\qquad
 k_7^2=\frac{8-3\sqrt7}{16},
\end{equation}
where $\beta(t)=\Gamma(t)^2/\Gamma(2t)$. We spell out the simplification
because confusing the elliptic modulus with its square would give an
incorrect constant.

Set $g_1=\Gamma(1/7)$, $g_2=\Gamma(2/7)$, $g_4=\Gamma(4/7)$, and
$g_{1/2}=\Gamma(1/14)$. Then
\[
 \frac{\beta(1/7)\beta(2/7)}{\beta(1/14)}
 =\frac{g_1^3g_2}{g_4g_{1/2}^2}.
\]
The gamma duplication formula at $1/14$ is
\[
 g_{1/2}g_4=2^{6/7}\sqrt\pi\,g_1.
\]
It follows that the beta quotient is
$g_1g_2g_4/(2^{12/7}\pi)=\G/(2^{12/7}\pi)$. Substitution into
\eqref{l7g:eq:betatable} gives
\[
 K[7]=\frac{7^{3/4}\G}{28\pi}
      =\frac{\G}{4\pi7^{1/4}},
\]
which is exactly \eqref{l7g:eq:Kgamma}. The classical table evaluation is
an external input; the algebraic conversion here checks that its
normalization matches the one used in the proof.

\section{Two derivations from manuscript 56}\label{l7g:app:derivations}

\subsection{The recurrence-to-ODE identity}
For completeness, write $\tht=z\,d/dz$. Then
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}n a_nz^n=\tht A,
 \qquad
 \sum_{n\ge0}n^2a_nz^n=\tht^2A.
\]
The left side of \eqref{l7g:eq:rec}, summed over $n\ge0$, is
$A'+zA''$. The first term on the right gives
$26z^2A''+39zA'+2A$, and the shifted term gives
$27z^3A''+54z^2A'+6zA$. Their difference is exactly
\eqref{l7g:eq:ODE}.

\subsection{A compact derivation of the coefficient algorithm}
Assume
\[
 a_n=C27^nn^{-3/2}\mathcal A_n,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_n\sim S_{27}(1/n).
\]
Divide \eqref{l7g:eq:rec} by $C27^nn^{1/2}$ and put $t=1/n$.
The three terms become
\begin{align*}
 27(1+t)^{1/2}S_{27}\!\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right),\qquad
 &(26+13t+2t^2)S_{27}(t),\\
 &\left(1-t+\frac29t^2\right)(1-t)^{-3/2}
 S_{27}\!\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right),
\end{align*}
which is $\mathcal E_{27}[S_{27}]=0$ in \eqref{l7g:eq:formalE}. The
coefficient of $t^2$ is $-215/36-28c_1$, giving $c_1=-215/1008$;
successive coefficients proceed triangularly: after the automatic
cancellations in degrees $0$ and $1$, each next coefficient determines one
new $c_j$ linearly. This proves that all $c_j$ are rational and supplies a
fast exact algorithm.

\subsection{Manuscript 58's proposal}
\begin{resultbox}
Let $G=\Gamma(1/7)\Gamma(2/7)\Gamma(4/7)$. Then
\[
 a(n)\sim\frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{G}\frac{27^n}{n^{3/2}},\qquad
 \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{a(n)}{27^n}=\frac{3G}{8\pi^2}.
\]
More precisely, for every fixed truncation order there is an asymptotic
expansion with a remainder of the next order. Its first corrections are
\[
 1-\frac{215}{1008n}-\frac{1265}{290304n^2}
   +\frac{4683055}{877879296n^3}+O(n^{-4}).
\]
The first three corrections were already calculated formally by
O'Brien \cite{l7g:obrien}.
\end{resultbox}

The separate Lucas-congruence statement is not affected by this report.

\subsection{Manuscript 56's proposal}
A compact statement suitable for adaptation to the OEIS entry after
independent review (its plain-text form is
\file{data/56-exact-OEIS_update.txt}):
\begin{resultbox}
Let
$\G=\Gamma(1/7)\Gamma(2/7)\Gamma(4/7)$. Then
\[
 a(n)\sim
 \frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{\G}\,27^n n^{-3/2}.
\]
More precisely,
\[
 a(n)=\frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{\G}\,27^n n^{-3/2}
 \left(1-\frac{215}{1008n}-\frac{1265}{290304n^2}
 +\frac{4683055}{877879296n^3}+O(n^{-4})\right).
\]
For the generating function $A(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}a(n)x^n$,
\[
 A(1/27)=\frac{3\G}{8\pi^2},
 \qquad
 A(1/125)=\frac{5\G}{16\pi^2}.
\]
At the dominant singularity,
\[
 A(x)=\frac{3\G}{8\pi^2}
 -\frac{2\pi\sqrt3}{\G}\sqrt{1-27x}
 +O(1-27x).
\]
\end{resultbox}
\mergenote{The first three corrections were calculated formally by
O'Brien (2016 thesis, \S8.4) \cite{l7g:obrien}. Manuscript~56's proposal
and its plain-text file do not say so; any submission must add this
credit.}
