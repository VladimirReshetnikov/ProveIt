\begin{abstract}
For OEIS A113226, the permutations avoiding the vincular pattern
$12$--$34$, equivalently the $\{3,2+2\}$-free naturally labelled posets of
Bevan, Cheon and Kitaev, Part~I derives a closed exponential generating
function, proves the stretched-exponential form conjectured by those
authors with exact constants and power $-5/6$, gives every algebraic
correction in powers of $n^{-1/3}$ by a finite Gaussian algorithm, an
exact quadratic-arithmetic recurrence and controlled Lambert-$W$ inverse
expansions. Part~II marks the number of distinct nonempty strict downsets
and of isolated elements: it explains the exponential transform behind
A113226 by a record-to-cycle bijection, proves a complex-uniform
all-orders saddle expansion of the marked generating function, precise
all-orders local large deviations for interior densities, Gaussian and
local limit laws, and a conditional Poisson law of isolated elements with
an explicit correction and a total-variation error of order $n^{-2/3}$.
All expansions are Poincar\'e expansions to each fixed order.
The report is AI-assisted and unrefereed; no statement of it is
formalized.
\end{abstract}

\section{The result and its context}\label{vav:sec:intro}

Let $A_n$ count permutations of $[n]$ avoiding the vincular pattern
$12$--$34$, with $A_0=1$, and put
\[
 H(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}A_n\frac{z^n}{n!},\qquad L(z)=\log H(z),\quad L(0)=0.
\]
An occurrence requires positions $i,i+1,j,j+1$ with $i+1<j$ and
$\pi_i<\pi_{i+1}<\pi_j<\pi_{j+1}$. This adjacency convention matters.
The sequence is OEIS A113226 \cite{oeis}.

Bevan--Cheon--Kitaev \cite{bck} identify these objects with
$\{3,2+2\}$-free naturally labelled posets, give a labelled binary-word
model and an insertion recurrence, and conjecture the asymptotic form
\[
 A_n\sim D\,n!\,\rho^{-n}\mu^{n^{1/3}}n^\beta,
 \qquad \rho=\log4.
\]
Their Conjectures 13 and 14 specify the exponential rate and the
stretched-exponential form, with fitted estimates for $D,\mu,\beta$.
Elizalde \cite{elizalde} obtained earlier bounds. The exact expression
below resolves both conjectural forms and identifies their constants.
\begin{writenote}
Elizalde's paper also bounds the avoiders of the sibling pattern
$1$--$23$--$4$ (the powered Catalan numbers, OEIS A113227) and leaves their
precise asymptotics open. That question is answered, by a different method,
in the sibling report
\path{oeis-sequence-asymptotics/a113227-powered-catalan/} of this
collection (batch 77, manuscripts 57 and 56); see ``About this report''.
\end{writenote}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:constants}
 c=\left(\frac{\pi^2}{4\rho}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
 D=2e^{-3}\sqrt{\frac{c}{3\pi}},\qquad C=3c,\qquad \mu=e^C.
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}\label{vav:thm:main}
The exponential generating function is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:egf}
 H(z)=\exp\left\{\frac z2+
 \frac{q(3\arcsin q-\pi/2)}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}\right\},
 \qquad q=\frac{e^{z/2}}2,
\end{equation}
with branches analytic at $z=0$. Its logarithm is equivalently the unique
analytic solution of
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:ode}
 (4-e^z)L'(z)-2L(z)=2+e^z-z,\qquad L(0)=0.
\end{equation}
For every fixed integer $J\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:main}
 A_n=D\,n!\,\rho^{-n}e^{3cn^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^{J}a_jn^{-j/3}
       +O_J(n^{-(J+1)/3})\right),
\end{equation}
where $a_0=1$ and all $a_j$ are given by the finite Gaussian coefficient
formula in Section~\ref{vav:sec:generator}. In particular,
\begin{align}
 a_1&=\frac{c^2(1-\rho)}2-\frac5{36c},\label{vav:eq:a1}\\
 a_2&=\frac{324c^6(\rho-1)^2+c^3(1908\rho-612)-35}{2592c^2}.
 \label{vav:eq:a2}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

The constants are
\begin{align*}
 c&=1.21188506246461864249\ldots,&
 D&=0.03570604125804483339\ldots,\\
 C&=3.63565518739385592747\ldots,&
 \mu&=37.92669385021515730\ldots.
\end{align*}
Thus $\beta=-5/6$. The paper's decimal estimates are finite-range fits;
they are not exact conjectured values. In particular, the amplitude
$D$ differs visibly from their estimate near $0.032$.

The theorem is an all-orders Poincar\'e expansion. It neither asserts
convergence of the coefficient series nor identifies all exponentially
small contributions. Its combinatorial foundation belongs to
\cite{bck}; the known sequence A136127 and its leading asymptotic are
also prior work \cite{oeis136,aval,testart}. Our contribution is scoped
to the exact A113226 transform, its complete coefficient asymptotics,
and the resulting inverse.
\begin{writenote}
Part~II extends Theorem~\ref{vav:thm:main} to the generating function
marked by the number $k$ of block pairs in \eqref{vav:eq:blocks} (the
number of distinct nonempty strict downsets) and by the isolated elements,
uniformly for the marking parameter in complex neighbourhoods of compact
positive intervals (Theorem~\ref{vav:rf:thm:first}); at $u=1$ it is the
expansion \eqref{vav:eq:main}.
\end{writenote}

\section{From labelled words to an integral equation}\label{vav:sec:transfer}

Proposition 7 of \cite{bck} gives a bijection with labelled binary words
beginning in zero, whose labels decrease within a zero run, increase
within a one run, and satisfy the condition that every one label exceeds
all earlier zero labels. Equivalently, an object has the block form
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:blocks}
 E_1,M_1,E_2,M_2,\ldots,E_k,M_k,E_{k+1},
\end{equation}
where $E_i,M_i$ for $i\le k$ are nonempty sets, $E_{k+1}$ is an
arbitrary set, and every label in $M_i$ exceeds all labels in
$E_1\cup\cdots\cup E_i$. Each block has its unique prescribed order.
The case $k=0$ includes the empty object and a single decreasing zero run.

Replace the integer labels by distinct coordinates in $[0,1]$ and
integrate over them. For fixed block cardinalities totaling $n$, divide
by the factorial of every block size. Each ordering chamber has volume
$1/n!$; the unordered coordinates inside each block supply exactly its
block factorial. The coefficient is therefore the number of valid
labelled objects divided by $n!$. There is no extra multinomial factor.
Coincident coordinates have measure zero.

Let $x$ be the largest coordinate among preceding zero blocks, initially
$x=0$. A new nonempty zero block has maximum at most $x$, with weight
$e^{zx}-1$, or creates a new maximum $t>x$, with density
$ze^{zt}\dd t$. Indeed, the maximum of $m$ coordinates has volume
density $mt^{m-1}$, and summing $z^mmt^{m-1}/m!$ gives $ze^{zt}$.
A following nonempty one block then has weight $e^{z(1-t)}-1$.
The final arbitrary zero block has weight $e^z$, independent of the
state. Its labels have no restriction relative to earlier one labels.

If $f(x)$ counts any number of additional zero/one pairs, then
\begin{align}
 f(x)={}&1+(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1)f(x)\notag\\
 &+\int_x^1 z(e^z-e^{zt})f(t)\dd t.\label{vav:eq:transfer}
\end{align}
This holds as a formal series, and analytically for small $|z|$.
Each application of the operator increases size by at least two, so the
formal solution is unique. Put
\[
 D_z(x)=e^{zx}+e^{z(1-x)}-e^z,\quad
 h(x)=D_z(x)f(x),\quad b(x)=z(e^z-e^{zx}).
\]
Then
\[
 h(x)=1+\int_x^1\frac{b(t)h(t)}{D_z(t)}\dd t,
 \qquad h'(x)=-\frac{b(x)}{D_z(x)}h(x),\qquad h(1)=1.
\]
Since $D_z(0)=1$ and $H(z)=e^zf(0)$, this yields
\begin{align}
 L(z)&=z+\int_0^1\frac{z(e^z-e^{zx})}{D_z(x)}\dd x\label{vav:eq:integral1}\\
 &=\frac z2+\frac{ze^z}{2}\int_0^1\frac{\dd x}{D_z(x)}.
 \label{vav:eq:integral2}
\end{align}
For the second equality average $x$ and $1-x$ and use
$e^{zx}+e^{z(1-x)}=D_z(x)+e^z$.

Set $v=z(x-1/2)$ and $q=e^{z/2}/2$. The substitution
$u=\tanh(v/2)$ in \eqref{vav:eq:integral2} gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:atan}
 L(z)=\frac z2+\frac{2q}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}
 \arctan\left(\frac{2q-1}{2q+1}\sqrt{\frac{1+q}{1-q}}\right).
\end{equation}
The arctangent vanishes at $q=1/2$ and its derivative is
$3/(2\sqrt{1-q^2})$. It therefore equals
$(3/2)\arcsin q-\pi/4$ near $q=1/2$.
This proves \eqref{vav:eq:egf}. Differentiation proves \eqref{vav:eq:ode}.

\section{Exact recurrences and the known cumulants}\label{vav:sec:cumulants}

Let $\ell_n=L^{(n)}(0)$, with $\ell_0=0$. Coefficient extraction from
\eqref{vav:eq:ode} gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:cumulant}
 3\ell_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom nk\ell_{k+1}
       +2\ell_n+1+2\ind{n=0}-\ind{n=1}\qquad(n\ge0).
\end{equation}
The empty sum is zero. Since $H'=L'H$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:exact}
 A_0=1,\qquad
 A_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk\ell_{k+1}A_{n-k}.
\end{equation}
These recurrences use $O(N^2)$ integer arithmetic operations for all
terms through $N$. This statement counts arithmetic operations, not
bit operations. It gives a fast recurrence of the kind asked for by
Callan \cite[Section 9]{callan}.

The cumulants begin
\[
 (\ell_n)_{n\ge1}=1,1,2,5,16,63,294,1585,9692,66275,\ldots.
\]
They are the known sequence A136127 shifted by one:
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:known}
 \ell_n=\mathrm{A136127}(n-1)\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
Here is an exact verification that also explains the nonbinomial
antidiagonal sum. The known poly-Bernoulli-relative array $T_{a,b}$ has
bivariate exponential generating function
\[
 T(x,y)=\log\frac1{e^x+e^y-e^{x+y}}
       =\sum_{a,b\ge1}T_{a,b}\frac{x^ay^b}{a!b!}.
\]
The antidiagonal identity recorded in \cite[Section 6]{testart} is
\[
 \mathrm{A136127}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{k+1,n-k}\quad(n\ge1),
 \qquad \mathrm{A136127}(0)=1.
\]
Equation \eqref{vav:eq:integral1} is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:transform}
 L(z)=z+\int_0^z T_x(t,z-t)\dd t.
\end{equation}
Since
\[
 \int_0^z\frac{t^{a-1}(z-t)^b}{(a-1)!b!}\dd t
     =\frac{z^{a+b}}{(a+b)!},
\]
\eqref{vav:eq:known} follows, including $n=1$ separately. Thus $H'/H$ is
the EGF of A136127. The existing objects, array and asymptotic for that
sequence are not new claims of this report. A direct labelled bijection
explaining the exponential transform remains an interesting question.
\begin{writenote}
Part~II, Section~\ref{vav:rf:sec:bijection}, gives such an explanation: a
record-to-cycle bijection whose components are ``separated cycles'', with
the component counts \eqref{vav:rf:eq:cumulants}. It explicitly does not
claim a direct bijection to a previously named excedance-permutation or
tree model of A136127, so that narrower question stays open (see the note
after the questions in Section~\ref{vav:sec:prior}).
\end{writenote}

\section{Analytic continuation and the singular expansion}\label{vav:sec:continuation}

For $|z|<\rho$, the elementary estimate
\begin{align*}
 |(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1)|
 &\le(e^{|z|x}-1)(e^{|z|(1-x)}-1)\\
 &\le(e^{|z|/2}-1)^2<1
\end{align*}
shows that \eqref{vav:eq:integral1} is analytic. The second inequality
follows by minimizing $e^{rx}+e^{r(1-x)}$ at $x=1/2$.
On $|z|=\rho$, equality can occur only at $z=\rho,x=1/2$.
More strongly, the linear ODE continues $L$ along every path avoiding
\[
 \rho+2\pi\ii k,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]
In a disk of radius $R$ with $\rho<R<|\rho+2\pi\ii|$, cut along
$[\rho,R)$, this continuation is single-valued and has no other
singularities. This follows from analytic ODE existence and uniqueness
on a simply connected domain.

Write $w=\rho-z$, with the square root positive for real $w>0$.
Equation \eqref{vav:eq:egf} gives a convergent local decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:local}
 L(z)=\frac\pi{\sqrt{e^w-1}}+R(w),
\end{equation}
where
\[
 R(w)=\frac{\rho-w}{2}
       -\frac{3\arccos(e^{-w/2})}{\sqrt{e^w-1}}
\]
is analytic at zero: the square roots in the quotient cancel.
Its Taylor expansion starts
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:Rexp}
 R(w)=\frac\rho2-3+\frac w2-\frac{w^2}{10}
                  -\frac{w^3}{210}+O(w^4).
\end{equation}
All coefficients can be generated without repeated differentiation of
inverse trigonometric functions. If $R(w)=\sum_{m\ge0}r_mw^m$, then
$r_0=\rho/2-3$ and
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:rrec}
 r_m=\frac{
 -\ind{m=1}-4(-1)^m/m!
 -4\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}
       \frac{(-1)^{m-k}kr_k}{(m-k+1)!}}
 {4m+2}\qquad(m\ge1).
\end{equation}
Indeed substitution in \eqref{vav:eq:ode} gives
\[
 4(1-e^{-w})R'(w)+2R(w)=\rho-w-2-4e^{-w}.
\]
Also define
\[
 s_m=[w^m]\sqrt{\frac w{e^w-1}},\qquad
 s_0=1,\quad s_1=-\frac14,\quad s_2=\frac1{96},\quad s_3=\frac1{384}.
\]
Put $t=1-z/\rho$, $K=\rho/2-3$ and $A=\pi/\sqrt\rho=2c^{3/2}$.
Then, convergently near $\sqrt t=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:puiseux}
 L(\rho(1-t))=K+A t^{-1/2}+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jt^{j/2},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:gj}
 g_{2m-1}=\pi s_m\rho^{m-1/2},\qquad
 g_{2m}=r_m\rho^m\quad(m\ge1).
\end{equation}
In particular $g_1=-\pi\sqrt\rho/4$ and $g_2=\rho/2$.
There is no logarithmic term. This fact determines the power
$n^{-5/6}$ in the main result.

\section{Contour estimates and a complete saddle expansion}\label{vav:sec:saddle}

Set $\delta=n^{-1/3}$, $t_0=c\delta^2$, and take the Cauchy circle
$z=\rho(1-t_0)e^{\ii\theta}$. For fixed sufficiently small $\theta_0$,
\eqref{vav:eq:puiseux} gives uniformly for $|\theta|\le\theta_0$
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:modlocal}
 \log|H(z)|=K+A\Re(t^{-1/2})+O(|t|^{1/2}),\qquad
 t=1-(1-t_0)e^{\ii\theta}.
\end{equation}
For $|\theta|\le\varepsilon t_0$, with $\varepsilon$ small and fixed,
the Taylor expansion of $(1+v)^{-1/2}$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:quaddecay}
 \Re(t^{-1/2})\le t_0^{-1/2}-b\theta^2t_0^{-5/2}
\end{equation}
for a constant $b>0$. Here $\Re t\ge t_0$ and $\Im t$ is comparable
to $-\theta$. For the complementary range
$\varepsilon t_0\le|\theta|\le\theta_0$, use
\[
 |t|^2=t_0^2+2(1-t_0)(1-\cos\theta)
\]
to obtain a fixed fractional loss
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:fracdecay}
 \Re(t^{-1/2})\le |t|^{-1/2}
       \le t_0^{-1/2}(1+b'\varepsilon^2)^{-1/4}.
\end{equation}
On the rest of the circle, $L$ is uniformly bounded as $n$ grows,
by analytic continuation across the compact arc of $|z|=\rho$
that excludes $\rho$.

Consequently every arc outside
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:window}
 |\theta|\le\delta^{5/2}\log(1/\delta)
\end{equation}
is smaller than the main coefficient scale by every negative power
of $n$. Estimate \eqref{vav:eq:quaddecay} treats the intermediate range;
\eqref{vav:eq:fracdecay} treats the remaining local arc. The bounded error
in \eqref{vav:eq:modlocal} cannot offset either loss. The modulus of the
Cauchy coefficient kernel is constant on this circle.

For a simple coefficient formula, deform the central arc to the vertical
segment
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:vertical}
 t=c\delta^2(1+\ii\sqrt\delta\,u).
\end{equation}
One can choose endpoints with $|\Im t|=\varepsilon t_0$.
They differ from the circular arc by $O(t_0^2)$ in real part. The
connecting segments stay in $\Re t\ge t_0$, with $|\Im t|$ comparable
to $\varepsilon t_0$, so the same fixed fractional loss applies.
Their change in $-n\log|1-t|$ is $O(nt_0^2)=O(\delta)$.
They are exponentially negligible, and no singularity is crossed.
Reversing orientation gives a positive Gaussian integral.

The leading phase on \eqref{vav:eq:vertical} is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:phase}
 A t^{-1/2}+nt=\frac{3c}\delta-\frac{3c}{4}u^2
                        +O(\sqrt\delta\,|u|^3).
\end{equation}
The Cauchy Jacobian is $c\delta^{5/2}/(2\pi)$; the remaining
$(1-t)^{-1}$ will be included explicitly below. Therefore the leading
factor, apart from $n!\rho^{-n}e^{3c/\delta}$, is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:prefactor}
 \frac{e^Kc\delta^{5/2}}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3c}}
     =2e^{-3}\sqrt{\frac c{3\pi}}\,n^{-5/6}.
\end{equation}
In particular $nt^2/2=O(\delta)$ affects the first correction, not
this constant.

\section{A finite generator for every correction}\label{vav:sec:generator}

Let $U$ be a centered Gaussian variable of variance $2/(3c)$.
Define the following formal series in $\sqrt\delta$:
\begin{align}
 Q(\delta,U)={}&2c\sum_{k\ge3}\binom{-1/2}{k}
                      (\ii U)^k\delta^{k/2-1}\notag\\
 &+\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{c^m}{m}\delta^{2m-3}
                      (1+\ii\sqrt\delta U)^m\notag\\
 &+\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c^m}{m}\delta^{2m}
                      (1+\ii\sqrt\delta U)^m\notag\\
 &+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jc^{j/2}\delta^j
                      (1+\ii\sqrt\delta U)^{j/2}.
 \label{vav:eq:Q}
\end{align}
The four lines are, respectively, the principal phase after removing
its constant, linear and quadratic terms;
$n[-\log(1-t)-t]$; the extra $-\log(1-t)$; and the Puiseux perturbation.
The coefficients in Theorem~\ref{vav:thm:main} are
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:gaussian}
 a_j=[\delta^j]\E e^{Q(\delta,U)}.
\end{equation}
Every requested coefficient involves finitely many terms. Odd powers
of $\sqrt\delta$ are odd in $U$ and integrate to zero. The Gaussian
moments then imply $a_j\in\mathbb Q[c,c^{-1},\rho]$.

We spell out why the formal calculation supplies the asserted error
bound. Restrict \eqref{vav:eq:vertical} to $|u|\le\log(1/\delta)$.
Its complement is superpolynomially small by the quadratic estimate.
On this window the principal phase remainder is
$O(\sqrt\delta(1+|u|^3))$. Every other summand and every fixed finite
Taylor remainder are bounded by a power of $\delta$ times a polynomial
in $u$, uniformly on the window. This follows from the convergent
local expansion \eqref{vav:eq:puiseux} and the analytic binomial and
logarithm series. The exact integrand after its leading normalization
is bounded by $C_0e^{-b_0u^2}$, since
$\sqrt\delta|u|^3=o(1+u^2)$ there. Expanding to two extra half-orders,
and bounding the exponential Taylor remainder by an integrable
polynomial times $e^{-b_0u^2}$, gives a remainder
$O_J(\delta^{J+1})$. The next odd half-order vanishes on the symmetric
window and on its Gaussian extension. No residual logarithmic loss
is hidden in the remainder. This completes the proof of
\eqref{vav:eq:main} and \eqref{vav:eq:gaussian}.

\]

\section{A controlled inverse and integer thresholds}\label{vav:sec:inverse}

There exists a smooth increasing realization $F(x)$ for all sufficiently
large $x$ with $F(n)=A_n$, whose logarithm has a differentiable all-orders
expansion. Here is a construction, to avoid assuming a canonical
analytic interpolation. Realize the formal logarithmic expansion by
a locally finite sum with successively later smooth cutoffs. Choose
the cutoffs so its tail and each fixed derivative satisfy the desired
bounds. If $G$ is the resulting smooth logarithm, the discrepancies
$\log A_n-G(n)$ are smaller than every negative power of $n$.
Multiply each discrepancy by a fixed smooth bump centered at $n$,
with value one there and support of radius less than $1/3$.
The bumps have disjoint supports; their sum and each fixed derivative
are flat to all algebraic orders. Exponentiating the corrected $G$
gives $F$. Its logarithmic derivative is asymptotic to
$\log(x/\rho)>0$, proving eventual monotonicity.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:logasym}
 \log F(x)=x\log\frac{x}{e\rho}+Cx^{1/3}
    +\alpha\log x+d+\sum_{j\ge1}\beta_jx^{-j/3},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
 C&=3c,&\alpha&=-\frac13,&d&=\log(D\sqrt{2\pi}),\\
 \beta_1&=a_1,&\beta_2&=a_2-\frac{a_1^2}{2},&
 \beta_3&=a_3-a_1a_2+\frac{a_1^3}{3}+\frac1{12}.
\end{align*}
The $1/12$ comes from Stirling's expansion for $\log\Gamma(x+1)$;
later Stirling terms are included at their respective orders.

For $Y\to\infty$ define
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:N}
 L_Y=\log Y,\qquad N=\frac{L_Y}{W(L_Y/(e\rho))},\qquad
 \ell=\log(N/\rho)=W(L_Y/(e\rho))+1.
\end{equation}
Thus $N\log(N/(e\rho))=L_Y$. Put
\begin{align}
 a&=-\frac C\ell,&
 b&=\frac13+\frac{\log\rho/3-d}{\ell},\label{vav:eq:inverseab}\\
 d_1&=-\frac{a^2/2+Ca/3+\beta_1}{\ell},&
 e_1&=-\frac{(a+C/3)b+\alpha a+\beta_2}{\ell}.
 \label{vav:eq:inversede}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:inverse}
 F^{-1}(Y)=N+N^{1/3}a+b+N^{-1/3}d_1+N^{-2/3}e_1
                           +O\left(\frac1{N\ell}\right).
\end{equation}
Omitting the two decaying corrections leaves an error
$O(N^{-1/3}/\ell)$, already $o(1)$.

To prove \eqref{vav:eq:inverse}, substitute its displayed approximation
in \eqref{vav:eq:logasym}. The terms of orders
$N^{1/3}$, $1$, $N^{-1/3}$ and $N^{-2/3}$ vanish successively by
\eqref{vav:eq:inverseab}--\eqref{vav:eq:inversede}. The remaining logarithmic
residual is $O(N^{-1})$. Its derivative is asymptotic to $\ell$, so
the mean value theorem divides that residual by a quantity comparable
to $\ell$. All four coefficient functions remain bounded as
$\ell\to\infty$.

Successive cancellation gives a complete inverse expansion in powers
of $N^{-1/3}$, beginning with $N^{1/3}$, with coefficients rational
in $\ell$. There is no separate growing logarithm in these coefficients,
since $\log N=\ell+\log\rho$. Every logarithmic residual is converted
to an inverse error by the same derivative bound. Changing the smooth
realization by a flat function leaves every algebraic order unchanged.

For the eventual integer threshold
$\tau(Y)=\min\{n:A_n\ge Y\}$, the monotone realization gives
$\tau(Y)=\lceil F^{-1}(Y)\rceil$ for sufficiently large $Y$.
If an approximation $X$ has an established bound
$|F^{-1}(Y)-X(Y)|\le E(Y)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:ceiling}
 \lceil X-E\rceil\le\tau(Y)\le\lceil X+E\rceil.
\end{equation}
An $o(1)$ approximation alone does not justify unconditional rounding
when $X$ approaches an integer. The numerical checks below are not
certificates of explicit constants in the asymptotic remainder.

\section{Exact marking and a direct bijection}\label{vav:rf:sec:marking}
Let $A_{n,k,j}$ count $\{3,2+2\}$-free naturally labelled posets on $[n]$ having $k$ distinct nonempty strict downsets and $j$ isolated elements. The total is A113226$(n)$ through the bijection of Bevan--Cheon--Kitaev (BCK) with $12$--$34$-avoiding permutations~\cite{bck}. The statistics here are defined on the poset side; an identification with a usual permutation statistic is not asserted.

BCK Proposition~7 writes a poset as
\[
 E_1,M_1,\ldots,E_k,M_k,E_{k+1}.
\]
The first $2k$ blocks are nonempty; the last is arbitrary and consists precisely of the isolated elements. The $M_i$ group maxima with equal nonempty downsets, and the $E_i$ are successive differences of these nested downsets. Each label of $M_i$ exceeds every label of $E_1\cup\cdots\cup E_i$. Zero blocks are written in decreasing order and one blocks in increasing order.

Put
\[
 A_n(u,s)=\sum_{k,j}A_{n,k,j}u^ks^j,\qquad
 H(z,u,s)=\sum_{n\ge0}A_n(u,s)\frac{z^n}{n!},\qquad H(z,u)=H(z,u,1).
\]
The marked transfer identity is
\begin{align}
 H(z,u,s)&=e^{(s-1)z}H(z,u),\label{vav:rf:eq:iso}\\
 \log H(z,u)&=z+\int_0^1
 \frac{uz(e^z-e^{zx})}{1-u(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1)}\dd x.\label{vav:rf:eq:exact}
\end{align}
To obtain it, apply the maximum-coordinate transfer of Part~I (Section~\ref{vav:sec:transfer}), putting weight $u$ on every added block pair. With
\[
 a(x)=(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1),\qquad b(x)=z(e^z-e^{zx}),
\]
the continuation function satisfies
\[
 f(x)=1+u a(x)f(x)+u\int_x^1 b(t)f(t)\dd t.
\]
For $h=(1-ua)f$, differentiation gives $h'=-ub h/(1-ua)$ and $h(1)=1$. The final block supplies $e^{sz}$. Integration over label-order chambers gives the coefficient normalization $1/n!$.

\subsection{The record and cycle construction}\label{vav:rf:sec:bijection}
A \emph{separated cycle} is a cyclic list of pairs $(E_i,M_i)$ of nonempty disjoint label sets such that every $E$ label is smaller than every $M$ label. Cycles are identified under rotation, never reflection. Each has a unique representative beginning with the $E$ block containing its largest $E$ label.

Given a BCK word, remove its final isolated block. Set $e_i=\max E_i$ and cut immediately before every strict left-to-right record of $e_1,\ldots,e_k$. In a segment beginning at $r$, the first maximum $e_r$ exceeds all later $E$-block maxima in that segment. Every $M$ label in the segment exceeds $e_r$ by the original word condition. The segment therefore represents a separated cycle in its unique maximum-$E$-first rotation. Retain the cycles as an unordered set, and the isolated labels as singleton components.

Conversely, rotate each separated cycle to that representative, sort the cycles by increasing largest $E$ label, and concatenate them. Every $M$ label exceeds all $E$ labels in its own cycle. Its cycle maximum exceeds every $E$ label of all earlier cycles, so the cross-cycle inequalities hold as well. Append the singleton labels as the final decreasing zero block. Each cycle start is an $E$-maximum record, and no later pair within that cycle is a record. The cuts recover exactly the original cycles. These operations are inverse, including empty objects, and preserve both statistics.

Let $\stirling{n}{m}$ denote a Stirling number of the second kind and let
$L_n(u)=n![z^n]\log H(z,u)$. We have $L_1=1$ and, for $n\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:cumulants}
 L_n(u)=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}m!(m-1)!u^m
       \sum_{a=m}^{n-m}\stirling{a}{m}\stirling{n-a}{m}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the $a$ labels assigned to the $E$ blocks of one component must be its $a$ smallest labels. Partition its two label sets into $m$ blocks, match the blocks in $m!$ ways, and cyclically order the pairs in $(m-1)!$ ways. The labelled set construction then gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:bell}
 A_0(u,1)=1,\qquad
 A_{n+1}(u,1)=\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr L_{r+1}(u)A_{n-r}(u,1).
\end{equation}
Thus the first polynomials are
$1,1,1+u,1+5u,1+17u+5u^2$.
At $u=1$ the component counts, including the singleton, are the shifted A136127 sequence identified in Part~I, \eqref{vav:eq:known}. This provides explicit cycle objects for the exponential transform; a direct bijection to a particular previously named excedance-permutation or tree model is not claimed.

For an analytic check of~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:cumulants}, set
\[
 T(x,y,u)=-\log(1-u(e^x-1)(e^y-1)).
\]
Then~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:exact} is $z+\int_0^zT_x(t,z-t,u)\dd t$. Expansion of $T$ and a beta integral give the same formula.

\section{A convergent local singular expansion}\label{vav:rf:sec:singular}
For $u>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:parameters}
 a=u^{-1/2},\quad \rho=2\log(1+a),\quad
 c=\left(\frac{\pi^2a}{4\rho}\right)^{1/3},\quad
 K=\frac\rho2-2-a,\quad D=e^K\sqrt{\frac{c}{3\pi}}.
\end{equation}
These functions extend holomorphically to a sufficiently small complex neighborhood of each compact positive $u$-interval, using the positive real branches.
With $w=\rho-z$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:sing}
 \log H(\rho-w,u)=\pi\sqrt a\,w^{-1/2}S(w,u)+R(w,u),
\end{equation}
where $S,R$ are jointly analytic near $w=0$, $S(0,u)=1$, and $R(0,u)=K$.
The following formulas determine every coefficient:
\begin{align}
 q&=\cosh(w/2)-a\sinh(w/2),& h&=a\cosh(w/2)-\sinh(w/2),\notag\\
 \mathcal T&=\coth((\rho-w)/4),& S&=\frac ha\sqrt{\frac{aw}{1-q^2}},\label{vav:rf:eq:SR}\\
 R&=\frac{\rho-w}{2}-\frac{2h}{1+q}
 \sum_{m\ge0}\frac{(-1)^m\mathcal T^{2m+1}}{2m+1}
                 \left(\frac{1-q}{1+q}\right)^m.\notag
\end{align}
The quotient in $S$ is analytic and equals one at the origin; the series for $R$ converges normally in a common sufficiently small neighborhood.

For derivation, symmetrize~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:exact}. With
$q=\cosh(z/2)-1/(2u e^{z/2})$ and $N=1-u+u e^z$, the elementary integral is
\[
 \log H=\frac z2+\frac{N}{u e^{z/2}\sqrt{1-q^2}}
 \arctan\left(\tanh(z/4)\sqrt{\frac{1+q}{1-q}}\right).
\]
Near the positive singularity use $\arctan X=\pi/2-\arctan(1/X)$ and $N/(u e^{z/2})=2h$. This yields~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:sing}--\eqref{vav:rf:eq:SR}. Apparent $q=-1$ points elsewhere are not presumed singular; some are removable.

Writing $t=1-z/\rho$, we obtain the convergent expansion
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:puiseux}
 \log H(\rho(1-t),u)=K+2c^{3/2}t^{-1/2}+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jt^{j/2}.
\end{equation}
If $S=\sum s_mw^m$ and $R=\sum r_mw^m$, then
\begin{align}
 g_{2m-1}&=\pi\sqrt a\,s_m\rho^{m-1/2},&g_{2m}&=r_m\rho^m,\notag\\
 s_1&=\frac{a^2-3}{8a},&s_2&=\frac{9a^4+10a^2-15}{384a^2},&r_1&=\frac{4-a^3}{6a}.\label{vav:rf:eq:g}
\end{align}
These specialize at $u=1$ to Part~I's expansion \eqref{vav:eq:puiseux}--\eqref{vav:eq:gj}.

\section{Complex uniformity of the first saddle}\label{vav:rf:sec:first}
\begin{theorem}\label{vav:rf:thm:first}
For each compact interval $\mathcal U\subset(0,\infty)$ there is a complex neighborhood $\Omega$ of $\mathcal U$ and holomorphic coefficients $a_j(u)$, $a_0=1$, such that for every fixed $J\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:first}
 \frac{A_n(u,1)}{n!}
 =D\rho^{-n}n^{-5/6}e^{3cn^{1/3}}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^J a_j(u)n^{-j/3}+O_{\mathcal U,J}(n^{-(J+1)/3})\right),
\end{equation}
uniformly on a fixed closed neighborhood of $\mathcal U$ contained in $\Omega$.
The normalized remainder is holomorphic and has the same fixed-derivative bounds on a smaller neighborhood. Uniformly for $s$ in any fixed compact subset of $\mathbb C$, the analogous result has leading amplitude $D e^{(s-1)\rho}$ and computable coefficients $a_j(u,s)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We supply the complex-parameter details because real pointwise asymptotics would be insufficient for the later limit and local laws. For real $u\in \mathcal U$, $r=\rho(u)$ and $|z|\le r$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:denom}
 |u(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1)|
 \le u(e^{|z|x}-1)(e^{|z|(1-x)}-1)\le1.
\end{equation}
On $|z|=r$, equality is possible only at $x=1/2,z=r$. For $0<x<1$, equality in the exponential power-series triangle inequality forces $z$ positive real; the endpoints $x=0,1$ give zero. Thus the denominator in~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:exact} is nonzero on the compact normalized set $z=\rho(u)\zeta$, $|\zeta|\le1$, $|\zeta-1|\ge\epsilon$. It remains nonzero in an open neighborhood of this set for $u$ in a small complex neighborhood of $\mathcal U$. Near $\zeta=1$, the jointly analytic local representation~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:sing} supplies continuation in a common slit neighborhood. The two representations agree on their initial intersection. This constructs the needed common dented domain, without classifying every apparent singularity of the elementary expression.

Set $\delta=n^{-1/3}$ and use the circle
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:circle}
 z=\rho(u)(1-c(u)\delta^2)e^{\ii\theta},\qquad -\pi\le\theta\le\pi.
\end{equation}
Shrink $\Omega$ so that $\Re c>c_*>0$, $\Re(1/c)>b_*>0$, and all the continuation and local expansion bounds hold uniformly. Near $\theta=0$, $\Re(1-(1-c\delta^2)e^{\ii\theta})>0$ for large $n$, so the circle lies in the common domain. On its fixed outer arc $\log H$ is bounded, whereas the main exponential modulus is $e^{3\Re(c)/\delta}$ and the radial Cauchy factor is $e^{\Re(c)/\delta+O(\delta)}$.

On the local arc put $\theta=\delta^2y$. Up to $O(\delta)$ for bounded $y$, the nonconstant leading phase, after removing the radial factor, is $\delta^{-1}\Phi_c(y)$, where
\[
 \Phi_c(y)=2c^{3/2}(c-\ii y)^{-1/2}-\ii y,\quad
 \Phi_c(0)=2c,\quad \Phi_c'(0)=0,\quad \Phi_c''(0)=-\frac{3}{2c}.
\]
For small $|y|$ the real-part loss is at least $b y^2/\delta$, up to a harmless small shift. More precisely the exact circle leaves an uncancelled linear term $O(\delta^{-1}\theta)=O(\delta |y|)$. On the Gaussian scale $y=\sqrt\delta X$ it is $O(\delta^{3/2}|X|)$; completing the square gives a negligible shift. Outside a growing Gaussian cutoff it is absorbed by the quadratic loss.

For $\epsilon\le |y|\le M$, there is a uniform fixed loss at real $u$, since
$\Re(c-\ii y)^{-1/2}<c^{-1/2}$, and it persists under small complex perturbations by compactness. If $|\theta|\ge M\delta^2$ within the local arc, then $|t|\ge b|\theta|$ and
\[
 |2c^{3/2}t^{-1/2}|\le C\delta^{-1}/\sqrt M.
\]
Choose $M$ large. This gives a fixed fractional loss from the main exponent. Hence every arc outside
$|\theta|\le\delta^{5/2}\log(1/\delta)$ is smaller than the main scale by every power of $n$, uniformly in $u$. Bounded $O(t^{1/2})$ perturbations do not change the conclusion.

Deform the central integral to
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:vertical}
 t=c\delta^2(1+\ii\sqrt\delta X),\qquad X\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation}
For real $u$, take short connecting curves at fixed scaled endpoints $|X|=\epsilon/\sqrt\delta$, inside open neighborhoods with a fixed leading-phase loss. Continue this choice of curves continuously as $u$ leaves the real interval, using a finite cover of $\mathcal U$. Their compact images remain in the loss region after shrinking the common neighborhood. No connector crosses $t=0$ or the cut. This specifies a uniform valid deformation for complex $u$.

\begin{samepage}
On $|X|\le\log(1/\delta)$ the phase is $3c/\delta-3cX^2/4+Q$, with
\begin{align}\label{vav:rf:eq:Q}
 Q={}&2c\sum_{m\ge3}\binom{-1/2}{m}(\ii X)^m\delta^{m/2-1}\notag\\
 &+\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{c^m}{m}\delta^{2m-3}(1+\ii\sqrt\delta X)^m
  +\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c^m}{m}\delta^{2m}(1+\ii\sqrt\delta X)^m\notag\\
 &+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jc^{j/2}\delta^j(1+\ii\sqrt\delta X)^{j/2}.
\end{align}
\end{samepage}
Thus $a_j=[\delta^j]\E e^Q$, where the formal Gaussian moments are
\[
 \E X^{2m}=(2m-1)!!\left(\frac{2}{3c}\right)^m,\qquad \E X^{2m+1}=0.
\]
For complex $c$ these mean analytic Gaussian integration; $\Re c>0$ ensures convergence. The leading Jacobian and Gaussian integral give $e^K\sqrt{c/(3\pi)}\delta^{5/2}$.

The convergent local series and the logarithmic/binomial expansions give uniform Taylor remainders of any requested power of $\delta$ times a polynomial in $X$. After multiplication by the Gaussian they are dominated by $C e^{-bX^2}$ times a polynomial. Expand two extra half-orders and use parity; the resulting remainder is $O(\delta^{J+1})$, without a logarithmic loss. Holomorphy and Cauchy estimates give parameter derivatives. Finally $e^{(s-1)z}$ changes $K$ to $K+(s-1)\rho$ and $g_2$ to $g_2-(s-1)\rho$, with all estimates uniform for bounded $s$.
\end{proof}

Put $T_0=c^2/2+g_1\sqrt c$. The first two coefficients are
\begin{align}
 a_1&=T_0-\frac5{36c}
 =c^2\left(\frac12+\frac{\rho(a^2-3)}{4a}\right)-\frac5{36c},\label{vav:rf:eq:a1}\\
 a_2&=\frac{T_0^2}{2}+cg_2-\frac{17g_1}{36\sqrt c}
                   -\frac{17c}{72}-\frac{35}{2592c^2}.\label{vav:rf:eq:a2}
\end{align}
Formula~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:Q} is a finite algorithm at every fixed order; the supplied program also evaluates the third order.

\section{The marking phase gap}\label{vav:rf:sec:gap}
\begin{lemma}\label{vav:rf:lem:gap}
For any compact positive interval $\mathcal U$ and $\epsilon>0$, there are $\eta>0$ and $C<\infty$ such that, uniformly for $u\in \mathcal U$ and $\epsilon\le|\theta|\le\pi$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:gap}
 |[z^n]H(z,u e^{\ii\theta},s)|\le C(\rho(u)+\eta)^{-n},
\end{equation}
with $s$ in any prescribed compact set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $|z|\le\rho(u)$, inequality~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:denom} is strict except at $x=1/2,z=\rho(u)$. At that exceptional point multiplication by $e^{\ii\theta}$ gives $e^{\ii\theta}\ne1$. The denominator is therefore nonzero on the entire compact parameter set, and remains nonzero for $|z|\le\rho(u)+\eta$ with uniform $\eta>0$. The integral and exponential are uniformly bounded there. Cauchy's estimate proves the result.
\end{proof}
This excludes other periodic saddles in the marking variable. It is a separate ingredient beyond a positive-real asymptotic formula.

\section{Precise local large deviations}\label{vav:rf:sec:ldp}
Use $v=\log u$ and put
\[
 f(v)=-\log\rho(e^v),\qquad d(v)=\log D(e^v),\qquad
 \alpha(u)=\frac{1}{(1+\sqrt u)\rho(u)},\qquad V(u)=f''(v).
\]
In this and the next section, primes on $c$ or $\rho$ mean derivatives of $c(e^v)$ or $\rho(e^v)$ with respect to $v$, evaluated at $v=\log u$. Then $f'=\alpha$. With $a=u^{-1/2}$ and $\ell=\log(1+a)$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:V}
 V(u)=\frac{a(a-\ell)}{4(1+a)^2\ell^2}>0.
\end{equation}
The map $\alpha:(0,\infty)\to(0,1/2)$ is increasing and onto.

\begin{theorem}\label{vav:rf:thm:local}
Suppose $k/n$ lies in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,1/2)$, and choose the unique $u>0$ with $\alpha(u)=k/n$. For each fixed $J\ge0$,
\begin{align}\label{vav:rf:eq:local}
 A_{n,k}:=\sum_jA_{n,k,j}
 ={}&\frac{n!D(u)\rho(u)^{-n}u^{-k}e^{3c(u)n^{1/3}}}
             {\sqrt{2\pi V(u)}\,n^{4/3}}\notag\\
 &\qquad\times\left(\sum_{j=0}^J b_j(u)n^{-j/3}
                      +O(n^{-(J+1)/3})\right),
\end{align}
uniformly in these integer pairs. The coefficients are analytic and explicitly computable. In particular
\begin{align}
 b_0={}&1,\qquad b_1=a_1-\frac{9(c')^2}{2V},\label{vav:rf:eq:b1}\\
 b_2={}&a_2-\frac{9a_1(c')^2}{2V}+\frac{81(c')^4}{8V^2}
       -\frac{3c''}{2V}-\frac{3c'd'}{V}+\frac{3c'f'''}{2V^2}.\label{vav:rf:eq:b2}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fourier inversion on the $u$-circle integrates $A_n(u e^{\ii\theta},1)u^{-k}e^{-\ii k\theta}$. For small $\theta$ apply Theorem~\ref{vav:rf:thm:first}. Positivity of $V$ gives
\[
 \Re\big(f(v+\ii\theta)-f(v)-\ii\alpha\theta\big)\le-b\theta^2.
\]
Since $c,d$ and their derivatives are real on the real axis, their real-part increments are $O(\theta^2)$. The normalized integrand is bounded by $C e^{-bn\theta^2}$, with a reduced $b>0$ if necessary. Outside a fixed neighborhood, Lemma~\ref{vav:rf:lem:gap} supplies an exponential-in-$n$ gain that beats every stretched factor. Only $|\theta|\le n^{-1/2}\log n$ contributes to algebraic orders.

Set $\delta=n^{-1/3}$ and $\theta=\delta^{3/2}X$. After removal of $e^{-VX^2/2}$ the formal exponent is
\begin{align}\label{vav:rf:eq:P}
 P={}&\sum_{m\ge3}\frac{f^{(m)}(v)}{m!}(\ii X)^m\delta^{3m/2-3}\notag\\
 &+3\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c^{(m)}(v)}{m!}(\ii X)^m\delta^{3m/2-1}
 +\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{d^{(m)}(v)}{m!}(\ii X)^m\delta^{3m/2}.
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:bgen}
 b_j=[\delta^j]\E\left[e^P
        \sum_{r\ge0}a_r(v+\ii\delta^{3/2}X)\delta^r\right],
 \qquad X\sim N(0,1/V),
\end{equation}
where $a_r(v+\ii\theta)$ means $a_r(e^{v+\ii\theta})$ and $a_0=1$.
Only finitely many terms are needed at each order. Odd half-powers have odd $X$ parity and integrate to zero. Uniform analytic Taylor bounds and a Gaussian majorant prove the remainder as in Theorem~\ref{vav:rf:thm:first}. The new Gaussian factor is $n^{-1/2}/\sqrt{2\pi V}$, giving~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:local}; expansion gives~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:b1}--\eqref{vav:rf:eq:b2}.
\end{proof}
The stretched exponential shifts the full marking saddle by
$-3c'/(Vn^{2/3})+O(n^{-1})$ in $v$. Its first logarithmic correction is consequently $-9(c')^2n^{-1/3}/(2V)$, agreeing with~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:b1}. Keeping the first-saddle coefficients unchanged would miss this term.

For a uniform object, write $K_n$ for its downset count. Dividing by~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:first} at $u=1$ gives
\begin{align}\label{vav:rf:eq:ldp}
 \Prob(K_n=k)&\sim\frac{D(u)}{D(1)\sqrt{2\pi nV(u)}}
  \exp\left(-nI(\alpha)+3[c(u)-c(1)]n^{1/3}\right),\\
 I(\alpha)&=\alpha\log u+\log\rho(u)-\log\rho(1),\qquad \alpha=k/n.\notag
\end{align}
The rate is nonnegative, vanishes only at $\alpha(1)$, and has
$I'=\log u$, $I''=1/V(u)>0$. All relative corrections follow by series division.

\section{Gaussian and isolated element laws}\label{vav:rf:sec:laws}
Put $\rho_0=\log4$ and $c_0=c(1)$. Uniform analyticity gives
\begin{align}
 \E K_n={}&\frac{n}{2\rho_0}
 +\frac{c_0(1-\rho_0)}{2\rho_0}n^{1/3}
 +\frac16+\frac{1}{12\rho_0}+O(n^{-1/3}),\label{vav:rf:eq:mean}\\
 \Var K_n={}&V(1)n+3c''\big|_{u=1}n^{1/3}+d''(0)+O(n^{-1/3}),\notag\\
 V(1)={}&\frac{1}{4\rho_0^2}-\frac{1}{8\rho_0}>0.\notag
\end{align}
Useful derivative identities are
$3c'=c(\alpha-1/2)$ and $3c''=c[V+(\alpha-1/2)^2/3]$.
The central and lattice local limits are
\begin{align}
 \frac{K_n-n/(2\rho_0)}{\sqrt{nV(1)}}&\ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,1),\label{vav:rf:eq:clt}\\
 \sup_{k\in\mathbb Z}\left|\sqrt{nV(1)}\Prob(K_n=k)
 -\phi\left(\frac{k-n/(2\rho_0)}{\sqrt{nV(1)}}\right)\right|&\longrightarrow0.\label{vav:rf:eq:llt}
\end{align}
Here $\phi(x)=e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$. For~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:llt}, the rescaled characteristic functions converge on each compact interval. Their tails vanish in $L^1$ by the Gaussian bound from Theorem~\ref{vav:rf:thm:local} and Lemma~\ref{vav:rf:lem:gap}. Fourier inversion bounds the uniform local error by this $L^1$ error. Centering by the true mean gives the same statements.

Let $J_n$ count isolated elements. Equation~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:iso} and the complex-uniform theorem imply
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:joint}
 \left(\frac{K_n-n/(2\rho_0)}{\sqrt{nV(1)}},J_n\right)
 \Longrightarrow (Z,P),\qquad
 Z\sim N(0,1),\quad P\sim\Pois(\rho_0),\quad Z\perp P.
\end{equation}
The joint transform factors in the limit into $e^{-t^2/2}e^{(s-1)\rho_0}$. Taking real $t$ and $s$ on the unit circle proves the assertion by the characteristic-function criterion.

There is also a uniform conditional law at atypical density. With $u$ determined by $k/n=\alpha(u)$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:conditional}
 \mathcal L(J_n\mid K_n=k)=\Pois(\rho(u))+O_{\TV}(n^{-2/3}).
\end{equation}
Indeed the analytic multiplier changes the first-saddle data to
\[
 a_1(u,s)=a_1(u),\quad a_2(u,s)=a_2(u)-(s-1)\rho c,
 \quad d_s=d+(s-1)\rho.
\]
Applying the second saddle and dividing gives, uniformly for bounded $s$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:conditionalcorrection}
 \E[s^{J_n}\mid K_n=k]=e^{(s-1)\rho}
 \left\{1-(s-1)\left(\rho c+\frac{3c'\rho'}{V}\right)n^{-2/3}
                  +O(n^{-1})\right\}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $R>1$ this error is holomorphic and uniform on $|s|\le R$; Cauchy coefficient bounds and summation of $R^{-j}$ prove~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:conditional}. Unconditionally,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:unconditional}
 \E[s^{J_n}]=e^{(s-1)\rho_0}
 \{1-(s-1)\rho_0c_0n^{-2/3}+O(n^{-1})\},
\end{equation}
so the same total-variation rate applies. Finite covariance is not zero despite the limiting independence: differentiating the tilted version gives
\[
 \Cov(K_n,J_n)=-\frac12+O(n^{-2/3}).
\]

       +c^6(98172\rho^2-173016\rho+28188)\\
