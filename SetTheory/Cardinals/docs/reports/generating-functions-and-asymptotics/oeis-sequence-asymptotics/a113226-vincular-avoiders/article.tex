\begin{abstract}
For OEIS A113226, the permutations avoiding the vincular pattern
$12$--$34$, equivalently the $\{3,2+2\}$-free naturally labelled posets of
Bevan, Cheon and Kitaev, Part~I derives a closed exponential generating
function, proves the stretched-exponential form conjectured by those
authors with exact constants and power $-5/6$, gives every algebraic
correction in powers of $n^{-1/3}$ by a finite Gaussian algorithm, an
exact quadratic-arithmetic recurrence and controlled Lambert-$W$ inverse
expansions. Part~II marks the number of distinct nonempty strict downsets
and of isolated elements: it explains the exponential transform behind
A113226 by a record-to-cycle bijection, proves a complex-uniform
all-orders saddle expansion of the marked generating function, precise
all-orders local large deviations for interior densities, Gaussian and
local limit laws, and a conditional Poisson law of isolated elements with
an explicit correction and a total-variation error of order $n^{-2/3}$.
All expansions are Poincar\'e expansions to each fixed order.
The report is AI-assisted and unrefereed; no statement of it is
formalized.

\emph{Added 5 October 2026 (batch 101).} Parts~III and~IV print two
further manuscripts. Part~III is an independent, same-day second proof of
Part~I's main theorem, by a diagonal-state argument directly on
permutations; it adds integral forms of Elizalde's block functions and
proves that the generating function is differentially algebraic but not
D-finite, so that A113226 is not P-recursive. Part~IV writes $A_n/n!$
exactly as an absolutely convergent sum of Hankel sectors at the
singularities $\log4+2\pi\mathrm ik$, isolates the first exponentially
small conjugate pair with a geometric error, and gives every fixed sector a
complete expansion; this answers Part~I's question on exponentially
improved expansions for each fixed sector. Its sector expansions are again
Poincar\'e expansions; the decomposition itself is exact.
\end{abstract}

\section{The result and its context}\label{vav:sec:intro}

Let $A_n$ count permutations of $[n]$ avoiding the vincular pattern
$12$--$34$, with $A_0=1$, and put
\[
 H(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}A_n\frac{z^n}{n!},\qquad L(z)=\log H(z),\quad L(0)=0.
\]
An occurrence requires positions $i,i+1,j,j+1$ with $i+1<j$ and
$\pi_i<\pi_{i+1}<\pi_j<\pi_{j+1}$. This adjacency convention matters.
The sequence is OEIS A113226 \cite{oeis}.

Bevan--Cheon--Kitaev \cite{bck} identify these objects with
$\{3,2+2\}$-free naturally labelled posets, give a labelled binary-word
model and an insertion recurrence, and conjecture the asymptotic form
\[
 A_n\sim D\,n!\,\rho^{-n}\mu^{n^{1/3}}n^\beta,
 \qquad \rho=\log4.
\]
Their Conjectures 13 and 14 specify the exponential rate and the
stretched-exponential form, with fitted estimates for $D,\mu,\beta$.
Elizalde \cite{elizalde} obtained earlier bounds. The exact expression
below resolves both conjectural forms and identifies their constants.
\begin{writenote}
Elizalde's paper also bounds the avoiders of the sibling pattern
$1$--$23$--$4$ (the powered Catalan numbers, OEIS A113227) and leaves their
precise asymptotics open. That question is answered, by a different method,
in the sibling report
\path{oeis-sequence-asymptotics/a113227-powered-catalan/} of this
collection (batch 77, manuscripts 57 and 56); see ``About this report''.
\end{writenote}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:constants}
 c=\left(\frac{\pi^2}{4\rho}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
 D=2e^{-3}\sqrt{\frac{c}{3\pi}},\qquad C=3c,\qquad \mu=e^C.
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}\label{vav:thm:main}
The exponential generating function is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:egf}
 H(z)=\exp\left\{\frac z2+
 \frac{q(3\arcsin q-\pi/2)}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}\right\},
 \qquad q=\frac{e^{z/2}}2,
\end{equation}
with branches analytic at $z=0$. Its logarithm is equivalently the unique
analytic solution of
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:ode}
 (4-e^z)L'(z)-2L(z)=2+e^z-z,\qquad L(0)=0.
\end{equation}
For every fixed integer $J\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:main}
 A_n=D\,n!\,\rho^{-n}e^{3cn^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^{J}a_jn^{-j/3}
       +O_J(n^{-(J+1)/3})\right),
\end{equation}
where $a_0=1$ and all $a_j$ are given by the finite Gaussian coefficient
formula in Section~\ref{vav:sec:generator}. In particular,
\begin{align}
 a_1&=\frac{c^2(1-\rho)}2-\frac5{36c},\label{vav:eq:a1}\\
 a_2&=\frac{324c^6(\rho-1)^2+c^3(1908\rho-612)-35}{2592c^2}.
 \label{vav:eq:a2}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

The constants are
\begin{align*}
 c&=1.21188506246461864249\ldots,&
 D&=0.03570604125804483339\ldots,\\
 C&=3.63565518739385592747\ldots,&
 \mu&=37.92669385021515730\ldots.
\end{align*}
Thus $\beta=-5/6$. The paper's decimal estimates are finite-range fits;
they are not exact conjectured values. In particular, the amplitude
$D$ differs visibly from their estimate near $0.032$.

The theorem is an all-orders Poincar\'e expansion. It neither asserts
convergence of the coefficient series nor identifies all exponentially
small contributions. Its combinatorial foundation belongs to
\cite{bck}; the known sequence A136127 and its leading asymptotic are
also prior work \cite{oeis136,aval,testart}. Our contribution is scoped
to the exact A113226 transform, its complete coefficient asymptotics,
and the resulting inverse.
\begin{writenote}
Part~II extends Theorem~\ref{vav:thm:main} to the generating function
marked by the number $k$ of block pairs in \eqref{vav:eq:blocks} (the
number of distinct nonempty strict downsets) and by the isolated elements,
uniformly for the marking parameter in complex neighbourhoods of compact
positive intervals (Theorem~\ref{vav:rf:thm:first}); at $u=1$ it is the
expansion \eqref{vav:eq:main}.
\end{writenote}
\begin{writenoteb}
Part~III (Report~87, same day, independent) proves
Theorem~\ref{vav:thm:main} a second time, by a diagonal-state argument on
permutations instead of the binary-word model
(Theorems~\ref{vav:dg:thm:egf} and~\ref{vav:dg:thm:asym}). The
``exponentially small contributions'' that the paragraph above leaves
unidentified are identified in Part~IV: $A_n/n!=\sum_kH_k(n)$ exactly, with
contour-defined sectors at $\rho+2\pi\ii k$
(Theorem~\ref{vav:hk:thm:sum}); the sector $H_0$ has exactly the expansion
\eqref{vav:eq:main} (divided by $n!$), and each other fixed sector has its
own complete expansion (Theorem~\ref{vav:hk:thm:allorders}).
Convergence of the coefficient series is still not asserted.
\end{writenoteb}

\section{From labelled words to an integral equation}\label{vav:sec:transfer}

Proposition 7 of \cite{bck} gives a bijection with labelled binary words
beginning in zero, whose labels decrease within a zero run, increase
within a one run, and satisfy the condition that every one label exceeds
all earlier zero labels. Equivalently, an object has the block form
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:blocks}
 E_1,M_1,E_2,M_2,\ldots,E_k,M_k,E_{k+1},
\end{equation}
where $E_i,M_i$ for $i\le k$ are nonempty sets, $E_{k+1}$ is an
arbitrary set, and every label in $M_i$ exceeds all labels in
$E_1\cup\cdots\cup E_i$. Each block has its unique prescribed order.
The case $k=0$ includes the empty object and a single decreasing zero run.

Replace the integer labels by distinct coordinates in $[0,1]$ and
integrate over them. For fixed block cardinalities totaling $n$, divide
by the factorial of every block size. Each ordering chamber has volume
$1/n!$; the unordered coordinates inside each block supply exactly its
block factorial. The coefficient is therefore the number of valid
labelled objects divided by $n!$. There is no extra multinomial factor.
Coincident coordinates have measure zero.

Let $x$ be the largest coordinate among preceding zero blocks, initially
$x=0$. A new nonempty zero block has maximum at most $x$, with weight
$e^{zx}-1$, or creates a new maximum $t>x$, with density
$ze^{zt}\dd t$. Indeed, the maximum of $m$ coordinates has volume
density $mt^{m-1}$, and summing $z^mmt^{m-1}/m!$ gives $ze^{zt}$.
A following nonempty one block then has weight $e^{z(1-t)}-1$.
The final arbitrary zero block has weight $e^z$, independent of the
state. Its labels have no restriction relative to earlier one labels.

If $f(x)$ counts any number of additional zero/one pairs, then
\begin{align}
 f(x)={}&1+(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1)f(x)\notag\\
 &+\int_x^1 z(e^z-e^{zt})f(t)\dd t.\label{vav:eq:transfer}
\end{align}
This holds as a formal series, and analytically for small $|z|$.
Each application of the operator increases size by at least two, so the
formal solution is unique. Put
\[
 D_z(x)=e^{zx}+e^{z(1-x)}-e^z,\quad
 h(x)=D_z(x)f(x),\quad b(x)=z(e^z-e^{zx}).
\]
Then
\[
 h(x)=1+\int_x^1\frac{b(t)h(t)}{D_z(t)}\dd t,
 \qquad h'(x)=-\frac{b(x)}{D_z(x)}h(x),\qquad h(1)=1.
\]
Since $D_z(0)=1$ and $H(z)=e^zf(0)$, this yields
\begin{align}
 L(z)&=z+\int_0^1\frac{z(e^z-e^{zx})}{D_z(x)}\dd x\label{vav:eq:integral1}\\
 &=\frac z2+\frac{ze^z}{2}\int_0^1\frac{\dd x}{D_z(x)}.
 \label{vav:eq:integral2}
\end{align}
For the second equality average $x$ and $1-x$ and use
$e^{zx}+e^{z(1-x)}=D_z(x)+e^z$.

Set $v=z(x-1/2)$ and $q=e^{z/2}/2$. The substitution
$u=\tanh(v/2)$ in \eqref{vav:eq:integral2} gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:atan}
 L(z)=\frac z2+\frac{2q}{\sqrt{1-q^2}}
 \arctan\left(\frac{2q-1}{2q+1}\sqrt{\frac{1+q}{1-q}}\right).
\end{equation}
The arctangent vanishes at $q=1/2$ and its derivative is
$3/(2\sqrt{1-q^2})$. It therefore equals
$(3/2)\arcsin q-\pi/4$ near $q=1/2$.
This proves \eqref{vav:eq:egf}. Differentiation proves \eqref{vav:eq:ode}.

\section{Exact recurrences and the known cumulants}\label{vav:sec:cumulants}

Let $\ell_n=L^{(n)}(0)$, with $\ell_0=0$. Coefficient extraction from
\eqref{vav:eq:ode} gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:cumulant}
 3\ell_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom nk\ell_{k+1}
       +2\ell_n+1+2\ind{n=0}-\ind{n=1}\qquad(n\ge0).
\end{equation}
The empty sum is zero. Since $H'=L'H$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:exact}
 A_0=1,\qquad
 A_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk\ell_{k+1}A_{n-k}.
\end{equation}
These recurrences use $O(N^2)$ integer arithmetic operations for all
terms through $N$. This statement counts arithmetic operations, not
bit operations. It gives a fast recurrence of the kind asked for by
Callan \cite[Section 9]{callan}.

The cumulants begin
\[
 (\ell_n)_{n\ge1}=1,1,2,5,16,63,294,1585,9692,66275,\ldots.
\]
They are the known sequence A136127 shifted by one:
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:known}
 \ell_n=\mathrm{A136127}(n-1)\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
Here is an exact verification that also explains the nonbinomial
antidiagonal sum. The known poly-Bernoulli-relative array $T_{a,b}$ has
bivariate exponential generating function
\[
 T(x,y)=\log\frac1{e^x+e^y-e^{x+y}}
       =\sum_{a,b\ge1}T_{a,b}\frac{x^ay^b}{a!b!}.
\]
The antidiagonal identity recorded in \cite[Section 6]{testart} is
\[
 \mathrm{A136127}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{k+1,n-k}\quad(n\ge1),
 \qquad \mathrm{A136127}(0)=1.
\]
Equation \eqref{vav:eq:integral1} is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:transform}
 L(z)=z+\int_0^z T_x(t,z-t)\dd t.
\end{equation}
Since
\[
 \int_0^z\frac{t^{a-1}(z-t)^b}{(a-1)!b!}\dd t
     =\frac{z^{a+b}}{(a+b)!},
\]
\eqref{vav:eq:known} follows, including $n=1$ separately. Thus $H'/H$ is
the EGF of A136127. The existing objects, array and asymptotic for that
sequence are not new claims of this report. A direct labelled bijection
explaining the exponential transform remains an interesting question.

\section{Analytic continuation and the singular expansion}\label{vav:sec:continuation}

For $|z|<\rho$, the elementary estimate
\begin{align*}
 |(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1)|
 &\le(e^{|z|x}-1)(e^{|z|(1-x)}-1)\\
 &\le(e^{|z|/2}-1)^2<1
\end{align*}
shows that \eqref{vav:eq:integral1} is analytic. The second inequality
follows by minimizing $e^{rx}+e^{r(1-x)}$ at $x=1/2$.
On $|z|=\rho$, equality can occur only at $z=\rho,x=1/2$.
More strongly, the linear ODE continues $L$ along every path avoiding
\[
 \rho+2\pi\ii k,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]
In a disk of radius $R$ with $\rho<R<|\rho+2\pi\ii|$, cut along
$[\rho,R)$, this continuation is single-valued and has no other
singularities. This follows from analytic ODE existence and uniqueness
on a simply connected domain.
\begin{writenoteb}
The points $\rho+2\pi\ii k$ are genuine singularities of every
continuation: by Part~III's Proposition~\ref{vav:dg:prop:structure} each is
an essential singularity on every branch, which also shows that the
generating function is not D-finite. Part~IV gives a global single-valued
branch on $\mathbb C$ minus the horizontal cuts $\rho+2\pi\ii k+[0,\infty)$
(period $4\pi\ii$, not $2\pi\ii$) and its exact local form at every
$\rho+2\pi\ii k$, with strength $\pi$ for even and $2\pi$ for odd $k$
(\eqref{vav:hk:eq:F} and Lemma~\ref{vav:hk:lem:local}).
\end{writenoteb}

Write $w=\rho-z$, with the square root positive for real $w>0$.
Equation \eqref{vav:eq:egf} gives a convergent local decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:local}
 L(z)=\frac\pi{\sqrt{e^w-1}}+R(w),
\end{equation}
where
\[
 R(w)=\frac{\rho-w}{2}
       -\frac{3\arccos(e^{-w/2})}{\sqrt{e^w-1}}
\]
is analytic at zero: the square roots in the quotient cancel.
Its Taylor expansion starts
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:Rexp}
 R(w)=\frac\rho2-3+\frac w2-\frac{w^2}{10}
                  -\frac{w^3}{210}+O(w^4).
\end{equation}
All coefficients can be generated without repeated differentiation of
inverse trigonometric functions. If $R(w)=\sum_{m\ge0}r_mw^m$, then
$r_0=\rho/2-3$ and
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:rrec}
 r_m=\frac{
 -\ind{m=1}-4(-1)^m/m!
 -4\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}
       \frac{(-1)^{m-k}kr_k}{(m-k+1)!}}
 {4m+2}\qquad(m\ge1).
\end{equation}
Indeed substitution in \eqref{vav:eq:ode} gives
\[
 4(1-e^{-w})R'(w)+2R(w)=\rho-w-2-4e^{-w}.
\]
Also define
\[
 s_m=[w^m]\sqrt{\frac w{e^w-1}},\qquad
 s_0=1,\quad s_1=-\frac14,\quad s_2=\frac1{96},\quad s_3=\frac1{384}.
\]
Put $t=1-z/\rho$, $K=\rho/2-3$ and $A=\pi/\sqrt\rho=2c^{3/2}$.
Then, convergently near $\sqrt t=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:puiseux}
 L(\rho(1-t))=K+A t^{-1/2}+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jt^{j/2},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:gj}
 g_{2m-1}=\pi s_m\rho^{m-1/2},\qquad
 g_{2m}=r_m\rho^m\quad(m\ge1).
\end{equation}
In particular $g_1=-\pi\sqrt\rho/4$ and $g_2=\rho/2$.
There is no logarithmic term. This fact determines the power
$n^{-5/6}$ in the main result.

\section{Contour estimates and a complete saddle expansion}\label{vav:sec:saddle}

Set $\delta=n^{-1/3}$, $t_0=c\delta^2$, and take the Cauchy circle
$z=\rho(1-t_0)e^{\ii\theta}$. For fixed sufficiently small $\theta_0$,
\eqref{vav:eq:puiseux} gives uniformly for $|\theta|\le\theta_0$
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:modlocal}
 \log|H(z)|=K+A\Re(t^{-1/2})+O(|t|^{1/2}),\qquad
 t=1-(1-t_0)e^{\ii\theta}.
\end{equation}
For $|\theta|\le\varepsilon t_0$, with $\varepsilon$ small and fixed,
the Taylor expansion of $(1+v)^{-1/2}$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:quaddecay}
 \Re(t^{-1/2})\le t_0^{-1/2}-b\theta^2t_0^{-5/2}
\end{equation}
for a constant $b>0$. Here $\Re t\ge t_0$ and $\Im t$ is comparable
to $-\theta$. For the complementary range
$\varepsilon t_0\le|\theta|\le\theta_0$, use
\[
 |t|^2=t_0^2+2(1-t_0)(1-\cos\theta)
\]
to obtain a fixed fractional loss
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:fracdecay}
 \Re(t^{-1/2})\le |t|^{-1/2}
       \le t_0^{-1/2}(1+b'\varepsilon^2)^{-1/4}.
\end{equation}
On the rest of the circle, $L$ is uniformly bounded as $n$ grows,
by analytic continuation across the compact arc of $|z|=\rho$
that excludes $\rho$.

Consequently every arc outside
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:window}
 |\theta|\le\delta^{5/2}\log(1/\delta)
\end{equation}
is smaller than the main coefficient scale by every negative power
of $n$. Estimate \eqref{vav:eq:quaddecay} treats the intermediate range;
\eqref{vav:eq:fracdecay} treats the remaining local arc. The bounded error
in \eqref{vav:eq:modlocal} cannot offset either loss. The modulus of the
Cauchy coefficient kernel is constant on this circle.

For a simple coefficient formula, deform the central arc to the vertical
segment
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:vertical}
 t=c\delta^2(1+\ii\sqrt\delta\,u).
\end{equation}
One can choose endpoints with $|\Im t|=\varepsilon t_0$.
They differ from the circular arc by $O(t_0^2)$ in real part. The
connecting segments stay in $\Re t\ge t_0$, with $|\Im t|$ comparable
to $\varepsilon t_0$, so the same fixed fractional loss applies.
Their change in $-n\log|1-t|$ is $O(nt_0^2)=O(\delta)$.
They are exponentially negligible, and no singularity is crossed.
Reversing orientation gives a positive Gaussian integral.

The leading phase on \eqref{vav:eq:vertical} is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:phase}
 A t^{-1/2}+nt=\frac{3c}\delta-\frac{3c}{4}u^2
                        +O(\sqrt\delta\,|u|^3).
\end{equation}
The Cauchy Jacobian is $c\delta^{5/2}/(2\pi)$; the remaining
$(1-t)^{-1}$ will be included explicitly below. Therefore the leading
factor, apart from $n!\rho^{-n}e^{3c/\delta}$, is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:prefactor}
 \frac{e^Kc\delta^{5/2}}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3c}}
     =2e^{-3}\sqrt{\frac c{3\pi}}\,n^{-5/6}.
\end{equation}
In particular $nt^2/2=O(\delta)$ affects the first correction, not
this constant.

\section{A finite generator for every correction}\label{vav:sec:generator}

Let $U$ be a centered Gaussian variable of variance $2/(3c)$.
Define the following formal series in $\sqrt\delta$:
\begin{align}
 Q(\delta,U)={}&2c\sum_{k\ge3}\binom{-1/2}{k}
                      (\ii U)^k\delta^{k/2-1}\notag\\
 &+\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{c^m}{m}\delta^{2m-3}
                      (1+\ii\sqrt\delta U)^m\notag\\
 &+\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c^m}{m}\delta^{2m}
                      (1+\ii\sqrt\delta U)^m\notag\\
 &+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jc^{j/2}\delta^j
                      (1+\ii\sqrt\delta U)^{j/2}.
 \label{vav:eq:Q}
\end{align}
The four lines are, respectively, the principal phase after removing
its constant, linear and quadratic terms;
$n[-\log(1-t)-t]$; the extra $-\log(1-t)$; and the Puiseux perturbation.
The coefficients in Theorem~\ref{vav:thm:main} are
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:gaussian}
 a_j=[\delta^j]\E e^{Q(\delta,U)}.
\end{equation}
Every requested coefficient involves finitely many terms. Odd powers
of $\sqrt\delta$ are odd in $U$ and integrate to zero. The Gaussian
moments then imply $a_j\in\mathbb Q[c,c^{-1},\rho]$.

We spell out why the formal calculation supplies the asserted error
bound. Restrict \eqref{vav:eq:vertical} to $|u|\le\log(1/\delta)$.
Its complement is superpolynomially small by the quadratic estimate.
On this window the principal phase remainder is
$O(\sqrt\delta(1+|u|^3))$. Every other summand and every fixed finite
Taylor remainder are bounded by a power of $\delta$ times a polynomial
in $u$, uniformly on the window. This follows from the convergent
local expansion \eqref{vav:eq:puiseux} and the analytic binomial and
logarithm series. The exact integrand after its leading normalization
is bounded by $C_0e^{-b_0u^2}$, since
$\sqrt\delta|u|^3=o(1+u^2)$ there. Expanding to two extra half-orders,
and bounding the exponential Taylor remainder by an integrable
polynomial times $e^{-b_0u^2}$, gives a remainder
$O_J(\delta^{J+1})$. The next odd half-order vanishes on the symmetric
window and on its Gaussian extension. No residual logarithmic loss
is hidden in the remainder. This completes the proof of
\eqref{vav:eq:main} and \eqref{vav:eq:gaussian}.

For example the coefficient of $\delta$ is
\[
 \frac{c^2(1-\rho)}2+\frac{35c}{64}\E U^4
                    -\frac{25c^2}{128}\E U^6
 =\frac{c^2(1-\rho)}2-\frac5{36c}.
\]
The first four values are

The accompanying \texttt{asymptotic\_coefficients.py} implements the

It generates $s_m$ and \eqref{vav:eq:rrec}, constructs \eqref{vav:eq:Q} to

 E_0=1,\qquad E_k=\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k jQ_jE_{k-j},

\end{equation}

\section{A controlled inverse and integer thresholds}\label{vav:sec:inverse}

There exists a smooth increasing realization $F(x)$ for all sufficiently
large $x$ with $F(n)=A_n$, whose logarithm has a differentiable all-orders
expansion. Here is a construction, to avoid assuming a canonical
analytic interpolation. Realize the formal logarithmic expansion by
a locally finite sum with successively later smooth cutoffs. Choose
the cutoffs so its tail and each fixed derivative satisfy the desired
bounds. If $G$ is the resulting smooth logarithm, the discrepancies
$\log A_n-G(n)$ are smaller than every negative power of $n$.
Multiply each discrepancy by a fixed smooth bump centered at $n$,
with value one there and support of radius less than $1/3$.
The bumps have disjoint supports; their sum and each fixed derivative
are flat to all algebraic orders. Exponentiating the corrected $G$
gives $F$. Its logarithmic derivative is asymptotic to
$\log(x/\rho)>0$, proving eventual monotonicity.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:logasym}
 \log F(x)=x\log\frac{x}{e\rho}+Cx^{1/3}
    +\alpha\log x+d+\sum_{j\ge1}\beta_jx^{-j/3},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
 C&=3c,&\alpha&=-\frac13,&d&=\log(D\sqrt{2\pi}),\\
 \beta_1&=a_1,&\beta_2&=a_2-\frac{a_1^2}{2},&
 \beta_3&=a_3-a_1a_2+\frac{a_1^3}{3}+\frac1{12}.
\end{align*}
The $1/12$ comes from Stirling's expansion for $\log\Gamma(x+1)$;
later Stirling terms are included at their respective orders.

For $Y\to\infty$ define
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:N}
 L_Y=\log Y,\qquad N=\frac{L_Y}{W(L_Y/(e\rho))},\qquad
 \ell=\log(N/\rho)=W(L_Y/(e\rho))+1.
\end{equation}
Thus $N\log(N/(e\rho))=L_Y$. Put
\begin{align}
 a&=-\frac C\ell,&
 b&=\frac13+\frac{\log\rho/3-d}{\ell},\label{vav:eq:inverseab}\\
 d_1&=-\frac{a^2/2+Ca/3+\beta_1}{\ell},&
 e_1&=-\frac{(a+C/3)b+\alpha a+\beta_2}{\ell}.
 \label{vav:eq:inversede}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:inverse}
 F^{-1}(Y)=N+N^{1/3}a+b+N^{-1/3}d_1+N^{-2/3}e_1
                           +O\left(\frac1{N\ell}\right).
\end{equation}
Omitting the two decaying corrections leaves an error
$O(N^{-1/3}/\ell)$, already $o(1)$.

To prove \eqref{vav:eq:inverse}, substitute its displayed approximation
in \eqref{vav:eq:logasym}. The terms of orders
$N^{1/3}$, $1$, $N^{-1/3}$ and $N^{-2/3}$ vanish successively by
\eqref{vav:eq:inverseab}--\eqref{vav:eq:inversede}. The remaining logarithmic
residual is $O(N^{-1})$. Its derivative is asymptotic to $\ell$, so
the mean value theorem divides that residual by a quantity comparable
to $\ell$. All four coefficient functions remain bounded as
$\ell\to\infty$.

Successive cancellation gives a complete inverse expansion in powers
of $N^{-1/3}$, beginning with $N^{1/3}$, with coefficients rational
in $\ell$. There is no separate growing logarithm in these coefficients,
since $\log N=\ell+\log\rho$. Every logarithmic residual is converted
to an inverse error by the same derivative bound. Changing the smooth
realization by a flat function leaves every algebraic order unchanged.

For the eventual integer threshold
$\tau(Y)=\min\{n:A_n\ge Y\}$, the monotone realization gives
$\tau(Y)=\lceil F^{-1}(Y)\rceil$ for sufficiently large $Y$.
If an approximation $X$ has an established bound
$|F^{-1}(Y)-X(Y)|\le E(Y)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{vav:eq:ceiling}
 \lceil X-E\rceil\le\tau(Y)\le\lceil X+E\rceil.
\end{equation}
An $o(1)$ approximation alone does not justify unconditional rounding
when $X$ approaches an integer. The numerical checks below are not
certificates of explicit constants in the asymptotic remainder.

\section{Exact marking and a direct bijection}\label{vav:rf:sec:marking}
Let $A_{n,k,j}$ count $\{3,2+2\}$-free naturally labelled posets on $[n]$ having $k$ distinct nonempty strict downsets and $j$ isolated elements. The total is A113226$(n)$ through the bijection of Bevan--Cheon--Kitaev (BCK) with $12$--$34$-avoiding permutations~\cite{bck}. The statistics here are defined on the poset side; an identification with a usual permutation statistic is not asserted.

BCK Proposition~7 writes a poset as
\[
 E_1,M_1,\ldots,E_k,M_k,E_{k+1}.
\]
The first $2k$ blocks are nonempty; the last is arbitrary and consists precisely of the isolated elements. The $M_i$ group maxima with equal nonempty downsets, and the $E_i$ are successive differences of these nested downsets. Each label of $M_i$ exceeds every label of $E_1\cup\cdots\cup E_i$. Zero blocks are written in decreasing order and one blocks in increasing order.

Put
\[
 A_n(u,s)=\sum_{k,j}A_{n,k,j}u^ks^j,\qquad
 H(z,u,s)=\sum_{n\ge0}A_n(u,s)\frac{z^n}{n!},\qquad H(z,u)=H(z,u,1).
\]
The marked transfer identity is
\begin{align}
 H(z,u,s)&=e^{(s-1)z}H(z,u),\label{vav:rf:eq:iso}\\
 \log H(z,u)&=z+\int_0^1
 \frac{uz(e^z-e^{zx})}{1-u(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1)}\dd x.\label{vav:rf:eq:exact}
\end{align}
To obtain it, apply the maximum-coordinate transfer of Part~I (Section~\ref{vav:sec:transfer}), putting weight $u$ on every added block pair. With
\[
 a(x)=(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1),\qquad b(x)=z(e^z-e^{zx}),
\]
the continuation function satisfies
\[
 f(x)=1+u a(x)f(x)+u\int_x^1 b(t)f(t)\dd t.
\]
For $h=(1-ua)f$, differentiation gives $h'=-ub h/(1-ua)$ and $h(1)=1$. The final block supplies $e^{sz}$. Integration over label-order chambers gives the coefficient normalization $1/n!$.

\subsection{The record and cycle construction}\label{vav:rf:sec:bijection}
A \emph{separated cycle} is a cyclic list of pairs $(E_i,M_i)$ of nonempty disjoint label sets such that every $E$ label is smaller than every $M$ label. Cycles are identified under rotation, never reflection. Each has a unique representative beginning with the $E$ block containing its largest $E$ label.

Given a BCK word, remove its final isolated block. Set $e_i=\max E_i$ and cut immediately before every strict left-to-right record of $e_1,\ldots,e_k$. In a segment beginning at $r$, the first maximum $e_r$ exceeds all later $E$-block maxima in that segment. Every $M$ label in the segment exceeds $e_r$ by the original word condition. The segment therefore represents a separated cycle in its unique maximum-$E$-first rotation. Retain the cycles as an unordered set, and the isolated labels as singleton components.

Conversely, rotate each separated cycle to that representative, sort the cycles by increasing largest $E$ label, and concatenate them. Every $M$ label exceeds all $E$ labels in its own cycle. Its cycle maximum exceeds every $E$ label of all earlier cycles, so the cross-cycle inequalities hold as well. Append the singleton labels as the final decreasing zero block. Each cycle start is an $E$-maximum record, and no later pair within that cycle is a record. The cuts recover exactly the original cycles. These operations are inverse, including empty objects, and preserve both statistics.

Let $\stirling{n}{m}$ denote a Stirling number of the second kind and let
$L_n(u)=n![z^n]\log H(z,u)$. We have $L_1=1$ and, for $n\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:cumulants}
 L_n(u)=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}m!(m-1)!u^m
       \sum_{a=m}^{n-m}\stirling{a}{m}\stirling{n-a}{m}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the $a$ labels assigned to the $E$ blocks of one component must be its $a$ smallest labels. Partition its two label sets into $m$ blocks, match the blocks in $m!$ ways, and cyclically order the pairs in $(m-1)!$ ways. The labelled set construction then gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:bell}
 A_0(u,1)=1,\qquad
 A_{n+1}(u,1)=\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr L_{r+1}(u)A_{n-r}(u,1).
\end{equation}
Thus the first polynomials are
$1,1,1+u,1+5u,1+17u+5u^2$.
At $u=1$ the component counts, including the singleton, are the shifted A136127 sequence identified in Part~I, \eqref{vav:eq:known}. This provides explicit cycle objects for the exponential transform; a direct bijection to a particular previously named excedance-permutation or tree model is not claimed.

For an analytic check of~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:cumulants}, set
\[
 T(x,y,u)=-\log(1-u(e^x-1)(e^y-1)).
\]
Then~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:exact} is $z+\int_0^zT_x(t,z-t,u)\dd t$. Expansion of $T$ and a beta integral give the same formula.

\section{A convergent local singular expansion}\label{vav:rf:sec:singular}
For $u>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:parameters}
 a=u^{-1/2},\quad \rho=2\log(1+a),\quad
 c=\left(\frac{\pi^2a}{4\rho}\right)^{1/3},\quad
 K=\frac\rho2-2-a,\quad D=e^K\sqrt{\frac{c}{3\pi}}.
\end{equation}
These functions extend holomorphically to a sufficiently small complex neighborhood of each compact positive $u$-interval, using the positive real branches.
With $w=\rho-z$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:sing}
 \log H(\rho-w,u)=\pi\sqrt a\,w^{-1/2}S(w,u)+R(w,u),
\end{equation}
where $S,R$ are jointly analytic near $w=0$, $S(0,u)=1$, and $R(0,u)=K$.
The following formulas determine every coefficient:
\begin{align}
 q&=\cosh(w/2)-a\sinh(w/2),& h&=a\cosh(w/2)-\sinh(w/2),\notag\\
 \mathcal T&=\coth((\rho-w)/4),& S&=\frac ha\sqrt{\frac{aw}{1-q^2}},\label{vav:rf:eq:SR}\\
 R&=\frac{\rho-w}{2}-\frac{2h}{1+q}
 \sum_{m\ge0}\frac{(-1)^m\mathcal T^{2m+1}}{2m+1}
                 \left(\frac{1-q}{1+q}\right)^m.\notag
\end{align}
The quotient in $S$ is analytic and equals one at the origin; the series for $R$ converges normally in a common sufficiently small neighborhood.

For derivation, symmetrize~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:exact}. With
$q=\cosh(z/2)-1/(2u e^{z/2})$ and $N=1-u+u e^z$, the elementary integral is
\[
 \log H=\frac z2+\frac{N}{u e^{z/2}\sqrt{1-q^2}}
 \arctan\left(\tanh(z/4)\sqrt{\frac{1+q}{1-q}}\right).
\]
Near the positive singularity use $\arctan X=\pi/2-\arctan(1/X)$ and $N/(u e^{z/2})=2h$. This yields~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:sing}--\eqref{vav:rf:eq:SR}. Apparent $q=-1$ points elsewhere are not presumed singular; some are removable.

Writing $t=1-z/\rho$, we obtain the convergent expansion
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:puiseux}
 \log H(\rho(1-t),u)=K+2c^{3/2}t^{-1/2}+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jt^{j/2}.
\end{equation}
If $S=\sum s_mw^m$ and $R=\sum r_mw^m$, then
\begin{align}
 g_{2m-1}&=\pi\sqrt a\,s_m\rho^{m-1/2},&g_{2m}&=r_m\rho^m,\notag\\
 s_1&=\frac{a^2-3}{8a},&s_2&=\frac{9a^4+10a^2-15}{384a^2},&r_1&=\frac{4-a^3}{6a}.\label{vav:rf:eq:g}
\end{align}
These specialize at $u=1$ to Part~I's expansion \eqref{vav:eq:puiseux}--\eqref{vav:eq:gj}.

\section{Complex uniformity of the first saddle}\label{vav:rf:sec:first}
\begin{theorem}\label{vav:rf:thm:first}
For each compact interval $\mathcal U\subset(0,\infty)$ there is a complex neighborhood $\Omega$ of $\mathcal U$ and holomorphic coefficients $a_j(u)$, $a_0=1$, such that for every fixed $J\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:first}
 \frac{A_n(u,1)}{n!}
 =D\rho^{-n}n^{-5/6}e^{3cn^{1/3}}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^J a_j(u)n^{-j/3}+O_{\mathcal U,J}(n^{-(J+1)/3})\right),
\end{equation}
uniformly on a fixed closed neighborhood of $\mathcal U$ contained in $\Omega$.
The normalized remainder is holomorphic and has the same fixed-derivative bounds on a smaller neighborhood. Uniformly for $s$ in any fixed compact subset of $\mathbb C$, the analogous result has leading amplitude $D e^{(s-1)\rho}$ and computable coefficients $a_j(u,s)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We supply the complex-parameter details because real pointwise asymptotics would be insufficient for the later limit and local laws. For real $u\in \mathcal U$, $r=\rho(u)$ and $|z|\le r$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:denom}
 |u(e^{zx}-1)(e^{z(1-x)}-1)|
 \le u(e^{|z|x}-1)(e^{|z|(1-x)}-1)\le1.
\end{equation}
On $|z|=r$, equality is possible only at $x=1/2,z=r$. For $0<x<1$, equality in the exponential power-series triangle inequality forces $z$ positive real; the endpoints $x=0,1$ give zero. Thus the denominator in~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:exact} is nonzero on the compact normalized set $z=\rho(u)\zeta$, $|\zeta|\le1$, $|\zeta-1|\ge\epsilon$. It remains nonzero in an open neighborhood of this set for $u$ in a small complex neighborhood of $\mathcal U$. Near $\zeta=1$, the jointly analytic local representation~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:sing} supplies continuation in a common slit neighborhood. The two representations agree on their initial intersection. This constructs the needed common dented domain, without classifying every apparent singularity of the elementary expression.

Set $\delta=n^{-1/3}$ and use the circle
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:circle}
 z=\rho(u)(1-c(u)\delta^2)e^{\ii\theta},\qquad -\pi\le\theta\le\pi.
\end{equation}
Shrink $\Omega$ so that $\Re c>c_*>0$, $\Re(1/c)>b_*>0$, and all the continuation and local expansion bounds hold uniformly. Near $\theta=0$, $\Re(1-(1-c\delta^2)e^{\ii\theta})>0$ for large $n$, so the circle lies in the common domain. On its fixed outer arc $\log H$ is bounded, whereas the main exponential modulus is $e^{3\Re(c)/\delta}$ and the radial Cauchy factor is $e^{\Re(c)/\delta+O(\delta)}$.

On the local arc put $\theta=\delta^2y$. Up to $O(\delta)$ for bounded $y$, the nonconstant leading phase, after removing the radial factor, is $\delta^{-1}\Phi_c(y)$, where
\[
 \Phi_c(y)=2c^{3/2}(c-\ii y)^{-1/2}-\ii y,\quad
 \Phi_c(0)=2c,\quad \Phi_c'(0)=0,\quad \Phi_c''(0)=-\frac{3}{2c}.
\]
For small $|y|$ the real-part loss is at least $b y^2/\delta$, up to a harmless small shift. More precisely the exact circle leaves an uncancelled linear term $O(\delta^{-1}\theta)=O(\delta |y|)$. On the Gaussian scale $y=\sqrt\delta X$ it is $O(\delta^{3/2}|X|)$; completing the square gives a negligible shift. Outside a growing Gaussian cutoff it is absorbed by the quadratic loss.

For $\epsilon\le |y|\le M$, there is a uniform fixed loss at real $u$, since
$\Re(c-\ii y)^{-1/2}<c^{-1/2}$, and it persists under small complex perturbations by compactness. If $|\theta|\ge M\delta^2$ within the local arc, then $|t|\ge b|\theta|$ and
\[
 |2c^{3/2}t^{-1/2}|\le C\delta^{-1}/\sqrt M.
\]
Choose $M$ large. This gives a fixed fractional loss from the main exponent. Hence every arc outside
$|\theta|\le\delta^{5/2}\log(1/\delta)$ is smaller than the main scale by every power of $n$, uniformly in $u$. Bounded $O(t^{1/2})$ perturbations do not change the conclusion.

Deform the central integral to
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:vertical}
 t=c\delta^2(1+\ii\sqrt\delta X),\qquad X\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation}
For real $u$, take short connecting curves at fixed scaled endpoints $|X|=\epsilon/\sqrt\delta$, inside open neighborhoods with a fixed leading-phase loss. Continue this choice of curves continuously as $u$ leaves the real interval, using a finite cover of $\mathcal U$. Their compact images remain in the loss region after shrinking the common neighborhood. No connector crosses $t=0$ or the cut. This specifies a uniform valid deformation for complex $u$.

\begin{samepage}
On $|X|\le\log(1/\delta)$ the phase is $3c/\delta-3cX^2/4+Q$, with
\begin{align}\label{vav:rf:eq:Q}
 Q={}&2c\sum_{m\ge3}\binom{-1/2}{m}(\ii X)^m\delta^{m/2-1}\notag\\
 &+\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{c^m}{m}\delta^{2m-3}(1+\ii\sqrt\delta X)^m
  +\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c^m}{m}\delta^{2m}(1+\ii\sqrt\delta X)^m\notag\\
 &+\sum_{j\ge1}g_jc^{j/2}\delta^j(1+\ii\sqrt\delta X)^{j/2}.
\end{align}
\end{samepage}
Thus $a_j=[\delta^j]\E e^Q$, where the formal Gaussian moments are
\[
 \E X^{2m}=(2m-1)!!\left(\frac{2}{3c}\right)^m,\qquad \E X^{2m+1}=0.
\]
For complex $c$ these mean analytic Gaussian integration; $\Re c>0$ ensures convergence. The leading Jacobian and Gaussian integral give $e^K\sqrt{c/(3\pi)}\delta^{5/2}$.

The convergent local series and the logarithmic/binomial expansions give uniform Taylor remainders of any requested power of $\delta$ times a polynomial in $X$. After multiplication by the Gaussian they are dominated by $C e^{-bX^2}$ times a polynomial. Expand two extra half-orders and use parity; the resulting remainder is $O(\delta^{J+1})$, without a logarithmic loss. Holomorphy and Cauchy estimates give parameter derivatives. Finally $e^{(s-1)z}$ changes $K$ to $K+(s-1)\rho$ and $g_2$ to $g_2-(s-1)\rho$, with all estimates uniform for bounded $s$.
\end{proof}

Put $T_0=c^2/2+g_1\sqrt c$. The first two coefficients are
\begin{align}
 a_1&=T_0-\frac5{36c}
 =c^2\left(\frac12+\frac{\rho(a^2-3)}{4a}\right)-\frac5{36c},\label{vav:rf:eq:a1}\\
 a_2&=\frac{T_0^2}{2}+cg_2-\frac{17g_1}{36\sqrt c}
                   -\frac{17c}{72}-\frac{35}{2592c^2}.\label{vav:rf:eq:a2}
\end{align}

\section{The marking phase gap}\label{vav:rf:sec:gap}
\begin{lemma}\label{vav:rf:lem:gap}
For any compact positive interval $\mathcal U$ and $\epsilon>0$, there are $\eta>0$ and $C<\infty$ such that, uniformly for $u\in \mathcal U$ and $\epsilon\le|\theta|\le\pi$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:gap}
 |[z^n]H(z,u e^{\ii\theta},s)|\le C(\rho(u)+\eta)^{-n},
\end{equation}
with $s$ in any prescribed compact set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $|z|\le\rho(u)$, inequality~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:denom} is strict except at $x=1/2,z=\rho(u)$. At that exceptional point multiplication by $e^{\ii\theta}$ gives $e^{\ii\theta}\ne1$. The denominator is therefore nonzero on the entire compact parameter set, and remains nonzero for $|z|\le\rho(u)+\eta$ with uniform $\eta>0$. The integral and exponential are uniformly bounded there. Cauchy's estimate proves the result.
\end{proof}
This excludes other periodic saddles in the marking variable. It is a separate ingredient beyond a positive-real asymptotic formula.

\section{Precise local large deviations}\label{vav:rf:sec:ldp}
Use $v=\log u$ and put
\[
 f(v)=-\log\rho(e^v),\qquad d(v)=\log D(e^v),\qquad
 \alpha(u)=\frac{1}{(1+\sqrt u)\rho(u)},\qquad V(u)=f''(v).
\]
In this and the next section, primes on $c$ or $\rho$ mean derivatives of $c(e^v)$ or $\rho(e^v)$ with respect to $v$, evaluated at $v=\log u$. Then $f'=\alpha$. With $a=u^{-1/2}$ and $\ell=\log(1+a)$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:V}
 V(u)=\frac{a(a-\ell)}{4(1+a)^2\ell^2}>0.
\end{equation}
The map $\alpha:(0,\infty)\to(0,1/2)$ is increasing and onto.

\begin{theorem}\label{vav:rf:thm:local}
Suppose $k/n$ lies in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,1/2)$, and choose the unique $u>0$ with $\alpha(u)=k/n$. For each fixed $J\ge0$,
\begin{align}\label{vav:rf:eq:local}
 A_{n,k}:=\sum_jA_{n,k,j}
 ={}&\frac{n!D(u)\rho(u)^{-n}u^{-k}e^{3c(u)n^{1/3}}}
             {\sqrt{2\pi V(u)}\,n^{4/3}}\notag\\
 &\qquad\times\left(\sum_{j=0}^J b_j(u)n^{-j/3}
                      +O(n^{-(J+1)/3})\right),
\end{align}
uniformly in these integer pairs. The coefficients are analytic and explicitly computable. In particular
\begin{align}
 b_0={}&1,\qquad b_1=a_1-\frac{9(c')^2}{2V},\label{vav:rf:eq:b1}\\
 b_2={}&a_2-\frac{9a_1(c')^2}{2V}+\frac{81(c')^4}{8V^2}
       -\frac{3c''}{2V}-\frac{3c'd'}{V}+\frac{3c'f'''}{2V^2}.\label{vav:rf:eq:b2}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fourier inversion on the $u$-circle integrates $A_n(u e^{\ii\theta},1)u^{-k}e^{-\ii k\theta}$. For small $\theta$ apply Theorem~\ref{vav:rf:thm:first}. Positivity of $V$ gives
\[
 \Re\big(f(v+\ii\theta)-f(v)-\ii\alpha\theta\big)\le-b\theta^2.
\]
Since $c,d$ and their derivatives are real on the real axis, their real-part increments are $O(\theta^2)$. The normalized integrand is bounded by $C e^{-bn\theta^2}$, with a reduced $b>0$ if necessary. Outside a fixed neighborhood, Lemma~\ref{vav:rf:lem:gap} supplies an exponential-in-$n$ gain that beats every stretched factor. Only $|\theta|\le n^{-1/2}\log n$ contributes to algebraic orders.

Set $\delta=n^{-1/3}$ and $\theta=\delta^{3/2}X$. After removal of $e^{-VX^2/2}$ the formal exponent is
\begin{align}\label{vav:rf:eq:P}
 P={}&\sum_{m\ge3}\frac{f^{(m)}(v)}{m!}(\ii X)^m\delta^{3m/2-3}\notag\\
 &+3\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{c^{(m)}(v)}{m!}(\ii X)^m\delta^{3m/2-1}
 +\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{d^{(m)}(v)}{m!}(\ii X)^m\delta^{3m/2}.
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:bgen}
 b_j=[\delta^j]\E\left[e^P
        \sum_{r\ge0}a_r(v+\ii\delta^{3/2}X)\delta^r\right],
 \qquad X\sim N(0,1/V),
\end{equation}
where $a_r(v+\ii\theta)$ means $a_r(e^{v+\ii\theta})$ and $a_0=1$.
Only finitely many terms are needed at each order. Odd half-powers have odd $X$ parity and integrate to zero. Uniform analytic Taylor bounds and a Gaussian majorant prove the remainder as in Theorem~\ref{vav:rf:thm:first}. The new Gaussian factor is $n^{-1/2}/\sqrt{2\pi V}$, giving~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:local}; expansion gives~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:b1}--\eqref{vav:rf:eq:b2}.
\end{proof}
The stretched exponential shifts the full marking saddle by
$-3c'/(Vn^{2/3})+O(n^{-1})$ in $v$. Its first logarithmic correction is consequently $-9(c')^2n^{-1/3}/(2V)$, agreeing with~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:b1}. Keeping the first-saddle coefficients unchanged would miss this term.

For a uniform object, write $K_n$ for its downset count. Dividing by~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:first} at $u=1$ gives
\begin{align}\label{vav:rf:eq:ldp}
 \Prob(K_n=k)&\sim\frac{D(u)}{D(1)\sqrt{2\pi nV(u)}}
  \exp\left(-nI(\alpha)+3[c(u)-c(1)]n^{1/3}\right),\\
 I(\alpha)&=\alpha\log u+\log\rho(u)-\log\rho(1),\qquad \alpha=k/n.\notag
\end{align}
The rate is nonnegative, vanishes only at $\alpha(1)$, and has
$I'=\log u$, $I''=1/V(u)>0$. All relative corrections follow by series division.

\section{Gaussian and isolated element laws}\label{vav:rf:sec:laws}
Put $\rho_0=\log4$ and $c_0=c(1)$. Uniform analyticity gives
\begin{align}
 \E K_n={}&\frac{n}{2\rho_0}
 +\frac{c_0(1-\rho_0)}{2\rho_0}n^{1/3}
 +\frac16+\frac{1}{12\rho_0}+O(n^{-1/3}),\label{vav:rf:eq:mean}\\
 \Var K_n={}&V(1)n+3c''\big|_{u=1}n^{1/3}+d''(0)+O(n^{-1/3}),\notag\\
 V(1)={}&\frac{1}{4\rho_0^2}-\frac{1}{8\rho_0}>0.\notag
\end{align}
Useful derivative identities are
$3c'=c(\alpha-1/2)$ and $3c''=c[V+(\alpha-1/2)^2/3]$.
The central and lattice local limits are
\begin{align}
 \frac{K_n-n/(2\rho_0)}{\sqrt{nV(1)}}&\ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,1),\label{vav:rf:eq:clt}\\
 \sup_{k\in\mathbb Z}\left|\sqrt{nV(1)}\Prob(K_n=k)
 -\phi\left(\frac{k-n/(2\rho_0)}{\sqrt{nV(1)}}\right)\right|&\longrightarrow0.\label{vav:rf:eq:llt}
\end{align}
Here $\phi(x)=e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$. For~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:llt}, the rescaled characteristic functions converge on each compact interval. Their tails vanish in $L^1$ by the Gaussian bound from Theorem~\ref{vav:rf:thm:local} and Lemma~\ref{vav:rf:lem:gap}. Fourier inversion bounds the uniform local error by this $L^1$ error. Centering by the true mean gives the same statements.

Let $J_n$ count isolated elements. Equation~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:iso} and the complex-uniform theorem imply
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:joint}
 \left(\frac{K_n-n/(2\rho_0)}{\sqrt{nV(1)}},J_n\right)
 \Longrightarrow (Z,P),\qquad
 Z\sim N(0,1),\quad P\sim\Pois(\rho_0),\quad Z\perp P.
\end{equation}
The joint transform factors in the limit into $e^{-t^2/2}e^{(s-1)\rho_0}$. Taking real $t$ and $s$ on the unit circle proves the assertion by the characteristic-function criterion.

There is also a uniform conditional law at atypical density. With $u$ determined by $k/n=\alpha(u)$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:conditional}
 \mathcal L(J_n\mid K_n=k)=\Pois(\rho(u))+O_{\TV}(n^{-2/3}).
\end{equation}
Indeed the analytic multiplier changes the first-saddle data to
\[
 a_1(u,s)=a_1(u),\quad a_2(u,s)=a_2(u)-(s-1)\rho c,
 \quad d_s=d+(s-1)\rho.
\]
Applying the second saddle and dividing gives, uniformly for bounded $s$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:conditionalcorrection}
 \E[s^{J_n}\mid K_n=k]=e^{(s-1)\rho}
 \left\{1-(s-1)\left(\rho c+\frac{3c'\rho'}{V}\right)n^{-2/3}
                  +O(n^{-1})\right\}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $R>1$ this error is holomorphic and uniform on $|s|\le R$; Cauchy coefficient bounds and summation of $R^{-j}$ prove~\eqref{vav:rf:eq:conditional}. Unconditionally,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:rf:eq:unconditional}
 \E[s^{J_n}]=e^{(s-1)\rho_0}
 \{1-(s-1)\rho_0c_0n^{-2/3}+O(n^{-1})\},
\end{equation}
so the same total-variation rate applies. Finite covariance is not zero despite the limiting independence: differentiating the tilted version gives
\[
 \Cov(K_n,J_n)=-\frac12+O(n^{-2/3}).
\]

\section{Statements and source context}\label{vav:dg:sec:intro}
A permutation $\pi\in S_n$ contains $12$--$34$ if there are indices
$i+1<j$ such that
$\pi_i<\pi_{i+1}<\pi_j<\pi_{j+1}$.
Write $a_n$ for the number avoiding this pattern and
$A(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n/n!$. The initial values are
\[
1,1,2,6,23,107,585,3669,25932,203768,1761109.
\]
This is A113226~\cite{oeis}; it also counts
$\{3,2+2\}$-free naturally labelled posets~\cite{bck}.
Throughout, logarithms are natural.

\begin{theorem}[Exact generating function]\label{vav:dg:thm:egf}
As an analytic germ at zero,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:exact}
 A(z)=\ee^{T(z)},\qquad
 T(z)=\int_1^{\ee^z}\frac{x\,dx}{x^2-\ee^zx+\ee^z}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with the branches specified by $w>0$ for
$0<z<\rho=\log4$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:atan}
 T(z)=\frac z2+\frac{\pi-3\arctan w}{w},
 \qquad w=\sqrt{4\ee^{-z}-1}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Set
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:constants}
 \rho=\log4,\quad
 \kappa=\left(\frac{\pi^2}{4\rho}\right)^{1/3},\quad
 C=\ee^{\rho/2-3}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{3\pi}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:main}
 \frac{a_n}{n!}
 =C\rho^{-n}\ee^{3\kappa n^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^{K}c_jn^{-j/3}
              +O_K(n^{-(K+1)/3})\right).

The first correction is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:c1}
 c_1=\frac{\kappa^2(1-\rho)}2-\frac5{36\kappa}
       =-0.398274243294777\ldots .
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{vav:dg:sec:algorithm} gives a finite exact algorithm for every $c_j$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Continuous labels and the diagonal state}\label{vav:dg:sec:diagonal}
Independent uniform labels in $(0,1)$ have each order type of length $n$
with probability $1/n!$. Thus the probability of avoidance is $a_n/n!$.
After an allowed nonempty prefix, let $y$ be its last label and $m$ the
minimum of the ascent tops already seen. Put $m=1$ if no ascent has occurred.
A proposed new ascent starting at $y$ is allowed exactly when $y\le m$.
Equality is allowed: the ascent ending at $y=m$ and the next ascent overlap,
so cannot alone form a four-entry occurrence.

Let $F(m,y;z)$ count allowed continuations, including the empty continuation,
and retain the diagonal value
$G(m;z)=F(m,m;z)$ separately. The diagonal is attained by a positive
proportion of prefixes, even though it has two-dimensional measure zero.
For $|z|<1$ all continuation series are uniformly absolutely convergent,
bounded by $(1-|z|)^{-1}$. We suppress $z$ in the notation.
Appending a uniform label gives, for $0<y<m$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:below}
 F(m,y)=1+z\int_0^yF(m,u)\,du
             +z\int_y^mG(u)\,du+z\int_m^1F(m,u)\,du.
\end{equation}
A descent keeps $m$; an ascent to $u<m$ creates the diagonal state $(u,u)$;
an ascent to $u>m$ keeps $m$. For $m<y<1$ only a descent is permitted:
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:above}
 F(m,y)=1+z\int_0^yF(m,u)\,du.
\end{equation}
On the diagonal every append is permitted, and apart from null equalities
the threshold stays $m$:
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:diag}
 G(m)=1+z\int_0^1F(m,u)\,du.
\end{equation}
These equations uniquely specify the continuation series coefficient by
coefficient, so no extraneous solution can enter.

Put $H(m)=1+z\int_0^mF(m,u)\,du$. Equation~\eqref{vav:dg:eq:above} and then
\eqref{vav:dg:eq:diag} give
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:upper-sol}
 F(m,y)=H(m)\ee^{z(y-m)}\quad(y>m),\qquad
 G(m)=H(m)\ee^{z(1-m)}.
\end{equation}
The left limit of \eqref{vav:dg:eq:below} at $y=m$ is $G(m)$, and differentiation
in $y$ gives $\partial_yF=z(F-G(y))$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:lower-sol}
 F(m,y)=\ee^{z(y-m)}G(m)
           +z\ee^{zy}\int_y^m\ee^{-zu}G(u)\,du\quad(y<m).
\end{equation}
Integrating this identity yields
\[
 H(m)=1+(1-\ee^{-zm})G(m)
                     +z\int_0^m(1-\ee^{-zu})G(u)\,du.
\]
Together with \eqref{vav:dg:eq:upper-sol}, this becomes the scalar Volterra equation
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:volterra}
 D(m)G(m)=1+z\int_0^m(1-\ee^{-zu})G(u)\,du,\qquad
 D(m)=\ee^{z(m-1)}+\ee^{-zm}-1.
\end{equation}
Near $z=0$, $D$ is uniformly nonzero. Thus $G(0)=\ee^z$ and
\[
 \frac{G'(m)}{G(m)}
  =\frac{z(1-\ee^{z(m-1)})}{D(m)}.
\]
Set $E=\ee^z$, $x=\ee^{zm}$ and $P_E(x)=x^2-Ex+E$.
Then $d\log G=(E-x)dx/P_E(x)$, so
\[
 \log G(1)=z+\int_1^E\frac{E-x}{P_E(x)}\,dx.
\]
Since $P_E(E)=E$, $P_E(1)=1$ and
$\int_1^E(2x-E)dx/P_E(x)=z$, the last expression equals $T(z)$ in
\eqref{vav:dg:eq:exact}. Finally, starting with the first uniform label,
\[
 A(z)=1+z\int_0^1F(1,y)\,dy=H(1)=G(1).
\]
This proves \eqref{vav:dg:eq:exact} as a germ at zero. Later continuation is
deduced from that identity, rather than from the original continuation
series outside $|z|<1$.

\section{Exact recurrences and the published block counts}\label{vav:dg:sec:recurrences}
Elementary differentiation of \eqref{vav:dg:eq:atan}, or of \eqref{vav:dg:eq:exact}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:ode}
 (4-\ee^z)T'(z)-2T(z)=2+\ee^z-z,\qquad T(0)=0.
\end{equation}
Writing $T(z)=\sum t_nz^n/n!$, we have $t_0=0$, $t_1=1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:rec-t}
 3t_{n+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom nj t_{j+1}+2t_n+1-\mathbf1_{\{n=1\}}
 \qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
The desired counts follow from
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:rec-a}
 a_0=1,\qquad
 a_n=\sum_{j=1}^n\binom{n-1}{j-1}t_ja_{n-j}.
\end{equation}
Both recurrences are exact. In particular, they do not constitute a
recurrence guessed from numerical data.

For comparison, Elizalde's functions $b_k,c_k$ satisfy
$b_0=z$, $c_0=\ee^z-1-z$ and, for $k\ge1$,
\[
 b_k''=k^2\ee^zb_{k-1},\qquad
 c_k''=k(k+1)\ee^zc_{k-1},
\]
with zero values and first derivatives at zero. They admit the integrals
\[
 b_k=\int_0^z(\ee^u-1)^k(\ee^{z-u}-1)^k\,du,\qquad
 c_k=\int_0^z(\ee^u-1)^{k+1}(\ee^{z-u}-1)^k\,du.
\]
Checking the recurrences and initial conditions proves these identities.
Geometric summation near zero then gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:T-block}
 \sum_{k\ge0}(b_k+c_k)
 =\int_0^z\frac{\ee^u\,du}
          {1-(\ee^u-1)(\ee^{z-u}-1)}=T(z).
\end{equation}
Thus, if $S=\sum_{k\ge1}(b_k+c_k)$, the exact formula is
$A=\exp(S+\ee^z-1)$. It lies between Elizalde's envelopes
$\exp S$ and $\exp(S+\ee^z+z-1)$, but is not inferred from them.

\subsection{Initial excedance sets}\label{vav:dg:sec:excedance}
Let $b_n$ count permutations of $[n]$ whose excedance set is an initial
interval, with $b_0=1$; this is A136127~\cite{oeis136}.
The classical extremal-excedance generating function, due to
Clark--Ehrenborg and reproduced in \cite[Eq.~(3.1)]{excedance}, is
\[
 Q(x,y)=\sum_{r,s\ge0}[b^ra^s]\frac{x^ry^s}{r!s!}
       =\frac{\ee^{-x-y}}{(\ee^{-x}+\ee^{-y}-1)^2}.
\]
Here $[b^ra^s]$ counts permutations of $r+s+1$ with precisely the first
$r$ positions excedances. A beta integral shows
\[
 B(z):=\sum_{n\ge0}b_n\frac{z^n}{n!}
       =1+\int_0^zQ(u,z-u)\,du.
\]
Put $M(x,y)=\ee^{-y}/(\ee^{-x}+\ee^{-y}-1)$.
Equation~\eqref{vav:dg:eq:T-block} reads
$T(z)=\int_0^zM(z-v,v)\,dv$; since $M(0,z)=1$ and
$\partial_xM=Q$, we get
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:excedance}
 T'(z)=B(z),\qquad t_n=b_{n-1}\ (n\ge1),\qquad A'=BA.
\end{equation}
The bivariate excedance formula is established prior work. The connection
\eqref{vav:dg:eq:excedance} with this single-pattern avoidance class follows from
the exact continuation solution above.

\begin{remark}[{[write]} The block integrals and the block grading]\label{vav:dg:rem:elizalde}
\emph{(a) Proof of the integral forms.} The source states that checking
the recurrences proves them; here is the check. For integers $m,n\ge0$
put $a=e^u-1$, $b=e^{z-u}-1$ and $I_{m,n}(z)=\int_0^z a^mb^n\,du$, so that
the displayed integrals are $b_k=I_{k,k}$ and $c_k=I_{k+1,k}$, and
$I_{0,0}=z=b_0$, $I_{1,0}=e^z-1-z=c_0$. We claim that for $m,n\ge1$
\[
 I_{m,n}''=mn\,e^zI_{m-1,n-1},\qquad I_{m,n}(0)=I_{m,n}'(0)=0 .
\]
The initial values are clear. Since $\partial_za=0$ and
$\partial_zb=1+b$, and $b=0$ at $u=z$,
\begin{align*}
 I_{m,n}'&=\int_0^z n a^mb^{n-1}(1+b)\,du,\\
 I_{m,n}''&=\mathbf 1_{\{n=1\}}(e^z-1)^m
 +\int_0^z\bigl[n(n-1)a^mb^{n-2}(1+b)^2+na^mb^{n-1}(1+b)\bigr]du,
\end{align*}
where the boundary term comes from $u=z$ and survives only for $n=1$. On
the other hand $\partial_ua=1+a$ and $\partial_ub=-(1+b)$ give
\[
 n\,\partial_u\bigl[a^mb^{n-1}(1+b)\bigr]
 =mn(1+a)(1+b)a^{m-1}b^{n-1}
 -n(n-1)a^mb^{n-2}(1+b)^2-na^mb^{n-1}(1+b).
\]
Integrate over $[0,z]$. The bracket vanishes at $u=0$ (where $a=0$) and at
$u=z$ (where $b=0$) unless $n=1$, when it equals $(e^z-1)^m$ there. Since
$(1+a)(1+b)=e^z$, the integral of the first term on the right is
$mn\,e^zI_{m-1,n-1}$, and the boundary contribution cancels the term
$\mathbf 1_{\{n=1\}}(e^z-1)^m$ above. This proves the claim. With
$(m,n)=(k,k)$ and $(k+1,k)$ it is Elizalde's pair of recurrences, and the
solution of $y''=f$ with $y(0)=y'(0)=0$ is unique, so induction on $k$
proves the integral forms. (The shipped proof note
\path{06-diagonal-exact_egf_proof.md} also mentions a combinatorial reading
by a distinguished threshold label, without detail.) Summing,
$\sum_k(b_k+c_k)=\int_0^z(1+a)\sum_k(ab)^k\,du=\int_0^ze^u\,du/(1-ab)$ for
small $|z|$, and the substitution $x=e^u$ in \eqref{vav:dg:eq:exact} gives
the same integral, since $1-ab=e^u+e^{z-u}-e^z$ and
$x^2-e^zx+e^z=e^u(e^u+e^{z-u}-e^z)$. This is \eqref{vav:dg:eq:T-block}.

\emph{(b) The index $k$ is not Part~II's block-pair grading.} One might
read $\sum_ku^k(b_k+c_k)$ as $\log H(z,u)$ of \eqref{vav:rf:eq:exact}.
It is not, except at $u=1$: since $b_k,c_k=O(z^3)$ for $k\ge1$,
$\sum_ku^k(b_k+c_k)=e^z-1+O(z^3)$ has $z^2$-coefficient $1/2$, whereas
$\log H(z,u)=z+L_2(u)z^2/2+O(z^3)$ with $L_2(u)=u$ by
\eqref{vav:rf:eq:cumulants}. (At intake the two were also compared
numerically at $u=0.7$; they differ there, and agree at $u=1$.)
\end{remark}

\section{Singularity and a complete contour argument}\label{vav:dg:sec:contour}
For $0<z<\rho$, direct integration of \eqref{vav:dg:eq:exact} gives
\[
 T(z)=\frac z2+
 \frac E{\sqrt{E(4-E)}}\left[
  \arctan\frac E{\sqrt{E(4-E)}}
  -\arctan\frac{2-E}{\sqrt{E(4-E)}}\right].
\]
The bracket is $\pi-3\arctan w$, proving \eqref{vav:dg:eq:atan}.
Writing $\delta=\rho-z$, its convergent local expansion is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:local}
\begin{split}
 T(\rho-\delta)
  ={}&\frac{\rho-\delta}{2}
     +\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\ee^\delta-1}}
     -3\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{(-1)^j(\ee^\delta-1)^j}{2j+1}\\
  ={}&\frac{\pi}{\sqrt\delta}+\frac\rho2-3
       -\frac\pi4\sqrt\delta+\frac\delta2+O(\delta^{3/2}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The square root is positive for positive real $\delta$.

To control other points of the convergence circle, use
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:analytic-int}
 T(z)=z\int_0^1
 \frac{\ee^{zv}}{1-(\ee^{zv}-1)(\ee^{z(1-v)}-1)}\,dv.
\end{equation}
For $|z|=r<\rho$,
\[
 |(\ee^{zv}-1)(\ee^{z(1-v)}-1)|
 \le(\ee^{rv}-1)(\ee^{r(1-v)}-1)
 \le(\ee^{r/2}-1)^2<1.
\]
On $|z|=\rho$, equality in the second inequality requires $v=1/2$.
There, equality in the first requires the degree-one and degree-two
terms of $\ee^{z/2}-1$ to have the same argument, forcing $z=\rho$.
Thus \eqref{vav:dg:eq:analytic-int} continues through every other point of the
circle. Combining compactness with \eqref{vav:dg:eq:local}, there is a fixed
$\epsilon>0$ for which $A$ continues to
$|z|<\rho+\epsilon$ slit along $[\rho,\rho+\epsilon)$.

Use a Cauchy contour consisting of the left part of the circle
$|z|=\rho+\epsilon$ and its vertical chord
\[
 \Re z=r=\rho(1-\kappa n^{-2/3}).
\]
The slit is excluded. The outer arc stays away from the singularity and
contributes an exponentially small error compared with the claimed main
term. On the chord $z=r+\ii y$, the local expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:decay}
 \Re T(r+\ii y)
 \le\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\rho-r}}
 -\frac{c}{\sqrt{\rho-r}}
       \min\left\{\frac{y^2}{(\rho-r)^2},1\right\}+C_0
\end{equation}
for small $|y|$, with fixed $c,C_0>0$.
This follows from the strict inequality
$\Re(1-\ii t)^{-1/2}<1$ for $t\ne0$ and its quadratic expansion at zero.
The remaining local terms are bounded. Also $|r+\ii y|\ge r$.
Consequently the part $|y|\ge n^{-5/6+\eta}$, for any sufficiently small
fixed $\eta>0$, loses a factor $\exp(-c n^{2\eta})$ relative to the saddle;
farther fixed parts lose $\exp(-c n^{1/3})$.
This is the coefficient-contour estimate needed in addition to the
real-axis expansion.

\begin{remark}[{[write]} The farther parts of the chord]\label{vav:dg:rem:farchord}
The last claim, which the source states without detail, holds with a much
larger loss. Take the arc on a circle $|z|=\rho+\epsilon_1$ with
$0<\epsilon_1<\epsilon$, so that the closed contour lies in the slit disc
of analyticity (on the source's circle $|z|=\rho+\epsilon$ itself analyticity
has not been shown; the shrinking is harmless, as the circle radius plays no
other role). Fix $y_0>0$ so small that \eqref{vav:dg:eq:decay} holds for
$|y|\le y_0$, and fix $0<\eta_0<\rho$. The set
$K_1=\{x+\ii y:\rho-\eta_0\le x\le\rho,\ y_0\le|y|,\ |x+\ii y|\le\rho+\epsilon_1\}$
is a compact subset of the slit disc (it avoids the real slit since
$|y|\ge y_0$), so $|A|\le M$ on $K_1$. For large $n$ the chord points with
$|y|\ge y_0$ lie in $K_1$, and $|r+\ii y|^2\ge r^2+y_0^2$ there. Since the
chord has length at most $2(\rho+\epsilon_1)$ and
$r^{-n}=\rho^{-n}\exp(\kappa n^{1/3}+O(n^{-1/3}))$, its part with
$|y|\ge y_0$ contributes to $a_n/n!$ at most
\[
 \frac{M(\rho+\epsilon_1)}{\pi r}\,
 \rho^{-n}e^{\kappa n^{1/3}+O(n^{-1/3})}
 \Bigl(1+\frac{y_0^2}{(\rho+\epsilon_1)^2}\Bigr)^{-(n+1)/2},
\]
which is smaller than the main term $C\rho^{-n}e^{3\kappa n^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}$
by a factor $O(e^{-c_1n})$ with
$c_1=\tfrac12\log(1+y_0^2/(\rho+\epsilon_1)^2)>0$; a fortiori it loses
$\exp(-cn^{1/3})$. The parts with $n^{-5/6+\eta}\le|y|\le y_0$ are those
treated by \eqref{vav:dg:eq:decay}.
\end{remark}

\section{The finite coefficient algorithm}\label{vav:dg:sec:algorithm}
Let $h=n^{-1/6}$, $\gamma=\sqrt{2\kappa/3}$, and define
\[
 u(h,v)=\kappa h^4+\ii\gamma h^5v,\qquad T_0=\rho/2-3.
\]
The orientation of the chord can be chosen so that $z=\rho(1-u)$.
Form the formal series
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:R}
\begin{split}
 R(h,v)={}&T(\rho(1-u(h,v)))
       -(h^{-6}+1)\log(1-u(h,v))\\
       &-3\kappa h^{-2}-T_0+\frac{v^2}{2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The negative powers cancel. The coefficient of $h^j$ in
$R\in h\mathbb C[v][[h]]$ has the parity of $j$ as a polynomial in $v$.
Let $Z$ be standard normal. Then
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:algorithm}
 c_j=\E\bigl([h^{2j}]\exp R(h,Z)\bigr).
\end{equation}
This uses only finitely many terms of \eqref{vav:dg:eq:local}; Gaussian moments
are $0$ in odd degree and $(2k-1)!!$ in degree $2k$.
It proves \eqref{vav:dg:eq:c1} by finite algebra.

For completeness, the remainder is controlled as follows.
In the central window $|v|\le h^{-\eta}$, choose $\eta>0$ sufficiently
small for a fixed desired truncation order. Taylor expansion of the
convergent local expression and then its exponential gives, through degree
$J$, a remainder bounded by $h^{J+1}$ times a fixed polynomial in $|v|$
times $\exp(C h|v|^3)$. The full exponent retains Gaussian decay
$\exp(-v^2/4)$, either by its local quadratic estimate or by
\eqref{vav:dg:eq:decay}. The remainder thus has integral $O(h^{J+1})$.
Expand through $J=2K+1$. Odd terms integrate to zero on the symmetric
window; extending each polynomial-Gaussian integral to the real line
costs less than every power of $h$.
The discarded contour is handled by \eqref{vav:dg:eq:decay}.
Finally $dz$ supplies $\rho\gamma h^5$, and $1/z$ is already included
in \eqref{vav:dg:eq:R}. The leading multiplier is
$\gamma/\sqrt{2\pi}=\sqrt{\kappa/(3\pi)}$.
This proves Theorem~\ref{vav:dg:thm:asym}, including its remainder at every order.

The result is a dominant-saddle asymptotic expansion. It does not assert
convergence of that expansion or describe all exponentially smaller complex
saddle contributions.

\section{Differential structure and infinitely many singularities}\label{vav:dg:sec:structure}
\begin{proposition}\label{vav:dg:prop:structure}
The germ $A$ is differentially algebraic and is not D-finite.
Consequently $(a_n)$ is not P-recursive.
Its logarithmic derivative $u=A'/A$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:DA}
 3(2u+1)u''-10(u')^2-(2u+8)u'+2u^2+u-1=0,
 \qquad u(0)=u'(0)=1.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating \eqref{vav:dg:eq:ode} gives
$(4-\ee^z)u'-(\ee^z+2)u=\ee^z-1$. Hence near zero,
\[
 \ee^z=\frac{4u'-2u+1}{u'+u+1},
\]
whose denominator at zero is $3$. Differentiate and use
$(\ee^z)'=\ee^z$ to obtain \eqref{vav:dg:eq:DA}.
Substitution $u=A'/A$ and multiplication by $A^4$ gives a third-order
polynomial differential equation for $A$.

For the other assertion put $z_k=\rho+2\pi\ii k$, $k\in\mathbb Z$.
The square root $w=\sqrt{4\ee^{-z}-1}$ can be continued from zero
along every finite path avoiding these points.
It never reaches $\ii$ or $-\ii$, because either value would require
$\ee^{-z}=0$. The arctangent can therefore be continued along the
lifted path as well. Each $z_k$ is accessible by such a path.
Near $z_k$, use $w$ as a local ramified coordinate, so
$z=z_k-\log(1+w^2)$.
The continued arctangent is regular at $w=0$ and has value $j\pi$
for some integer $j$, by the identity $\tan(\arctan w)=w$.
Thus every such branch satisfies
\[
 T(z)=\frac z2+\frac{\pi(1-3j)}{w}-3+O(w^2).
\]
Since $1-3j\ne0$, $\exp T$ has an essential singularity in the
coordinate $w$ and cannot extend holomorphically through $z_k$.
A D-finite germ can have finite singularities only at the finitely
many zeros of the leading polynomial of its linear differential
equation. This excludes D-finiteness.
Finally, P-recursiveness is preserved by multiplication or division
by $n!$, by clearing shifted factorial ratios in the recurrence.
P-recursiveness of $a_n$ would therefore make $A$ D-finite.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenoteb}
This proposition is new relative to Parts~I--II, which state nothing on
D-finiteness. The shipped \path{code/06-diagonal-check_structure.py}
verifies the elimination exactly and prints the cleared third-order
polynomial equation for $A$ (\path{data/06-diagonal-structure_checks.json});
at intake the residual of \eqref{vav:dg:eq:DA} on the exact series was below
$10^{-38}$ through $z^{37}$. The non-D-finiteness that
\cite{bck} discuss concerns a different class (the shipped source screen
and internal review say so: interval orders, the unlabelled Fishburn
series), so it is not a prior proof of this proposition. The local strengths
$\pi(1-3j)$ are consistent with Part~IV: on the branch of
Lemma~\ref{vav:hk:lem:local} the strength is $\pi$ at $\rho+2\pi\ii k$ for
even $k$ ($j=0$) and $2\pi$ for odd $k$ ($j=1$, with the opposite sign of
$w$).
\end{writenoteb}

\section{Inversion and integer thresholds}\label{vav:dg:sec:inverse}
For $y\to\infty$, put $N(y)=\min\{n:a_n\ge y\}$.
The expansion implies $a_{n+1}/a_n\sim n/\rho$, so the sequence is
eventually strictly increasing. For fixed $K$, let
\[
 f_K(x)=\Gamma(x+1)C\rho^{-x}
       \ee^{3\kappa x^{1/3}}x^{-5/6}
       \sum_{j=0}^{K}c_jx^{-j/3}.
\]
It is positive and strictly increasing on a tail, with logarithmic
derivative $\log(x/\rho)+O(x^{-2/3})$.
Let $x_K(y)$ denote its large inverse.

\begin{theorem}[Threshold inversion]\label{vav:dg:thm:inverse}
For each fixed $K$, there is a constant $D_K>0$ such that, for all large $y$,
\[
 \left\lceil x_K-\varepsilon_K\right\rceil
 \le N(y)\le
 \left\lceil x_K+\varepsilon_K\right\rceil,\qquad
 \varepsilon_K=
 \frac{D_Kx_K^{-(K+1)/3}}{\log x_K}.
\]
In particular, the ceiling of $x_K$ is exact whenever its distance from
the integers exceeds $\varepsilon_K$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The logarithmic error at integer arguments in \eqref{vav:dg:eq:main} is
$O(x^{-(K+1)/3})$. The mean value theorem divides this error by
the logarithmic derivative of $f_K$, which is asymptotic to $\log x$.
Taking $D_K$ sufficiently large gives the two comparisons on each side
of the threshold. Values in an initial finite segment do not matter
as $y\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

For an explicit compatible inverse expansion, let $X$ solve
\[
 \log\Gamma(X+1)-X\log\rho=\log y,\qquad
 \Psi(X)=\psi(X+1)-\log\rho,
\]
and set
\[
 q(X)=3\kappa X^{1/3}-\frac56\log X+\log C
                 +\log\left(\sum_{j\ge0}c_jX^{-j/3}\right).
\]
Lagrange reversion in the gamma-core variable gives
\begin{equation}\label{vav:dg:eq:invformal}
 x\sim X+\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}
 \left(\frac1{\Psi(X)}\frac d{dX}\right)^{k-1}
             \frac{q(X)^k}{\Psi(X)}.
\end{equation}
Every finite truncation is justified by Taylor expansion of the finite
models and the derivative estimate above. The first displacement is
$O(X^{1/3}/\log X)=o(X)$, and successive reversion terms decrease.
For example,
\begin{align}
 x={}&X-\frac{3\kappa X^{1/3}-(5/6)\log X+\log C+c_1X^{-1/3}}
                  {\Psi(X)}
       +\frac{3\kappa^2X^{-1/3}}{\Psi(X)^2}\nonumber\\
    &-\frac{9\kappa^2X^{2/3}\Psi'(X)}{2\Psi(X)^3}
       +O\left(\frac{X^{-2/3}}{\log X}\right).
\end{align}
No choice of an interpolation of the discrete sequence is being asserted;
the finite models and Theorem~\ref{vav:dg:thm:inverse} specify the inverse meaning.

\begin{proposition}[{[write]} The displayed reversion]\label{vav:dg:prop:reversion}
Fix $K\ge1$, let $q_K$ be $q$ with the sum truncated at $j=K$ (so
$\log f_K=G+q_K$ with $G(x)=\log\Gamma(x+1)-x\log\rho$), and let $x=x_K(y)$
and $X=X(y)$ be as above. Then, as $y\to\infty$,
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $x=X-\dfrac{q_K}{\Psi}+\dfrac{q_Kq_K'}{\Psi^2}
-\dfrac{\Psi'q_K^2}{2\Psi^3}+O\bigl(X^{-1}(\log X)^{-3}\bigr)$, all
functions evaluated at $X$; the two correction groups are the $k=1$ and
$k=2$ terms of \eqref{vav:dg:eq:invformal} with $q=q_K$;
\item[(ii)] the displayed expansion before this proposition holds for $x=x_K$,
with its remainder $O(X^{-2/3}/\log X)$;
\item[(iii)] for every $k\ge1$ the $k$-th term of
\eqref{vav:dg:eq:invformal}, with $q=q_K$, is
$O\bigl(X^{1-2k/3}(\log X)^{-k}\bigr)$; so successive terms decrease by
factors $O(X^{-2/3}/\log X)$, and the third term is of the order of the
remainder in (i).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We use $\Psi=G'$, $\Psi(x)=\log(x/\rho)+O(1/x)$, $\Psi'(x)=\psi'(x+1)>0$
and $\Psi^{(m)}(x)=O(x^{-m})$ for $m\ge1$ (standard polygamma asymptotics),
and $q_K^{(m)}(x)=O(x^{1/3-m})$ for $m\ge0$; more precisely
$q_K=3\kappa x^{1/3}+O(\log x)$ and $q_K'=\kappa x^{-2/3}+O(x^{-1})$.
Write $q,q',\Psi,\Psi'$ for values at $X$ and $d=x-X$.

\emph{Size of $d$.} By definition $G(x)+q_K(x)=G(X)$, and $x,X\to\infty$.
If $x\ge2X$, then $G(x)-G(X)\ge(x-X)\Psi(X)\ge\tfrac x2\Psi(X)$, which is
incompatible with $G(x)-G(X)=-q_K(x)=O(x^{1/3})$ for large $X$; if
$x\le X/2$, then $G(X)-G(x)\ge\tfrac X2\Psi(X/2)$, incompatible with
$O(x^{1/3})=O(X^{1/3})$. So $X/2<x<2X$, and the mean value theorem with
$\Psi\ge\Psi(X/2)\ge\tfrac12\log X$ on $[X/2,2X]$ gives
$d=O(X^{1/3}/\log X)$.

\emph{Expansion.} Taylor's theorem on $[X/2,2X]$ gives
$G(x)-G(X)=\Psi d+\tfrac12\Psi'd^2+O(X^{-2}|d|^3)$ and
$q_K(x)=q+q'd+O(X^{-5/3}d^2)$, so
\[
 \Psi d+q+q'd+\tfrac12\Psi'd^2=O\bigl(X^{-1}(\log X)^{-2}\bigr).
\]
Since $q'd=O(X^{-1/3}/\log X)$ and $\Psi'd^2=O(X^{-1/3}(\log X)^{-2})$,
division by $\Psi$ gives $d=-q/\Psi+d_1$ with
$d_1=O(X^{-1/3}(\log X)^{-2})$. Substituting back,
$q'd=-qq'/\Psi+O(X^{-1}(\log X)^{-2})$ and
$\Psi'd^2=\Psi'q^2/\Psi^2+O(X^{-1}(\log X)^{-3})$, and a second division
by $\Psi$ gives (i). The $k=1$ term of \eqref{vav:dg:eq:invformal} is
$-q/\Psi$ and the $k=2$ term is
$\tfrac12\Psi^{-1}(q^2/\Psi)'=qq'/\Psi^2-\Psi'q^2/(2\Psi^3)$.

\emph{(ii).} Since $K\ge1$,
$q_K=3\kappa X^{1/3}-\tfrac56\log X+\log C+c_1X^{-1/3}+O(X^{-2/3})$, so
$-q_K/\Psi$ is the first displayed fraction up to $O(X^{-2/3}/\log X)$.
Next $q_Kq_K'=3\kappa^2X^{-1/3}+O(X^{-2/3}\log X)$ and
$q_K^2=9\kappa^2X^{2/3}+O(X^{1/3}\log X)$, with $\Psi'=O(1/X)$; hence the
other two groups of (i) equal $3\kappa^2X^{-1/3}/\Psi^2$ and
$-9\kappa^2X^{2/3}\Psi'/(2\Psi^3)$ up to $O(X^{-2/3}/\log X)$.

\emph{(iii).} Say that $g\in\mathcal S(\mu)$, for a positive weight $\mu$,
if $g^{(m)}(X)=O(\mu(X)X^{-m})$ for every $m\ge0$. By Leibniz's rule
$\mathcal S(\mu_1)\mathcal S(\mu_2)\subset\mathcal S(\mu_1\mu_2)$, and
$g\in\mathcal S(\mu)$ implies $g'\in\mathcal S(\mu/X)$ directly from the
definition. We have $q_K\in\mathcal S(X^{1/3})$,
and $1/\Psi\in\mathcal S(1/\log X)$: each derivative of $1/\Psi$ of order
$m\ge1$ is a sum of products $\Psi^{(j_1)}\cdots\Psi^{(j_r)}/\Psi^{r+1}$
with $j_1+\dots+j_r=m$, $j_i\ge1$, hence $O(X^{-m}(\log X)^{-2})$. So
$q_K^k/\Psi\in\mathcal S(X^{k/3}/\log X)$, and $g\mapsto\Psi^{-1}g'$ maps
$\mathcal S(\mu)$ into $\mathcal S(\mu/(X\log X))$. The $k$-th term is
therefore in $\mathcal S(X^{k/3}(X\log X)^{-(k-1)}(\log X)^{-1})
=\mathcal S(X^{1-2k/3}(\log X)^{-k})$.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and main conclusions}\label{vav:hk:sec:scope}
Let $a_n$ denote the number of permutations of $[n]$ avoiding the vincular pattern $12$--$34$, and put
\[
 A(z)=\sum_{n\geq0}\frac{a_n}{n!}z^n,\qquad b_n=[z^n]A(z).
\]
The identification is OEIS A113226~\cite{oeis}. Our one mathematical dependency on the earlier delivered report~87, \emph{Exact Vincular Avoidance Asymptotics}~\cite{hkprior}, is its exact generating function
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:input}
 A(z)=\exp T(z),\qquad
 T(z)=\frac z2+\frac{\pi-3\arctan w}{w},\qquad
 w=\sqrt{4e^{-z}-1},
\end{equation}
with the real positive square root for real $z<\rho:=\log4$. The earlier report proves its combinatorial origin and the complete dominant expansion. We do not repeat or claim those results as new here. Starting from~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:input}, the analytic arguments below are self-contained.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:parameters}
 \zeta_k=\rho+2\pi\ii k,\qquad
 \epsilon_k=(-1)^k,\qquad
 g_k=\frac{3-\epsilon_k}{2}\in\{1,2\}.
\end{equation}
The main results are an exact normalization and its complete sector expansions:
\begin{align}
 b_n&=\sum_{k\in\Z}H_k(n),\qquad n\geq1,\label{vav:hk:eq:intromain}\\
 b_n&=H_0(n)+2\Rea H_1(n)+O_R(R^{-n}),
 \quad |\zeta_1|<R<|\zeta_2|,\label{vav:hk:eq:intropair}\\
 H_k(n)&\sim C_k\zeta_k^{-n}e^{3\kappa_kn^{1/3}}n^{-5/6},\label{vav:hk:eq:introasymp}\\
 \kappa_k&=\left(\frac{g_k^2\pi^2}{4\zeta_k}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
 C_k=e^{\zeta_k/2-3}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_k}{3\pi}}.\label{vav:hk:eq:introconstants}
\end{align}
All roots in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:introconstants} are principal. The series in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:intromain} is absolutely convergent, and $H_k$ is defined by the complete contour in Section~\ref{vav:hk:sec:hankel}. Formula~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:introasymp} extends to every algebraic order for each fixed $k$.

An all-orders formal expansion does not, by itself, determine an exponentially small correction. In~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:intropair}, $H_0$ is an exact contour-defined quantity. Replacing it by a fixed finite dominant expansion would leave an algebraic error much larger than $H_1$. Also, the real pair can exhibit oscillatory cancellation; our error estimates use absolute envelopes and do not assert a relative equivalent at its near-zeros.

generating function \eqref{vav:hk:eq:input} is also Part~I's
Theorem~\ref{vav:thm:main}, proved independently there; so the one input of
this Part is proved twice in this report. In Part~I's letters,
\eqref{vav:hk:eq:introasymp} at $k=0$ reads $H_0(n)\sim D\rho^{-n}e^{3cn^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}$,
the right side of \eqref{vav:eq:main} divided by $n!$; for $k\ne0$ the ratio
$|H_k(n)/H_0(n)|$ decays like $(\rho/|\zeta_k|)^n$ up to stretched factors,
which is why these sectors are invisible in Part~I's Poincar\'e expansion.
\end{writenoteb}

\section{A global branch and the parity of local strengths}\label{vav:hk:sec:branch}
Let
\[
 \Omega=\C\setminus\bigl(({-\infty},-2]\cup[2,\infty)\bigr).
\]
On $\Omega$, use the branches of $\sqrt{4-t^2}$ and $\arcsin(t/2)$ with values $2$ and $0$ at $t=0$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:F}
 F(t)=t\exp\left\{
 \frac{\bigl(3\arcsin(t/2)-\pi/2\bigr)t}{\sqrt{4-t^2}}
 \right\},\qquad A(z)=F(e^{z/2}).
\end{equation}
The preimage of the two $t$-cuts is exactly the family
\[
 \zeta_k+[0,\infty),\qquad k\in\Z.
\]
Thus~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:F} gives a single-valued holomorphic branch on their complement $D$. On the real interval $z<\rho$, the identity
\[
 \arcsin(e^{z/2}/2)=\frac\pi2-\arctan\sqrt{4e^{-z}-1}
\]
proves agreement with~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:input}; the identity theorem then identifies the continuation.

Oddness of $\arcsin$ and evenness of the normalized square root give
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:shift}
 F(-t)=-F(t)\exp\left(\frac{\pi t}{\sqrt{4-t^2}}\right).
\end{equation}
In particular $A(z+4\pi\ii)=A(z)$, whereas a $2\pi\ii$ shift is not a period. The branch is real under conjugation: $A(\overline z)=\overline{A(z)}$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact local branch]\label{vav:hk:lem:local}
For sufficiently small $\delta\in\C\setminus(-\infty,0]$, with principal $\sqrt\delta$, one has $A(\zeta_k-\delta)=\exp T_k(\zeta_k-\delta)$, where
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:local}
 T_k(\zeta_k-\delta)=\frac{\zeta_k-\delta}{2}
 +\frac{g_k\pi}{\sqrt{e^\delta-1}}
 -3\frac{\arctan\sqrt{e^\delta-1}}{\sqrt{e^\delta-1}}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, with $T_{0,k}=\zeta_k/2-3$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:localexp}
 T_k(\zeta_k-\delta)
 =g_k\pi\delta^{-1/2}+T_{0,k}
 -\frac{g_k\pi}{4}\delta^{1/2}+\frac\delta2
 +O(\delta^{3/2}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $w=\sqrt{e^\delta-1}$. For even $k$, $t=e^{(\zeta_k-\delta)/2}=2e^{-\delta/2}$ and $\arcsin(t/2)=\pi/2-\arctan w$. For odd $k$, $t=-2e^{-\delta/2}$ and $\arcsin(t/2)=-\pi/2+\arctan w$, while $t/\sqrt{4-t^2}=-1/w$. Substitution gives~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:local}. The quotient $\arctan w/w$ is analytic in $w^2$ near zero. Expanding it and $(e^\delta-1)^{-1/2}$ proves~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:localexp}. The logarithm is fixed modulo $2\pi\ii$; its exponential retains the important factor $e^{\zeta_k/2}=2(-1)^k$.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenoteb}
Part~I's Section~\ref{vav:sec:continuation} continues the EGF only in a
disc of radius less than $|\zeta_1|$, cut along $[\rho,R)$, and notes that
the ODE continues it along paths avoiding $\rho+2\pi\ii k$. The global
branch \eqref{vav:hk:eq:F} on the domain $D$ with horizontal cuts is new
here; it is ``canonical'' only in the sense the source states in
Section~\ref{vav:hk:sec:hankel} (this branch, these cuts). By
Lemma~\ref{vav:hk:lem:local} every $\zeta_k$ is a genuine essential
singularity of this branch, in agreement with
Proposition~\ref{vav:dg:prop:structure} of Part~III (see the note after
it); the $k=0$ case of \eqref{vav:hk:eq:local} is Part~I's
\eqref{vav:eq:local} with $w=\delta$ there.
\end{writenoteb}

\section{Bank values and the full-cut sectors}\label{vav:hk:sec:hankel}
For $x>0$, write
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:bankmod}
 q(x)=\sqrt{1-e^{-x}},\qquad L(x)=\atanh q(x)=\acosh(e^{x/2}),\qquad
 B(x)=2e^{x/2}e^{-3L(x)/q(x)}.
\end{equation}
Denote the upper and lower limits on the $k$th cut by $A_k^+(x)$ and $A_k^-(x)$.
\begin{lemma}[Banks and their bounds]\label{vav:hk:lem:banks}
For every $k\in\Z$ and $x>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:banks}
 A_k^\pm(x)=\epsilon_kB(x)\exp\left(\pm\frac{\ii g_k\pi}{q(x)}\right).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:banklimits}
 B(x)\longrightarrow2e^{-3}\quad(x\downarrow0),\qquad
 B(x)=\frac14e^{-x}\{1+O(xe^{-x})\}\quad(x\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Thus $B$ is bounded and integrable on $(0,\infty)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the upper bank, $\delta=-x-\ii0$, so the local square root continues as $-\ii q$. Also $\arctan(-\ii q)=-\ii\atanh q$. The lower bank has the conjugate choices. This gives~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:banks}, first near the endpoint and then along the complete bank by continuation. As $x\downarrow0$, $L/q\to1$. At infinity, $q=1+O(e^{-x})$ and $L=x/2+\log2+O(e^{-x})$, giving~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:banklimits}. In fact both individual banks have leading value $-e^{-x}/4$ at infinity.
\end{proof}

Fix
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:radius}
 0<r<\min(\rho/2,\pi).
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal C_k(r)$ be the endpoint circle traversed clockwise from the lower bank of $\zeta_k+r$ to its upper bank. Equivalently,
\[
 z=\zeta_k-re^{\ii\alpha},\qquad \alpha:\pi\longrightarrow-\pi.
\]
For integer $n\geq1$, define the \emph{canonical full-cut sector}
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:H}
 H_k(n)=\frac1{2\pi\ii}\left\{
 \int_{\mathcal C_k(r)}\frac{A(z)}{z^{n+1}}\dd z
 +\int_r^\infty\frac{A_k^+(x)-A_k^-(x)}{(\zeta_k+x)^{n+1}}\dd x
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The path runs inward along the lower bank, clockwise around the endpoint, and outward along the upper bank. A pole test fixes the sign: with $A(z)=1/(\rho-z)$, a clockwise circle contributes $-\operatorname{Res}_{z=\rho}(A(z)z^{-n-1})=\rho^{-n-1}$.

Cauchy's theorem in the cut annulus between two radii satisfying~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:radius} proves that~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:H} is independent of $r$. Accordingly, the radius may later depend on $n$. The word ``canonical'' here refers to the specified global branch and its horizontal cuts.

\begin{remark}[Why the endpoint loop matters]
The jump integral in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:H} converges absolutely even if its lower limit is replaced by zero, because $B$ is bounded. The problem with a jump-only formula is therefore not divergence. It is the omission of the endpoint loop, whose integrand grows like $\exp(g_k\pi/\sqrt\delta)$ on the left of the endpoint. There is no vanishing small-circle estimate. Radius independence and integrable bank values imply that the shrinking loop has a finite integral limit, but do not make that limit zero. Retaining a fixed positive radius is sufficient for all our arguments.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[{[write]} An explicit majorant for the banks]\label{vav:hk:rem:bankbound}
For every $x>0$, $B(x)<2e^{-x}(1+q(x))^{-3}\le2e^{-x}$; in particular
$\int_0^\infty B(x)\dd x<2$. Indeed $q\in(0,1)$ and
$(1+q)/(1-q)=(1+q)^2/(1-q^2)=(1+q)^2e^{x}$, so
$L=\atanh q=\tfrac12\log\frac{1+q}{1-q}=\tfrac x2+\log(1+q)>0$. Hence
$L/q>L$ and $3L/q>\tfrac{3x}2+3\log(1+q)$, which gives the bound. The same
identity gives $L=\acosh(e^{x/2})$ and the limit $L=x/2+\log2+O(e^{-x})$ used
in Lemma~\ref{vav:hk:lem:banks}. The bound $B\le2e^{-x}$ is the one the
delivered \path{code/07-sectors-check_sector_numerics.py} uses, in a comment
without proof, for its omitted-bank tail bounds.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact decomposition and uniform sector tails}\label{vav:hk:sec:decomposition}
\begin{theorem}[Absolutely convergent sector decomposition]\label{vav:hk:thm:sum}
For every integer $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:sum}
 b_n=\sum_{k\in\Z}H_k(n),\qquad
 H_{-k}(n)=\overline{H_k(n)}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed admissible $r$, there is $C_r<\infty$, independent of $k,n$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:uniformbound}
 |H_k(n)|\leq C_r(|\zeta_k|-r)^{-n-1}.
\end{equation}
The series in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:sum} is therefore absolutely convergent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Periodicity leaves only two translated endpoint-circle branches. Let $M_r$ be the maximum of $|A|$ on these two compact circle banks. Since $\Rea\zeta_k=\rho>0$,
\[
 |\zeta_k+x|\geq|\zeta_k|\quad(x\geq0),\qquad
 |z|\geq|\zeta_k|-r\quad(z\in \mathcal C_k(r)).
\]
Thus~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:uniformbound} follows with
\[
 C_r=rM_r+\frac1\pi\int_r^\infty B(x)\dd x.
\]
Since $|\zeta_k|\sim2\pi|k|$, the bound implies absolute convergence for $n\geq1$.

It remains to prove the exact identity. On the lines $\Ima z=(2j+1)\pi$, write $s=e^{x/2}$. Formula~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:F} gives the common modulus
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:strip}
 B_*(x)=s\exp\left(-\frac{3s\asinh(s/2)}{\sqrt{4+s^2}}\right).
\end{equation}
It is $O(e^{x/2})$ as $x\to-\infty$ and $O(e^{-x})$ as $x\to\infty$, so $M_*:=\int_\R B_*(x)\dd x<\infty$.

The vertical sides also vanish. At zero, $F(t)=t(1+O(t))$. At infinity, uniformly in the closed upper or lower half-plane with the respective bank limits,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:infinity}
 F(t)=-t^{-2}\left\{1+O\left(\frac{\log|t|}{|t|^2}\right)\right\}.
\end{equation}
For example, in the upper half-plane,
$\arcsin(t/2)=\pi/2+\ii\log t+O(t^{-2})$ and
$t/\sqrt{4-t^2}=\ii+O(t^{-2})$; the lower half-plane is analogous. These expansions also hold in the boundary limits. Hence $A(z)=O(e^{\Rea z/2})$ on left vertical sides and $O(e^{-\Rea z})$ on right vertical sides.

Fix $K\geq0$, set $Y_K=(2K+1)\pi$, and apply the residue theorem to $A(z)z^{-n-1}$ in the rectangle with horizontal sides at $\pm Y_K$, slit along the cuts $|k|\leq K$ and with radius-$r$ endpoint disks removed. The external rectangle is counterclockwise; the slit boundaries are clockwise with the analytic domain to the left. Each is lower bank inward, clockwise endpoint loop, upper bank outward, exactly the orientation of~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:H}. Send the two vertical sides to infinity. Their integrals vanish, and the bank integrals converge absolutely. The only enclosed pole is at zero, with residue $b_n$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:recterror}
 b_n=\sum_{|k|\leq K}H_k(n)+E_K(n),\qquad
 |E_K(n)|\leq\frac{M_*}{\pi Y_K^{n+1}}.
\end{equation}
Letting $K\to\infty$ proves~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:sum}. Reflection of the entire oriented contour, including $1/(2\pi\ii)$, proves conjugacy.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact dominant normalization]\label{vav:hk:cor:pair}
For $|\zeta_1|<R<|\zeta_2|$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:pair}
 b_n=H_0(n)+2\Rea H_1(n)+O_R(R^{-n}).
\end{equation}
More generally, a fixed number of sectors may be retained, with a geometric remainder separated from the next singularity modulus.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose $r$ still smaller so that $R<|\zeta_2|-r$. For $n\geq1$, factor $R^{-n}$ in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:uniformbound} and use
\[
 \left(\frac{R}{|\zeta_k|-r}\right)^n
 \leq\frac{R}{|\zeta_k|-r},\qquad |k|\geq2.
\]
The remaining inverse-square sum converges. Conjugacy gives~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:pair}. The same argument applies after any fixed finite collection of nearest cuts.
\end{proof}

This theorem and its corollary are the exponentially improved form asked
for in Part~I's Question~4, and are new. The delivered numerical checks
test each $H_k$ against its own asymptotics (Section~\ref{vav:hk:sec:checks}),
not the sum \eqref{vav:hk:eq:sum}. The sum itself was checked independently

$H_0+2\sum_{k=1}^6\Rea H_k$ agreed with $b_n$ to a relative error
$3.5\times10^{-12}$ at $n=6$ (where the two last sectors still have size

$1.5\times10^{-39}$ at $n=30$; $H_0(n)$ was real, and at $n=30$,

\end{writenoteb}

\section{The complex saddle on the horizontal endpoint circle}\label{vav:hk:sec:saddle}
Fix $k$ for this section, and abbreviate $\zeta=\zeta_k$, $g=g_k$,
$\phi=\arg\zeta$, $\kappa=\kappa_k$. Then
\[
 |\phi|<\frac\pi2,\qquad \arg\kappa=-\frac\phi3,\qquad \Rea\kappa>0.
\]
Use radius
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:rn}
 r_n=\left|\left(\frac{g\pi\zeta}{2n}\right)^{2/3}\right|,
 \qquad \delta_s=\zeta\kappa n^{-2/3}=r_ne^{2\ii\phi/3}.
\end{equation}
For large $n$ this is admissible in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:H}. On $\delta=r_ne^{\ii\alpha}$, $-\pi\leq\alpha\leq\pi$, the exponent, after removing the constant and $\zeta^{-n-1}$, is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:leadphase}
 \frac{n\delta}{\zeta}+\frac{g\pi}{\sqrt\delta}+O(n^{-1/3}),
\end{equation}
uniformly on the whole circle. Indeed, the local analytic correction is $O(\sqrt{r_n})$ and the quadratic kernel correction is $O(nr_n^2)$.

The leading real part of~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:leadphase} is
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:fphi}
 |\kappa|n^{1/3}f_\phi(\alpha),\qquad
 f_\phi(\alpha)=\cos(\alpha-\phi)+2\cos(\alpha/2).
\end{equation}
Its derivative factors as
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:derivative}
 f_\phi'(\alpha)=-2\sin(3\alpha/4-\phi/2)
                         \cos(\alpha/4-\phi/2).
\end{equation}
Throughout the angular interval the cosine is positive, and the sine argument lies strictly between $-\pi$ and $\pi$. Hence the unique global maximum is
\[
 \alpha_s=2\phi/3,\qquad f_\phi(\alpha_s)=3\cos(\phi/3).
\]
This proves the principal saddle selection without rotating any cut.

Writing $\alpha=\alpha_s+\theta$, the leading complex phase becomes
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:phasecentral}
 \kappa n^{1/3}\left(e^{\ii\theta}+2e^{-\ii\theta/2}\right),
\end{equation}
with second derivative $-3\kappa n^{1/3}/2$ at zero. Since $\Rea\kappa>0$, the real $\theta$ contour already has a decaying complex Gaussian. For some $c_k>0$, the real-part deficit is bounded below by
\[
 c_kn^{1/3}\min(\theta^2,1)
\]
on the fixed angular interval. In particular, angles outside
$|\theta|\leq n^{-1/6+\eta}$ have relative loss $e^{-c_kn^{2\eta}}$, for any fixed sufficiently small $\eta>0$.

The bank integrals are exponentially smaller. Lemma~\ref{vav:hk:lem:banks} and monotonicity of $|\zeta+x|$ for $x\geq0$ give
\begin{align}\label{vav:hk:eq:bankminor}
 \int_{r_n}^\infty B(x)|\zeta+x|^{-n-1}\dd x
 &\leq \|B\|_1|\zeta+r_n|^{-n-1}\notag\\
 &=O_k\left(|\zeta|^{-n-1}
 e^{-|\kappa|\cos\phi\,n^{1/3}+O(n^{-1/3})}\right).
\end{align}
This has strictly smaller exponential rate than the saddle envelope
$|\zeta|^{-n}e^{3\Rea\kappa n^{1/3}}$.

Finally the clockwise loop, with the orientation reversed after parametrization, is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:circleintegral}
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
 r_ne^{\ii\alpha} A(\zeta-r_ne^{\ii\alpha})
 (\zeta-r_ne^{\ii\alpha})^{-n-1}\dd\alpha.
\end{equation}
At $\alpha_s$ the numerator is $\delta_s$, and $\delta_s/\zeta=\kappa n^{-2/3}$. Gaussian evaluation yields
\[
 e^{\zeta/2-3}\zeta^{-n}e^{3\kappa n^{1/3}}
 \frac{\kappa n^{-2/3}}{2\pi}
 \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3\kappa n^{1/3}}}
 =C_k\zeta_k^{-n}e^{3\kappa_kn^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}.
\]
The negative sign of $e^{\zeta_k/2}$ for odd $k$ is therefore part of the amplitude, rather than an arbitrary phase convention.
\begin{writenoteb}
The positive constant $c_k$ of this section is not a coefficient $c_{k,j}$
of Section~\ref{vav:hk:sec:allorders}. At $k=0$ the saddle circle is
centred at $\rho$ with radius $r_n=\rho c\,n^{-2/3}$ and $\delta_s=r_n$, so
the saddle point $\rho-\delta_s=\rho(1-cn^{-2/3})$ is Part~I's
$\rho(1-t_0)$ and Part~III's chord abscissa; Part~I and Part~III deform to
a vertical segment there, while this Part stays on the circle. The internal
audit \path{07-sectors-audit-sector_remainders_review.md} notes that its
window exponent $\eta$ here equals $\eta/6$ in
Section~\ref{vav:hk:sec:allorders}'s $h$-based window, a reuse of notation.
\end{writenoteb}

\section{All orders and explicit correction coefficients}\label{vav:hk:sec:allorders}
The circle formulation also provides a finite coefficient algorithm directly on the original contour. Put
\[
 h=n^{-1/6},\quad \alpha=\alpha_s+hy,\quad
 u(h,y)=\kappa h^4e^{\ii hy},\quad T_0=\zeta/2-3,
\]
and define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal T(u)&=\frac{\zeta(1-u)}2
 +\frac{g\pi}{\sqrt{e^{\zeta u}-1}}
 -3\frac{\arctan\sqrt{e^{\zeta u}-1}}{\sqrt{e^{\zeta u}-1}},\label{vav:hk:eq:Tcal}\\
 S(h,y)&=\mathcal T(u(h,y))-(h^{-6}+1)\log(1-u(h,y))\notag\\
 &\hspace{7mm}-3\kappa h^{-2}-T_0+\frac{3\kappa}{4}y^2+\ii hy.\label{vav:hk:eq:S}
\end{align}
The last term is the logarithm of the circle Jacobian factor $e^{\ii hy}$. The relation $g\pi/\sqrt{\zeta\kappa}=2\kappa$ on the chosen branches cancels all negative powers in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:S}; the constant term also cancels. Thus
\[
 S(h,y)\in h\C[y][[h]],
\]
and the exact formal identity $S(-h,-y)=S(h,y)$ shows that its coefficient of $h^j$ has parity $j$ as a polynomial in $y$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete expansion of each fixed sector]\label{vav:hk:thm:allorders}
For every fixed $k\in\Z$ and $K\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:allorders}
 H_k(n)=C_k\zeta_k^{-n}e^{3\kappa_kn^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}
 \left\{\sum_{j=0}^Kc_{k,j}n^{-j/3}
       +O_{k,K}(n^{-(K+1)/3})\right\}.
\end{equation}
The coefficients are determined by
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:cj}
 c_{k,j}=\sqrt{\frac{3\kappa}{4\pi}}
 \int_\R e^{-3\kappa y^2/4}[h^{2j}]e^{S(h,y)}\dd y.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{align}
 c_{k,0}&=1,\label{vav:hk:eq:c0}\\
 c_{k,1}&=\frac{\kappa^2(1-\zeta)}2-\frac5{36\kappa},\label{vav:hk:eq:c1}\\
 c_{k,2}&=\frac{\kappa^4(1-\zeta)^2}{8}
          +\frac{\kappa(53\zeta-17)}{72}
          -\frac{35}{2592\kappa^2}.\label{vav:hk:eq:c2}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the central window $|y|\leq h^{-\eta}$, with a fixed $0<\eta<1$, the circle contribution equals
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:centralintegral}
 e^{T_0}\zeta^{-n}e^{3\kappa h^{-2}}
 \frac{\kappa h^5}{2\pi}
 \int_{-h^{-\eta}}^{h^{-\eta}}
 e^{-3\kappa y^2/4}e^{S(h,y)}\dd y.
\end{equation}
For every finite order $J$, Taylor expansion of the convergent expression~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:Tcal}, the logarithm, and the exponential has a remainder bounded by
\[
 C_Jh^{J+1}(1+|y|^{m_J})
 \exp\{C_Jh(1+|y|^3)\}
\]
for some finite $m_J$. For detail, write $a(\delta)=\sqrt{\delta/(e^\delta-1)}$ with $a(0)=1$, and $b(\delta)=-\delta/2-3(\arctan\sqrt{e^\delta-1}/\sqrt{e^\delta-1}-1)$. Both are analytic at zero. The nonconstant part of $\mathcal T$ is $2\kappa h^{-2}e^{-\ii hy/2}a(\zeta\kappa h^4e^{\ii hy})+b(\zeta\kappa h^4e^{\ii hy})$. After cancellation of the negative powers, the leading residual phase is cubic in $y$ times $h$. On $0\leq t\leq h$, every fixed $t$-derivative of $S(t,y)$ is polynomially bounded in $|y|$, and $|S(t,y)|\leq Ct(1+|y|^3)$ because $|ty|\leq h^{1-\eta}$. Taylor's theorem and the finite derivative formulas for $e^S$ give the displayed bound. For $h|y|\to0$, its last exponential is absorbed by a smaller fixed Gaussian because $\Rea\kappa>0$. The integrated remainder is consequently $O(h^{J+1})$.

Expand through $J=2K+1$. Odd polynomial coefficients integrate to zero on the symmetric window. Extending each polynomial--Gaussian integral to $\R$ costs less than every power of $h$. The angular minor-arc estimate in Section~\ref{vav:hk:sec:saddle} controls the omitted circle, and~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:bankminor} controls the two banks. This proves an integrated error $O(h^{2K+2})$ relative to the leading Gaussian factor. Since
\[
 \int_\R e^{-3\kappa y^2/4}\dd y=\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3\kappa}},
\]
with principal root, the prefactor in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:centralintegral} is exactly $C_kn^{-5/6}$. This proves~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:allorders} and~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:cj}.

The normalized moments are
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:moments}
 \mathbb E(y^{2m})=(2m-1)!!\left(\frac2{3\kappa}\right)^m,
 \qquad \mathbb E(y^{2m+1})=0.
\end{equation}
Only finitely many Taylor terms are used at each order, so~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:cj} is an algebraic algorithm. The first two terms of the exponent are
\begin{equation}\label{vav:hk:eq:S12}
 S=\ii\left(y-\frac{\kappa y^3}{8}\right)h
 +\left(\frac{\kappa^2(1-\zeta)}2+\frac{3\kappa y^4}{64}\right)h^2
 +O(h^3).
\end{equation}
Taking the moments of $[h^2]e^S$ gives~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:c1}. For~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:c2}, all required terms are generated by
\begin{align}\label{vav:hk:eq:S4}
 S={}&\frac{\kappa}{h^2}
       (e^{\ii hy}+2e^{-\ii hy/2}-3)
       +\frac{3\kappa y^2}4+\ii hy\notag\\
    &+\frac{\kappa^2h^2}{2}(e^{2\ii hy}-\zeta e^{\ii hy/2})
       +\kappa(1+\zeta/2)h^4e^{\ii hy}+O(h^6),
\end{align}
where the displayed exponentials are expanded through degree four. Applying~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:moments} to $[h^4]e^S$ yields~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:c2}. The supplied symbolic script verifies both formulas exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Dependence on the sector index]
All constants in the local asymptotic proof may depend on fixed $k$. No interchange of the $k\to\infty$ and $n\to\infty$ limits is claimed. Uniform control of the infinite remainder instead comes from the exact fixed-radius bound~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:uniformbound}. This separates the two roles cleanly.
\end{remark}

\section{The first conjugate pair with an absolute error}\label{vav:hk:sec:pair}
Let
\[
 \mathcal E_1(n)=|\zeta_1|^{-n}
 e^{3\Rea\kappa_1 n^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}.
\]
Combining Corollary~\ref{vav:hk:cor:pair} and Theorem~\ref{vav:hk:thm:allorders} gives, for every fixed $K\geq0$ and $|\zeta_1|<R<|\zeta_2|$,
\begin{align}\label{vav:hk:eq:finalpair}
 b_n-H_0(n)
 ={}&2\Rea\left\{C_1\zeta_1^{-n}e^{3\kappa_1n^{1/3}}n^{-5/6}
       \sum_{j=0}^Kc_{1,j}n^{-j/3}\right\}\notag\\
 &+O_K\bigl(\mathcal E_1(n)n^{-(K+1)/3}\bigr)+O_R(R^{-n}).
\end{align}
The leading oscillatory factor can equivalently be written
\[
 2|C_1|\mathcal E_1(n)
 \cos\bigl(-n\arg\zeta_1+3\Ima\kappa_1n^{1/3}+\arg C_1\bigr).
\]
Formula~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:finalpair}, rather than an equivalent for this cosine, remains valid near cancellation indices.

For numerical orientation,
\begin{align*}
 |\zeta_1|&=6.434301023424\ldots,\\
 |\zeta_2|&=12.642605841879\ldots,\\
 \kappa_1&=1.037853533783\ldots
            -0.502894353735\ldots\ii,\\
 C_1&=-0.033949227189\ldots
            +0.007791818790\ldots\ii,\\
 c_{1,1}&=-3.546963506118\ldots
            -2.440313169662\ldots\ii.
\end{align*}
At $k=0$, the leading constant is $0.035706041258\ldots$ and $c_{0,1}=-0.398274243295\ldots$, agreeing with report~87. The exact difference $b_n-H_0(n)$ is exponentially smaller than the dominant scale, so uniqueness of Poincare coefficients also identifies the complete $k=0$ expansion with that earlier one.

\section{Further questions with specific next steps}\label{vav:hk:sec:questions}
\begin{writenoteb}
This section serves as Part~IV's ``Further questions and research''
section in the sense of the collection's standing rule; Report~94 states no
unproved claim outside it, and none of its claims was found wrong. The
first question below refines Part~I's Question~4, which Part~IV answers
for each fixed $k$; the third is a case of Part~I's Question~3 (certified
constants), still open. Report~87's open items are in
Section~\ref{vav:dg:sec:questions}. A further question, not asked by any
source: Part~II's marked generating function $H(z,u)$ has, for $u\ne1$,
its own complex singularities, and its sectors are not treated
(Part~II excludes them, Section~\ref{vav:rf:sec:checks}).
\end{writenoteb}
\paragraph{Uniformly growing sector index.}
The present local expansion fixes $k$, while the exact tail estimate is uniform. A useful next problem is a simultaneous expansion when $|k|$ grows with $n$. Since $|\delta_s|\asymp(|k|/n)^{2/3}$, the small-neighborhood requirement naturally suggests first treating $|k|=o(n)$ and tracking the angular-gap constants. This scaling observation is motivation, not a uniform theorem.

\paragraph{Late coefficients and optimal truncation.}
Determine large-$j$ growth of $c_{0,j}$ and whether an optimally truncated dominant expansion can be connected quantitatively to the exact $H_0$. Such a result would relate the contour normalization to a resummation procedure. No Borel summability or resurgence claim follows merely from the finite-order results proved here.

\paragraph{Certified numerical extraction.}
Interval quadrature of~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:H}, using explicit cut-bank tails and a saddle-centered circle, could produce certified values for exponentially small sectors without subtracting two enormous nearly equal dominant quantities. A useful implementation target is a verified absolute error below the $|k|\geq2$ tail in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:pair}.

\paragraph{Oscillatory cancellation.}
The phase in~\eqref{vav:hk:eq:finalpair} describes the first pair's sign changes and near-zeros. Establishing quantitative lower bounds away from zero on selected integer subsequences would require control of the arithmetic sampling of this phase. The current absolute-error formulation deliberately avoids such an unproved lower bound.

\paragraph{Other combinatorial transforms.}
The key global feature is a periodic exponential pullback with two different singular strengths. It is natural to seek other exact combinatorial EGFs with this structure and to test whether the same full-cut normalization supplies their beyond-all-orders terms. An application must verify its global branch and boundary decay separately; local singular expansions alone are insufficient.
