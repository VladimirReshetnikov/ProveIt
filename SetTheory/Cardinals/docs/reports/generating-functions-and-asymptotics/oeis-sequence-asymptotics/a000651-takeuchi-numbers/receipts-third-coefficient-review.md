## Independent exact algebra

The audit starts with literal manuscript formulas for g, p1, p2, H3 and p3. It constructs the log product coefficients directly from finite power sums. It derives the shifted-model Taylor coefficients using n-differentiation, forms the exponential coefficients in exact rational polynomial arithmetic, and takes Poisson expectations with moments generated by M_(r+1)=w(M_r'+M_r). The first three expected residual coefficients vanish. The fourth, before p3, agrees identically with the manuscript's H3.

Exact rational checks give:

- `-w*p3' + 3*(w+1)*p3 + H3 = 0`
- `lim_(w→∞) p3(w)/w^4 = 1/72`
- The p3 numerator has degree 13 and denominator degree 9

For j=3, the homogeneous ODE solution is c exp(3w)w^3. No nonzero such function has polynomial growth. The rational p3 and every fixed derivative have polynomial growth on w≥1, with no pole there. Therefore it is exactly the canonical quadrature solution, not merely a solution up to a normalization ambiguity.

## Explicit fifth-order local remainder

With X=1+w+k and central range k≤w², the independently computed final log coefficients A1,A2,A3,A4 have maximum joint growth degrees 2,3,4,5. Thus |A_r|≤C_r X^(r+1). The rational coefficients have denominator factors only w+1 (apart from constants), so the degree estimates bound the whole interval w≥1 after enlarging constants.

Scaled derivative remainder checks give:

- F sixth derivative: n^-5 times a rational function of growth degree 0
- g fifth derivative: n^-5 times a rational function of growth degree 1
- (p1/n) fourth derivative: n^-5 times a rational function of growth degree 1
- (p2/n²) third derivative: n^-5 times a rational function of growth degree 2
- (p3/n³) second derivative: n^-5 times a rational function of growth degree 4

On [n-k-1,n]⊂[n/2,n], the inverse powers of the shifted argument differ from those at n by bounded factors, and W of the shifted argument is at most w and differs from w by a bounded amount. Including jump powers gives respectively j^6, j^5(1+w), j^4(1+w), j^3(1+w)^2, and j²(1+w)^4, all bounded by C X^6. The product-log remainder is also O(k^6/n^5). This verifies the claimed log remainder O(X^6/n^5) uniformly.

Since X²/n→0 uniformly in the central range, exponential expansion through n^-4 leaves O(X^10/n^5). One explicit justification is to first truncate exp(Z) by ordinary degree four, where Z=Σ_(r=1)^4 A_r/n^r and |Z|≤C X²/n; the degree-five exponential remainder is O(X^10/n^5). The finitely many monomials in the retained exponential polynomial whose n-order exceeds four obey the same bound. The separate log remainder is smaller. Poisson expectation satisfies E(1+w+K)^10=O((1+w)^10). Thus the local row defect is O((1+w)^10/n^5) after fourth-order cancellation. The tail bounds in the arbitrary-order local lemma apply unchanged to this rational p3, including the negligible forcing.

## Transfer, normalization, and inverse

Using the established descending-chain stability statement, the bounded normalized true solution has additive defect f_r=O((1+W(r))^10/r^5). Its absolute tail sum is O((1+W(n))^10/n^4). Choose the coupling failure exponent D>4; the exceptional contribution is smaller. The existing positive-state row argument supplies a positive limiting constant, and p3(W(n))/n³→0 identifies it with the same C. Taking logarithms therefore retains the error O((1+w)^10/n^4).

The finite third-order log model has derivative asymptotic to W(x), uniformly comparable to W(n) on [n/2,2n]. Its inverse at T_n is first localized to this interval and then the mean-value theorem divides the log error by a positive multiple of W(n). Since (1+w)/w stays bounded for w≥1, the inverse error is O((1+w)^9/n^4), as stated. This is a theoretical inverse statement using exact C; it does not certify numerical values of C or a numerical onset.
