\begin{abstract}
Two manuscripts on one family of exact finite sums, bound into one report:
$a_p(n)=\sum_{0\le k\le n/(p+1)}(n-pk)^{pk}\binom{n-pk}{k}$, which for
$p=1,2,3$ are OEIS A360592, A360479 and A360747. Part~I (Report~230, the base)
proves every fixed algebraic order for every fixed $p\ge1$ from an exact
residue-conditioned Poisson representation: a small deformation for $p\ge2$
with explicit rational generators, and at the critical endpoint $p=1$ a
centered resummation with half-power corrections only and first correction
$c/4+11c^3/8$, $c=\sqrt{e/2}$; it identifies the first corrections of the three
OEIS refinements as wrong (an extra $1/(8c_p)$ at $p=1,2$, a nonzero
$n^{-1/4}$ term at $p=3$ whose true value is zero) while their leading
equivalents stand; it proves, for $p\ge2$, weighted density expansions, moment
refinements and sharp total-variation constants for the inactive-vertex count
against two conditioned Poisson references, and controlled Newton and Lagrange
inverses with a two-ceiling threshold envelope. Part~II (Report~235) answers
Part~I's question on the critical marked law: for $p=1$ the globally optimal
parity-conditioned binomial approximation of the inactive count has total
variation $\sim\frac58\log2\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\mu^{3/2}/n^2$, attained by an
explicit law, with every asymptotically optimal parameter sequence
characterized. The write quotes the three OEIS entries with their revision
numbers, checks the source's transcription of their formulas exactly and the
refutation against the exact sums, recomputes every coefficient, polynomial
identity and printed decimal of both Parts with its own code, compares the
inverses with the repository's transseries volume, and gathers the open
questions. The report is unrefereed, and nothing in it is formalized.
\end{abstract}

\begin{writenote}[What is not claimed, collected from both Parts]
\emph{Part~I:} every asymptotic assertion keeps $p$ and the order fixed; no
secondary exponential sector, optimal truncation, growing-$p$ limit, effective
inverse onset, certified constant or critical marked-law theorem; the
transcendental diagnostics are not interval-certified, and the $p=3$
truncation through $t^{12}$ is still about $66\%$ wrong at $n=10^4$; the OEIS
comparison concerns snapshots without verified revision histories, and no
first-priority or exhaustive-search claim is made; the classical Poisson,
Charlier, root-filter, Taylor, Newton, Lagrange and Lambert machinery is
credited. \emph{Part~II:} the infimum is over the parity-conditioned binomial
family only; no exact finite-$n$ minimizer, no optimality over all positive
families, no effective error certificate, no claim for other $p$ or a growing
modulus; positive binomial fitting, Charlier corrections and Hermite
absolute-moment limits are credited to the literature it names, and the median
minimization is called elementary $L^1$ projection; no worldwide priority claim
and no claim of external review; the coupling appendix is supplementary and
makes no global approximation claim. \emph{The write adds:} its checks are
exact or floating computations; its transseries remark claims no novelty for
any inversion.
\end{writenote}

\begin{partabstract}
For the exact sums
\[
 a_p(n)=\sum_{0\le k\le n/(p+1)}(n-pk)^{pk}\binom{n-pk}{k},
\]
we prove every fixed algebraic order for each fixed integer $p\ge1$.
An exact residue-conditioned Poisson representation gives a small deformation
when $p\ge2$ and a critical, nonvanishing deformation when $p=1$.
The latter requires a centered resummation and has half-power corrections,
with no odd quarter powers. We calculate corrected coefficients for OEIS
A360592, A360479, and A360747; their observed leading equivalents remain valid.
For $p\ge2$ we prove weighted density expansions, mean and variance refinements,
and sharp total-variation constants for the inactive-vertex count, including an
improved tilted Poisson reference. Explicit finite smooth models yield controlled
Newton and convergent local Lagrange inverses, arbitrary fixed index accuracy,
and a two-ceiling envelope for exact thresholds. The proofs include global
tail bounds and never differentiate an unspecified discrete remainder.
A self-contained exact-arithmetic package accompanies the article.

\noindent\textbf{Scope.} All asymptotic assertions keep $p$ and the requested order fixed.
No secondary exponential sector, optimal truncation theorem, growing-$p$ limit,
effective finite inverse onset, or critical marked-law theorem is asserted.
\end{partabstract}

\part{Optimal positive binomial approximation for the critical inactive count}\label{spb:ml:part}

\begin{partabstract}
Choose a term of $a(n)=\sum_k(n-k)^k\binom{n-k}{k}$ with probability proportional to its weight, and set $R_n=n-2k$. We determine the globally asymptotically best approximation of $R_n$ by a binomial distribution conditioned to the parity of $n$, with arbitrary integer trial count and arbitrary success probability whenever conditioning is defined. If $\mu\sim\sqrt{en/2}$ is the explicit tilted Poisson parameter, the optimal total variation error is
\[
\inf_B\TV(\Law(R_n),B)\sim
\frac{5\log2}{8}\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,\frac{\mu^{3/2}}{n^2}.
\]
An explicit positive construction attains this bound. Every asymptotically optimal parameter sequence has a uniquely determined mean adjustment at scale $n^{-1}$ and trial count localized to $o(n^{1/4})$. The proof supplies all fixed order marked expansions, a polynomially weighted common reference density comparison, support and rounding estimates, and a localization argument covering rare parity events. Its fine step uses an exact characteristic function Jacobian rather than inferring moment closeness from total variation. The classical Charlier, Hermite, and binomial fitting mechanisms are credited; the contribution is the complete model specific argument and its constants. Computation corroborates the proof and carries separate limitations.

\end{partabstract}
