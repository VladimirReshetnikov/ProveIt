\section{Controlled inversion of the leading model}\label{ssm:sec:inverse}
A relative $1+o(1)$ count equivalent is an $o(1)$ logarithmic approximation. Because the logarithm grows quadratically, this already gives a sharper continuous-index statement than an $o(1)$ index error, but it does not justify a certified finite-input rounding rule.

Let $s=1$ designate even positive indices and $s=0$ designate odd positive indices. Put
\begin{equation}\label{ssm:eq:inverseconstants}
 a=\frac{\log2}{2},\qquad b=\frac{\log2}{2}+\log q,\qquad
 c_s=\log Z_0-\frac{\ell^2}{2}+\frac{sJ}{4},
\end{equation}
and define, for $t>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{ssm:eq:Fs}
 F_s(t)=at^2+bt+s\ell\sqrt t+c_s.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{ssm:thm:matrix} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{ssm:eq:logerror}
 \log A_n=F_s(n)+o(1)
\end{equation}
on the indicated parity. For sufficiently large $y$, let $r_s(y)$ be the unique sufficiently large solution of $F_s(r_s(y))=y$.

\begin{proposition}[Continuous inverse and its precision]\label{ssm:prop:inverse}
The inverse is well-defined eventually and
\begin{equation}\label{ssm:eq:inversederivative}
 r_s'(y)\sim\frac1{(\log2)r_s(y)}.
\end{equation}
At the actual sequence values,
\begin{equation}\label{ssm:eq:indexerror}
 r_s(\log A_n)=n+o(n^{-1}).
\end{equation}
Writing $u=\sqrt{y/a}$, its elementary model expansion is
\begin{equation}\label{ssm:eq:modelinverse}
 r_s(y)=u-\frac b{2a}-\frac{s\ell}{2a}u^{-1/2}
 +\left(\frac{b^2}{8a^2}-\frac{c_s}{2a}\right)u^{-1}
 +O(u^{-3/2}).
\end{equation}
If $y=\log A_n$, the right side without its displayed remainder equals $n+o(u^{-1})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We have
\[
 F_s'(t)=2at+b+\frac{s\ell}{2\sqrt t}\sim(\log2)t>0
\]
eventually, and $F_s(t)\to\infty$. This proves existence, uniqueness on the increasing tail, and \eqref{ssm:eq:inversederivative}. Equation~\eqref{ssm:eq:logerror} first implies $r_s(\log A_n)\sim n$. The mean value theorem then divides the $o(1)$ logarithmic discrepancy by a derivative asymptotic to $(\log2)n$, proving \eqref{ssm:eq:indexerror}.

For \eqref{ssm:eq:modelinverse}, substitute
$t=u+A+Bu^{-1/2}+Du^{-1}$ in $F_s(t)-y$. Cancellation of the coefficients of $u$, $u^{1/2}$, and $1$ gives
\[
 A=-\frac b{2a},\qquad B=-\frac{s\ell}{2a},\qquad
 D=\frac{b^2}{8a^2}-\frac{c_s}{2a}.
\]
The residual is $O(u^{-1/2})$ and $F_s'(t)\asymp u$, so one further mean value estimate gives the $O(u^{-3/2})$ model remainder. At an actual sequence value, \eqref{ssm:eq:indexerror} contributes only a known $o(u^{-1})$ error, which dominates the finer model remainder. This establishes the last assertion without postulating an unknown count coefficient.
\end{proof}

The parity displacement in this continuous inverse first appears at order $u^{-1/2}$. It is a consequence of the known square-root term in the logarithm; it is not an additional term of a proved all-orders expansion for $A_n$.

\subsection{Integer thresholds need two ceilings}
Define
\[
 \lceil t\rceil_s=2\left\lceil\frac{t-(1-s)}2\right\rceil+(1-s),
\]
the least integer at least $t$ of the parity designated by $s$. Let $N_s(x)$ be the least positive index of that parity with $A_n\ge x$.

\begin{proposition}[Eventual threshold brackets]\label{ssm:prop:threshold}
For every $\eps>0$, once $x$ is sufficiently large,
\begin{equation}\label{ssm:eq:ceilings}
 \left\lceil r_s(\log x-\eps)\right\rceil_s
 \le N_s(x)\le
 \left\lceil r_s(\log x+\eps)\right\rceil_s.
\end{equation}
The least unrestricted positive index with $A_n\ge x$ is $\min(N_0(x),N_1(x))$. The required lower bound on $x$ is not made effective here.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The sequence is nondecreasing: append an all-positive last row and column to a valid matrix. This is an injection, since every existing row sum increases by one and the new row sum is positive. The sequence is unbounded by Theorem~\ref{ssm:thm:matrix}.

For all sufficiently large indices of the indicated parity, \eqref{ssm:eq:logerror} implies $|\log A_n-F_s(n)|\le\eps$. Below the lower ceiling in \eqref{ssm:eq:ceilings}, an integer of that parity is strictly less than $r_s(\log x-\eps)$, so $\log A_n<\log x$. At the upper ceiling, $F_s(n)\ge\log x+\eps$, hence $\log A_n\ge\log x$. Taking $x$ large enough also excludes the finitely many earlier indices. This proves both bounds.
\end{proof}

The two ceilings may coincide, but the theorem does not certify when they do for a prescribed finite input. Such a certificate requires either an explicit remainder bound with a known validity range or exact counts at adjacent candidate indices. More decimal digits in $\ell$ or a smaller numerical residual in $F_s(t)=y$ cannot supply that missing asymptotic error bound.

its symbols carry the subscript ``vol'' here.

(1)~\emph{Strict monotonicity} (proved by the write). $A_0=A_1=1$, and
$A_{n+1}>A_n$ for every $n\ge1$. \emph{Proof.} The proof of
Proposition~\ref{ssm:prop:threshold} maps each counted matrix of order $n$
injectively to a counted matrix of order $n+1$ whose last row and column
are all $+1$. For $n\ge1$, the symmetric matrix of order $n+1$ whose only
entry $-1$ is at position $(n+1,n+1)$ has row sums $n+1$ in its first $n$
rows and $n-1\ge0$ in the last, so it is counted by $A_{n+1}$ but lies
outside the image. At $n=0$ the only matrix of order~$1$ with nonnegative
row sum is $(+1)$, so $A_1=1=A_0$. \qed

\noindent Hence $(A_n)_{n\ge1}$ is strictly increasing, and the least
unrestricted index $\min(N_0(x),N_1(x))$ of
Proposition~\ref{ssm:prop:threshold} is \emph{exactly} the integer staircase
$N_\ast^{\rm vol}(x)$ of \file{p0:def:three-inverses}(1) with $n_1=1$ (an
instance). The parity ceiling $\lceil t\rceil_s$ is exactly the residue-class
ceiling $\rho+r\lceil(\nu-\rho)/r\rceil$ of \file{p0:eq:residue-staircase},
with $r_{\rm vol}=2$, $\rho=1-s$ and $t$ in place of $\nu$.

(2)~\emph{An admissible core, but not a Lambert one.} Put
$X_\ast=\max\{1,|b|/\log2\}$. On $(X_\ast,\infty)$ the function $F_s$ is
$C^2$, $F_s'(t)=t\log2+b+s\ell/(2\sqrt t)>|b|+b\ge0$,
$F_s''(t)=\log2-s\ell/(4t^{3/2})\ge\log2-\ell/4>0$ (as $\ell<1/\sqrt2$), and
$F_s'(t)\to+\infty$, $tF_s'(t)\to+\infty$, $F_s(t)\to+\infty$. So $F_s$ is
an admissible core in the sense of \file{p0:def:core}, with
$\Lambda_\infty^{\rm vol}=+\infty$; for $y>F_s(X_\ast)$ its core solution is
the unique solution of $F_s(t)=y$ in $(X_\ast,\infty)$, and for all large
$y$ it is $r_s(y)$ (an instance of that definition). The phase
is neither of the volume's two cores, $a_{\rm vol}X+b_{\rm vol}\log X$
(\file{p0:thm:lambert-core}) and $X(\kappa_{\rm vol}\log X+d_{\rm vol})$
(\file{p0:prop:factorial-core}): it has no logarithm, its leading balance
is quadratic, and no Lambert function occurs.

(3)~\emph{An instance after a change of variables.} The four-term model
expansion~\eqref{ssm:eq:modelinverse} is the start of the unique formal
solution in \file{p0:thm:core-reversion}. Put $t_{\rm vol}=u^{-1/2}$ and
$r_s(y)=u(1+E)$, so that $y=au^2$. Dividing $F_s(u(1+E))-au^2=0$ by $au^2$
gives exactly
\[
 2E+E^2+f_{\rm vol}(t_{\rm vol},E)=0,\qquad
 f_{\rm vol}(t,w)=\frac bat^2(1+w)+\frac{s\ell}at^3(1+w)^{1/2}+\frac{c_s}at^4,
\]
that is \file{p0:eq:core-master} with $\Lambda_{\rm vol}=2$,
$h_{\rm vol}(w)=w^2$ and
$R_{\rm vol}=\mathbb Q[a^{-1},b,s\ell,c_s]$ (the binomial series of
$(1+w)^{1/2}$ has rational coefficients, and $f_{\rm vol}\in
t\,R_{\rm vol}[[t,w]]$). Its unique solution
$E=\sum_{n\ge1}e_n^{\rm vol}t_{\rm vol}^n$ has
$e_1^{\rm vol}=0$, $e_2^{\rm vol}=-b/(2a)$, $e_3^{\rm vol}=-s\ell/(2a)$,
$e_4^{\rm vol}=b^2/(8a^2)-c_s/(2a)$, the coefficients $A$, $B$, $D$ of the
proof of Proposition~\ref{ssm:prop:inverse}, so
$u(1+e_2^{\rm vol}u^{-1}+e_3^{\rm vol}u^{-3/2}+e_4^{\rm vol}u^{-2})$ is the
right side of~\eqref{ssm:eq:modelinverse} without its remainder (checked by
the write in SymPy from the triangular recurrence
\file{p0:eq:core-triangular}, the displayed equation re-derived
from~\eqref{ssm:eq:Fs}). The volume's theorem is formal: the analytic
remainder $O(u^{-3/2})$, and the statement at $y=\log A_n$, are the
source's mean-value arguments.

(4)~\emph{Analogues only.} Proposition~\ref{ssm:prop:threshold} is not an
instance of \file{p0:thm:staircase}: $r_s$ is not the inverse of an
interpolation of $(A_n)$ (Proposition~\ref{ssm:prop:inverse} gives only
$r_s(\log A_n)=n+o(n^{-1})$, not the identity $\nu_{\rm vol}(A_n)=n$ of an
interpolated inverse), and the brackets come from the two
envelopes $F_s\pm\eps$ at the integers of one parity. Its conclusion has the
shape of the separation condition \file{p0:thm:staircase}(2) (when the two
ceilings of~\eqref{ssm:eq:ceilings} coincide, $N_s(x)$ is their common
value) and of the residue-class formula \file{p0:thm:staircase}(4) with
$r_{\rm vol}=2$. Likewise~\eqref{ssm:eq:indexerror} gives
$n=\lfloor r_s(\log A_n)+\tfrac12\rfloor$ for all sufficiently large $n$ of
parity~$s$, the analogue of \file{p0:thm:staircase}(3), with an
ineffective threshold. No novelty is claimed for the inversions.
\end{remark}
