## Main imported analytic and structural inputs

1. B. D. McKay and N. C. Wormald, Asymptotic enumeration by degree sequence of graphs of high degree, European J. Combin.11(1990),565–580. DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/S0195-6698(13)80042-X; actual author PDF https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/papers/highdeg.pdf. Theorems2 and3(ii) provide the uniform dense pointwise enumeration formula. The article checks its density, concentration and graphicality conditions explicitly.
2. B. D. McKay, I. M. Wanless and N. C. Wormald, Asymptotic enumeration of graphs with a given upper bound on the maximum degree, CPC11(2002),373–392. DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/S0963548302005229; actual author PDF https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/papers/deghalfn_final.pdf. Inspected Theorems1–3, Corollary2, Lemmas2/4 and equations14/17/18/29. The base q, all-capped constant Z0, J, and square-root cap shift are prior results. Its switching method motivates the fully derived unequal-cap switching. Lemma2 and equation29 supply the quantitative finite-saddle mass estimate; weak convergence alone is not used to infer that rate. Theorem3's polynomial maximum-to-mean condition is not assumed for the matrix soft-boundary weight.
3. C. Greenhill and B. D. McKay, Counting loopy graphs with given degrees, Linear Algebra Appl.436(2012),901–926. DOI printed in journal PDF https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2011.03.052; author preprint https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.0080v3. Journal p.902 (preprint p.3) explicitly gives the diagonal-to-one-extra-vertex correspondence. Theorem1.4 is a dense exact-row-sum precursor with arbitrary diagonal, not a capped-row-sum relative equivalent. The bridge is credited as prior work, with an independent proof for the present conventions. Avoid the unrelated DOI currently shown in the arXiv abstract metadata.

This is a bounded comparison of actual contents, not a claim of exhaustive historical absence or priority. The report proves a leading relative 1+o(1) statement and an f=O(sqrt(M)) extension; it does not claim all-order count coefficients, a quantitative full-count error, or an effective onset.
