\begin{abstract}
Let $a_n$ count ascent sequences avoiding the classical pattern 120 (OEIS A202061), let $\mu=7.2958969432\ldots$ be the largest root of $\mu^3-8\mu^2+5\mu+1$, and put $D_n=n\log\mu-\log a_n$, $L=\log n$ and $F=n^{1/3}L^{2/3}$. This report prints five manuscripts, each refining the one before, as five Parts. Part~\ref{a61:part:fd} proves an exact positive height-walk representation, $D_n=\Theta(F)$, the order of the inverse correction, and that the generating function is not D-finite. Part~\ref{a61:part:sh} proves $D_n/F\to C_\star=(3\pi^2\alpha^2/(2v))^{1/3}=2.2326253076\ldots$. Part~\ref{a61:part:o2} adds the term $(7C_\star/3)F\log L/L$ with an $O(F/L)$ remainder and an explicit finite-height constant. Part~\ref{a61:part:o3} identifies the next constant, $C_\star\kappa F/L$ with $\kappa=-1.9401481831\ldots$, proving a conjecture stated in Part~\ref{a61:part:o2}. Part~\ref{a61:part:ao} proves, for every fixed $J$, $D_n/(C_\star F)=\sum_{j\le J}P_j(\kappa+\tfrac73\log L)L^{-j}+o(L^{-J})$ with universal polynomials $P_j$, and the matching inverse expansion. No multiplicative equivalent, amplitude, power-law prefactor, exponentially complete transseries or effective threshold is claimed. The report is AI-assisted and unrefereed, and none of it is formalized.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{The family: three ascent-sequence reports}
\phantomsection\label{a61:guide:family}
A202061 is one of the three classes of ascent sequences avoiding a length-3 pattern that Duncan and Steingr\'imsson introduced and Conway, Conway, Elvey Price and Guttmann studied together \cite{Conway}. Batch 77 opened a report for each, in the same collection directory: \repopath{a202058-ascent-000-growth} (000-avoiding, OEIS A202058; factorial growth), \repopath{a202062-ascent-201-enumeration} (201-avoiding, OEIS A202062; algebraic generating function, $a_n\sim C\mu^nn^{-9/2}$), and this report (120-avoiding; exponential growth with a stretched logarithmic deficit). A202061 and A202062 share the growth constant $\mu$, the largest root of $\mu^3-8\mu^2+5\mu+1$. Their regimes and methods are otherwise disjoint, which is why the three are separate reports. The A202062 report gives a second route to $\mu$ for A202061; see the note after Corollary~\ref{a61:fd:cor:consequences}.

\item Part~\ref{a61:part:o2}'s conjecture \eqref{a61:o2:eq:conjecture} was stated as conditional; it is now proved in Part~\ref{a61:part:o3}.

\paragraph{Abstract of the source.}
\begin{quote}\small
For the number $a_n$ of classical 120-avoiding ascent sequences, we derive an exact positive height-walk representation and prove
\[
 n\log\mu-\log a_n=\Theta\!\left(n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}\right),
 \qquad \mu=7.295896943239772\ldots.
\]
This rules out the previously estimated pure $n^{3/8}$ stretched correction. The proof uses a positive binomial block formula, a critical jump tail, a finite-height radius bound, and a legal all-length staircase construction. We also obtain the order of the inverse correction and prove that the generating function is not D-finite. No exact stretched constant, asymptotic equivalent, or all-orders transseries is claimed. Exact-arithmetic certificates and independent finite diagnostics accompany the argument.
\end{quote}

\section{Results and scope}
An ascent sequence is a word $w_1\cdots w_n$ of nonnegative integers with $w_1=0$ and
\[
 w_i\le1+\asc(w_1\cdots w_{i-1})\quad(i\ge2),\qquad
 \asc(w)=\#\{i:w_i<w_{i+1}\}.
\]
It avoids the classical pattern 120 if there are no $i<j<k$ with $w_k<w_i<w_j$. Let $a_n$ count these words, including $a_0=1$, and let $A(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nx^n$. This is OEIS A202061 \cite{OEIS}.

\begin{theorem}\label{a61:fd:thm:main}
Let $\mu$ be the largest root of
\[
 \mu^3-8\mu^2+5\mu+1=0,
 \qquad \mu=7.29589694323977237457\ldots,
\]
and put $\Phi(n)=n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}$. There exist $c,C>0$ and $n_0$ such that, for every integer $n\ge n_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:main}
 \mu^n e^{-C\Phi(n)}\le a_n\le C\mu^n e^{-c\Phi(n)}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\[
 n\log\mu-\log a_n=\Theta(\Phi(n)),\qquad
 \frac{\log(n\log\mu-\log a_n)}{\log n}\longrightarrow\frac13.
\]
The radius of convergence is $\rho=1/\mu$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{a61:fd:cor:consequences}
The following consequences hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item No equivalent $C_0\mu^n\exp(-d n^\gamma)n^g$, with $C_0,d>0$ and fixed $g\in\mathbb R$, is possible for $\gamma>1/3$ or for $\gamma=1/3$.
\item For $N(Y)=\min\{n\ge0:a_n\ge Y\}$ and $Y\to\infty$,
\[
 N(Y)=\frac{\log Y}{\log\mu}
 +\Theta\!\left((\log Y)^{1/3}(\log\log Y)^{2/3}\right).
\]
\item $A(x)$ is not D-finite over $\C(x)$; equivalently, $(a_n)$ is not P-recursive over $\C[n]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\section{An exact normalized recurrence}\label{a61:fd:sec:recurrence}
For an avoiding prefix, let $v$ be the largest lower endpoint of an already occurring increasing pair, or $0$ if there is none. A future letter smaller than $v$ would complete a 120 pattern, and every letter at least $v$ obeying the ascent bound is permitted. Delete the seen values smaller than $v$ and translate the surviving alphabet by $-v$. The normalized state is $(a,l,S)$, where $a$ is the number of previous ascents minus $v$, $l$ is the translated last letter, and $S$ is the set of seen translated surviving values. Write
\[
 S=\{0=s_0<s_1<\cdots<s_k\},\quad m=s_k,\quad
 h=a+1-m,\quad d_i=s_i-s_{i-1}.
\]
Every ascent sequence satisfies $\max w\le\asc(w)$, so $h\ge1$.

Appending $i\in\{0,\ldots,a+1\}$ sets
\[
 p=\max\bigl(\{s\in S:s<i\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)
\]
and gives the exact child
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:state}
 (a',l',S')=
 \left(a+\one_{l<i}-p,\ i-p,\ \{s-p:s\in S,\ s\ge p\}\cup\{i-p\}\right).
\end{equation}
Indeed $p$ is the greatest newly forbidden lower threshold, and the insertion of $i-p$ records the new letter. Thus this recurrence follows directly from the class definition. In every normalized state, the last letter is either $0$ or the least positive member of $S$: if $i>0$, there was no old seen value strictly between $p$ and $i$.

Let $E_h(R)$ be the suffix generating function when the last letter is $0$ and the list of positive gaps is $R$. Let $G_h(d,R)$ be the suffix generating function when the gap list is $(d,R)$ and the last letter is $d$. All empty suffixes have weight $1$. Define
\[
 D_h(d,R)=\sum_{q=1}^dG_h(q,(d-q,R)),\quad e_h=E_h(\varnothing),\quad
 N_h=\sum_{q=1}^hG_{h+1-q}(q,\varnothing),
\]
where a zero gap is omitted. Write $(Tf)_h=f_{h+1}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{a61:fd:lem:operator}
Put $c=x/(1-x)$ and $\mathcal B=x+x^2T/(1-x)$. There are formal series $R_d(T),P_d(T),Q_d(T)$ with nonnegative integer coefficients in $x,T$ such that
\begin{align}
 D_h(d,R)&=R_d(T)E_h(R),\notag\\
 E_h((d,R))&=P_d(T)E_h(R),\qquad
 G_h(d,R)=Q_d(T)E_h(R),\label{a61:fd:eq:op}
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
 P_d&=1+cTR_d,\qquad Q_d=1+\mathcal B R_d,\notag\\
 (1-\mathcal B)R_d&=1+\sum_{q=1}^{d-1}Q_qP_{d-q}.
 \label{a61:fd:eq:oprec}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Classify the next letter as zero, in an old gap, or a new maximum. For $R=(d_1,\ldots,d_k)$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:E}
 (1-x)E_h(R)=1+xN_h+x\sum_{j=1}^kD_{h+1}(d_j,(d_{j+1},\ldots,d_k)).
\end{equation}
From last letter $d$, selecting in the first gap gives no ascent; selecting in a later gap does. Subtraction from \eqref{a61:fd:eq:E} therefore gives
\[
 G_h(d,R)=E_h((d,R))+x(1-T)D_h(d,R).
\]
Subtracting \eqref{a61:fd:eq:E} for $R$ from its version for $(d,R)$ also gives
\[
 E_h((d,R))=E_h(R)+cTD_h(d,R).
\]
Together these imply $G_h(d,R)=E_h(R)+\mathcal B D_h(d,R)$. Induct on $d$, splitting the definition of $D$ into $q=d$ and $q<d$. For $q<d$, both shorter-gap identities apply to the arbitrary tail. All resulting operators commute, being series in the same shift $T$. The $q=d$ term gives the factor $1-\mathcal B$, whose inverse exists $x$-adically and has nonnegative coefficients. This proves the identities, existence, uniqueness and positivity.
\end{proof}

Use an ordinary variable $t$ for the shift and set $Q(x;z,t)=\sum_{q\ge1}Q_q(x,t)z^q$, with similar notation for $P,R$. With $b=1-x$ and $\mathcal B=x+x^2t/b$, summing \eqref{a61:fd:eq:oprec} yields
\[
 R=Q(1+P),\qquad Q=\frac{z}{1-z}+\mathcal B R,
 \qquad P=\frac{z}{1-z}+\frac{xt}{b}R.
\]
Elimination gives the exact jump equation
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:Qquad}
 xt(1-z)Q^2+[b(z-b)+xt(x-z)]Q+bz=0,
\end{equation}
with $Q(x;0,t)=0$ and $Q(0;z,t)=z/(1-z)$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:Q1}
 Q_1(x,t)=\frac1{1-x-x^2t/(1-x)}.
\end{equation}

\section{Positive height walks and block weights}\label{a61:fd:sec:walk}
The empty-gap case of \eqref{a61:fd:eq:E} is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:walk}
 e_h=\frac1{1-x}+\frac{x}{1-x}\sum_{q=1}^h(Q_q(x,T)e)_{h+1-q},
 \qquad A(x)=1+xe_1(x).
\end{equation}
Interpret a term indexed by $q,r$ as a macro-step that first drops by $q-1$, then rises by $r$, has weight
\[
 \frac{x}{1-x}[t^r]Q_q(x,t),
\]
and changes height from $h$ to $h+1-q+r$. Its legality condition is exactly $1\le q\le h$. The terminal weight is $1/(1-x)$ at every height. This is a positive formal representation of the original sequence; no additional word-level bijection is required for coefficient selection. Every nonterminal step has positive $x$-degree, and only finitely many shifts occur at any fixed degree. Hence the formal Neumann series is well defined and uniquely solves \eqref{a61:fd:eq:walk}.

Put $\beta=xt/b$ and $\eta=x^2t/b^2$. Equation~\eqref{a61:fd:eq:Qquad} is equivalently
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:W}
 Q=\frac{z+xW}{1-z},\qquad
 W=\frac{z}{b}\frac{(1+\beta)(1+W)}{1-\eta(1+W)},\qquad [z^0]W=0.
\end{equation}
Lagrange inversion gives, for $j\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:lagrange}
 [z^j]W=\frac{(1+\beta)^j}{b^j}
 \sum_{k\ge0}\mathcal C(j,k)\eta^k,
 \quad \mathcal C(j,k)=\frac1j\binom{j+k-1}{k}\binom{j+k}{k+1}>0.
\end{equation}
For $\ell,r\ge0$ and $q\ge1$, write $B(\ell,q,r)=[x^\ell t^r]Q_q(x,t)$. Expanding \eqref{a61:fd:eq:lagrange} yields
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:blockexact}
 B(\ell,q,r)=\one_{\ell=0,r=0}
 +\sum_{j=1}^q\ \sum_{k=0}^r
 \mathcal C(j,k)\binom{j}{r-k}
 \binom{j+\ell-2}{\ell-r-k-1}.
\end{equation}
The summation includes only $0\le r-k\le j$ and $\ell\ge r+k+1$. In particular, keeping $j=q$ and one admissible $k$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:blocklower}
 B(\ell,q,r)\ge\mathcal C(q,k)\binom{q}{r-k}
 \binom{q+\ell-2}{\ell-r-k-1}.
\end{equation}

\section{An exact critical point}
Let $\alpha$ be the unique positive root of $7\alpha^3+14\alpha^2-7\alpha-1=0$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:constants}
 \kappa=\frac{-21\alpha^2+17\alpha+5}{29},\qquad
 \mu=\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha-\kappa},\qquad
 z_\st=\frac{\kappa(1-\alpha-\kappa)}{(\alpha-\kappa)(2\alpha+\kappa)}.
\end{equation}
The following bounds and identities are certified by rational interval arithmetic and polynomial reduction in the supplied program:
\begin{align*}
 \alpha&=0.5100033746178888\ldots,& \kappa&=0.2830305201361994\ldots,\\
 z_\st&=0.1980622641951617\ldots,& z_\st^3-5z_\st^2+6z_\st-1&=0,\\
 0&<\kappa<\alpha,& \alpha+\kappa&<1.
\end{align*}
The value of $\mu$ satisfies the cubic and interval in Theorem~\ref{a61:fd:thm:main}, which identify its largest real root. Descartes' rule gives uniqueness of the positive $\alpha$ root, isolated in
\[
 \frac{51000337}{10^8}<\alpha<\frac{51000339}{10^8}.
\]

For $L(v)=v\log v$, let
\begin{align*}
 S(a,d,u)={}&2L(a+u)-L(a)-2L(u)-L(d-u)-L(a-d+u)\\
 &+L(1+a)-L(1-d-u)-L(a+d+u).
\end{align*}
This is the factorial entropy of \eqref{a61:fd:eq:blocklower} divided by $\ell$, for $a=q/\ell$, $d=r/\ell$ and $u=k/\ell$. At $(\alpha,\alpha,\kappa)$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:entropy}
 S_u=0,\qquad S_a+S_d=0,\qquad S_d=\log z_\st,\qquad S=\log\mu.
\end{equation}
For clarity, the two stationary identities reduce to
\begin{align*}
 (\alpha+\kappa)^2(\alpha-\kappa)(1-\alpha-\kappa)
 &=\kappa^3(2\alpha+\kappa),\\
 (\alpha+\kappa)^2(1+\alpha)(1-\alpha-\kappa)
 &=\alpha(\alpha-\kappa)(2\alpha+\kappa)^2.
\end{align*}
The displayed expression for $z_\st$ gives $S_d$. Euler's identity applied to the homogeneous factorial entropy then gives $S=\log((1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha-\kappa))$. These are finite algebraic verifications; global maximality of $S$ is not needed.

Set $\rho=1/\mu$, $b_\st=1-\rho$, $t_\st=1/z_\st$, and
\begin{align}\label{a61:fd:eq:critical}
 \beta_\st&=\frac{\rho}{b_\st z_\st},&
 \eta_\st&=\frac{\rho^2}{b_\st^2z_\st},&
 Q_\st&=\frac{b_\st z_\st}{b_\st z_\st+\rho},\\
 W_\st&=\frac{(1-z_\st)Q_\st-z_\st}{\rho},&
 m_\st&=\frac{\rho}{b_\st}t_\st Q_\st
 =\frac{\rho}{b_\st z_\st+\rho}.\notag
\end{align}
The exact certificate proves the fixed-point identity \eqref{a61:fd:eq:W} at these values, and
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:margins}
 W_\st=1+\beta_\st>0,\quad
 \eta_\st(2+\beta_\st)^2=1,\quad
 0<\eta_\st(1+W_\st)<\tfrac12,\quad
 \tfrac25<m_\st<\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
Numerically, $W_\st=1.8019377358\ldots$ and $m_\st=0.4450418679\ldots$.

\begin{lemma}\label{a61:fd:lem:criticalbound}
The jump series converges at $(\rho,z_\st,t_\st)$, with $Q(\rho;z_\st,t_\st)\le Q_\st$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:ehcritical}
 e_h(\rho)\le\frac{t_\st^h}{b_\st(1-m_\st)},\qquad
 A(\rho)\le1+\frac{\rho t_\st}{b_\st(1-m_\st)}<\infty.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand the denominator on the right of \eqref{a61:fd:eq:W} geometrically. Iteration starting from $0$ is nonnegative and bounded by the fixed-point supersolution $W_\st$, by \eqref{a61:fd:eq:margins}. Monotone convergence proves the assertion about $Q$. For the height weight $t_\st^h$, the sum of an unrestricted transition row divided by its starting weight is at most
\[
 \frac{\rho}{b_\st}\sum_{q\ge1,r\ge0}
 [t^r]Q_q(\rho,t)\,t_\st^{1-q+r}
 =\frac{\rho}{b_\st}t_\st Q(\rho;z_\st,t_\st)\le m_\st<1.
\]
Each legal row is a subsum. The terminal weight $1/b_\st$ is at most $t_\st^h/b_\st$, so the nonnegative Neumann series is bounded by a geometric series. This proves \eqref{a61:fd:eq:ehcritical}. No spectral-radius identification from a discriminant is being assumed.
\end{proof}

\section{The all length lower bound}
\begin{lemma}\label{a61:fd:lem:uniform}
For each fixed $D>0$ there exist $c_D,C_D>0$ and $\ell_D$ such that the conditions
\[
 \ell\ge\ell_D,\quad |q-\alpha\ell|\le2,\quad
 |k-\kappa\ell|\le1,\quad
 |r-q|\le D\sqrt{\ell\log\ell}
\]
imply admissibility in \eqref{a61:fd:eq:blocklower} and
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:uniform}
 B(\ell,q,r)\ge c_D\ell^{-C_D}\mu^\ell z_\st^{r-q}.
\end{equation}
If $|r-q|\le D\sqrt\ell$, one can use the more precise factor $c_D\ell^{-3}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All normalized factorial arguments lie in one compact subset of the positive interior, for all sufficiently large $\ell$. The exact identities
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal C(q,k)&=\frac{q}{(q+k)(k+1)}\binom{q+k}{k}^{\!2},\\
 \binom{q+\ell-2}{\ell-r-k-1}
 &=\frac{(\ell-r-k)(q+r+k)}{(q+\ell)(q+\ell-1)}
 \binom{q+\ell}{\ell-r-k}
\end{align*}
and uniform Stirling bounds give a lower bound
$c\ell^{-3}\exp(\ell S(q/\ell,r/\ell,k/\ell))$. Taylor's theorem and \eqref{a61:fd:eq:entropy} yield
\[
 \ell S=\ell\log\mu+(r-q)\log z_\st+O_D(\log\ell).
\]
Indeed the first differential is exactly $(r-q)\log z_\st$, and the quadratic remainder is $O((r-q)^2/\ell+1)$. This proves \eqref{a61:fd:eq:uniform}; with square-root perturbations the remainder is bounded.
\end{proof}

Choose a sufficiently large fixed integer $J$. For $j\ge J$, define
\[
 h_j=\lfloor j^2\log j\rfloor,\quad q_j=\lfloor h_j/2\rfloor,
 \quad \ell_j=\lfloor q_j/\alpha\rfloor,\quad
 k_j=\text{a nearest integer to }\kappa\ell_j.
\]
An ascent step from $h_j$ to $h_{j+1}$ uses
\[
 r_j^+=q_j-1+(h_{j+1}-h_j),
\]
and a descent step from $h_j$ to $h_{j-1}$ uses
\[
 r_j^-=q_j-1+(h_{j-1}-h_j).
\]
Both preserve the exact increment $1-q+r$. As $\ell_j=\Theta(j^2\log j)$ and $h_{j+1}-h_j=O(j\log j)$, every such step satisfies Lemma~\ref{a61:fd:lem:uniform} with the same $D$. Its down parameter obeys $q_j\le h_j$, so it is legal. Retain just the leading $x$ in the macro factor $x/(1-x)$; then its length is $\ell_j+1$ and multiplicity at least $B(\ell_j,q_j,r_j^{\pm})$.

Seed the walk from height $1$ to $h_J$ using $q=1,r=h_J-1$. Equation~\eqref{a61:fd:eq:Q1} gives a coefficient $1$ at its leading degree $\ell=2(h_J-1)$, so the macro-step has length $2h_J-1$. The final return from $h_J$ to $1$ uses $q=h_J,r=0,\ell=0$, of length $1$ and multiplicity $1$.

Ascend from $h_J$ to $h_K$ and descend to $h_J$, then add the seeds and the original leading letter. The total length is
\begin{align}\label{a61:fd:eq:LK}
 L_K&=2h_J+1+\sum_{j=J}^{K-1}(\ell_j+1)
                +\sum_{j=J+1}^{K}(\ell_j+1)\notag\\
 &=\frac{K^3\log K}{3\alpha}+O(K^3),
\end{align}
and
\[
 L_{K+1}-L_K=\ell_K+\ell_{K+1}+2=\Theta(h_K).
\]
The total increment of the large blocks is zero. If there are $M$ such blocks, then $\sum(r-q)=-M$, so their height-tilt factors multiply to $z_\st^{-M}$. There is no uncancelled exponentially expensive endpoint factor.

To cover every sufficiently large integer $n$, choose maximal $K$ with $L_K\le n$ and let $R=n-L_K$. A bounded residual is absorbed by the terminating geometric series. Otherwise split $R=m_1+m_2$ into two nearly equal integers, both above a fixed threshold, and insert two self-loops at height $h_K$. For length $m$ choose
\[
 \ell=m-1,\qquad q=\text{a nearest integer to }\alpha\ell,
 \qquad r=q-1,\qquad k=\text{a nearest integer to }\kappa\ell.
\]
The loop has exactly zero increment and meets Lemma~\ref{a61:fd:lem:uniform}. Furthermore,
\[
 q\le\frac{\alpha R}{2}+O(1)
 \le\frac{h_K+h_{K+1}}4+O(1)<h_K
\]
for all sufficiently large $K$, proving legality.

There are $O(K)$ large blocks and at most two repair loops, each with $\ell=O(K^2\log K)$. Multiply \eqref{a61:fd:eq:uniform}. The extra factor $\mu^{-1}$ for each macro-step's leading $x$, and the fixed seed lengths, cost at most $\exp(O(K))$. Polynomial factors cost at most $\exp(O(K\log K))$, while the tilt telescopes as above. Thus
\[
 \log a_n\ge n\log\mu-O(K\log K).
\]
By \eqref{a61:fd:eq:LK} and $R=O(h_K)$, one has
$K=\Theta((n/\log n)^{1/3})$, hence $K\log K=\Theta(\Phi(n))$. This proves the lower half of \eqref{a61:fd:eq:main} for all large integers, not merely a subsequence.

\section{A finite height radius gain}
\subsection{A fixed gap derivative bound}
For fixed $t=t_\st$ and variable $x$, put $b=1-x$, $\beta=xt_\st/b$ and $\eta=x^2t_\st/b^2$. The coefficient formula \eqref{a61:fd:eq:lagrange} implies
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:fixedgap}
 Q_q(x,t_\st)=1+x\sum_{j=1}^q W_j(x),\qquad
 W_j(x)=\left(\frac{1+\beta}{b}\right)^j
 \frac{N_{j-1}(\eta)}{(1-\eta)^{2j-1}},
\end{equation}
where $N_0=1$ and, for $j\ge2$,
\[
 N_{j-1}(v)=\frac1{j-1}\sum_{l=0}^{j-2}
 \binom{j-1}{l}\binom{j-1}{l+1}v^l.
\]
Here is a formal proof, with no analytic continuation needed. If $F_j(v)=\sum_{k\ge0}\mathcal C(j,k)v^k$, its coefficient ratio gives
\[
 v(1-v)F_j''+[2-(2j+2)v]F_j'-j(j+1)F_j=0.
\]
Substitute $F_j=(1-v)^{1-2j}N$. The equation becomes
\[
 v(1-v)N''+[2-(4-2j)v]N'-(2-j)(1-j)N=0.
\]
With $N(0)=1$, its unique formal coefficients satisfy
\[
 (l+1)(l+2)n_{l+1}=(l+2-j)(l+1-j)n_l.
\]
They are exactly the displayed coefficients of $N_{j-1}$, and terminate after $j-2$. For $j=1$, $\mathcal C(1,k)=1$ directly gives $F_1=(1-v)^{-1}$.

Since $\eta(\rho)=0.12737466\ldots<1$, choose a fixed interval $[\rho,\rho+\epsilon_0]$ on which $b>0$ and $0<\eta<1$. The positive polynomial $N_{j-1}$ has degree at most $j$, so
\[
 0\le\frac{\eta N_{j-1}'(\eta)}{N_{j-1}(\eta)}\le j.
\]
Logarithmic differentiation of \eqref{a61:fd:eq:fixedgap} gives a constant $C_0$, independent of $j$, such that $0\le(\log W_j)'\le C_0j$ throughout this interval. Increasing a constant $C_1$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:derivative}
 Q_q(x,t_\st)\le Q_q(\rho,t_\st)
       \exp(C_1(q+1)(x-\rho))
\end{equation}
for all $q\ge1$ and $x$ in the interval.

\subsection{The critical jump tail}
At $x=\rho,t=t_\st$, let $b=b_\st$ and $s=b^2-\rho^2t_\st>0$. The discriminant of \eqref{a61:fd:eq:Qquad} as a quadratic in $Q$ factors, as a polynomial in $z$, as
\[
 \Delta(z)=s^2(1-z/z_\st)(1-z/z_2),\qquad
 z_2=\frac{s^2}{(b+\rho t_\st)^2z_\st}.
\]
The exact certificate verifies $0<z_\st<z_2<1$, with $z_2=0.8817230195\ldots$. The branch with $Q(0)=0$ is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:sqrt}
 Q(\rho;z,t_\st)=
 \frac{s-(b-\rho t_\st)z-s\sqrt{1-z/z_\st}\sqrt{1-z/z_2}}
      {2\rho t_\st(1-z)}.
\end{equation}
The rational nonsingular part is analytic in $|z|<1$, and the multiplier of $\sqrt{1-z/z_\st}$ is analytic in $|z|<z_2$. Consequently, for some fixed $C_2$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:qtail}
 0\le Q_q(\rho,t_\st)z_\st^q\le C_2q^{-3/2}\qquad(q\ge1).
\end{equation}
For an elementary coefficient justification, rescale $z=z_\st w$. The coefficients of $\sqrt{1-w}$ have absolute value at most $C(m+1)^{-3/2}$. The analytic multiplier has coefficients $O(R^{-m})$ for some $R>1$. In the convolution, indices at most $q/2$ yield $O(q^{-3/2})$ by geometric summation, and the other indices yield an exponentially small bound. The rational part is exponentially smaller as well. This proves \eqref{a61:fd:eq:qtail} without a transfer theorem.

\subsection{The cutoff estimate}
Let $a_n^{[\le H]}$ denote the coefficients in \eqref{a61:fd:eq:walk} restricted to macro-boundary heights in $\{1,\ldots,H\}$. This is a coefficientwise positive subclass of the formal walk expansion. Set
\[
 \delta_H=\epsilon\frac{\log(H+1)}{H+1},\qquad x_H=\rho+\delta_H,
\]
with a sufficiently small fixed $\epsilon>0$. Write $A_q=Q_q(\rho,t_\st)z_\st^q$. By \eqref{a61:fd:eq:derivative}, the weighted row sum of the cutoff at $x_H$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:rowcut}
 \frac{x_H}{1-x_H}t_\st e^{C_1\delta_H}
       \sum_{q\le H}A_qe^{C_1q\delta_H}.
\end{equation}
Its unperturbed infinite mass is at most $m_\st<1$. Moreover, \eqref{a61:fd:eq:qtail} implies
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:tailperturb}
 \sum_{q\le H}A_q(e^{C_1q\delta_H}-1)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Indeed split at $q=1/\delta_H$. The lower part is $O(\sqrt{\delta_H})$, using $e^{C_1q\delta_H}-1=O(q\delta_H)$ there. The upper part is bounded by
\[
 C e^{C_1H\delta_H}\sqrt{\delta_H}
 =O\!\left(H^{C_1\epsilon-1/2}\sqrt{\log H}\right)=o(1)
\]
if $C_1\epsilon<1/2$. The prefactor in \eqref{a61:fd:eq:rowcut} tends to its critical value. Thus the row mass is uniformly at most a fixed $\bar m<1$ for large $H$. The same weighted geometric bound as in Lemma~\ref{a61:fd:lem:criticalbound}, now in the finite cutoff, gives a uniform upper bound on its generating function at $x_H$. Positive coefficient extraction yields
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:cutbound}
 a_n^{[\le H]}\le Cx_H^{-n}
 \le C\mu^n\exp\!\left(-c\frac{n\log(H+1)}{H+1}\right).
\end{equation}
All constants are independent of $H,n$ once $H$ is sufficiently large.

\section{The first hit bound and the upper estimate}
Keep $W=W_\st$ fixed and write the right side of \eqref{a61:fd:eq:W} as
\[
 F(x,z,t;W)=\frac{z}{1-x}
 \frac{(1+xt/(1-x))(1+W)}{1-x^2t(1+W)/(1-x)^2}.
\]
Let $f(x,\theta)=F(x,z_\st e^{-\theta},t_\st e^\theta;W_\st)$. By \eqref{a61:fd:eq:margins}, $f(\rho,0)=W_\st$ and $f_\theta(\rho,0)=0$. Specifically, with $u=\eta_\st(1+W_\st)$,
\[
 \frac{f_\theta}{f}(\rho,0)
 =-\frac1{1+\beta_\st}+\frac{u}{1-u}=0.
\]
Also $f_x(\rho,0)>0$, since the positive factors increase with $x$ and the denominator remains positive. Choose a fixed sufficiently large $D>0$ and put
\[
 x_\theta=\rho e^{-D\theta^2},\qquad
 t_\theta=t_\st e^\theta,\qquad z_\theta=1/t_\theta.
\]
Taylor's theorem gives
\[
 f(x_\theta,\theta)=W_\st+
 \left(\tfrac12 f_{\theta\theta}(\rho,0)-\rho D f_x(\rho,0)\right)\theta^2
 +O(\theta^3).
\]
Make the bracket negative. For $0\le\theta\le\theta_0$ with sufficiently small $\theta_0>0$, the positive fixed-point map then has the supersolution $W_\st$ and positive denominator. Iteration from zero gives
\[
 Q(x_\theta;z_\theta,t_\theta)
 \le\frac{z_\theta+x_\theta W_\st}{1-z_\theta}.
\]
Its unrestricted weighted row mass satisfies
\[
 m_\theta=\frac{x_\theta}{1-x_\theta}t_\theta
 Q(x_\theta;z_\theta,t_\theta)
 \le \frac{x_\theta}{1-x_\theta}t_\theta
       \frac{z_\theta+x_\theta W_\st}{1-z_\theta}.
\]
The explicit supersolution bound on the right is continuous at zero, where it equals $m_\st<1$. Reducing $\theta_0$ makes all such masses at most one fixed $\bar m<1$. The sum of the weights of all finite macro-prefixes starting at height $1$, including the factor $t_\theta^h$ at their final height but no terminating weight, is therefore at most
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:prefix}
 t_\theta\sum_{k\ge0}\bar m^k\le C.
\end{equation}

Let $p_{m,h}$ count the first-hit macro-prefixes of length $m$ whose first height above $H$ is $h>H$. The remaining unrestricted suffix has generating function $e_h$. By \eqref{a61:fd:eq:ehcritical}, for the complementary coefficients at fixed word length $n$,
\[
 a_n^{[>H]}\rho^n
 \le C\rho\sum_{m\le n-1,\,h>H}p_{m,h}\rho^m t_\st^h.
\]
Here $\rho$ accounts for the original leading letter, and the use of $e_h(\rho)$ only enlarges the permitted remaining lengths. For $0<\theta\le\theta_0$,
\[
 \rho^m\le x_\theta^m e^{D\theta^2n},\qquad
 t_\st^h\le t_\theta^h e^{-\theta H}.
\]
First-hit prefixes are a subset of all prefixes, so \eqref{a61:fd:eq:prefix} implies
\[
 a_n^{[>H]}\le C\mu^n\exp(D\theta^2n-\theta H).
\]
When $H/(2Dn)\le\theta_0$, use $\theta=H/(2Dn)$ to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{a61:fd:eq:hit}
 a_n^{[>H]}\le C\mu^n\exp\!\left(-\frac{H^2}{4Dn}\right).
\end{equation}
Continuation at the critical tilt in \eqref{a61:fd:eq:ehcritical} is what cancels the final height weight. No constraint on the final height of the entire walk is imposed.

Take $H=\lfloor n^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}\rfloor$. It is $o(n)$, so \eqref{a61:fd:eq:hit} applies for large $n$. Both exponents in \eqref{a61:fd:eq:cutbound} and \eqref{a61:fd:eq:hit} are at least a positive constant times $\Phi(n)$. Adding the two positive subclasses proves the upper half of \eqref{a61:fd:eq:main}. Together with the lower bound, this completes Theorem~\ref{a61:fd:thm:main}.

\section{Inverse order and non D finiteness}\label{a61:fd:sec:nondfinite}
The main theorem gives $c\Phi(n)-O(1)\le n\log\mu-\log a_n\le C\Phi(n)$. If a pure stretched equivalent in Corollary~\ref{a61:fd:cor:consequences}(1) held, its deficit would be $dn^\gamma-g\log n-\log C_0+o(1)$. This grows faster than $\Phi(n)$ for $\gamma>1/3$ and more slowly for $\gamma=1/3$, contradicting the two-sided bounds.

The sequence is nondecreasing: append a copy of the last letter to an avoiding ascent sequence. The ascent condition persists. A new 120 occurrence using the appended letter would already occur using its previous copy. This extension is injective. Hence $N(Y)$ is well defined for large $Y$.

Let $L=\log Y$ and $\lambda=\log\mu$. Applying the upper bound at $n=N(Y)$ first gives $N(Y)\ge L/\lambda-O(1)$ and then
\[
 \lambda N(Y)-L\ge c\Phi(N(Y))-O(1)\ge c'\Phi(L).
\]
Conversely set $n=\lceil L/\lambda+K\Phi(L)\rceil$. Since $\Phi(n)/\Phi(L)\to\lambda^{-1/3}$, any sufficiently large fixed $K$ makes $\lambda n-C\Phi(n)\ge L$. The coefficient lower bound implies $a_n\ge Y$, hence $N(Y)\le n$. This proves the stated inverse order. It is a positive correction of a known order, with no asserted leading coefficient or exact rounding rule.

For non-D-finiteness, the stretched upper bound gives
\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}n^k a_n\rho^n<\infty\qquad(k=0,1,2,\ldots).
\]
Thus $A$ and all its derivatives extend continuously along the real radius to $\rho$. Suppose $A$ were D-finite over $\Q(x)$. Its coefficients are exponentially bounded nonnegative integers, their algebraic conjugates have the same bound, and all common denominators are $1$. It would therefore be a Siegel G-function. The G-function regularity theorem gives a convergent finite local sum of terms
\[
 (x-\rho)^\alpha(\log(x-\rho))^\beta H(x-\rho),
\]
with rational $\alpha$, nonnegative integer $\beta$ and analytic $H$, on a slit neighborhood of $\rho$; see \cite[Definition 2.2 and Theorem 2.1(c)]{GB}.

Such a convergent finite power-log expansion can be smooth to every order along a radius only if it is analytic at the endpoint. To see this, combine terms with the same rational exponents modulo integers and the same logarithmic powers. The possible exponents form a discrete set bounded below. A least surviving negative integer power is unbounded; a least noninteger power fails to have a bounded derivative at some finite order; and a least nonzero logarithmic term fails similarly. The absence of all these terms leaves a convergent ordinary power series. Consequently $A$ would be analytic at $\rho$, contrary to Pringsheim's theorem for a nonnegative series of finite radius. Thus $A$ is not D-finite over $\Q(x)$.

Finally, a complex differential relation of bounded order and polynomial degree for a rational series produces a homogeneous linear system with rational entries and finitely many unknowns. Its rank is attained by finitely many rows. If it has a nonzero complex solution, it has a nonzero rational solution. Hence complex D-finiteness would imply rational D-finiteness here, proving Corollary~\ref{a61:fd:cor:consequences}(3). This argument uses G-function arithmetic regularity; it does not assert that all D-finite functions have regular singularities.

\section{Results and the shared foundation}
An ascent sequence is a nonnegative integer word with $w_1=0$ and
$w_i\le1+\#\{j<i-1:w_j<w_{j+1}\}$ for $i\ge2$. It avoids classical 120 if no $i<j<k$ satisfy $w_k<w_i<w_j$. The counting sequence, including $a_0=1$, is OEIS A202061 \cite{OEIS}.

Let $z$ be the smallest positive root of $z^3-5z^2+6z-1=0$, and set
\begin{align}\label{a61:sh:eq:constants}
 \rho&=3z^2-10z+2, &\mu&=\rho^{-1}, &t&=z^{-1},\notag\\
 \alpha&=\frac{8z^2-29z+9}{7},&
 \kappa&=\frac{17z^2-59z+13}{7},&
 v&=\frac{2z^2-7z+2}{2}.
\end{align}
Here $\mu$ is the largest root of $\mu^3-8\mu^2+5\mu+1=0$ and
\[
 \alpha=0.510003374617888819\ldots,\qquad
 v=0.346010735815047935\ldots.
\]
All logarithms are natural.

\begin{theorem}\label{a61:sh:thm:main}
With $\Phi(n)=n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:main}
 \frac{n\log\mu-\log a_n}{\Phi(n)}\longrightarrow
 C_\st=\left(\frac{3\pi^2\alpha^2}{2v}\right)^{1/3}
 =2.232625307612844922\ldots.
\end{equation}
If $a_n^{[\le H]}$ denotes the positive height-walk expansion restricted to boundary heights at most $H$, and $\rho_H$ is its generating-function radius, then
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:radius}
 \log(\rho_H/\rho)\sim\frac\alpha2\frac{\log H}{H}.
\end{equation}
Finally, put $\lambda=\log\mu$ and $N(Y)=\min\{n:a_n\ge Y\}$. Then
\begin{align}\label{a61:sh:eq:inverse}
 N(Y)={}&\frac{\log Y}{\lambda}
 +C_\st\lambda^{-4/3}(\log Y)^{1/3}(\log\log Y)^{2/3}\notag\\
 &+o\!\left((\log Y)^{1/3}(\log\log Y)^{2/3}\right),
\end{align}
where $C_\st\lambda^{-4/3}=0.893568257650894909\ldots$.
\end{theorem}

\section{The exact positive walk and the required kernel estimates}\label{a61:sh:sec:inputs}
Part~\ref{a61:part:fd} establishes formal suffix series $e_h(x)$, $h\ge1$, satisfying the positive walk equation \eqref{a61:fd:eq:walk}, with $(Tf)_h=f_{h+1}$ and $A(x)=1+xe_1(x)$. The positive series $Q(x;u,T)=\sum_{q\ge1}Q_q(x,T)u^q$ is the root with $Q(x;0,T)=0$ of the jump equation \eqref{a61:fd:eq:Qquad}. This Part writes the jump variable as $u$ (Part~\ref{a61:part:fd}: $z$) and the shift variable as $T$ (Part~\ref{a61:part:fd}: $t$), because $z$ and $t$ denote the critical constants here.
A macro-step with parameters $q,r$ changes height by $d=1-q+r$, has weight $x[t_0^r]Q_q(x,t_0)/(1-x)$, and is legal precisely when $q\le h$ at its start. Its endpoint is automatically positive. All weights are nonnegative.

The exact coefficient formula for $B(\ell,q,r):=[x^\ell T^r]Q_q(x,T)$ is \eqref{a61:fd:eq:blockexact}, with $\mathcal C(j,k)$ as in \eqref{a61:fd:eq:lagrange}; only admissible binomial terms are included. Let $k(L,q,d)$ be the coefficient of total macro-degree $L\ge1$ for a step with increment $d$, and define the critically tilted weight
\[
 w(L,q,d)=k(L,q,d)\rho^L t^d.
\]
The unrestricted row mass is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:mass}
 m=\sum_{L,q,d}w(L,q,d)=\frac{\rho}{(1-\rho)z+\rho}
 =0.4450418679\ldots<1.
\end{equation}
Part~\ref{a61:part:fd} also gives a constant $D>0$ such that $a_n^{[>H]}\rho^n\le C\exp[-H^2/(4Dn)]$ for $H=o(n)$: this is the first-hit bound \eqref{a61:fd:eq:hit}, multiplied by $\rho^n$.
The terminal factor $t^{1-h}$ at final height $h$ must be retained in all critically tilted coefficient sums.

\subsection{Uniform transfer at fixed down parameter}
Let $u_c(x,T)$ be the smaller positive discriminant root in $u$ in \eqref{a61:fd:eq:Qquad}. Near $(x,T)=(\rho,t)$ the two discriminant roots are distinct, positive and below $1$. The explicit square-root branch therefore gives uniformly
\[
 Q_q(x,T)=c(x,T)u_c(x,T)^{-q}q^{-3/2}(1+O(q^{-1})),
 \qquad c(x,T)>0,
\]
where $c$ is analytic. In particular, the same expression without $1+O(q^{-1})$ is an upper bound after increasing a constant, for all $q\ge1$.

Define
\[
 \Psi(s,\theta)=-\log\bigl(u_c(\rho e^s,te^\theta)te^\theta\bigr).
\]
Implicit differentiation of the quadratic discriminant gives the exact identities
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:psi}
 \Psi(0,0)=\Psi_\theta(0,0)=0,\qquad
 \Psi_s(0,0)=\alpha^{-1},\qquad
 \Psi_{\theta\theta}(0,0)=v/\alpha.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:psiexp}
 \Psi(s,\theta)=\frac{s+v\theta^2/2}{\alpha}
 +O(s^2+|s\theta|+|\theta|^3).
\end{equation}
The fixed-$q$ height variance is $\nu=v/\alpha=(1+2z-z^2)/2>0$. Analytic transfer also proves its Gaussian limit; only a positive mass on fixed intervals is used in Section~\ref{a61:sh:sec:radius}. All identities in \eqref{a61:sh:eq:psi} are checked exactly by the supplied symbolic certificate.

\subsection{A local positive lower bound}
For $L(v)=v\log v$, the factorial entropy of the $j=q$ summand in \eqref{a61:fd:eq:blockexact} is
\begin{align*}
 S(a,b,u)={}&2L(a+u)-L(a)-2L(u)-L(b-u)-L(a-b+u)\\
 &+L(1+a)-L(1-b-u)-L(a+b+u).
\end{align*}
At $(a,b,u)=(\alpha,\alpha,\kappa)$,
\[
 S=\log\mu,\qquad S_u=0,\qquad S_a+S_b=0,\qquad S_b=\log z.
\]
The Hessian $\mathcal H$ of $S(a,a+s,u)$ there is negative definite. Its inverse covariance $(-\mathcal H)^{-1}$ has $s,s$ entry $v$. The upper-left $2\times2$ block, in coordinates $(a,s)$, is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:Sigma}
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}
 -(11z^2-37z+4)/7&2(5z^2-17z+2)/7\\
 2(5z^2-17z+2)/7&(2z^2-7z+2)/2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its conditional $q$-center has shift $\beta(d-1)$, where $\beta=\Sigma_{12}/v$.

\begin{lemma}\label{a61:sh:lem:local}
Fix $C>0$ and $K>3$. For all sufficiently large $n$, uniformly for $\ell\ge(\log n)^K$ and $|d|\le C\sqrt{\ell\log n}$, summing \eqref{a61:fd:eq:blockexact} over a fixed-width $O(\sqrt\ell)$ window centered at
$q=\alpha\ell+\beta(d-1)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:local}
 \rho^\ell t^d\sum_q B(\ell,q,q+d-1)
 \ge c\ell^{-2}\exp[-d^2/(2v\ell)-o(1)].
\end{equation}
All retained $q$ satisfy $|q-\alpha\ell|\le C'\sqrt{\ell\log n}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Retain only the positive $j=q$ terms, and restrict $(q,k)$ to fixed-width $O(\sqrt\ell)$ windows about their joint Gaussian conditional centers at fixed $d-1$. Uniform Stirling gives each summand a factor at least $c\ell^{-3}$. Taylor expansion of the tilted entropy gives
\[
 -\frac{(d-1)^2}{2v\ell}-O(1)
 -O\left(\frac{(\log n)^{3/2}}{\sqrt\ell}\right).
\]
All factorial arguments remain in a fixed positive interior set. The last remainder is $o(1)$, and the number of retained integer pairs is at least $c\ell$. Replacing $d-1$ by $d$ changes the exponential by $1+o(1)$ and the tilt by a fixed factor. This proves \eqref{a61:sh:eq:local}. It requires no global coefficient-tail estimate or full local asymptotic equivalent.
\end{proof}

\section{A sharp upper coefficient bound}
Set
\[
 H=n^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3},\qquad F=H^2/n=\Phi(n),\qquad
 a=F/n,\qquad b=F/H,
\]
so $b^2=a$ and $aH=\log n$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform row calibration]\label{a61:sh:lem:row}
Fix $R,\epsilon>0$, $0<B<\alpha/3$, and a continuously differentiable function $f$ on $[0,1]\times[0,R]$ with uniformly continuous derivatives, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:HJ}
 -f_s(s,y)+\frac v2 f_y(s,y)^2\le\frac{B}{y+\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
For elapsed degree $m$, current height $h$, and legal next step $(L,q,d)$, restrict to $m+L\le n$ and $1\le h+d\le RH$. Then the row sum of
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:transform}
 w(L,q,d)\exp\!\left(F\left[f\!\left(\frac mn,\frac hH\right)
 -f\!\left(\frac{m+L}{n},\frac{h+d}{H}\right)\right]\right)
\end{equation}
is at most a fixed $\bar m<1$ for all sufficiently large $n$, uniformly in $m,h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $L/n,|d|/H\le\eta$, uniform differentiability gives the upper linear majorant
\[
 a[-f_s+\omega]L-bf_y d+b\omega|d|,
\]
where $\omega$ is arbitrarily small when the fixed $\eta$ is small. For either sign of $d$ this is $\lambda L+\theta d$ with
\[
 \lambda=a[-f_s+\omega],\qquad \theta=b[-f_y\pm\omega].
\]
Choose $\omega$ so that, for some $B<B'<\alpha/3$,
\[
 \lambda+v\theta^2/2\le aB'/(y+\epsilon).
\]
The bounded positive macro prefactor and \eqref{a61:sh:eq:psiexp} show uniformly for $q\le h\le RH$ that
\[
 q\Psi(\lambda,\theta)
 \le\frac{B'}\alpha\frac{q\log n}{h+\epsilon H}+o(1),
\]
since $H(a^2+ab+b^3)=O(n^{-1/3}(\log n)^{4/3})=o(1)$.

For the tail $q>Q$, split at $H/(\log n)^2$. Below this threshold the exponential factor is $1+o(1)$, giving $O(Q^{-1/2})$. Above it the total is at most
\[
 C\left(\frac{H}{(\log n)^2}\right)^{-1/2}n^{B'/\alpha}=o(1).
\]
For $q\le Q$, fixed-$q$ generating functions provide exponential domination on a fixed neighborhood of $(\rho,t)$. First restricting $L,d$ to a finite set and then controlling its complement shows a uniform limsup at most the unrestricted critical row. Equality is not asserted near $m=n$, where the degree cutoff can remove terms. Splitting the two signs of $d$ is needed only in the vanishing $q>Q$ tail. Thus these local steps have row limsup at most $m<1$.

For the remaining steps, global Lipschitz continuity bounds the exponent in \eqref{a61:sh:eq:transform} by $CaL+Cb|d|$. If $L>\eta n$, an additional small fixed length tilt $s_0>0$ bounds its sum by $\exp[-s_0\eta n+O(H)]=o(1)$; fixed-$q$ transfer at $\rho e^{s_0}$ has at most exponential growth in $q\le RH$. If $|d|>\eta H$, use an additional signed height tilt $\theta_0$. Equation~\eqref{a61:sh:eq:psiexp} at vanishing length tilt gives $\Psi\le C_1\theta_0^2+o(1)$. Choosing $\theta_0$ small enough that $C_1R\theta_0^2<\eta\theta_0/2$ gives an $\exp[-cH+o(H)]$ bound. Two signs cover both height tails. These bounds are uniform in $m,h$ and complete the proof.
\end{proof}

Suppose also $f(1,y)\le Py$ for a finite $P$. Summing all finite transformed prefixes of degree at most $n-1$ gives at most $(1-\bar m)^{-1}$. The exponential transformations telescope. The exact terminal coefficient then gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:calbound}
 a_n^{[\le RH]}\rho^n\le C\exp[-Ff(0,1/H)].
\end{equation}
Indeed a prefix of degree $m$ ending at $h$ leaves the factor
\[
 \rho^{n-1-m}t^{1-h}\exp[Ff(m/n,h/H)],
\]
which is uniformly bounded: $f(s,y)\le Py+C(1-s)$, whereas $F/H,F/n\to0$. The original leading letter contributes only the fixed factor $\rho$. This accounts for every possible unused terminal geometric degree and for arbitrary final height.

Combine \eqref{a61:sh:eq:calbound} with \eqref{a61:fd:eq:hit}, choosing $R$ so large that $R^2/(4D)>f(0,0)$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:dualbound}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n\log\mu-\log a_n}{\Phi(n)}\ge f(0,0).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Explicit calibrations attaining the constant}
Take a smooth bounded increasing function $p$ on $[0,1]$ with $p'>0$. Set
\[
 f(s,y)=g(s)+p(s)y,\qquad g(1)=0,\qquad
 g'=\frac v2p^2-2\sqrt{Bp'}+\epsilon p'.
\]
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 -f_s+\frac v2f_y^2
 =2\sqrt{Bp'}-p'(y+\epsilon)\le\frac B{y+\epsilon}.
\]
Thus \eqref{a61:sh:eq:dualbound} applies, and
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:dual}
 f(0,0)=\int_0^1\left(2\sqrt{Bp'}-\frac v2p^2\right)ds
 -\epsilon[p(1)-p(0)].
\end{equation}
To evaluate attainable values, put $M_0=(2vB/\pi^2)^{1/3}$ and parametrize
\[
 s=\frac{u-\sin u\cos u}{\pi},\qquad y=M_0\sin^2u,
 \qquad 0<u<\pi.
\]
Then $y'=\pi M_0\cot u$, $y''=-vB/y^2$, and $p_\st=-y'/v$ is increasing with $p_\st'=B/y^2$. Both $p_\st^2$ and $\sqrt{p_\st'}$ are integrable at the endpoints. Restrict $p_\st$ to $[\delta,1-\delta]$ and linearly reparametrize to $[0,1]$. The resulting smooth bounded $p$ makes the integral in \eqref{a61:sh:eq:dual} converge, as $\delta\to0$, to
\[
 \int_0^1\left(\frac{2B}{y}-\frac{y'^2}{2v}\right)ds
 =\frac{3B}{M_0}=3\left(\frac{\pi^2B^2}{2v}\right)^{1/3}.
\]
Here $y'^2/(2v)=B(1/y-1/M_0)$ and $\int_0^1 B/y\,ds=2B/M_0$. First let $\epsilon\to0$ for each fixed truncation, then $\delta\to0$, and finally $B\uparrow\alpha/3$. This proves
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:liminf}
 \liminf\frac{n\log\mu-\log a_n}{\Phi(n)}\ge C_\st.
\end{equation}

\section{A sharp lower coefficient bound at every integer length}
Write $L=\log n$ and choose
\begin{align*}
 M&=\operatorname{round}\!\left[\left(\frac{12\pi^2\alpha^2n}{vL}\right)^{1/3}\right],&
 J&=\lceil L^3\rceil,&\eta&=L^{-1/4},\\
 s_j&=\sin^2(\pi j/M),&
 S&=\sum_{j=J}^{M-J-1}s_j,&
 H_0&=\frac{\alpha n}{(1-\eta)S},\qquad h_j=\lfloor H_0s_j\rfloor.
\end{align*}
There are $m=M-2J$ large blocks. Put $h_0=h_J=h_{M-J}$ and $n'=n-m-2h_0-1$. Elementary sine estimates give
\[
 S\sim M/2,\quad H_0\sim2\alpha n/M,\quad
 h_0=\Theta(L^7),\quad \min_jh_j=\Theta(L^7),\quad H_0/M^2=\Theta(L).
\]
Choose positive integers $\ell_j=(n'/S)s_j+O(1)$ whose sum is exactly $n'$ by rounding down and distributing the fewer than $m$ remaining units. Let $g_j=h_{j+1}-h_j$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:centers}
 \sum_jg_j=0,\qquad
 \ell_j=\frac{(1-\eta)h_j}{\alpha}[1-o(\eta)]+O(1),\qquad
 |g_j|\le C\sqrt{\ell_jL},\quad\ell_j\ge cL^7.
\end{equation}
For each block independently allow
\[
 \ell=\ell_j+u_j,\quad |u_j|\le w_j=\lfloor\ell_j/L^2\rfloor,
 \qquad d=g_j+v_j,\quad |v_j|\le b_j=\lfloor\sqrt{\ell_j}\rfloor,
\]
and retain choices satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:exactconstraints}
 \sum_ju_j=0,\qquad\sum_jv_j=0.
\end{equation}
For every pair $(\ell,d)$ use the shifted $q$-window from Lemma~\ref{a61:sh:lem:local}.

\subsection{Legality under both global constraints}
The actual starting height of block $j$ is $h_j+E_j$, where $E_j$ is the partial sum of the $v_i$. By using both this prefix and its complementary suffix in \eqref{a61:sh:eq:exactconstraints},
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:errorheight}
 |E_j|\le\min\left(\sum_{i<j}b_i,\sum_{i\ge j}b_i\right)
 \le C h_j/\sqrt L.
\end{equation}
On the ascending half, $b_i\le C\sqrt{n/M}\sin(\pi i/M)$, and
\[
 \sum_{i=J}^{j-1}\sin(\pi i/M)
 \le CM[1-\cos(\pi j/M)]\le CM s_j.
\]
Divide by $h_j\asymp(n/M)s_j$ and use $M^{3/2}/\sqrt n=\Theta(L^{-1/2})$. The descending half follows from the complementary suffix and symmetry. Rounding errors are smaller because $h_j\ge cL^7$.

All selected $q$ satisfy
\[
 q\le\alpha\ell_j(1+O(L^{-2}))+C\sqrt{\ell_jL}.
\]
The margin $\eta h_j$ dominates \eqref{a61:sh:eq:errorheight}, the square-root term and all rounding errors: $\eta=L^{-1/4}$, while $|E_j|/h_j=O(L^{-1/2})$ and $\sqrt{L/h_j}=O(L^{-3})$. Thus $q\le h_j+E_j$, and the entire selected walk is legal and positive.

\subsection{Exact constraints and exact degree}
The coefficients of the generating polynomial of an integer interval $[-w,w]$ are symmetric and unimodal. Convolution preserves those properties, so the number of length choices with zero total displacement is at least
\[
 \frac{\prod_j(2w_j+1)}{2\sum_jw_j+1}.
\]
The analogous bound holds separately for the increment choices. Hence the number of pairs satisfying \eqref{a61:sh:eq:exactconstraints} is at least
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:numberchoices}
 \frac{\prod_j(2w_j+1)(2b_j+1)}{(2n+1)^2}.
\end{equation}
For clarity, convolution preserves symmetric unimodality because each such sequence is a nonnegative linear combination of centered interval indicators, whose pairwise convolutions are symmetric unimodal.

Seed from height $1$ to $h_0$ using $q=1,r=h_0-1$, of degree $2h_0-1$ and multiplicity at least one. After the large blocks return to $h_0$, return to height $1$ using $q=h_0,r=0$, of degree one and multiplicity one. These seeds follow from $Q_1(x,T)=1/(1-x-x^2T/(1-x))$ and $B(0,q,0)=1$. Including the original leading letter and the leading $x$ of every macro-step, the total degree is exactly
\[
 2h_0+1+m+\sum_j\ell_j=n.
\]
The large-block height increments sum to zero. Thus all height tilts cancel, the fixed seed costs $O(h_0)=O(L^7)=o(\Phi(n))$, and fixed per-block factors cost $O(M)=o(\Phi(n))$. This holds at every sufficiently large integer $n$.

\subsection{The kinetic and polynomial costs}
Uniformly throughout the rectangles,
\[
 \frac{d^2}{\ell}\le\frac{g_j^2}{\ell_j}+O(\sqrt L)+O(1),
 \qquad \log\ell=\log\ell_j+o(1).
\]
Combine Lemma~\ref{a61:sh:lem:local}, \eqref{a61:sh:eq:numberchoices}, $w_j\sim\ell_j/L^2$ and $b_j\sim\sqrt{\ell_j}$ to obtain
\begin{align}\label{a61:sh:eq:lowercost}
 \log(a_n\rho^n)\ge{}&-\sum_j\frac{g_j^2}{2v\ell_j}
 -\frac12\sum_j\log\ell_j\notag\\
 &-O(M\sqrt L+M\log L+\log n+L^7).
\end{align}
The final line is $o(\Phi(n))$.

The sine profile gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:kinetic}
 \sum_j\frac{g_j^2}{\ell_j}
 =(1+o(1))\frac{4\pi^2\alpha^2n}{M^2}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, before rounding, it is $(H_0^2S/n')\sum_j(s_{j+1}-s_j)^2/s_j$. Taylor expansion with an absolute remainder, followed by summation, gives
\[
 \sum_j\frac{(s_{j+1}-s_j)^2}{s_j}
 =(1+o(1))\frac{4\pi^2}{M^2}\sum_j\cos^2(\pi j/M)
 =(1+o(1))\frac{2\pi^2}{M}.
\]
No pointwise relative expansion at the vanishing central derivative is used. The endpoint removal has $J/M\to0$ and $J\to\infty$, and roundings are negligible since $\ell_j\ge cL^7$.

Also
\begin{equation}\label{a61:sh:eq:entropycost}
 \frac12\sum_j\log\ell_j=(1+o(1))\frac M3L.
\end{equation}
Here $\log(n'/S)=\frac23L+O(\log L)$ and $\sum_j\log s_j=O(M)+o(M)$. The latter follows from
\[\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}\sin(\pi j/M)=M/2^{M-1}.\]
The omitted endpoint terms are $O(JL)=o(M)$.

Equations~\eqref{a61:sh:eq:lowercost}--\eqref{a61:sh:eq:entropycost} give the leading cost
\[
 \frac{2\pi^2\alpha^2n}{vM^2}+\frac M3L.
\]
Our choice of $M$ makes the first term asymptotic to $ML/6$, so the total is asymptotic to $ML/2=C_\st\Phi(n)$. Therefore
\[
 \limsup\frac{n\log\mu-\log a_n}{\Phi(n)}\le C_\st.
\]
Together with \eqref{a61:sh:eq:liminf}, this proves \eqref{a61:sh:eq:main}.

\section{The finite height radius}\label{a61:sh:sec:radius}
Let $K_H(x)$ be the finite matrix of exact macro-transition series on heights $1,\ldots,H$. For fixed $q,r$,
\[
 [T^r]Q_q(x,T)=\one_{r=0}
 +\sum_{j=1}^q\sum_{k=\max(0,r-j)}^r
 \mathcal C(j,k)\binom{j}{r-k}
 \frac{x^{r+k+1}}{(1-x)^{j+r+k}}.
\]
Hence every entry is rational and analytic for $0<x<1$. It is positive: $q=h,r=h'-1$ connects any $h$ to any $h'$. The strictly increasing Perron eigenvalue starts at zero and diverges at one, so it crosses one at a unique $\rho_H$. The initial vector and the positive terminal vector have positive Perron projections, so the resulting pole is noncancelled. This is the restricted radius. Diagonal similarity by $t^h$ does not change it.

Fix $0<\delta<1$ and put $s_-=(1-\delta)\alpha\log H/(2H)$. Uniform transfer gives
\[
 Q_q(\rho e^{s_-},t)z^q
 \le Cq^{-3/2}\exp(qs_-/\alpha+O(qs_-^2)).
\]
The $q\le H$ row mass differs from its critical value by $o(1)$, splitting at $H/(\log H)^2$ as before. The upper tail is bounded by
$C H^{-\delta/2}\log H=o(1)$. Thus all rows are below one and $\rho_H>\rho e^{s_-}$.

For the reverse bound set $s_+=(1+\delta)\alpha\log H/(2H)$ and choose
$\max(1/2,1-\delta/2)<\gamma<1$. Put $w=\lfloor H^\gamma\rfloor$ and restrict to heights $[H-w,H]$. In every row retain $q\in[H-2w,H-w]$, all legal. Uniform transfer gives
\[
 Q_q(\rho e^{s_+},t)z^q
 \ge cH^{-3/2+(1+\delta)/2}.
\]
For these $q$, the tilted actual increment $d=1+r-q$ has mean $O(\log H)$ and variance asymptotic to $\nu q$, where $\nu=v/\alpha>0$. The transfer formula and its derivatives give, for fixed real $u$,
\[\mathbb E\exp(ud/\sqrt q)\longrightarrow\exp(\nu u^2/2)\]
uniformly in this range: the mean term is $O(\log H/\sqrt H)=o(1)$ and the cubic remainder vanishes. Hence weak Gaussian convergence places a fixed positive mass in either inward increment interval $[0,\sqrt H]$ or $[-\sqrt H,0]$, according to the half of the strip containing the start height. Since $w/\sqrt H\to\infty$, these increments remain in the strip. Each restricted row sum is therefore at least
\[
 cH^{\gamma-1+\delta/2}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The Perron row bound implies $\rho_H<\rho e^{s_+}$. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ proves \eqref{a61:sh:eq:radius}. Only weak Gaussian convergence is needed here, not a lattice local limit theorem.
\begin{writenote}
Part~\ref{a61:part:o2}, equation~\eqref{a61:o2:rad:theorem}, refines \eqref{a61:sh:eq:radius} to
\[
 \log(\rho_H/\rho)=\frac\alpha H\Bigl\{\frac12\log H+\log\log H+c_*+o(1)\Bigr\}
\]
with an explicit constant $c_*$.
\end{writenote}


\section{Inverse asymptotics and remaining questions}
Appending the final letter gives an injection from avoiding words of length $n$ to those of length $n+1$, so $a_n$ is nondecreasing. Set $L=\log Y$ and insert
\[
 n=\frac L\lambda+
 \left(C_\st\lambda^{-4/3}\pm\epsilon\right)L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3}
\]
into \eqref{a61:sh:eq:main}. Since
$\Phi(n)\sim\lambda^{-1/3}L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3}$, the two choices fall below and above the threshold for sufficiently large $Y$. Integer rounding costs $O(1)$. Letting $\epsilon\downarrow0$ proves \eqref{a61:sh:eq:inverse}.

The theorem is an exact first-order equivalent for the logarithmic deficit. It is not a multiplicative equivalent for $a_n$: the $o(\Phi(n))$ remainder may diverge. No amplitude, power-law prefactor, second-order coefficient, transseries, exact integer inverse rule, or finite-data crossover threshold is established. The leading proof discards integrated block-length factors, endpoint layers, the defective critical mass and fluctuations about the minimizing profile at lower order. Recovering these is separate work.

\section{Statement and dependency boundary}

$w_i\le1+\#\{j<i-1:w_j<w_{j+1}\}$ for $i\ge2$. It avoids classical 120 if no $i<j<k$ satisfy $w_k<w_i<w_j$. Its counting sequence, with $a_0=1$, is OEIS A202061 \cite{OEIS}.
Let $z$ be the smallest positive root of $z^3-5z^2+6z-1=0$, and set
\begin{align*}
 \rho&=3z^2-10z+2,& \mu&=\rho^{-1},&t&=z^{-1},\\
 \alpha&=\frac{8z^2-29z+9}{7},&&

\end{align*}

$\alpha=0.510003374617888819\ldots$ and
$v=0.346010735815047935\ldots$. Set $z_*=z$ and $t_*=t$. All logarithms are natural, and $L=\log n$. Our principal scales and constants are

 H=n^{2/3}L^{1/3},\qquad F=\frac{H^2}{n}=n^{1/3}L^{2/3},

\section{Coefficient lower bound by balanced rectangles}
\label{a61:o2:low:section}

We prove the one-sided refinement
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:target}
 D_n\le C_*F+\frac{7C_*}{3}\frac{F\log L}{L}
          +O(F/L),\qquad L=\log n.
\end{equation}
Its new ingredient is a symmetric set of balanced increment bridges.
It allows a height margin of order $1/L$, while Jensen's inequality
removes the otherwise excessive linear variation of the kinetic cost.

\subsection{The local positive kernel estimate}

Let $B(\ell,q,r)$ denote the exact positive block multiplicity in the
frozen height-walk representation, before the leading macro multiplier
$x/(1-x)$. We use only its audited local lower bound: for every fixed
$C>0$, uniformly when $\ell\ge cL^7$ and
$|d|\le C\sqrt{\ell L}$, summing over a fixed-width conditional Gaussian
window in $q$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:kernel}
 \sum_{q\ \mathrm{in\ window}}
 \rho^\ell t_*^d B(\ell,q,q+d-1)
 \ge c_0\ell^{-2}
       \exp\left\{-\frac{d^2}{2v\ell}-\epsilon_n\right\},
 \qquad \epsilon_n\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Every $q$ in this window satisfies
$|q-\alpha\ell|\le C_0\sqrt{\ell L}$.
The estimate follows by retaining the $j=q$ summand in the exact
binomial formula and summing $q$ and its inner saddle variable in
fixed $O(\sqrt\ell)$ windows around their conditional centers.
Uniform Stirling and the audited Hessian identity give the exponent
$d^2/(2v\ell)$, while the cubic remainder is
$O(L^{3/2}/\sqrt\ell)=o(1)$. Thus no global kernel equivalent or
multiplicative amplitude is needed here. We retain only the leading
$x$ in $x/(1-x)$, at a fixed cost per macro-block.

\subsection{A sine-square path with a small legality margin}

Put
\[
 A_0=\frac{2\pi^2\alpha^2}{v},\qquad
 M=\operatorname{round}\left[\left(\frac{6A_0n}{L}\right)^{1/3}\right],
 \quad J=\lceil L^3\rceil,\quad \eta=4/L,\quad m=M-2J.
\]
For $J\le j\le M-J$ define
\[
 s_j=\sin^2(\pi j/M),\quad
 S=\sum_{j=J}^{M-J-1}s_j,\quad
 H_0=\frac{\alpha n}{(1-\eta)S},\quad
 h_j=\lfloor H_0s_j\rfloor.
\]
Then $h_J=h_{M-J}=:h_0$. Set
$n'=n-m-2h_0-1$ and choose positive integers $\ell_j$ such that
\[
 \sum_{j=J}^{M-J-1}\ell_j=n',\qquad
 \ell_j=\frac{n'}S s_j+O(1).
\]
Flooring followed by distributing the rounding residual gives this
choice for every sufficiently large integer $n$. Let
$g_j=h_{j+1}-h_j$, so $\sum g_j=0$.
Uniformly over these indices,
\begin{gather}
 h_j\asymp (n/M)s_j,\qquad \ell_j\asymp(n/M)s_j,
 \qquad \min_j(h_j,\ell_j)\asymp LJ^2\asymp L^7,
 \label{a61:o2:low:scales}\\
 |g_j|\le C\sqrt{\ell_jL},\qquad
 \alpha\ell_j\le(1-4/L)h_j+O(1).
 \label{a61:o2:low:margin}
\end{gather}
The last inequality uses $n'/n<1$; replacing $H_0s_j$ by its floor
changes its right side by only $O(1)$.

For each block allow integer deviations
\[
 |u_j|\le w_j:=\lfloor\ell_j/L\rfloor,
 \qquad |v_j|\le b_j:=\lfloor\sqrt{\ell_j}\rfloor.
\]
Impose the two exact constraints
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:balance}
 \sum_j u_j=0,\qquad \sum_jv_j=0.
\end{equation}
The actual internal block length and increment are
$\ell_j+u_j$ and $g_j+v_j$, respectively.

\subsection{Many balanced increment bridges stay inside the margin}

First take independent random variables $V_j$ uniform on the integer
intervals $[-b_j,b_j]$. A centered interval has symmetric unimodal
coefficients, and convolution preserves this property. Therefore its
central coefficient is at least the average coefficient, giving
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:bridge-count}
 \mathbb P\left\{\sum_jV_j=0\right\}
 \ge\frac1{2\sum_jb_j+1}\ge\frac1{2n+1}.
\end{equation}
For an interior boundary $k$ let
$E_k=\sum_{j=J}^{k-1}V_j$. Define the event
\[
 G=\{|E_k|\le h_k/L\text{ for every }J\le k\le M-J\}.
\]
For $J\le k\le M/2$, elementary sine bounds and $n/M^3\asymp L$ give
\[
 \sum_{j=J}^{k-1}b_j^2\le CLk^3,\qquad
 \max_{J\le j<k}b_j\le C\sqrt L\,k,\qquad
 h_k/L\ge ck^2.
\]
Bernstein's inequality consequently yields
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:bernstein}
 \mathbb P\{|E_k|>h_k/L\}
 \le 2\exp\left\{-\frac{ck^4}{Lk^3+\sqrt L\,k^3}\right\}
 \le 2\exp(-ck/L)\le 2\exp(-cL^2).
\end{equation}
For $k>M/2$, use the complementary suffix, which equals $-E_k$
when the total sum vanishes. The same unconditional estimate applies
with $M-k$ in place of $k$. Dividing by
\eqref{a61:o2:low:bridge-count} and taking a union bound over at most $M$
boundaries proves
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:good-probability}
 \mathbb P\left(G^c\mid\sum_jV_j=0\right)
 \le CnM\exp(-cL^2)\le\exp(-c'L^2).
\end{equation}
At the two endpoints the bridge error is zero. In particular, at least
half of the balanced increment vectors are good for large $n$.
No independence after conditioning is asserted or needed.

Both balance and $G$ are invariant under the simultaneous sign
reversal $V_j\mapsto-V_j$. The uniform distribution on good balanced
increment vectors therefore has
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:increment-moments}
 \mathbb E V_j=0,\qquad \mathbb E V_j^2\le b_j^2\le\ell_j.
\end{equation}
Independently, choose the length vector uniformly among the balanced
vectors in the $u$-rectangles. It too is centrally symmetric, so
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:length-moments}
 \mathbb E U_j=0,\qquad |U_j|\le\ell_j/L.
\end{equation}
Independence here is between the two complete vectors, not between
coordinates of a conditioned vector.

Every rectangle choice so retained is legal. At the beginning of
block $j$ its actual height is at least $h_j-h_j/L$. For every $q$ in
the local window of \eqref{a61:o2:low:kernel},
\begin{align*}
 q&\le\alpha(\ell_j+u_j)+C\sqrt{\ell_jL}\\
  &\le(1-4/L)(1+1/L)h_j+O(1)+C\sqrt{\ell_jL}
   \le h_j-2h_j/L
\end{align*}
for all large $n$. The last step uses $h_j\ge cL^7$, which makes
$\sqrt{\ell_jL}=o(h_j/L)$. Thus $q$ does not exceed the actual height;
all macro-boundary heights stay positive. The perturbed lengths and
increments still satisfy the uniform hypotheses of
\eqref{a61:o2:low:kernel}.

The same central-coefficient argument for the length balance,
together with \eqref{a61:o2:low:good-probability}, gives the following lower
bound for the cardinality of the retained rectangle set $\mathcal R$:
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:rectangles}
 |\mathcal R|\ge
 \frac{\prod_j(2w_j+1)(2b_j+1)}{2(2n+1)^2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Jensen's inequality removes the linear fluctuations}

Let expectation be the product of the two uniform balanced-vector
measures just constructed. Define
\[
 X=\sum_j\left\{
       \frac{(g_j+V_j)^2}{2v(\ell_j+U_j)}
       +2\log(\ell_j+U_j)\right\}.
\]
By independence between the complete vectors and the centered
moments in \eqref{a61:o2:low:increment-moments}--\eqref{a61:o2:low:length-moments},
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:factor-expectation}
 \mathbb E\frac{(g_j+V_j)^2}{\ell_j+U_j}
 =(g_j^2+\mathbb EV_j^2)\,
       \mathbb E\frac1{\ell_j+U_j}.
\end{equation}
The exact identity $(1+x)^{-1}=1-x+x^2/(1+x)$ gives
\[
 \mathbb E\frac1{\ell_j+U_j}
 \le\frac1{\ell_j}(1+C/L^2).
\]
Since the deterministic kinetic sum is $O(ML)$, as also verified below,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:kinetic-expectation}
 \mathbb E\sum_j\frac{(g_j+V_j)^2}{\ell_j+U_j}
 \le\sum_j\frac{g_j^2}{\ell_j}+O(M).
\end{equation}
Concavity gives $\mathbb E\log(\ell_j+U_j)\le\log\ell_j$.
The useful direction of Jensen's inequality is
\[
 \sum_{\mathcal R}e^{-X}
   =|\mathcal R|\,\mathbb Ee^{-X}
   \ge|\mathcal R|\exp(-\mathbb EX).
\]
Thus the apparently large first-order fluctuation of the energy does
not create an $O(M\sqrt L)$ loss.

Combining these facts with \eqref{a61:o2:low:kernel},
\eqref{a61:o2:low:rectangles}, and
\[
 \log(2w_j+1)\ge\log\ell_j-\log L+O(1),\qquad
 \log(2b_j+1)\ge\tfrac12\log\ell_j+O(1),
\]
leaves the logarithmic cost
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:cost}
 D_n\le\sum_j\frac{g_j^2}{2v\ell_j}
       +\frac12\sum_j\log\ell_j+m\log L
       +O(M+L+h_0).
\end{equation}
For clarity, the exact seeds are unchanged from the audited
construction: the ascent from height $1$ to $h_0$ has degree
$2h_0-1$, the final descent has degree $1$, and the original first
letter has degree $1$. Together with the $m$ leading $x$ factors and
$\sum\ell_j=n'$, the total degree is exactly $n$.
The total height tilt cancels because the full walk returns to $1$.
Seed costs are $O(h_0)=O(L^7)=o(M)$; fixed block constants cost $O(M)$.

\subsection{Deterministic sums at the required precision}

We record enough precision to justify the $O(M)$ remainder. First,
\[
 S=M/2+O(J^3/M^2+1),\qquad n'=n-O(M+L^7).
\]
Writing $\delta=\pi/M$, the exact identity
\[
 \frac{s_{j+1}-s_j}{\sqrt{s_j}}
 =\sin\delta\left[
  2\cos(\pi j/M)\cos\delta
  +\frac{\cos(2\pi j/M)}{\sin(\pi j/M)}\sin\delta\right]
\]
and elementary summation give
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:sine-energy}
 \sum_j\frac{(s_{j+1}-s_j)^2}{s_j}
 =\frac{2\pi^2}{M}
  +O\left(\frac{J+\log M}{M^2}\right).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the main square is $2\pi^2/M+O(J/M^2)$, the cross term is
$O(\log M/M^2)$ by
$\sum1/\sin(\pi j/M)=O(M\log M)$, and the final square is
$O(1/(M^2J))$ by
$\sum1/\sin^2(\pi j/M)=O(M^2/J)$.

Before integer rounding the kinetic sum is $H_0^2S/n'$ times the left
side of \eqref{a61:o2:low:sine-energy}. The factor
$(1-4/L)^{-2}=1+O(1/L)$ contributes $O(M)$, while the trigonometric
error contributes $O((J+L)L)=O(L^4)=o(M)$. Height rounding changes the
sum by at most
$C\sum(|g_j|/\ell_j+1/\ell_j)=O(L)$; length rounding changes it by at
most $C\sum g_j^2/\ell_j^2=O(ML/L^7)=o(M)$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:kinetic-sum}
 \sum_j\frac{g_j^2}{\ell_j}
   =\frac{4\pi^2\alpha^2n}{M^2}+O(M).
\end{equation}
The same estimates, before seeking their leading constant, give the
$O(ML)$ bound used in \eqref{a61:o2:low:kinetic-expectation}.

The sine product
$\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}\sin(\pi j/M)=M/2^{M-1}$ yields
\[
 \sum_{j=J}^{M-J-1}\log s_j
 =-2M\log2+O(J\log M+\log M).
\]
Length rounding changes the logarithmic sum by
$O(\sum1/\ell_j)=o(1)$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:entropy-sum}
 \frac12\sum_j\log\ell_j
   =\frac M2\log(n/M)+O(M).
\end{equation}
Also $(M-m)\log L=O(J\log L)=o(M)$. Substituting
\eqref{a61:o2:low:kinetic-sum}--\eqref{a61:o2:low:entropy-sum} into
\eqref{a61:o2:low:cost} proves
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:low:intermediate}
 D_n\le\frac{A_0n}{M^2}
          +\frac M2\log(n/M)+M\log L+O(M).
\end{equation}

Finally, the rounded choice of $M$ satisfies
\[
 \frac{A_0n}{M^2}=\frac{ML}{6}+O(L),\qquad
 \frac{ML}{2}=C_*F+O(L),\qquad
 \log(n/M)=\frac23L+\frac13\log L-\frac13\log(6A_0)+o(1).
\]
Since $L=o(M)$, \eqref{a61:o2:low:intermediate} is
\[
 D_n\le\frac{ML}{2}+\frac76M\log L+O(M),
\]
which is exactly \eqref{a61:o2:low:target}.
The coefficient $7/6$ per macro-block consists of $1/6$ from the
optimal length scale inside $\tfrac12\log(n/M)$ and $1$ from the
usable length-window width $\ell/L$.

\section{The coefficient upper bound by a finite length calibration}\label{a61:o2:sec:upper}
We prove
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:target}
 D_n\ge C_\st F+\frac{7C_\st}{3}\frac{F\log L}{L}-O(F/L).
\end{equation}
Unlike a fixed-parameter leading calibration, the construction below retains its quantitative accuracy as $n$ varies.

\subsection{The truncated row threshold}
For a fixed constant $C_0$ and $r\to\infty$, put
\[
 \kappa(r)=\tfrac12\log r+\log\log r-C_0/2.
\]
For fixed $Q$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:tail}
 \sum_{Q<q\le r}q^{-3/2}e^{\kappa(r)q/r}
 \le C Q^{-1/2}+o(1)+C e^{-C_0/2}.
\end{equation}
The error is uniform when the minimum allowed $r$ tends to infinity. To see this, split at $r/\kappa$ and $r/2$. The first range is $O(Q^{-1/2})$. The second is at most
\[
 C(\kappa/r)^{1/2}e^{\kappa/2}=O(r^{-1/4}\log r)=o(1).
\]
For $q\ge r/2$, sum the geometric progression after bounding $q^{-3/2}$ by $Cr^{-3/2}$; this gives
\[
 Cr^{-1/2}e^\kappa/\kappa\le Ce^{-C_0/2}.
\]
Here $\kappa\sim\tfrac12\log r$. Constants can be chosen independent of fixed $C_0$ once $r$ is large enough. Integer rounding does not change these bounds.

Together with \eqref{a61:o2:eq:transfer}, this means the defective mass $m_\st$ can absorb the large-$q$ tail whenever vanishing tilts satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:tailcondition}
 q\Psi(s,\theta)\le q\kappa(r)/r+o(1),\qquad q\le h\le r,
\end{equation}
uniformly with $r\to\infty$. Indeed, fix $Q$ large, then fix $C_0$ large; the finite $q\le Q$ rows converge to their critical weights, and \eqref{a61:o2:upp:tail} makes the remainder smaller than a fixed fraction of $1-m_\st$.

\subsection{The variable potential and its affine dual}
Choose a sufficiently large fixed $R$ at the end. Put $\delta=\varepsilon=L^{-12}$ and
\begin{align}
 V_n(y)&=\frac{\alpha}{2L(y+\varepsilon)}
  \left\{\log[H(y+\varepsilon)]
          +2\log\log[H(y+\varepsilon)]-C_0\right\},\label{a61:o2:upp:potential}\\
 B_n&=\frac\alpha{2L}\{\log H+2\log\log H-C_0\},\label{a61:o2:upp:B}\\
 J_n(A)&=\min_{0\le y\le R}\{Ay+V_n(y)\},\qquad A>0.\label{a61:o2:upp:dual}
\end{align}
All logarithms are defined for large $n$, as $\varepsilon H\to\infty$; $B_n$ remains bounded away from zero and infinity.

For the Kepler arch with parameter $B=B_n$, let
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:arch}
 y_*(s)=M\sin^2u,\quad
 s=\frac{u-\sin u\cos u}{\pi},\quad
 M^3=\frac{2vB}{\pi^2},\quad
 p_*(s)=-y_*'(s)/v.
\end{equation}
Then $p_*'=B/y_*^2>0$. Define, for $0\le s\le1$,
\begin{align}
 p_n(s)&=p_*(\delta+(1-2\delta)s),\notag\\
 g_n(1)&=0,\qquad g_n'(s)=vp_n(s)^2/2-J_n(p_n'(s)),\notag\\
 f_n(s,y)&=g_n(s)+p_n(s)y.\label{a61:o2:upp:calibration}
\end{align}
The defining minimum gives the exact inequality
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:HJ}
 -\partial_s f_n+\frac v2(\partial_y f_n)^2
 =J_n(p_n')-p_n'y\le V_n(y).
\end{equation}

Near either endpoint, the arch has $p_*=O(s^{-1/3})$, $p_*'=O(s^{-4/3})$ and $p_*''=O(s^{-7/3})$, interpreted with distance to the appropriate endpoint. Uniformly in the compact range of $B_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:derivatives}
 \|p_n\|_\infty=O(L^4),\qquad
 \|p_n'\|_\infty=O(L^{16}),\qquad
 \|p_n''\|_\infty=O(L^{28}).
\end{equation}
The potential is strictly convex for large $n$. In fact, writing $u=y+\varepsilon$, $x=\log(Hu)$ and $\phi(x)=x+2\log x-C_0$, its second derivative has the sign of
\[
 \phi''-3\phi'+2\phi
 =2x+4\log x-2C_0-3-6/x-2/x^2>0.
\]
The minimum in \eqref{a61:o2:upp:dual} is therefore unique. Consequently $J_n$ is $C^1$ with $0\le J_n'(A)\le R$, including when the minimizer reaches a boundary. Since
$g_n''=vp_np_n'-J_n'(p_n')p_n''$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:Ctwo}
 \|f_n\|_{C^2}=O(L^{28}),\qquad
 \|\partial_s f_n\|_\infty=O(L^{16}),\qquad
 \|\partial_y f_n\|_\infty=O(L^4)
\end{equation}
on $[0,1]\times[0,R]$. For the value bound one can use $0<J_n(A)\le AR+V_n(R)$.

\subsection{Calibration value and endpoint losses}
For $A$ in the range of $p_n'$, the dual obeys
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:dual-bound}
 J_n(A)\ge2\sqrt{B_nA}
 -\frac CL\sqrt A(1+|\log A|)-\varepsilon A.
\end{equation}
Here is a direct uniform proof. Put $a=\alpha/(2L)$, $B=B_n$ and $u=y+\varepsilon$. Since $\log u\ge-12\log L$,
\[
 V_n(y)\ge\frac{B+a\log u-a}{u},\qquad
 B+a\log u-a\ge B/2
\]
for large $n$; the first inequality uses
$2\log(\log(Hu)/\log H)\ge-1$. Set $\lambda=\sqrt{B/A}$ and $r=u/\lambda$. After adding $A\varepsilon$, the expression $Ay+V_n(y)$ divided by $\sqrt{AB}$ is at least
\[
 r+\frac{1+(a/B)(\log\lambda-1)+(a/B)\log r}{r}.
\]
Outside $r\in[1/8,8]$ the cruder bound $r+1/(2r)\ge4$ suffices. Inside that interval, $r+1/r\ge2$ and the remaining term is bounded below by
$-C(a/B)(1+|\log\lambda|)$. This proves \eqref{a61:o2:upp:dual-bound}.

The endpoint singularities are integrable with the needed logarithm:
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:log-integrability}
 \int_0^1\sqrt{p_n'}(1+|\log p_n'|)\,ds\le C,
 \qquad \int_0^1p_n'\,ds=O(\delta^{-1/3}).
\end{equation}
The first comparison is with $s^{-2/3}(1+|\log s|)$ near an endpoint. Integrating \eqref{a61:o2:upp:dual-bound} yields
\begin{align}
 f_n(0,0)\ge{}&\int_0^1\{2\sqrt{B_np_n'}-vp_n^2/2\}\,ds\notag\\
 &-O(1/L)-O(\varepsilon\delta^{-1/3}).\label{a61:o2:upp:value1}
\end{align}
For the full arch this integral equals
\[
 C(B_n)=3\left(\frac{\pi^2B_n^2}{2v}\right)^{1/3}.
\]
For completeness, $y_*'^2/(2v)=B_n(1/y_*-1/M)$ and
$\int_0^1 B_n/y_*\,ds=2B_n/M$, giving the value $3B_n/M$.
Both integrands discarded by cutting off $[0,\delta]$ and $[1-\delta,1]$ have absolute size $O(s^{-2/3})$, so their loss is $O(\delta^{1/3})$. The linear reparametrization contributes $O(\delta)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:value}
 f_n(0,0)\ge C(B_n)-O(1/L),
\end{equation}
since $\delta=\varepsilon=L^{-12}$. This is a finite-$n$ estimate, with no interchange of unquantified limits.

\subsection{Uniform contraction of transformed rows}
At elapsed macro-length $k$ and height $h$, retain legal transitions with $k+e\le n-1$ and $1\le h+d\le RH$. Write $s=k/n$, $y=h/H$ and transform each weight to
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:transformed}
 w(e,q,d)\exp\{F[f_n(s,y)-f_n(s+e/n,y+d/H)]\}.
\end{equation}
Set $a=F/n$ and $b=F/H$, so $b^2=a$ and $aH=L$.
On the range
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:smallsteps}
 e\le HL^{40},\qquad |d|\le\sqrt H L^{40},
\end{equation}
Taylor's formula gives uniformly
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:taylor}
 F[f_n(s,y)-f_n(s+e/n,y+d/H)]
 =-a(\partial_s f_n)e-bp_n(s)d+o(1).
\end{equation}
There is no $d^2$ term because $f_n$ is affine in height. By \eqref{a61:o2:upp:Ctwo}, the absolute remainder is at most
\[
 CFL^{28}\left\{(HL^{40}/n)^2
                   +(HL^{40}/n)(L^{40}/\sqrt H)\right\}
       =n^{-1/3}L^{O(1)}=o(1).
\]
Put $\lambda=-a\partial_sf_n$ and $\theta=-bp_n$. Both tilts vanish uniformly. Multiplication of the remainder in \eqref{a61:o2:eq:psi} by $q\le RH$ still gives $o(1)$, because the derivatives grow only polylogarithmically. By \eqref{a61:o2:upp:HJ}, with $r=h+\varepsilon H$,
\begin{align}
 q\Psi(\lambda,\theta)
 &\le\frac q\alpha aV_n(y)+o(1)\notag\\
 &=\frac q{2r}\{\log r+2\log\log r-C_0\}+o(1)
   =\frac q r\kappa(r)+o(1).\label{a61:o2:upp:row-exponent}
\end{align}
Since $r\ge\varepsilon H\to\infty$ uniformly, the truncated-row argument bounds the contribution of \eqref{a61:o2:upp:smallsteps} by a fixed number below one, choosing $C_0$ sufficiently large once and for all.

We now check all omitted steps. The global derivative bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:global-majorant}
 F[f_n(s,y)-f_n(s+e/n,y+d/H)]
       \le CaL^{16}e+CbL^{16}|d|.
\end{equation}
For $e>HL^{40}$, add a fixed small positive length tilt $\lambda_0$, split the two signs of $d$, and apply \eqref{a61:o2:eq:transfer} in its fixed neighborhood. The resulting upper bound is
\[
 C\exp\{-\lambda_0HL^{40}+O(H)\}=o(1).
\]
For $|d|>\sqrt H L^{40}$, add the corresponding signed height tilt
$\theta_0=cL^{40}/\sqrt H$, with $c>0$ small and fixed. The generating row for the majorant is at most
\[
 C\exp\{C_RH[aL^{16}+(bL^{16}+\theta_0)^2]\}.
\]
The Chernoff factor is $\exp(-cL^{80})$. Since
$Ha=L$ and $\sqrt H b=\sqrt L$, the positive exponent is
$C_Rc^2L^{80}+O(L^{57})$. Taking $c<1/(2C_R)$ proves this tail is $o(1)$. The root-Taylor remainders are $n^{-1/3}L^{O(1)}=o(1)$ as well. These estimates allow unrestricted lengths and destinations and hence cover every omitted retained transition.

Leaving a fixed margin when choosing $Q$ and $C_0$ above proves the uniform transformed row bound
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:contraction}
 \text{each row in \eqref{a61:o2:upp:transformed} has sum at most }
 \overline m<1
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $n$, uniformly in $k$ and $h$.

\subsection{Telescoping and the terminal factor}
The sum of all transformed finite prefixes is at most $(1-\overline m)^{-1}$. Telescoping \eqref{a61:o2:upp:transformed} therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:upp:coefficient}
 a_n^{[\le RH]}\rho^n\le C\exp\{-Ff_n(0,1/H)\},
\end{equation}
provided the exact terminal factor \eqref{a61:o2:eq:terminal} remains bounded after undoing the transformation. It does: by \eqref{a61:o2:upp:Ctwo},
\[
 f_n(s,y)\le p_n(1)y+CL^{16}(1-s).
\]
With $r_0=n-1-k\ge0$, the residual factor is bounded by
\[
 \rho^{r_0}t^{1-h}
       \exp\{bp_n(1)h+CaL^{16}(r_0+1)\}.
\]
Both $bp_n(1)$ and $aL^{16}$ tend to zero, so the fixed negative exponents $\log\rho$ and $-\log t$ dominate uniformly. No endpoint height restriction is imposed, and the terminal weight is not discarded. Also
\[
 F[f_n(0,1/H)-f_n(0,0)]=bp_n(0)=o(1).
\]
Choose $R$ fixed so that $R^2/(4D_0)>C_\st+1$. Combining \eqref{a61:o2:eq:first-hit}, \eqref{a61:o2:upp:value} and \eqref{a61:o2:upp:coefficient} gives
\[
 D_n\ge FC(B_n)-O(F/L).
\]
Finally,
\begin{align*}
 \log H&=\tfrac23L+\tfrac13\log L,\\
 2\log\log H&=2\log L+2\log(2/3)+O((\log L)/L),\\
 B_n&=\tfrac\alpha3+\tfrac{7\alpha}{6}\frac{\log L}{L}+O(1/L),\\
 C(B_n)&=C_\st\left(1+\tfrac73\frac{\log L}{L}\right)+O(1/L).
\end{align*}
This proves \eqref{a61:o2:upp:target}; together with the coefficient lower construction it proves Theorem~\ref{a61:o2:thm:main}.

\section{The second inverse correction}
Let $\lambda=\log\mu$ and $N(Y)=\min\{n:a_n\ge Y\}$. The foundation proves $a_n$ is nondecreasing by appending its last letter. The two-sided logarithmic expansion therefore has the following inverse consequence.
\begin{corollary}\label{a61:o2:cor:inverse}
As $Y\to\infty$, with $S=\log Y$,
\begin{align}\label{a61:o2:eq:inverse}
 N(Y)={}&\frac S\lambda
 +C_\st\lambda^{-4/3}S^{1/3}(\log S)^{2/3}
 \left[1+\frac73\frac{\log\log S}{\log S}
                 +O\!\left(\frac1{\log S}\right)\right].
\end{align}
Here $C_\st\lambda^{-4/3}=0.893568257650894909\ldots$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set $G(S)=S^{1/3}(\log S)^{2/3}$ and $E(S)=G(S)/\log S$.
Uniformly for $n=S/\lambda+O(G(S))$, elementary differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
 n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}
   &=\lambda^{-1/3}G(S)+O(E(S)),\\
 \frac{\log\log n}{\log n}
   &=\frac{\log\log S}{\log S}+O(1/\log S).
\end{align*}
The first error includes the constant shift $-\log\lambda$ in $\log n$; the change caused by $n-S/\lambda=O(G(S))$ is still smaller. Theorem~\ref{a61:o2:thm:main} consequently implies
\[
 D_n=C_\st\lambda^{-1/3}G(S)
       \left[1+\frac73\frac{\log\log S}{\log S}\right]+O(E(S))
\]
uniformly in that window. Choose integers just below and above
\[
 \frac S\lambda+C_\st\lambda^{-4/3}G(S)
       \left[1+\frac73\frac{\log\log S}{\log S}\right]
       \pm K E(S)
\]
for a sufficiently large fixed $K$. Since $\log a_n=\lambda n-D_n$, the lower choice is below the logarithmic target $S$ and the upper choice is above it, eventually. Monotonicity brackets $N(Y)$ between them. Integer rounding costs $O(1)=o(E(S))$, proving \eqref{a61:o2:eq:inverse}.
\end{proof}

\section{The explicit constant in the finite-height radius}
\label{a61:o2:rad:section}

This auxiliary refinement is independent of the matching global
coefficient bounds. Let $\rho_H$ be the radius obtained by restricting
all macro-boundary heights to $1,\ldots,H$. Define
\[
 b=1-\rho,\qquad
 C_q=\frac{\sqrt{-16z_*^2+55z_*-10}}{2\sqrt\pi},\qquad
 m_*=\frac{\rho}{bz_*+\rho},\qquad
 a_*=\frac{\rho t_*C_q}{1-\rho},
\]
\[
 c_*=\log\frac{1-m_*}{2a_*}
 =0.8667962458055399529886348076493374\ldots.
\]
We prove
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:theorem}
 \log(\rho_H/\rho)
  =\frac\alpha H\left\{\frac12\log H+\log\log H
                          +c_*+o(1)\right\}.
\end{equation}
The proof must address the killed boundary: the unrestricted row
threshold alone is insufficient to identify the finite-height Perron
threshold.

\subsection{Exact tilted rows and their endpoint mass}

Write $k(\ell,q,d)$ for the exact macro-transition coefficients,
including the multiplier $x/(1-x)$, and put $x=\rho e^s$,
$T=t_*e^\theta$. At fixed $q$ the full tilted row is
\[
 A_q(s,\theta)
 :=\sum_{\ell,d}k(\ell,q,d)x^\ell T^d
 =\frac{xT}{1-x}Q_q(x,T)T^{-q}.
\]
Uniformly in a sufficiently small fixed complex neighborhood of
$(s,\theta)=(0,0)$, square-root coefficient extraction gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:transfer}
 A_q(s,\theta)=a(s,\theta)q^{-3/2}
               e^{q\Psi(s,\theta)}(1+O(q^{-1})),
 \qquad a(0,0)=a_*,
\end{equation}
where
$\Psi(s,\theta)=-\log[z_c(\rho e^s,t_*e^\theta)t_*e^\theta]$.
The expansion and its first four parameter derivatives are uniform
on a smaller neighborhood. Indeed, the exact quadratic branch has
separated analytic discriminant roots and nonvanishing rational
prefactors near the critical point. Expanding the analytic multiplier
at its smaller root, then using the binomial coefficients of the
square root, gives the uniform error in \eqref{a61:o2:rad:transfer}.
Cauchy's formula applied on a slightly larger parameter neighborhood
also bounds its derivatives. Finitely many small $q$ are handled by
their analytic generating functions.

The discriminant identities give
\[
 \Psi(0,0)=\Psi_\theta(0,0)=0,\qquad
 \Psi_s(0,0)=\alpha^{-1},\qquad
 \Psi_{\theta\theta}(0,0)=\nu:=v/\alpha>0.
\]
Thus, for $s=O(\log H/H)$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:derivatives}
 \Psi(s,0)=s/\alpha+O(s^2),\quad
 \Psi_\theta(s,0)=O(s),\quad
 \Psi_{\theta\theta}(s,0)=\nu+O(s).
\end{equation}

For fixed $c\in\mathbb R$ set
\[
 s_H(c)=\frac\alpha H
            \left(\frac12\log H+\log\log H+c\right),
 \qquad R_H(c)=\sum_{q=1}^H A_q(s_H(c),0).
\]
We claim, locally uniformly in $c$, that
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:row-limit}
 R_H(c)\longrightarrow m_*+2a_* e^c.
\end{equation}
The same limit holds when the upper cutoff is $H-W$, provided
$W\log H/H\to0$.

To prove this, put $k=H\Psi(s_H(c),0)$, so
$k=\tfrac12\log H+\log\log H+c+o(1)$.
Below $H/(\log H)^2$, summable $q^{-3/2}$ domination recovers the
critical total mass $m_*$.
The range $H/(\log H)^2<q\le H/2$ contributes at most
$C(\log H)H^{-1/2}e^{k/2}=o(1)$. On the remaining range the
endpoint Laplace sum is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:laplace-sum}
 \sum_{H/2<q\le H}q^{-3/2}e^{kq/H}
 =\frac{H^{-1/2}e^k}{k}(1+o(1))\longrightarrow2e^c.
\end{equation}
For example, write $q=H-r$ and sum the geometric window
$r=O(H/k)$; replacing $(1-r/H)^{-3/2}$ by $1$ has relative error
$O(k^{-1})$. The more remote terms are negligible. Removing the last
$W$ terms has relative cost $O(kW/H)=o(1)$.
This proves \eqref{a61:o2:rad:row-limit}.

In particular, $c_*$ is the unique solution of
$m_*+2a_* e^c=1$. If $c<c_*$, every
diagonally tilted row of the height-$H$ matrix at $x=\rho e^{s_H(c)}$
has mass at most $R_H(c)<1$. The Perron eigenvalue is therefore below
$1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:lower-radius}
 \rho_H>\rho e^{s_H(c)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{A homogeneous kernel on a high strip}

Fix $c>c_*$ and any $1/2<\gamma<1$. Set
\[
 W=\lfloor H^\gamma\rfloor,\quad Q=H-W,\quad
 I=\{H-W+1,\ldots,H\},\quad x=\rho e^{s_H(c)}.
\]
Every $q\le Q$ is legal throughout $I$. Thus the fixed subkernel
\[
 a_d=\sum_{q=1}^Q\sum_\ell k(\ell,q,d)x^\ell t_*^d,
 \qquad R=\sum_d a_d
\]
is translation invariant on the strip before killing its exits, and
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:supercritical-mass}
 R\longrightarrow m_*+2a_* e^c>1.
\end{equation}
This fixed subkernel avoids any comparison of height-dependent row
errors against a small endpoint test-vector entry.

The differentiated transfer formula implies the uniform estimates
\begin{gather}
 \sum_d a_dd=O(\log H),\quad
 \sum_d a_dd^2=\Theta(H),\quad
 \sum_d a_d|d|^3=O(H^{3/2}),\label{a61:o2:rad:moments}\\
 \sum_d a_d e^{c_1|d|/\sqrt H}\le C
 \quad\hbox{for some fixed }c_1,C>0.\label{a61:o2:rad:exponential-moment}
\end{gather}
Here, conditionally on $q$, the increment moment generating function
is $A_q(s,\theta)/A_q(s,0)$. Its mean is $O(1+qs)$ and its variance is
$\nu q+O(qs+1)$. Evaluating at $\theta=\pm c_1/\sqrt H$ gives a
uniformly bounded moment ratio, since its logarithm is
$O(qs/\sqrt H+q/H+H^{-1/2})=O(1)$.
This proves the upper moment bounds and the mean bound after summing
with the row weights. The lower second-moment bound follows from
\eqref{a61:o2:rad:laplace-sum}, which places a fixed positive mass at
$q=H-O(H/\log H)$, where the conditional variance is at least a
constant times $H$.

Spending half the exponential moment to dominate a polynomial gives,
for each fixed integer $r\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:tail}
 \sum_{|d|>W}a_d|d|^r
 \le C_rH^{r/2}e^{-c_2W/\sqrt H}=o(1).
\end{equation}

\subsection{An exactly centered sine test}

Put $\delta=\pi/(W+1)$ and truncate to $|d|\le W$. Define
\[
 S(\tau)=\sum_{|d|\le W}a_de^{\tau d}\sin(\delta d).
\]
Since $d$ and $\sin(\delta d)$ have the same sign in this range,
$S'(\tau)>0$. Using $|\sin u-u|\le|u|^3/6$ and
\eqref{a61:o2:rad:moments}--\eqref{a61:o2:rad:tail} gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:sine-at-zero}
 S(0)=O(\delta\log H+\delta^3H^{3/2}).
\end{equation}
For every fixed sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, uniformly on
$|\tau|\le\varepsilon/\sqrt H$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:sine-derivative}
 S'(\tau)\ge c\delta H.
\end{equation}
Indeed, choose a large fixed $B$. By the second-moment lower bound
and the exponential moment, at least $cH$ of that moment lies in
$|d|\le B\sqrt H$. There $e^{\tau d}\ge e^{-\varepsilon B}$ and
$d\sin(\delta d)\ge\delta d^2/2$ for large $H$.

The intermediate value theorem, applied to
\eqref{a61:o2:rad:sine-at-zero}--\eqref{a61:o2:rad:sine-derivative}, now gives a
unique root $\tau_H$ with
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:tilt}
 |\tau_H|=O\left(\frac{\log H}{H}
                     +\frac{H^{1/2}}{W^2}\right)
          =o(H^{-1/2}),\qquad S(\tau_H)=0.
\end{equation}
Set
\[
 C_H=\sum_{|d|\le W}a_de^{\tau_Hd}\cos(\delta d).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:rad:cosine-mass}
 C_H=R+o(1).
\end{equation}
The omitted tail is $o(1)$ by \eqref{a61:o2:rad:tail}; the tilt error is
$O(|\tau_H|\sqrt H)=o(1)$ by the exponential moment; and the cosine
error is $O(\delta^2H)=o(1)$.

Index $I$ by $i=1,\ldots,W$, and take the positive test vector
$f_i=\sin(\delta i)$, extended by zero outside this interval. For all
$|d|\le W$ one has
\[
 f_{i+d}\ge\sin(\delta(i+d)).
\]
Inside the strip this is equality. Outside, the sine on the right is
nonpositive because $1-W\le i+d\le2W$.
Consequently the killed, exponentially tilted subkernel $B_H$
satisfies
\begin{align*}
 (B_Hf)_i
 &\ge\sum_{|d|\le W}a_de^{\tau_Hd}\sin(\delta(i+d))\\
 &=f_iC_H+\cos(\delta i)S(\tau_H)=C_Hf_i.
\end{align*}
Collatz--Wielandt gives $r(B_H)\ge C_H$. The positive diagonal
similarities by $t_*^h$ and $e^{\tau_Hh}$ preserve spectral radii,
and retaining transitions can only decrease them. Hence
\[
 r(K_H(x))\ge r(B_H)\ge C_H=R+o(1)>1.
\]
It follows that $\rho_H<\rho e^{s_H(c)}$ whenever
$c>c_*$ and $H$ is large. Combining this with
\eqref{a61:o2:rad:lower-radius} and letting $c\to c_*$ proves
\eqref{a61:o2:rad:theorem}.

The constant $c_*$ is a local-row and finite-height constant.
This argument does not identify the coefficient in the global
$O(F/L)$ deficit remainder.



\section{What the theorem does not determine}
Equation~\eqref{a61:o2:eq:main} leaves an $O(n^{1/3}(\log n)^{-1/3})$ error in $\log a_n$, which diverges. Exponentiating it therefore does not give an asymptotic equivalent for $a_n$. The coefficient of that error scale, fluctuation determinants, a power-law prefactor, and an all-orders transseries are not established here. The finite-height shift is an auxiliary spectral statement; it does not by itself determine the next global deficit constant. The inverse result also has a diverging error and is not an exact integer rounding rule.
\begin{writenote}
The coefficient of the $F/L$ scale is determined in Part~\ref{a61:part:o3} (Theorem~\ref{a61:o3:thm:main}), and the inverse result is refined in Corollary~\ref{a61:o3:inv3:theorem} and Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:inverse}. The other limitations of this paragraph hold for the whole report.
\end{writenote}


The coefficient $7/3$ has two consistent origins. In the coefficient construction, $\tfrac12\log(n/M)$ contributes $\tfrac16\log L$ per block and the usable length-window width contributes $\log L$, giving $\tfrac76\log L$ per block.  \kappa_* =\log Y-2\log3+2c_*
           =-1.94014818313627\ldots.
\]
A natural candidate for the next global term is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o2:eq:conjecture}
 D_n\ \stackrel{\mathrm{conj.}}{=}\
 C_*F\left[1+\frac73\frac{\log L}{L}
                  +\frac{\kappa_*}{L}+o(1/L)\right].
\end{equation}
Here is the conditional calculation, including its missing step. If the exact local row threshold transfers to a global action with error $o(F/L)$ and no additional contribution at that scale, its potential would be
\[
 \frac{\alpha}{2h}\{\log h+2\log\log h+2c_*+o(1)\}.
\]
After setting $h=Hy$, the first perturbation of the leading action is
\[
 \frac{\alpha F}{2L}\int_0^1
 \frac{\tfrac73\log L+\log y+2\log(2/3)+2c_*}{y}\,ds.
\]
For the leading Kepler minimizer $y=Y\sin^2u$ and
$s=(u-\sin u\cos u)/\pi$, one has
\[
 \frac{ds}{y}=\frac{2\,du}{\pi Y},\qquad
 \frac{\int_0^1(\log y)/y\,ds}{\int_0^1 1/y\,ds}
   =\log Y-2\log2,
\]
using $\int_0^\pi\log(\sin u)\,du=-\pi\log2$.
Since $C_* =\alpha/Y$, the resulting constant is exactly
$\log Y-2\log3+2c_*$.

The unproved premise is the global $o(F/L)$ transfer, equivalently an $o(M)$ error at the macro-block scale. The present balanced-rectangle lower construction intentionally loses $O(M)$ through its length and increment windows, profile margin, and fixed kernel factors. The exact finite-height constant alone does not remove those losses. Thus \eqref{a61:o2:eq:conjecture} is a research target, not a consequence of Theorem~\ref{a61:o2:thm:main}; it cannot be used as an established coefficient estimate or inverse correction.

Part~\ref{a61:part:ao}, at $J=1$, is a second proof. \section{Statement and exact constants}

$w_i\le1+\#\{j<i-1:w_j<w_{j+1}\}$ for $i\ge2$. It avoids classical
120 if no $i<j<k$ satisfy $w_k<w_i<w_j$. Let $a_n$ count such words,
with $a_0=1$; this is OEIS A202061 \cite{OEIS}.

Let $z_*$ be the smallest positive root of
$z^3-5z^2+6z-1=0$, and define
\begin{align*}

\end{align*}

\begin{align*}

\section{Positive kernel and dependency boundary}
The exact positive height-walk representation has suffix series
\[
 e_h=\frac1{1-x}+\frac{x}{1-x}\sum_{q=1}^h(Q_q(x,T)e)_{h+1-q},
 \qquad (Tf)_h=f_{h+1},\qquad A(x)=1+xe_1(x).
\]
Here $Q(x;u,T)=\sum_{q\ge1}Q_q(x,T)u^q$ is the root vanishing at
$u=0$ of
\[
 xT(1-u)Q^2+[(1-x)(u-(1-x))+xT(x-u)]Q+(1-x)u=0.
\]
A macro-step with $q,r$ changes height by $d=1-q+r$, has generating
weight $x[T_0^r]Q_q(x,T_0)/(1-x)$, and is legal exactly when $q$ does
not exceed its starting height. Its endpoint is then positive.
Let $k(e,q,d)$ be its multiplicity at total macro degree $e$ and set
\[
 w(e,q,d)=k(e,q,d)\rho^e t_*^d,
 \qquad W_q(s,\theta)=\sum_{e,d}w(e,q,d)e^{se+\theta d}.
\]
Total macro degree here includes the leading multiplier.
The critical row mass is $\sum_qW_q(0,0)=m_*<1$.

The audited quadratic-branch analysis supplies uniform analytic
square-root transfer, including its first four parameter derivatives,
and the root expansion
\[
 \Psi(s,\theta)
 =\frac{s+v\theta^2/2}{\alpha}
      +O(s^2+|s\theta|+|\theta|^3).
\]
Section~\ref{a61:o3:up3:section} restates its exact amplitude and the
quantitative bounds needed for the coefficient upper estimate.

For the patch in the coefficient lower construction we use only the
previously audited local positive bound. For any fixed $K>3$, internal
block degree $\ell\ge L^K$ and $|d|\le C\sqrt{\ell L}$, summing over
a fixed sufficiently wide $O(\sqrt\ell)$ conditional $q$ window gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:eq:local-kernel}
 \sum_q\rho^\ell t_*^d B(\ell,q,q+d-1)
 \ge c\ell^{-2}\exp\left[-\frac{d^2}{2v\ell}-o(1)\right].
\end{equation}
The conditional center is
$q=\alpha\ell+\beta(d-1)$, where
$\beta=-4(z_*-3)(2z_*-1)/7$; every retained $q$ differs from
$\alpha\ell$ by $O(\sqrt{\ell L})$.
Using the leading $x$ term makes its total macro degree $\ell+1$.
The local estimate follows from a positive summand, uniform Stirling
and the exact Hessian identity; a full coefficient equivalent is not
assumed.

The exact residual terminal factor at elapsed macro degree $k$ and
height $h$ is $\rho^{n-1-k}t_*^{1-h}$, apart from the original fixed
leading $\rho$. The foundation also gives a fixed $D_0>0$ with
\[
 a_n^{[>RH]}\rho^n\le C\exp[-R^2F/(4D_0)]
\]
for fixed $R$ and all sufficiently large $n$.

\section{Upper coefficient bound at the constant scale}\label{a61:o3:up3:section}

For brevity write \(C=C_*\) in this section. Put \(B_0=\alpha/3\) and
\(Y_0=(2vB_0/\pi^2)^{1/3}\). We prove the one-sided estimate

\begin{equation}
D_n\ge CF\left[1+\frac{7\log L}{3L}+\frac\kappa L\right]+o(F/L),
\qquad \kappa=\log Y_0-2\log3+2c_*.
\label{a61:o3:up3:target}
\end{equation}

The inputs are the exact positive macro-kernel and terminal
representation, the uniform square-root transfer with analytic parameter
derivatives, the critical root derivative identities, and the
fixed-ceiling first-hit estimate. Only the exact row mass and tail
amplitude are needed from the finite-height calculation; its spectral
comparison is not used.

The endpoint geometric sum has relative error \(O(1/\log r)\), but
converting its main term to \(2e^c\) adds an error
\(O(\log\log r/\log r)\). Uniformly in the range below this is
\(O(\log L/L)\), smaller than the explicit row slack \(L^{-1/4}\).

\subsection{Exact analytic input and amplitude}

Let \(z\) be the smallest positive root of \(z^3-5z^2+6z-1=0\), and set

\[
\rho=3z^2-10z+2,\quad t=z^{-1},\quad
\alpha=(8z^2-29z+9)/7,\quad v=(2z^2-7z+2)/2.
\]

For the exact critically tilted macro-step weights \(w(e,q,d)\), define

\[
W_q(\sigma,\theta)=\sum_{e,d}w(e,q,d)e^{\sigma e+\theta d}.
\]

There is one fixed parameter neighborhood such that

\begin{equation}
W_q(\sigma,\theta)
 =a(\sigma,\theta)q^{-3/2}e^{q\Psi(\sigma,\theta)}
   (1+E_q(\sigma,\theta)),\qquad
|\partial^j E_q|\le C_j/q\quad (0\le j\le4),
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-1}
\end{equation}

uniformly for sufficiently large \(q\); finitely many remaining \(q\)
are analytic in that same neighborhood. The amplitude is analytic and
positive at the origin. The root expansion is

\begin{equation}
\Psi(\sigma,\theta)
 =\frac{\sigma+v\theta^2/2}{\alpha}
   +O(\sigma^2+|\sigma\theta|+|\theta|^3).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-2}
\end{equation}

The exact mass and amplitude are

\[
m_*:=\sum_{q\ge1}W_q(0,0)=\frac{\rho}{(1-\rho)z+\rho}<1,
\] \begin{equation}
C_q=\frac{\sqrt{-16z^2+55z-10}}{2\sqrt\pi},\qquad
 a_*:=a(0,0)=\frac{\rho C_q}{(1-\rho)z},\qquad
 c_*:=\log\frac{1-m_*}{2a_*}.
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-3}
\end{equation}

For clarity, the amplitude is that of the actual macro-kernel, including
its factor \(\rho t/(1-\rho)\). Omitting the factor \(t\) would change
the constant in \eqref{a61:o3:up3:target}.

These formulas can also be recovered directly from the quadratic for
\(Q\). With \(b=1-x\),

\[
\mathcal B=b(\zeta-b)+xT(x-\zeta),\qquad
\Delta=\mathcal B^2-4xT(1-\zeta)b\zeta,
\]

and smaller discriminant root \(z_c\), the square-root singular
amplitude is

\[
C_q(x,T)=\frac{\sqrt{-z_c\Delta_\zeta(x,z_c,T)}}
 {4\sqrt\pi xT(1-z_c)}.
\]

Substituting \((x,T,z_c)=(\rho,z^{-1},z)\) and reducing modulo the cubic
gives \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-3}. Also, at the root,

\[
\frac{xT}{1-x}Q(x,z,T)
 =-\frac{\mathcal B(x,z,T)}{2(1-x)(1-z)},
\]

which reduces to the stated \(m_*\). The algebraic identities here
follow by polynomial reduction modulo the defining cubic; no numerical
approximation is used in the proof.

\subsection{Quantitative head estimate}

For real \((\sigma,\theta)\) with \(Q(|\sigma|+|\theta|)=o(1)\),
differentiating \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-1} along the line segment from the origin
gives

\[
|\nabla W_q(\sigma',\theta')|\le Cq^{-1/2},\qquad 1\le q\le Q,
\]

uniformly on that segment. Indeed, derivatives of \(a\) and \(E_q\) are
bounded, derivatives of \(e^{q\Psi}\) supply at most a factor \(Cq\),
and the exponential remains bounded since
\(|q\Psi|\le C Q(|\sigma|+|\theta|)=o(1)\). The finitely many small
indices are absorbed by analyticity. Therefore

\begin{equation}
\sum_{q\le Q}W_q(\sigma,\theta)
 \le \sum_{q\le Q}W_q(0,0)
      +C(|\sigma|+|\theta|)\sqrt Q
 \le m_*+C(|\sigma|+|\theta|)\sqrt Q.
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-4}
\end{equation}

This is a quantitative bound for an increasing head cutoff. No
unspecified finite-cutoff continuity is used.

\subsection{Uniform endpoint-tail lemma}

Put \(\ell=\log L\),

\[
H=n^{2/3}L^{1/3},\quad \epsilon=L^{-12},\quad Q=\lceil L^4\rceil,
\]

and suppose

\[
\epsilon H\le r\le (R+1)H,\qquad
k_r=\tfrac12\log r+\log\log r+c,
\]

where \(R\) is fixed and \(c\) remains in a fixed compact interval.
Uniformly in these choices,

\begin{equation}
\sum_{Q<q\le r}q^{-3/2}e^{k_rq/r}
 =2e^c+O\!\left(\frac{\ell}{L}+L^{-2}
                  +n^{-1/6}L^5\right).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-5}
\end{equation}

Only the upper-bound version of \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-5} is needed; the equality
follows from the same splitting.

To prove it, note that \(\log r=(2/3)L+O(\ell)\) uniformly and split the
sum at \(r/L^2\) and \(r/2\). For large \(n\), these points exceed
\(Q\).

\begin{itemize}
\item
  On \(Q<q\le r/L^2\), the exponential is at most \(e^{C/L}\), so the
  sum is \(O(Q^{-1/2})=O(L^{-2})\).
\item
  On \(r/L^2<q\le r/2\), the sum is bounded by \[
  C\frac{L}{\sqrt r}e^{k_r/2}
  \le C L^{3/2}r^{-1/4}
  \le C n^{-1/6}L^5.
  \]
\item
  On \(r/2<q\le r\), write \(q=\lfloor r\rfloor-j\). The geometric
  distribution proportional to \(e^{-k_rj/r}\) has mean \(O(r/k_r)\). On
  the retained range, the factor \((q/r)^{-3/2}\) differs from 1 by at
  most a constant times \((r-q)/r\). Summing this bound gives
  \begin{equation}
  \sum_{r/2<q\le r}q^{-3/2}e^{k_rq/r}
  =\frac{r^{-1/2}e^{k_r}}{k_r}
    \left[1+O(k_r^{-1}+k_r/r)\right].
  \label{a61:o3:up3:eq-6}
  \end{equation} The geometric tail beyond this range is exponentially
  small in \(k_r\); nonintegral \(r\) changes the answer by relative
  \(O(k_r/r)\). Since \[
  \frac{r^{-1/2}e^{k_r}}{k_r}=e^c\frac{\log r}{k_r}
  =2e^c\left[1+O\!\left(\frac{\log\log r}{\log r}\right)\right],
  \] this is \(2e^c+O(\ell/L)\).
\end{itemize}

In particular, the error in \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-5} is \(o(L^{-1/4})\).

\subsection{Refined potential and exact finite-n calibration}

Use two different names for the cutoffs:

\[
\delta=\epsilon=L^{-12},\qquad d_n=L^{-1/4},\qquad
\tau=1-2\delta,\qquad a=\alpha/(2L).
\]

Here \(d_n\) is a row-mass slack, not the endpoint cutoff. Define

\begin{equation}
V_n(y)=\frac{\alpha}{L(y+\epsilon)}
 \left[\frac12\log(H(y+\epsilon))
       +\log\log(H(y+\epsilon))+c_*-d_n\right],\quad 0\le y\le R,
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-7}
\end{equation}

and

\begin{equation}
B=B_0+a\left[\frac73\ell+2\log(2/3)+2c_*-2d_n\right].
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-8}
\end{equation}

For large \(n\), \(B\) lies in a fixed compact subset of \((0,\infty)\).
Let \(p_*\) be the full Kepler momentum for this \(B\), with

\[
y_*(x)=Y\sin^2 u,\quad x=(u-\sin u\cos u)/\pi,\quad
Y=(2vB/\pi^2)^{1/3},\quad
p_*=-y_*'/v,\quad p_*'=B/y_*^2.
\]

Set

\[
p_n(s)=p_*(\delta+\tau s),\quad A(s)=p_n'(s)>0,\quad
J_n(A)=\min_{0\le y\le R}(Ay+V_n(y)),
\]

and choose \(g_n(1)=0\), \(g_n'=vp_n^2/2-J_n(p_n')\). Then

\[
f_n(s,y)=g_n(s)+p_n(s)y
\]

obeys the exact inequality

\begin{equation}
-\partial_sf_n+\frac v2(\partial_yf_n)^2
 =J_n(A)-Ay\le V_n(y).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-9}
\end{equation}

The potential is strictly convex for large \(n\): its second derivative
has leading positive term proportional to
\(\log(H(y+\epsilon))/(L(y+\epsilon)^3)\), while all remaining terms are
smaller uniformly. Hence \(J_n\) is \(C^1\), including any boundary
regimes, and \(0\le J_n'\le R\). The same exact estimates as in the
second-order calibration argument give

\[
\|p_n\|_\infty=O(L^4),\quad
\|p_n'\|_\infty=O(L^{16}),\quad
\|p_n''\|_\infty=O(L^{28}),
\] \begin{equation}
\|f_n\|_{C^2}=O(L^{28}),\quad
\|(f_n)_s\|_\infty=O(L^{16}),\quad
\|(f_n)_y\|_\infty=O(L^4).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-10}
\end{equation}

For example, \(g_n''=vp_np_n'-J_n'(p_n')p_n''\); no second derivative of
the optimizer is required.

\subsection{Uniform Legendre expansion, including the cutoff endpoints}

For \(u=y+\epsilon\), an exact rearrangement of \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-7} is

\[
V_n(y)=\frac{B+a\log u+R_n(u)}u,
\] \begin{equation}
R_n(u)=2a\log\left(1+\frac{\ell+3\log u}{2L}\right).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-11}
\end{equation}

On \(\epsilon\le u\le R+\epsilon\), the logarithm's argument is positive
and uniformly tends to 1. In this range,

\begin{equation}
|R_n(u)|\le C\frac{\ell+|\log u|}{L^2},\qquad
|(u\partial_u)^jR_n(u)|\le C/L^2\quad (j=1,2).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-12}
\end{equation}

Let \(\lambda=\sqrt{B/A}\), for \(A=A(s)\) along the truncated arch.
Because \(A=\tau B/y_*^2\),

\begin{equation}
\lambda=\frac{y_*(\delta+\tau s)}{\sqrt\tau},\qquad
cL^{-8}\le\lambda\le C_1.
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-13}
\end{equation}

Choose the fixed \(R\) large enough that \(\lambda\le(R+\epsilon)/3\)
for all large \(n\). Also \(\epsilon/\lambda=O(L^{-4})\). Consequently
the admissible domain for \(r=u/\lambda\) contains \([1/2,2]\), and
\(|\log\lambda|=O(\ell)\) uniformly.

After adding \(\epsilon A\) and dividing the objective by \(\sqrt{BA}\),
the minimization becomes

\begin{equation}
\Phi(r)=r+\frac1r+
 \frac{a}{B}\frac{\log\lambda+\log r}{r}
 +\frac{R_n(\lambda r)}{Br}.
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-14}
\end{equation}

Globally on its admissible domain, \(B+a\log u+R_n(u)\ge B/2\) for large
\(n\). Thus \(\Phi(r)\ge r+1/(2r)\), which is at least 4 outside
\([1/8,8]\). At \(r=1\), \(\Phi(1)=2+o(1)\). Therefore its minimizer
belongs to \([1/8,8]\). On that fixed interval the perturbation and its
first two derivatives are bounded by

\[
C\left[\frac{1+|\log\lambda|}{L}
       +\frac{\ell+|\log\lambda|}{L^2}\right]=o(1).
\]

The strict separation of \(r+1/r\) from 2 outside \([1/2,2]\) then puts
the minimizer in \((1/2,2)\). It is an interior minimizer of the
original constrained problem. The derivative equation, and the lower
bound for \(2r^{-3}\) on this compact interval, give

\begin{equation}
r_{\min}=1+O((1+|\log\lambda|)/L).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-15}
\end{equation}

Taylor expansion at 1, using \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-12}, now proves the genuinely
uniform value estimate

\begin{equation}
J_n(A)=2\sqrt{BA}-\epsilon A
       +a\frac{\log\lambda}{\lambda}
       +O\left(\frac{\sqrt A}{L^2}
          [1+\log^2 A+\ell]\right).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-16}
\end{equation}

In particular the perturbation's square, which could have mattered after
endpoint integration, is retained explicitly.

For \(j=0,1,2\), the Kepler endpoint estimates give

\begin{equation}
\int_0^1\sqrt{A(s)}\,[1+|\log A(s)|^j],ds=O(1).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-17}
\end{equation}

To check this uniformly, change variables to \(x=\delta+\tau s\). Near
either endpoint, the full-arch comparison is \(x^{-2/3}(1+|\log x|^j)\),
which is integrable; reparameterization changes the bounds only by fixed
factors. Therefore the integrated error in \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-16} is

\begin{equation}
O((1+\ell)/L^2)=o(1/L).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-18}
\end{equation}

Also

\begin{equation}
\epsilon\int_0^1A(s)\,ds
 =\epsilon[p_n(1)-p_n(0)]=O(L^{-8}).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-19}
\end{equation}

The complete-arch action is

\[
\int_0^1[2\sqrt{Bp_*'}-vp_*^2/2],dx=3B/Y=:C(B).
\]

Cutting off its two ends and applying the factors \(\tau^{-1/2}\) and
\(\tau^{-1}\) changes the action by \(O(\delta^{1/3})=O(L^{-4})\). This
follows by separately bounding both discarded integrands by
\(Cx^{-2/3}\).

Finally,

\[
\int_0^1\frac{\log\lambda(s)}{\lambda(s)}\,ds
 =\frac1{\sqrt\tau}\int_\delta^{1-\delta}
  \frac{\log y_*(x)-\tfrac12\log\tau}{y_*(x)},dx
\] \begin{equation}
 =\frac2Y(\log Y-2\log2)
  +O(\delta^{1/3}(1+|\log\delta|)).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-20}
\end{equation}

Indeed, \(dx/y_*=2\,du/(\pi Y)\), and
\(\int_0^\pi\log\sin u\,du=-\pi\log2\). The omitted logarithmic endpoint
integrals are \(O(\delta^{1/3}(1+|\log\delta|))\), by the same
integrable comparison.

Integrating \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-16} now yields

\begin{equation}
f_n(0,0)=C(B)+a\frac2{Y(B)}(\log Y(B)-2\log2)
          +O\!\left(\frac{1+\ell}{L^2}+L^{-4}\right).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-21}
\end{equation}

Since \(C'(B)=2/Y(B)\) and \(C=\alpha/Y_0\), Taylor expansion of
\eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-21} at \(B_0\) gives

\begin{equation}
f_n(0,0)=C\left[1+\frac{7\ell}{3L}
                      +\frac{\log Y_0-2\log3+2c_*}{L}
                      -\frac{2d_n}{L}\right]
             +O\!\left(\frac{(1+\ell)^2}{L^2}+L^{-4}\right).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-22}
\end{equation}

The identity \(2\log(2/3)-2\log2=-2\log3\) is the source of the constant
in \eqref{a61:o3:up3:target}. The slack costs \(2Cd_n/L=2CL^{-5/4}=o(1/L)\).

\subsection{Quantitatively contracting transformed rows}

Retain source and destination heights in \([1,RH]\), and legal steps
satisfying \(q\le h\) and \(k+e\le n-1\). Let \(s=k/n\), \(y=h/H\),

\[
\eta=F/n,\qquad \beta=F/H,\qquad \beta^2=\eta,\quad \eta H=L.
\]

The transformed step has weight

\begin{equation}
w(e,q,d)\exp\{F[f_n(s,y)-f_n(s+e/n,y+d/H)]\}.
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-23}
\end{equation}

On \(e\le HL^{40}\), \(|d|\le\sqrt H L^{40}\), use \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-10} and
the fact that \(f_n\) is affine in height. The Taylor remainder is at
most

\begin{equation}
CFL^{28}\left[(HL^{40}/n)^2
 +(HL^{40}/n)(L^{40}/\sqrt H)\right]
 \le Cn^{-1/3}L^{110}.
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-24}
\end{equation}

The linear tilts are

\[
\sigma=-\eta(f_n)_s,\qquad \theta=-\beta p_n,
\]

so \(|\sigma|\le Cn^{-2/3}L^{50/3}\), \(|\theta|\le Cn^{-1/3}L^{13/3}\).
In particular \(Q(|\sigma|+|\theta|)=o(1)\), as required for
\eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-4}.

By \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-2} and \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-9}, uniformly for
\(q\le h\le RH\),

\begin{equation}
q\Psi(\sigma,\theta)
 \le\frac{q\eta}{\alpha}V_n(y)+Cn^{-1/3}L^{34}
 =\frac{q}{r}\left[\frac12\log r+\log\log r+c_*-d_n\right]
       +Cn^{-1/3}L^{34},
\quad r=h+\epsilon H.
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-25}
\end{equation}

This estimate does not extend legality: we only enlarge the upper bound
from \(q\le h\) to \(q\le r\). On \(q>Q\), \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-1} gives
\(a(\sigma,\theta)=a_*+O(|\sigma|+|\theta|)\) and \(E_q=O(Q^{-1})\).
Combining \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-4}, \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-5}, \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-24}, and
\eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-25}, the good-step row has mass at most

\begin{equation}
m_*+2a_*e^{c_*-d_n}
 +O\!\left(\frac{\ell}{L}+L^{-2}
              +n^{-1/6}L^5+n^{-1/3}L^{110}\right).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-26}
\end{equation}

All estimates are uniform in the source height and elapsed length. The
head contributes its true critical mass only once. Since
\(2a_*e^{c_*}=1-m_*\), \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-26} is

\begin{equation}
1-(1-m_*)d_n+O(d_n^2)+o(d_n).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-27}
\end{equation}

For completeness, the omitted steps have much smaller mass. The full
transformed exponent is bounded by \(C\eta L^{16}e+C\beta L^{16}|d|\).
For \(e>HL^{40}\), a fixed small extra positive length tilt in the
common analytic neighborhood gives an upper bound

\[
\exp[-cHL^{40}+O(H)].
\]

For \(|d|>\sqrt H L^{40}\), splitting the two signs and adding
\(\theta_0=cL^{40}/\sqrt H\) gives an upper bound

\[
\exp[-cL^{80}+C_Rc^2L^{80}+O(L^{57})].
\]

Choose \(c>0\) fixed and sufficiently small after \(R\); this is at most
\(\exp[-c'L^{80}]\) for large \(n\). All cubic discriminant errors still
tend to zero, since every relevant tilt is \(n^{-1/3}\) or \(n^{-2/3}\)
times a fixed power of \(L\). These bounds use unrestricted destination
sums and hence cover all omitted steps. They are \(o(d_n)\).

Consequently, for some fixed \(b_0>0\), every complete transformed row
has mass at most

\begin{equation}
1-b_0d_n,\qquad
\text{and one may take } b_0=(1-m_*)/2
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-28}
\end{equation}

for all sufficiently large \(n\). This establishes the required
quantitative contraction rather than relying on a qualitative
finite-height limit.

\subsection{Terminal, renewal, and first-hit errors}

The positive transformed Neumann sum is bounded by \((b_0d_n)^{-1}\).
Its logarithm is \(O(\log L)=o(F/L)\).

The terminal representation is exactly the one in the exact positive
macro-kernel: after elapsed macro-length \(k\), its factor is
\(\rho^{n-1-k}t^{1-h}\), apart from the fixed initial factor. Since
\(g_n(1)=0\), \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-10} gives

\[
f_n(s,h/H)\le p_n(1)h/H+CL^{16}(1-s).
\]

Writing \(r_0=n-1-k\ge0\), the terminal product is bounded by a fixed
constant times the exponential of

\[
r_0[\log\rho+O(\eta L^{16})]
+h[-\log t+\beta p_n(1)]+O(\eta L^{16}).
\]

The two small corrections tend to zero, while \(\log\rho<0\) and
\(-\log t<0\). The terminal product is therefore uniformly bounded
independently of \(n,k,h\). The initial-height adjustment is

\[
F[f_n(0,1/H)-f_n(0,0)]=\beta p_n(0)=o(1).
\]

Thus the confined coefficients satisfy

\begin{equation}
a_n^{[\le RH]}\rho^n\le C_2d_n^{-1}e^{-Ff_n(0,0)+o(1)}.
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-29}
\end{equation}

The imported first-hit estimate is

\begin{equation}
a_n^{[>RH]}\rho^n\le C_3\exp[-R^2F/(4D_0)]
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-30}
\end{equation}

for a fixed \(D_0>0\). Choose the fixed \(R\), in addition to the
Legendre-domain requirement above, so that \(R^2/(4D_0)>C+1\). Equation
\eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-22} shows \(f_n(0,0)\to C\), so \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-30} is
exponentially smaller than \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-29}. Taking logarithms after
adding the two positive contributions gives

\begin{equation}
D_n\ge Ff_n(0,0)-O(\log L).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-31}
\end{equation}

Combining \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-22} and \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-31} proves
\eqref{a61:o3:up3:target}. More explicitly, this construction yields

\begin{equation}
D_n\ge CF\left[1+\frac{7\ell}{3L}+\frac\kappa L\right]
 -2CF L^{-5/4}
 -O\!\left(F\frac{(1+\ell)^2}{L^2}+FL^{-4}+\log L\right).
\label{a61:o3:up3:eq-32}
\end{equation}

Every error in \eqref{a61:o3:up3:eq-32} is \(o(F/L)\). No endpoint, terminal,
or geometric-series term remains at the target scale.

\section{A matching coefficient lower bound}
\label{a61:o3:lo3:section}

We prove
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:target}
 D_n\le C_*F\left[1+\frac{7\log L}{3L}+\frac\kappa L\right]
                  +o(F/L).
\end{equation}
The complete row is essential at this precision. At its threshold,
critical small-$q$ transitions retain mass $m_*$, while the remaining
mass $1-m_*$ concentrates near the height cutoff. Discarding the
former would change the desired constant.

Our construction samples independent tilted rows along a deterministic
arch, except for a short central interval. A deterministic patch in
that interval enforces both exact physical degree and exact endpoint
height. Its cost is $o(F/L)$, so no lattice local limit theorem for
an exact global constraint is needed.

\subsection{An interpolated legal cutoff}

Set $h_0=L^{21}$ and $\eta=2/L^2$. For real $h\ge h_0$, put
$u=(1-\eta)h$ and define
\[
 \chi_h(q)=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&q\le\lfloor u\rfloor,\\
  u-\lfloor u\rfloor,&q=\lfloor u\rfloor+1,\\
  0,&q>\lfloor u\rfloor+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
All weights lie in $[0,1]$. They therefore define a lower subkernel of
the exact positive combinatorial kernel whenever its retained $q$
values are legal. The interpolation is a proof device; it does not
assert fractional combinatorial multiplicities.

Throughout this section $e$ is the \emph{total} macro degree, including
the leading $x/(1-x)$ multiplier. Define
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:row}
 R_h(s,\theta)=\sum_{e,q,d}\chi_h(q)w(e,q,d)e^{se+\theta d}.
\end{equation}
Let $V(h)>0$ be the small positive solution of $R_h(V(h),0)=1$, and put
\[
 N(h)=\sum_{e,q,d}q\chi_h(q)w(e,q,d)e^{V(h)e}.
\]
The row is below $1$ at $s=0$, strictly increases in $s$, and exceeds
$1$ in the established endpoint regime. The solution is unique.

The exact local-row result from the pinned second-order report gives,
first in terms of the actual cutoff $u$,
\[
 V(h)=\frac\alpha u
       \left[\frac12\log u+\log\log u+c_*+\epsilon(u)\right],
 \qquad\sup_{u\ge(1-\eta)h_0}|\epsilon(u)|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence, for each fixed $R$, uniformly on $h_0\le h\le RH$,
\begin{align}
 V(h)&=\frac\alpha h
       \left[\frac12\log h+\log\log h+c_*+o(1)\right],
       \label{a61:o3:lo3:potential}\\
 N(h)&=(1-m_*)h[1+o(1)].\label{a61:o3:lo3:qmean}
\end{align}
The cutoff shrink changes the bracket in \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:potential} by
$O(L^{-1})$. For arbitrarily large $h$, we use only
$V(h)\asymp\log h/h$ and the derivative bounds below.

The functions are continuous and piecewise continuously
differentiable. Implicit differentiation on each interpolation
interval gives
\[
 V'(h)=-\frac{(1-\eta)W_{\lfloor u\rfloor+1}(V(h),0)}
                      {(R_h)_s(V(h),0)}.
\]
The differentiated square-root transfer and the positive endpoint
amplitude imply
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:derivative-bounds}
 c\frac{\log h}{h^2}\le -V'(h)\le C\frac{\log h}{h^2},
 \qquad |N'(h)|\le C\log h,
 \qquad ch\le N(h)\le Ch.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the endpoint weight is $\Theta(\log h/h)$, the length
mean $(R_h)_s$ is $\Theta(h)$, the $q$-weighted boundary term is
$O(\log h)$, and the mixed $q$--length moment is $O(h^2)$.
These estimates also hold as one-sided bounds at the junctions.

\subsection{An elementary energy minimum constructs the arch}

For $Y>h_0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:energy-function}
 \mathcal J_n(Y)=nV(Y)+2\sqrt{\frac2v}
             \int_{h_0}^Y\sqrt{V(h)-V(Y)}\,dh.
\end{equation}
It is continuous and tends to infinity as $Y\to\infty$, because the
integral over $[Y/4,Y/2]$ is at least $c\sqrt{Y\log Y}$.
Also $\mathcal J_n(h_0)=nV(h_0)\gg F$.

For any absolutely continuous physical-time trial path $h(t)\ge h_0$
with endpoints $h_0$ and peak $Y$, the square inequality gives
\[
 \frac{\dot h^2}{2v}+V(h)
 \ge V(Y)+|\dot h|\sqrt{\frac{2(V(h)-V(Y))}{v}}.
\]
Its integral is at least $\mathcal J_n(Y)$. The trial obtained by
adding $h_0$ to the leading Kepler arch has action $O(F)$.
Consequently $\mathcal J_n$ has an interior global minimum with
value $O(F)$.

At a smooth point, differentiating the integral gives
\[
 \mathcal J_n'(Y)=V'(Y)
 \left[n-\sqrt{\frac2v}\int_{h_0}^Y
                       (V(h)-V(Y))^{-1/2}\,dh\right].
\]
The derivative under the integral is dominated locally by a constant
times $(Y-h)^{-1/2}$. At a cutoff junction, the two one-sided
derivatives have the same bracket and strictly negative factors
$V'$. An interior minimum therefore satisfies, in either case,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:duration}
 n=\sqrt{\frac2v}\int_{h_0}^Y(V(h)-V(Y))^{-1/2}\,dh.
\end{equation}
Choose such a minimizing $Y$, and write $\lambda=V(Y)$.

Construct the ascending physical-time path by
$\dot h=\sqrt{2v(V(h)-\lambda)}$ and reflect it about its peak.
Equation~\eqref{a61:o3:lo3:duration} makes its duration exactly $n$.
It attains equality in the square inequality, so its action is
$\mathcal J_n(Y)$ and is no greater than any trial action.
No nonsmooth Euler--Lagrange theorem or derivative of the $o(1)$
remainder in \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:potential} is being invoked.

Its peak satisfies $Y=\Theta(H)$. The upper bound follows from
kinetic coercivity and action $O(F)$. If $Y\le\sqrt n$, then
$nV(Y)\gg F$, a contradiction. Thus $\log Y\ge L/2$ eventually,
and $nV(Y)=O(F)$ then gives $Y\ge cH$.

Along this path define the signed momentum
\[
 \theta=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2(V(h)-\lambda)}v},
 \qquad \frac{dt}{dj}=\frac{N(h)}\alpha.
\]
Its macro-coordinate velocity is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:macro-flow}
 \frac{dh}{dj}=\frac v\alpha N(h)\theta.
\end{equation}
The continuous macro duration is
\[
 m=2\int_{h_0}^Y
 \frac{dh}{(v/\alpha)N(h)\sqrt{2(V(h)-V(Y))/v}}
 =\Theta\!\left(\sqrt{Y/\log Y}\right)=\Theta(F/L).
\]
For this estimate, below $Y/2$ the potential difference is comparable
to $\log h/h$; above $Y/2$ it is comparable to
$(L/Y^2)(Y-h)$, by \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:derivative-bounds}.

Round $m$ to an integer $M$ and reparameterize to $j\in[0,M]$,
changing the speed by $1+O(M^{-1})$. Denote its sampled heights and
momenta by $h_j$ and $\theta_j$. The profile is symmetric,
$h_j=h_{M-j}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:profile-regularity}
 |\theta_j|\le C\sqrt{L/h_j},\qquad
 |\theta'(j)|\le CL/h_j,\qquad |h''(j)|\le CL^2.
\end{equation}
Before reparameterization,
$\theta'=N(h)V'(h)/\alpha$. Furthermore, with
$f=(v/\alpha)N\theta$,
\[
 ff'=(v/\alpha)^2
       [NN'\theta^2+N^2V'/v]=O(L^2).
\]
The apparent square-root singularity at the peak cancels in these
macro-coordinate expressions.

\subsection{Independent complete row laws}

At position $j$, take the probability law on $(e,q,d)$ proportional
to
\[
 \chi_{h_j}(q)w(e,q,d)e^{\lambda e+\theta_jd},
\]
and call its row mass $R_j$. Uniform differentiated transfer gives
\begin{align}
 R_j&=1+O(L^{3/2}/\sqrt{h_j})=1+o(1),
                 \label{a61:o3:lo3:row-comparison}\\
 \mathbb Ed_j&=(v/\alpha)N(h_j)\theta_j+O(L^2),
                 \label{a61:o3:lo3:drift}\\
 \mathbb Ee_j&=N(h_j)/\alpha+O(L^{3/2}\sqrt{h_j}).
                 \label{a61:o3:lo3:degree-mean}
\end{align}
Here is the comparison underlying these statements. Uniformly for
all $q$, after including the finitely many small indices, the
reduced analytic factor $W_qe^{-q\Psi}$ has bounded logarithmic
first derivatives. Since
$\lambda+v\theta_j^2/2=V(h_j)$,
\[
 \Psi(\lambda,\theta_j)-\Psi(V(h_j),0)
       =O((L/h_j)^{3/2}).
\]
Multiplication by $q\le h_j$ yields
\eqref{a61:o3:lo3:row-comparison}. The derivative
$\Psi_\theta=\nu\theta+O(\lambda+\theta^2)$, with
$\nu=v/\alpha$, gives \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:drift}; the change of the $q$ mean
costs at most
$O(\theta_jh_jL^{3/2}/\sqrt{h_j})=O(L^2)$.
Likewise $\Psi_s=\alpha^{-1}+O(|\theta|+\lambda)$ gives
\eqref{a61:o3:lo3:degree-mean}.

The centered logarithmic moment generating functions satisfy
\begin{align}
 \log\mathbb Ee^{z(d_j-\mathbb Ed_j)}
     &\le Cz^2h_jL,
          &&|z|\le c/\sqrt{h_jL},\label{a61:o3:lo3:height-mgf}\\
 \log\mathbb Ee^{z(e_j-\mathbb Ee_j)}
     &\le Cz^2h_j^2,
          &&|z|\le c/h_j.\label{a61:o3:lo3:length-mgf}
\end{align}
To verify these bounds, differentiate the exact normalized row twice
throughout the stated extra-tilt intervals. At fixed $q$, increment
variance is $O(q)$ and conditional mean is
$O(q\sqrt{L/h_j})$; variation of this mean over $q\le h_j$ adds at
most $O(h_jL)$. Length has conditional variance $O(q)$ and conditional
mean $O(q)$, so its total variance is $O(h_j^2)$. The same bounds
persist under the extra tilt. Taylor's theorem for the logarithmic
row proves \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:height-mgf}--\eqref{a61:o3:lo3:length-mgf}.

\subsection{Sample both ends and leave a central gap}

Let $r$ be the smallest integer at least $M^{3/4}$ with $r\equiv M\pmod 2$, and put
$a=(M-r)/2$, $b=(M+r)/2$. Leave positions $a,\ldots,b-1$ unsampled.
Sample all others independently from the row laws. View the left
part forward from seed height $\lfloor h_0\rfloor$ and the right
part backward from that same terminal seed height. With probability
tending to one, every sampled boundary has error
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:tube}
 |E_j|\le h_j/(10L^2).
\end{equation}
We give quantitative bounds for this assertion.

On the ascending half while $h\le Y/2$,
$f(h)\ge c\sqrt{h\log h}$. The number of preceding positions is
$O(\sqrt{h/\log h})$, and
\[
 \sum_{i<j}h_iL\le CLh^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log h}.
\]
By \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:drift} and \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:profile-regularity}, the
accumulated mean mismatch from the deterministic profile is bounded by
\[
 CL^2\sqrt{h/\log h}+O(h/M)=o(h/L^2),
 \qquad h\ge h_0=L^{21}.
\]
Bernstein's inequality from \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:height-mgf}, at threshold
$h/(20L^2)$, has exponent at least
\[
 c\sqrt{h\log h}/L^5
     \ge cL^{11/2}\sqrt{\log L}.
\]
Near the peak, use total variance $O(nL)$ and threshold $cH/L^2$;
the exponent is at least $cF/L^5\gg L$. A union bound over
$M=\exp(O(L))$ boundaries still tends to zero. The right half uses
its complementary suffix from the fixed terminal height, with the
same estimates. No conditioning is used.

Every retained $q$ satisfies
$\lceil(1-2/L^2)h_j\rceil\le h_j-h_j/L^2$ eventually, so
\eqref{a61:o3:lo3:tube} makes it legal at the actual starting height.
Positivity of the boundary heights follows as well.

Two further estimates hold together with probability tending to one:
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:repair-fluctuations}
 \left|\sum e_j-\sum\mathbb Ee_j\right|\le LH\sqrt M,
 \qquad |\Delta|\le L\sqrt{nL}.
\end{equation}
The sums run over sampled positions and $\Delta$ is the exact height
difference between the two central endpoints. Chebyshev's inequality
already suffices: the degree variance is $O(MH^2)$ and height variance
is $O(nL)$. The total drift bias $O(ML^2)$ is $o(\sqrt{nL})$.
The parity choice makes the deterministic heights at $a$ and $b$
exactly equal.

\subsection{Repair physical degree and height exactly}

Let $X$ be the sampled total degree. The ascent seed, final descent
seed and original first letter have total degree
$2\lfloor h_0\rfloor+1$. Define the required patch degree
\[
 D=n-(2\lfloor h_0\rfloor+1)-X.
\]
The complete sum of the model means $N(h_j)/\alpha$ differs from its
physical-time integral $n$ by $O(HL)$: total variation of $N$ is
$O(HL)$ by \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:derivative-bounds}. The cumulative error in
\eqref{a61:o3:lo3:degree-mean} is also $O(HL)$, since
$\sum_j\sqrt{h_j}=O(M\sqrt H)$.
The unsampled middle has model degree
$r(1-m_*)Y/\alpha[1+o(1)]$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:patch-degree}
 \frac D{rY}\longrightarrow\frac{1-m_*}\alpha
\end{equation}
uniformly on the event \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:repair-fluctuations}, because
$r\gg L\sqrt M$ and $r\gg L$. In particular $D>0$.

Choose integer patch total degrees $L_i$, differing by at most one,
with sum $D$. For the local positive Gaussian estimate use internal
block degrees $\ell_i=L_i-1$ and retain its leading $x$ term.
Thus the sampled rows and the patch both count full physical degree
exactly; there is no unaccounted one-unit shift per sampled block.
Choose integer increments $d_i$, differing by at most one, with
sum $\Delta$. Linear interpolation keeps all patch heights at
$Y[1+o(1)]$.

By \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:patch-degree}, every shifted $q$ saddle is
$(1-m_*)Y[1+o(1)]$, with a fixed positive legality margin below
those heights. Moreover
$|d_i|\le |\Delta|/r+1=o(\sqrt{YL})$.
The audited local lower estimate therefore supplies a patch whose
critical logarithmic cost is at most
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:patch-cost}
 CrL+C\frac{\Delta^2}{rY}
    \le CrL+C\frac{ML^3}{r}=o(M).
\end{equation}
Its exact degree and endpoint height are the prescribed ones for
every retained outside realization.

There is no overcounting. The total number $M$ and the patch positions
are fixed; deleting those positions from a completed marked
macro-path recovers its outside data. Selecting the patch degree and
increment pattern, then summing its legal $q$ windows, retains a
subset of the exact positive macro-path expansion. Every outside
fractional $\chi$ factor is at most one. Thus these weighted
contributions are genuine lower bounds for the original coefficient.
The exact seeds cost $O(h_0)=o(M)$.

\subsection{Remove the tilts}

The sampled product of subkernel critical weights
$\chi_{h_j}(q_j)w(e_j,q_j,d_j)$ equals its product probability
multiplied by
\[
 \exp\left\{-\lambda\sum e_j-\sum\theta_jd_j\right\}
                      \prod R_j.
\]
Since every $\chi_{h_j}$ lies in $[0,1]$, this product is a lower
bound on the corresponding product of original critical weights.
By \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:row-comparison}, $\sum\log R_j=o(M)$.
The difference between $\lambda\sum e_j$ and $\lambda n$ is
$O(rL)+\operatorname{polylog}(n)=o(M)$.
Put $g_j=h_{j+1}-h_j$. On each sampled interval, summation by parts
and \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:tube} give
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:tilt-cancellation}
 \sum\theta_j(d_j-g_j)=o(M).
\end{equation}
The interior bound is
$C\sum(L/h_j)(h_j/L^2)=O(M/L)$.
At the central edges,
$\theta=O((r/M)\sqrt{L/H})$ and $|E|\le CH/L^2$, giving
$O(r/L)$. Seed-boundary terms are polylogarithmic.

The omitted middle deterministic action is $O(rL)=o(M)$. A Stieltjes
quadrature estimate gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:action-sum}
 \sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\theta_jg_j
  =2\sqrt{\frac2v}\int_{h_0}^Y\sqrt{V(h)-V(Y)}\,dh+o(M).
\end{equation}
Its error is bounded by
$C\int |\theta'||h'|\,dj\le C\int_{h_0}^Y(L/h)\,dh=O(L^2)$,
plus endpoint roundings.
Summing the retained independent realizations, whose probability is
$1-o(1)$, and inserting the patch and seeds proves
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:action-bound}
 D_n\le\mathcal J_n(Y)+o(M),\qquad M=\Theta(F/L).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Evaluate a fixed trial action}

No perturbed-peak expansion is required. By construction,
$\mathcal J_n(Y)$ is no greater than the action of
\[
 h_{\rm trial}(t)=h_0+H y_*(t/n),\qquad
 y_*(s)=Y_0\sin^2u,\quad
 s=\frac{u-\sin u\cos u}{\pi},
\]
where $Y_0=(2v\alpha/(3\pi^2))^{1/3}$.
Uniformly on $L^{-6}\le y\le R$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:o3:lo3:trial-potential}
 \frac nF V(Hy)=\frac{\alpha}{3y}
  +\frac{\alpha}{2Ly}
    \left[\frac73\log L+\log y+2\log(2/3)+2c_*+o(1)\right].
\end{equation}
The row-threshold error is uniform, and the elementary log-log
expansion has an additional bracket error
$O((\log L+|\log y|)/L)=o(1)$ in this range.
Adding $h_0$ changes the resulting bulk integral by $o(F/L)$.

The trial portions $y\le L^{-6}$ cost only
$O(F/L^3)+\operatorname{polylog}(n)=o(F/L)$.
For the potential, use $V(h)\le C\log h/h$ and
$y_*(s)\asymp s^{2/3}$; the portion with $Hy_*\le h_0$ costs only
a polynomial in $L$. The kinetic endpoint integral has the same
$O(F/L^3)$ bound. Thus \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:trial-potential} may be integrated
along the full leading arch at $o(F/L)$ error.

Its leading kinetic-plus-potential action is $C_*F$, and
\[
 \frac\alpha2\int_0^1\frac{ds}{y_*(s)}=C_*,\qquad
 \frac{\int_0^1(\log y_*)\,ds/y_*}
      {\int_0^1ds/y_*}=\log Y_0-2\log2.
\]
Indeed $ds/y_*=2\,du/(\pi Y_0)$. The trial action is consequently
\[
 C_*F\left[1+\frac{7\log L}{3L}
       +\frac{\log Y_0-2\log3+2c_*}{L}\right]+o(F/L).
\]
Combined with \eqref{a61:o3:lo3:action-bound}, this proves
\eqref{a61:o3:lo3:target}. The $o(F/L)$ error includes all endpoint,
normalization, fluctuation and exact-degree repair costs.

\section{The inverse constant}
\label{a61:o3:inv3:section}

Let $\lambda_0=\log\mu$ and define the integer threshold
$N(Y)=\min\{n:a_n\ge Y\}$. The frozen foundation proves that $a_n$
is nondecreasing, by appending a copy of its last letter.
Put
\[
 B_*=C_*\lambda_0^{-4/3},\qquad
 \kappa_{\rm inv}=\kappa-\frac23\log\lambda_0.
\]
Numerically,
\begin{align*}
 B_*&=0.893568257650894909124642189453\ldots,\\
 \kappa_{\rm inv}&=-2.398003540515347428750752039232\ldots.
\end{align*}

\begin{corollary}\label{a61:o3:inv3:theorem}
With $T=\log Y$,
\begin{align}
 N(Y)={}&\frac{T}{\lambda_0}
 +B_*T^{1/3}(\log T)^{2/3}\notag\\
 &+\frac{7B_*}{3}T^{1/3}(\log T)^{-1/3}\log\log T
 +B_*\kappa_{\rm inv}T^{1/3}(\log T)^{-1/3}\notag\\
 &+o\!\left(T^{1/3}(\log T)^{-1/3}\right).
 \label{a61:o3:inv3:expansion}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write $F_T=T^{1/3}(\log T)^{2/3}$ and
$G_T=T^{1/3}(\log T)^{-1/3}$. Uniformly for
$x=T/\lambda_0+O(F_T)$,
\[
 x^{1/3}(\log x)^{2/3}
 =\lambda_0^{-1/3}
       \left[F_T-\frac23(\log\lambda_0)G_T+o(G_T)\right],
\]
\[
 x^{1/3}(\log x)^{-1/3}\log\log x
 =\lambda_0^{-1/3}G_T\log\log T+o(G_T),
 \quad
 x^{1/3}(\log x)^{-1/3}=\lambda_0^{-1/3}G_T+o(G_T).
\]
The feedback from changing $x$ by $O(F_T)$ is
$O(F_T^2/T)=O(T^{-1/3}(\log T)^{4/3})=o(1)$.
Thus substituting the real expression on the right of
\eqref{a61:o3:inv3:expansion}, without its remainder, into the smooth
asymptotic formula for $\log a_n$ gives $T+o(G_T)$.
For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, round that expression minus
$\varepsilon G_T$ downward and plus $\varepsilon G_T$ upward.
Theorem~\ref{a61:o3:thm:main} puts the corresponding logarithmic coefficients
strictly below and above $T$ for large $T$. Monotonicity brackets
$N(Y)$ between them. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the
corollary. The integer rounding error is $O(1)=o(G_T)$.
\end{proof}

The shift $-(2/3)\log\lambda_0$ is necessary: it comes from
$\log(T/\lambda_0)=\log T-\log\lambda_0$. It was hidden inside the
previous $O(G_T)$ inverse remainder and becomes visible at the present
precision.
\begin{writenote}
Here $T=\log Y$ (Part~\ref{a61:part:ao} writes $T=\log p$). This corollary is the case $J=1$ of Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:inverse}, with the same constant $\kappa_{\rm inv}$; the previous inverse result is Corollary~\ref{a61:o2:cor:inverse}.
\end{writenote}



\section{Meaning and limits of the result}
The lower construction gives a variational interpretation at the
precision proved here. With the interpolated cutoff potential $V$
from Section~\ref{a61:o3:lo3:section}, endpoints $h_0=L^{21}$, and physical
time interval $[0,n]$, the energy minimum satisfies
\[
 D_n=\min_{h(0)=h(n)=h_0,\ h\ge h_0}
       \int_0^n\left[\frac{\dot h^2}{2v}+V(h)\right]dt
       +o(F/L).
\]
Indeed, the coefficient lower construction bounds $D_n$ above by this
minimum plus $o(F/L)$; the fixed Kepler trial bounds the minimum by
the right side of Theorem~\ref{a61:o3:thm:main}; and the coefficient upper
bound matches that value. This is a finite-precision action statement,
not an all-orders reduction.

The exact local constant $c_*$ enters through the mass needed to fill
the defective row $m_*<1$. The remaining term
$\log Y_0-2\log3$ comes from the weighted logarithmic height along the
Kepler arch. The weight makes its angular parameter uniform, so
$\log\sin^2u$ contributes $-2\log2$.

The theorem still leaves an unquantified $o(F/L)$ error in the logarithm,
which can diverge. It does not imply a multiplicative equivalent for
$a_n$, a power-law prefactor, a fluctuation determinant, or an exact
integer inverse rule. Higher inverse-log terms are a possible next
research direction, but no all-orders expansion or transseries is
asserted in this report. No unreviewed formal hierarchy is included in
the proved theorem or its reproducibility certificates.
\begin{writenote}
The higher inverse-log terms are established, for every fixed order, in Part~\ref{a61:part:ao}. No all-orders expansion uniform in the order, and no transseries, is asserted there either.
\end{writenote}

\section{The sequence and the main theorem}
An ascent sequence is a word of nonnegative integers with $w_1=0$ and
\[
 w_i\le 1+\#\{j<i-1:w_j<w_{j+1}\}\qquad(i\ge2).
\]
It avoids classical 120 if there are no indices $i<j<k$ with
$w_k<w_i<w_j$. Let $a_n$ count these words, with $a_0=1$; this is
OEIS A202061 \cite{OEIS}. All logarithms in this report are natural.

Let $z_*$ be the smallest positive root of
$z^3-5z^2+6z-1=0$. Define the critical constants
\begin{align*}
 \rho&=3z_*^2-10z_*+2,&\mu&=\rho^{-1},&t_*&=z_*^{-1},\\
 \alpha&=\frac{8z_*^2-29z_*+9}{7},&
 v&=\frac{2z_*^2-7z_*+2}{2},\\
 m_*&=\frac{\rho}{(1-\rho)z_*+\rho},&
 C_q&=\frac{\sqrt{-16z_*^2+55z_*-10}}{2\sqrt\pi},\\
 a_*&=\frac{\rho t_* C_q}{1-\rho},&
 c_*&=\log\frac{1-m_*}{2a_*}.
\end{align*}
Here $\rho,\alpha,v,a_*>0$, $\mu>1$, and $0<m_*<1$.
The constants in the answer are
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:constants}
 Y_0=\left(\frac{2v\alpha}{3\pi^2}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
 C=\frac{\alpha}{Y_0},\qquad
 \kappa=\log Y_0-2\log3+2c_*.
\end{equation}
Their numerical values include
\begin{align*}
 Y_0&=0.228432139006429054268160391628\ldots,\\

\begin{theorem}[All finite inverse-logarithmic orders]\label{a61:ao:thm:main}
There are universal polynomials $P_j(B)$, independent of all the
critical constants, such that for each fixed integer $J\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:main}
 \frac{D_n}{CF}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{P_j(B)}{L^j}+o(L^{-J})
 \qquad(n\longrightarrow\infty\text{ through integers}).
\end{equation}
Here $P_0=1$, $\deg P_j=j$, and
\[
 [B^j]P_j(B)=\binom{2/3}{j}(3/2)^j\qquad(j\ge1).
\]
A finite recurrence generating all $P_j$ is given in
Section~\ref{a61:ao:sec:recurrence}. In particular,
\[
 P_1(B)=B,\qquad P_2(B)=-\frac{B^2}{4}+\frac{7B}{2}-10.
\]
\end{theorem}

logarithmic scales would split one polynomial layer into several terms;
we consistently index whole powers of $L^{-1}$.

The quantifiers are essential: $J$ is chosen first, and all auxiliary

provide an $o(1)$ error in $\log a_n$.

\section{Positive kernel and imported foundation}\label{a61:ao:sec:kernel}
We state the enumerative and analytic inputs explicitly. Their proofs,
exact identities, and earlier mathematical reviews are retained in the
unchanged third-order dependency (Part~\ref{a61:part:o3} of this report, and through it Parts~\ref{a61:part:fd}--\ref{a61:part:o2}). This report proves the
stronger reduction and subsequent all-finite-orders calculation from
those inputs; it does not rederive the entire enumerative foundation.
The audits in the companion package are research verification, not
journal peer review or proof-assistant certification.

The exact positive height-walk representation has suffix series
\[
 e_h=\frac1{1-x}+\frac{x}{1-x}\sum_{q=1}^h(Q_q(x,T)e)_{h+1-q},
 \qquad (Tf)_h=f_{h+1},\qquad A(x)=1+xe_1(x).
\]
Here $Q(x;u,T)=\sum_{q\ge1}Q_q(x,T)u^q$ is the root vanishing at
$u=0$ of
\[
 xT(1-u)Q^2+[(1-x)(u-(1-x))+xT(x-u)]Q+(1-x)u=0.
\]
A macro-step with indices $q,r$ changes height by $d=1-q+r$, has
weight $x[T_0^r]Q_q(x,T_0)/(1-x)$, and is legal exactly when $q$ does
not exceed its starting height. Its destination is then positive.
If $k(e,q,d)$ is its multiplicity at total macro degree $e$, put
\[
 w(e,q,d)=k(e,q,d)\rho^e t_*^d,\qquad
 W_q(s,\theta)=\sum_{e,d}w(e,q,d)e^{se+\theta d}.
\]
The degree $e$ includes the leading multiplier. The critical row mass
is $\sum_qW_q(0,0)=m_*<1$. This missing mass is important; it must not
be lost by replacing complete rows with only their large-$q$ tails.

The quadratic branch supplies the uniformly differentiated analytic
square-root transfer
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:transfer}
 W_q(s,\theta)=a(s,\theta)q^{-3/2}e^{q\Psi(s,\theta)}
 [1+E_q(s,\theta)],\qquad a(0,0)=a_*,
\end{equation}
where $a$ and $\Psi$ are analytic near $(0,0)$, and $E_q=O(q^{-1})$
with the parameter derivatives used below. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:Psi}
 \Psi(s,\theta)=\frac{s+v\theta^2/2}{\alpha}
 +O(s^2+|s\theta|+|\theta|^3).
\end{equation}
The imported transfer controls the first four parameter derivatives.
Together with finitely many positive small-$q$ terms, it gives uniformly
bounded first and second logarithmic derivatives of the reduced weights
$W_qe^{-q\Psi}$ for every $q\ge1$. Higher finite inverse-log orders do
not require arbitrarily many derivatives of this exact discrete row:
only the scalar endpoint Laplace expansion is taken to higher orders.

The exact repair uses the local positive Gaussian lower bound. For
fixed $K_0>3$, internal block degree $\ell\ge L^{K_0}$, and
$|d|\le C_0\sqrt{\ell L}$, summing a fixed sufficiently wide
$O(\sqrt\ell)$ conditional $q$ window gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:patch}
 \sum_q\rho^\ell t_*^d B(\ell,q,q+d-1)
 \ge c\ell^{-2}\exp\left[-\frac{d^2}{2v\ell}-o(1)\right].
\end{equation}
Its center is $q=\alpha\ell+\beta_0(d-1)$, where
$\beta_0=-4(z_*-3)(2z_*-1)/7$; all retained $q$ differ from
$\alpha\ell$ by $O(\sqrt{\ell L})$. The leading $x$ term makes the
whole macro degree $\ell+1$. The bound follows from a positive
summand, uniform Stirling estimates, and the exact Hessian identity;
it does not assume a full coefficient equivalent.

At elapsed macro degree $k$ and height $h$, the exact residual terminal
factor is $\rho^{n-1-k}t_*^{1-h}$, apart from the fixed original leading
$\rho$. Finally, for some fixed $D_0>0$, the first-hit estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:firsthit}
 a_n^{[>RH]}\rho^n\le C_0\exp[-R^2F/(4D_0)]
\end{equation}
for fixed $R$ and sufficiently large $n$. These are the full imported
positive-kernel dependencies used below. No unproved finite-height
spectral equivalence is invoked.

\section{Reduction to a classical action}\label{a61:ao:sec:reduction}
Write $\Srow(r)=\sum_{j\ge0}(3/2)_jr^j$ as a formal series. The symbol
$(3/2)_j$ is the rising factorial. For $T$ large set $A=\log T+c_*$
and define the explicit smooth truncation
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:kN}
 k_N(T)=\frac T2+\log T+c_*+
 \sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{p_j(\log T+c_*)}{T^j},\qquad
 \phi_N(h)=\frac{\alpha k_N(\log h)}h.
\end{equation}
The polynomials $p_j$ are uniquely determined by substitution into
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:localimplicit}
 k-\frac T2-\log k+\log\Srow(1/k)
 +\log\frac{a_*}{1-m_*}=0.
\end{equation}
For example,
\begin{align*}
 p_1(A)&=2A-3,\\
 p_2(A)&=-2A^2+10A-\tfrac{33}{2},\\
 p_3(A)&=\tfrac83A^3-24A^2+86A-120.
\end{align*}
\begin{writenote}
Equation \eqref{a61:ao:eq:localimplicit} is the linear--logarithmic equation $aX+b\log X=L$ with $a=1$, $b=-1$, perturbed by $\log\mathcal S(1/k)$; its large solution is the Lambert-core theorem \texttt{p0:thm:lambert-core} of the transseries volume (see the Guide). No novelty is claimed for this step.
\end{writenote}

Choose a fixed $h_b>1$, after $N$, so that $\phi_N>0$,
$\phi_N'<0$, and $\phi_N''>0$ on $[h_b,\infty)$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:actiondef}
 \A_N(n)=\min_{\substack{h(0)=h(n)=h_b\\h\ge h_b}}
 \int_0^n\left[\frac{\dot h^2}{2v}+\phi_N(h)\right]\dd t,
\end{equation}
over absolutely continuous paths with finite action. The logarithmic
formula is never used down to height zero. Different sufficiently
large fixed $h_b$ change the minimum by $O(1)$.

\begin{theorem}[Tunable action reduction]\label{a61:ao:thm:reduction}
For every fixed integer $K\ge0$, choosing $N=K+3$ gives
\[
 D_n=\A_N(n)+o(M_0/L^K).
\]
\end{theorem}
The following subsections prove the theorem with explicit error rates.
Letters used as local cutoff exponents are subscripted to distinguish
them from the polynomial argument $B$.
\begin{writenote}
\emph{A tempting false reading.} $B=\kappa+\frac73\log L=\kappa+\frac73\log\log n$ is not a constant: it grows without bound, and each $P_j(B)/L^j$ is a polynomial in $\log\log n$ divided by $(\log n)^j$. It is unrelated to the block coefficient $B(\ell,q,q+d-1)$ in \eqref{a61:ao:eq:patch} (Part~\ref{a61:part:fd}'s $B(\ell,q,r)$), to the calibration bound $B$ of Part~\ref{a61:part:sh}, to $B_n$ of Part~\ref{a61:part:o2}, and to the constant $B_*=0.8935\ldots$ of Part~\ref{a61:part:o3}.
\end{writenote}

\subsection{Quantitative local Watson expansion}
Let $R_r(s,\theta)$ denote the exact row with all destinations retained
and fractional $q$ cutoff at real $r$: the cutoff $\chi_r(q)$ is one
through $\lfloor r\rfloor$, equals $r-\lfloor r\rfloor$ at
$\lfloor r\rfloor+1$, and is zero thereafter. The tilt $s$ counts the
entire macro degree. For $T=\log r$ and
$k=r\Psi(s,\theta)=T/2+\log T+O(1)$, every fixed $j_0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:Watson}
 \begin{split}
 R_r(s,\theta)={}&m_*+
 a_*\frac{e^{k-T/2}}k
 \left[\sum_{j=0}^{j_0}(3/2)_jk^{-j}+O_{j_0}(k^{-j_0-1})\right]\\
 &+O_{j_0}(r^{-1/4}T^{c_{j_0}}+|s|+|\theta|).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The same estimate holds uniformly within a fixed distance of this
band, with any fixed number of $k$ derivatives of the normalized
scalar endpoint expansion. Only the first two parameter derivatives
of the exact row are needed in the other arguments.

Here is a proof that controls the critical head. The reduced-factor
comparison following \eqref{a61:ao:eq:transfer}, on a row of bounded total
mass, permits replacement of the reduced weights by critical weights
with error $O(|s|+|\theta|)$. Split the scalar sum at $r/k^2$ and
$r/2$. On the first part, $e^{kq/r}-1\le C_0kq/r$; summing the
critical $q^{-3/2}$ tail gives a perturbation $O(r^{-1/2})$, while the
missing critical tail is $O(kr^{-1/2})$. The middle part is at most
\[
 C_0\frac{k}{\sqrt r}e^{k/2}=O(r^{-1/4}T^{c_0}).
\]
On the last half, the Riemann-sum and fractional-last-term error is
relative $O(k/r)$, and the transfer remainder costs relative $O(1/r)$.
The resulting endpoint integral is
\[
 r^{-1/2}e^k\int_0^{1/2}(1-u)^{-3/2}e^{-ku}\dd u.
\]
Taylor expansion of $(1-u)^{-3/2}$ and integration of each $u^j$ give
$(3/2)_j k^{-j-1}$. The integrated remainder is $O(k^{-j_0-2})$;
extending the moment integrals to infinity adds an exponentially
small term. Differentiating the normalized integral in $k$ inserts
powers of $u$, giving the claimed differentiated remainders. All
estimates are uniform under fractional cutoff interpolation.

There is a unique small positive root $s_r$ with $R_r(s_r,0)=1$.
The imported first approximation places its $k$ in the stated band.
Taking logarithms in \eqref{a61:ao:eq:Watson}, and using the implicit
$k$ derivative $1+O(1/T)$, proves
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:rowroot}
 \frac{rs_r}{\alpha}=k_N(T)
 +O_N\left(\frac{(1+\log T)^{N+1}}{T^{N+1}}\right)
 +O_N(r^{-1/4}T^{c_N}).
\end{equation}
Indeed finite substitution leaves this residual and the implicit
derivative is bounded below by $1/2$. The discrepancy
$r\Psi(s_r,0)-rs_r/\alpha=O(T^2/r)$ is absorbed in the algebraic
error. Direct differentiation of the explicit truncation gives, for
fixed $j$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:potentialderivative}
 \phi_N^{(j)}(h)=\frac\alpha2(-1)^j j!\frac{\log h}{h^{j+1}}
 [1+O_{N,j}(\log\log h/\log h)].
\end{equation}
This proves the asserted eventual positivity, decrease, and convexity.

The exact fractional-cutoff root is continuous and piecewise $C^1$;
it need not be $C^2$ at integer knots. The lower construction uses
only its audited one-sided first derivative bounds. Smooth higher
derivatives belong to \eqref{a61:ao:eq:kN} or a finite scalar surrogate,
never to an unjustifiably differentiated discrete remainder.

We also need a quantitative row gap. Fix $K$ and $E$; let
$N=K+3$, $\delta=L^{-K-2}$, and
$HL^{-E}\le r\le(R+1)H$. If the tilts are a negative fixed power of
$n$ times a fixed power of $L$ and
\[
 r\Psi(s,\theta)\le k_N(\log r)-\delta+o(\delta),
\]
then, uniformly,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:rowgap}
 R_r(s,\theta)\le1-c\delta.
\end{equation}
To see this even if the actual $k$ is far below the endpoint band,
first dominate the row by $1+O(|s|+|\theta|)$ times the positive,
increasing scalar envelope
\[
 G_r(k)=\sum_q\chi_r(q)W_q(0,0)e^{kq/r}.
\]
Monotonicity permits replacement of $k$ by its upper bound in the
endpoint band. There, \eqref{a61:ao:eq:rowroot} has error $o(\delta)$, the
envelope mass is bounded, and the $k$ derivative tends to
$1-m_*>0$. The envelope root differs from the exact row root by an
algebraically small error. The mean-value theorem now proves
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:rowgap}.

\subsection{Action facts and endpoint bounds}
The following estimates apply to $V=\phi_N$, and their coarse versions
also apply to the exact fractional-cutoff potential. For endpoint
$a$ and proposed peak $Y$, put
\[
 J_a(Y)=nV(Y)+2\sqrt{2/v}\int_a^Y\sqrt{V(h)-V(Y)}\dd h.
\]
The pointwise square inequality shows that any path with this peak has
action at least $J_a(Y)$. At an interior minimum of $J_a$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:durationgeneral}
 n=\sqrt{2/v}\int_a^Y[V(h)-V(Y)]^{-1/2}\dd h.
\end{equation}
The rising solution
$\dot h=\sqrt{2v[V(h)-V(Y)]}$, reflected at its peak, has this exact
duration and action. The minimum exists: $J_a(a)=nV(a)$ is too large,
while $J_a(Y)\to\infty$ as $Y\to\infty$. At a fractional-cutoff
junction the one-sided derivatives of $J_a$ have strictly negative
potential factors multiplying the same continuous duration bracket;
thus the argument still supplies an exact-duration arch.

For fixed $a$, for $a$ a fixed power of $L$, or for
$a=H\epsilon$ with $\epsilon=L^{-E}$, a shifted or regularized Kepler
trial has action $O(F)$. Kinetic coercivity bounds the peak above by
$O(H)$; the lower bound $nV(Y)$ bounds it below by a positive multiple
of $H$. Thus $Y\asymp H$. Convexity of $\phi_N$ gives a unique smooth
minimizing arch, symmetric about its midpoint and strictly concave in
its interior.

For $h\le Y/2$, $V(h)-V(Y)\asymp\log h/h$, once $h_b$ is large.
For $Y/2\le h\le Y$ the difference is comparable to
$(L/Y^2)(Y-h)$. The energy formula therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \int_{\{h\le a\}}[\dot h^2/(2v)+V(h)]\dd t
 &\le C_0\sqrt{a\log a},\qquad h_b\le a\le Y/2,
 \label{a61:ao:eq:endpointaction}\\
 \int_0^n\frac{\dd t}{h(t)}&\le C_0n/H=C_0M_0.
 \label{a61:ao:eq:reciprocal}
\end{align}
For the second bound, split the height integral at $Y/L^{C_1}$,
$C_1>2$. Above this height, $\log h\asymp L$ and the beta-integral
comparison gives $O(\sqrt{Y/L})$. Below it, $\log h\ge\log h_b>0$
gives $O(\sqrt Y L^{-C_1/2})$. The peak singularity is integrable.

Comparing the peak minima for $h_b\le a_1\le a_2\ll H$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:endpointcomparison}
 0\le\A_{a_1}(n)-\A_{a_2}(n)
 \le2\sqrt{2/v}\int_{a_1}^{a_2}\sqrt{V(h)}\dd h
 \le C_0\sqrt{a_2\log a_2}.
\end{equation}
Both minimizing peaks exceed $a_2$ in the ranges used here.
Raising the endpoint to a fixed power of $L$ costs only a fixed power
of $L$; raising it to $H\epsilon$ costs $O(F\sqrt\epsilon)$.

\subsection{Normalized rows and exact integer repair}
Fix $K$ and choose
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:cutoffs}
 B_0=K+3,\quad\eta=L^{-B_0},\quad A_0=4B_0+10,
 \quad h_0=L^{A_0},\quad N=K+3.
\end{equation}
Let $V_\eta(h)$ be the exact row root at fractional cutoff
$(1-2\eta)h$, and let $N_\eta(h)$ be its $q$ moment. Constants in the
pinned normalized-row proof are uniform as $\eta\to0$, since the
cutoff factor eventually lies in $[1/2,1]$. In particular,
\[
 V_\eta\asymp\frac{\log h}h,\quad
 -V_\eta'\asymp\frac{\log h}{h^2},\quad
 N_\eta\asymp h,\quad |N_\eta'|\le C_0\log h,
\]
with one-sided derivatives at the knots. Use the exact duration-$n$
energy arch, with peak $Y\asymp H$, energy
$\lambda_0=V_\eta(Y)$, and signed momentum $\theta$. Change time by
$\dd t/\dd j=N_\eta(h)/\alpha$. Its real macro duration is
$\asymp M_0$. Round that duration to an integer $M$, and sample the
symmetric arch at the $M$ slots as in the pinned lower construction.
The estimates needed below are
\[
 |\theta_j|\le C_0\sqrt{L/h_j},\qquad
 |\theta'|\le C_0L/h_j,\qquad |h''|\le C_0L^2.
\]
At each nonpatch slot independently sample the complete row
proportional to
\[
 \chi_{(1-2\eta)h_j}(q)w(e,q,d)e^{\lambda_0e+\theta_jd}.
\]
The normalization and means satisfy
\begin{align*}
 R_j&=1+O(L^{3/2}/\sqrt{h_j}),\\
 \mathbb E d_j&=(v/\alpha)N_\eta(h_j)\theta_j+O(L^2),\\
 \mathbb E e_j&=N_\eta(h_j)/\alpha+O(L^{3/2}\sqrt{h_j}).
\end{align*}
Centered log moment-generating functions are bounded by $C_0z^2h_jL$
for $|z|\le c/\sqrt{h_jL}$, and, for length, by $C_0z^2h_j^2$ for
$|z|\le c/h_j$. These use only the preceding one-sided bounds, small
tilts, and differentiated analytic transfer.

Remove $r_0=M^{3/4}+O(1)$ central slots, choosing parity so their
deterministic edge heights agree. The left prefix and independent
right complementary suffix start from the two fixed seeds. Require
the stronger error tube $|E_j|\le\eta h_j/10$. Below $Y/2$, cumulative
variance is at most $C_0Lh^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log h}$, while deterministic
mismatch is at most
\[
 C_0L^2\sqrt{h/\log h}+C_0h/M=o(\eta h).
\]
Bernstein's exponent at threshold $\eta h$ is at least
\[
 c\eta^2\frac{\sqrt{h\log h}}L
 \ge cL^{A_0/2-2B_0-1}\sqrt{\log L}
 =cL^4\sqrt{\log L}.
\]
The quadratic regime is valid because threshold times maximal
subexponential scale divided by variance is
$O(\eta\sqrt{\log h/L})$. Near the peak the exponent is at least
$c\eta^2F/L$. Both dominate $\log M=O(L)$, so the union over all
boundaries has probability $1-o(1)$. Each retained
$q\le(1-2\eta)h+1$ is legal on this tube.

Retain also the original high-probability bounds
\[
 |X-\mathbb EX|\le LH\sqrt M,\qquad
 |\Delta|\le L\sqrt{nL},
\]
where $X$ is outside degree and $\Delta$ the patch height gap. Total
degree bias remains $O(HL)$. The missing degree divided by $r_0Y$
tends to $(1-m_*)/\alpha$; rounding, seeds, and fluctuations are
negligible relative to $r_0Y$. The exact integer patch in the pinned
proof therefore applies with its fixed legality margin around
$q=(1-m_*)Y[1+o(1)]$. It imposes exact total degree and exact final
height using \eqref{a61:ao:eq:patch}, at logarithmic cost
\[
 O(r_0L+ML^3/r_0)=o(M_0/L^K).
\]
The patch locations are fixed, deletion recovers the outside marked
data, and fractional weights are at most one; this is the required
weighted injection. The seeds cost only a fixed power of $L$.

For completeness, every other lower-construction error has a tunable
rate:
\begin{align*}
 \text{row normalizations: }&O(ML^{3/2-A_0/2}),\\
 \text{interior summation by parts: }&O(ML\eta),\\
 \text{central boundary terms: }&O(r_0L\eta),\\
 \text{omitted central tilt and action: }&O(r_0L),\\
 \text{Stieltjes quadrature: }&O(L^2).
\end{align*}
The first two are $o(M_0/L^K)$ by \eqref{a61:ao:eq:cutoffs}; the remaining
ones have either a power saving in $M$ or only fixed powers of $L$.
Macro-duration rounding and degree-mean bias are included in the exact
patch, and their deterministic action corrections are fixed powers of
$L$. The negative logarithm of retained probability is $o(1)$.
Consequently
\[
 D_n\le\A_{V_\eta,h_0}(n)+o(M_0/L^K).
\]

Compare this minimum with the $\phi_N$ arch with endpoints $h_0$.
Set $a=HL^{-C_1}$, $C_1=2K+8$. On $h\ge a$, the row-root expansion
and cutoff displacement give
\[
 |V_\eta(h)-\phi_N(h)|\le\frac{C_0}h
 \left[\eta L+\frac{(1+\log L)^{N+1}}{L^{N+1}}
 +n^{-c}L^{c'}\right].
\]
Its integral along the smooth arch is $o(M_0/L^K)$ by
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:reciprocal}. Below $a$, both potentials are at most
$C_0\log h/h$, so \eqref{a61:ao:eq:endpointaction} bounds the comparison by
$O(F L^{-C_1/2})=o(M_0/L^K)$. Finally
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:endpointcomparison} replaces $h_0$ by $h_b$ at a
fixed-power-of-$L$ cost. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:lowercoefficient}
 D_n\le\A_N(n)+o(M_0/L^K).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact smooth-potential calibration}
Keep $N=K+3$, and put
\[
 \epsilon=L^{-E},\quad E=2K+8,\qquad
 \delta=L^{-K-2},\qquad u=y+\epsilon,
\]
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:softpotential}
 U_n(y)=\frac nF\left[\phi_N(Hu)-\frac{\alpha\delta}{Hu}\right]
 =\frac{\alpha}{Lu}[k_N(\log(Hu))-\delta].
\end{equation}
On any fixed $0\le y\le R$, \eqref{a61:ao:eq:potentialderivative} gives
\[
 U_n\asymp u^{-1},\quad -U_n'\asymp u^{-2},\quad
 U_n''\asymp u^{-3},\quad |U_n^{(j)}|\le C_ju^{-j-1}.
\]
The potential is positive, decreasing, and convex on the whole
half-line for large $n$, because $H\epsilon\to\infty$.

Let $y_n$ minimize
$\int_0^1[y'^2/(2v)+U_n(y)]\dd t$ over nonnegative paths with
endpoints zero. A regularized Kepler trial bounds the action by a
constant. Kinetic coercivity gives a uniform $H^1$ bound and uniform
compactness, proving existence; convexity proves uniqueness. The
first variation against nonnegative tests gives
$y_n''\le vU_n'(y_n)<0$ in distributions. Concavity and the endpoint
values imply strict interior positivity; the Euler equation then is
$y_n''=vU_n'(y_n)<0$. The arch is symmetric and has a single peak in
a fixed positive compact interval. Choose $R$ above it.

The energy relation implies
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:softreciprocal}
 \int_0^1\frac{\dd t}{y_n(t)+\epsilon}\le C_0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, with $Y$ its peak,
$U_n(y)-U_n(Y)\ge c[(y+\epsilon)^{-1}-(Y+\epsilon)^{-1}]$;
the rising reciprocal-height integral is at most a constant times
\[
 \sqrt{Y+\epsilon}\int_0^Y
 (y+\epsilon)^{-1/2}(Y-y)^{-1/2}\dd y\le C_0.
\]
All required derivatives grow only as fixed powers of $L$; for example,
\[
 |y_n'|\le C_0\epsilon^{-1/2},\quad
 |y_n''|\le C_0\epsilon^{-2},\quad
 |y_n'''|\le C_0\epsilon^{-7/2}.
\]
Define
\begin{gather*}
 p=-y_n'/v,\quad A_1=p'=-U_n'(y_n)>0,\qquad
 J_1(A_1)=\min_{0\le y\le R}\{A_1y+U_n(y)\},\\
 g(1)=0,\quad g'=vp^2/2-J_1(p'),\qquad
 f(t,y)=g(t)+p(t)y.
\end{gather*}
Strict convexity places the minimizer in $J_1(p'(t))$ exactly at
$y_n(t)$, including its boundary values. Thus, for all $0\le y\le R$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:HJ}
 -f_t+vf_y^2/2=J_1(A_1)-A_1y\le U_n(y).
\end{equation}
Integration by parts using $y_n(0)=y_n(1)=0$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:calibrationvalue}
 f(0,0)=\int_0^1[y_n'^2/(2v)+U_n(y_n)]\dd t.
\end{equation}
There is no perturbative Legendre-minimum error. Since
$0\le J_1'(A_1)\le R$, the displayed path bounds give
$\|f\|_{C^2}\le L^{P_0}$ for some fixed $P_0=P_0(K)$. The constrained
Legendre value is $C^1$ at its boundary transition, which suffices
for $g''$ and this $C^2$ Taylor bound.

Let $\A_\epsilon$ denote the unperturbed physical $\phi_N$ action
with endpoints $H\epsilon$. Evaluating the two functionals at each
other's minimizers and using \eqref{a61:ao:eq:softreciprocal} gives
\[
 \A_\epsilon-C_0M_0\delta\le Ff(0,0)\le\A_\epsilon.
\]
Together with \eqref{a61:ao:eq:endpointcomparison}, this proves
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:calibrationcomparison}
 Ff(0,0)=\A_N(n)+O(F\sqrt\epsilon+M_0\delta)
 =\A_N(n)+o(M_0/L^K).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Transformed-row contraction and upper coefficient bound}
On legal states $1\le h\le RH$, $q\le h$, and $k+e\le n-1$, use the
exact transformed weight
\[
 w(e,q,d)\exp\{F[f(k/n,h/H)-f((k+e)/n,(h+d)/H)]\}.
\]
Set $\gamma=F/n$, $\beta=F/H$, so $\beta^2=\gamma$ and $\gamma H=L$.
Choose a fixed $T_0>P_0+2$. On the good-step region
$e\le HL^{T_0}$, $|d|\le\sqrt H L^{T_0}$, the $C^2$ Taylor error in
the exponent is $n^{-1/3}L^{P_1}$ for fixed $P_1$. Its linear tilts
are $s=-\gamma f_t$ and $\theta=-\beta p$. By
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:Psi} and \eqref{a61:ao:eq:HJ}, at $r=h+H\epsilon$,
\[
 r\Psi(s,\theta)\le\frac{r\gamma}{\alpha}U_n(h/H)
 +n^{-1/3}L^{P_2}
 =k_N(\log r)-\delta+n^{-1/3}L^{P_2}.
\]
Enlarge the legal cutoff $q\le h$ to the fractional cutoff $r$.
Every analytic tilt and amplitude error is a negative fixed power of
$n$ times a fixed power of $L$. Since $r\ge H\epsilon$,
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:rowgap} applies, and the good-step row is at most
$1-c\delta$, after the Taylor factor is included.

The discarded steps contribute $o(\delta)$ uniformly. The entire
transformed exponent is bounded by
$C_0\gamma L^{P_0}e+C_0\beta L^{P_0}|d|$.
For $e>HL^{T_0}$, an extra fixed small positive degree tilt in the
common analytic neighborhood gives
$\exp[-cHL^{T_0}+C_0H]$. For $|d|>\sqrt H L^{T_0}$, use both signs
of the extra height tilt $\theta_0=cL^{T_0}/\sqrt H$. Its logarithmic
bound, from the root expansion, is
\[
 -cL^{2T_0}+C_Rc^2L^{2T_0}
 +O(L^{P_0+T_0+1/2}+L^{P_0+1}+L).
\]
The base-height quadratic term $O(L^{2P_0+1})$ is absorbed here since
$T_0>P_0+2$. Choose $c$ small after $R$. Cubic errors are
$n^{-1/3}$ times a fixed power of $L$, leaving
$\exp[-c'L^{2T_0}]$. These unrestricted-destination bounds cover all
omitted legal steps. The complete row is therefore at most
$1-c_0\delta$.

The positive Neumann sum is bounded by $1/(c_0\delta)$, with
logarithm $O(\log L)$. The exact terminal factor is
$\rho^{n-1-k}t_*^{1-h}$. Because $g(1)=0$, terminal calibration only
changes the coefficients of $n-1-k$ and $h-1$ by
$O(\gamma L^{P_0})$ and $O(\beta L^{P_0})$; these are negligible
relative to the fixed negative coefficients $\log\rho$ and
$-\log t_*$. The terminal factor is uniformly bounded, and the
initial-height correction $\beta p(0)$ is $o(1)$. Hence
\[
 D_n^{[\le RH]}\ge Ff(0,0)-O(\log L).
\]
Choose $R$ also large enough that the exponent in
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:firsthit} exceeds a fixed trial-action upper bound.
The outside contribution is then exponentially smaller than the
confined upper coefficient bound. Equations
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:calibrationcomparison} and \eqref{a61:ao:eq:lowercoefficient}
complete the proof of Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:reduction}.

\subsection{An explicit error ledger}
Let $Q_K=M_0/L^K$. With the choices above, the errors divided by
$Q_K$ are bounded as follows:
\begin{align*}
 \text{normalization: }&O(L^{-K-19/2}),\\
 \text{interior summation: }&O(L^{-2}),\\
 \text{central boundaries: }&O(M^{-1/4}L^{-2}),\\
 \text{patch and omitted action: }&
 O(M^{-1/4}L^{K+1}+M^{-3/4}L^{K+3}),\\
 \text{bulk root truncation: }&O((1+\log L)^{K+4}/L^4),\\
 \text{bulk cutoff displacement: }&O(L^{-2}),\\
 \text{small heights and softened endpoints: }&O(L^{-3}),\\
 \text{upper potential slack: }&O(L^{-2}).
\end{align*}
Seeds, deterministic rounding, and Stieltjes quadrature are fixed
powers of $L$, hence $o(Q_K)$. The renewal logarithm is $O(\log L)$,
and retained-event logarithm is $o(1)$. Taylor and amplitude errors
are algebraically small in $n$ and are $o(\delta)$ at row level.
No step silently promotes an unspecified $o(M_0)$ bound to arbitrary
inverse-log precision.


\section{Endpoint-safe expansion of the action}\label{a61:ao:sec:action}
This part is a deterministic calculation. It applies to any fixed
$\alpha>0$, $v>0$, and real $c$, with $c_*$ in \eqref{a61:ao:eq:kN}
replaced by $c$. Define $Y_0,C$ as before and
$\kappa=\log Y_0-2\log3+2c$. Write $\A_{N,b}$ for the action
with fixed lower endpoint $b$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-order action expansion]\label{a61:ao:thm:action}
For each fixed $J\ge0$ and $N\ge J+2$, choose a sufficiently large
fixed $b$ so that $V_N(h)=\alpha k_N(\log h)/h$ is positive,
decreasing, and strictly convex on $[b,\infty)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:actionexpansion}
 \frac{\A_{N,b}(n)}{CF}
 =\sum_{j=0}^J\frac{P_j(B)}{L^j}
 +O_{J,N}\left(\frac{(1+\log L)^{m_{J,N}}}{L^{J+1}}\right)
\end{equation}
for some finite $m_{J,N}$. The polynomials are independent of
$N,b,\alpha,v,c$ and satisfy Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:main}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Exact peak parameterization}
The direct method gives a minimizing path: a constant trial bounds the
kinetic energy, which bounds its maximum; weak $H^1$ compactness and
uniform convergence then give a minimizer. Strict convexity gives
uniqueness. The first variation shows it lies above $b$ in the
interior and solves $h''=vV_N'(h)<0$. It is symmetric with one peak
$Y>b$, and conservation of energy gives
\[
 \frac{h'^2}{2v}=V_N(h)-V_N(Y).
\]
Conversely, the resulting arch at each peak is stationary and hence
minimizing. Its duration is continuous and one-to-one in $Y$, ranges
from zero to infinity, and is therefore increasing. Smooth dependence
follows from the linearized boundary-value operator, whose quadratic
form is
\[
 \int[\eta'^2/v+V_N''(h)\eta^2]\dd t>0
\]
for nonzero endpoint-vanishing variations.

Put $T=\log Y$, $z_0=b/Y$, $k=k_N(T)$, and
\[
 q_T(z)=\frac{k_N(T+\log z)}k,\qquad
 I_\pm(T)=\int_{z_0}^1[q_T(z)/z-1]^{\pm1/2}\dd z.
\]
Then the exact duration and action are
\begin{align}
 n(Y)&=\sqrt{\frac2{v\alpha}}Y^{3/2}k^{-1/2}I_-(T),
 \label{a61:ao:eq:duration}\\
 \A(Y)&=\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}v}\sqrt{Yk}
 [I_-(T)+2I_+(T)].\label{a61:ao:eq:peakaction}
\end{align}
The second formula follows also from
$n\alpha k/Y+2\sqrt{2\alpha/v}\sqrt{Yk}I_+$.
Both limiting integrals are $\pi/2$, giving
$Y\sim Y_0H$ and $\A\sim CF$.

\subsection{The lower endpoint must be removed before expansion}
Fix a requested order and take $\delta_0=T^{-C_2}$ with $C_2$ large
enough after that order. The function $k_N$ is positive and increasing
on $[\log b,T]$ when $b$ is sufficiently large. Thus, on
$z_0\le z\le\delta_0$,
\[
 c_0/T\le q_T(z)\le1,\qquad
 q_T(z)/z-1\ge q_T(z)/(2z).
\]
The actual, unexpanded endpoint integrals satisfy
\begin{align}
 \int_{z_0}^{\delta_0}[q_T(z)/z-1]^{1/2}\dd z
 &=O(\sqrt{\delta_0}),\label{a61:ao:eq:tailplus}\\
 \int_{z_0}^{\delta_0}[q_T(z)/z-1]^{-1/2}\dd z
 &=O(\sqrt T\,\delta_0^{3/2}).\label{a61:ao:eq:tailminus}
\end{align}
Every coefficient integrand extended from $\delta_0$ down to zero
has a tail bounded by a fixed finite sum of
\[
 \int_0^{\delta_0}z^{-1/2}(1+|\log z|)^m\dd z
 =O\bigl(\sqrt{\delta_0}(1+|\log\delta_0|)^m\bigr).
\]
Increasing $C_2$ makes these tails smaller than any prescribed finite
inverse power of $T$. In particular, no uniform large-height
expansion at heights of order $b$ has been assumed.

\subsection{Cancellation at the peak and integrable moments}
Factor
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:removable}
 \frac{q_T(z)}z-1=\frac{1-z}z(1+r_T(z)),\qquad
 r_T(z)=\frac{k_N(T+\log z)-k_N(T)}{k_N(T)(1-z)}.
\end{equation}
The apparent singularity at $z=1$ is removable, with limit
$-k_N'(T)/k_N(T)$. On $\delta_0\le z\le1$,
\[
 |r_T(z)|\le\frac{C_0|\log z|}{T(1-z)}
 \le\frac{C_1(1+|\log z|)}T
 =O(\log T/T).
\]
For the second inequality, use $1-z\ge1/2$ when $z\le1/2$ and
$-z\log z\le1-z$ when $z\ge1/2$. Finite Taylor expansion of the
explicit smooth $k_N$ about $T$ and expansion of $1/k_N(T)$ are
therefore uniform on this interval. Each numerator polynomial in
$w=\log z$ has zero constant term. Taylor remainders retain a factor
$w$, so division by $1-z$ adds no nonintegrable singularity.
The binomial expansions of $(1+r_T)^{\pm1/2}$ have remainders
$T^{-J-1}$ times fixed powers of $1+\log T$ at a requested order.

The leading weights are
\[
 w_-(z)=z^{1/2}(1-z)^{-1/2},\qquad
 w_+(z)=z^{-1/2}(1-z)^{1/2}.
\]
All coefficient terms are linear combinations of
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:momentintegrand}
 w_\sigma(z)\frac{(\log z)^m}{(1-z)^r},\qquad m\ge r.
\end{equation}
They are integrable at $z=1$ since $\log z=-(1-z)+O((1-z)^2)$,
and at $z=0$ since the worst power there is $z^{-1/2}$. Together with
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:tailplus}--\eqref{a61:ao:eq:tailminus}, this proves legitimate
finite-order termwise integration with the stated uniform remainders.

The beta-moment rule is
\begin{align}
 M_-(r,m)&=\left.\partial_a^m\mathrm B(a,1/2-r)\right|_{a=3/2},
 \label{a61:ao:eq:betamoments}\\
 M_+(r,m)&=\left.\partial_a^m\mathrm B(a,3/2-r)\right|_{a=1/2}.
 \nonumber
\end{align}
For a negative second beta argument this denotes analytic continuation
of the derivative. The differentiated integrand itself is convergent
by $m\ge r$; the formula does not assign values to divergent
integrals. One may continue the convergent differentiated integral in
the second argument, or integrate by parts using the order-$m$ zero
at $z=1$. For exact computation without spurious Gamma poles, first use
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:betarecurrence}
 \mathrm B(a,b_0-r)=\mathrm B(a,b_0)
 \prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{a+b_0-r+j}{b_0-r+j},
 \quad b_0\in\{1/2,3/2\},
\end{equation}
and only then differentiate.

A duration derivative is needed for inversion, so it is useful to
check it directly. On $z\le\delta_0$,
$q_T\ge c/T$ and $|\partial_Tq_T|\le C_0/T$ imply another
$O(\sqrt Tz^{1/2})$ integrand bound after differentiating $I_-$.
The moving lower-limit term is
$O(\sqrt T(b/Y)^{3/2})$, exponentially small. The artificial cut adds
no larger error. On the central interval, differentiating the
removable expression \eqref{a61:ao:eq:removable} gives
$O(T^{-2}(1+\log T)^m)$ against the same integrable weight. Thus
\[
 I_-'(T)=O(T^{-2}(1+\log T)^m)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \frac{\dd\log n}{\dd T}=\frac32+O(1/T).
\]
This proves an inverse derivative bounded away from zero; it does not
differentiate an unspecified asymptotic remainder.

\subsection{Fixed cores and moving cutoffs}
For a positive decreasing convex potential, differentiation with
respect to the common endpoint $a$ gives the boundary-momentum identity
\[
 \frac{\partial\A_a(n)}{\partial a}
 =-2\sqrt{2/v}\sqrt{V(a)-V(Y_a(n))}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 0\le\A_b(n)-\A_a(n)
 \le2\sqrt{2/v}\int_b^a\sqrt{V(h)}\dd h
 =O(\sqrt{a\log a})
\]
for the present potential. At $a=H\epsilon$ this is
$O(F\sqrt\epsilon)$. A fixed positive decreasing $C^2$ convex
extension to $[0,b]$ exists, for example the quadratic matching
$V,V',V''$ at $b$. Changing from endpoint $b$ to endpoint zero for
such an extension costs $O(1)$. Two fixed admissible core extensions
therefore differ by $O(1)$ at fixed duration. A negative well or an
$n$-dependent core is not an admissible substitute.

Uniformly for minimizing arches with $b\le a=o(Y)$,
\[
 \int_0^n\frac{\dd t}{h(t)}=O(n/Y).
\]
One may verify this by dividing by $n/Y$: the resulting ratio is
$R(T)/I_-(T)$, where
\[
 R(T)=\int_{a/Y}^1z^{-1}[q_T(z)/z-1]^{-1/2}\dd z.
\]
On $z\ge T^{-C_2}$, use a constant multiple of
$z^{-1/2}(1-z)^{-1/2}$; below it use
$C_0\sqrt Tz^{-1/2}$. Taking $C_2>1$ bounds $R(T)$ uniformly while
$I_-$ stays positive. The estimate remains uniform for
$V_N-\alpha\eta_1/h$ with $\eta_1=o(1)$, because positivity,
decrease, and convexity persist. Comparison at each other's
minimizers bounds the action perturbation by $O(M_0\eta_1)$.

\section{A universal polynomial recurrence}\label{a61:ao:sec:recurrence}
The peak variable $k$ makes the coefficient algorithm especially
simple. Replace $\Srow$ by a sufficiently long finite truncation
$\Srow_m$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:Tfromk}
 T(k)=2k-2\log k+2\log\Srow_m(1/k)-2c-2\log2.
\end{equation}
Its derivative is $2+O(1/k)>0$. Its smooth inverse agrees with the
explicit $k_N(T)$ to every order that can enter
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:actionexpansion}. More quantitatively, with $m\ge N+2$,
substitution of $k_N$ in the implicit equation leaves residual
$O(T^{-N-1}(1+\log T)^{N+1})$. The implicit derivative is
$1+O(1/k)$, so the same bound holds for the difference of the two
solutions; their first derivative difference is
$O(T^{-N-2}(1+\log T)^{N+1})$. On the central interval the difference
of their removable $r_T$ expressions is
$O(T^{-N-3}(1+\log T)^{N+2})$, while peak normalizations differ
relatively by $O(T^{-N-2}(1+\log T)^{N+1})$. The lower tails are
already controlled. Thus the finite smooth surrogate may be used
for coefficient extraction without assuming differentiability of an
exact discrete row root.

Let $r=1/k$ be formal, $w=\log z$, and
$\Delta(r,w)=k(T+w)-k(T)$. Subtracting two copies of
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:Tfromk} gives
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:Deltarecurrence}
 \Delta=\frac w2+\log(1+r\Delta)
 -\log\Srow\left(\frac r{1+r\Delta}\right)+\log\Srow(r).
\end{equation}
Iteration from $\Delta=w/2$ determines every coefficient. They are
polynomials in $w$ vanishing at $w=0$. At every fixed order this is
also a uniform analytic expansion for $|w|\le C_2\log k$, because
its right side has $\Delta$ derivative $O(1/k)$.

Define formal series with constant term one by coefficientwise
finite beta-moment integration:
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:Jrecurrence}
 J_\sigma(r)=\frac2\pi\int_0^1w_\sigma(z)
 \left[1+\frac{r\Delta(r,\log z)}{1-z}\right]^{\sigma/2}\dd z,
 \qquad \sigma=-1,+1.
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{a61:ao:sec:action} supplies the analytic justification; this
notation does not assert convergence of an infinite integrated
series. With $g=\log2$, the first terms are
\begin{align*}
 J_-(r)&=1+(1-g)r+\tfrac32(1-g)r^2+O(r^3),\\
 J_+(r)&=1-gr-\tfrac32gr^2+O(r^3).
\end{align*}
Now put $x=1/L$ and $q=3k/L$. Taking logarithms of
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:duration} and using \eqref{a61:ao:eq:Tfromk} gives the universal
implicit equation
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:qrecurrence}
 q=1+x\left[\tfrac32B+3\log2+\tfrac72\log q
 -3\log\Srow(3x/q)-\log J_-(3x/q)\right].
\end{equation}
Eliminating $Y$ between duration and action gives the final recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:Precurrence}
 \sum_{j\ge0}P_j(B)x^j
 =q^{2/3}J_-(3x/q)^{-1/3}
 \frac{J_-(3x/q)+2J_+(3x/q)}3.
\end{equation}
To compute through $x^J$, truncate each series after degree $J$ and
perform finitely many iterations starting at $q=1$. No summation of
a divergent infinite Watson series is involved.

\subsection{Why the recurrence is asymptotic rather than only formal}
The leading duration relation reads
\[
 \log n=\tfrac32T-\tfrac12\log T+\text{constant}
 +O(\log T/T),
\]
so the actual peak satisfies
\[
 T=\tfrac23L+\tfrac13\log L+\log Y_0+O(\log L/L).
\]
Finite substitution determines its inverse expansion. The direct
derivative bound $\dd\log n/\dd T=3/2+O(1/T)$ implies, by the
mean-value theorem, that an approximate inverse with residual
$O(L^{-J-1}(1+\log L)^m)$ has an error of the same order. Equivalently
the derivative in $k$ is $3+O(1/k)$. The endpoint-safe integration
estimates are uniform at the fixed order, and all finite algebraic
operations preserve their remainders. This proves
\eqref{a61:ao:eq:actionexpansion}.

In \eqref{a61:ao:eq:qrecurrence}, dependence on $q$ beyond the constant term
is multiplied by $x$. The coefficient of $x$ has degree one in $B$;
for $j\ge2$, the coefficient of $x^j$ in $q$ has degree at most
$j-1$. Equation~\eqref{a61:ao:eq:Precurrence} therefore gives
$\deg P_j\le j$. Its $B^j$ term arises only from the $j$ uses of
$(3B/2)x$ in $q^{2/3}$, proving
\[
 [B^j]P_j=\binom{2/3}{j}(3/2)^j\ne0.
\]
For $J\ge1$, apply Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:reduction} with $K=J-1$ and
$N=J+2$, exactly meeting Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:action}'s requirement. Its
error is $o(F/L^J)$. The leading-only case follows at once. This
proves Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:main}.

\subsection{Explicit checked polynomials}
The first five polynomials are
\begin{align*}
 P_0(B)&=1,\\
 P_1(B)&=B,\\
 P_2(B)&=-\frac{B^2}{4}+\frac{7B}{2}-10,\\
 P_3(B)&=\frac{4B^3-105B^2+774B-2050-27\pi^2}{24},\\
 P_4(B)&=-\frac1{48}\bigl[7B^4-273B^3+3444B^2
 -(19768+189\pi^2)B\\
 &\hspace{34mm}+1944\zeta(3)+999\pi^2+46290\bigr].
\end{align*}
They are exact expressions. The companion includes the recurrence
generator and an independent direct-substitution verification of
$P_0$ through $P_4$ in the original $T=\log Y$ duration and action
integrals, without importing the generator. The second calculation
derives its own local row polynomials and Gamma-jet beta moments;
all duration residuals and coefficient differences vanish through
$L^{-4}$, and the auxiliary constants cancel.

For a short transparent check of $P_2$, the four convergent moments
needed are
\begin{align*}
 \int_0^1z^{1/2}(1-z)^{-3/2}\log z\dd z&=-2\pi(1-g),\\
 \int_0^1z^{1/2}(1-z)^{-5/2}\log^2z\dd z&=\tfrac{8\pi}3(1-g),\\
 \int_0^1z^{-1/2}(1-z)^{-1/2}\log z\dd z&=-2\pi g,\\
 \int_0^1z^{-1/2}(1-z)^{-3/2}\log^2z\dd z&=8\pi g.
\end{align*}
Set $a=3B/2+3g$. Then
\[
 q=1+ax+\left[\tfrac72a-\tfrac{33}2+3g\right]x^2+O(x^3),
\]
while $W(r)=J_-(r)^{-1/3}[J_-(r)+2J_+(r)]/3$ obeys
\[
 W(r)=1-\tfrac{2g}3r+\left(\tfrac19-g-\tfrac{g^2}9\right)r^2
 +O(r^3).
\]
Substitution of $r=3x/q$ in $q^{2/3}W(r)$ cancels every $\log2$ term
and yields $P_1=B$ and $P_2=-(B-10)(B-4)/4$.

\section{The inverse threshold expansion}\label{a61:ao:sec:inverse}
For $p>1$ define
\[
 N(p)=\min\{n\ge0:a_n\ge p\},\qquad
 \lambda=\log\mu>0,\quad T=\log p,\quad S=\log T.
\]
The sequence is nondecreasing: append a copy of the last letter to an
avoiding ascent sequence. This preserves the ascent condition and
creates no new 120 occurrence, since any new occurrence using the
appended letter would already use its identical predecessor. Deleting
the final letter recovers the original word, so the map is injective.
The asymptotic growth makes $N(p)$ finite for sufficiently large $p$.

\begin{theorem}[All finite inverse orders]\label{a61:ao:thm:inverse}
Put
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{inv}}=\kappa-\tfrac23\log\lambda,
 \qquad B_{\mathrm{inv}}=\kappa_{\mathrm{inv}}+\tfrac73\log S.
\]
For every fixed integer $J\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:inverse}
 \begin{split}
 N(p)={}&\frac T\lambda+
 \frac C{\lambda^{4/3}}T^{1/3}S^{2/3}
 \sum_{j=0}^J\frac{P_j(B_{\mathrm{inv}})}{S^j}\\
 &+o\bigl(T^{1/3}S^{2/3-J}\bigr)
 \qquad(p\to\infty).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The polynomials are exactly those of Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:main}.
\end{theorem}
The power $\lambda^{-4/3}$ includes one factor $\lambda^{-1}$ from
inversion and one factor $\lambda^{-1/3}$ from the argument
$n=T/\lambda$. The constant shift is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{inv}}=-2.39800354051534742875\ldots$.

\subsection{Covariance of the finite polynomial hierarchy}
In the peak variable $k$, the logarithmic duration equation is
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:covariantpeak}
 \begin{split}
 L={}&3k-\tfrac72\log k-\tfrac32\kappa-\tfrac72\log3-3\log2\\
 &+3\log\Srow(1/k)+\log J_-(1/k).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
The parameters $L$ and $\kappa$ enter only through $L+3\kappa/2$.
\begin{writenote}
Equation \eqref{a61:ao:eq:covariantpeak} has the same linear--logarithmic form, with $a=3$, $b=-\frac72$. The inverse theorem above reverses $\lambda n-D_n$ about $n=T/\lambda$ and brackets $N(p)$ by monotonicity, as in theorems \texttt{p0:thm:perturbed-inversion} and \texttt{p0:thm:staircase} of the transseries volume. At $J=1$ it is Corollary~\ref{a61:o3:inv3:theorem}.
\end{writenote}

Elimination of the peak height gives
\[
 \frac{\A(n)}{Cn^{1/3}}=(3k)^{2/3}J_-(1/k)^{-1/3}
 \frac{J_-(1/k)+2J_+(1/k)}3,
\]
with no other $L$ or $\kappa$ dependence. These equations are
interpreted at each finite order using the smooth surrogate and
uniform remainders proved above.

Define the finite expression
\[
 f_J(L,\kappa)=L^{2/3}\sum_{j=0}^J
 \frac{P_j(\kappa+\frac73\log L)}{L^j}.
\]
For every fixed $J,Q$ and $\kappa$ in a compact interval,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:covariance}
 \begin{split}
 f_J(L-\sigma,\kappa)-f_J(L,\kappa-2\sigma/3)
 =O_{J,Q}\bigl(L^{2/3-J-1}(1+\log L)^{m_J}\bigr)
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
uniformly for $|\sigma|\le Q$. One proof expands the finite smooth
peak equations one order further; their dependence on
$L+3\kappa/2$ gives the formal identity, and all action estimates are
uniform on compact parameter sets. Alternatively, Taylor-expand the
two finite polynomial-log expressions in $\sigma/L$. The peak
identity matches every coefficient through degree $J$, and ordinary
finite Taylor remainders give \eqref{a61:ao:eq:covariance}. No convergent
infinite series is needed.

Equivalently, the polynomials satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:derivativeidentity}
 P_{j+1}'(B)=(1-3j/2)P_j(B)+\tfrac72P_j'(B).
\end{equation}
Indeed the formal function $f$ depends on $L+3\kappa/2$, hence
$f_L=(2/3)f_\kappa$; coefficient comparison gives the identity.
It determines $P_{j+1}$ only up to an additive constant. The beta
moments supply the remaining information.

\subsection{A squeeze using smooth truncations only}
Fix $J$ and define
\[
 d_J(x)=Cx^{1/3}f_J(\log x,\kappa),\qquad
 R_J(x)=x^{1/3}(\log x)^{2/3-J}.
\]
The coefficient theorem says, at every sufficiently large integer,
\begin{equation}\label{a61:ao:eq:discreteremainder}
 \log a_n=\lambda n-d_J(n)+r_J(n),\qquad
 r_J(n)=o(R_J(n)).
\end{equation}
Put
\[
 x_0=T/\lambda,\quad x_*=x_0+d_J(x_0)/\lambda,
 \quad F_T=T^{1/3}S^{2/3},\quad E_J=T^{1/3}S^{2/3-J}.
\]
The explicit finite sum is smooth and, uniformly for
$x=x_0+O(F_T)$,
\[
 d_J(x)=O(F_T),\qquad d_J'(x)=O(T^{-2/3}S^{2/3}).
\]
The degree bounds on $P_j$ prove these directly by differentiating
finitely many terms. Therefore
\[
 d_J(x_*)-d_J(x_0)=O(T^{-1/3}S^{4/3})=o(1),
 \qquad \lambda x_*-d_J(x_*)=T+o(1).
\]
Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and set
\[
 n_- =\lfloor x_*-\varepsilon E_J\rfloor,\qquad
 n_+ =\lceil x_*+\varepsilon E_J\rceil.
\]
Both lie in $x_0+O(F_T)$, and $E_J\to\infty$ at every fixed order,
so integer rounding is $o(E_J)$. The derivative estimate gives
$d_J(n_\pm)-d_J(x_*)=o(1)$. Also
$r_J(n_\pm)=o(E_J)$: the defining eventual all-integer bound for
$r_J$ applies at these two indices, and $R_J(n_\pm)/E_J$ stays
between fixed positive constants. No local regularity of the exact
remainder is assumed. Substituting in \eqref{a61:ao:eq:discreteremainder},
\[
 \log a_{n_-}=T-\lambda\varepsilon E_J+o(E_J)<T,
 \qquad
 \log a_{n_+}=T+\lambda\varepsilon E_J+o(E_J)>T
\]
for large $T$. Monotonicity puts $N(p)$ between these indices; then
letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves
$N(p)=x_*+o(E_J)$.

Finally apply \eqref{a61:ao:eq:covariance} with $L=S$ and
$\sigma=\log\lambda$:
\[
 \frac{d_J(T/\lambda)}\lambda
 =\frac C{\lambda^{4/3}}T^{1/3}f_J(S,\kappa_{\mathrm{inv}})
 +O\bigl(T^{1/3}S^{2/3-J-1}(1+\log S)^{m_J}\bigr).
\]
The last error is $o(E_J)$, proving \eqref{a61:ao:eq:inverse}. Only the
smooth finite approximant was differentiated; the discrete deficit
was never treated as a differentiable function. The proof gives no
exact integer rounding rule and no $o(1)$ absolute inverse error.

\section{What remains open}\label{a61:ao:sec:scope}
Theorem~\ref{a61:ao:thm:main} establishes all finite orders in one dominant
sector of the logarithm. In this restricted sense it is an
inverse-logarithmic asymptotic hierarchy, with both $L^{-1}$ and
$\log L$ present. The term ``transseries'' is sometimes used broadly
for such structures, but an exponentially complete transseries has
not been obtained here.

A multiplicative equivalent for $a_n$ would need control of the
logarithm down to $o(1)$ after every relevant subleading term is
included. The remainder after any fixed order here may diverge.
Thus even knowing all $P_j$ does not identify a multiplicative
amplitude or a power-law prefactor. For example, adding $\log n$
or a scale $n^\varepsilon$ with fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/3$ to a
logarithmic model changes none of the present finite-order
statements, while changing its exponential substantially.

The following are concrete further questions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can the row-to-action reduction be sharpened to smaller
powers of $n$, then ultimately to $O(1)$ or $o(1)$ precision?
This would require finer discrete normalization, repair, endpoint,
and fluctuation estimates than the tunable bounds used here.
\item What additional sectors occur below $n^{1/3}$ times inverse
powers of $\log n$? A fluctuation determinant, a logarithmic term,
and a multiplicative amplitude cannot be inferred from the present
action hierarchy.
\item How do $P_j$ and the hidden remainder constants grow as
$j\to\infty$? The factorial Watson coefficients suggest examining
optimal truncation or resummation, but no such result follows from
fixed-order estimates alone.
\item Can effective constants and computable thresholds be proved?
The present asymptotics do not certify finite integer values of
$N(p)$ or guarantee an accuracy for a given numerical input.
\item Which other positive macro-kernels share the same universal
polynomials after their own amplitude and action reductions are
established? The deterministic calculation is universal in
$\alpha,v,c$, while the discrete reduction remains a separate proof
obligation for each model.
\end{enumerate}
Earlier dependencies retain their original historical scope and
open-question statements. The present report supersedes only their
question about this all-finite-orders dominant sector. It makes no
additional literature-priority claim.
\begin{writenote}
In this report the ``earlier dependencies'' are Parts~\ref{a61:part:fd}--\ref{a61:part:o3}. The question this Part supersedes is the one about lower-order corrections and an all-orders inverse expansion (Part~\ref{a61:part:fd}, Section~\ref{a61:fd:sec:closing}, and the closing sections of Parts~\ref{a61:part:sh}--\ref{a61:part:o3}). Their other open questions remain open: a multiplicative equivalent, amplitude or prefactor; an exponentially complete transseries; explicit thresholds.
\end{writenote}
