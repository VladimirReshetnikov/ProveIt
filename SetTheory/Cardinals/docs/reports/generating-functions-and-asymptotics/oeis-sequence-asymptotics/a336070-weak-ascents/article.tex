\begin{abstract}
A weak ascent sequence (B\'enyi--Claesson--Dukes) starts at $0$ and never exceeds one more than the number of its weak ascents $x_i\le x_{i+1}$; its count $a_n$ is OEIS A336070. More generally, a difference ascent sequence with parameter $d$ (Dukes--Sagan) counts the indices with $x_{i+1}>x_i-d$; $d=0$ gives the Fishburn numbers and $d=1$ gives A336070. Put $\mu=6/\pi^2$. This report prints three manuscripts as Parts~I--III. Part~I proves, for every fixed $d\ge0$, that $(a_n^{(d)}/n!)^{1/n}\to\mu$ and $a^{(d)}_{n+1}/((n+1)a^{(d)}_n)\to\mu$, with exponential concentration of the ascent count, and for $d=1$ that $\log(a_n/(n!\mu^n))=\frac12(\log n)^2+O((\log n)^2/\log\log n)$, with a signed Lambert-$W$ threshold inverse. Part~II weights each equal adjacent pair (level) by $w>0$, proves $\log H_n(w)=\log n!+n\log\mu+\frac w2(\log n)^2+O((\log n)^2/\log\log n)$ uniformly on compact weight intervals, and derives a large-deviation principle at speed $(\log n)^2$ with rate $x\log(2x/w)-x+w/2$ for the number of levels, the exact zero-level rate and a threshold inverse. Part~III reduces both remainders to $O(\log n)$, proves the correction $\frac d2(\log n)^2$ with remainder $O_d(\log n)$ for every fixed integer $d\ge1$, and deduces threshold inverses with bounded additive error and sharper moment bounds for the level count. A relative-error equivalent, an amplitude, the coefficient of a possible $\log n$ term, a central limit theorem and uniformity in a growing $d$ remain open. The report is unrefereed, and nothing in it is formalized.
\end{abstract}

\begin{partabstract}
Let $a_n$ count the weak ascent sequences of B\'enyi, Claesson and Dukes, recorded as OEIS A336070. We prove that both the factorial-normalized root and the scaled consecutive ratio tend to $\mu=6/\pi^2$. The weak-ascent count divided by the length is exponentially concentrated at $\mu$. \section{Scope and principal conclusions}
A weak ascent of a finite sequence $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ of nonnegative integers is an index $i<n$ with $x_i\le x_{i+1}$. A weak ascent sequence has $x_1=0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:definition}
0\le x_{i+1}\le1+\wasc(x_1,\ldots,x_i)\qquad(1\le i<n).
\end{equation}
We count the empty sequence once, so $a_0=1$. These are the sequences in A336070 \cite{oeis,benyi}, beginning
\[
1,1,2,6,23,106,567,3440,23286,173704,1414102,\ldots.
\]
The definition matters: other papers have used the informal name ``weak ascent sequence'' for different globally constrained objects. All assertions below concern \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:definition}.

Put
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:chi}
g(v)=\frac{\log v}{v-1},\quad g(1)=1,\qquad
\chi(v)=\int_v^\infty\frac{\log t}{t(t-1)}\dd t,
\qquad \mu=\frac6{\pi^2}.
\end{equation}
The integrand has its removable value at $t=1$. For a uniformly chosen length-$n$ weak ascent sequence, let $K_n$ be its weak-ascent count. Write
\[
H_n(v)=\sum_{|x|=n}v^{\wasc(x)}\quad(n\ge1).
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{wasc:gr:thm:main}
For every fixed $v>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:tilt}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\frac{H_n(v)}{n!}\le-\log\chi(v).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:rootratio}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{a_n}{n!}\right)^{1/n}=\mu,
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n+1}}{(n+1)a_n}=\mu.
\end{equation}
For every $\eta>0$ there are $c_\eta>0$ and $n_\eta$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:concentration}
\Prob\!\left(\left|\frac{K_n}{n}-\mu\right|\ge\eta\right)
\le \ee^{-c_\eta n}\qquad(n\ge n_\eta).
\end{equation}
The same statements hold for every fixed nonnegative integer $d$ when weak ascents and their sequence constraint are replaced by $d$-ascents and the corresponding constraint of Section~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:fixedd}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{wasc:gr:thm:logsquare}
For weak ascent sequences, namely $d=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:logsquare}
\log\frac{a_n}{n!\mu^n}=\frac12(\log n)^2+
O\!\left(\frac{(\log n)^2}{\log\log n}\right).
\end{equation}
In particular the quotient of this logarithm by $(\log n)^2$ tends to $1/2$.
For $N(X)=\min\{n\ge0:a_n\ge X\}$, $L=\log X$, and
$x=L/W(\mu L/\ee)$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:inverseintro}
N(X)=x-\frac{(\log x)^2}{2\log(\mu x)}
+O\!\left(\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}\right)\qquad(X\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Here $W$ is the principal Lambert function. The logarithmic correction theorem is specific to $d=1$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{What remains open.}
Equation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:logsquare} is a logarithmic asymptotic, not a multiplicative equivalent. The error inside the exponential need not tend to zero. No amplitude, coefficient of a smaller correction, full tilted pressure, central limit theorem, or transseries is established. The upper bound \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:tilt} need not be an equality away from $v=1$. The inverse error in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:inverseintro} diverges and has an unspecified asymptotic constant; it is not a bounded-error inverse or a certified finite numerical error bar.

\section{Exact state recurrence and formal generating functions}\label{wasc:gr:sec:exact}
\subsection{Positive recurrence and extension identity}
At length $n\ge1$, write $(K,L)$ for the number of weak ascents and the final letter. Starting from $(0,0)$, appending $j$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:transition}
(K,L)\longmapsto(K+\ind_{\{j\ge L\}},j),\qquad 0\le j\le K+1.
\end{equation}
Induction gives $0\le L\le K\le n-1$: without an ascent the new letter is below $L$, and with an ascent it is at most $K+1$, the new count. Consequently every such sequence is an inversion sequence and $a_n\le n!$.

Let $F(n,k,l)$ count sequences in state $(k,l)$, with $F(1,0,0)=1$ and every out-of-range entry zero. For $0\le j\le k\le n$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:recurrence}
F(n+1,k,j)=\sum_{l=j+1}^{k}F(n,k,l)
+\sum_{l=0}^{\min(j,k-1)}F(n,k-1,l).
\end{equation}
The first sum appends a strict descent and preserves the count; the second appends a weak ascent and increases it. Empty sums are zero. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:sums}
a_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{l=0}^kF(n,k,l),\qquad
H_n(v)=\sum_{k,l}F(n,k,l)v^k.
\end{equation}
Prefix and suffix sums compute a new layer in $O(n^2)$ integer operations and all layers through $N$ in $O(N^3)$ integer operations; this is an arithmetic-operation count, not a bit-complexity bound.

There are exactly $K+2$ children of each state. Counting them in two ways gives the crucial identity
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:children}
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\E_n(K_n+2)\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
The ratio limit will follow from this identity and concentration, not from a root-limit inference.

\subsection{Catalytic equation and its polynomial meaning}
Define $g_0(u)=u$ and, for $n\ge1$,
\[
g_n(u)=u^2H_n(u)=\sum_{k,l}F(n,k,l)u^{k+2},\qquad
G(u,z)=\sum_{n\ge0}g_n(u)z^n.
\]
The exact equation of Auli--Elizalde, translated to this refinement, is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:kernel}
G(u,z)=u(1-u)+uG(u+zu(1-u),z),\qquad a_n=g_n(1).
\end{equation}
This is an identity in $\Q[u][[z]]$, the ring of formal power series in $z$ whose coefficients are polynomials in $u$. It is not an analytic assertion about convergence at positive $z$. At $z^0$ it yields $g_0=u$ after cancellation of $1-u$ in $\Q[u]$. For $n\ge1$, formal Taylor expansion gives
\[
(1-u)g_n(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}
\frac{u^{j+1}(1-u)^j}{j!}\,g_{n-j}^{(j)}(u).
\]
Every term is divisible by $1-u$, so division in the polynomial ring is legitimate and yields the unique recursion
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:derivative}
g_n(u)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}
\frac{u^{j+1}(1-u)^{j-1}}{j!}\,g_{n-j}^{(j)}(u).
\end{equation}
For example,
\[
g_1=u^2,\quad g_2=2u^3,\quad g_3=u^3+5u^4,
\quad g_4=9u^4+14u^5.
\]
Only after polynomial cancellation do we set $u=1$. This gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:derivativeatone}
a_n=g'_{n-1}(1),\qquad n\ge1,
\end{equation}
consistent with \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:children} for $n\ge2$. Setting $u=1$ directly in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:kernel} would merely give a tautology and lose the useful information.

The factorial growth proved below implies that the ordinary series $G(1,z)$ has radius zero. Its Borel transform
\[
B(u,t)=\sum_{n\ge0}g_n(u)\frac{t^n}{n!}
\]
obeys the formal identity
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:borel}
B(u,t)=u+\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{u^{j+1}(1-u)^{j-1}}{j!}
\mathcal I^j\bigl[\partial_u^jB(u,t)\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal I$ integrates formally from $0$ to $t$. Each coefficient receives only finitely many contributions. Equation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:derivativeatone} gives $\partial_tB(1,t)=\partial_uB(1,t)$ formally. The root theorem establishes the radius $\pi^2/6$ of $B(1,t)$, but does not identify its singular type or justify analytic continuation of \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:borel}.

\section{The frozen positive matrix}\label{wasc:gr:sec:matrix}
Fix $M\ge1$ and $v>0$. On the alphabet $\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ define
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:matrix}
T_v(l,j)=v^{\ind_{\{j\ge l\}}},\qquad 0\le l,j<M.
\end{equation}
For $v\ne1$ set $r=v^{-1/M}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:eigen}
f_l=r^l,\qquad \lambda_M(v)=\frac{v-1}{1-r}.
\end{equation}
At $v=1$, take $f_l=1$ and $\lambda_M(1)=M$.

\begin{lemma}\label{wasc:gr:lem:frozen}
The positive vector $f$ satisfies $T_vf=\lambda_M(v)f$. For every integer $b\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:matrixbound}
T_v^b\mathbf1\le\max(v,v^{-1})\lambda_M(v)^b\mathbf1
\end{equation}
entrywise. Uniformly for $v$ in any fixed compact subset of $(0,\infty)$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:eigenestimate}
\lambda_M(v)\le\frac{M}{g(v)}+C
\end{equation}
with a constant independent of $M$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first row has value
\[
(T_vf)_0=v\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}r^j=\frac{v-1}{1-r}=\lambda_M(v),
\]
and consecutive rows differ by $(1-v)r^l=\lambda_M(v)(r-1)r^l$. This proves the identity. It remains valid for $v<1$, when both numerator and denominator of $\lambda_M(v)$ are negative. At $v=1$, $T_v$ is the all-ones matrix.

If $m_f=\min_l f_l$ and $M_f=\max_l f_l$, positivity implies
\[
T_v^b\mathbf1\le \lambda_M(v)^b f/m_f
\le(M_f/m_f)\lambda_M(v)^b\mathbf1.
\]
Here $M_f/m_f=\max(v,v^{-1})^{(M-1)/M}\le\max(v,v^{-1})$.
Finally let $q=\log v$ and define the continuous removable function
\[
h(s)=\frac1{1-\ee^{-s}}-\frac1s,\qquad h(0)=\frac12.
\]
Then $\lambda_M(v)-M/g(v)=(v-1)h(q/M)$. For marks in a fixed compact set, $q/M$ stays in a fixed compact interval and this difference is bounded.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{wasc:gr:lem:block}
In a block of $b$ extensions starting from $(K,L)$, let $K'$ be the final weak-ascent count. Then
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:block}
\sum_{\text{actual block paths}}v^{K'-K}
\le\max(v,v^{-1})\lambda_{K+b+2}(v)^b.
\end{equation}
On a fixed compact mark range the eigenvalue on the right is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:blockeig}
\lambda_{K+b+2}(v)\le K/g(v)+C(b+1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The ascent count increases by at most $b$. Every actual letter lies in the alphabet $\{0,\ldots,K+b+1\}$. Embedding the actual paths into all paths of that frozen alphabet preserves each weight $v^{K'-K}$ exactly and adds only nonnegative weights. Apply Lemma~\ref{wasc:gr:lem:frozen} with $M=K+b+2$. This reasoning also works when $v<1$: it adds positive terms, rather than relying on monotonicity of $v^K$ in $K$. The compact bound follows from \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:eigenestimate} and boundedness of $1/g$.
\end{proof}

\section{Calibrated block proof of the tilted upper bound}\label{wasc:gr:sec:calibration}
\subsection{A statewise calibration}
Fix the terminal mark $v_0>0$ and put $\nu=1/\chi(v_0)$. The function $\chi$ is positive, strictly decreasing, and smooth on $(0,\infty)$, with
\[
\chi'(v)=-g(v)/v,\qquad \chi(v)\longrightarrow0\quad(v\to\infty).
\]
For $0<\tau\le1$, define $v(\tau)\ge v_0$ uniquely by
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:curve}
\tau=\nu\chi(v(\tau)),\qquad \theta(\tau)=\log v(\tau).
\end{equation}
Differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:calibration}
\theta'(\tau)=-\frac1{\nu g(v(\tau))},\qquad
\frac{y}{\nu g(v(\tau))}=-\theta'(\tau)y\quad(y\ge0).
\end{equation}
This is a global identity for every possible scaled state $y$, not an equation presumed to hold along a typical trajectory. For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $\theta$ and its first two derivatives are bounded on $[\varepsilon,1]$. The terminal value $\theta(1)=\log v_0$ can be negative.

For $v\ge2$,
\[
\chi(v)\le2\int_v^\infty\frac{\log t}{t^2}\dd t
=\frac{2(\log v+1)}v=O(v^{-1/2}).
\]
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:curve} therefore implies
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:thetaendpoint}
|\theta(\varepsilon)|=O(\log(1/\varepsilon)),\qquad
\varepsilon|\theta(\varepsilon)|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{One block and cancellation}
Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, take $k=\lceil\varepsilon n\rceil$, and divide the extensions from length $k$ to length $n$ into $J=O(1/\delta)$ blocks of lengths $b_j\le\delta n$, allowing harmless integer endpoint adjustments. On a block starting at scaled time $\tau$, put $b=b_j$, $\theta=\theta(\tau)$, and
\[
\Delta\theta=\theta(\tau+b/n)-\theta(\tau),\qquad v=\ee^\theta.
\]
Since $0\le K'-K\le b$,
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:blockpotential}
\sum_{\text{paths}}\ee^{\theta(\tau+b/n)K'}
&\le \ee^{\theta K+\Delta\theta K+b|\Delta\theta|}
\max(v,v^{-1})\lambda_{K+b+2}(v)^b.
\end{align}
The constants below may depend on $\varepsilon,v_0$, but are independent of $n$, the state, and the subsequently chosen small $\delta$.

Apply $\log x\le x-1$ to $x=\lambda/(n\nu)$ and use \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:blockeig} and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:calibration}:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:lambdaexponent}
\lambda^b\le(n\nu/\ee)^b
\exp\!\left(-\frac bn\theta'(\tau)K+C\frac{b(b+1)}n\right).
\end{equation}
Taylor's theorem gives
\[
\Delta\theta=\frac bn\theta'(\tau)+O(b^2/n^2),\qquad
|\Delta\theta|=O(b/n).
\]
Every reachable state has $K\le n$. Thus the terms linear in $K$ cancel between \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:blockpotential} and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:lambdaexponent}, and the remaining state term is $O(b^2/n)$. The eigenvector condition number contributes $O(1)$ to the logarithm per block. We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:oneblockfinal}
\sum_{\text{paths}}\ee^{\theta(\tau+b/n)K'}
\le \ee^{\theta(\tau)K}(n\nu/\ee)^b
\exp\!\bigl(C(b^2/n+1)\bigr).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Positive telescope and ordered limits}
Let $Q_j$ denote the positive operator that sums over actual paths in block $j$, and let $V_j(K,L)=\exp(\theta(\tau_j)K)$. Equation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:oneblockfinal} says $Q_jV_{j+1}\le c_jV_j$ for a scalar $c_j$ independent of the state. Positivity allows backward composition of these inequalities. This does not require a shared alphabet or a shared eigenvector between successive blocks.

Using $\sum b_j=n-k$ and $\sum b_j^2/n\le\delta n$, and then summing over length-$k$ prefixes, yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:telescope}
H_n(v_0)\le k!\exp(k|\theta(k/n)|)
(n\nu/\ee)^{n-k}\exp\!\bigl(C(n\delta+J)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here $a_k\le k!$ and $K\le k-1$ give the prefix bound for either sign of $\theta(k/n)$. Stirling's formula reduces \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:telescope} to
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:orderedlimit}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\frac{H_n(v_0)}{n!}
\le \varepsilon\log\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)\log\nu
+\varepsilon|\theta(\varepsilon)|+C\delta.
\end{align}
First fix $v_0,\varepsilon,\delta$ and take the indicated limit in $n$. Next send $\delta\downarrow0$ with $\varepsilon$ fixed. Finally send $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ and apply \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:thetaendpoint}. The result is \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:tilt}. No uniformity in $\varepsilon$ of the block error constant is needed because that error has already been removed before the last limit.

\section{The exact constant and the ratio limit}\label{wasc:gr:sec:ratio}
\subsection{Ordinary ascent sequences supply the lower bound}
Let $F_n^{\mathrm{Fish}}$ be the ordinary Fishburn number, counting ordinary ascent sequences with strict ascents. Every ordinary ascent sequence satisfies \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:definition}, because each prefix has at least as many weak ascents as strict ascents. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:fishburninclusion}
F_n^{\mathrm{Fish}}\le a_n\le n!.
\end{equation}
We use the established result recorded by Hwang--Jin \cite[Eq.~(1.1)]{hwang}:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:fishburn}
F_n^{\mathrm{Fish}}=C_F n!\mu^n\sqrt n\,(1+O(n^{-1})),
\qquad C_F>0.
\end{equation}
Their displayed form uses a positive constant times $(6/(\ee\pi^2))^n n^{n+1}$; Stirling gives \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:fishburn}. The actual value of $C_F$ is not needed here. This is an external theorem about ordinary Fishburn numbers, not about the weak-ascent sequence.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{wasc:lv:part} records the value $C_F=12\sqrt3\,\pi^{-5/2}\ee^{\pi^2/12}$ in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:fishburn}, with the same references, and the corresponding primitive constant $C_P=C_F\ee^{-\pi^2/6}$ in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:primitiveratio}.
\end{writenote}

At $v=1$, substitute $x=1/t$ and expand a nonnegative geometric series:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:constant}
\chi(1)=\int_0^1\frac{-\log x}{1-x}\dd x
=\sum_{m\ge1}\int_0^1x^{m-1}(-\log x)\dd x
=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac1{m^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\end{equation}
Monotone convergence justifies the exchange. The upper bound \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:tilt} at $v=1$ and the lower inclusion \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:fishburn} coincide at $\mu$, proving the first limit in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:rootratio}.

\subsection{Two-sided exponential concentration}
Set
\[
P(q)=-\log\chi(\ee^q).
\]
This is smooth near zero and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:pressurederivative}
P(0)=\log\mu,\qquad
P'(q)=\frac{g(\ee^q)}{\chi(\ee^q)},\qquad P'(0)=\mu.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:tilt} with the unweighted root limit gives, for every fixed real $q$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:mgfupper}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\E_n\ee^{qK_n}
\le P(q)-P(0).
\end{equation}
A lower tilted bound at $q\ne0$ has not been used or proved.

Given $\eta>0$, differentiability permits a sufficiently small $q>0$ with
\[
P(q)-P(0)-q(\mu+\eta)<0.
\]
Exponential Markov inequality then makes $\Prob(K_n/n\ge\mu+\eta)$ exponentially small. A sufficiently small $q<0$ satisfies
\[
P(q)-P(0)-q(\mu-\eta)<0
\]
and gives the lower tail, since $\ee^{qK_n}\ge\ee^{qn(\mu-\eta)}$ on that event. Combining the two bounds and slightly reducing the exponent proves \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:concentration}. Empty tail events cause no difficulty.

Since $0\le K_n/n\le1$, convergence in probability implies convergence of the first moment: for instance split $\E|K_n/n-\mu|$ at any fixed $\eta>0$ and use the tail bound. Thus $\E K_n/n\to\mu$. Equation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:children} now gives
\[
\frac{a_{n+1}}{(n+1)a_n}=
\frac{n}{n+1}\frac{\E K_n}{n}+\frac2{n+1}\longrightarrow\mu.
\]
This completes the weak-ascent part of Theorem~\ref{wasc:gr:thm:main}.

\section{A preliminary logarithmic square upper bound}\label{wasc:gr:sec:logsquare}
This section gives the simpler $3/4$ upper bound that supplies the calibration and small-state estimates for the sharp proof in Sections~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:sharpupper} and~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:matchinglower}. It uses special reachability geometry for weak ascents. All calibrations now depend on the target length $n$; the uniform estimates are derived afresh.

\subsection{Reachability and the moving calibration}
\begin{lemma}\label{wasc:gr:lem:geometry}
At length $i$ with $K$ weak ascents, put $M=K+2$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:geometry}
i\le\frac{(K+1)(K+2)}2,\qquad M\ge\sqrt{2i}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split the sequence at every weak ascent into $K+1$ strictly decreasing runs. Number the runs $r=0,\ldots,K$. The first letter of run $r$ is at most $r$ by the state support, so that run has at most $r+1$ nonnegative letters. Sum these bounds. Since $M(M-1)\ge2i$, the displayed weaker lower bound on $M$ follows.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{wasc:lv:part} reproves this lemma as \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:reachable}; Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}, \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:reachable}, proves it for every fixed $d\ge1$ (the runs between $d$-ascents are strictly decreasing).
\end{writenote}

For $0<s\le n$, define $v(s)\ge1$, $q(s)$, and $S(s)$ by
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:movingcalibration}
s=n\mu\chi(v(s)),\qquad q(s)=\log v(s),\qquad
S(s)=n\mu g(v(s)).
\end{equation}
Differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:Sderivative}
q'(s)=-1/S(s),\qquad
S'(s)=\frac{v}{v-1}-\frac1{\log v}\in[1/2,1].
\end{equation}
To see the interval assertion, put $q=\log v\ge0$. The expression is the mean of $x\in[0,1]$ under density proportional to $\ee^{qx}$. It equals $1/2$ at $q=0$, increases with $q$ because its derivative is a variance, and is at most one. Furthermore $S(0+)=0$, $S(n)=n\mu$, and $q(n)=0$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:Sqbounds}
\frac s2\le S(s)\le s,\qquad 0\le q(s)\le2\log(n/s).
\end{equation}
Expanding the integrand for large $v$ and integrating also gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:chiasymptotic}
\chi(v)=\frac{\log v+1}{v}+O\!\left(\frac{\log v}{v^2}\right),
\qquad v\chi(v)=\log v+1+o(1).
\end{equation}

\subsection{An exact moving positive potential}
For every reachable state at time $i$ set
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:potential}
V_i(K,L)=\exp\!\left(q(i)K-\frac{q(i)L}{K+2}\right).
\end{equation}
Then $V_n=1$. In a single extension let $M=K+2$, $a=\ind_{\{j\ge L\}}$, $q=q(i+1)$, $S=S(i+1)$, and $\Delta q=q(i+1)-q(i)\le0$. Since $0\le j<M$ and $q\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:denominatorloss}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{qj}{M+a}\right)
\le\exp(q/M)\exp(-qj/M).
\end{equation}
Indeed the exponent difference is $qja/[M(M+a)]\le q/M$. Summing the exact frozen eigenvector identity of Lemma~\ref{wasc:gr:lem:frozen}, then dividing by $V_i$, yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:movingonestep}
\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}V_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{V_i(K,L)}
\le\lambda_M(\ee^q)\exp(\Delta qK+q/M+|\Delta q|).
\end{equation}
The old-versus-new comparison contributes $-\Delta q L/M\le|\Delta q|$; this is why the moving denominator cannot simply be ignored.

Because $S$ is increasing,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:qdecrement}
\Delta q=-\int_i^{i+1}\frac{\dd s}{S(s)}\le-1/S(i+1),
\qquad |\Delta q|\le1/S(i)\le2/i.
\end{equation}
The elementary inequality $2\sinh(x/2)\ge x$ for $x\ge0$ gives
\[
\frac{x}{1-\ee^{-x}}\le\ee^{x/2},
\qquad
\lambda_M(\ee^q)\le\frac{M}{g(\ee^q)}\exp(q/(2M)),
\]
with removable interpretations at zero. Let
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:ralpha}
r=M/S,\qquad \alpha=3q/(2S),\qquad \Psi(r)=\log r-r+1.
\end{equation}
Taking logarithms of \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:movingonestep}, dividing its bound by $n\mu/\ee$, and using $K=M-2$ gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:globalstep}
\log\left(\frac{\sum_jV_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{(n\mu/\ee)V_i(K,L)}\right)
&\le \log r+1-\frac{M-2}{S}+\frac{3q}{2M}+\frac2i\notag\\
&\le\Psi(r)+\frac\alpha r+\frac6i.
\end{align}
This estimate holds for every reachable state, including states with very small $K$ relative to $i$.

\subsection{Uniform treatment of small reachable states}
Put $T=\log n$, $U=\log T$, and choose
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:cutoff}
k=\left\lceil\frac{100T^2}{U^2}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Only sufficiently large $n$ is considered, so $U>0$ and $1\le k<n$. For $i\ge k$, \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:Sqbounds} gives $\alpha\le6T/i=o(1)$ uniformly, hence eventually $\alpha\le1/8$.

For $0\le\alpha\le1/8$ define $h_\alpha(r)=\Psi(r)+\alpha/r$. Its derivative is
\[
h_\alpha'(r)=\frac{r-r^2-\alpha}{r^2}.
\]
Its maximum over $[m,\infty)$ occurs at the endpoint or is no larger than its value at
\[
r_+(\alpha)=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\alpha}}2.
\]
The latter point has
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:nearone}
h_\alpha(r_+(\alpha))\le\alpha+2\alpha^2.
\end{equation}
For a proof, the derivative of this value in $\alpha$ is $1/r_+(\alpha)$. Since $r_+\ge1-2\alpha$, this derivative is at most $1+4\alpha$ on the stated interval, and the value at zero is zero.

The geometry gives $r\ge m=\sqrt{2i}/S(i+1)$. Using $S(i+1)\ge(i+1)/2$, $q(i+1)\le2T$, and dropping the nonpositive term $-m$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:boundary}
h_\alpha(m)\le-\tfrac12\log i+1+\tfrac32\log2+\frac{3T}{\sqrt{2i}}.
\end{equation}
The right side decreases with $i$. At $i=k$ it is bounded by
\[
-\left(1-\frac3{\sqrt{200}}\right)U+\log U+O(1),
\]
which tends to $-\infty$. Thus the endpoint value is nonpositive for every $i\ge k$, once $n$ is sufficiently large. Equations \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:nearone} and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:boundary} prove the uniform statewise bound
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:absorption}
\Psi(r)+\alpha/r\le\alpha+2\alpha^2.
\end{equation}
The cutoff is used to control all reachable states; no typical-state assumption has entered.

\subsection{Accumulation and the prefix cost}
Combine \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:globalstep} and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:absorption}, and compose the positive one-step inequalities backward from $V_n=1$. Summing the initial states at length $k$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:finetelescope}
a_n\le k!\ee^{kq(k)}(n\mu/\ee)^{n-k}
\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}(\alpha_i+2\alpha_i^2+6/i)\right),
\quad \alpha_i=\frac{3q(i+1)}{2S(i+1)}.
\end{equation}
The prefix estimate follows from $a_k\le k!$ and $V_k(K,L)\le\ee^{kq(k)}$.

Since $(q/S)'=-(1+qS')/S^2<0$, a right-endpoint integral estimate gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:mainaccumulation}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\alpha_i
\le\frac32\int_k^n\frac{q(s)}{S(s)}\dd s
=\frac34q(k)^2.
\end{equation}
Also,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:smallerrors}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\alpha_i^2=O(T^2/k)=O(U^2)=o(T^2),
\qquad \sum_{i=k}^{n-1}1/i=O(T)=o(T^2).
\end{equation}
As $k/n\to0$, the calibration implies $v(k)\to\infty$. Using \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:chiasymptotic} and $q(k)\le2T$,
\[
q(k)=T-\log k+\log\mu+\log(q(k)+1)+o(1)=T+O(\log T).
\]
For the lower estimate in the last equality use $\log(q(k)+1)\ge0$; the upper estimate uses $q(k)\le2T$. Hence $q(k)/T\to1$.

Stirling's formula and $k/(n\mu)=\chi(v(k))$ give the precise prefix cancellation
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:prefixcancel}
\log\frac{k!\ee^{kq(k)}(n\mu/\ee)^{n-k}}{n!\mu^n}
&=k\log\frac{k\ee^{q(k)}}{n\mu}+\tfrac12\log(k/n)+O(1/k)\notag\\
&=k\log(v(k)\chi(v(k)))+\tfrac12\log(k/n)+O(1/k)\notag\\
&=O(k\log T)+O(T)=O(T^2/U)+O(T)=o(T^2).
\end{align}
In particular an apparent $k\log n$ cost has canceled; it cannot be discarded before this calculation. Equations \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:finetelescope}--\eqref{wasc:gr:eq:prefixcancel} prove the preliminary upper limit $3/4$.

Finally \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:fishburn} and inclusion give
\[
\log\frac{a_n}{n!\mu^n}\ge\tfrac12\log n+\log C_F+O(n^{-1}),
\]
so the normalized lower limit is at least zero. The next two sections sharpen both sides to one half.

\section{The sharp upper logarithmic correction}\label{wasc:gr:sec:sharpupper}
The preceding $3/4$ bound can be improved to $1/2$ by a quadratic corrector. We give the finite identity and all uniform estimates, because replacing a worst-case state by a stationary average without these estimates would not be valid.

\subsection{A circulant chain and an exact Poisson identity}
For $q>0$ and $M\ge1$ set
\[
r=\ee^{-q/M},\qquad B=\frac r{1-r},\qquad D=1-r^M.
\]
The Doob transform of the frozen matrix, defined by
$P(l,j)=T_{\ee^q}(l,j)f_j/(\lambda_M(\ee^q)f_l)$, is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:doob}
P(l,j)=\frac{1-r}{1-r^M}r^{(j-l)\bmod M}.
\end{equation}
It is a stochastic circulant matrix, so the uniform law on the alphabet is stationary. The forward circular increment $W=((j-l)\bmod M)/M$ has a truncated geometric distribution independent of $l$. Write $a(l,j)=\ind_{\{j\ge l\}}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:bcdef}
b_l=\E_l[q\,a(l,J)J/M],\qquad c=\frac1M\sum_{l=0}^{M-1}b_l.
\end{equation}
Stationarity gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:cupper}
0\le c\le q(M-1)/(2M)\le q/2.
\end{equation}
Direct geometric summation gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:bcformula}
b_l&=\frac q{MD}\left(l+B-(M+B)r^{M-l}\right),\notag\\
c&=\frac q{M^2D}\left(\frac{M(M-1)}2+MB-(M+B)BD\right).
\end{align}
Define, also at the auxiliary endpoint $l=M$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:hquadratic}
h(q,M,l)=\left(\frac cB+\frac q{2MDB}\right)l-\frac{ql^2}{2MDB}.
\end{equation}
Then the exact finite Poisson equation is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:poisson}
(Ph)_l-h_l=c-b_l\qquad(0\le l<M).
\end{equation}
Here $h_l$ denotes \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:hquadratic}; its value at $M$ need not equal the value at zero.

To verify \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:poisson} algebraically, set $y_l=h_l+c-b_l$. For $0\le l<M-1$, subtraction in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:bcformula}--\eqref{wasc:gr:eq:hquadratic} gives
\[
y_{l+1}-y_l=(c-b_l)/B=(y_l-h_l)/B.
\]
The same relation holds at the cyclic boundary, because the sum of the right side is zero and the sum of cyclic differences is zero. Thus $y_l-r y_{l+1}=(1-r)h_l$ with cyclic indices. If $C$ is the forward cyclic shift, $I-rC$ is invertible and
\[
y=\frac{1-r}{1-r^M}\sum_{s=0}^{M-1}r^s C^s h=Ph,
\]
which proves the identity. For $M=1$ it is immediate. All formulas have continuous limits at $q=0$, with $b=c=h=0$ and $P$ the uniform matrix.

\subsection{Uniform corrector and moment estimates}
Put $Q=1+q$, and view $M$ as real when differentiating the displayed formulas. For $H(q,M,x)=h(q,M,Mx)$, elementary simplification gives the stable form
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:stableh}
H(q,M,x)&=A x(1-x)+J_0x,\notag\\
A&=\frac{q^2}{2(1-\ee^{-q})}\,\beta(q/M),\quad
\beta(z)=\frac{\ee^z-1}{z},\notag\\
J_0&=Q_0(q)-Q_0(q/M),\quad Q_0(z)=\frac z{\ee^z-1}.
\end{align}
Removable values are used at zero. Choose a sufficiently large absolute constant $C_0$. Uniformly for $M\ge C_0Q$ and $0\le x\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:hestimates}
|H|+|H_x|\le C Q^2,\quad |H_q|\le C Q,\quad
|H_M|\le C Q^3/M^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\beta$ and its first two derivatives are bounded on $[0,1]$. The function $q^2/[2(1-\ee^{-q})]$ is $O(Q^2)$ with derivative $O(Q)$, while $Q_0$ and $Q_0'$ are bounded on $[0,\infty)$. Differentiating \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:stableh} proves the estimates. One may first choose these bounds on $q/M\le1$ and increase $C_0$ afterward, so the constants do not depend circularly on the final choice of $C_0$.

The seam jump is particularly small:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:seam}
H(q,M,1)-H(q,M,0)=J_0\in[-1,0].
\end{equation}
Integrating $H_x$ along a forward circular arc and crossing this seam at most once gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:circularh}
|h(q,M,J)-h(q,M,l)|\le C Q^2W+1.
\end{equation}
For each fixed integer $j\ge0$ and fixed $A_0>0$, the geometric law \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:doob} gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:geometricmoments}
\E[W^j\exp(A_0Q^2W/M)]\le C_j Q^{-j},
\end{equation}
provided $C_0$ is large enough for that $A_0$. For $q\ge1$, the added exponent is at most $qW/2$, so the sum is bounded using $\ee^{-qW/2}$. Its normalization is $O(q/M)$ and its $j$th moment sum is $O(M/q^{j+1})$, uniformly for $M/q$ bounded below. These estimates follow, for example, by comparing the geometric sums with the integrals of $x^j\ee^{-qx/2}$ plus the endpoint term. For $q\le1$, use $W\le1$ and boundedness of $Q$. The law is independent of $l$, so all bounds are uniform in the current letter.

For $0\le\zeta\le1$, set
\[
Z=q\,a(l,J)J/M+\zeta[h(q,M,J)-h(q,M,l)].
\]
Equation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:poisson} says $\E_l Z=(1-\zeta)b_l+\zeta c$. By \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:circularh} and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:geometricmoments},
\[
\E_l[Z^2\ee^{|Z|/M}]\le C Q^2.
\]
Apply $|\ee^x-1-x|\le x^2\ee^{|x|}/2$ and then $\log(1+x)\le x$ to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:correctorexp}
\log\E_l\ee^{Z/M}\le\frac{(1-\zeta)b_l+\zeta c}{M}
+\frac{C Q^2}{M^2}.
\end{equation}
This controls the random fluctuation around the stationary mean, instead of merely substituting that mean in a pointwise expression.

\subsection{The corrected moving potential and its three state ranges}
Use the calibration $q(s),S(s)$ and cutoff $k$ of Section~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:logsquare}. Let $\zeta(r)$ be zero for $r\le1/2$, equal to $4r-2$ on $[1/2,3/4]$, and one for $r\ge3/4$. It is bounded by one and has Lipschitz constant four. Define
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:correctedpotential}
\widetilde V_i(K,L)=\exp\!\left(q(i)K-\frac{q(i)L}{M}
+\zeta\!\left(\frac{M}{S(i)}\right)\frac{h(q(i),M,L)}M\right),
\quad M=K+2.
\end{equation}
The terminal potential is exactly one. For a step at time $i$, freeze $q=q(i+1)$, $S=S(i+1)$, and $r_0=M/S$. When $M\ge0.49S$, we have $M\ge c i$ and $i/Q\to\infty$ uniformly for $i\ge k$. Thus \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:hestimates} applies.

Replacing the actual next corrector
\[
\zeta((M+a)/S)h(q,M+a,j)/(M+a)
\]
by its frozen version $\zeta(M/S)h(q,M,j)/M$ costs $O(Q^2/i^2)$ in the exponent. At fixed $j$, changing $M$ changes $x=j/M$ by $O(1/M)$, so \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:hestimates}, the derivative of $1/M$, and the Lipschitz estimate for $\zeta$ give this bound. Replacing the current corrector by its frozen $q,S$ values has the same cost: $|\Delta q|\le2/i$, $|\Delta S|\le1$, and
\[
|\Delta\zeta|\le4M|\Delta S|/(S(i)S(i+1)).
\]
These estimates also handle the corners of the piecewise linear cutoff.

The eigenvector denominator discrepancy is bounded above by $qaj/M^2$. Factoring out the exact frozen eigenvector and applying \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:correctorexp}, together with the eigenvalue correction $q/(2M)$, gives, if $r_0\ge3/4$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:correctedstep}
\log\frac{\sum_j\widetilde V_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{(n\mu/\ee)\widetilde V_i(K,L)}
\le\Psi(r_0)+\frac{q}{Sr_0}
+C\left(\frac{Q^2}{i^2}+\frac1i\right).
\end{equation}
Here $\zeta=1$ and $c\le q/2$; the calibration terms are those in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:globalstep}. In the intermediate range $0.49\le r_0<3/4$, the same argument with $b_l\le q$, $c\le q/2$ gives $3q/(2Sr_0)$ in place of $q/(Sr_0)$. Since $\Psi(r_0)\le\Psi(3/4)<0$ there and all other terms are uniformly $o(1)$, this entire range has a negative normalized logarithmic bound for large $n$.

If $r_0<0.49$, both cutoff terms vanish for large $i$:
\[
M/S(i)\le0.49(1+2/i)<1/2,\qquad
(M+a)/S(i+1)\le0.49+2/(i+1)<1/2.
\]
Use the uncorrected estimate \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:globalstep}. Put $\alpha=3q/(2S)=o(1)$ and $m=\sqrt{2i}/S$. If $m>0.49$ the range is empty. Otherwise $h_\alpha(r)=\Psi(r)+\alpha/r$ has no local maximum on $[m,0.49]$: its smaller critical point is a minimum and its larger one tends to one. Its supremum is therefore at an endpoint. Equation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:boundary} bounds $h_\alpha(m)$ by a quantity tending uniformly to $-\infty$; the other endpoint satisfies $h_\alpha(0.49)\to\Psi(0.49)<0$ uniformly. Both are eventually bounded above by a fixed negative constant, absorbing the additional $6/i$. This proves negativity in the full small-state range without applying a corrector estimate where $M$ might be too small.

Finally for $\beta=q/S=o(1)$, the same critical-point calculation as \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:nearone} gives
\[
\sup_{r\ge3/4}[\Psi(r)+\beta/r]\le\beta+2\beta^2.
\]
Since $\beta^2=O(Q^2/i^2)$, the three ranges combine into the uniform one-step bound
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:finalsharpstep}
\frac{\sum_j\widetilde V_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{\widetilde V_i(K,L)}
\le(n\mu/\ee)\exp\!\left(\frac{q(i+1)}{S(i+1)}
+C\left[\frac{(1+q(i+1))^2}{i^2}+\frac1i\right]\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sharp upper accumulation}
At time $k$, a nonzero cutoff implies $M>S(k)/2\ge k/4$. Hence the added corrector exponent is $O((1+q(k))^2/k)$, and
\[
\sum_{|x|=k}\widetilde V_k\le k!\exp\!\left(kq(k)+O((1+q(k))^2/k)\right).
\]
The exact prefix cancellation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:prefixcancel} remains unchanged at order $o(T^2)$. Backward iteration of \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:finalsharpstep}, using the decreasing function $q/S$, gives
\[
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q(i+1)}{S(i+1)}\le\int_k^n\frac qS\dd s=\frac12q(k)^2.
\]
The other errors sum to $O(T^2/k+T)=o(T^2)$, and the new prefix error is $O(U^2)$. Since $q(k)/T\to1$, we have proved
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:halfupper}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(a_n/(n!\mu^n))}{(\log n)^2}\le\frac12.
\end{equation}

\section{The matching lower logarithmic correction}\label{wasc:gr:sec:matchinglower}
The lower bound uses an exact change of measure on legal extension paths. The quadratic corrector controls their expected gain, while a martingale estimate keeps the alphabet size close enough to the calibration. No counting principle for presumed typical paths is needed.

\subsection{A legal path measure and Jensen bound}
Retain $T=\log n$, $U=\log T$, and $k=\lceil100T^2/U^2\rceil$. Choose the single legal prefix of $k$ zeros; its state is $K_k=k-1$, $L_k=0$. For each $i=k,\ldots,n-1$, sample the next letter from the geometric Doob matrix \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:doob} with $q=q(i+1)$ and $M=K_i+2$. All letters $0,\ldots,M-1$ are legal, and every legal completion has positive probability. Let $\E_*$ and $\Prob_*$ refer to this path measure, which is distinct from the uniform law on all weak ascent sequences.

Since $K$ never decreases, $M\ge k+1$ along every sampled path. Thus $M/(1+q)\ge k/(1+2T)\to\infty$ uniformly. We may use all corrector estimates without a state cutoff. Define
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:uncutpotential}
U_i(K,L)=\exp\!\left(q(i)K-\frac{q(i)L}{M}+\frac{h(q(i),M,L)}M\right).
\end{equation}
Its terminal value is one. For a step to $(K+a,j)$, put
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:likelihood}
R_i=\frac{U_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{U_i(K,L)P_i(L,j)}.
\end{equation}
Multiplication along a path telescopes the potential ratios. Since its probability is the product of its transition probabilities, the exact number of completions of the selected prefix is
\[
U_k(k-1,0)\,\E_*\exp\!\left(\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\log R_i\right).
\]
Here $h(q,M,0)=0$, so Jensen's inequality and inclusion of these completions among all objects give
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:jensen}
\log a_n\ge(k-1)q(k)+\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_*\log R_i.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Tracking the random alphabet size}
Set $M_i=K_i+2$, $X_i=L_i/M_i$, and $Y_i=K_i-X_i$. If $W_i=((j-L_i)\bmod M_i)/M_i$ and $a=\ind_{\{j\ge L_i\}}$, then exactly
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:Yincrement}
Y_{i+1}-Y_i=1-W_i+aX_{i+1}/M_i.
\end{equation}
Indeed $j/M_i-X_i=W_i-(1-a)$ and $j/M_i=X_{i+1}(1+a/M_i)$. The increment in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:Yincrement} belongs to $[0,2]$. Its conditional mean is at least $1/2$, since the decreasing geometric weights on $0,\ldots,M_i-1$ imply $\E_*(W_i\mid\mathcal F_i)\le1/2$.

The centered increments therefore form a martingale with differences bounded in absolute value by two. For completeness, the elementary bounded-difference estimate follows by applying the conditional exponential bound $\E_*(\ee^{tD_i}\mid\mathcal F_i)\le\ee^{2t^2}$ to each centered difference $D_i$, iterating, and optimizing exponential Markov inequality. It gives a tail at most $\exp(-z^2/(8m))$ for a downward displacement $z$ over $m$ increments. Since $Y_k=k-1$, the event $M_i<i/4$ requires a downward displacement at least $i/4+k/2-1$ from the accumulated lower drift. It is empty at $i=k$. Thus for absolute $c,C>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:badstate}
\Prob_*(M_i<i/4)\le C\ee^{-ci}\qquad(k\le i\le n)
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $n$.

The exact conditional geometric mean is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:Wmean}
\E_*(W_i\mid\mathcal F_i)=
\frac1{M_i(\ee^{q/M_i}-1)}-\frac1{\ee^q-1},\qquad q=q(i+1).
\end{equation}
Let $m(q)=1-1/q+1/(\ee^q-1)$, with its removable value $1/2$ at zero, so $S'(s)=m(q(s))$. Expanding $z/(\ee^z-1)$ at zero, uniformly because $M_i\ge k\gg1+q$, gives
\[
1-\E_*(W_i\mid\mathcal F_i)=m(q)+O(1/M_i).
\]
The last term in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:Yincrement} lies in $[0,1/M_i]$. Moreover $m'(q)$ is the variance of a tilted variable in $[0,1]$ and is bounded. Using $|q(i+1)-q(i)|\le2/i$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:drifttracking}
\left|\E_*(Y_{i+1}-Y_i\mid\mathcal F_i)-[S(i+1)-S(i)]\right|
\le C(1/M_i+1/i).
\end{equation}
On the good event $M_i\ge i/4$, $1/M_i\le4/i$. On its complement, $M_i\ge k$ and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:badstate} applies. Minkowski's inequality in $L^2$ now gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:reciprocaltracking}
\left\|\sum_{i=k}^{m-1}1/M_i\right\|_2\le C\log n\qquad(k\le m\le n).
\end{equation}
Explicitly, the good part is bounded deterministically by $4\sum1/i$, and the norm of the bad part is at most $k^{-1}\sum_{i\ge k}C\ee^{-ci/2}$.

The centered martingale has second moment $O(m)$ by orthogonality of its increments. Sum \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:drifttracking}, use \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:reciprocaltracking} and $|Y_k-S(k)|\le k$, and note that replacing $Y_i$ by $M_i$ or $S(i)$ by $S(i+1)$ changes the difference by a bounded amount. We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:quadratictracking}
\E_*[(M_i-S(i+1))^2]\le C(i+T^2+k^2),\qquad k\le i<n.
\end{equation}
This deliberately coarse estimate is sufficient at logarithmic-square scale.

\subsection{Expected gain for one step}
Freeze $q=q(i+1)$, $S=S(i+1)$, $M=M_i$, $l=L_i$, and $\Delta q=q(i+1)-q(i)$. Substituting the exact Doob formula into \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:likelihood} gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:logRexact}
\log R_i={}&\log\lambda_M(\ee^q)+\Delta qK_i-\Delta ql/M
+\frac{qaj}{M(M+a)}\notag\\
&+\frac{h(q,M+a,j)}{M+a}-\frac{h(q(i),M,l)}M.
\end{align}
The estimates \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:hestimates}, including the change of $x=j/M$ at fixed $j$, show that replacing the last two corrector terms by $[h(q,M,j)-h(q,M,l)]/M$ costs at most
\[
C\left(\frac{Q^2}{M^2}+\frac{Q}{Mi}\right),\qquad Q=1+q.
\]
Also $qaj/[M(M+a)]\ge qaj/M^2-q/M^2$. The Poisson identity then implies that the conditional expectation of the last term on the first line and all of the second line in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:logRexact} is at least
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:gaincorrector}
\frac cM-C\left(\frac{Q^2}{M^2}+\frac{Q}{Mi}\right).
\end{equation}

We need a lower estimate as well as \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:cupper}:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:clower}
q/2-C(1+q/M)\le c\le q/2.
\end{equation}
To verify it, sample a stationary uniform starting letter. The output letter $J$ is then uniform, and
\[
q(M-1)/(2M)-c=q\,\E_{\mathrm{stat}}[(1-a)J/M].
\]
Conditional on a forward increment $d$, exactly $d$ starting letters wrap, and their outputs are $0,\ldots,d-1$. Hence
\[
\E_{\mathrm{stat}}[(1-a)J/M\mid d]=d(d-1)/(2M^2)\le W^2/2.
\]
The un-tilted case of \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:geometricmoments} gives $\E_{\mathrm{stat}}W^2\le C/(1+q)^2$, proving \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:clower}.

For $q/M=o(1)$ uniformly, the exact eigenvalue has the lower expansion
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:eigenlower}
\log\lambda_M(\ee^q)\ge\log(n\mu)+\log(M/S)
+q/(2M)-Cq^2/M^2.
\end{equation}
This follows from $x/(1-\ee^{-x})=\ee^{x/2}(x/2)/\sinh(x/2)$ and the bounded Taylor remainder of $\log(\sinh(x/2)/(x/2))$ near zero. The calibration gives
\[
\Delta q K_i\ge-K_i/S-C/i,\qquad -\Delta ql/M\ge0.
\]
For the first estimate, compare $1/S(s)$ on $[i,i+1]$ with $1/S(i+1)$ using $|S'|\le1$, $S(s)\ge i/2$, and $K_i\le i$. Combining these bounds with \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:gaincorrector}--\eqref{wasc:gr:eq:eigenlower}, using $K_i=M-2$ and dropping the favorable $2/S$, yields
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:expectedstep}
\E_*(\log R_i\mid\mathcal F_i)-\log(n\mu/\ee)
\ge\Psi(M/S)+\frac qM
-C\left(\frac1i+\frac1M+\frac{Q^2}{M^2}+\frac{Q}{Mi}\right).
\end{align}

\subsection{Summing the tracking errors and normalizing}
On the good event $M_i\ge i/4$, the ratio $M_i/S(i+1)$ belongs to a fixed compact interval, for example $[1/5,3]$ for large $i$. Since $\Psi(1)=\Psi'(1)=0$,
\[
\Psi(M_i/S)\ge-C(M_i-S)^2/i^2.
\]
On the bad event $M_i\ge k$, while the deterministic support gives $M_i\le i+1$, so $|\Psi(M_i/S)|\le C+T$. Equations \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:badstate} and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:quadratictracking} imply
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:psitotal}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_*\Psi(M_i/S(i+1))
&\ge-C\left(T+\frac{T^2+k^2}{k}\right)-o(1)\notag\\
&=-O(T+U^2+k)=-o(T^2).
\end{align}
For the positive main term, on good states
\[
1/M_i\ge1/S-C|M_i-S|/i^2.
\]
Drop its nonnegative contribution on bad states, bound the missing $q/S$ there by \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:badstate}, and use Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:quadratictracking}. This gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:qMtotal}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_*\frac{q(i+1)}{M_i}
&\ge\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q(i+1)}{S(i+1)}
-CT\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{\sqrt i+T+k}{i^2}-o(1)\notag\\
&\ge\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q(i+1)}{S(i+1)}
-O(T/\sqrt k+T^2/k+T)\notag\\
&=\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q(i+1)}{S(i+1)}-o(T^2).
\end{align}
The error terms on the right side of \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:expectedstep} sum in expectation to $O(T+T^2/k)=o(T^2)$ by the same good/bad split. Since $q/S$ decreases, its right Riemann sum differs from its integral by at most $q(k)/S(k)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:lowerriemann}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q(i+1)}{S(i+1)}
=\frac12q(k)^2+o(T^2).
\end{equation}

The selected prefix has no multiplicity $k!$, but the missing logarithm is small at this scale. Specifically, subtract \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:prefixcancel} from the desired normalization, or apply Stirling directly, to obtain
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:lowerprefix}
&(k-1)q(k)+(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)-\log(n!\mu^n)\notag\\
&\hspace{16mm}=O(k\log k+k\log(1+q(k))+T)
=O(T^2/U)=o(T^2).
\end{align}
Insert \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:expectedstep}--\eqref{wasc:gr:eq:lowerprefix} into Jensen's inequality \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:jensen}. As $q(k)/T\to1$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:halflower}
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(a_n/(n!\mu^n))}{(\log n)^2}\ge\frac12.
\end{equation}
Together with \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:halfupper}, this proves the matching leading logarithmic correction
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:matching}
\log\frac{a_n}{n!\mu^n}=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)(\log n)^2,
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
a_n=n!\mu^n\exp\!\left(\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)(\log n)^2\right).
\end{equation}
The error inside the exponential can still diverge. Thus \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:matching} is a logarithmic asymptotic, not a relative-error coefficient equivalent and not an amplitude formula.


\subsection{Retaining a quantitative remainder}\label{wasc:gr:sec:quantitative}
The paired bounds prove more than the little-$o$ statement in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:matching}. All constants in the preceding estimates are independent of $n$ after fixing a sufficiently large corrector constant $C_0$. Their error budget is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item both prefix discrepancies are $O(T^2/U)$;
\item $q(k)=T+O(U)$ gives $q(k)^2-T^2=O(TU+U^2)$;
\item the upper corrector and harmonic sums are $O(U^2+T)$;
\item the lower expected slack is $O(k+T+U^2)$;
\item the lower main-term replacement and remaining errors are $O(T+U^2+U)$;
\item the right Riemann-sum discrepancy is $O(T/k)$, and the bad-state tails are exponentially small in $k$, times at most polynomial functions of $T$.
\end{itemize}
With $k=O(T^2/U^2)$, each item is $O(T^2/U)$; for example $TU/(T^2/U)=U^2/T\to0$. Eventual negativity of the small-state ranges introduces no $n$-dependent error constant. Thus the upper and lower arguments establish exactly \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:logsquare}, proving the counting assertion of Theorem~\ref{wasc:gr:thm:logsquare}.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
The two places where this budget reaches $T^2/U$ are the prefix discrepancies, which come from the cutoff $k\asymp T^2/U^2$ and the crude prefix bound $a_k\le k!$. Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part} removes both: a capped slope $\min(q/M,p)$ controls every small state (Section~\ref{wasc:sh:sec:upper}), so the cutoff can be $k=\lceil AT\rceil$, and a run decomposition of the prefixes (Section~\ref{wasc:sh:sec:prefix}) replaces $k!$; keeping the exact normalization \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:normalcancel} together then makes every error $O(T)$.
\end{writenote}

\section{Every fixed difference ascent parameter}\label{wasc:gr:sec:fixedd}
Fix an integer $d\ge0$. A $d$-ascent is an index with $x_{i+1}>x_i-d$. A $d$-ascent sequence starts at zero and obeys
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:ddef}
0\le x_{i+1}\le1+\dasc(x_1,\ldots,x_i).
\end{equation}
This is the convention of Dukes--Sagan \cite{dukes}; $d=0$ is ordinary ascent and $d=1$ is weak ascent. Write $a_n^{(d)}$, $H_n^{(d)}(v)$, and $K_n^{(d)}$ for its count, tilted count, and random statistic.

\subsection{The exact state and a finite band comparison}
The transition is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:dtransition}
(K,L)\longmapsto(K+\ind_{\{j>L-d\}},j),\qquad0\le j\le K+1.
\end{equation}
If the indicator is zero, then $j\le L-d\le L\le K$; if it is one, then $j\le K+1$, the new count. This proves $0\le L\le K\le n-1$ for every $d\ge0$, and hence $a_n^{(d)}\le n!$. Every ordinary ascent is a $d$-ascent, so at every prefix an ordinary ascent sequence satisfies \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:ddef}. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:dinclusion}
F_n^{\mathrm{Fish}}\le a_n^{(d)}\le n!.
\end{equation}
The inclusion is not statistic-preserving and is used only at unweighted mark one.

On an $M$-letter alphabet put
\[
T_{d,v}(l,j)=v^{\ind_{\{j>l-d\}}},\qquad
C_d(v)=|d-1|\,|v-1|\max(v,v^{-1}).
\]
For the weak-ascent eigenvector $f_l=v^{-l/M}$, at most $|d-1|$ entries per row differ between $T_{d,v}$ and $T_{1,v}$. Since $f_j/f_l\le\max(v,v^{-1})$, absolute rowwise comparison gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:dsupersolution}
T_{d,v}f\le\bigl(\lambda_M(v)+C_d(v)\bigr)f.
\end{equation}
At $d=0$ only the diagonal changes; at $d\ge2$ a band below the weak-ascent threshold changes. Alphabet truncation only reduces its size, so the estimate also holds when $d>M$. Taking absolute differences makes it valid for $v<1$.

By positivity and comparison with $\mathbf1$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:dblockmatrix}
T_{d,v}^b\mathbf1\le\max(v,v^{-1})
\bigl(\lambda_M(v)+C_d(v)\bigr)^b\mathbf1.
\end{equation}
For a fixed $d$ and a compact positive mark range,
\[
\lambda_M(v)+C_d(v)\le M/g(v)+O_d(1).
\]
This is a direct positive supersolution estimate; no perturbation theorem for eigenvalues or eigenvectors is assumed.

\subsection{Applying the calibration and deriving the conclusions}
A block of $b$ actual transitions from $(K,L)$ embeds in the alphabet of size $K+b+2$, with the same weights $v^{K'-K}$. Equation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:dblockmatrix} bounds its total by
\[
\max(v,v^{-1})\bigl(\lambda_{K+b+2}(v)+C_d(v)\bigr)^b,
\]
whose eigenvalue majorant is $K/g(v)+O_d(b+1)$. The hypotheses used in Section~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:calibration} have now all been verified: bounded count increments, support $K\le n$, a prefix bound $k!$, uniformly conditioned positive comparison on compact marks, and this last block majorant. The same curve \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:curve} gives the cancellation \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:calibration}. Repeating \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:blockpotential}--\eqref{wasc:gr:eq:orderedlimit}, with constants depending additionally on the fixed $d$, proves
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:dtilt}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\frac{H_n^{(d)}(v)}{n!}
\le-\log\chi(v)\qquad(v>0).
\end{equation}
At $v=1$, \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:dinclusion} supplies the matching Fishburn lower bound, hence
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}(a_n^{(d)}/n!)^{1/n}=\mu.
\]
Subtracting this unweighted limit from \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:dtilt} gives exactly the moment-generating upper bound \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:mgfupper} for $K_n^{(d)}$. The two Chernoff signs prove exponential concentration at $\mu$. Every state still has precisely $K+2$ children, so
\[
\frac{a_{n+1}^{(d)}}{a_n^{(d)}}=\E(K_n^{(d)}+2),\qquad
\frac{a_{n+1}^{(d)}}{(n+1)a_n^{(d)}}\longrightarrow\mu.
\]
This proves all fixed-$d$ assertions of Theorem~\ref{wasc:gr:thm:main}.

\paragraph{Why fixed means fixed.}
The constants in the finite-band estimate depend on $d$. Nothing above is uniform for $d=d(n)\to\infty$. For example, when $d\ge n$, every transition of every length-$n$ inversion sequence is a $d$-ascent. The constraint then permits all inversion sequences and the count is $n!$, whose factorial-normalized root is one. Subexponential factors for different fixed $d$ also need not agree. Sections~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:logsquare}--\ref{wasc:gr:sec:matchinglower} establish the sharper logarithmic correction only for $d=1$.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
For fixed $d\ge1$ the subexponential factors do differ. By Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}, \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:maindifference}, for fixed integers $d,d'\ge1$,
\[
\log\frac{a_n^{(d)}}{a_n^{(d')}}=\frac{d-d'}2(\log n)^2+O_{d,d'}(\log n),
\]
which tends to $\pm\infty$ when $d\ne d'$. The same holds with $d'=0$ by Remark~\ref{wasc:sh:rem:dzero}, where the factor is $C_F\sqrt n$. The logarithmic-square correction is therefore $\frac d2(\log n)^2$ for every fixed $d\ge1$, not only for $d=1$; the statement about $d=d(n)\to\infty$ is unchanged, and Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part} claims no uniformity in $d$ either.
\end{writenote}

\section{Threshold inversion with integer bracketing}\label{wasc:gr:sec:inverse}
All logarithms are natural. For $X>1$, let $L=\log X$ and $N=N(X)$. Since every nonempty object has at least two children, the counts strictly increase for $n\ge1$; the duplicate $a_0=a_1=1$ is irrelevant here. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:threshold}
\log a_{N-1}<L\le\log a_N.
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:f}
f(x)=x\log(\mu x/\ee),\qquad f'(x)=\log(\mu x).
\end{equation}
The large positive solution of $f(x)=L$ is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:W}
x=x(L)=\frac{L}{W(\mu L/\ee)},\qquad x\sim L/\log L.
\end{equation}
Indeed, putting $w=W(\mu L/\ee)$ gives $we^w=\mu L/\ee$, so $\mu x/\ee=e^w$ and $f(x)=xw=L$.

\subsection{Consequence of the root theorem for every fixed d}
Stirling and the root theorem give
\[
\log a_n^{(d)}=f(n)+o(n).
\]
For the threshold $N_d(X)$, the analogue of \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:threshold} first implies $N_d\sim L/\log L$. Since $f(N_d)-f(N_d-1)=O(\log N_d)$, those same inequalities give $f(N_d)-L=o(N_d)$. The mean value theorem between $N_d$ and $x(L)$ now has derivative asymptotic to $\log L$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:dinverse}
N_d(X)=\frac{L}{W(\mu L/\ee)}+o\!\left(\frac{L}{(\log L)^2}\right)
\end{equation}
for each fixed $d$. This is the inverse accuracy justified by the common root theorem alone.

\subsection{The signed weak-ascent inverse}
Set $g(x)=f(x)+\tfrac12(\log x)^2$. Theorem~\ref{wasc:gr:thm:logsquare} and Stirling give
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:logerror}
\log a_n=g(n)+O\!\left(\frac{(\log n)^2}{\log\log n}\right).
\end{equation}
The Stirling contribution $\tfrac12\log(2\pi n)+O(1/n)$ is smaller than this error. Since $g(N)-g(N-1)=O(\log N)$, both threshold inequalities \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:threshold} imply
\[
g(N)-L=O\!\left(\frac{(\log N)^2}{\log\log N}\right).
\]
The root theorem first gives $N\sim x\sim L/\log L$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:newtonstart}
y=x-\frac{(\log x)^2}{2\log(\mu x)}.
\end{equation}
This displacement is $O(\log x)$. Taylor expansion of the explicitly smooth function $f$, with $f'(x)=\log(\mu x)$ and $f''(x)=1/x$, gives
\[
f(y)=L-\tfrac12(\log x)^2+O((\log x)^2/x).
\]
Also $(\log y)^2-(\log x)^2=O((\log x)^2/x)$, so $g(y)-L=O((\log x)^2/x)$. Between $N$ and $y$, the derivative $g'$ is asymptotic to $\log x$. The mean value theorem therefore gives
\[
N-y=O(\log x/\log\log x),
\]
proving \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:inverseintro}. Only the smooth main term was differentiated; no regularity of the unknown counting remainder was assumed.

For an explicit asymptotic bracketing formulation, the error statement supplies some constants $C,n_0$ valid for all $n\ge n_0$. Choose any sufficiently large constant $A$ compared with $C$. For all sufficiently large $L$, the integers
\[
n_- =\left\lfloor y-A\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
n_+ =\left\lceil y+A\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}\right\rceil
\]
satisfy $\log a_{n_-}<L\le\log a_{n_+}$, hence $n_-<N(X)\le n_+$. This follows from $g'\sim\log x$ and the two-sided error in \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:logerror}; integer rounding changes the smooth value by only $O(\log x)$.

These existence constants are not numerically certified here. To validate a threshold for a particular finite $X$, use the exact recurrence and integer comparisons. The leading displacement is also $-\tfrac12\log L+o(\log L)$, since $\log x\sim\log L$, but \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:inverseintro} retains the more informative smooth correction and a smaller error.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Superseded: Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}, Corollary~\ref{wasc:sh:cor:inverse} with $c=1$, proves $N(X)=x-\frac{(\log x)^2}{2\log(\mu x)}+O(1)=x-\frac12\log x+O(1)$; the integer brackets above can then be taken of bounded width. The existence constants are still not certified (Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}, question~3 of Section~\ref{wasc:sh:sec:questions}).

\section{Further questions and a refined formal starting point}\label{wasc:gr:sec:questions}
\subsection{The prefactor remains the main asymptotic problem}
The quadratic corrector improves the preliminary coefficient $3/4$ to $1/2$, and the exact path measure supplies a matching lower bound. Their quantitative remainder is still much larger than the potential next scales $\log n\log\log n$ or $\log n$. Determining those scales and their coefficients requires further analysis. In particular, the fixed prefix cutoff deliberately sacrifices lower-order precision in return for global control of every reachable state.

A full coefficient equivalent would require an additive $o(1)$ error after specifying all divergent terms in the logarithm, together with the constant term that determines the amplitude. The present $O((\log n)^2/\log\log n)$ remainder is far from that accuracy. More refined calibration of the initial boundary and higher correctors are possible directions; their effectiveness remains to be proved.

Other precise questions include a full limit for $n^{-1}\log(H_n(v)/n!)$ away from $v=1$, a large-deviation principle, variance asymptotics and a central limit theorem, dependence of the subexponential factor on fixed $d$, and crossover regimes when $d$ grows. The upper-pressure argument alone does not settle any of them.

The dependence on fixed $d$ is answered for every fixed $d\ge1$ at the logarithmic-square scale (coefficient $\frac d2$, remainder $O_d(\log n)$); for $d=0$ the Fishburn asymptotic gives no such term. \subsection{Level marking and the solvable zero-level anchor}
For an additional exact starting point, let $\ell$ mark adjacent equalities, and let $\mathcal F(t,u,v,\ell)$ count nonempty weak ascent sequences, where $t$ marks length, $u$ weak ascents, and $v$ the last entry. Put $\mathcal W_\ell(t,u)=\mathcal F(t,u,1,\ell)$. Appending a new letter to $(k,l)$ assigns weight $1$ below $l$, $\ell u$ at $l$, and $u$ above $l$. Finite geometric sums therefore give
\begin{align}\label{wasc:gr:eq:levelkernel}
\mathcal F=t+\frac{t}{1-v}\bigl[
\mathcal W_\ell+(\ell u-1+(1-\ell)uv)\mathcal F
-uv^2\mathcal W_\ell(t,uv)\bigr].
\end{align}
The numerator is divisible by $1-v$ coefficientwise. This is a direct formal derivation consistent with the level-marked framework of Mansour \cite{mansour}.

The formal kernel root and substitution are
\[
v_\ell(t,u)=\frac{1+t-\ell ut}{1+(1-\ell)ut},\qquad
\phi_\ell(t,u)=u v_\ell(t,u),
\]
so kernel cancellation gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:levelW}
\mathcal W_\ell(t,u)=\frac{t(1-u)}{1+(1-\ell)ut}
+u v_\ell(t,u)^2\mathcal W_\ell(t,\phi_\ell(t,u)).
\end{equation}
At $\ell=1$, this is equivalent to \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:kernel} through $G(u,t)=u+u^2\mathcal W_1(t,u)$. Thus it does not remove the nonlinear endpoint problem.

At $\ell=0$, $\phi_0=u(1+t)/(1+ut)$ is a M\"obius map. If $y=1/u$, its iterates satisfy
\[
y_{j+1}=\frac{y_j+t}{1+t},\qquad
y_j=1+\frac{y_0-1}{(1+t)^j}.
\]
The zero-level objects are precisely ordinary ascent sequences without adjacent equal letters. If $A_F(z)$ is the ordinary Fishburn series and $P(t)=1+\mathcal W_0(t,1)$, deleting repeated adjacent letters preserves strict ascent counts, and reinserting positive runs gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:gr:eq:zeroanchor}
A_F(z)=P\!\left(\frac z{1-z}\right),\qquad
P(t)=A_F\!\left(\frac t{1+t}\right).
\end{equation}
These are formal identities. Differentiating the level-marked equation around $\ell=0$ offers a perturbative route, but passage to $\ell=1$ would require new uniform remainder estimates. For positive level weight, a repeated level itself enlarges the future alphabet, so \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:zeroanchor} cannot simply be extended unchanged.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026, attribution]
The identity \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:zeroanchor} is not new, and this Part states it without credit. It is the binomial transform between Fishburn and primitive Fishburn objects, Jel\'inek's Fact~5.1 \cite{jelinek} (Report~107 cites Facts~5.1 and~5.2 of the same paper, and credits Fact~5.2, the ratio $\ee^{-\pi^2/6}$, to Drmota). Ascent sequences with no two equal adjacent letters (\emph{primitive} ascent sequences), and runs of equal letters in ascent sequences, were studied earlier by Dukes, Kitaev, Remmel and Steingr\'imsson \cite{dkrs}. Part~\ref{wasc:lv:part} proves the identity by the same run compression, in coefficient form \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:forwardtransform}--\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:inversebinomial}, and uses it for the zero-level atom (Section~\ref{wasc:lv:sec:zero}). The series $P(t)$ here has the coefficients $P_n$ of Part~\ref{wasc:lv:part} ($P_0=1$); it is not the pressure $P(q)$ of Section~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:ratio}.
\end{writenote}

\section{Definitions and principal results}\label{wasc:lv:sec:results}
A weak ascent of $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is an index $i<n$ with $x_i\le x_{i+1}$.
A weak ascent sequence starts at $x_1=0$ and obeys
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:def}
0\le x_{i+1}\le1+\wasc(x_1,\ldots,x_i),\qquad 1\le i<n.
\end{equation}
A \emph{level} is an equal adjacent pair. Put
\[
E(x)=\#\{i<n:x_i=x_{i+1}\},\qquad
H_n(w)=\sum_{|x|=n}w^{E(x)},\qquad \mu=\frac6{\pi^2}.
\]
The empty sequence has weight one, so $H_0(w)=H_1(w)=1$.
The present $w$ marks levels only. The auxiliary mark $v=\ee^q$ appearing in the proof marks every weak ascent and is a different variable.

\begin{theorem}[Weighted logarithmic asymptotic]\label{wasc:lv:thm:weighted}
For every compact interval $\Wc=[w_-,w_+]\subset(0,\infty)$ there are
$C_{\Wc},n_{\Wc}>0$ such that, for $w\in\Wc$ and $n\ge n_{\Wc}$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:weighted}
\left|\log H_n(w)-\log(n!)-n\log\mu-\frac w2(\log n)^2\right|
\le C_{\Wc}\frac{(\log n)^2}{\log\log n}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
At $w=1$, $H_n(1)=a_n$ and this is the counting assertion of Part~\ref{wasc:gr:part}, Theorem~\ref{wasc:gr:thm:logsquare}, with the same remainder; the proof below is a second, self-contained route. Superseded in its remainder: Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}, Theorem~\ref{wasc:sh:thm:main}, proves \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:weighted} with $C_{\Wc}\log n$ in place of $C_{\Wc}(\log n)^2/\log\log n$, with the same compact uniformity.
\end{writenote}

For $w>0$, let $\Prob_{n,w}$ assign $x$ probability $w^{E(x)}/H_n(w)$, and write
$E_n=E(x)$ and $b_n=(\log n)^2$. At $w=1$ this is the uniform law.

\begin{theorem}[Level law]\label{wasc:lv:thm:law}
For each fixed $w>0$ and $t\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:mgfmain}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1{b_n}\log\E_{n,w}\ee^{tE_n}
=\Lambda_w(t):=\frac w2(\ee^t-1).
\end{equation}
Moreover $E_n/b_n\to w/2$ in probability and in every fixed $L^p$, $1\le p<\infty$;
all fixed positive moments converge to those of the constant $w/2$. Uniformly for $w$ in a compact positive interval,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:meanmain}
\E_{n,w}E_n=\frac w2b_n+O_{\Wc}\!\left(\frac{b_n}{\sqrt{\log\log n}}\right).
\end{equation}
The variables $E_n/b_n$ obey a full large-deviation principle on $\R$ at speed $b_n$, with good rate function
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:rate}
I_w(x)=\begin{cases}
x\log(2x/w)-x+w/2,&x\ge0,\\
+\infty,&x<0,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here $0\log0=0$. For each $\eta>0$, some $c_{w,\eta}>0$ gives, for all sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:concentrationmain}
\Prob_{n,w}\bigl(|E_n/b_n-w/2|\ge\eta\bigr)
\le2\ee^{-c_{w,\eta}b_n}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
The mean estimate \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:meanmain} is superseded by Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}, Corollary~\ref{wasc:sh:cor:moments}: $\E_{n,w}E_n=\frac w2b_n+O_{\Wc}((\log n)^{3/2})$, with absolute central moments $O((\log n)^{3p/2})$ and variance $O((\log n)^3)$. The remainder of the theorem stands as stated; with Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}'s remainder the error in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:scaledmgf} becomes $O_{\Wc,J}(1/\log n)$.
\end{writenote}

\begin{theorem}[Exact zero-level rate]\label{wasc:lv:thm:zeromain}
Let $P_n$ be the number of primitive ordinary ascent sequences, meaning ordinary ascent sequences with no adjacent repetitions. Then $H_n(0)=P_n$, and for fixed $w>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:zeromain}
\Prob_{n,w}(E_n=0)=\frac{P_n}{H_n(w)},\qquad
\log\Prob_{n,w}(E_n=0)=-\frac w2b_n+O_{\Wc}\!\left(\frac{b_n}{\log\log n}\right).
\end{equation}
The error is compact-uniform for positive $w$. In particular the exact atom has logarithmic rate $-I_w(0)=-w/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
With Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}'s remainder the error in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:zeromain} becomes $O_{\Wc}(\log n)$, compact-uniformly; neither manuscript states this. The deduction is \wtag\ Remark~\ref{wasc:lv:rem:zerosharp} at the end of Section~\ref{wasc:lv:sec:zero}.
\end{writenote}

\begin{theorem}[Threshold inverse]\label{wasc:lv:thm:inversemain}
For $w>0$, define $N_w(X)=\min\{n\ge0:H_n(w)\ge X\}$.
For $L=\log X$ and the principal Lambert function $W_0$, put
\[
x=\frac{L}{W_0(\mu L/\ee)}.
\]
As $X\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:inverse}
N_w(X)=x-\frac{w(\log x)^2}{2\log(\mu x)}
+O_{\Wc}\!\left(\frac{\log x}{\log\log x}\right).
\end{equation}
This holds uniformly for $w$ in a fixed compact positive interval. For a positive integer $w$, $H_n(w)$ is an integer count obtained by coloring each level independently in $w$ colors; for real $w>0$ it is a weighted threshold.
\end{theorem}

\section{Exact state transfer and monotonicity}\label{wasc:lv:sec:states}
At length $i$, write $K$ for the number of weak ascents, $l$ for the last letter, and $M=K+2$. The allowed next letter is $j\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:transition}
a=\ind_{\{j\ge l\}},\quad e=\ind_{\{j=l\}},\qquad
(K,l)\longmapsto(K+a,j),\quad\hbox{weight multiplier }w^e.
\end{equation}
Induction gives $0\le l\le K\le i-1$. Thus each sequence is an inversion sequence and there are at most $i!$ unweighted prefixes.
Let $A_i(K,l;w)$ be the total weight in that state. Starting with
$A_1(0,0;w)=1$, the exact positive recurrence adds
\[
A_{i+1}(K+a,j;w)\mathrel{+}=w^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}A_i(K,l;w)
\quad(0\le j\le K+1).
\]
Summing all states yields $H_i(w)$, a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients. The first polynomials are
\begin{align*}
H_1(w)&=1,& H_2(w)&=1+w,\\
H_3(w)&=2+3w+w^2,&H_4(w)&=5+11w+6w^2+w^3.
\end{align*}
Exactly one of the $K+2$ children is a level, so
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:monotone}
\frac{H_{i+1}(w)}{H_i(w)}=\E_{i,w}(K+1+w)\ge1+w>1\qquad(i\ge1).
\end{equation}
This proves strict increase for every real $w>0$ beyond the initial equality $H_0=H_1$. In particular $H_i(w)\ge(1+w)^{i-1}$, and every sufficiently large threshold is attained.

We will also need a deterministic lower bound on reachable $M$. Split a sequence into strictly decreasing runs, with a new run beginning at each weak ascent. The run beginning after the $r$th weak ascent starts with a letter at most $r$, so has at most $r+1$ entries. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:reachable}
i\le\frac{(K+1)(K+2)}2,\qquad M\ge\sqrt{2i}.
\end{equation}

\section{Calibration and the frozen weighted kernel}\label{wasc:lv:sec:calibration}
\subsection{The calibrated trajectory}
Fix the terminal length $n$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:gchi}
g(v)=\frac{\log v}{v-1},\qquad
\chi(v)=\int_v^\infty\frac{\log t}{t(t-1)}\dd t,
\end{equation}
using removable values at $v=1$. Since $\chi(1)=\sum_{m\ge1}m^{-2}=\pi^2/6$, the equations
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:calibrate}
s=n\mu\chi(\ee^{q(s)}),\qquad S(s)=n\mu g(\ee^{q(s)})
\end{equation}
define $q(s)\ge0$ and $S(s)>0$ for $0<s\le n$. Equivalently,
\[
\frac{s}{n\mu}=\int_{q(s)}^\infty\frac{u}{\ee^u-1}\dd u.
\]
Differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:calderiv}
q'=-\frac1S,\qquad S'=\alpha(q):=\frac{\ee^q}{\ee^q-1}-\frac1q,
\qquad q(n)=0,\quad S(0+)=0.
\end{equation}
The function $\alpha(q)$ is the mean of $x\in[0,1]$ under a density proportional to $\ee^{qx}$. Hence $1/2\le\alpha\le1$, and its derivative, a variance, is bounded. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:calbounds}
\frac s2\le S(s)\le s,\qquad 0\le q(s)\le2\log(n/s).
\end{equation}
Write $q_i=q(i)$ and $S_i=S(i)$. With $\Delta q=q_{i+1}-q_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:caldiscrete}
\Delta q\le-\frac1{S_{i+1}},\qquad |\Delta q|\le\frac2i,
\qquad 0\le S_{i+1}-S_i\le1.
\end{equation}
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:qsover}
(q/S)'=-\frac{1+q\alpha(q)}{S^2}\le0,
\qquad\int_k^n\frac{q(s)}{S(s)}\dd s=\frac12q_k^2.
\end{equation}
Expanding $(\ee^u-1)^{-1}=\ee^{-u}+O(\ee^{-2u})$ in the tail integral gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:chitail}
\chi(\ee^q)=(q+1)\ee^{-q}+O((q+1)\ee^{-2q})\qquad(q\to\infty).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact Perron and Doob formulas}
Freeze $q\ge0$, $v=\ee^q$, and integer $M\ge1$. For $0\le l,j<M$, put
\[
T(l,j)=v^{\ind_{\{j\ge l\}}},\qquad f_l=\ee^{-ql/M}.
\]
At $q>0$, write $r=\ee^{-q/M}$. Geometric summation gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:eigen}
Tf=\lambda f,\qquad \lambda=\frac{v-1}{1-r}.
\end{equation}
At $q=0$, $f=1$ and $\lambda=M$. The equality weight changes just the diagonal:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:weightedeigen}
T_w=T+(w-1)vI,\quad T_wf=\lambda_w f,\quad
\lambda_w=\lambda+(w-1)v>0.
\end{equation}
Let $P(l,j)=T(l,j)f_j/(\lambda f_l)$ and
$P_w(l,j)=T_w(l,j)f_j/(\lambda_w f_l)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:kernel}
P(l,j)=\frac{1-r}{1-r^M}r^{(j-l)\bmod M}.
\end{equation}
Both are circulant stochastic kernels, with uniform stationary law. Define
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:delta}
t=\frac v\lambda=\frac{1-\ee^{-q/M}}{1-\ee^{-q}},\qquad
D_w=1+(w-1)t,\qquad \delta=\frac{(w-1)t}{D_w}.
\end{equation}
The exact identity is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:mixture}
P_w=(1-\delta)P+\delta I.
\end{equation}
When $w<1$, $\delta<0$: this is an algebraic identity, \emph{not} a probability mixture. The actual off-diagonal entries are $P(l,j)/D_w$, and the diagonal entries are $wt/D_w$. They are strictly positive.
At $q=0$ all formulas use the exact limits
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:qzero}
\lambda_w=M+w-1,\quad t=1/M,\quad\delta=\frac{w-1}{M+w-1},\quad
P_w(l,j)=\frac{w^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}}{M+w-1}.
\end{equation}
Throughout the proof $w\in\Wc=[w_-,w_+]\subset(0,\infty)$ and constants indexed by $\Wc$ are uniform there. Put $Q=1+q$. Globally,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:dominate}
0<t\le\min(1,Q/M),\quad D_w\ge\min(1,w_-),\quad
P_w(l,j)\le\frac{\max(1,w_+)}{\min(1,w_-)}P(l,j).
\end{equation}
Indeed $1-\ee^{-q/M}\le q/M$ and $q/(1-\ee^{-q})=q+q/(\ee^q-1)\le q+1$; also $t\le1$ directly.
For $M\ge C_{\Wc}Q$ with sufficiently large constant,
\begin{align}
 t&=q/M+O(1/M+Q^2/M^2),\label{wasc:lv:eq:texpand}\\
 \log(\lambda_w/\lambda)&=(w-1)q/M+O_{\Wc}(1/M+Q^2/M^2),
 \qquad |\delta|\le C_{\Wc}Q/M.\label{wasc:lv:eq:lambdaexpand}
\end{align}
To see the uniformity at zero, let $Q_0(z)=z/(\ee^z-1)$ and
$\gamma(z)=(1-\ee^{-z})/z$, both with removable values. Then
$t=(q+Q_0(q))\gamma(q/M)/M$, with $0\le Q_0\le1$ and $\gamma(z)=1+O(z)$. Taylor-expand $\log(1+(w-1)t)$ only after $|w-1|t\le1/2$ is assured. The $O(1/M)$ error is necessary at $q=0$.
For small states, where such expansion may fail, the global one-sided estimate remains valid:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:globalperturb}
\log(\lambda_w/\lambda)\le(w_+-1)_+Q/M,
\end{equation}
by $\log(1+x)\le x$ for $w>1$, and by its nonpositive sign for $w\le1$.

\section{The exact quadratic corrector}\label{wasc:lv:sec:corrector}
\subsection{Finite Poisson identity and stable formula}
Initially take $q>0$. Under $P(l,\cdot)$ let the next index be $J$, and write
\[
b_l=\E_P[q\ind_{\{J\ge l\}}J/M],\qquad c=\frac1M\sum_{l=0}^{M-1}b_l,
\qquad B=\frac r{1-r},\quad D_0=1-r^M.
\]
Geometric summation gives
\begin{align}
b_l&=\frac q{MD_0}\{l+B-(M+B)r^{M-l}\},\label{wasc:lv:eq:bexact}\\
c&=\frac q{M^2D_0}\{M(M-1)/2+MB-(M+B)BD_0\}.\label{wasc:lv:eq:cexact}
\end{align}
Define the polynomial, including its endpoint at $l=M$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:hpoly}
h_l=\left(\frac cB+\frac q{2MD_0B}\right)l-\frac{q l^2}{2MD_0B}.
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}\label{wasc:lv:lem:poisson}
On $0\le l<M$, $(P-I)h=c-b$. Also $h_0=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $y_l=h_l+c-b_l$. Direct subtraction in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:bexact}--\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:hpoly} gives
$y_{l+1}-y_l=(y_l-h_l)/B$ for $l<M-1$. Since $\sum_l(c-b_l)=0$, summing the differences shows the same equation at the cyclic boundary. Thus
$y_l-r y_{l+1}=(1-r)h_l$ with cyclic indices. If $C$ denotes forward cyclic shift, inversion of $I-rC$ gives
\[
y=\frac{1-r}{1-r^M}\sum_{d=0}^{M-1}r^d C^d h=Ph.
\]
This proves the claim. At $q=0$, set $h=b=c=0$; all identities extend continuously.
\end{proof}
For estimates set $H(q,M,x)=h(q,M,Mx)$, allowing real $M$ in derivatives. Algebra in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:hpoly} gives the stable representation
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:hstable}
H=A x(1-x)+J_0x,\quad
A=\frac{q^2}{2(1-\ee^{-q})}\beta(q/M),\quad
J_0=Q_0(q)-Q_0(q/M),\quad\beta(z)=\frac{\ee^z-1}{z}.
\end{equation}
All values at zero are removable, and $H(0,M,x)=0$. If $M\ge C_0Q$ for a sufficiently large absolute $C_0$, then uniformly in $0\le x\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:hderiv}
|H|+|H_x|\le CQ^2,\quad |H_q|\le CQ,\quad
|H_M|\le CQ^3/M^2,\quad -1\le J_0\le0.
\end{equation}
Indeed $\beta$ and its derivatives are bounded for $0\le q/M\le1$; the factor
$q^2/[2(1-\ee^{-q})]$ is $O(Q^2)$ with derivative $O(Q)$, including its expansion $q/2+O(q^2)$ at zero. The functions $Q_0,Q_0'$ are bounded on $[0,\infty)$ and $Q_0$ decreases from one to zero. Differentiating \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:hstable} now proves the bounds. The last inequality controls exactly the seam jump $H(1)-H(0)$.

\subsection{Geometric moments and the corrector constant}
Let $V=((J-l)\bmod M)/M$. Its distribution under $P$ is independent of $l$, with weights proportional to $r^{MV}$. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:Vmean}
\E_PV=\frac1{M(\ee^{q/M}-1)}-\frac1{\ee^q-1}.
\end{equation}
Nonincreasing weights give $0\le\E_PV\le1/2$; the displayed formula gives
$\E_PV\le1/q$ when $q>0$. Hence $\E_PV=O(Q^{-1})$, including at zero.
For every fixed integer $j\ge0$ and constant $A_0>0$, enlarging $C_0$ in terms of $A_0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:tiltedmoment}
\E_P[V^j\exp(A_0Q^2V/M)]\le C_jQ^{-j}.
\end{equation}
For $q\ge1$, arrange $A_0Q^2/M\le q/2$. The tilted geometric weights are bounded by $\exp(-qV/2)$, their normalization is $O(q/M)$, and the $j$th moment sum is $O(M/q^{j+1})$. These follow either by a geometric sum or comparison with its decreasing exponential integral, uniformly when $M/q$ is bounded below. For $q\le1$, use $V\le1$ and bounded $Q$.
By the positive entrywise bound \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:dominate}, \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:tiltedmoment} holds under $P_w$ too, with compact-uniform constants. This does not use \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:mixture} as a positive mixture.

Uniform stationarity yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:cbounds}
0\le c\le\frac{q(M-1)}{2M}\le\frac q2,
\qquad c\ge\frac q2-C(1+q/M).
\end{equation}
For the lower estimate, compare $c$ with $q\E(J/M)=q(M-1)/(2M)$ under a uniform starting state. The deficit is $q\E[(1-a)J/M]$. Conditional on forward integer increment $d$, wrapping starts produce outputs $0,\ldots,d-1$, so that conditional expectation equals $d(d-1)/(2M^2)\le V^2/2$. Equation~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:tiltedmoment} gives the required bound. Stationarity is used to compute $c$, never as a claim about the evolving last letter.

\subsection{Weighted first moment and exponential estimate}
Integrating $H_x$ around a forward circular arc, using the seam jump at most once, gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:arc}
|h_J-h_l|\le CQ^2V+1.
\end{equation}
For $0\le\zeta\le1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:Z}
Z=q\ind_{\{J\ge l\}}J/M+\zeta(h_J-h_l).
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{wasc:lv:lem:poisson} gives $\E_PZ=(1-\zeta)b_l+\zeta c\in[0,q]$. On the diagonal $Z=ql/M$, so the algebraic identity \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:mixture} gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:weightedZ}
\E_{P_w}Z=\E_PZ+\delta(ql/M-\E_PZ)
=(1-\zeta)b_l+\zeta c+O_{\Wc}(Q^2/M).
\end{equation}
For the uncut case $\zeta=1$, this reads $\E_{P_w}Z=c+\delta(ql/M-c)$.
Also \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:arc} and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:tiltedmoment}, for $j=0,2$, imply
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:Zmoment}
\E_{P_w}[Z^2\ee^{|Z|/M}]\le C_{\Wc}Q^2.
\end{equation}
Here $|Z|\le CQ+CQ^2V$; the two moment estimates control each term after squaring.
The exponential Taylor remainder and $\log(1+x)\le x$ therefore prove
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:expZ}
\log\E_{P_w}\ee^{Z/M}\le\frac{(1-\zeta)b_l+\zeta c}{M}
+C_{\Wc}\frac{Q^2}{M^2}.
\end{equation}
This is a genuine fluctuation bound, not merely a stationary average. At $q=0$, $Z=0$ exactly.

\section{The compact uniform upper bound}\label{wasc:lv:sec:upper}
\subsection{Moving cutoff and regular states}
Write $T=\log n$ and $U=\log T$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:uppercutoff}
D_*=\frac32+(w_+-1)_+,\quad B_* =\max(100,72D_*^2),\quad
k=\left\lceil B_*T^2/U^2\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
We henceforth take $n$ sufficiently large that $U>0$ and $k<n$. The dependence of $B_*$ on the weight interval is important for small states.
Let $\zeta(r)=0$ for $r\le1/2$, $\zeta(r)=4r-2$ for $1/2\le r\le3/4$, and $\zeta(r)=1$ for $r\ge3/4$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:upperpotential}
F_i(K,l)=\exp\left(q_iK-q_i l/M+\zeta(M/S_i)h(q_i,M,l)/M\right),
\quad M=K+2.
\end{equation}
At the terminal time, $F_n=1$ exactly. For $i\ge k$, abbreviate $q=q_{i+1}$, $S=S_{i+1}$, $Q=1+q$, $R=M/S$, and $\Delta q=q_{i+1}-q_i$.
First suppose $R\ge0.49$. Then $M\ge0.245(i+1)$, while $i/Q\to\infty$ uniformly for $i\ge k$. All corrector estimates apply throughout the parameter interpolations below.

Replacing the actual next-state correction
$\zeta((M+a)/S)h(q,M+a,j)/(M+a)$ by
$\zeta(M/S)h(q,M,j)/M$ costs $O_{\Wc}(Q^2/i^2)$.
To verify this, differentiating $H(q,M,j/M)/M$ at fixed $j$ gives
$O(Q^2/M^2+Q^3/M^3)$, and the cutoff change costs $O(Q^2/(SM))$.
Replacing the current correction by its value at $q,S$ costs the same order: use
$|\Delta q|\le2/i$, $H_q=O(Q)$, and cutoff change at most $4M/(S_iS)$.
The corners of $\zeta$ cause no problem because only its Lipschitz bound is used.
The remaining eigenvector-denominator difference satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:denomdiscrep}
-\frac{qj}{M+a}+\frac{qj}{M}
=\frac{qaj}{M(M+a)}\le\frac{qaj}{M^2}.
\end{equation}
Factoring the exact weighted eigenvector therefore gives the pointwise estimate
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:regulartransfer}
\sum_j w^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}\frac{F_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{F_i(K,l)}
\le\lambda_w\exp\left(\Delta qK-\Delta q\,l/M+C_{\Wc}Q^2/i^2\right)
\E_{P_w}\ee^{Z/M},
\end{equation}
with $Z$ from \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:Z} and $\zeta=\zeta(M/S)$.
The inequality $2\sinh(z/2)\ge z$ yields, for all $q\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:eigenupper}
\lambda\le\frac M{g(\ee^q)}\exp(q/(2M)).
\end{equation}
Put $\Psi(R)=\log R-R+1$. For $R\ge3/4$ the cutoff equals one.
The leading contributions in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:regulartransfer} are $q/(2M)$ from
\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:eigenupper}, $(w-1)q/M$ from \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lambdaexpand}, and $c/M\le q/(2M)$ from \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:expZ}. Their sum is at most $wq/M$, even if $w<1/2$.
The calibration contribution is exactly
\[
\log(M/g)-K/S-\log(n\mu/\ee)=\Psi(R)+2/S,
\]
and $-\Delta q\,l/M\le2/i$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:regularbound}
\log\left\{\frac{\sum_j w^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}F_{i+1}/F_i}{n\mu/\ee}\right\}
\le\Psi(R)+\frac{wq}{SR}+C_{\Wc}(Q^2/i^2+1/i).
\end{equation}
For $0.49\le R<3/4$, use $(1-\zeta)b_l+\zeta c\le q$ instead. The bound is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:intermediate}
\Psi(R)+C_{\Wc}(Q/i+Q^2/i^2+1/i).
\end{equation}
Since $\Psi(R)\le\Psi(3/4)<0$, this is negative throughout the intermediate range for all sufficiently large $n$, uniformly on $\Wc$.
For the full-corrector range, let $\beta_*=wq/S=o(1)$. When $0\le\beta_*\le1/8$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:psiopt}
\sup_{R\ge3/4}\{\Psi(R)+\beta_*/R\}\le\beta_*+2\beta_*^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed the derivative has numerator $R-R^2-\beta_*$. The maximum is at
$r_+=(1+\sqrt{1-4\beta_*})/2\in(3/4,1]$; the maximum value has derivative
$1/r_+\le1+4\beta_*$. Integrate from zero. The added quadratic term is $O_{\Wc}(Q^2/i^2)$.

\subsection{All small states}
Suppose $R<0.49$. For sufficiently large $i$, both correction cutoffs vanish:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:cutoffzero}
M/S_i\le0.49(1+2/i)<1/2,\qquad (M+a)/S\le0.49+2/(i+1)<1/2.
\end{equation}
No derivative estimate or expansion in $q/M$ will be used here. Apply the crude discrepancy bound $qaj/M^2\le q/M$, the exact eigenvector, \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:globalperturb}, and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:eigenupper}. This gives globally in the small range
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:smallbound}
\log\left\{\frac{\sum_j w^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}F_{i+1}/F_i}{n\mu/\ee}\right\}
\le\Psi(R)+\frac{D_*Q}{SR}+6/i.
\end{equation}
Set $\alpha_*=D_*Q/S$ and $m=\sqrt{2i}/S$. Reachability \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:reachable} gives $R\ge m$; also $\alpha_*\le6D_*T/i=o(1)$ uniformly for $i\ge k$.
If $[m,0.49]$ is nonempty, the function
$h_{\alpha_*}(r)=\Psi(r)+\alpha_*/r$ has its maximum there at an endpoint for large $n$.
Its derivative has numerator $r-r^2-\alpha_*$; the smaller critical point is a minimum and the larger tends to one, outside this interval.
At the reachable endpoint,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:smallendpoint}
h_{\alpha_*}(m)
\le-\tfrac12\log i+1+\log(2\sqrt2)+\frac{3D_*T}{\sqrt{2i}}.
\end{equation}
Here use $S\ge i/2$ and $Q\le3T$, discarding the favorable $-m$.
The right side decreases with $i$. By \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:uppercutoff}, at $i=k$ it is at most
\[
-U+\log U+O_{\Wc}(1)+U/4=-3U/4+\log U+O_{\Wc}(1)\longrightarrow-\infty.
\]
At the other endpoint,
$h_{\alpha_*}(0.49)=\Psi(0.49)+\alpha_*/0.49$ tends uniformly to a fixed negative number.
Both endpoint bounds absorb $6/i$ in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:smallbound}. If $m>0.49$ the interval is empty. This settles every reachable small state, including $M$ much smaller than $q$.
Combining the three ranges with \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:psiopt} proves the global one-step inequality
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:globalstep}
\sum_j w^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}F_{i+1}(K+a,j)
\le F_i(K,l)\frac{n\mu}{\ee}
\exp\left\{\frac{wq_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}+
C_{\Wc}\left(\frac{Q_{i+1}^2}{i^2}+\frac1i\right)\right\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Prefix and telescope}
At length $k$, a nonzero cutoff forces $M>S_k/2\ge k/4$, so the absolute corrector exponent is $O(Q_k^2/k)$. There are at most $k!$ prefixes, each with at most $k-1$ levels, and $K\le k-1$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:upperprefix}
\sum_{|x|=k}w^{E(x)}F_k(K,l)
\le k!\exp\{kq_k+k\log\max(1,w_+)+CQ_k^2/k\}.
\end{equation}
The corrector estimates are valid when the cutoff is nonzero; when it is zero there is no correction to bound.
Positivity and $F_n=1$ permit backward iteration of \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:globalstep}. Since $q/S$ decreases,
\[
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}\le\frac12q_k^2,
\qquad\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{Q_{i+1}^2}{i^2}=O(T^2/k),\qquad
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac1i=O(T).
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:uppertelescope}
\log H_n(w)\le\log(k!)+kq_k+(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)+\frac w2q_k^2
+O_{\Wc}(k+Q_k^2/k+T+T^2/k).
\end{equation}
As $k=n^{o(1)}$, \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:chitail} implies $q_k\to\infty$, and taking logarithms in the calibration gives
\[
q_k=T-\log k+\log(q_k+1)+\log\mu+O(\ee^{-q_k})
=T+O_{\Wc}(U).
\]
Stirling's formula and $k/(n\mu)=\chi(\ee^{q_k})$ give the useful exact cancellation
\begin{align}\label{wasc:lv:eq:uppercancel}
&\log(k!)+kq_k+(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)-\log(n!)-n\log\mu\notag\\
&\hspace{10mm}=k\log[\ee^{q_k}\chi(\ee^{q_k})]+\tfrac12\log(k/n)+O(1/k)
=O_{\Wc}(kU+T).
\end{align}
The term $wq_k^2/2$ is $wT^2/2+O_{\Wc}(TU+U^2)$. Since $k=O_{\Wc}(T^2/U^2)$ and $TU=O(T^2/U)$ eventually, every remaining error in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:uppertelescope} is $O_{\Wc}(T^2/U)$. This proves the upper half of Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:weighted}.

\section{The matching weighted lower bound}\label{wasc:lv:sec:lower}
\subsection{A positive path law and its exact likelihood}
For this lower bound choose $k=\lceil100T^2/U^2\rceil$; any fixed positive multiplier would suffice. Begin with the single legal prefix of $k$ zeros. Its state is
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}, Section~\ref{wasc:sh:sec:lower}, repeats this section almost word for word with the cutoff $k=\lceil AT\rceil$; its closing paragraph replaces \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowercancel} and the final error count by the exact normalization \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:normalcancel}, giving the lower bound with error $O_{\Wc}(\log n)$.
\end{writenote}
$K_k=k-1$, $L_k=0$, $M_k=k+1$ and its weight is $w^{k-1}$.
At each time $i=k,\ldots,n-1$, sample $j$ from $P_w$, with parameters
$q=q_{i+1}$ and $M=M_i$. Denote this path law by $\Prob_w^*$, with expectation $\E_w^*$.
The weak-ascent count never decreases, so every path has
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:prefixsupport}
M_i\ge k+1,\qquad \frac{M_i}{1+q_{i+1}}\ge\frac{k}{1+2T}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{equation}
All corrector and eigenvalue expansions therefore apply on every sampled path, uniformly for $w\in\Wc$ once $n$ is large enough.
Use the uncut potential
\[
F_i(K,l)=\exp\{q_iK-q_i l/(K+2)+h(q_i,K+2,l)/(K+2)\}.
\]
For a step let
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:likelihoodratio}
R_i^w=\frac{w^eF_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{F_i(K,l)P_w(l,j)}.
\end{equation}
The product telescopes, and $F_n=1$, so the total weighted mass of completions of this fixed prefix is exactly
\[
w^{k-1}F_k(k-1,0)\E_w^*\exp\left(\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\log R_i^w\right).
\]
It is a positive sub-sum of $H_n(w)$ even for $w<1$. Jensen gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:jensen}
\log H_n(w)\ge(k-1)\log w+(k-1)q_k+
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_w^*\log R_i^w.
\end{equation}
There is no $k!$ factor in this single-prefix lower bound.

\subsection{Tracking the sampled state}
Put $X_i=L_i/M_i$, $Y_i=K_i-X_i$, and
$V_i=((j-L_i)\bmod M_i)/M_i$. A deterministic calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:tracker}
Y_{i+1}-Y_i=1-V_i+aX_{i+1}/M_i\in[0,2].
\end{equation}
Indeed $j/M_i-X_i=V_i-(1-a)$, while $j/M_i=X_{i+1}(1+a/M_i)$.
This includes equality steps, for which $V_i=0$ and $a=1$.
Since the identity part of \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:mixture} has $V=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:driftperturb}
\E_{P_w}V=(1-\delta)\E_PV,\qquad
|\E_{P_w}V-\E_PV|\le C_{\Wc}/M.
\end{equation}
For the second inequality both factors matter: $|\delta|=O_{\Wc}(Q/M)$ and
$\E_PV=O(Q^{-1})$. At $q=0$,
$\E_{P_w}V=(M-1)/(2(M+w-1))$, still differing from the unweighted mean by $O_{\Wc}(1/M)$.
Equation~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:Vmean} also gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:ordinarydrift}
1-\E_PV=\alpha(q)+\frac{1-Q_0(q/M)}q=\alpha(q)+O(1/M),
\end{equation}
where the quotient has value $1/(2M)$ at zero. The last term in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:tracker} lies in $[0,1/M_i]$. Boundedness of $\alpha'$ and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:caldiscrete} now give
\begin{align}
\E_w^*[Y_{i+1}-Y_i\mid\mathcal F_i]&\ge\frac12-C_{\Wc}/M_i\ge\frac13,
\label{wasc:lv:eq:driftpositive}\\
\left|\E_w^*[Y_{i+1}-Y_i\mid\mathcal F_i]-(S_{i+1}-S_i)\right|
&\le C_{\Wc}(1/M_i+1/i).\label{wasc:lv:eq:drifttrack}
\end{align}
The threshold for the second inequality in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:driftpositive} may depend on $\Wc$.
Subtract conditional means in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:tracker} to obtain a martingale with increments bounded in absolute value by two. Since $Y_k=k-1$, the event $M_i<i/8$ forces its displacement below
\[
i/8-(k-1)-(i-k)/3=-5i/24-2k/3+1.
\]
Azuma's inequality proves, for absolute $c,C>0$ after a sufficiently large compact-uniform threshold,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:lowprob}
\Prob_w^*(M_i<i/8)\le C\ee^{-ci}\qquad(k\le i<n).
\end{equation}
At $i=k$ the event is empty. No independence is assumed.
On good states $M_i\ge i/8$, $1/M_i\le8/i$; on bad states use $M_i\ge k$ and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowprob}. The $L^2$ triangle inequality yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:recipL2}
\left\|\sum_{i=k}^{m-1}1/M_i\right\|_2
\le8\sum_{i=k}^{m-1}1/i+\frac1k\sum_{i=k}^{m-1}C\ee^{-ci/2}
\le CT.
\end{equation}
The martingale variance is $O(m)$, and $|Y_k-S_k|\le k$. Sum \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:drifttrack}, use \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:recipL2}, and note $M_i-Y_i=2+X_i$. We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:trackingL2}
\E_w^*[(M_i-S_{i+1})^2]\le C_{\Wc}(i+T^2+k^2).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conditional one-step gain}
Freeze $q=q_{i+1}$, $S=S_{i+1}$, $M=M_i$, $l=L_i$, and $\Delta q=q_{i+1}-q_i$.
Substituting the Doob kernel into \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:likelihoodratio} cancels $w^e$ exactly and gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:lv:eq:exactlogR}
\log R_i^w={}&\log\lambda_w+\Delta qK_i-\Delta q\,l/M
+\frac{qaj}{M(M+a)}\notag\\
&+\frac{h(q,M+a,j)}{M+a}-\frac{h(q_i,M,l)}M.
\end{align}
Using \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:hderiv}, replacement of the two corrector terms by
$[h(q,M,j)-h(q,M,l)]/M$ costs at most
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:lowerreplacement}
C(Q^2/M^2+Q/(Mi)).
\end{equation}
For the next-state change, differentiating at fixed $j$ includes both $H_x$ and $H_M$, giving $O(Q^2/M^2+Q^3/M^3)$; the current-time change costs $O(Q|\Delta q|/M)$. Equation~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:prefixsupport} justifies the whole interpolation.
Also
$qaj/[M(M+a)]\ge qaj/M^2-q/M^2$.
Take conditional expectation and use the exact weighted corrector identity
\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:weightedZ} at $\zeta=1$, then \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:cbounds}. The last three terms of \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:exactlogR} are at least
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:correctedlower}
q/(2M)-C_{\Wc}(1/M+Q^2/M^2+Q/(Mi)).
\end{equation}
The eigenvalue satisfies exactly
\[
\lambda=n\mu\frac MS\frac{q/M}{1-\ee^{-q/M}}.
\]
The identity
$\log[z/(1-\ee^{-z})]=z/2+\log[(z/2)/\sinh(z/2)]$ implies, in the present range,
\[
\log\lambda\ge\log(n\mu)+\log(M/S)+q/(2M)-Cq^2/M^2.
\]
Together with \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lambdaexpand},
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:weightedeigenlower}
\log\lambda_w\ge\log(n\mu)+\log(M/S)
+(w-1/2)q/M-C_{\Wc}(1/M+Q^2/M^2).
\end{equation}
A negative intermediate coefficient when $w<1/2$ causes no inequality reversal.
Since $1/S(s)$ changes by $O(1/i^2)$ on $[i,i+1]$ and $K_i\le i$, calibration gives
\[
\Delta qK_i\ge-K_i/S-C/i,\qquad -\Delta q\,l/M\ge0.
\]
Using $K_i=M-2$ and dropping the favorable $2/S$, combine these inequalities to obtain
\begin{align}\label{wasc:lv:eq:lowerstep}
&\E_w^*[\log R_i^w\mid\mathcal F_i]-\log(n\mu/\ee)\notag\\
&\quad\ge\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})+\frac{wq_{i+1}}{M_i}
-C_{\Wc}\left(\frac1i+\frac1{M_i}+\frac{Q_{i+1}^2}{M_i^2}+
\frac{Q_{i+1}}{M_i i}\right).
\end{align}
Thus the net leading gain is exactly $wq/M$.

\subsection{Summation and prefix normalization}
On good states, $M_i/S_{i+1}\in[1/16,2]$; the double zero of $\Psi$ at one gives
$\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})\ge-C(M_i-S_{i+1})^2/i^2$.
On bad states, $M_i\ge k$ and $S_{i+1}\le n$, so $|\Psi|\le C+T$.
Equations~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowprob} and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:trackingL2} imply
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:psisum}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_w^*\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})
\ge-C_{\Wc}(T+T^2/k+k)-o(1).
\end{equation}
On good states,
$1/M_i\ge1/S_{i+1}-C|M_i-S_{i+1}|/i^2$.
Drop the nonnegative bad-state part of $q_{i+1}/M_i$, retaining the exponentially small loss of $q_{i+1}/S_{i+1}$. Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:trackingL2}, with $q\le2T$, yield
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:qrecipsum}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_w^*\frac{q_{i+1}}{M_i}
\ge\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}
-C_{\Wc}(T/\sqrt{k}+T^2/k+T)-o(1).
\end{equation}
The explicit errors in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowerstep} sum in expectation to
$O_{\Wc}(T+T^2/k)$: on good states use $M_i\ge i/8$, and on bad states use $M_i\ge k$ and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowprob}. Every omitted bad-state sum is an exponentially small factor $\ee^{-ck}$ times a polynomial in $T,k$.
The decreasing function $q/S$ has right Riemann sum differing from its integral by at most $q_k/S_k=O(T/k)$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:lowerriemann}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}=\frac12q_k^2+O(T/k).
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowerstep}--\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowerriemann} gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:lowergain}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_w^*\log R_i^w
\ge(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)+\frac w2q_k^2
-C_{\Wc}(k+T+T^2/k+T/\sqrt{k}).
\end{equation}
Again \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:chitail} gives $q_k=T+O(U)$ and $q_k^2=T^2+O(TU+U^2)$.
For $v=\ee^{q_k}$, Stirling and $k=n\mu\chi(v)$ give
\begin{align}\label{wasc:lv:eq:lowercancel}
&(k-1)q_k+(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)-\log(n!\mu^n)\notag\\
&\quad=k[\log(v\chi(v))-\log k+1]-q_k
-\tfrac12\log(2\pi n)+O(1/n).
\end{align}
Its absolute size is
$O(k\log k+k\log(1+q_k)+T)=O(T^2/U)$.
The weighted prefix adds $(k-1)\log w=O_{\Wc}(k)$.
Substitute \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowergain}--\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lowercancel} into \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:jensen}.
All remaining errors are bounded by
\[
O_{\Wc}\{k\log k+k\log(1+q_k)+k+T+T^2/k+T/\sqrt{k}+TU+U^2\}
=O_{\Wc}(T^2/U).
\]
This proves the matching lower half, and completes Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:weighted}.

\section{The probability law}\label{wasc:lv:sec:probability}
Write $b_n=(\log n)^2$ and $U_n=\log\log n$. For each real $t$, the finite sum defining $H_n$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:exactmgf}
\E_{n,w}\ee^{tE_n}=\frac{H_n(w\ee^t)}{H_n(w)}.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:weighted}, used at the two positive weights, yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:scaledmgf}
\Lambda_{n,w}(t):=\frac1{b_n}\log\E_{n,w}\ee^{tE_n}
=\frac w2(\ee^t-1)+O_{\Wc,J}(1/U_n),
\end{equation}
uniformly when $w\in\Wc$ and $t$ lies in a fixed compact real interval $J$.
Indeed $w\ee^t$ then stays in a fixed compact positive interval. The tilt is fixed before taking $n\to\infty$; \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:scaledmgf} is not a fluctuation-scale local expansion.

\subsection{Concentration and moments}
Let $X_n=E_n/b_n$. For $\eta>0$ and a small fixed $t>0$, Chernoff's inequality gives
\[
\Prob_{n,w}(X_n\ge w/2+\eta)
\le\exp\{b_n[\Lambda_{n,w}(t)-t(w/2+\eta)]\}
\le\ee^{-c_{w,\eta}b_n}
\]
for large $n$, because the limiting exponent has derivative $-\eta$ at zero.
A small fixed $t<0$ gives the same conclusion for $X_n\le w/2-\eta$.
If that threshold is negative the event is empty; when it is zero the negative-tilt argument still works. This proves \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:concentrationmain}.

The mean is obtained without differentiating the counting remainder. The function
$\Lambda_{n,w}$ is convex and differentiable, with
$\Lambda_{n,w}'(0)=\E_{n,w}E_n/b_n$ and $\Lambda_{n,w}(0)=0$.
For $h>0$, its secant slopes give
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:secants}
-\frac{\Lambda_{n,w}(-h)}h\le\Lambda_{n,w}'(0)
\le\frac{\Lambda_{n,w}(h)}h.
\end{equation}
Letting first $n\to\infty$ and then $h\downarrow0$ proves
$\E_{n,w}E_n\sim (w/2)b_n$.
More quantitatively, use the uniform error in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:scaledmgf} on a fixed enlarged compact interval and put $h=U_n^{-1/2}$.
The secant errors are $O_{\Wc}(h+1/(hU_n))=O_{\Wc}(U_n^{-1/2})$.
This proves \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:meanmain} without any derivative bound on the remainder.

For moment convergence, fix $t>0$. Equation~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:scaledmgf} gives
$\Lambda_{n,w}(t)\le C$ eventually. For $y\ge2C/t$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:tailUI}
\Prob_{n,w}(X_n\ge y)\le\exp(-tb_n y/2).
\end{equation}
Since $b_n\ge1$ eventually, this is a uniform exponential tail. Tail integration gives uniform integrability of $X_n^p$ for every fixed $p>0$ (indeed of every higher fixed moment). Together with concentration, this gives convergence of every positive moment and $L^p$ convergence for $p\ge1$.
In particular $\operatorname{Var}_{n,w}(E_n)=o((\log n)^4)$; no equivalent or sharper variance order follows here.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}, \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:variance}, sharpens this to the upper bound $\operatorname{Var}_{n,w}(E_n)=O((\log n)^3)$; the variance order is still unknown (Section~\ref{wasc:lv:sec:further}).
\end{writenote}

\subsection{A full large deviation principle}
For completeness we prove the LDP by elementary tilting. For any closed $F\subset\R$ and open $G\subset\R$, the claim is
\begin{align}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}b_n^{-1}\log\Prob_{n,w}(X_n\in F)
&\le-\inf_{x\in F}I_w(x),\label{wasc:lv:eq:LDPupper}\\
\liminf_{n\to\infty}b_n^{-1}\log\Prob_{n,w}(X_n\in G)
&\ge-\inf_{x\in G}I_w(x).\label{wasc:lv:eq:LDPlower}
\end{align}
Use $\log0=-\infty$ and the usual empty-set convention.
The Legendre supremum of $\Lambda_w(t)=w(\ee^t-1)/2$ is
\[
\sup_{t\in\R}\{tx-\Lambda_w(t)\}=I_w(x).
\]
For $x>0$ its maximizer satisfies $\ee^t=2x/w$; for $x=0$ the supremum is $w/2$, approached as $t\to-\infty$; and for $x<0$ it is infinite. The rate is lower semicontinuous and tends to infinity as $x\to\infty$, so it is good.

\paragraph{Upper bound.}
For $t>0$ and $R>0$,
\[
\limsup_n b_n^{-1}\log\Prob_{n,w}(X_n\ge R)\le\Lambda_w(t)-tR.
\]
Fix $t=1$ and send $R\to\infty$ to obtain exponential tightness. Negative $X_n$ are impossible.
Let $C\subset[0,\infty)$ be compact and $a<\inf_C I_w$. For each $x\in C$ choose a finite $t_x$ with $t_xx-\Lambda_w(t_x)>a$. This is possible even at zero since $a<I_w(0)$.
Continuity gives a neighborhood $V_x$ on which $t_xy-\Lambda_w(t_x)>a$.
Markov's inequality, valid for either sign of $t_x$, and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:scaledmgf} give
\[
\limsup_n b_n^{-1}\log\Prob_{n,w}(X_n\in V_x)\le-a.
\]
A finite subcover gives the compact upper bound. Let $a$ increase to $\inf_C I_w$; combine with exponential tightness to obtain \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:LDPupper} for every closed set.

\paragraph{Lower bound at positive points.}
Fix $x>0$ and $t=\log(2x/w)$. Tilt $\Prob_{n,w}$ by $\ee^{tE_n}$ and divide by its expectation. The new law is exactly $Q_n=\Prob_{n,w\ee^t}=\Prob_{n,2x}$.
The concentration just proved implies $Q_n(|X_n-x|<\varepsilon)\to1$, exponentially fast.
Undoing the tilt,
\begin{align*}
\Prob_{n,w}(|X_n-x|<\varepsilon)
&=\E_{Q_n}\left[\ee^{b_n\Lambda_{n,w}(t)-tb_nX_n}
\ind_{\{|X_n-x|<\varepsilon\}}\right]\\
&\ge\ee^{b_n[\Lambda_{n,w}(t)-tx-|t|\varepsilon]}
Q_n(|X_n-x|<\varepsilon).
\end{align*}
The logarithm of the final probability divided by $b_n$ tends to zero. Thus the liminf is at least $-I_w(x)-|t|\varepsilon$.
If $G$ contains $x$, use arbitrarily small $\varepsilon$ whose interval is contained in $G$, then optimize over positive $x\in G$.

\paragraph{The endpoint zero.}
If an open $G$ contains zero, it contains positive points approaching zero. Right-continuity of $I_w$ at zero therefore supplies the same lower bound there. If $G$ has no nonnegative point, the lower claim is vacuous. This finishes the full LDP and Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:law}.
This endpoint step concerns neighborhoods of zero; it does not determine the exact atom. The latter follows in the next section.

\section{The exact zero level event}\label{wasc:lv:sec:zero}
\subsection{Identification and the established Fishburn input}
An ordinary ascent sequence uses the same defining bound as \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:def}, with strict ascents instead of weak ascents. If a weak ascent sequence has no levels, its weak ascents are strict in every prefix. It is therefore exactly a primitive ordinary ascent sequence. This proves
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:zerocombo}
H_n(0)=P_n.
\end{equation}
Here evaluating at zero means taking the constant term of the polynomial $H_n(w)$; each zero-level word contributes one.
Let $F_n$ be the ordinary Fishburn number, with $F_0=1$. Compress each maximal constant run in an ordinary ascent sequence to one entry. Strict-ascent counts before each run are unchanged, so the compressed word remains legal and primitive. Conversely, replacing each entry of a primitive word by any positive number of equal copies preserves all bounds. These operations are inverse, giving
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:forwardtransform}
F_n=\sum_{m=1}^n\binom{n-1}{m-1}P_m\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
This identity, and its series form below, is Jel\'inek's Fact~5.1 \cite{jelinek}; Part~\ref{wasc:gr:part} states it as \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:zeroanchor} without credit.
\end{writenote}
In formal ordinary series, $F(z)=P(z/(1-z))$ and $P(t)=F(t/(1+t))$. These compositions are coefficientwise legitimate because the inner series has zero constant term, even though the outer series has radius zero. Coefficient extraction yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:inversebinomial}
P_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^j\binom{n-1}{j}F_{n-j},\qquad n\ge1,\qquad P_0=1.
\end{equation}
The upper index is $n-1$, not $n$.
The same substitution counts primitive Fishburn matrices: replace every occupied entry in a zero-one Fishburn matrix by a positive integer. Thus the number of primitive matrices of size $n$ agrees with $P_n$ by the same inverse substitution, without requiring a statistic-preserving bijection.
There is no analogous run-compression claim for the general positive-weight weak-ascent model: inserted equalities create additional weak ascents and change subsequent allowed alphabets.

The only external coefficient asymptotic used in this section is the established ordinary Fishburn estimate~\cite{jelinek,hwang},
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:fishburn}
F_m=C_Fm!\mu^m\sqrt m\,(1+O(1/m)),\qquad
C_F=12\sqrt3\,\pi^{-5/2}\ee^{\pi^2/12}.
\end{equation}
The primitive/all limiting ratio below is already Jel\'inek's Fact~5.2, credited to Drmota~\cite{jelinek}; the transform proof is prior work too. We include its summability argument to make the endpoint application explicit, and not as a novelty claim.

\subsection{Summable domination and quantitative ratio}
Write $R=1/\mu=\pi^2/6$ and
$F_m=C_Fm!\mu^m\sqrt m\,b_m$. Then $b_m=1+O(1/m)$ and every $b_m>0$.
There are constants $0<c\le C<\infty$ with $c\le b_m\le C$ for all $m\ge1$, by absorbing finitely many small $m$ into the asymptotic bounds.
Exact factorial cancellation gives, for $0\le j\le n-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:domterm}
\binom{n-1}{j}\frac{F_{n-j}}{F_n}
=\frac{R^j}{j!}(1-j/n)^{3/2}\frac{b_{n-j}}{b_n}.
\end{equation}
Extend this term by zero for $j\ge n$. It is bounded absolutely by $(C/c)R^j/j!$, a summable sequence. For each fixed $j$, the factor after $R^j/j!$ tends to one. Dominated convergence in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:inversebinomial} gives $P_n/F_n\to\ee^{-R}$, with the full near-$n$ range controlled.
For a quantitative bound choose $D$ such that $|b_m-1|\le D/m$ for all $m\ge1$.
With $m=n-j$ and $d=(m/n)^{3/2}$,
\begin{align}\label{wasc:lv:eq:ratiobound}
|d b_m/b_n-1|
&\le|d-1|+d|b_m-b_n|/b_n\notag\\
&\le\frac{3j}{2n}+\frac Dc d(1/m+1/n)
\le\frac{3j/2+2D/c}{n}.
\end{align}
Here the derivative of $x^{3/2}$ is bounded by $3/2$ on $[0,1]$, and
$d/m=\sqrt m/n^{3/2}\le1/n$. Summing \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:ratiobound} against $R^j/j!$ costs $O(1/n)$; the missing tail obeys
\[
\sum_{j\ge n}\frac{R^j}{j!}\le\frac1n\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{jR^j}{j!}
=R\ee^R/n.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:primitiveratio}
\frac{P_n}{F_n}=\ee^{-R}+O(1/n),\qquad
P_n=C_Pn!\mu^n\sqrt n\,(1+O(1/n)),\quad
C_P=12\sqrt3\,\pi^{-5/2}\ee^{-\pi^2/12}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Application at positive weight}
For fixed $w>0$, the numerator of $\Prob_{n,w}(E_n=0)$ is $P_n$, independent of $w$. Thus
\[
\log\Prob_{n,w}(E_n=0)=\log P_n-\log H_n(w).
\]
Equation~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:primitiveratio} supplies
$\log P_n=\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\tfrac12\log n+\log C_P+O(1/n)$.
Subtract Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:weighted}; the half-logarithm and constant are absorbed by its error. This proves Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:zeromain}, including compact-uniformity.
The combination with the weighted theorem is the present endpoint conclusion. The primitive ratio is prior work. No relative-error equivalent for $\Prob_{n,w}(E_n=0)$ follows from the present denominator estimate.
At $w=0$ the induced law is concentrated on primitive objects and $E_n=0$ identically; this is a separate endpoint model, not a uniform extension to $n$-dependent weights approaching zero.
\begin{remark}[\wtag, 5 October 2026]\label{wasc:lv:rem:zerosharp}
With Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part}'s Theorem~\ref{wasc:sh:thm:main}, for every compact $\Wc\subset(0,\infty)$,
\[
\log\Prob_{n,w}(E_n=0)=-\frac w2(\log n)^2+O_{\Wc}(\log n)\qquad(w\in\Wc).
\]
\emph{Proof.} By \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:zerocombo} the numerator is $P_n$, so $\log\Prob_{n,w}(E_n=0)=\log P_n-\log H_n(w)$. By \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:primitiveratio}, $\log P_n=\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac12\log n+\log C_P+O(1/n)$, with no dependence on $w$. By \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:mainweighted}, $\log H_n(w)=\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac w2(\log n)^2+O_{\Wc}(\log n)$, uniformly for $w\in\Wc$. Subtracting, the factorial and exponential terms cancel and $\frac12\log n+\log C_P+O(1/n)=O(\log n)$ is absorbed. \qed

In the scale of Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:zeromain}, the error is $O(\sqrt{b_n})$ instead of $O(b_n/\log\log n)$. It is still a logarithmic statement: since the $O_{\Wc}(\log n)$ of Part~\ref{wasc:sh:part} is not identified, no relative-error equivalent for the atom follows.
\end{remark}

\section{The threshold inverse}\label{wasc:lv:sec:inverse}
Equation~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:monotone} proves that $N_w(X)$ is well-defined and that no eventual-monotonicity assumption is needed, even for real weights. The initial equality $H_0=H_1=1$ is irrelevant as $X\to\infty$.
By Stirling and Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:weighted}, for integers $n\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:inversecount}
\log H_n(w)=f(n)+\frac w2(\log n)^2+
O_{\Wc}\!\left(\frac{(\log n)^2}{\log\log n}\right),\qquad
f(t)=t\log(\mu t/\ee).
\end{equation}
Let $L=\log X$, and let $x=L/W_0(\mu L/\ee)$, so $f(x)=L$.
For sufficiently large $x$, define
\[
z=x-\frac{w(\log x)^2}{2\log(\mu x)},\qquad
A_w(t)=f(t)+\frac w2(\log t)^2.
\]
Uniformly on a fixed compact $\Wc$, $z-x=O_{\Wc}(\log x)$.
Taylor's theorem, using $f'(t)=\log(\mu t)$ and $f''(t)=1/t$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:lv:eq:inversecancellation}
A_w(z)=L+O_{\Wc}((\log x)^2/x).
\end{equation}
Indeed the first-order change of $f$ cancels $w(\log x)^2/2$, while the quadratic remainder of $f$ and the change in the log-square term are both $O_{\Wc}((\log x)^2/x)$.
Put $d_x=\log x/\log\log x$. For a sufficiently large fixed $C_{\Wc}$, form the integer brackets
\[
m_- =\lfloor z-C_{\Wc}d_x\rfloor,\qquad
m_+ =\lceil z+C_{\Wc}d_x\rceil.
\]
On this range $A_w'(t)=\log(\mu t)+w\log t/t\sim\log x$ uniformly. Therefore
\[
A_w(m_\pm)-L=\pm C_{\Wc}\frac{(\log x)^2}{\log\log x}(1+o(1))
+O_{\Wc}(\log x),
\]
where rounding contributes only $O(\log x)$. Choose $C_{\Wc}$ larger than the uniform error constant in \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:inversecount}. Equations~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:inversecount}--\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:inversecancellation} then ensure, for all sufficiently large $X$,
\[
\log H_{m_-}(w)<L<\log H_{m_+}(w).
\]
Exact monotonicity gives $m_-<N_w(X)\le m_+$, proving \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:inverse} with its signed negative correction. This is an integer-bracket argument, not a formal differentiation of a sequence or an assumed coefficient ratio.
For positive integer $w$, independently coloring each level realizes every summand $w^{E(x)}$ as an exact number of colored words. For arbitrary real $w>0$, the same proof concerns a weighted sum. The inverse error diverges; it is not a bounded-error inverse or a certified finite numerical error bar.

\section{Main results and their meaning}\label{wasc:sh:sec:results}
The weak ascent count is OEIS \href{https://oeis.org/A336070}{A336070}. Its factorial scale hides a substantial subexponential correction. This report sharpens that correction to a polynomial multiplicative uncertainty and, as a consequence, determines a threshold inverse up to a bounded additive error.

A weak ascent sequence is a nonnegative integer word $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ with $x_1=0$ and
\[
x_{i+1}\le 1+\#\{t<i:x_{t+1}\ge x_t\}.
\]
Let $E(x)=\#\{i<n:x_{i+1}=x_i\}$ and
\[
H_n(w)=\sum_{|x|=n}w^{E(x)},\qquad \mu=6/\pi^2.
\]
Here $w>0$ weights equal adjacent pairs, called \emph{levels}. The empty word has weight one. Independently, for an integer $d\ge1$, a \emph{difference $d$ ascent} is an index with $x_{i+1}>x_i-d$; a difference ascent sequence satisfies the same upper bound with the number of these $d$-ascents. Denote its unweighted count by $a_n^{(d)}$. In particular $a_n^{(1)}=H_n(1)$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp logarithmic remainders]\label{wasc:sh:thm:main}
For every compact $\Wc=[w_-,w_+]\subset(0,\infty)$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:mainweighted}
\log H_n(w)=\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac w2(\log n)^2+O_{\Wc}(\log n),
\end{equation}
uniformly for $w\in\Wc$. For every fixed integer $d\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:maindifference}
\log a_n^{(d)}=\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac d2(\log n)^2+O_d(\log n).
\end{equation}
The constants and the starting threshold for $n$ can depend on the indicated compact interval or fixed integer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
This theorem strengthens Part~\ref{wasc:gr:part}, Theorem~\ref{wasc:gr:thm:logsquare} (the case $w=1$, equivalently $d=1$), and Part~\ref{wasc:lv:part}, Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:weighted}, whose remainders were $O((\log n)^2/\log\log n)$. It answers, for every fixed $d\ge1$ and at the logarithmic-square scale, Part~\ref{wasc:gr:part}'s question on the dependence of the subexponential factor on fixed $d$ (Section~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:questions}). The case $d=0$ of \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:maindifference}, which this Part's proof excludes, follows from the Fishburn asymptotic: \wtag\ Remark~\ref{wasc:sh:rem:dzero}.
\end{writenote}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded error threshold inverses]\label{wasc:sh:cor:inverse}
For $B_n=H_n(w)$ or $a_n^{(d)}$, set $c=w$ or $d$, respectively, and
\[
N(Y)=\min\{n\ge1:B_n\ge Y\},\quad L=\log Y,\quad
x=\frac{L}{W_0(\mu L/\ee)},
\]
where $W_0$ is the principal real Lambert function. Then
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:maininverse}
N(Y)=x-\frac{c(\log x)^2}{2\log(\mu x)}+O(1)
=x-\frac c2\log x+O(1).
\end{equation}
The weighted error is uniform on each fixed positive compact interval; the difference-ascent error may depend on fixed $d$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
This corollary supersedes the inverses \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:inverseintro} and \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:dinverse} of Part~\ref{wasc:gr:part} and Theorem~\ref{wasc:lv:thm:inversemain} of Part~\ref{wasc:lv:part}, and the statements there that those inverses are not bounded-error inverses.
\end{writenote}

The two models have different meanings. Substituting an integer $w=d$ does not turn levels into difference ascents. For example $H_3(2)=12$, whereas $a_3^{(2)}=6$. The coincidence of the two correction coefficients reflects related transfer calculations, not an equality of the families.

Exponentiating \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:mainweighted} says that $H_n(w)$ lies between
$n!\mu^n\exp\{(w/2)\log^2 n\}$ times $n^{-C}$ and the same expression times $n^C$, eventually. This is a polynomial-factor uncertainty. It is not a relative-error equivalent and it does not identify a power of $n$, an amplitude, a full transseries, or a finite certified error constant. The bounded inverse is substantially more precise than its leading Lambert term, but is not eventual exact integer recovery.

\paragraph{Scope and method.}
The proof is self-contained. A frozen geometric kernel and an exact quadratic correction are combined with a calibration curve. A capped slope controls every small state, including transitions across the cap. A run decomposition removes the crude factorial prefix loss. These two improvements allow a prefix of length $k\asymp\log n$, and retaining the exact normalization cancellation makes every accumulated error $O(\log n)$. For fixed $d$, a shifted row replaces the eigenvector formula and a separate boundary argument recovers its full coefficient. No numerical fit supplies any analytic step.

The ordinary-ascent endpoint $d=0$ is the classical Fishburn family and is separate; the zero prefix used below would not have $k-1$ ascents there. For real $d>0$, integrality of the letters makes the effective parameter $\lceil d\rceil$. No uniformity for $d=d(n)$, or for weights approaching zero or infinity, is claimed. No OEIS identifiers for unrestricted $d=2,3$ are asserted.

\end{writenote}

\section{Exact states and recurrences}\label{wasc:sh:sec:states}
At length $i$, let $K$ be the relevant ascent count and $l$ the last letter, and put $M=K+2$. A legal extension chooses $j\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$. For weak ascents it updates
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:transition}
a=\ind_{\{j\ge l\}},\quad e=\ind_{\{j=l\}},\qquad
(K,l)\longmapsto(K+a,j),\quad\hbox{multiplier }w^e.
\end{equation}
In the difference model, $a=\ind_{\{j>l-d\}}$ and the multiplier is one. Induction gives $0\le l\le K\le i-1$: a non-ascent decreases the letter, while an ascent permits at most the new $K$. In particular every letter $x_i$ is at most $i-1$.

Starting with $A_1(0,0;w)=1$, the exact weighted recurrence is
\[
A_{i+1}(K+a,j;w)\mathrel{+}=w^e A_i(K,l;w),\qquad 0\le j\le K+1.
\]
Summing states gives $H_i(w)$. For example
\[
H_1=1,\quad H_2=1+w,\quad H_3=2+3w+w^2,\quad
H_4=5+11w+6w^2+w^3.
\]
If $C_d(n,k,l)$ counts difference-ascent states, the equivalent incoming form is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:drecurrence}
C_d(n+1,k,j)=\sum_{l=j+d}^{k}C_d(n,k,l)
+\sum_{l=0}^{\min(k-1,j+d-1)}C_d(n,k-1,l).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{wasc:sh:eq:drecurrence} is for $n\ge1$ and $0\le j\le k\le n$. Define states outside their support to be zero; empty sums also vanish. The first sum is a non-ascent; the second is an ascent. This formula also gives the $d=0$ benchmark, although our asymptotic proof excludes that endpoint.

Exactly one weak extension is a level, so
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:monotone}
H_{i+1}(w)=\sum_{K,l}(K+1+w)A_i(K,l;w)\ge(1+w)H_i(w)>H_i(w).
\end{equation}
Similarly $a_{i+1}^{(d)}=\sum_{K,l}(K+2)C_d(i,K,l)\ge2a_i^{(d)}$. These strict inequalities for $i\ge1$ justify first-crossing inverses.

Split a sequence at each ascent into runs of non-ascents. For $d\ge1$ each such run is strictly decreasing. The run beginning after the $r$th ascent starts at most at $r$, so has at most $r+1$ entries. Thus both models satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:reachable}
i\le (K+1)(K+2)/2,\qquad M\ge\sqrt{2i}.
\end{equation}

\section{A logarithmic cutoff and exact normalization}\label{wasc:sh:sec:cutoff}
Write $T=\log n$. Choose a sufficiently large fixed $A$, depending only on $\Wc$ or on fixed $d$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:cutoff}
k=\lceil AT\rceil.
\end{equation}
The size requirements on $A$ will be made explicit by inequalities below. Local kernel and corrector constants are selected first; $A$ is then fixed before $n$ tends to infinity. In particular, all relevant local estimates hold when $Q/M$ is sufficiently small and fixed. They do not require $Q/M\to0$: the exponential-moment proof only needs its extra tilt to be at most $q/2$, and the logarithm expansion only needs $|(w-1)t|\le1/2$.

For $i\ge k$ and $T\ge2$, $Q_i/i\le3/A$. A global calibration bound is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:globalchi}
\ee^q\chi(\ee^q)
=\int_0^\infty\ee^{-u}\{q+u+Q_0(q+u)\}\dd u\le q+2.
\end{equation}
This identity uses $t/(\ee^t-1)=\ee^{-t}(t+Q_0(t))$, and is valid at $q=0$. The positive tail expansion \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:chitail} also gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:cutoffcal}
q_k=T+\log(\mu/A)+O_A(T^{-1}),\qquad q_k^2=T^2+O_A(T).
\end{equation}
Here is a noncircular derivation. Since $k/n\to0$, $q_k\to\infty$. Taking logarithms of the tail yields
\[
q_k=T-\log k+\log(q_k+1)+\log\mu+O(\ee^{-q_k}).
\]
Together with $q_k\le2T$, this first gives
$T-\log T+O_A(1)\le q_k\le T+O_A(1)$, hence $q_k/T\to1$.
Rewrite the identity as
\[
q_k-T=\log(\mu/A)+\log((q_k+1)/T)-\log(k/(AT))+O(\ee^{-q_k}).
\]
It gives a bounded difference and then \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:cutoffcal} by substitution. The ceiling contributes only $O_A(T^{-1})$.

The deterministic zero-prefix normalization is especially important. With $v=\ee^{q_k}$, Stirling and $k=n\mu\chi(v)$ give
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:normalcancel}
D_n&:=(k-1)q_k+(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)-\log(n!\mu^n)\notag\\
&=k[\log(v\chi(v))-\log k+1]-q_k-\tfrac12\log(2\pi n)+O(n^{-1}).
\end{align}
By \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:cutoffcal} and the tail expansion,
$v\chi(v)/k=A^{-1}+O_A(T^{-1})$, so $D_n=O_A(T)$. The terms $\log(v\chi(v))$ and $\log k$ must be kept together. Estimating them separately would lose an avoidable $T\log T$.

\section{The capped weighted upper bound}\label{wasc:sh:sec:upper}
\subsection{Constants and the capped potential}
Put $\Psi(R)=\log R-R+1$, $\eta_0=-\Psi(0.49)>0$, $\eta_1=-\Psi(3/4)>0$, and $b=(w_+-1)_+$. Choose a fixed $p>0$ with
\[
p\le1,\qquad (3/2+b)p\le\eta_0/4.
\]
Let $\zeta(R)$ be zero for $R\le1/2$, linear with slope four on $[1/2,3/4]$, and one for $R\ge3/4$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:upperpotential}
F_i(K,l)=\exp\{q_iK-\min(q_i/M,p)l+\zeta(M/S_i)h(q_i,M,l)/M\},\quad M=K+2.
\end{equation}
Where $\zeta=0$, define the whole corrector term to be zero, without invoking local derivative estimates. At time $n$, $F_n=1$ exactly.

Call a state uncapped if $q_i/M\le p$, assigning equality to this case. Along any path $q_i$ decreases and $M_i$ increases; therefore every successor of an uncapped state is uncapped. A capped state has
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:capsupport}
M<2T/p,\quad M/S_i<4/(Ap),\quad
(M+a)/S_{i+1}\le4/(Ap)+2/(i+1).
\end{equation}
Take $A\ge32/p$, so both correctors vanish on every transition from a capped state, eventually.

Select local constants $C_0,C_1$ for the fixed region $M\ge C_0Q$ first. After $p$ is fixed, put
\[
B_{\Wc,p}=\max(1,w_+)\frac{\ee^{p+1}(1+\ee)}{1-\ee^{-p}}.
\]
Choose $A\ge\max(16C_0,32/p,32w_+,6\ee B_{\Wc,p})$, enlarged further so the intermediate-state error below is at most $\eta_1/2$. Each requirement depends only on fixed constants. Subsequent increases in $n$ make all $1/i$ and $1/M$ residuals small uniformly for $i\ge k$. There is no circular choice.

\subsection{Regular uncapped states}
Fix $i\ge k$, and abbreviate $q=q_{i+1}$, $Q=1+q$, $S=S_{i+1}$, $R=M/S$, and $\Delta q=q-q_i$. If $R\ge0.49$, then $M\ge0.245(i+1)$, so sufficiently large $A$ makes the cap inactive and ensures $M\ge C_0(1+q_i)$ on all interpolation segments.

Replace the next corrector by $\zeta(M/S)h(q,M,j)/M$, and the current corrector by $\zeta(M/S)h(q,M,l)/M$. Each replacement costs $O_{\Wc}(Q^2/i^2)$. For detail, differentiating $H(q,M,j/M)/M$ at fixed $j$ gives
\[
\frac{H_M}{M}-\frac{(j/M)H_x+H}{M^2}
=O(Q^2/M^2+Q^3/M^3).
\]
The next cutoff change costs $O(Q^2/(SM))$; the time change costs $O(Q|\Delta q|/M)$; and the current cutoff change is bounded by its Lipschitz constant times $M|S-S_i|/(S_iS)$. Thus its corners cause no gap. Also
\[
-\frac{qj}{M+a}+\frac{qj}{M}=\frac{qaj}{M(M+a)}\le\frac{qaj}{M^2}.
\]
Factoring the frozen eigenvector gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:regulartransfer}
\sum_j w^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}\frac{F_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{F_i(K,l)}
&\le\lambda_w\exp\{\Delta qK-\Delta q\,l/M+C_{\Wc}Q^2/i^2\}\notag\\
&\hspace{18mm}\cdot\E_{P_w}\exp(Z/M),
\end{align}
with $Z$ from \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:Z} and $\zeta=\zeta(R)$.
Since $2\sinh(z/2)\ge z$, the global bound
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:eigenupper}
\lambda\le\frac M{g(\ee^q)}\exp(q/(2M))
\end{equation}
holds without a small-ratio assumption. For $R\ge3/4$, the eigenvalue correction, the weight correction, and the corrector mean contribute, respectively,
\[
q/(2M),\qquad (w-1)q/M+O_{\Wc}(1/M+Q^2/M^2),\qquad c/M\le q/(2M).
\]
Their sum is bounded by $wq/M$ plus the stated errors, also when $w<1$. Calibration gives
\[
\log(M/g)-K/S-\log(n\mu/\ee)=\Psi(R)+2/S,
\qquad-\Delta q\,l/M\le2/i.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:regularbound}
\log\frac{\sum_jw^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}F_{i+1}/F_i}{n\mu/\ee}
\le\Psi(R)+\frac{wq}{SR}+C_{\Wc}(Q^2/i^2+1/i).
\end{equation}
For $0.49\le R<3/4$, use $(1-\zeta)b_l+\zeta c\le q$ instead. The bound is
\[
\Psi(R)+C_{\Wc}(Q/i+Q^2/i^2+1/i)<0.
\]
Indeed $\Psi(R)\le-\eta_1$ and $Q/i\le3/A$: a sufficiently small fixed ratio suffices, rather than a ratio claimed to vanish.

For $\beta=wq/S\le1/8$, elementary maximization gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:psimax}
\sup_{R\ge3/4}\{\Psi(R)+\beta/R\}\le\beta+2\beta^2.
\end{equation}
The interior maximum is at $R_+=(1+\sqrt{1-4\beta})/2$. The derivative of its value with respect to $\beta$ is $1/R_+\le1+4\beta$; integrate from zero. The endpoint is no larger for this interval of $\beta$. Thus \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:regularbound} is at most
$wq/S+C_{\Wc}(Q^2/i^2+1/i)$.

\subsection{Small uncapped states}
If $R<0.49$, eventually both cutoffs vanish because
\[
M/S_i\le0.49(1+2/i)<1/2,\qquad (M+a)/S\le0.49+2/(i+1)<1/2.
\]
All successors remain uncapped. The denominator discrepancy is at most $q/M$. Use only the exact eigenvector, \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:eigenupper}, and the global weight bound \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:globalperturb} to obtain
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:smalluncapped}
\log\frac{\sum_jw^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}F_{i+1}/F_i}{n\mu/\ee}
&\le\Psi(R)+\frac{3q}{2M}+b\frac QM+\frac6i\notag\\
&\le-\eta_0+(3/2+b)p+b/M+6/i.
\end{align}
Since $M\ge\sqrt{2i}$, the last expression is at most $-\eta_0/2$ eventually. No corrector estimate is used here.

\subsection{Every capped state and every cap crossing}
For a capped current state, both correctors vanish by \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:capsupport}. Freeze instead $f_l^{\rm cap}=\ee^{-pl}$. Direct summation gives
\[
\frac{(T_qf^{\rm cap})_l}{f_l^{\rm cap}}
=\sum_{j<l}\ee^{p(l-j)}+\ee^q\sum_{j\ge l}\ee^{-p(j-l)}
\le\frac{\ee^{pM}+\ee^q}{1-\ee^{-p}}.
\]
The weighted row is at most $\max(1,w_+)$ times this expression. Because $pM<q_i\le q+2/i$, its numerator is at most $\ee^q(1+\ee^{2/i})$.

The actual next slope is $p_{\rm new}=\min(q/(M+a),p)$. When the successor stays capped, its ratio to the frozen vector is one. If it becomes uncapped, its log ratio is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:capcross}
(p-p_{\rm new})j\le p(M+a)-q<q_i-q+p\le p+2/i.
\end{equation}
This covers a crossing caused by the change of time, by the increment of $M$, or by both. At cap equality the expression is continuous and the same bound holds. The remaining temporal factor is $\exp(\Delta qK)\le1$. Hence for $i\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:caprow}
\sum_jw^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}\frac{F_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{F_i(K,l)}
\le B_{\Wc,p}\ee^q.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:globalchi} and $(i+1)=n\mu\chi(\ee^q)$, its normalized value is at most
\[
\frac{\ee B_{\Wc,p}(q+2)}{i+1}\le\frac{3\ee B_{\Wc,p}}A\le\frac12.
\]
This is a pointwise bound for every capped reachable state. No spectral expansion is made in that range.

All states have now been covered, including both cutoff corners and every cap equality. Combining the bounds yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:globalstep}
\sum_jw^{\ind_{\{j=l\}}}F_{i+1}(K+a,j)
\le F_i(K,l)\frac{n\mu}{\ee}
\exp\left\{\frac{wq_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}+C_{\Wc}\left(\frac{Q_{i+1}^2}{i^2}+\frac1i\right)\right\}.
\end{equation}

\section{The sharper prefix entropy and upper telescope}\label{wasc:sh:sec:prefix}
A length-$k$ weak prefix with $r$ strict descents has $K=k-1-r$ and entries in $\{0,\ldots,k-1\}$. Cut it at its descents into $r+1$ nondecreasing runs. Discarding all legality and boundary restrictions, and permitting empty runs, gives the upper bound
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:prefixcount}
N_{k,r}\le[z^k](1-z)^{-k(r+1)}
=\binom{k(r+2)-1}{k}\le[\ee(r+2)]^k.
\end{equation}
The maximal-run decomposition is unique; the coefficient already sums over run lengths, so no missing independent choice of cut locations occurs.

By \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:cutoffcal}, $q_k\ge ak$ eventually, where $a=1/(2A)$. The function $1+\log(r+2)-(a/2)r$ has a finite upper bound $C_a$ on $r\ge0$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:runpenalty}
\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}\ee^{-q_kr}[\ee(r+2)]^k
\le\ee^{kC_a}\sum_{r\ge0}\ee^{-kar/2}
\le\frac{\ee^{kC_a}}{1-\ee^{-a/2}}.
\end{equation}
The level weight is at most $\max(1,w_+)^{k-1}$. The last-letter part of the capped potential is nonpositive. A nonzero prefix corrector forces $M>S_k/2\ge k/4$, so the local bounds apply, and its exponent is $O_{\Wc}(Q_k^2/k)$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:prefixmass}
\log\sum_{|x|=k}w^{E(x)}F_k(K,l)
\le(k-1)q_k+O_{\Wc}(k+Q_k^2/k).
\end{equation}
Unlike a crude $k!$ bound, this estimate retains the strong penalty for many descents.

Positivity and $F_n=1$ allow \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:globalstep} to telescope backward. The decreasing function $q/S$ satisfies
\[
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}\le\frac12q_k^2,
\qquad
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\left(\frac{Q_{i+1}^2}{i^2}+\frac1i\right)=O(T^2/k+T).
\]
Combining with \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:prefixmass} gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:upperfinish}
\log H_n(w)&\le(k-1)q_k+(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)+\frac w2q_k^2\notag\\
&\quad+O_{\Wc}(k+Q_k^2/k+T+T^2/k)\notag\\
&=\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac w2T^2+O_{\Wc}(T),
\end{align}
using \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:cutoffcal} and the cancellation \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:normalcancel}.

\section{The matching weighted lower bound}\label{wasc:sh:sec:lower}
\subsection{A positive path law and its exact likelihood}
Use the same logarithmic cutoff $k=\lceil AT\rceil$, increasing the fixed $A$ if needed for the local kernel bounds. Begin with the single legal prefix of $k$ zeros. Its state is
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
This section repeats Part~\ref{wasc:lv:part}, Section~\ref{wasc:lv:sec:lower}, word for word except for the cutoff (here $k=\lceil AT\rceil$ instead of $\lceil100T^2/U^2\rceil$), the lower bound $A/3$ in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:prefixsupport} and the sentences after it, the name $\widetilde F_i$ of the uncut potential (there $F_i$), and the closing paragraph, which here uses the exact normalization \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:normalcancel}. It is printed in full because Section~\ref{wasc:sh:sec:difference} cites its equations.
\end{writenote}
$K_k=k-1$, $L_k=0$, $M_k=k+1$ and its weight is $w^{k-1}$.
At each time $i=k,\ldots,n-1$, sample $j$ from $P_w$, with parameters
$q=q_{i+1}$ and $M=M_i$. Denote this path law by $\Prob_w^*$, with expectation $\E_w^*$.
The weak-ascent count never decreases, so every path has
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:prefixsupport}
M_i\ge k+1,\qquad \frac{M_i}{1+q_{i+1}}\ge\frac{k}{1+2T}\ge A/3\qquad(T\ge1).
\end{equation}
Choose $A/3$ at least as large as every fixed local threshold. All corrector and eigenvalue expansions then apply on every sampled path and on each short interpolation segment, uniformly for $w\in\Wc$. The ratio need not diverge. The later drift requirement $C_{\Wc}/M_i\to0$ follows separately because $k\to\infty$.
Use the uncut potential
\[
\widetilde F_i(K,l)=\exp\{q_iK-q_i l/(K+2)+h(q_i,K+2,l)/(K+2)\}.
\]
For a step let
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:likelihoodratio}
R_i^w=\frac{w^e\widetilde F_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{\widetilde F_i(K,l)P_w(l,j)}.
\end{equation}
The product telescopes, and $\widetilde F_n=1$, so the total weighted mass of completions of this fixed prefix is exactly
\[
w^{k-1}\widetilde F_k(k-1,0)\E_w^*\exp\left(\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\log R_i^w\right).
\]
It is a positive sub-sum of $H_n(w)$ even for $w<1$. Jensen gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:jensen}
\log H_n(w)\ge(k-1)\log w+(k-1)q_k+
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_w^*\log R_i^w.
\end{equation}
There is no $k!$ factor in this single-prefix lower bound.

\subsection{Tracking the sampled state}
Put $X_i=L_i/M_i$, $Y_i=K_i-X_i$, and
$V_i=((j-L_i)\bmod M_i)/M_i$. A deterministic calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:tracker}
Y_{i+1}-Y_i=1-V_i+aX_{i+1}/M_i\in[0,2].
\end{equation}
Indeed $j/M_i-X_i=V_i-(1-a)$, while $j/M_i=X_{i+1}(1+a/M_i)$.
This includes equality steps, for which $V_i=0$ and $a=1$.
Since the identity part of \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:mixture} has $V=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:driftperturb}
\E_{P_w}V=(1-\delta)\E_PV,\qquad
|\E_{P_w}V-\E_PV|\le C_{\Wc}/M.
\end{equation}
For the second inequality both factors matter: $|\delta|=O_{\Wc}(Q/M)$ and
$\E_PV=O(Q^{-1})$. At $q=0$,
$\E_{P_w}V=(M-1)/(2(M+w-1))$, still differing from the unweighted mean by $O_{\Wc}(1/M)$.
Equation~\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:Vmean} also gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:ordinarydrift}
1-\E_PV=\alpha(q)+\frac{1-Q_0(q/M)}q=\alpha(q)+O(1/M),
\end{equation}
where the quotient has value $1/(2M)$ at zero. The last term in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:tracker} lies in $[0,1/M_i]$. Boundedness of $\alpha'$ and \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:caldiscrete} now give
\begin{align}
\E_w^*[Y_{i+1}-Y_i\mid\mathcal F_i]&\ge\frac12-C_{\Wc}/M_i\ge\frac13,
\label{wasc:sh:eq:driftpositive}\\
\left|\E_w^*[Y_{i+1}-Y_i\mid\mathcal F_i]-(S_{i+1}-S_i)\right|
&\le C_{\Wc}(1/M_i+1/i).\label{wasc:sh:eq:drifttrack}
\end{align}
The threshold for the second inequality in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:driftpositive} may depend on $\Wc$.
Subtract conditional means in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:tracker} to obtain a martingale with increments bounded in absolute value by two. Since $Y_k=k-1$, the event $M_i<i/8$ forces its displacement below
\[
i/8-(k-1)-(i-k)/3=-5i/24-2k/3+1.
\]
Azuma's inequality proves, for absolute $c,C>0$ after a sufficiently large compact-uniform threshold,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:lowprob}
\Prob_w^*(M_i<i/8)\le C\ee^{-ci}\qquad(k\le i<n).
\end{equation}
At $i=k$ the event is empty. No independence is assumed.
On good states $M_i\ge i/8$, $1/M_i\le8/i$; on bad states use $M_i\ge k$ and \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:lowprob}. The $L^2$ triangle inequality yields
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:recipL2}
\left\|\sum_{i=k}^{m-1}1/M_i\right\|_2
\le8\sum_{i=k}^{m-1}1/i+\frac1k\sum_{i=k}^{m-1}C\ee^{-ci/2}
\le CT.
\end{equation}
The martingale variance is $O(m)$, and $|Y_k-S_k|\le k$. Sum \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:drifttrack}, use \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:recipL2}, and note $M_i-Y_i=2+X_i$. We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:trackingL2}
\E_w^*[(M_i-S_{i+1})^2]\le C_{\Wc}(i+T^2+k^2).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conditional one-step gain}
Freeze $q=q_{i+1}$, $S=S_{i+1}$, $M=M_i$, $l=L_i$, and $\Delta q=q_{i+1}-q_i$.
Substituting the Doob kernel into \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:likelihoodratio} cancels $w^e$ exactly and gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:exactlogR}
\log R_i^w={}&\log\lambda_w+\Delta qK_i-\Delta q\,l/M
+\frac{qaj}{M(M+a)}\notag\\
&+\frac{h(q,M+a,j)}{M+a}-\frac{h(q_i,M,l)}M.
\end{align}
Using \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:hderiv}, replacement of the two corrector terms by
$[h(q,M,j)-h(q,M,l)]/M$ costs at most
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:lowerreplacement}
C(Q^2/M^2+Q/(Mi)).
\end{equation}
For the next-state change, differentiating at fixed $j$ includes both $H_x$ and $H_M$, giving $O(Q^2/M^2+Q^3/M^3)$; the current-time change costs $O(Q|\Delta q|/M)$. Equation~\eqref{wasc:sh:eq:prefixsupport} justifies the whole interpolation.
Also
$qaj/[M(M+a)]\ge qaj/M^2-q/M^2$.
Take conditional expectation and use the exact weighted corrector identity
\eqref{wasc:lv:eq:weightedZ} at $\zeta=1$, then \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:cbounds}. The last three terms of \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:exactlogR} are at least
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:correctedlower}
q/(2M)-C_{\Wc}(1/M+Q^2/M^2+Q/(Mi)).
\end{equation}
The eigenvalue satisfies exactly
\[
\lambda=n\mu\frac MS\frac{q/M}{1-\ee^{-q/M}}.
\]
The identity
$\log[z/(1-\ee^{-z})]=z/2+\log[(z/2)/\sinh(z/2)]$ implies, in the present range,
\[
\log\lambda\ge\log(n\mu)+\log(M/S)+q/(2M)-Cq^2/M^2.
\]
Together with \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:lambdaexpand},
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:weightedeigenlower}
\log\lambda_w\ge\log(n\mu)+\log(M/S)
+(w-1/2)q/M-C_{\Wc}(1/M+Q^2/M^2).
\end{equation}
A negative intermediate coefficient when $w<1/2$ causes no inequality reversal.
Since $1/S(s)$ changes by $O(1/i^2)$ on $[i,i+1]$ and $K_i\le i$, calibration gives
\[
\Delta qK_i\ge-K_i/S-C/i,\qquad -\Delta q\,l/M\ge0.
\]
Using $K_i=M-2$ and dropping the favorable $2/S$, combine these inequalities to obtain
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:lowerstep}
&\E_w^*[\log R_i^w\mid\mathcal F_i]-\log(n\mu/\ee)\notag\\
&\quad\ge\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})+\frac{wq_{i+1}}{M_i}
-C_{\Wc}\left(\frac1i+\frac1{M_i}+\frac{Q_{i+1}^2}{M_i^2}+
\frac{Q_{i+1}}{M_i i}\right).
\end{align}
Thus the net leading gain is exactly $wq/M$.

\subsection{Summation and prefix normalization}
On good states, $M_i/S_{i+1}\in[1/16,2]$; the double zero of $\Psi$ at one gives
$\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})\ge-C(M_i-S_{i+1})^2/i^2$.
On bad states, $M_i\ge k$ and $S_{i+1}\le n$, so $|\Psi|\le C+T$.
Equations~\eqref{wasc:sh:eq:lowprob} and \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:trackingL2} imply
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:psisum}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_w^*\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})
\ge-C_{\Wc}(T+T^2/k+k)-o(1).
\end{equation}
On good states,
$1/M_i\ge1/S_{i+1}-C|M_i-S_{i+1}|/i^2$.
Drop the nonnegative bad-state part of $q_{i+1}/M_i$, retaining the exponentially small loss of $q_{i+1}/S_{i+1}$. Cauchy--Schwarz and \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:trackingL2}, with $q\le2T$, yield
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:qrecipsum}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_w^*\frac{q_{i+1}}{M_i}
\ge\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}
-C_{\Wc}(T/\sqrt{k}+T^2/k+T)-o(1).
\end{equation}
The explicit errors in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:lowerstep} sum in expectation to
$O_{\Wc}(T+T^2/k)$: on good states use $M_i\ge i/8$, and on bad states use $M_i\ge k$ and \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:lowprob}. Every omitted bad-state sum is an exponentially small factor $\ee^{-ck}$ times a polynomial in $T,k$.
The decreasing function $q/S$ has right Riemann sum differing from its integral by at most $q_k/S_k=O(T/k)$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:lowerriemann}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{q_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}=\frac12q_k^2+O(T/k).
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:lowerstep}--\eqref{wasc:sh:eq:lowerriemann} gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:lowergain}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_w^*\log R_i^w
\ge(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)+\frac w2q_k^2
-C_{\Wc}(k+T+T^2/k+T/\sqrt{k}).
\end{equation}

The prefix contributes $(k-1)\log w=O_{\Wc}(k)$. At $k=\lceil AT\rceil$, every error in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:lowergain} is $O_{\Wc}(T)$. Apply \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:cutoffcal} and \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:normalcancel} to \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:jensen} to obtain
\[
\log H_n(w)\ge\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac w2T^2-O_{\Wc}(T).
\]
Together with \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:upperfinish}, this proves \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:mainweighted}. The lower potential is uncut and is distinct from the capped upper potential. All its transitions retain positive sampling probability, including when $w<1$; the signed identity for $P_w$ has been used only algebraically.

\section{Fixed difference ascents and the shifted row}\label{wasc:sh:sec:difference}
Fix an integer $d\ge1$ for the remainder of this section. Write $s_d=d-1$ and
\[
l'=\max(l-s_d,0),\qquad m=l-l'=\min(s_d,l).
\]
For integer letters, the ascent indicator is precisely $a=\ind_{\{j\ge l'\}}$. This shift is $d-1$, not $d$. The clipping at zero will matter in the lower bound.

\subsection{Exact normalization and corrector cancellation}
For $f_l=\ee^{-ql/M}$ and $T_d(l,j)=\ee^{q\ind_{\{j\ge l'\}}}$, the weak row identity gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:shiftrow}
(T_df)_l=\lambda f_{l'}=\lambda\ee^{mq/M}f_l,\qquad
P_d(l,j)=\frac{T_d(l,j)f_j}{\lambda\ee^{mq/M}f_l}=P(l',j).
\end{equation}
This row normalizer depends on $l$ near zero. We do not call $f$ a Perron eigenvector for $T_d$. The normalized kernel need not be circulant or have a uniform stationary law.

Under $P_d(l,\cdot)$ the forward fraction
$V=((J-l')\bmod M)/M$ has the same truncated geometric law as in Section~\ref{wasc:lv:sec:corrector}. Apply the weak Poisson identity at $l'$ to obtain
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:shiftcancel}
\E_{P_d}\{qaJ/M+h_J-h_l\}=c+h_{l'}-h_l.
\end{equation}
The shift is nonwrapping, so the derivative bound implies
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:shiftcost}
|h_{l'}-h_l|\le C_dQ^2/M\qquad(M\ge C_0Q).
\end{equation}
For $0\le\zeta\le1$, put $Z_d=qaJ/M+\zeta(h_J-h_l)$. Its exact mean is
\[
\E_{P_d}Z_d=(1-\zeta)b_{l'}+\zeta c+\zeta(h_{l'}-h_l).
\]
The circular arc from $l'$ to $J$, followed by \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:shiftcost}, gives
$|Z_d|\le C_d(Q+Q^2V+Q^2/M)$. On enlarging a fixed $C_{0,d}$, the geometric moment proof applies on $M\ge C_{0,d}Q$ and gives
\[
\E_{P_d}[Z_d^2\ee^{|Z_d|/M}]\le C_dQ^2.
\]
The exponential Taylor remainder therefore proves
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dexp}
\log\E_{P_d}\ee^{Z_d/M}
\le\frac{(1-\zeta)b_{l'}+\zeta c}{M}+C_dQ^2/M^2.
\end{equation}
All these estimates hold on a sufficiently small fixed interval of $q/M$, not merely as that ratio tends to zero. At $q=0$, $P_d$ is uniform and the undivided corrector is exactly zero.

\subsection{The fixed difference capped upper bound}
Use the same calibration and choose a fixed $p>0$ such that
\[
(d+1/2)p< -\Psi(0.49)/2.
\]
Then choose a sufficiently large fixed $A=A_d$, and $k=\lceil AT\rceil$. Use the capped potential \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:upperpotential}, with the difference-ascent $K$. The monotonicity of $q_i/M_i$ and all cutoff comparisons remain exact in this model.

For $R=M/S_{i+1}\ge0.49$, choose $A>10/p$ and large enough for $M\ge C_{0,d}(1+q_i)$. The cap is inactive. The moving-corrector replacements have the same $O_d(Q^2/i^2)$ costs calculated in Section~\ref{wasc:sh:sec:upper}. Factoring \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:shiftrow}, then using \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dexp}, gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dregularfull}
\log\frac{\sum_jF_{i+1}(K+a,j)/F_i(K,l)}{n\mu/\ee}
&\le\Psi(R)+(m+1/2)q/M\notag\\
&\quad+\frac{(1-\zeta)b_{l'}+\zeta c}{M}
+C_d(Q^2/i^2+1/i).
\end{align}
For $R\ge3/4$, $c\le q/2$ and $m\le d-1$ make the coefficient at most $d$. For $0.49\le R<3/4$, use $b_{l'}\le q$, giving $d+1/2$. The negative margin of $\Psi$ absorbs the latter term for sufficiently large fixed $A$, since $Q/i\le3/A$. For $R\ge3/4$, apply \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:psimax} with $\beta=dq/S\le4d/A\le1/8$ to get the bound
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dregular}
\frac{dq}{S}+C_d(Q^2/i^2+1/i).
\end{equation}

When an uncapped state has $R<0.49$, both correctors vanish and all its successors remain uncapped. The uncorrected exact row and the denominator discrepancy bounded by $q/M$ give
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dsmall}
\log\frac{\sum_jF_{i+1}/F_i}{n\mu/\ee}
\le\Psi(R)+(d+1/2)q/M+6/i
\le\Psi(0.49)+(d+1/2)p+6/i<0
\end{equation}
eventually. This uses no local corrector estimate in a small state.

For a capped current state, take $A>16/p$, so both endpoint correctors vanish as in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:capsupport}. The exact clipped threshold gives
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dcaprow}
\frac{(T_d f^{\rm cap})_l}{f_l^{\rm cap}}
&=\sum_{j<l'}\ee^{p(l-j)}+\ee^q\sum_{j\ge l'}\ee^{p(l-j)}\notag\\
&\le\frac{\ee^{pM}+\ee^q\ee^{p(d-1)}}{1-\ee^{-p}}.
\end{align}
If $l'=0$ the first sum is empty. The second bound uses $l-l'\le d-1$ and remains valid even if $d>M$. The actual next slope costs at most $\ee^{p+2/i}$ by \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:capcross}. Also $\ee^{pM}<\ee^{q_i}\le\ee^{q+2/i}$ and $\exp(\Delta qK)\le1$. One may therefore take
\[
B_{d,p}=\frac{\ee^{p+2}(\ee^2+\ee^{p(d-1)})}{1-\ee^{-p}}
\]
to obtain a row bound $B_{d,p}\ee^q$. By \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:globalchi}, the normalized row is at most $4\ee B_{d,p}/A$ for $T\ge1$. Choose $A\ge8\ee B_{d,p}$, making it at most $1/2$. The bound covers all capped successors and all capped-to-uncapped transitions pointwise.

Select $p$ after the local constants and $A$ after $p$, the cap constant, and all fixed intermediate-state margins. Also enlarge $A$ to satisfy the lower-chain thresholds below. These are finitely many compatible conditions depending only on $d$. The combined result, for every reachable state and every $i\ge k$, is
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dglobal}
\sum_j F_{i+1}(K+a,j)\le F_i(K,l)\frac{n\mu}{\ee}
\exp\left\{\frac{dq_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}+C_d\left(\frac{Q_{i+1}^2}{i^2}+\frac1i\right)\right\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The shifted prefix encoding}
If a length-$k$ difference-ascent word has $r$ non-$d$-ascents, then $K=k-1-r$. Define
\[
y_i=x_i+(d-1)i\qquad(1\le i\le k).
\]
This transformation is injective, $0\le y_i\le dk-1$, and
\[
y_{i+1}\ge y_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{i+1}>x_i-d.
\]
The $r$ non-ascents are exactly the strict descents of $y$. Split at those descents, discard the inter-run restrictions, and permit empty runs. Over an alphabet of size $dk$ this proves
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dprefixcount}
N_{k,r}^{(d)}\le\binom{dk(r+1)+k-1}{k}
\le[\ee(d(r+1)+1)]^k\le[\ee(d+1)(r+2)]^k.
\end{equation}
The canonical ordered block tuple contains the boundary locations, so again no additional binomial factor is missing.

With $q_k\ge k/(2A)$, the geometric-penalty argument \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:runpenalty} gives $\sum_rN_{k,r}^{(d)}\ee^{-q_kr}=\exp(O_d(k))$. The prefix slope is nonpositive; a nonzero corrector has $M>k/4$ and costs $O_d(Q_k^2/k)$. Thus
\[
\sum_{|x|=k}F_k(K,l)\le\exp\{(k-1)q_k+O_d(T)\}.
\]
Telescope \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dglobal} and apply \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:cutoffcal}--\eqref{wasc:sh:eq:normalcancel}. The accumulated local error is $O_d(T^2/k+T)=O_d(T)$, proving
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dupper}
\log a_n^{(d)}\le\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac d2T^2+O_d(T).
\end{equation}

\subsection{A positive lower path law and its tracker}
Start from the legal prefix of $k$ zeros. Its state is $(K_k,L_k)=(k-1,0)$, with $M_k=k+1$. At time $i$, sample $j$ under $P_d$ frozen at $q=q_{i+1}$ and $M=M_i$, then make the true update. Every legal continuation has positive probability. Since $M_i\ge k+1$, sufficiently large fixed $A$ guarantees all local hypotheses along every path and every interpolation segment.

Use the uncut lower potential
\[
\widetilde F_i(K,l)=\exp\{q_iK-q_i l/M+h(q_i,M,l)/M\},\quad M=K+2,
\]
and the likelihood ratio
\[
R_i^{(d)}=\frac{\widetilde F_{i+1}(K+a,j)}{\widetilde F_i(K,l)P_d(l,j)}.
\]
The exact pathwise telescope and Jensen give
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:djensen}
\log a_n^{(d)}\ge(k-1)q_k+\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_d^*\log R_i^{(d)}.
\end{equation}
There is no factorial prefix multiplier.

Put $X_i=L_i/M_i$ and $Y_i=K_i-X_i$. With $V=((j-l')\bmod M_i)/M_i$, direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dtracker}
Y_{i+1}-Y_i=1-V+aX_{i+1}/M_i+m/M_i\in[0,2].
\end{equation}
Indeed $j/M_i-l'/M_i=V-(1-a)$ and $j/M_i=X_{i+1}(1+a/M_i)$. The shift term is positive. Nonincreasing geometric weights give $\E V\le1/2$, so the conditional drift is at least $1/2$. Subtracting conditional means yields martingale increments bounded by two. From $Y_k=k-1$, Azuma's inequality proves
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dlowprob}
\Prob_d^*(M_i<i/4)\le C\ee^{-ci}.
\end{equation}
For $i=k$ the event is empty. For $i>k$, that event forces a negative martingale displacement of at least $i/4+k/2-1$ in magnitude. Consequently, for every fixed $u>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dinverse}
\E_d^*M_i^{-u}\le(4/i)^u+C(k+1)^{-u}\ee^{-ci}\le C_u i^{-u}.
\end{equation}

The exact geometric mean from \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:Vmean} gives
\[
1-\E V=\alpha(q)+\frac{1-Q_0(q/M_i)}q=\alpha(q)+O(1/M_i).
\]
The quotient at zero is $1/(2M_i)$; boundedness of $[1-Q_0(z)]/z$ on a fixed small interval proves uniformity. Add the last two terms of \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dtracker} and the one-step calibration change to obtain
\[
\left|\E_d^*[Y_{i+1}-Y_i\mid\mathcal F_i]-(S_{i+1}-S_i)\right|
\le C_d(1/M_i+1/i).
\]
By \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dinverse}, $\|\sum_{j=k}^{i-1}1/M_j\|_2\le C\sum_{j=k}^{i-1}1/j\le CT$. The martingale variance is $O(i)$, the initial mismatch is $O(k)$, and $M_i-Y_i=2+X_i$ is bounded. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dtracking}
\E_d^*[(M_i-S_{i+1})^2]\le C_d(i+T^2+k^2).
\end{equation}
These are statements about the specified auxiliary path law, with no claim of stationarity of the actual last letter.

\subsection{The exact lower gain and the clipped shift}
Substituting \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:shiftrow} into the likelihood ratio gives exactly
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dexactR}
\log R_i^{(d)}={}&\log\lambda+\Delta qK_i-\Delta q\,l/M+mq/M
+\frac{qaj}{M(M+a)}\notag\\
&+\frac{h(q,M+a,j)}{M+a}-\frac{h(q_i,M,l)}M.
\end{align}
The state-dependent $mq/M$ has positive sign and cannot be omitted. Replacing the corrector difference by $(h_J-h_l)/M$ costs $O(Q^2/M^2+Q/(Mi))$, as calculated explicitly for the weighted case. Also $qaj/[M(M+a)]\ge qaj/M^2-q/M^2$. Use \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:shiftcancel}--\eqref{wasc:sh:eq:shiftcost} and $c\ge q/2-C(1+q/M)$.

The same local eigenvalue inequality gives
\[
\log\lambda\ge\log(n\mu)+\log(M/S)+q/(2M)-Cq^2/M^2,
\]
while $\Delta qK_i\ge-K_i/S-C/i$ and $-\Delta q\,l/M\ge0$. Combining these identities yields
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dlowerstep}
\E_d^*[\log R_i^{(d)}\mid\mathcal F_i]-\log(n\mu/\ee)
&\ge\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})+\frac{dq_{i+1}}{M_i}
-\frac{(s_d-m)q_{i+1}}{M_i}\notag\\
&\quad-C_d\left(\frac1i+\frac1{M_i}+\frac{Q_{i+1}^2}{M_i^2}
+\frac{Q_{i+1}}{M_i i}\right).
\end{align}
The sole deficit in the full-shift gain is $(s_d-m)q/M$, supported on $l<s_d$.

\subsection{Summing the lower boundary deficit}
For every $q\ge0$ the largest row probability of the geometric law is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:drowmax}
\frac{1-\ee^{-q/M}}{1-\ee^{-q}}\le\frac{1+q}{M},
\end{equation}
with value $1/M$ at zero. Use $1-\ee^{-q/M}\le q/M$ and $q/(1-\ee^{-q})\le q+1$. Let $B_i=\ind_{\{L_i<s_d\}}$. If $s_d=0$ there is no deficit. Otherwise $k>s_d$ eventually, and conditioning on the entire previous history gives, for $i\ge k+1$,
\[
\Prob_d^*(L_i<s_d\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})
\le s_d(1+q_i)/M_{i-1}.
\]
The event and the denominator are not independent. Instead use the exact monotonicity $M_i\ge M_{i-1}$ \emph{before} taking expectation:
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dboundaryconditional}
\E_d^*\left[\frac{q_{i+1}}{M_i}B_i\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{i-1}\right]
\le s_d q_{i+1}(1+q_i)M_{i-1}^{-2}.
\end{equation}
By \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dinverse}, $q_i\le2T$, and summation of $i^{-2}$, the total for $i\ge k+1$ is $O_d(T^2/k)$. The initial state is at the boundary when $d>1$ and contributes explicitly
\[
(s_d-m_k)q_{k+1}/M_k=s_dq_{k+1}/(k+1)=O_d(T/k).
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dboundarytotal}
\sum_{i=k}^{n-1}\E_d^*\frac{(s_d-m_i)q_{i+1}}{M_i}=O_d(T^2/k).
\end{equation}
This proof requires neither stationarity nor independence at the lower boundary.

\subsection{Finishing the fixed difference lower bound}
On good states $M_i\ge i/4$, the ratio $M_i/S_{i+1}$ lies in $[1/8,2]$, where
$\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})\ge-C(M_i-S_{i+1})^2/i^2$.
On the complementary event use $M_i\ge k+1$ and $|\Psi|\le C+T$. Equations \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dlowprob} and \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dtracking} give
\[
\sum_i\E_d^*\Psi(M_i/S_{i+1})\ge-C_d(T+T^2/k+k)-o(1).
\]
On good states $1/M_i\ge1/S_{i+1}-C|M_i-S_{i+1}|/i^2$. Drop the nonnegative bad-state contribution and retain the exponentially small loss of $q_{i+1}/S_{i+1}$. Cauchy--Schwarz then yields
\[
\sum_i\E_d^*\frac{q_{i+1}}{M_i}
\ge\sum_i\frac{q_{i+1}}{S_{i+1}}
-C_d(T/\sqrt{k}+T^2/k+T)-o(1).
\]
The explicit local errors in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dlowerstep} sum to $O_d(T+T^2/k)$ by \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dinverse}. Bad-state sums contain $\ee^{-ck}$ times a polynomial in $T,k$, with no extra factor $n$. The right Riemann sum is $q_k^2/2+O(T/k)$. Combining with \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dboundarytotal},
\begin{align}\label{wasc:sh:eq:dlowergain}
\sum_i\E_d^*\log R_i^{(d)}
&\ge(n-k)\log(n\mu/\ee)+\frac d2q_k^2\notag\\
&\quad-C_d(k+T+T^2/k+T/\sqrt{k}).
\end{align}
At $k=\lceil AT\rceil$ the error is $O_d(T)$. Insert this in \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:djensen}, then use the sharp calibration and exact normalization from Section~\ref{wasc:sh:sec:cutoff}. This proves
\[
\log a_n^{(d)}\ge\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac d2T^2-O_d(T).
\]
Together with \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:dupper}, it completes Theorem~\ref{wasc:sh:thm:main}.

\section{Bounded error threshold inversion}\label{wasc:sh:sec:inverse}
Let $B_n$ be one of the two positive increasing quantities in Corollary~\ref{wasc:sh:cor:inverse}, with correction coefficient $c$. Define the smooth functions
\[
f(t)=t\log(\mu t/\ee),\qquad G_c(t)=f(t)+\frac c2(\log t)^2.
\]
Stirling and Theorem~\ref{wasc:sh:thm:main} imply
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:inverseinput}
\log B_n=G_c(n)+O(\log n).
\end{equation}
The half-logarithmic Stirling term is included in this error. All constants are compact-uniform in the weighted case.

Write $L=\log Y$ and $x=L/W_0(\mu L/\ee)$. If $u=W_0(\mu L/\ee)$, then $u\ee^u=\mu L/\ee$, so $\mu x/\ee=\ee^u$ and $f(x)=L$ exactly. The leading growth gives $N(Y)\sim x$: evaluate \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:inverseinput} near $(1\pm\varepsilon)x$, where the main-term gap is of order $\varepsilon x\log x$, dominating all log-square and logarithmic errors. Monotonicity then brackets the first crossing, uniformly over any fixed weight interval.

At $N=N(Y)$, the neighboring integers satisfy
\[
\log B_{N-1}<L\le\log B_N.
\]
Using \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:inverseinput} at both indices and
$G_c(N)-G_c(N-1)=O(\log N)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:inversebracket}
G_c(N)-L=O(\log N).
\end{equation}
This uses neither regularity nor monotonicity of the unknown remainder.

Put
\[
y=x-\frac{c(\log x)^2}{2\log(\mu x)}.
\]
Then $y-x=O(\log x)$, $f'(x)=\log(\mu x)$, and $f''(t)=1/t$. Taylor expansion of the known smooth terms alone gives
\[
f(y)=L-\frac c2(\log x)^2+O((\log x)^2/x),\qquad
(\log y)^2-(\log x)^2=O((\log x)^2/x).
\]
Therefore $G_c(y)-L=O((\log x)^2/x)$. Both $N$ and $y$ are asymptotic to $x$, and along their joining segment
\[
G_c'(t)=\log(\mu t)+c\log(t)/t\sim\log x.
\]
The mean-value theorem applied to \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:inversebracket} proves $N-y=O(1)$. Finally
\[
\frac{(\log x)^2}{\log(\mu x)}=\log x-\log\mu+O(1/\log x),
\]
which proves both forms of \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:maininverse}.

\paragraph{What the bounded error means.}
The correction is negative: the log-square enhancement makes the threshold occur earlier than the leading factorial inverse predicts. The integer rounding uncertainty is already included in $O(1)$. The proof supplies an eventually bounded distance, with unspecified constants, and does not supply a limiting constant or a computable finite bracket. A next coefficient of order $\log n$ in the counting logarithm would affect the inverse at constant order.
\begin{remark}[\wtag, 5 October 2026; the endpoint $d=0$]\label{wasc:sh:rem:dzero}

\[
\log a^{(0)}_n=\log(n!)+n\log\mu+O(\log n),\qquad N(Y)=x+O(1).
\]
\emph{Proof.} By the Fishburn asymptotic \eqref{wasc:gr:eq:fishburn} (Hwang--Jin \cite{hwang}), $\log a^{(0)}_n=\log(n!)+n\log\mu+\frac12\log n+\log C_F+O(1/n)$, which is \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:maindifference} at $d=0$. For the inverse, every state has exactly $K+2\ge2$ children for every $d\ge0$ (Part~\ref{wasc:gr:part}, Section~\ref{wasc:gr:sec:fixedd}), so $a^{(0)}_{i+1}\ge2a^{(0)}_i$ for $i\ge1$ and the first crossing is well defined. The proof of Corollary~\ref{wasc:sh:cor:inverse} in this section uses only \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:inverseinput}, which now holds with $c=0$, and this monotonicity; with $c=0$, $G_0=f$, $y=x$, and the mean-value step gives $N-x=O(1)$. \qed

\section{Sharper moment bounds for the number of levels}\label{wasc:sh:sec:moments}
Sample a weak ascent sequence with probability $w^{E(x)}/H_n(w)$, and let $E_n$ count its levels. Put $T=\log n$ and $m_w=(w/2)T^2$.

\begin{corollary}[Quantitative level moments]\label{wasc:sh:cor:moments}
Uniformly for $w$ in a fixed compact $\Wc\subset(0,\infty)$,
\begin{align}
\E_{n,w}E_n&=m_w+O_{\Wc}(T^{3/2}),\label{wasc:sh:eq:mean}\\
\E_{n,w}|E_n-m_w|^p&=O_{\Wc,p}(T^{3p/2})\qquad(p>0\text{ fixed}),\label{wasc:sh:eq:moments}\\
\operatorname{Var}_{n,w}(E_n)&=O_{\Wc}(T^3).\label{wasc:sh:eq:variance}
\end{align}
The same absolute moment bound holds when centered at the actual mean. These are upper bounds, not fluctuation-scale equivalents or a central limit theorem.
\end{corollary}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
This supersedes the mean estimate \eqref{wasc:lv:eq:meanmain} of Part~\ref{wasc:lv:part}, whose error was $O(b_n/\sqrt{\log\log n})$, and its remark that the variance is $o((\log n)^4)$.
\end{writenote}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\delta>0$ and apply the compact-uniform theorem on
$\Wc^*=[w_-\ee^{-\delta},w_+\ee^{\delta}]$. The exact identity
\[
\E_{n,w}\ee^{tE_n}=H_n(w\ee^t)/H_n(w)
\]
gives, for $|t|\le\delta$ and $Z=E_n-m_w$,
\begin{equation}\label{wasc:sh:eq:centermgf}
\log\E_{n,w}\ee^{tZ}
=\frac w2T^2(\ee^t-1-t)+O_{\Wc^*}(T)
\le C_0T+C_1T^2t^2.
\end{equation}
To bound the mean without differentiating that error, use convexity of the exact finite log-MGF. Its derivative at zero lies between its secant slopes at $\pm h$. With $h=T^{-1/2}$, expansion of the explicit leading term gives
$m_w+O_{\Wc}(T^2h+T/h)=m_w+O_{\Wc}(T^{3/2})$, proving \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:mean}.

Chernoff's inequality for both signs of $t$ gives, with $b=2C_1\delta$,
\begin{align}
\Prob_{n,w}(|Z|\ge u)&\le\min\{1,2\exp(C_0T-u^2/(4C_1T^2))\},
&&0\le u\le bT^2,\label{wasc:sh:eq:tailgauss}\\
\Prob_{n,w}(|Z|\ge u)&\le\min\{1,2\exp(C_0T-\delta u/2)\},
&&u\ge bT^2.\label{wasc:sh:eq:taillinear}
\end{align}
For the first estimate take $t=u/(2C_1T^2)$; once it reaches $\delta$, use $t=\delta$. Choose a fixed $B>0$ with $B^2\ge8C_0C_1$, and split
\[
\E|Z|^p=p\int_0^\infty u^{p-1}\Prob(|Z|>u)\dd u
\]
at $BT^{3/2}$ and $bT^2$. The initial interval costs $O(T^{3p/2})$. On the middle interval \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:tailgauss} is at most $2\exp(-u^2/(8C_1T^2))$, whose integral over all positive $u$ is $O_p(T^p)$. On the last interval \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:taillinear} is at most $2\exp(-\delta u/4)$ for large $n$, and its integral is exponentially small in $T^2$ times a polynomial. This proves \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:moments}. For $p=2$, variance is at most $\E(E_n-m_w)^2$. Centering at the actual mean follows from \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:mean} and $|a+b|^p\le C_p(|a|^p+|b|^p)$.
\end{proof}

In particular $E_n/T^2\to w/2$ in every fixed positive absolute moment, and the mean error is improved by the sharp remainder. The variance upper bound does not assert that the variance has order $T^3$. The fixed-tilt log-MGF still has leading term $(w/2)T^2(\ee^t-1)$, but this fact alone does not prove Poisson approximation, mod-Poisson convergence, a central or local limit theorem, or sharp tail prefactors.

\section{Further questions justified by the result}\label{wasc:sh:sec:questions}
The logarithmic remainder isolates several concrete next problems without resolving them.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=4pt]
\item Determine whether the residual
$\log B_n-\log(n!)-n\log\mu-(c/2)\log^2n$ has a coefficient times $\log n$ plus a bounded remainder. The present bounds allow this possibility but do not imply it.
\item Identify an amplitude and a relative-error equivalent, if such a regular expansion exists. This requires sharper prefix matching and more detailed control of the evolving state, beyond an $O(\log n)$ loss.
\item Find effective constants and starting thresholds for a certified finite inverse bracket. Bounded-error asymptotics alone cannot decide an exact integer crossing.
\item For levels, determine the variance scale and establish, if valid, a central or local limit theorem. The moment bounds here are too weak to select that scale.
\item Understand transitions when $w$ tends to zero or $d$ grows with $n$. The constants in the present proof depend on a fixed positive compact weight interval or fixed $d$, so no such transition follows by substitution.
\end{enumerate}
A full transseries would require substantially more information than the logarithmic pressure expansion proved here. The present result supplies a rigorous starting point for these refinements, rather than evidence that any particular one must hold.

\item \emph{Why the coefficients $w/2$ and $d/2$ coincide.} Section~\ref{wasc:sh:sec:results} says the coincidence ``reflects related transfer calculations'' (in the proofs, the shift $l'=l-(d-1)$ contributes $mq/M$ with $m\le d-1$, as the level weight contributes $(w-1)q/M$), and that the families differ ($H_3(2)=12$, $a_3^{(2)}=6$). A structural or combinatorial explanation is not given.
\item \emph{Real $d>0$.} This Part's remark that the effective parameter is $\lceil d\rceil$ is correct: for integers, $y>-d$ if and only if $y\ge1-\lceil d\rceil$, that is, $y>-\lceil d\rceil$ (checked at the write). This uses the strict inequality $x_{i+1}>x_i-d$ in the definition of a $d$-ascent: under $x_{i+1}\ge x_i-d$ the effective parameter would be $\lfloor d\rfloor+1$, which differs from $\lceil d\rceil$ exactly at integers (with $d=1$ it would count $24$ sequences of length~$4$ instead of $a_4=23$). Consequently, for real $d>0$, \eqref{wasc:sh:eq:maindifference} holds with $\lceil d\rceil$ in place of $d$: the coefficient of $(\log n)^2$ is $\frac12\lceil d\rceil$, not $\frac d2$. No question remains there; uniformity in $d$ is item~5 above.
\end{enumerate}
