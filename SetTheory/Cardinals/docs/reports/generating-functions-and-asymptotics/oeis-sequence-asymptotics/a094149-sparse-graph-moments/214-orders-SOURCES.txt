PROOF INPUTS AND SAME-COUNT ANTECEDENTS

Michel Bauer and Olivier Golinelli (2001)
Random incidence matrices: moments of the spectral density
https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0007127v2
https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0007127v2
https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1004879905284
Sections 5.3-5.6 and conclusion inspected. Section 5.3 identifies fixed limiting
adjacency moments with normalized tree walks; Section 5.4 gives the refined
recurrence. Section 5.5 equation (9), S(k,l) <= I(k,l) <= Catalan_l S(k,l),
is the essential credited external theorem used in the complement proof.
Section 5.6 equation (13) gives I(k,l) ~ 2 S(k,l) for fixed l>=2 as k grows.
It does not provide a uniform error across the growing Bell saddle, so that
fixed-l equivalent cannot simply be summed to obtain this report's equivalent.

Report213 (4 October 2026), The full sparse graph moment equivalent
Companion report previously circulated as PDF and reproducibility ZIP.
No public publication URL is asserted. The full equivalent and superpolynomial
complement theorem are inherited from this report. Report214 Appendix B recalls
a complete proof of the needed complement theorem, including the BG input,
so the public package is readable without a copy of the companion report.

A. Khorunzhy and V. Vengerovsky (2000)
On asymptotic solvability of random graph's laplacians
https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0009028
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0009028
Six-page primary text inspected. Its first model is the adjacency matrix with
p/N edge probability, specialized to p=1. Equations (6a,b) give a refined
limiting-even-moment recurrence; equation (7) uses ordered tree walks. Remarks
and equation (9) give rough growth bounds and unbounded support. Its indexing
must be translated rather than copying m_k and m_(2k) interchangeably.

Alexey Spiridonov, Spectra of sparse graphs and matrices, senior thesis
https://alexey.im/papers/sparse-random-graphs-senior-thesis.pdf
Sections 3.3 and 4.2 and bibliography inspected via primary PDF text. Section
3.3 equation (7) gives Bell/Catalan-times-Bell bounds; Conjecture 3.3 gives a
historical Catalan-Stirling upper bound already implied by BG's stronger
coefficientwise theorem. It is not represented as a current unsolved claim.
Related 2006 project, Section 2 inspected:
https://alexey.im/papers/sparse-random-graphs-near-zero-final-project-S06-18.318.pdf
It summarizes the rough bounds for moment determinacy; later work concerns
small positive eigenvalues near zero.

CLOSE RELATED TREE-WALK AND DEFECT METHODS

Eva-Maria Hainzl and Elie de Panafieu (2024)
Tree walks and the spectrum of random graphs
https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08347v1
https://arxiv.org/html/2405.08347v1
https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.AofA.2024.11
Definitions and main theorems inspected. Theorem 3 treats generating functions
at fixed excess half-length minus edge count. Theorem 4 expands fixed moments
as mean degree grows. Neither inspected statement gives the fixed-mean-degree,
growing-moment Bell equivalent or fixed-collision expansion. This comparison
is limited to the inspected preprint/conference statements.

Eva-Maria Hainzl (2025/2026)
Formulas and asymptotics of hypergraph Catalan numbers
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08865v2
https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08865v2
Version 2 dated 23 March 2026. Definitions, Theorems 1 and 2, Proposition 1,
and the proof structure inspected. In this source k is fixed: each edge is
traversed exactly 2k times, and tree size n tends to infinity. Theorem 1 gives
factorial-power asymptotics; Proposition 1 quantifies star-like defects and
includes an inverse factorial defect factor. It is genuine prior dominant-star
and defect-expansion methodology. The objects and quantifiers differ from
unrestricted edge multiplicities summed at fixed total half-length here.

CONDENSATION AND SPECTRAL BACKGROUND

Janson, Jonsson and Stefansson, Random trees with superexponential branching
weights (2011)
https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.2810
https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8113/44/48/485002
Definitions and Theorems 2.3-2.6, Remarks 2.8-2.9 inspected. Theorem 2.4 gives
Z_N ~ exp(lambda)(N-1)! for specified factorial weights, with Poisson defects.
The counted objects are weighted planted planar trees at fixed size.
Janson, Simply generated trees, conditioned Galton-Watson trees, random
allocations and condensation, Probability Surveys 9 (2012)
https://doi.org/10.1214/11-PS188
https://emis.de/ft/46862
Published Examples 10.8-10.9, Remark 18.5, Theorems 18.1/18.6/19.34,
Remark 19.35, Example 19.36 and Section 21.5 inspected. These involve allocation
or weighted-tree normalizations, rather than adjacency closed-walk moments.
Published and arXiv numbering differ. No theorem from these sources is used
as a proof input in Report214.

Hiesmayr and McKenzie, The Spectral Edge of Constant Degree Erdos-Renyi Graphs
https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11007
https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.70011
Primary Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 4.10 framework inspected. These are high-probability
finite-graph eigenvalue expansions on controlled neighborhoods. They do not
by themselves permit an annealed high-moment transfer; no such transfer is used.

Other bounded primary checks covered Khorunzhy math/9905015, Khorunzhiy's
2003 DMTCS paper (doi:10.46298/dmtcs.3346), Khorunzhiy arXiv:1904.01339, and
Semerjian-Cugliandolo arXiv:cond-mat/0202406 Sections 4.4-4.5. Their inspected
regimes concern growing mean degree/norms, degree Bell moments, or physical
single-defect spectral approximations. No assertion of exhaustive nonoverlap
with every theorem or version of these papers is made.
