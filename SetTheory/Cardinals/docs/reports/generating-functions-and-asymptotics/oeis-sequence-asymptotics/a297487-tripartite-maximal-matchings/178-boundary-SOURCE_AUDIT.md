The abstract describes extremal cardinalities and edge domination. It does not by itself establish or exclude enumerative asymptotic overlap.
2. R. Azor, J. Gillis, J. D. Victor, *Combinatorial Applications of Hermite Polynomials*, SIAM Journal on Mathematical Analysis 13 (1982), 879–890. [Publisher](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0513062). Primary abstract and metadata inspected; full text not obtained. This is relevant classical inhomogeneous-pairing theory. The shifted-Hermite identity in the report is proved directly by the classical generating function; it is not presented as a newly discovered special-function identity.
3. R. B. Paris, *Asymptotics of Integrals of Hermite Polynomials*, Applied Mathematical Sciences 4 (2010), 3043–3056. [Primary PDF](https://www.m-hikari.com/ams/ams-2010/ams-61-64-2010/parisAMS61-64-2010.pdf). Section 5 and unequal-index end cases inspected. Formula (5.7) treats a four-equal-Hermite moment, with saddle proportional to n and ordinary 1/n corrections; its positive sum (5.1) differs from the reciprocal-(2j)! boundary sum. The inspected final cases are unshifted. No shifted mixed-index n^(2/3) boundary theorem was located in those sections. The discrete Laplace and Poisson-Jacobi techniques are classical.

Luke's *The Special Functions and Their Approximations*, Vol. I (1969), Chapter 7, is referenced there but was not obtained in full. The Luke comparison gap remains open.

Nearby descriptions concerned matching-support normalization, preorder matching-support, fixed-forbidden-distance involutions, and partition sums of acyclic orientations of complete multipartite graphs. The actual objects differ. Whole-repository searches for exact graph phrases and A297487 found no match; a numeric fragment search found unrelated substrings in large integers. These were not treated as absence certificates.

A fresh bounded search of available earlier reports used graph spellings, prefix fragments, boundary maximal matchings, shifted Hermite phrases, and title searches for tripartite, maximal matchings, and Hermite. Content result sets were capped at 20 or 30 results; narrow title queries reached terminal result sets. Relevant candidate sections were actually read beyond snippets. Report176 was the direct related predecessor. Other inspected candidates concerned derivative-coefficient Hermite limits, Bell-polynomial identities, Riccati factorial transseries, interaction-combinator boundary matchings, and preorder matching-support polynomials. None of the inspected candidate sections was the shifted tripartite boundary theorem.

This comparison was deliberately bounded. Ranked or fuzzy content search, unexhausted broad-search pages, incomplete extraction, and archives not redundantly materialized prevent an exhaustive corpus absence conclusion. Private search receipts and unrelated source material are not included in the deliverable.
