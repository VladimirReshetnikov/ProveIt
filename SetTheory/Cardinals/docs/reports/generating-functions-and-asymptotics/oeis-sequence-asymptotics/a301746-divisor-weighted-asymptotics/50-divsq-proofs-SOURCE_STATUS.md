## Closest checked primary literature

1. Walter Bridges, Benjamin Brindle, Kathrin Bringmann, Johann Franke, *Asymptotic expansions for partitions generated by infinite products*, Mathematische Annalen 390 (2024), 2593–2632. DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-024-02807-x ; full author preprint https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11864

   The checked general theorem treats positive real simple poles (and the allowed zero-pole exception) rather than a fourth-order pole at 1. The paper is important prior art for arbitrary-order infinite-product coefficient asymptotics, not a sequence-specific resolution that can be directly applied without changing hypotheses. It would be misleading to imply that arbitrary-order saddle expansions themselves are new.

2. Bruce C. Berndt, Nicolas Robles, Alexandru Zaharescu, Dirk Zeindler, *Partitions with multiplicities associated with divisor functions*, Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications 533 (2024), article 127987. DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2023.127987 ; primary publisher page https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022247X23009903 ; author's institutional record https://research.ibm.com/publications/partitions-with-multiplicities-associated-with-divisor-functions

   The publisher's introduction defines the weight sigma_{k1,k2}(n)=Σ_{d1*d2=n}d1^{k1}d2^{k2}, with Dirichlet series ζ(s−k1)ζ(s−k2), and uses unrestricted colored-partition products. Thus its coincident-pole case has a double pole; the weight at k1=k2=0 is d(n), not d(n)^2. It is very relevant prior art for logarithmic saddle reversion and divisor-weighted minor-arc methods. The publisher explicitly says that a complete asymptotic expansion is available by its method. The indexed author manuscript also uses Lambert W. It does not directly state A301746's fourth-order-pole exclusion product.

3. Lida Ahmadi, Ricardo Gómez-Aíza, Mark Daniel Ward, *A Unified Treatment of Families of Partition Functions*, La Matematica 3 (2024), 1257–1296. DOI and full primary publisher text https://doi.org/10.1007/s44007-024-00134-w

   This is particularly close prior art that a prospective article must cite. Its two families allow plus and minus products over independent multiplicative indices, with Mellin kernels Γ(s)ζ(s−1)^iζ(s+1)^{j+1}ζ(s)^k and, for the exclusion case, an extra factor 1−2^{-s}. Therefore it already handles high-order poles at 1 for tau_k(n) weights and proves several OEIS conjectures with Mellin/residue/saddle methods. Its (i,j,k)=(0,0,4) exclusion weight is d_4(n), not d(n)^2: the extra denominator ζ(2s) in the present problem is absent from that family. The resulting different analytic continuation matters for explicit deeper arithmetic sectors, but does not make the basic logarithmic-pole saddle method novel.

## Search scope and defensible conclusion

Queries included the exact OEIS ID; the generating function and squared-divisor wording; partitions with divisor-function multiplicities; high-order poles in Meinardus-type theorems; and the three primary papers above. No sequence-specific proof, explicit two-correction inverse, or all-order exact-cumulant formula for A301746 was located in those sources.

This does NOT establish that no prior result exists. The strong defensible statement is: the inspected OEIS entry has a conjectural leading logarithmic equivalent, and the supplied proof notes give a self-contained resolution plus stronger rigorously specified expansions, using well-established analytic/probabilistic methods. Any external novelty claim would require another targeted search using the exact final theorem and a review of broader general weighted-partition/local-limit theorems.

## Article-readiness recommendation

Mathematically: ready for independent review and article drafting after checking PROOF_NOTES.md and numerical diagnostics. The most informative presentation distinguishes (a) explicit every-fixed-log-order formulas from (b) sharper arbitrary-order relative expansions retaining exact saddle data. Its purpose can be to close the OEIS gap and give usable inversion formulas without asserting literature-wide novelty.

Do not advertise a full explicit transseries. Possible ζ-zero contributions to the Mellin continuation have not been isolated or bounded as an exponentially improved sector expansion.
