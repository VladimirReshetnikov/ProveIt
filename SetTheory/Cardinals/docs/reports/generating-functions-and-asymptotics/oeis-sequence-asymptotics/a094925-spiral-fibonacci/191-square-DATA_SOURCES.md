# Data attribution and bounded source scope

## Included fixture

`data/oeis_fixtures.json` contains only the 64 displayed integers of OEIS
A078510, indices 0 through 63, and a few attribution fields. They were
transcribed from the supplied official record, revision 21 dated
11 September 2025, and reviewed on 4 October 2026:

- https://oeis.org/A078510
- https://github.com/oeis/oeisdata/blob/main/seq/A078/A078510.seq

The record credits Neil Fernandez (5 January 2003) for the sequence. The
recurrence a(n)=a(n-1)+a(A265409(n)), n>1, is credited to Antti Karttunen
(13 December 2015). The predecessor's quarter-square plateau rule is prior:
https://oeis.org/A265409 . The 64th included term is a(63)=1056.
No amplitude digits, full b-file, raw record, third-party program, diagram,
or copied explanatory article text is included.

The fixture is fixed by a SHA-256 checksum in the mandatory checker.
Attribution, schema, types, index range, checksum, and computed values are
checked separately. Changing a fixture is not a supported way to update or
validate this release.

## Model sources and established methods

Neil Fernandez, *Spiro-Fibonacci Sequences*, last modified 11 January 2003:
https://borve.org/primeness/spirofib.html . The inspected article describes the
square nearest-two-earlier-cell model, examples, and seed superposition. The
geometric construction, seed model, and repeated corner use are prior work.

Kevin Ryde, *Math::NumSeq::SpiroFibonacci*, version 75:
https://fastapi.metacpan.org/v1/source/KRYDE/Math-NumSeq-75/lib/Math/NumSeq/SpiroFibonacci.pm .
Its complete source and documentation were inspected as text, not executed.
They document queue generation and three uses of the inward value around a
corner. No source from that implementation is included here.

Petr Kundrat, *Asymptotic properties of the discretized pantograph equation*
(2005):
https://www.cs.ubbcluj.ro/journal/studia-mathematica/archive/2005-1/kundrat.pdf .
Its comparison theorem assumes positive damping absent from this recurrence.
His *On the asymptotics of the difference equation with a proportional delay*
(2006):
https://www.opuscula.agh.edu.pl/vol26/3/art/opuscula_math_2638.pdf .
The inspected theorem concerns a fixed proportional delay and a contractive
nondelayed coefficient, also different from the present setting.

John A. D. Appleby and Evelyn Buckwar, *A Constructive Comparison Technique
for Determining the Asymptotic Behaviour of Linear Functional Differential
Equations with Unbounded Delay*, DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s12591-010-0057-z . The publisher full text was
unavailable. After the initial abstract/bibliography review, selected theorem
statements on printed pages 278 and 282 were independently inspected in a
publicly readable OCR reproduction, whose header matches the DOI/title/authors:
https://www.scribd.com/document/1006861920/Appleby-2010 . OCR transcription
limitations apply. Theorems 4, 8, and 9 concern the continuous positive-damping
model x'(t)=-a x(t)+b x(t-tau(t)); Theorem 4 assumes a>b>0, and Theorem 8
and the growth case of Theorem 9 assume b>a>0. Their inspected conclusions
are logarithmic-rate limits (Theorem 8 normalized by integral 1/sigma and
Theorem 9 by integral 1/tau), not the present zero-damping discrete full
equivalent. This is a selected-statement comparison, not a claim that every
result in the article or all variable-delay theory has been excluded.
No third-party full text or OCR reproduction is redistributed.

The public ProveIt source tree was inspected at revision
9ba11eaf7c1b6a27ccfc0bab63e22f97768cb704:
https://github.com/VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt/tree/9ba11eaf7c1b6a27ccfc0bab63e22f97768cb704 .
Its Lambert-W guide covers constant-delay characteristic spectra; its
transseries/inversion work supplies general implicit inversion, residual-to-root
error transport, and staircase cautions. Those are established methods. The
present recurrence is square-spiral and has a qualitatively different growth
scale from the hexagonal rule treated in Report190.

The bounded review did not locate the full square-spiral equivalent stated in
Report191 in the inspected sources. This is not a worldwide priority claim,
an exhaustive literature search, or clearance of uninspected sources. The
sequence-specific normalization and its proof are distinguished from the
classical methods used to obtain them.

## Code and publication scope

The Python modules are authored implementations. The offline build,
reproduction, manifest, and guard infrastructure adapts the preceding authored
report's package. Geometry is independently generated by unit steps; inverse
coefficients are independently checked by sparse factorial sums and rational
polynomial division. No downloaded third-party program is executed or
redistributed. Mandatory checks use integers and `fractions.Fraction` only.
The optional mpmath diagnostic is lazy-loaded in its own script and is never
imported or run by mandatory verification or the release builder.
