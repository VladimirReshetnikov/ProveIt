\begin{abstract}
Let $a_n^{(b)}$ count ascent sequences of length $n$ in which each value occurs at most $b$ times (the array OEIS A294220; cap three is A317784, cap two A202058). This report prints three manuscripts as three Parts. Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} proves, for every fixed $b\ge3$, that $(a_n^{(b)}/n!)^{1/n}\to\mu_b=1/T_b$ with $T_b=\int_0^\infty\log E_b(v)/(E_b(v)-1)\,dv$ and $E_b$ the truncated exponential, with a coarse error $O_b(n/\log n)$ in $\log a_n^{(b)}$, a controlled Lambert-$W$ inverse in the length, the large-cap expansion $T_b=\pi^2/6+(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1)+O(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b)$ with explicit constants, and a rounding-safe inverse in the cap with counterexamples to naive rounding; the cap-two input $T_2=3\pi^2/8$ is taken from the sibling report on A202058. Part~\ref{amc:part:sec} writes $T_b-\pi^2/6$ exactly as a convergent sum of positive Taylor sectors $A_{r,b}$, proves that the remainder after any fixed number $K$ of sectors is asymptotic to sector $K+1$, and expands every fixed sector to all algebraic orders in $1/b$; in particular the remainder of Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} is $\sim\frac8{9\sqrt\pi}\sqrt b\,(4/9)^b$. Part~\ref{amc:part:bm}, which writes the cap as $r$, proves for every fixed $r\ge2$ both the root limit and the consecutive-ratio limit $a^{(r)}_{n+1}/((n+1)a^{(r)}_n)\to\mu_r$, exponentially concentrated multiplicity, distinct-value and ascent proportions with a full large-deviation principle, and an $O_r(n^{r+2})$ counting algorithm; it re-derives the large-cap analysis in the variable $r+1$ and adds a continuous cap and exact-sector inverse envelopes. No prefactor or all-orders expansion in $n$, no joint regime $b=b(n)$, and no uniformity in a growing number of sectors is claimed. The report is unrefereed, and none of it is formalized.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{What the report proves}
\phantomsection\label{amc:guide:results}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}} (source 47). Lemma~\ref{amc:lem:swap} and Section~\ref{amc:sec:compaction}: an exact compaction by a suffix involution, for every cap. Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed}: for every fixed $b\ge3$ the exponential generating function has radius $T_b$, and $\log a_n^{(b)}=\log n!-n\log T_b+O_b(n/\log n)$, by uniform positive-operator barriers (Sections~\ref{amc:sec:barrier}--\ref{amc:sec:comparison}) and a coefficient extraction at every length (Section~\ref{amc:sec:coefficients}). Section~\ref{amc:sec:lengthinverse}: the length inverse $N_b(X)=n_0+O_b(L/(\log L)^3)$, $n_0=L/W_0(L/(eT_b))$. Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:cap} and Section~\ref{amc:sec:largecap}: monotone convergence $T_b\downarrow\pi^2/6$, the exact first variation $(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1)$ and the explicit bound $0\le T_b-\pi^2/6-(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1)\le160b^{5/2}(4/9)^b$ for every $b\ge3$. Section~\ref{amc:sec:capinverse}: the $W_{-1}$ cap inverse with integer brackets \eqref{amc:eq:rounding}, \eqref{amc:eq:explicitround}, and counterexamples to rounding by $\lceil b_0\rceil$.
\item \textbf{Part~\ref{amc:part:sec}} (source 52). Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact}: $T_b-\pi^2/6=\sum_{r\ge1}A_{r,b}$ with strictly positive sectors, for every $b\ge2$; for fixed $K$ the remainder $\RR_{K,b}$ is positive and $\RR_{K,b}\sim A_{K+1,b}$. Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:algebraic}: $A_{r,b}=C_rb^{(3-r)/2}\beta_r^b(\sum_jc_{r,j}b^{-j}+\cdots)$ to every fixed order, with $\beta_r=(r/(r+1))^r$, rational $c_{r,j}$, explicit $c_{r,1}$, three corrections for $r=2,3$, and $A_{1,b}=(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1)$ exactly. Corollary~\ref{amc:sec:cor:reciprocal}: the same for $6/\pi^2-\mu_b$.
\item \textbf{Part~\ref{amc:part:bm}} (bundle Report~99; cap written $r$). Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:growth}: for every fixed $r\ge2$, $(a^{(r)}_n/n!)^{1/n}\to\mu_r$ and $a^{(r)}_{n+1}/((n+1)a^{(r)}_n)\to\mu_r$; the ratio limit is new for $r\ge3$. Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:endpoints}: the proportions of values used $k$ times, of distinct values and of ascents converge, with exponentially small deviations; Theorems~\ref{amc:bm:thm:pressure} and~\ref{amc:bm:thm:LDP}: the weighted growth formula $\lim N^{-1}\log(H_N(\bu)/N!)=-\log\chi(\bu)$ and a full large-deviation principle for the endpoint. Proposition~\ref{amc:bm:prop:recurrence} and Corollary~\ref{amc:bm:cor:algorithm}: a histogram-preserving sorted recurrence and $O_r(N^{r+2})$ enumeration. Corollary~\ref{amc:bm:cor:inverse}: a length inverse (weaker than Section~\ref{amc:sec:lengthinverse}). Section~\ref{amc:bm:sec:largecap}: the large-cap analysis again, in $R=r+1$, with a continuous cap, exact-sector envelopes $R_K$, their displacements and a two-candidate cap selection (Corollary~\ref{amc:bm:cap:two-candidates}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{How the Parts depend on one another}
\phantomsection\label{amc:guide:dependencies}
Part~\ref{amc:part:sec} is an analysis of the integral $T_b$ alone; its only combinatorial input is Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}'s identification $\mu_b=1/T_b$ (Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed}), used in Corollary~\ref{amc:sec:cor:reciprocal}. Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}'s Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:cap} is the case $K=1$ of Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact} with the first sector \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r1}, except that Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} also proves explicit constants, valid for every $b\ge3$, and Part~\ref{amc:part:sec} does not. Neither Part strengthens the other's results in the length $n$, and Part~\ref{amc:part:sec} proves no new inverse. Part~\ref{amc:part:bm} is self-contained and uses neither earlier Part. It re-proves the root limit of Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed} (weaker error), the compaction of Lemma~\ref{amc:lem:swap}, the monotonicity in Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:cap}, a weaker length inverse, and, in the variable $R=b+1$, Section~\ref{amc:sec:largecap} and Theorems~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact} and~\ref{amc:sec:thm:algebraic}; it strengthens Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} in the length $n$ by the ratio limit and the endpoint laws.

\begin{partsource}

\begin{partabstract}
Let $a_n^{(b)}$ count ascent sequences of length $n$ in which each value occurs at most $b$ times. For every fixed $b\ge3$ we prove the exact factorial root constant
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{a_n^{(b)}}{n!}\right)^{1/n}
 =\left[\int_0^\infty\frac{\log E_b(v)}{E_b(v)-1}\,dv\right]^{-1},
 \qquad E_b(v)=\sum_{j=0}^b\frac{v^j}{j!}.
\]
The separate cap-two proof gives the same formula. We establish exact compaction by a suffix involution, uniform positive-operator barriers, and a coefficient argument for every $n$. A non-optimal logarithmic error supports a controlled inverse for the required length. We also prove a large-cap expansion of the constant, including an exact zeta first variation and an exponentially smaller remainder, and obtain a rounding-safe cap inverse. This report does not determine fixed-cap prefactors, ratio limits, or all-orders coefficient expansions. The limits in length and cap are kept separate.
\end{partabstract}

\section{Results and interpretation}\label{amc:sec:intro}
An ascent sequence is a word $w_1\cdots w_n$ of nonnegative integers with $w_1=0$ and
\[
 0\le w_i\le1+\asc(w_1\cdots w_{i-1})\quad(i\ge2),\qquad
 \asc(w)=\#\{i:w_i<w_{i+1}\}.
\]
Write $a_0^{(b)}=1$ and let $a_n^{(b)}$ count the words with every label used at most $b$ times. Equivalently, these words avoid the constant pattern of length $b+1$. This is the array in OEIS A294220; cap two is A202058 and cap three is A317784 \cite{array,cap3}.

\begin{theorem}\label{amc:thm:fixed}
For every fixed integer $b\ge3$, set
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:Tb}
 T_b=\int_0^\infty\frac{\log E_b(v)}{E_b(v)-1}\,dv,
 \qquad \mu_b=T_b^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The integrand is interpreted as $1$ at zero. The exponential generating function
$A_b(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n^{(b)}t^n/n!$ has radius $T_b$, and
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:main}
 \log a_n^{(b)}=\log(n!)-n\log T_b+O_b\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently $(a_n^{(b)}/n!)^{1/n}\to\mu_b$ and
\[
 a_n^{(b)}=n!\,\mu_b^n\exp\!\left(O_b\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)\right).
\]
\end{theorem}
The subscript $b$ permits constants and starting indices to depend on the fixed cap. The logarithmic error is deliberately coarse; it is not an estimate of the true subexponential scale. In particular, the theorem does not imply $a_n^{(b)}\sim C_bn!\mu_b^n$ or $a_{n+1}^{(b)}/((n+1)a_n^{(b)})\to\mu_b$.
\begin{writenotedated}{5 October 2026}
The ratio limit $a_{n+1}^{(b)}/((n+1)a_n^{(b)})\to\mu_b$ is proved for every fixed $b\ge2$ in Part~\ref{amc:part:bm}, Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:growth}, without a rate; $a_n^{(b)}\sim C_bn!\mu_b^n$ remains open.
\end{writenotedated}

The separately audited cap-two report \cite{cap2report} proves $T_2=3\pi^2/8$ and a stronger logarithmic error $O(n^{9/13}(\log n)^3)$, so \eqref{amc:eq:main} also holds for $b=2$. Its analytic proof is not repeated here. For $b=1$, the unique word at each length is strictly increasing, $T_1=\infty$, and the normalized root is zero.

\section{Exact compaction and the counting operator}\label{amc:sec:compaction}
For any ascent-sequence prefix, $\max(w)\le\asc(w)$. Indeed a new maximum creates an ascent, whereas a nonascent cannot raise the maximum. Thus the top admissible label $1+\asc(w)$ is unused and strictly larger than the last value.

Delete labels whose budgets have been exhausted. The remaining active labels, in numerical order, have positive budgets $\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{m-1}\in\{1,\ldots,b\}$. Let $k$ be the number of active labels at or below the actual last value. This can be a virtual boundary when that value has just been deleted. Choosing active rank $i$ is an ascent exactly when $i\ge k$. Decrease $\beta_i$ by one; remove it and set $k'=i$ if it reaches zero, and otherwise set $k'=i+1$. An ascent creates precisely one additional top label of budget $b$.

\begin{lemma}[Adjacent-budget interchange]\label{amc:lem:swap}
Suppose $x<y$ are adjacent active labels with both at or below the last value. Exchanging their positive remaining budgets, while retaining the last boundary, preserves the number of legal suffixes of every length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a suffix, reverse each maximal contiguous block consisting only of $x,y$, and then interchange $x$ and $y$ throughout that block. This is an involution that exchanges their total usages. Every intermediate usage is at most the total, so the exchanged quotas hold at every stage.

Inside a binary block, a comparison $p<q$ becomes the same comparison between the complemented pair in reverse order. Its total number of ascents is preserved. No possible outside label lies strictly between $x$ and $y$: the two labels were adjacent among the initially active labels, exhausted labels never return, and future labels are all above the initial alphabet. Thus boundary comparisons with outside letters are unchanged. Entry into an initial binary block is nonascending both before and after the map, since the initial last value is at least $y$.

Ascent totals therefore agree at each block end and before every unchanged outside letter. The timing inside a block can change, but both block labels were already admissible initially, and the admissible upper bound never decreases. This proves legality as well as invertibility.
\end{proof}
The lemma allows permutations below the boundary, not arbitrary sorting above it. Start with a canonical state in which budgets increase with rank. After choosing budget $r>1$, move its decremented entry left to the beginning of the old budget-$r$ group. Every swap lies among the first $i+1$ entries, hence below the new boundary $i+1$, which stays fixed. Exhaustion leaves an already sorted tuple and boundary $i$. A new top label is appended. This gives an exact reduction at every step. The last boundary does not follow the moved budget.

Write $N_0=u$ for the number of unused labels and $N_j=s_j$ for those used $j$ times, $1\le j<b$. Groups occur in increasing rank order as
\[
 N_{b-1},N_{b-2},\ldots,N_1,N_0,\qquad
 m=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}N_j,\quad u\ge1,\quad0\le k<m.
\]
If rank $i$ belongs to group $j$, put $\alpha=\one_{i\ge k}$. Its child is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:transition}
 N'=N-e_j+\one_{j+1<b}e_{j+1}+\alpha e_0,
 \qquad
 k'=\begin{cases}i,&j=b-1,\\i+1,&j<b-1.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
These transitions preserve $u\ge1$ and $k<m$: a choice of the final unused top label is always ascending and creates another. They satisfy $m'\le m+1$.
Define the positive operator and its series by
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:operator}
 (\TT f)(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(x_i),\qquad
 F(t,x)=\sum_{r\ge0}\TT^r\one(x)\frac{t^r}{r!}.
\end{equation}
Let $x_1$ have $s_1=u=k=1$ and all other $s_j=0$. Then
\[
 \TT^r\one(x_1)=a_{r+1}^{(b)},\qquad F(t,x_1)=A_b'(t).
\]
All series identities below allow $+\infty$ and use nonnegative sums when necessary.

\section{Characteristic curve and a uniform barrier}\label{amc:sec:barrier}
For this section fix $b\ge3$ and write $\theta=(b-1)/b>1/2$. Parameterize time by $v\ge0$:
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:characteristic}
 q=\log E_b(v),\quad p_j=\log E_{b-j}(v)\ (1\le j<b),\quad
 p_0=q,\ p_b=0,\quad
 t(v)=\int_0^v\frac{\log E_b(z)}{E_b(z)-1}\,dz.
\end{equation}
Its endpoint is $T_b<\infty$: the integrand is $O_b(\log v/v^b)$ at infinity. A dot denotes a time derivative. Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:ODE}
 \dot q=e^{p_1}\frac{1-e^{-q}}q,
 \qquad \dot p_j=\frac{e^q-1}{q}e^{p_{j+1}-p_j}.
\end{equation}
At zero the removable values are all $1$, so every $p_j,q$ is strictly increasing. Polynomial asymptotics give
\begin{align}
 p_j&=\frac{b-j}{b}q+c_j+O_b(e^{-q/b}),\label{amc:eq:poly}\\
 e^{p_1}&\sim c_b e^{\theta q},\qquad
 \dot q\sim c_b e^{\theta q}/q,\qquad
 c_b=\frac{(b!)^{\theta}}{(b-1)!}.
\end{align}
Fixed-order differentiated estimates follow from the underlying rational functions of $v$, rather than differentiation of an arbitrary error term.

Define
\[
 \rho_0=1,\quad\rho_b=0,\quad
 \rho_j=\frac{\dot p_j}{\dot q}
 =\frac{E_b(v)E_{b-j-1}(v)}{E_{b-1}(v)E_{b-j}(v)}\quad(1\le j<b).
\]
They satisfy $1=\rho_0\ge\rho_1\ge\cdots\ge\rho_{b-1}>0$. To see this, put $d_r=v^r/r!$ and note
\[
 E_r^2-E_{r-1}E_{r+1}
 =d_r\left(E_r-\frac{v}{r+1}E_{r-1}\right)\ge0;
\]
the bracket has positive coefficients. Also $\rho_j\to(b-j)/b$. Positivity, continuity, and rational asymptotics yield fixed-cap constants $c>0,K<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:weights}
 c\le\rho_j\le1,\qquad |(\rho_j)_q|\le K\quad(0\le j<b, q\ge0).
\end{equation}

For a state $x$, let
\[
 D=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}\rho_jN_j,\quad
 h(y)=\int_0^y\rho_{\mathrm{group}(z)}\,dz,\quad
 r(y)=h(y)/D,\quad r=r(k),
\]
where group weights are constant on unit rank intervals. Put
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:psi}
 \psi(t,x)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{b-1}p_js_j+qu-qr\right).
\end{equation}
Here $D\ge u\ge1$, $cm\le D\le m$, $0\le r\le1$, and $\psi(0,x)=1$.

\subsection{Exact child rank and its bounds}
If rank $i$ belongs to group $j$, write $H=h(i)$ and $d_j=\rho_j-\rho_{j+1}\in[0,1]$. Every compaction swap is below the new boundary, so its weight sum is unchanged. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:rank}
 D_i=D-d_j+\alpha,\qquad
 h_{x_i}(k_i)=H+\rho_{j+1},\qquad
 r_i=\frac{H+\rho_{j+1}}{D-d_j+\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This includes saturation through $\rho_b=0$. Since $D_i\ge1$ and $D_i\ge D-1$, we have $D_i\ge D/2$ and
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:rankbound}
 |r_i-r(i)|\le\frac4D.
\end{equation}
For an ascent, $i\ge k$ and $D_i\le D+1$, whence the crucial one-sided bound
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:ascentbound}
 r(k)-r_i\le\frac HD-\frac H{D+1}\le\frac1{D+1}\le\frac12.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Frozen integral and residual}
For real $y$ in group $j$ set
\[
 g(y)=\exp\left(p_{j+1}-p_j+q\one_{y\ge k}-q(r(y)-r(k))\right).
\]
The exact transition ratio $\psi(t,x_i)/\psi(t,x)$ replaces $r(i)$ by $r_i$ in $g(i)$. The characteristic equations imply
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:frozen}
 \int_0^m g(y)\,dy=\dot qD=\sum_{j=1}^{b-1}\dot p_js_j+\dot q u=:\lambda.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $\phi(y)=e^{-qh(y)/D}$ one has
$\phi_y=-(q\rho_j/D)\phi$ and
$e^{p_{j+1}-p_j}=q\dot q\rho_j/(e^q-1)$.
Using $\phi(0)=1,\phi(m)=e^{-q}$, the integrals before division by $\phi(k)$ are
\[
 \frac{\dot qD}{e^q-1}(1-\phi(k)),\qquad
 \frac{\dot qD e^q}{e^q-1}(\phi(k)-e^{-q}),
\]
whose sum is $\dot qD\phi(k)$. Continuity handles $q=0$.

By \eqref{amc:eq:poly}, every frozen ratio is at most $C_be^{\theta q}$. On each of at most $b+1$ intervals cut at group boundaries and $k$, $g$ decreases. All endpoints are integers; hence the left-sum error is bounded by the sum of left endpoint values:
\[
 \left|\sum_i g(i)-\lambda\right|\le C_be^{\theta q}.
\]
Exact descending ratios obey the same bound; exact ascending ratios are at most $C_be^{(\theta+1/2)q}$ by \eqref{amc:eq:ascentbound}. Apply
$|e^z-e^w|\le|z-w|\max(e^z,e^w)$, \eqref{amc:eq:rankbound}, and $m/D\le1/c$ to obtain
\[
 \left|\frac{\TT\psi}{\psi}-\sum_i g(i)\right|
 \le C_b q e^{(\theta+1/2)q}.
\]
Finally $|h_q|,|D_q|\le Km$ imply $|r_q|\le2K/c$. Thus
\[
 \frac{\partial_t\psi}{\psi}=\lambda-\dot q(r+qr_q),
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{\partial_t\psi}{\psi}-\lambda\right|
 \le C_b(1+q)e^{\theta q}.
\]
Together these give the state-uniform estimate
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:residual}
 \left|\frac{\partial_t\psi}{\psi}-\frac{\TT\psi}{\psi}\right|
 \le C_b(1+q)e^{(\theta+1/2)q}=:C(q).
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:Err}
 \Err(q)=\int_0^q\frac{C(z)}{\dot q(z)}\,dz
 =O_b((1+q)^3e^{q/2}),\qquad g_\pm=e^{\pm\Err}\psi.
\end{equation}
Then $\partial_tg_-\le\TT g_-$ and $\partial_tg_+\ge\TT g_+$, and both start at $1$. Since $\theta>1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:smaller}
 \Err(q)=o(e^{p_1(q)}).
\end{equation}
This strict inequality explains why this particular proof excludes $b=2$.

\section{Comparison on the infinite state space}\label{amc:sec:comparison}
Let $X$ be the continuous-time chain with each rank-choice edge at rate $1$. Its generator is $L=\TT-mI$ and its total rate is $m$. Because $m$ grows by at most one per jump, comparison with a linear pure-birth process proves nonexplosion. Expanding by finite jump paths cancels survival factors against the potential factor and gives the extended-real identity
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:FK}
 F(t,x)=\EE_x\exp\left(\int_0^t m(X_s)\,ds\right).
\end{equation}
Let $\tau_M$ be the first exit from $m\le M$ and $Y_s=\exp(\int_0^s m(X_r)\,dr)$. The stopped region is finite. Applying stopped Dynkin comparison to $Y_sg_+(t-s,X_s)$ and discarding its nonnegative exit term gives $F(t,x)\le g_+(t,x)$ upon letting $M\to\infty$.

For the lower bound fix $\delta>0$ with $t+\delta<T_b$. Strict increase of all $p_j,q$ gives an $\eta>0$, uniform for $0\le v\le t$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:exitratio}
 g_-(v,y)\le C e^{-\eta m(y)}g_+(v+\delta,y).
\end{equation}
Indeed the population terms each decrease by at least $\eta$ in the log ratio; its rank term is bounded above by $q(t+\delta)$, and the $\Err$ terms have favorable signs. The stopped subsolution inequality is
\[
 g_-(t,x)\le\EE_x[Y_t;\tau_M>t]
 +\EE_x[Y_{\tau_M}g_-(t-\tau_M,X_{\tau_M});\tau_M\le t].
\]
On exit $m(X_{\tau_M})=M+1$. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:exitratio} and the stopped supersolution inequality at horizon $t+\delta$ bound the second term by
$Ce^{-\eta(M+1)}g_+(t+\delta,x)\to0$. Nonexplosion handles the first term. We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:comparison}
 e^{-\Err(q)}\psi(t,x)\le F(t,x)\le e^{\Err(q)}\psi(t,x),
 \qquad0\le t<T_b.
\end{equation}
No uniqueness assertion for an infinite differential system is required.

\section{The radius and the coefficient limit}\label{amc:sec:coefficients}
Let $x_N$ have $s_1=N,u=1,k=N$ and all other populations zero; the increasing prefix of length $N$ reaches it. Its rank formula yields
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:growing}
 \log\psi(t,x_N)=Np_1(q)+\frac q{\rho_1N+1},\qquad
 \log F(t,x_N)=Np_1(q)+O_b(\Err(q)+q),
\end{equation}
with error independent of $N$. In particular $F(t,x_N)\ge e^{Np_1-\Err}$.
The positive semigroup identity and one increasing-prefix path at every length give, for $h>0$,
\begin{align*}
 F(t+h,x_1)&=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{h^r}{r!}(\TT^rF(t,\cdot))(x_1)\\
 &\ge\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{h^r}{r!}F(t,x_{r+1})
 \ge\exp(p_1-\Err+h e^{p_1}).
\end{align*}
As $t\uparrow T_b$, \eqref{amc:eq:smaller} makes the last expression diverge. Monotonicity forces $F(T_b+h,x_1)=\infty$. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:comparison} gives finiteness below $T_b$. Thus $F(t,x_1)$ and $A_b$ have exact radius $T_b$, proving the normalized-root limsup. The next argument is needed for the full limit.

\subsection{A concentration estimate from barrier values}
Put $p=p_1$, $d=(\log t)_q$ and $M(q)=p_q/d=t\dot p$. Direct characteristic asymptotics give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:M}
 p_q\to\theta,\quad p_{qq}\to0,\quad
 d\sim\frac{qe^{-\theta q}}{c_bT_b},\quad
 d'/d\to-\theta,\quad
 M(q)\sim\frac{\theta c_bT_b e^{\theta q}}q.
\end{equation}
These limits are uniform after bounded shifts in $q$. For $c_j=\TT^j\one(x_N)$ define
\[
 \PP(J=j)=\frac{c_jt(q)^j}{j!F(t(q),x_N)},\qquad K=NM(q).
\]
The deterministic center $K$ need not be the exact mean. We use values of \eqref{amc:eq:growing}, never derivatives of its error.
Set
\[
 H_q(v)=p(q+v)-p(q)-M(q)(\log t(q+v)-\log t(q)).
\]
For $|v|\le1$, $H_q(0)=H_q'(0)=0$ and
\[
 H_q''(v)\to\theta^2e^{-\theta v},\qquad
 M(q)d(q+v)\to\theta e^{-\theta v}
\]
uniformly. Consequently there are fixed-cap constants $B,c>0$ with
\[
 |H_q(v)|\le Bv^2,\qquad
 M(q)|\log t(q+v)-\log t(q)|\ge c|v|.
\]
The exponential Markov bounds at $q\pm\gamma\varepsilon$, with fixed small $\gamma>0$, now give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:chernoff}
 \PP(|J-K|>\varepsilon K)
 \le2\exp\{-c_b' N\varepsilon^2+2\Err(q+1)+O_b(q+1)\}
\end{equation}
for sufficiently large $q$ and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Explicitly the main exponent is at most
$N(B\gamma^2\varepsilon^2-c\gamma\varepsilon^2)$; choosing $\gamma$ small makes it negative. Uniformity permits shrinking $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{Extraction for each sufficiently large length}
For every sufficiently large integer $n$, choose
\[
 q=\tfrac12\log n,\qquad\varepsilon=(\log n)^{-2},\qquad
 N=\left\lfloor\frac{(1-4\varepsilon)n}{M(q)}\right\rfloor.
\]
Then
\begin{align*}
 N&\asymp_b n^{1-\theta/2}\log n,&
 N\varepsilon^2&\asymp_b\frac{n^{1-\theta/2}}{(\log n)^3},\\
 K&=(1-4\varepsilon)n+O_b(n^{\theta/2}/\log n),&
 \Err(q+1)&=O_b(n^{1/4}(\log n)^3).
\end{align*}
Since $1/2<\theta<1$, $N\varepsilon^2$ dominates the error and both $N$ and the floor loss in $K$ are $o(\varepsilon n)$. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:chernoff} puts probability at least $1/2$ in $|J-K|\le\varepsilon K$. Some integer $j$ there satisfies
\[
 \frac{c_jt(q)^j}{j!}\ge\frac{F(t(q),x_N)}{4\varepsilon K+6}.
\]
For large $n$ it obeys $(1-6\varepsilon)n\le j$ and $N+j\le n$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:lowercoef}
 \log c_j\ge\log(j!)-j\log t(q)+Np(q)-\Err(q)-O_b(\log n).
\end{equation}
The increasing prefix reaches $x_N$ in $N-1$ transitions, so $a_{N+j}^{(b)}\ge c_j$. Appending the new maximum $1+\asc(w)$ repeatedly gives an injective extension to length $n$, so $a_n^{(b)}\ge c_j$.

\subsection{A quantitative logarithmic error}\label{amc:sec:logerror}
From \eqref{amc:eq:ODE},
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:tailt}
 T_b-t(q)=O_b((1+q)e^{-\theta q}).
\end{equation}
In \eqref{amc:eq:lowercoef} the term $Np(q)$ is nonnegative. Also
$\log(n!/j!)\le6\varepsilon n\log n=6n/\log n$ and
\[
 |-j\log t(q)+n\log T_b|
 =O_b(\varepsilon n+n(1+q)e^{-\theta q}).
\]
All latter errors, and $\Err(q)$, are $o_b(n/\log n)$ for $q=\frac12\log n$. Hence
\[
 \log a_n^{(b)}\ge\log(n!)-n\log T_b-O_b(n/\log n).
\]
For the matching upper estimate use a single coefficient of $F(t,x_1)$:
\[
 a_n^{(b)}\le (n-1)!F(t(q),x_1)t(q)^{-(n-1)}.
\]
Equations~\eqref{amc:eq:growing} and~\eqref{amc:eq:tailt} bound its excess logarithm above $\log(n!)-n\log T_b$ by
\[
 O_b\bigl(\Err(q)+q+\log n+n(1+q)e^{-\theta q}\bigr)
 =o_b(n/\log n).
\]
This proves \eqref{amc:eq:main}, and therefore the full root limit. The proof uses no unproved ratio asymptotic.

\section{Inverse in the sequence length}\label{amc:sec:lengthinverse}
For fixed $b\ge2$ and $X>1$, define the integer threshold
\[
 N_b(X)=\min\{n\ge0:a_n^{(b)}\ge X\},\qquad L=\log X.
\]
The injective extension above makes the counts nondecreasing, and Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed} makes them unbounded. Let $W_0$ denote the principal real branch of Lambert $W$, characterized by $W_0(z)e^{W_0(z)}=z$ for $z>0$. As $X\to\infty$ with $b$ fixed,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:lengthinverse}
 n_0=\frac{L}{W_0(L/(eT_b))},\qquad
 N_b(X)=n_0+O_b\left(\frac{L}{(\log L)^3}\right)
 =n_0\left(1+O_b((\log L)^{-2})\right).
\end{equation}
To prove it, Stirling and \eqref{amc:eq:main} give
$\log a_n^{(b)}=G(n)+O_b(n/\log n)$ for
$G(x)=x\log(x/(eT_b))$. The threshold inequalities at $N=N_b(X)$ and $N-1$, together with $G(N)-G(N-1)=O_b(\log N)$, imply
$L=G(N)+O_b(N/\log N)$.
Thus $N\sim L/\log L$, while $G(n_0)=L$ exactly. Since
$G'(x)=\log(x/T_b)\asymp\log L$ between comparable $N$ and $n_0$, the mean value theorem gives \eqref{amc:eq:lengthinverse}.

This is a controlled leading inverse, not an exact rounding rule: its error tends to infinity. One may expand $W_0$ itself in logarithms, but such a formal expansion cannot improve the error inherited from the unknown coefficient correction. For $b=2$, the separate report gives a sharper inverse using its stronger logarithmic bound.

\section{Large caps and an exact first variation}\label{amc:sec:largecap}
Put $f(x)=\log x/(x-1)$, continuously extended at $1$, and $\tau=\pi^2/6$. The representation
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:frep}
 f(x)=\int_0^1\frac{dt}{1+t(x-1)}
\end{equation}
shows $f'<0$ and $f''>0$ on $[1,\infty)$. Thus $E_b$ increasing strictly in $b$ implies $T_b$ decreasing strictly. The cap-two integrand dominates all later integrands and is integrable. Dominated convergence and the nonnegative geometric series give
\[
 T_b\downarrow\int_0^\infty\frac{v}{e^v-1}\,dv
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k^2}=\tau.
\]
Write
\[
 R_b(v)=e^v-E_b(v),\quad
 \Delta_b(v)=f(E_b(v))-f(e^v),\quad
 L_b=\int_0^\infty[-f'(e^v)]R_b(v)\,dv.
\]
Convexity gives $\Delta_b(v)\ge[-f'(e^v)]R_b(v)\ge0$. We will prove
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:variation}
 L_b=(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1),\qquad
 0\le T_b-\tau-L_b=O\!\left(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Evaluation of the linear term}
For $v>0$,
\[
 -f'(e^v)=\sum_{h\ge2}\bigl((h-1)v-1\bigr)e^{-hv},\qquad
 R_b(v)=\sum_{k\ge b+1}\frac{v^k}{k!}.
\]
Although individual terms can have either sign near zero, their integrated absolute values are bounded by
\[
 \sum_{k\ge b+1}\sum_{h\ge2}
 \left(\frac{(h-1)(k+1)}{h^{k+2}}+\frac1{h^{k+1}}\right)<\infty
 \quad(b\ge2).
\]
Fubini is therefore legitimate. Integration and then summation in $k$ yield
\[
 L_b=\sum_{h\ge2}\sum_{k\ge b+1}\frac{(h-1)k-1}{h^{k+2}}
 =\sum_{h\ge2}\frac{b+1}{h^{b+2}}
 =(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1).
\]

\subsection{A uniform bound for the nonlinear remainder}
There is an absolute constant $C$ with
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:fsecond}
 0\le f''(z)\le C\frac{1+\log z}{z^3}\quad(z\ge1).
\end{equation}
This follows from smoothness at $1$ and the derivative asymptotics at infinity, or directly from \eqref{amc:eq:frep}. For $0\le v\le b$, the function $e^{-v}E_b(v)$ decreases, since its derivative is $-e^{-v}v^b/b!$. Stirling therefore gives
\[
 e^{-v}E_b(v)\ge e^{-b}E_b(b)\ge e^{-b}b^b/b!\ge c b^{-1/2}
\]
with absolute $c>0$. Taylor's theorem on $[E_b(v),e^v]$, using $\log z\le v$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:remainderpoint}
 0\le\Delta_b(v)-[-f'(e^v)]R_b(v)
 \le C b^{3/2}(1+v)e^{-3v}R_b(v)^2.
\end{equation}
Let $m=b+1$. Expanding the square, grouping $S=k+\ell$, and using
$\sum_{k=m}^{S-m}\binom Sk\le2^S$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \int_0^\infty(1+v)e^{-3v}R_b(v)^2\,dv
 &\le\sum_{S\ge2m}2^S
 \left(\frac1{3^{S+1}}+\frac{S+1}{3^{S+2}}\right)\\
 &=O\!\left(b(4/9)^b\right).
\end{align*}
All these integrands and terms are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies. This bounds the integrated remainder on $[0,b]$ by $O(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b)$.

For $v\ge b\ge3$, $E_b(v)\ge v^b/b!\ge2$ and $\log E_b(v)\le v$. Convexity implies the remainder is at most $\Delta_b(v)\le f(E_b(v))$, and hence its tail is at most
\[
 2b!\int_b^\infty v^{1-b}\,dv
 =\frac{2b!b^{2-b}}{b-2}
 =O(b^{3/2}e^{-b})
 =O\!\left(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b\right).
\]
This proves \eqref{amc:eq:variation} and \eqref{amc:eq:capstrong} with constants independent of $b$.

Since
\[
 L_b=\frac{b+1}{2^{b+2}}+O(b3^{-b}),
\]
the remainder is smaller than the leading term by an exponentially decaying factor. The reciprocal identity
\[
 \mu_\infty-\mu_b=\frac{T_b-\tau}{\tau T_b},\qquad\mu_\infty=\frac1\tau=\frac6{\pi^2},
\]
then gives the useful quantified form
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:gap}
 \mu_\infty-\mu_b
 =D(b+1)2^{-b}\left[1+O\!\left(b^{3/2}(8/9)^b\right)\right],
 \qquad D=\frac9{\pi^4}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the additive contributions $O(b3^{-b})$ and $O(b^24^{-b})$ are absorbed by $O(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b)$. This also proves \eqref{amc:eq:caplead}. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:capstrong} is a rigorously controlled first-variation expansion; it is not an all-orders transseries.

\section{Inverse in the multiplicity cap}\label{amc:sec:capinverse}
For small $\varepsilon>0$ let
\[
 b_\varepsilon=\min\{b\ge2:\mu_b\ge\mu_\infty-\varepsilon\}.
\]
Monotonicity proves existence. The large real solution of the leading model
$D(b+1)2^{-b}=\varepsilon$ is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:capinverse}
 b_0=-\frac1{\log2}W_{-1}\!\left(-\frac{\varepsilon\log2}{2D}\right)-1,
\end{equation}
where $W_{-1}$ is the real branch with $W_{-1}(z)\le-1$ on $[-1/e,0)$. The branch is essential; $W_0$ gives the irrelevant small root.

There are constants $C$ and $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, for $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, with
\[
 \eta=C b_0^{3/2}(8/9)^{b_0},
\]
one has the integer-safe bracket
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:rounding}
 \left\lceil b_0-\eta\right\rceil
 \le b_\varepsilon\le
 \left\lceil b_0+\eta\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
No interpolation of the exact sequence $T_b$ to real $b$ is assumed.

Here is the discrete justification. For the real leading model $h(x)=D(x+1)2^{-x}$,
\[
 (\log h)'(x)=\frac1{x+1}-\log2
\]
is negative and bounded away from zero for large $x$. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:gap} first places $b_\varepsilon$ within a fixed bounded distance of $b_0$: at integers several units below or above $b_0$, the fixed change of $h$ dominates the vanishing relative error. In that bounded window the relative error is uniformly $O(b_0^{3/2}(8/9)^{b_0})$. Increasing $C$ if necessary, every integer $b<b_0-\eta$ fails the threshold and every integer $b\ge b_0+\eta$ meets it. Monotonicity handles all integers outside the window, giving \eqref{amc:eq:rounding}.

A useful explicit strengthening, proved by the same estimates, is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:explicitround}
 \lfloor b_0\rfloor+1\le b_\varepsilon\le
 \left\lceil b_0+2000b_0^{3/2}(8/9)^{b_0}\right\rceil
 \qquad(b_0\ge13).
\end{equation}
Here are details to make its constants and strict lower bound reproducible. The bound
$f''(z)\le16(1+\log z)/z^3$ and $b!\le3\sqrt b(b/e)^b$ make
\eqref{amc:eq:remainderpoint} valid with $216$. Its integrated central bound is
$64b^{3/2}(b+4)(4/9)^b$, and its tail is at most $6b^{5/2}(4/9)^b$.
Thus $0\le H_b:=T_b-\tau-L_b\le160b^{5/2}(4/9)^b$ for every $b\ge3$.
\begin{writenote}
In Part~\ref{amc:part:sec}'s notation, $L_b=A_{1,b}$ and $H_b=\mathcal R_{1,b}$, and Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact} with \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r2} gives $H_b\sim A_{2,b}\sim\frac8{9\sqrt\pi}\sqrt b\,(4/9)^b$. The explicit bound $160b^{5/2}(4/9)^b$ above is therefore valid but not sharp in its power of $b$; it is kept because it is explicit, and the integer brackets below use it. ($H_b$ is not Part~\ref{amc:part:sec}'s harmonic number $H_r$.)
\end{writenote}
Put $M_b=(b+1)2^{-b-2}$ and $V_b=L_b-M_b$. Integral comparison gives
\[
 L_b\le (b/2)2^{-b},\qquad
 (b+1)3^{-b-2}\le V_b\le(b+4)3^{-b-2}.
\]
Hence $M_bL_b/V_b\le(9b/8)(3/4)^b\le729/512<\tau$, so
\[
 d_b:=\mu_\infty-\mu_b\ge\frac{L_b}{\tau(\tau+L_b)}>
 \frac{M_b}{\tau^2}=h(b).
\]
The upper bounds above also give
$0<d_b/h(b)-1\le644\phi(b)$, where $\phi(x)=x^{3/2}(8/9)^x$.
For $x\ge13$, $\phi$ decreases, and for $x\ge2$,
$(\log h)'(x)<-1/3$. Every integer $b\le b_0$ fails the threshold, proving
its strict lower bracket. For $m=\lceil b_0+2000\phi(b_0)\rceil$,
\[
 \frac{d_m}{\varepsilon}\le
 e^{-2000\phi(b_0)/3}(1+644\phi(b_0))<1,
\]
proving the upper bracket. The constants are conservative for moderate caps.
In particular, at $\varepsilon=h(n)$ for an integer $n\ge3$, one has
$b_0=n$ but $b_\varepsilon\ge n+1$. This supplies actual counterexamples
to unconditional rounding by $\lceil b_0\rceil$ at arbitrarily small tolerances.

Thus $b_\varepsilon=b_0+O(1)$, and away from vanishingly small neighborhoods of integer transitions the threshold is the expected ceiling. Exactly near those transitions, an unconditional rounding prescription is not justified by an asymptotic equivalence. In particular this is an inverse for the cap of the limiting growth constant, not a cap selection guarantee for finite-length counts.

\begin{partabstract}
The cap integral has exact positive Taylor sectors and an exponentially sharp remainder after any fixed number of sectors. Each fixed sector has an algebraic expansion to arbitrary fixed order; its coefficients are rational and algorithmically computable. We give the leading constants, the first three corrections in sectors two and three, and a precise reciprocal corollary for the growth constant. This supplements the separately delivered bounded-multiplicity report [Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} of this report]: its combinatorial identification and inversion results are prerequisites, not reproved here. The statements are fixed-sector results. Finite algebraic truncations cannot generally resolve subsequent exponential sectors, and no uniform growing-sector or accumulation-scale theorem is asserted.
\end{partabstract}

\section{Objects and relationship to the main report}\label{amc:sec:sec:objects}
For integer $b\ge2$, define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:objects}
 E_b(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{b}\frac{v^j}{j!},\qquad
 f(x)=\frac{\log x}{x-1},\qquad
 T_b=\int_0^\infty f(E_b(v))\,dv,
\end{equation}
with $f(1)=1$. Set $\tau=\pi^2/6$, $m=b+1$, $R_b(v)=e^v-E_b(v)$, and $H_r=\sum_{j=1}^r1/j$.
The integral is finite for $b\ge2$ because its integrand is $O_b(\log v/v^b)$ at infinity. Also
$\int_0^\infty f(e^v)\,dv=\int_0^\infty v/(e^v-1)\,dv=\tau$.

The main report, Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} of this report (Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed}), proves, for each fixed cap $b\ge3$,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{a_n^{(b)}}{n!}\right)^{1/n}=\mu_b=T_b^{-1},
\]
where $a_n^{(b)}$ counts ascent sequences in which every value occurs at most $b$ times. Its cited cap-two theorem gives the same identification for $b=2$. The present addendum refines the large-$b$ analysis of $T_b$ after that fixed-cap length limit. It does not strengthen the main report's error in $n$ or give joint $b,n$ asymptotics.

The main report established the exact first variation and a nonlinear bound $O(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b)$ (Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}, \eqref{amc:eq:variation}). The theorem below sharpens that remainder to a full fixed-sector hierarchy, starting with $A_{2,b}\asymp b^{1/2}(4/9)^b$. The main report's controlled length inverse and rounding-safe cap inverse (Sections~\ref{amc:sec:lengthinverse} and~\ref{amc:sec:capinverse}) remain the supplied inversion results; no sharper inverse is claimed in this addendum.

\section{Exact positive sectors and the sharp remainder}
Define, for $r\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:sector}
 a_r(x)=\frac{(-1)^r}{r!}f^{(r)}(x)
       =\int_0^1\frac{t^r\,dt}{(1-t+tx)^{r+1}},\qquad
 A_{r,b}=\int_0^\infty a_r(e^v)R_b(v)^r\,dv.
\end{equation}
These sectors are strictly positive. Define their primary exponential bases by
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:beta}
 \beta_r=\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^r.
\end{equation}
They strictly decrease to $e^{-1}$; in particular every fixed $\beta_r$ is greater than $e^{-1}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{amc:sec:thm:exact}
For each integer $b\ge2$, the following is an exact convergent identity:
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:infinite}
 T_b-\tau=\sum_{r\ge1}A_{r,b}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed integer $K\ge1$, as $b\to\infty$ through integers,
\begin{align}
 T_b-\tau&=\sum_{r=1}^K A_{r,b}+\RR_{K,b},\label{amc:sec:eq:remainder}\\
 0<\RR_{K,b}&=O_K\!\left(b^{(2-K)/2}\beta_{K+1}^b\right),\label{amc:sec:eq:order}\\
 \RR_{K,b}&=A_{K+1,b}
 \left[1+O_K\!\left(b^{-1/2}
 \left(\frac{\beta_{K+2}}{\beta_{K+1}}\right)^b\right)\right].\label{amc:sec:eq:equivalence}
\end{align}
In particular $\RR_{K,b}\sim A_{K+1,b}$, with a positive exact remainder. The estimates hold for fixed $K$; the identity \eqref{amc:sec:eq:infinite} does not license summing sector asymptotic formulas.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The integral representation $f(x)=\int_0^1(1-t+tx)^{-1}\,dt$ gives \eqref{amc:sec:eq:sector}. For $x=e^v$, $R=R_b(v)$, $E=x-R$, and $D=1-t+tx$, the finite geometric identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:positive-remainder}
 f(E)-f(x)-\sum_{r=1}^K a_r(x)R^r
 =R^{K+1}\int_0^1\frac{t^{K+1}\,dt}{D^{K+1}(D-tR)}.
\end{equation}
Since $0\le tR/D\le R/x<1$, this lies between
$a_{K+1}(x)R^{K+1}$ and $(x/E)a_{K+1}(x)R^{K+1}$. Expanding the infinite geometric series and using monotone convergence proves \eqref{amc:sec:eq:infinite}; in particular the sectors are integrable.

There is an absolute $c>0$ such that $e^{-v}E_b(v)\ge c$ for $0\le v\le b$. Indeed $e^{-v}E_b(v)$ is decreasing, while Stirling's bounds show that the $\lfloor\sqrt b/2\rfloor+1$ summands of $e^{-b}E_b(b)$ immediately below degree $b$ are each at least a constant times $b^{-1/2}$. For those terms use
\[
 e^{-b}\frac{b^{b-j}}{(b-j)!}
 =\frac{e^{-b}b^b}{b!}\prod_{h=0}^{j-1}(1-h/b),
 \qquad \log(1-u)\ge-2u\quad(0\le u\le1/2).
\]
For sufficiently large $b$ and $v\ge b$, $E_b(v)\ge v^b/b!\ge2$ and $\log E_b(v)\le v$. Thus
\[
 0\le\int_b^\infty[f(E_b(v))-f(e^v)]\,dv
 \le\frac{2b!\,b^{2-b}}{b-2}=O(b^{3/2}e^{-b}).
\]
The integrated geometric bounds now give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:sandwich}
 A_{K+1,b}\le\RR_{K,b}
 \le c^{-1}A_{K+1,b}+O(b^{3/2}e^{-b}).
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:algebraic} below, together with $\beta_{K+1}>e^{-1}$, proves \eqref{amc:sec:eq:order}. Applying the same bound with $K+1$ and using
$\RR_{K,b}=A_{K+1,b}+\RR_{K+1,b}$ gives \eqref{amc:sec:eq:equivalence}. Finally, the derivative of $-r\log(1+1/r)$ is negative, by $\log(1+x)>x/(1+x)$, proving the asserted ordering of the bases.
\end{proof}

\section{Every algebraic order within a fixed sector}
\begin{theorem}\label{amc:sec:thm:algebraic}
For every fixed integer $r\ge1$ and every fixed $N\ge0$, there are rational coefficients $c_{r,j}$, with $c_{r,0}=1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:allorders}
 A_{r,b}=C_r b^{(3-r)/2}\beta_r^b
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^N\frac{c_{r,j}}{b^j}+O_{r,N}(b^{-N-1})\right),
 \qquad C_r=\frac{r^{r+3/2}}{(r+1)^2}(2\pi)^{(1-r)/2}.
\end{equation}
The first correction is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:first-coefficient}
 c_{r,1}=\frac{3-r}{2}-\frac{r^2(r+1)}2
          +\frac{23r}{12}+\frac{13}{12r}-\frac{r+1}{r}H_r.
\end{equation}
In particular, the first three corrections in sectors two and three are
\begin{align}
 A_{2,b}&=\frac8{9\sqrt\pi}\sqrt b\left(\frac49\right)^b
 \left(1-\frac{27}{8b}+\frac{5669}{128b^2}
             -\frac{962949}{1024b^3}+O(b^{-4})\right),\label{amc:sec:eq:r2}\\
 A_{3,b}&=\frac{81\sqrt3}{32\pi}\left(\frac{27}{64}\right)^b
 \left(1-\frac{43}{3b}+\frac{33952}{81b^2}
             -\frac{42671116}{2187b^3}+O(b^{-4})\right).\label{amc:sec:eq:r3}
\end{align}
The first sector has the exact formula
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:r1}
 A_{1,b}=(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1).
\end{equation}
Consequently its algebraic coefficients are $c_{1,1}=1$ and $c_{1,j}=0$ for $j\ge2$; its smaller exponential terms are retained by \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r1}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Separate the derivative correction}
Changing variables in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:sector} gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:derivative}
 a_r(e^v)=e^{-(r+1)v}
 \frac{v-\sum_{j=1}^r(1-e^{-v})^j/j}{(1-e^{-v})^{r+1}},
\end{equation}
with continuous value $1/(r+1)$ at $v=0$. For $v\ge1$ this equals
$e^{-(r+1)v}(v-H_r)+O_r((1+v)e^{-(r+2)v})$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:J}
 J_{r,b}=\int_0^\infty(v-H_r)e^{-(r+1)v}R_b(v)^r\,dv.
\end{equation}
The integrand of $J_{r,b}$ need not be positive. Coefficientwise,
$R_b(v)^r\le\sum_{k\ge rm}r^kv^k/k!$. Hence, for fixed $h>r$,
\[
 \int_0^\infty(1+v)e^{-hv}R_b(v)^r\,dv
 \le\sum_{k\ge rm}\frac{r^k}{h^{k+1}}
                  \left(1+\frac{k+1}{h}\right)
 =O_{r,h}\!\left(m(r/h)^{rm}\right).
\]
The bounded derivative factors on $[0,1]$ and $R_b(v)\le e/m!$ give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:derivative-error}
 A_{r,b}=J_{r,b}
 +O_r\!\left(m\left(\frac r{r+2}\right)^{rm}+(m!)^{-r}\right).
\end{equation}
The bases $(r/(r+2))^r$ are at most $1/3<e^{-1}$, so these corrections are below every fixed primary sector. All constants here may depend on the fixed $r$.

For completeness, \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r1} can be evaluated without asymptotics. Put
$F(k)=\int_0^\infty e^{-kv}R_b(v)\,dv=1/[(k-1)k^m]$ for $k\ge2$.
Expanding $a_1(e^v)=\sum_{k\ge2}((k-1)v-1)e^{-kv}$ and integrating gives
\[
 A_{1,b}=\sum_{k\ge2}\bigl(-(k-1)F'(k)-F(k)\bigr)
        =m\sum_{k\ge2}k^{-m-1}.
\]
Interchanging the sum and integral is valid by absolute integrability: replacing each linear factor by $(k-1)v+1$ gives an integrated summable bound. This also yields $J_{1,b}=m/2^{m+1}$.
\begin{writenote}
Since $A_{1,b}$ is Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}'s $L_b$, this is a second proof of the first identity in \eqref{amc:eq:variation}. Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} (Section~\ref{amc:sec:largecap}, ``Evaluation of the linear term'') integrates the double series in $(h,k)$ term by term; this Part integrates against $F(k)=1/((k-1)k^m)$ and differentiates in $k$.
\end{writenote}

\subsection{Uniform tail expansion and saddle localization}
Write $s=v/m$ and
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:Sm}
 R_b(ms)=\frac{(ms)^m}{m!}S_m(s),\qquad
 S_m(s)=\sum_{\ell\ge0}s^\ell\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(1+i/m)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed $d<1$, to arbitrary fixed order and with any fixed number of derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:sigma}
 S_m(s)=\sum_{j=0}^N\frac{\sigma_j(s)}{m^j}+O_{N,d}(m^{-N-1}),
 \quad \sigma_0(s)=\frac1{1-s},\quad
 \sigma_j(s)=-\frac{s}{1-s}\sigma_{j-1}'(s),
\end{equation}
uniformly on $0\le s\le d$. To justify the remainder, expand
$\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(1+iu)^{-1}$ at $u=0$. Its absolute order-$N$ remainder at $u=1/m$ is bounded by
$m^{-N-1}(\ell(\ell+1)/2)^{N+1}$. This follows by bounding its alternating derivative at positive $u$ by its value at zero. Summing the polynomial bound against $s^\ell$ proves uniformity, also after fixed derivatives. The exact differential equation
$(1-s)S_m+(s/m)S_m'=1$ then proves the recurrence in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:sigma}.

Substitution in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:J} gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:laplace}
 J_{r,b}=\frac{m^{rm+2}}{(m!)^r}
 \int_0^\infty (s-H_r/m)S_m(s)^r e^{m\phi_r(s)}\,ds,
 \qquad\phi_r(s)=r\log s-(r+1)s.
\end{equation}
Its unique nondegenerate saddle is $s_r=r/(r+1)$, with
$\lambda_r=-\phi_r''(s_r)=(r+1)^2/r$. Choose fixed $0<a<s_r<d<1$.
On $[0,a]$, use $S_m\le(1-a)^{-1}$ and the phase gap below the maximum. On $[d,1]$, the term ratios in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:Sm} give $S_m\le m+1$, and another phase gap defeats that polynomial. Finally the original integral over $v\ge m$ is $O_r(me^{-m})$, since $R_b(v)\le e^v$. These contributions are exponentially smaller than the saddle term after Stirling's bounds are included.

On $[a,d]$, \eqref{amc:sec:eq:sigma} and the interior Laplace expansion therefore apply to every fixed order. The leading term is
\[
 J_{r,b}\sim D_r m^{(3-r)/2}\beta_r^m,
 \qquad D_r=r^{3/2}(r+1)^{r-2}(2\pi)^{(1-r)/2}.
\]
Since $D_r\beta_r=C_r$, this proves the leading constant in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:allorders}. The same argument supplies a genuine remainder at each algebraic order, not merely a formal series. Together with \eqref{amc:sec:eq:derivative-error}, it establishes the all-orders assertion of Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:algebraic}.

\subsection{A finite algorithm for all coefficients}
The included author script uses the following exact operations to find $c_{r,0},\ldots,c_{r,N}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Set $z=m^{-1/2}$ and $s=s_r+uz$. Expand
$(s-H_rz^2)(\sum_{j=0}^N\sigma_j(s)z^{2j})^r$ through degree $2N$ in $z$.
\item Multiply by the truncated exponential of the higher phase and Stirling terms
\[
 \sum_{k\ge3}\frac{r(-1)^{k-1}}{k}(u/s_r)^kz^{k-2}
 -r\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k-1)}z^{4k-2},
\]
retaining only the terms needed through $z^{2N}$.
\item Apply normalized Gaussian moments: $u^{2j}\mapsto(2j-1)!!/\lambda_r^j$, $u^{2j+1}\mapsto0$. Divide by $s_r(1-s_r)^{-r}$ to obtain relative coefficients $d_{r,j}$ in powers of $m^{-1}$.
\item Put $p=(3-r)/2$. The coefficients in $b$ are the coefficients of $q^j$ in
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^N d_{r,j}q^j(1+q)^{p-j}\pmod{q^{N+1}},\qquad q=b^{-1}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
All these quantities are rational except for the already factored leading constant. In particular the first-order calculation gives
\[
 d_{r,1}=-\frac{r^2(r+1)}2+\frac{23r}{12}
          +\frac{13}{12r}-\frac{r+1}{r}H_r,
\]
and $c_{r,1}=d_{r,1}+p$, proving \eqref{amc:sec:eq:first-coefficient}. The explicit coefficients in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r2}--\eqref{amc:sec:eq:r3} are independently reproduced as described in Section~\ref{amc:sec:sec:checks}.

\section{Reciprocation and the limits of exponential resolution}
\begin{corollary}\label{amc:sec:cor:reciprocal}
Use the combinatorial identification $\mu_b=T_b^{-1}$ from the main report (Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed}), and write $\mu_\infty=1/\tau=6/\pi^2$. For every fixed $K\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:mu}
 \mu_\infty-\mu_b=\tau^{-2}\sum_{r=1}^K A_{r,b}+\widetilde\RR_{K,b},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:mu-rem}
 \widetilde\RR_{K,b}=\tau^{-2}A_{K+1,b}
 \left[1+O_K\!\left(b^{-1/2}
 \left(\frac{\beta_{K+2}}{\beta_{K+1}}\right)^b\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Thus $\widetilde\RR_{K,b}>0$ for all sufficiently large $b$ for each fixed $K$. No all-$b$ positivity of this reciprocal remainder is asserted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $\delta_b=T_b-\tau$. The exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:reciprocal-exact}
 \mu_\infty-\mu_b=\frac{\delta_b}{\tau^2}
            -\frac{\delta_b^2}{\tau^2(\tau+\delta_b)}
\end{equation}
shows the scope of the linear sector transfer. Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact} with $K=1$ and \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r1} give $\delta_b=O(b2^{-b})$, so the second term is $O(b^2 4^{-b})$.
For fixed $K$, this is exponentially smaller even than $A_{K+2,b}$, since $\beta_{K+2}>e^{-1}>1/4$. Substituting \eqref{amc:sec:eq:equivalence} in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:reciprocal-exact} therefore gives \eqref{amc:sec:eq:mu-rem}. This proof uses no expansion with a growing number of sectors.
\end{proof}
In particular,
\[
 \frac6{\pi^2}-\mu_b\sim\frac{9(b+1)}{\pi^4 2^b}.
\]
Within each fixed primary sector, the coefficients of the deficit are $\tau^{-2}C_r c_{r,j}$. The exact reciprocal correction in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:reciprocal-exact} still exists on smaller exponential scales and must not be dropped in a purported complete transseries.

\paragraph{The essential scale warning.}
Theorems~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact} and \ref{amc:sec:cor:reciprocal} require exact sectors, or approximations with errors controlled on the next exponential scale. If each $A_{r,b}$, $1\le r\le K$, is replaced by only $N_r+1$ terms of \eqref{amc:sec:eq:allorders}, one must add the error
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:truncation}
 O\!\left(\sum_{r=1}^K
       b^{(3-r)/2-N_r-1}\beta_r^b\right).
\end{equation}
For any fixed truncation order, such a remainder in an earlier nonterminating sector generally exceeds all later exponential sectors. For example, subtracting only the displayed four terms of $A_{2,b}$ does not isolate $A_{3,b}$. Subtracting the exact sector does. The exact first sector is a useful exceptional simplification, not a cure for this issue in the later sectors.

The word ``transseries'' here describes an exact positive-sector decomposition with fixed-sector all-orders expansions. It does not assert optimal truncation, summability, uniform estimates for $r=r(b)$, a justified sum of all sector asymptotic expansions, or a theorem at the accumulation scale $e^{-b}$. Reciprocation and any subsequent inversion must preserve these distinctions.
\begin{writenote}
The word is used here in this narrow sense only. This Part constructs no transseries in the sense of the repository's transseries volume (\repopath{Analysis/Transseries/}), no Borel sum and no Stokes data, which is why it stays beside Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} in this collection.

\section{Definitions and principal results}\label{amc:bm:sec:results}
An \emph{ascent sequence} of length $N\ge1$ is a word $a_1\cdots a_N$ of nonnegative integers such that $a_1=0$ and
\[
 a_i\le1+\asc(a_1\cdots a_{i-1})\quad(2\le i\le N),
 \qquad \asc(a_1\cdots a_k)=\#\{i<k:a_i<a_{i+1}\}.
\]
Let $c_{N,r}$ count these words with each value occurring at most $r$ times, and set $c_{0,r}=1$. Throughout the combinatorial proof, $r\ge2$ is fixed. The word length is denoted by $N$; a separate cap index $R=r+1$ will be introduced explicitly in Section~\ref{amc:bm:sec:largecap}.

Put
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:defconstant}
 E_j(w)=\sum_{k=0}^j\frac{w^k}{k!},\qquad
 g(v)=\frac{\log v}{v-1},\quad g(1)=1,\qquad
 J_r=\int_0^\infty g(E_r(w))\dd w,\quad \mu_r=J_r^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The integral is positive and finite: its integrand is continuous at zero and is $O_r(\log w/w^r)$ at infinity.

\begin{theorem}\label{amc:bm:thm:growth}
For every fixed integer $r\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:mainlimits}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\frac{c_{N,r}}{N!}\right)^{1/N}
 =\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{c_{N+1,r}}{(N+1)c_{N,r}}
 =\mu_r.
\end{equation}
In particular $\log c_{N,r}=N\log N+N(\log\mu_r-1)+o_r(N)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{amc:bm:thm:endpoints}
For a uniform word counted by $c_{N,r}$, let $N_k$ be the number of values occurring exactly $k$ times, let $D$ be its number of distinct values, and let $A$ be its ascent count. Define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:bj}
 b_j=\frac{\mu_r}{(r-j)!}\int_0^\infty[-g'(E_r(w))]w^{r-j}\dd w
 \quad(1\le j\le r).
\end{equation}
Then $b_j>0$, $\sum_jb_j=\mu_r$, and
\begin{align}
 \frac{N_k}{N}&\longrightarrow b_{r-k} &&(1\le k<r),\label{amc:bm:eq:multlimits}\\
 \frac{N_r}{N}&\longrightarrow\frac{1-\sum_{j<r}(r-j)b_j}{r},\notag\\
 \frac DN&\longrightarrow\frac{1+\sum_{j<r}jb_j}{r},\qquad
 \frac AN\longrightarrow b_r+\frac{1+\sum_{j<r}jb_j}{r}.\notag
\end{align}
Every displayed convergence holds in mean and in probability, with exponentially small probabilities for any fixed positive deviation.
\end{theorem}
Here ``exponentially small'' means that for each fixed deviation $\eta>0$ there are $c>0$ and $N_0$, depending on $r$ and $\eta$, such that the relevant probability is at most $\ee^{-cN}$ for $N\ge N_0$.

\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:gpositive}
 g(v)=\int_0^1\frac{\dd t}{1+t(v-1)}

\[
 J_r\longrightarrow\int_0^\infty\frac{w}{\ee^w-1}\dd w
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\int_0^\infty w\ee^{-kw}\dd w
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
\]

\[
 I(a)=\int_1^\infty\frac{2\log((s^2+a)/(1+a))}{s^2-1}\dd s.

\section{Exact sorting of residual capacities}\label{amc:bm:sec:compaction}
After a nonempty prefix, the allowed original labels are $0,\ldots,A+1$, where $A$ is its ascent count. Delete labels already used $r$ times, and renumber the remaining labels in increasing order. Give each remaining label its residual capacity in $\{1,\ldots,r\}$. Capacity $r$ means unused. Let $L$ be the number of surviving labels less than or equal to the previous last label. A next compact label $i$ creates an ascent exactly when $i\ge L$.

Selecting capacity $j$ decreases that capacity by one. For $j=1$ the label is deleted and the new threshold is $i$; for $j\ge2$ it survives and the new threshold is $i+1$. Every ascent adds a new capacity-$r$ label at the right. These rules describe the original process exactly before any sorting.

\begin{lemma}[Adjacent exchange]\label{amc:bm:lem:exchange}
Suppose two adjacent available labels have compact indices $i,i+1$ with $i+1<L$. Exchanging their residual capacities preserves the number of legal suffixes of every length, refined by the final residual-capacity histogram and the total number of new ascents.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Temporarily keep exhausted labels as permanently forbidden positions rather than deleting them. The two selected labels are adjacent among usable labels; any intervening forbidden original labels cannot occur in a future suffix. Both selected labels are already available and are at most the previous last label.

In any legal suffix, find each maximal contiguous run containing only these two labels. Reverse the run and interchange the two labels. Reversal followed by complementation preserves its number of internal ascents. Comparisons at its ends with a label outside the pair are unchanged, because every other usable label lies below both or above both. At the initial boundary neither selected label can make an ascent. Therefore the total new ascent count is preserved.

Inside a transformed run, the intermediate ascent counts can differ. This causes no illegality: both run labels were already available initially and availability only increases. On exiting the run, accumulated ascent counts agree, so every subsequent external label has the same bound as before. The usages of the two selected labels are exchanged. Exchanging their initial residual capacities therefore preserves the final capacity constraints; partial usages cannot exceed their total usages. The final residual capacities are exchanged as well, preserving their histogram. The transformation is an involution, so it is a bijection. Deleting forbidden labels afterward merely restores the compact indexing.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sorted recurrence]\label{amc:bm:prop:recurrence}
Let $X_j$ count labels of residual capacity $j$, and put $C_j=\sum_{k\le j}X_k$, $C_0=0$. Starting from
\[
 \bX=\boldsymbol e_{r-1}+\boldsymbol e_r,\qquad L=1
\]
after the initial zero, the exact transfer consists, for every $j$ and every integer $C_{j-1}\le i<C_j$, of
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:transfer}
 \bX'=\bX-\boldsymbol e_j+\ind{j\ge2}\boldsymbol e_{j-1}
       +\ind{i\ge L}\boldsymbol e_r,
 \qquad L'=i+\ind{j\ge2}.
\end{equation}
It counts $c_{N,r}$ and preserves its full terminal capacity histogram.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume capacities are sorted increasingly. If capacity $j\ge2$ is selected at index $i$, its new capacity is $j-1$. Bubble it left through earlier members of its old category $j$ until it reaches the category boundary. Each exchanged pair has upper index at most $i$, while the new threshold is $i+1$. Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:exchange} applies to every exchange, leaving the threshold fixed. Thus the suffix count is unchanged and the capacities are sorted again. For $j=1$ deletion already preserves sorting; any fresh capacity-$r$ label belongs at the right. The initial state is sorted. Backward induction on suffix length proves the transfer, and the same induction with histogram refinements proves its marginal interpretation.
\end{proof}

The threshold remains a comparison marker; it does not follow the capacity being moved during the suffix-count equivalence. This distinction prevents an incorrect literal-history interpretation of the sorting step.

At level $N$, write $N_k$ for the original multiplicity counts and $D=\sum_kN_k$. The histogram refinement gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:histrelations}
 X_j=N_{r-j}\ (j<r),\qquad X_r=A+2-D,
 \qquad N=rN_r+\sum_{j<r}(r-j)X_j.
\end{equation}
Also $M=\sum_jX_j\le N+1$, every coordinate changes by at most one in a step, and each cumulative boundary $C_j$ changes by at most one. For $j<r$ the latter change is $\ind{\text{category }j+1}-\ind{\text{category }1}$; for $j=r$ it is $\ind{\text{ascent}}-\ind{\text{category }1}$.

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial enumeration]\label{amc:bm:cor:algorithm}
For fixed $r$, the coefficients through length $N$ can be computed using $O_r(N^{r+2})$ arithmetic operations and $O_r(N^{r+1})$ memory.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At level $k$, there are $O_r(k^r)$ possible vectors $\bX$ with coordinate sum at most $k+1$, each with $O(k)$ last thresholds. For each vector form prefix sums of its counts over $L$. A choice $i$ sends the prefix sum over $L\le i$ to its ascent endpoint in~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:transfer}, and the complementary sum to its nonascent endpoint. There are $O(k)$ choices over all categories together. Thus one vector costs $O_r(k)$, one level costs $O_r(k^{r+1})$, and all levels cost $O_r(N^{r+2})$. Only two consecutive levels are stored. This counts arithmetic operations rather than bit operations on integers of increasing size.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite upper and lower block estimates}\label{amc:bm:sec:blocks}
The two bounds in this section count finite collections of paths. Their asymptotic use later requires no limiting eigenvalue theorem or path large-deviation principle.

\subsection{An explicit positive matrix}
\begin{lemma}\label{amc:bm:lem:matrix}
Let $v>0$, $w_0,\ldots,w_D\ge0$, $W=\sum_z w_z>0$, $w_* =\max_z w_z$, and $F(v)=(v-1)/\log v$, with $F(1)=1$. Define
\[
 K_{\ge}(L,z)=w_zv^{\ind{z\ge L}},\qquad
 K_{>}(L,z)=w_zv^{\ind{z>L}}.
\]
Use $K=K_{\ge}$ if $v\ge1$ and $K=K_>$ if $v<1$. Then, for every integer $b\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:matrixbound}
 K^b\boldsymbol1\le\max(v,v^{-1})\Lambda^b\boldsymbol1,
 \qquad \Lambda\le F(v)W+|v-1|w_*.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $v>1$, and put $c=v-1$. The equation
\[
 \prod_{z=0}^D(1-cw_z/\lambda)=1/v,\qquad \lambda>cw_*
\]
has one solution, since the product increases continuously from zero to one. Set $f_0=1$ and $f_{z+1}=(1-cw_z/\lambda)f_z$. Then $f_{D+1}=1/v$ and $1/v\le f_z\le1$. Telescoping gives $\sum_z w_zf_z=\lambda/v$; the zeroth row and successive row differences now give $K_{\ge}f=\lambda f$. Positivity yields $K_{\ge}^b\boldsymbol1\le v\lambda^b\boldsymbol1$.

At $B=cW/\log v+cw_*$, the inequality $-\log(1-t)\le t/(1-t)$ gives
\[
 \sum_z-\log(1-cw_z/B)\le\frac{cW}{B-cw_*}=\log v.
\]
Monotonicity puts $B$ at or above the root, proving the stated bound for $\lambda$.

If $0<v<1$, put $d=1-v$. The product $\prod_z(1+dw_z/\lambda)$ decreases continuously from infinity to one, so its equation with $1/v$ again has one root. The same recurrence, now $f_{z+1}=(1+dw_z/\lambda)f_z$, gives $1\le f_z\le1/v$ and $K_{\ge}f=\lambda f$. For $B=dW/\log(1/v)=F(v)W$,
\[
 \sum_z\log(1+dw_z/B)\le dW/B=\log(1/v),
\]
so $\lambda\le B$. Since $K_>=K_{\ge}+d\operatorname{diag}(w_z)$,
\[
 K_>f\le(\lambda+dw_*)f.
\]
The bounds on $f$ give~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:matrixbound}. At $v=1$ the matrix has identical rows and the result follows with $\Lambda=W$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform domination of every tilted block}
Use arbitrary positive marks $u_1,\ldots,u_r$, and set $u_0=1$, $v=u_r$, $c_j=u_{j-1}/u_j$. A category-$j$ transition has weight $c_jv^{\ind{i\ge L}}$; the product over a path is $\bu^{\bX'-\bX}$.

Consider $b$ steps starting at $\bX$, and use its initial cumulative boundaries $C_j$. Every output threshold $z$ of category $j$ lies in the fixed interval
\[
 I_j=[C_{j-1}-b+\ind{j\ge2},\ C_j+b-1+\ind{j\ge2}]\cap\N_0.
\]
Its size is at most $X_j+2b+1$. This follows from the one-step cumulative-boundary bound after~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:histrelations}. Use a finite matrix index range containing every initial and output threshold, and set $w_z=\sum_j c_j\ind{z\in I_j}$.

For category 1 the exact ascent indicator is $[z\ge L]$; for every other category it is $[z>L]$. Thus the weak-threshold matrix dominates when $v\ge1$, and the strict-threshold matrix dominates when $v<1$. If two category intervals overlap, their weights add; this simply allows extra positive paths. Backward induction on the number of remaining steps proves domination uniformly over every hidden category vector with a given threshold. Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:matrix} therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:blockupper}
 \sum_{\text{block paths}}\bu^{\bX'-\bX}
 \le\max(v,v^{-1})\Lambda^b,
 \qquad \Lambda\le N\rho(\bX/N;\bu)+C(b+1),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:symbol}
 \rho(\bx;\bu)=\frac1{g(u_r)}\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{u_{j-1}}{u_j}x_j.
\end{equation}
Here $C$ is uniform on a fixed compact set of positive marks, since $\sum_z w_z\le\sum_jc_j(X_j+2b+1)$ and $w_*\le\sum_jc_j$. Boundary states with zero coordinates cause no difficulty.

\subsection{Buffered distinct labels}
Write $A(m,k)$ for the number of permutations of $m$ objects with $k$ ascents.
\begin{lemma}\label{amc:bm:lem:lowerblock}
Start at a sorted state with $L\le X_1$. Fix $b\ge2$ and integers $q_j\ge0$ with $\sum_jq_j=b$, $q_1,q_r\ge1$. Provided the binomial arguments are large enough, for every $0\le k\le b-3$ there are at least
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:exactlower}
 A(b-2,k)\prod_{j=1}^r\binom{X_j-2b}{q_j}
\end{equation}
paths with endpoint increments
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:increments}
 \Delta X_j=q_{j+1}-q_j\ (j<r),\qquad
 \Delta X_r=k+1-q_r,
\end{equation}
and with final threshold $L'\le X'_1$. The same statement uses $A(0,0)=1$ when $b=2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $q_j$ distinct integer labels from $C_{j-1}+b,\ldots,C_j-b-1$. Each remains in category $j$ whenever it is selected, because every boundary has moved by at most $b$. This argument concerns integer positions in the sorted transfer, so it remains true after capacities are rearranged. All selected labels are distinct.

Place the largest selected label first and the smallest last. The first belongs to category $r$ and makes an ascent since $L\le X_1$. Every later comparison of distinct labels is an ascent precisely when the next integer is larger than the preceding integer: the previous threshold equals that integer or that integer plus one. The first-to-middle and middle-to-last comparisons are descents. Hence a middle permutation with $k$ ascents gives exactly $k+1$ block ascents. All such paths have the increments~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:increments}. The last label belongs to category 1 and is less than the initial $X_1-b$, whereas $X'_1\ge X_1-b$, so $L'\le X'_1$. Multiplying the independent label choices by the Eulerian count proves~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:exactlower}.
\end{proof}
There is no sign restriction on the increments in this lemma. Positive drifts will only be needed for repeating a fixed initial block.

\subsection{Eulerian entropy without a local limit theorem}
Let $U$ be uniform on $[0,1]$, and define
\[
 M(t)=\mathbb E\ee^{tU}=\frac{\ee^t-1}{t},\quad M(0)=1,
 \qquad I(a)=\sup_{t\in\R}\{ta-\log M(t)\},\quad 0<a<1.
\]
The tilted mean $M'(t)/M(t)$ increases strictly from zero to one, since its derivative is the positive tilted variance. Thus there is a unique tilt with mean $a$.

\begin{lemma}\label{amc:bm:lem:eulerian}
For every fixed $a\in(0,1)$ there are integers $k_m=ma+O(m^{2/3})$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:eulerianentropy}
 \log A(m,k_m)\ge\log(m!)-mI(a)+o(m).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For independent uniforms $U_1,\ldots,U_m$ with sum $S_m$, the cube-slice identity is
\[
 \mathbb P(k\le S_m\le k+1)=A(m,k)/m!.
\]
Here is an algebraic verification. Inclusion--exclusion for the unit cube inside a simplex gives
\[
 \operatorname{vol}\{S_m\le t\}
 =\frac1{m!}\sum_{j=0}^m(-1)^j\binom mj(t-j)_+^m.
\]
Subtracting the values at $k+1$ and $k$ gives $m!^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}(-1)^j\binom{m+1}j(k+1-j)^m$. This is the Eulerian formula: it has the initial value $A(1,0)=1$ and, by substitution, satisfies
$A(m,k)=(k+1)A(m-1,k)+(m-k)A(m-1,k-1)$, obtained by inserting the largest element in a permutation. This proves the identity.

Tilt each uniform by density $\ee^{tu}/M(t)$, with mean $a$. Its variance is finite, so Chebyshev's inequality gives probability tending to one to $|S_m-ma|\le m^{2/3}$. This window meets $O(m^{2/3})$ unit bins. At least one has tilted probability at least a positive constant times $m^{-2/3}$. Returning to the original measure on that bin costs at most
\[
 \exp\{-tma+m\log M(t)-|t|O(m^{2/3})\}.
\]
The logarithm is $-mI(a)+o(m)$. Multiplying by $m!$ proves the claim. The bin is selected, not prescribed in advance.
\end{proof}

For positive $\bx$, choice proportions $p_j>0$ summing to one, and $a\in(0,1)$, put
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:entropy}
 \cL(\bx;\bp,a)=\sum_jp_j\log x_j-\sum_jp_j\log p_j-I(a).
\end{equation}
Let $b=\delta N+O(1)$, $X_j=Nx_j+o(N)$, and $q_j=bp_j+O(1)$ with their sum adjusted to $b$. Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:eulerian} supplies a middle-permutation bin with $k+1=ba+O(b^{2/3})$. Stirling's formula and~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:exactlower} give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:blockentropy}
 \log Q\ge b\log N+b\cL(\bx;\bp,a)
   -O_r\left(N\delta^2\sum_jx_j^{-1}\right)+o(N).
\end{equation}
Indeed, expanding the falling factorial in $\binom{Nx_j-2b+o(N)}{q_j}$ costs $O(b^2/(Nx_j))+o(N)$ when $\delta$ is small compared with $x_j$. The difference between $(b-2)!$ and $b!$ costs only $O(\log N)$. The estimate is uniform on positive compact subsets for a fixed finite partition of time.

\section{The characteristic and its initial boundary}\label{amc:bm:sec:calibration}
For positive $\bu=(u_1,\ldots,u_r)$, set $u_0=1$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:chi}
 P_{\bu}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^r\frac{u_js^{r-j}}{(r-j)!},\qquad
 \chi(\bu)=\int_0^\infty g(P_{\bu}(s))\dd s,
 \qquad D=\sum_{j=1}^r u_{j-1}\partial_{u_j}.
\end{equation}
This integral and the derivatives used below are locally uniformly convergent on the positive orthant. Indeed $P_{\bu}(s)$ has positive leading coefficient $1/r!$, while its derivatives are polynomials of degree at most $r-1$. Formula~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:gpositive} bounds derivatives of $g$, or direct differentiation gives the required integrable tails. Since $DP_{\bu}=\partial_sP_{\bu}$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:Dchi}
 D\chi=-g(u_r).
\end{equation}

Along $u_j(w)=E_j(w)$, the binomial identity gives $P_{E(w)}(s)=E_r(w+s)$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:path}
 \tau(w)=\mu_r\int_w^\infty g(E_r(z))\dd z,
 \qquad x_j(w)=-\mu_rE_j(w)\chi_{u_j}(E(w)).
\end{equation}
Then $\tau$ decreases from one to zero as $w$ increases from zero to infinity. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:positivepath}
 x_j(w)=\frac{\mu_rE_j(w)}{(r-j)!}
 \int_0^\infty[-g'(E_r(w+s))]s^{r-j}\dd s>0.
\end{equation}
The derivative is negative by~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:gpositive}, with $g'(1)=-1/2$.

\begin{lemma}\label{amc:bm:lem:path}
Writing derivatives with respect to $\tau$ by dots, define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:pa}
 p_j=\frac{(u_{j-1}/u_j)x_j}{\mu_rg(u_r)},\qquad
 a=-\frac{u_rg'(u_r)}{g(u_r)}
 =\frac{u_r}{u_r-1}-\frac1{\log u_r},
\end{equation}
with $a=1/2$ at $u_r=1$. Then every $p_j>0$, $\sum_jp_j=1$, $0<a<1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:drifts}
 \dot x_j=p_{j+1}-p_j\ (j<r),\qquad \dot x_r=a-p_r.
\end{equation}
If $\theta_j=\log u_j$, then $\theta(1)=0$ and, for every $\by\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:globalcal}
 \rho(\by;\bu(\tau))/\mu_r=-\dot\btheta(\tau)\cdot\by.
\end{equation}
On the path, $\rho(\bx;\bu)=\mu_r$ and $\dot\btheta\cdot\bx=-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:Dchi} gives the sum of the $p_j$. The value of $a$ is the mean of a uniform variable tilted by $t=\log u_r$, so it lies strictly between zero and one. Differentiating~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:Dchi} gives
\[
 D\chi_{u_j}+\chi_{u_{j+1}}=-\ind{j=r}g'(u_r),
 \qquad \chi_{u_{r+1}}=0.
\]
Use $u'_j(w)=u_{j-1}$ and $\tau'(w)=-\mu_rg(u_r)$ to differentiate~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:path}; the result is~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:drifts}. Also
\[
 \dot\theta_j=-\frac{u_{j-1}/u_j}{\mu_rg(u_r)},
\]
which proves~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:globalcal}. Substituting the sum of the $p_j$ proves the last claims.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The singular origin}
\begin{lemma}\label{amc:bm:lem:origin}
As $w\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \tau(w)&\sim\frac{\mu_r r!r}{r-1}w^{1-r}\log w,\label{amc:bm:eq:tauorigin}\\
 \frac{x_j(w)}{\tau(w)}&\longrightarrow
 \alpha_j=\frac{(r-1)r!(r+j-2)!}{j!(2r-1)!}>0,\label{amc:bm:eq:alpha}\\
 p_j(w)&\longrightarrow\frac{j\alpha_j}{r-1},\qquad
 \sum_jj\alpha_j=r-1.\notag
\end{align}
Furthermore $\bx(0)=0$, $\btheta(\tau)=O_r(\log(1/\tau))$, and $\btheta\cdot\bx\to0$ as $\tau\downarrow0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The elementary estimates
\[
 g(E_r(z))\sim\frac{r!r\log z}{z^r},\quad
 -g'(E_r(z))\sim\frac{(r!)^2r\log z}{z^{2r}},\quad
 E_j(w)\sim w^j/j!
\]
prove~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:tauorigin} by integration. In~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:positivepath}, substitute $s=wt$. After division by its leading power and logarithm, dominated convergence applies with a constant multiple of
$t^{r-j}(1+t)^{-2r}(1+\log(1+t))$, which is integrable. The beta integral gives
\[
 \frac1{(r-j)!}\int_0^\infty t^{r-j}(1+t)^{-2r}\dd t
 =\frac{(r+j-2)!}{(2r-1)!}.
\]
This proves~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:alpha}. Since $u_{j-1}/u_j\sim j/w$, the formula for $p_j$ follows; taking limits in $\sum p_j=1$ gives the displayed sum. The remaining conclusions follow from $x_j=O(w^{1-r}\log w)$, $\theta_j=O(\log w)$, and~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:tauorigin}. A crude power bound on the latter also directly gives $\log w=O(\log(1/\tau))$.
\end{proof}

For a small fixed $\varepsilon>0$, set $\boldsymbol\beta=\bx(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$. Define the straight-line controls $\boldsymbol q,a_0$ by
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:linecontrols}
 \beta_j=q_{j+1}-q_j\ (j<r),\quad
 \beta_r=a_0-q_r,\quad \sum_jq_j=1.
\end{equation}
They are unique: $q_j=q_1+\sum_{k<j}\beta_k$, and the sum fixes $q_1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{amc:bm:lem:prefixfeasible}
For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, every $\beta_j>0$, every $q_j>0$, and $0<a_0<1$. Moreover
\[
 1-a_0\sim\frac1{r\log w(\varepsilon)},\qquad
 \varepsilon\cL(\boldsymbol\beta;\boldsymbol q,a_0)\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Integrate~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:drifts} from zero to $\varepsilon$ and use $\bx(0)=0$. The unique controls in~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:linecontrols} are the time averages of $\bp$ and $a$. Their positivity and strict feasibility follow from Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:path}; positivity of $\beta_j$ follows from~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:alpha}. This avoids relying only on the limiting relation $a_0\to1$.

The exact identity
\[
 (1-a(v))g(v)=\frac1{v-1}-\frac{\log v}{(v-1)^2}
\]
shows that its composition with $v=E_r(z)$ has tail integral asymptotic to $r!w^{1-r}/(r-1)$. Divide by $\tau(w)/\mu_r$ in~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:tauorigin} to obtain the asserted average. For $a\ge1/2$, the maximizing tilt in $I(a)$ is nonnegative. Since $M(t)\ge\int_a^1\ee^{tu}\dd u\ge(1-a)\ee^{ta}$,
\[
 0\le I(a)\le\log\frac1{1-a}.
\]
Thus $I(a_0)=O(\log\log w)$. The other terms in~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:entropy} stay bounded because $\boldsymbol\beta$ and $\boldsymbol q$ tend to strictly positive vectors. Finally $\varepsilon\log\log w(\varepsilon)\to0$ by~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:tauorigin}.
\end{proof}

\section{Matching growth bounds}\label{amc:bm:sec:growth}
\subsection{Finite seeds with every category large}
For every fixed $B$ we need a reachable state with all coordinates much larger than $B$ and with $L\le X_1$. This requirement can be met by explicit words.

If $r\ge3$, take $M$ large and begin with $0,1,\ldots,(r-1)M-1$. Partition these labels into $r-1$ groups of $M$ labels. For each target residual capacity $j=1,\ldots,r-1$, append that group in increasing order exactly $r-1-j$ times. All appended labels were already available, and each appears at most $r-1$ times overall. The final counts are $X_j=M$ for every $j<r$. The group destined for capacity 1 produces at least $M-1$ additional ascents, so $X_r\ge M$. Histogram-preserving compaction gives a canonical path to such counts. Selecting compact label zero once then gives $L=0$ and leaves every category count at least $M-1$.

For $r=2$, use the three increasing runs
\[
 0,\ldots,M;\qquad 0,\ldots,M;\qquad M+1,\ldots,2M+1,
\]
then append $M+1$. The result is an ascent sequence with each multiplicity at most two. Its terminal state is $X_1=M$, $X_2=M+1$, $L=0$. These seeds have length independent of the later word length $N$.

\subsection{A repeated straight-line prefix}
Fix a sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ and the feasible controls of Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:prefixfeasible}. Choose a large but fixed block length $B$. Pick integers $q_j^{(B)}=Bq_j+O(1)$ summing to $B$, and choose the favorable Eulerian bin from Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:eulerian} at mean $a_0$. The resulting increments
\[
 d_j=q_{j+1}^{(B)}-q_j^{(B)}\ (j<r),\qquad
 d_r=k_B+1-q_r^{(B)}
\]
satisfy $d_j/B\to\beta_j$. They are all strictly positive for sufficiently large $B$. Start with a sufficiently large fixed seed and repeat this block. Every block ends with $L\le X_1$, so Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:lowerblock} applies uniformly to all paths produced.

At block number $\ell$, $X_j=\ell d_j+O_B(1)$. Its exact lower count is therefore
\[
 \ell^B C_B(1+O_B(1/\ell)),\qquad
 C_B=A(B-2,k_B)\prod_j\frac{d_j^{q_j^{(B)}}}{q_j^{(B)}!}.
\]
Taking products over $m$ blocks gives logarithm
$B\log(m!)+m\log C_B+O_B(\log m)$. Stirling's formula yields the factorial-normalized rate per letter
\[
 \frac{\log C_B}{B}-\log B
 \longrightarrow\cL(\boldsymbol\beta;\boldsymbol q,a_0)
 \quad(B\to\infty).
\]
A prefix of length $\varepsilon N+O_B(1)$ consequently contributes, after subtracting its $\varepsilon N\log N$ term, the action
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:prefixaction}
 \varepsilon\log\varepsilon-\varepsilon
 +\varepsilon\cL(\boldsymbol\beta;\boldsymbol q,a_0),
\end{equation}
in the successive limits $N\to\infty$ and $B\to\infty$. This tends to zero as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

At fixed $B$ the actual prefix slopes are $\boldsymbol\beta_B=\boldsymbol d/B$, not exactly $\boldsymbol\beta$. Translate the nominal later path by $\varepsilon(\boldsymbol\beta_B-\boldsymbol\beta)$. For large enough fixed $B$ it remains in a positive compact region. Its velocities do not change, and its action converges uniformly to that of the desired path. This explicitly handles rounded or irrational endpoints.

\subsection{The lower bound}
On the characteristic, substitute~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:pa} into~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:entropy}. The equality $I(a)=a\log v+\log g(v)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:actionidentity}
 \cL(\bx;\bp,a)=\log\mu_r-\btheta\cdot\dot\bx.
\end{equation}
For example, the categorical part equals
$\log\mu_r+\log g(v)-\sum_jp_j\log(u_{j-1}/u_j)$;
its remaining mark contribution is exactly $\btheta\cdot\dot\bx$ by~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:drifts}. Integration by parts and Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:path} give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:suffixaction}
 \int_\varepsilon^1\cL(\bx;\bp,a)\dd\tau
 =(1-\varepsilon)(\log\mu_r-1)
   +\btheta(\varepsilon)\cdot\bx(\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
The right side tends to $\log\mu_r-1$.

To realize this action with finite paths, partition $[\varepsilon,1]$ into a fixed finite mesh. On each interval take the averages of $p_j$ and $a$. The drift equations are linear, so these averages give exactly the nominal endpoint increment. Round the category counts to integers and choose the favorable Eulerian bin. Every block then has a fixed category-vector endpoint, apart from the accumulated $o(N)$ errors from bin selection and rounding; its final threshold always satisfies $L\le X_1$. The errors are $o(N)$ because the partition is fixed before $N$ grows. The path remains in a positive compact set, where entropy is uniformly continuous. Applying~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:blockentropy} and multiplying gives the sum of the interval actions, with error $O_{r,\varepsilon}(N\delta)+o(N)$ as the mesh $\delta$ decreases.

A precise order of limits is: fix $\varepsilon$, fix an accurate prefix block $B$, fix a sufficiently fine finite later mesh, let $N\to\infty$, refine the mesh, improve $B$, and finally let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The last two finite refinements can be interchanged on the positive compact region. Any bounded number of leftover positions is filled only at the very end by new maxima; the extension is injective and changes the normalized logarithm by $o(N)$. We do not need a uniform estimate as $B$ grows with $N$.

Combining~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:prefixaction} and~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:suffixaction} proves
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\frac{c_{N,r}}{N!}\ge\log\mu_r,
\]
since $\log N!=N\log N-N+o(N)$.

\subsection{The upper block telescope}
Fix $\varepsilon>0$. On $[\varepsilon,1]$ the mark curve and its first two derivatives are bounded. Partition the steps from $k=\lceil\varepsilon N\rceil$ to $N$ into finitely many blocks of lengths $b\le\delta N$. At the start of one block write $\btheta=\btheta(\tau)$ and $\Delta\btheta=\btheta(\tau+b/N)-\btheta(\tau)$. Since $|X'_j-X_j|\le b$,~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:blockupper} implies
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{\text{paths}}\ee^{\btheta(\tau+b/N)\cdot\bX'}
 &\le\ee^{\btheta\cdot\bX+\Delta\btheta\cdot\bX+b\|\Delta\btheta\|_1}
       \max(v,v^{-1})\Lambda^b.
\end{align*}
Use $\log z\le z-1$ on $z=\Lambda/(N\mu_r)$, together with~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:globalcal}, to get
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:essentiale}
 \Lambda^b\le(N\mu_r/\ee)^b
 \exp\left\{-\frac bN\dot\btheta(\tau)\cdot\bX
       +C_{r,\varepsilon}\frac{b(b+1)}N\right\}.
\end{equation}
The factor $\ee^{-b}$ is essential: it is the constant $-1$ in $\log z\le z-1$.

Taylor's theorem gives $\Delta\btheta=(b/N)\dot\btheta+O_{r,\varepsilon}(b^2/N^2)$ and $\|\Delta\btheta\|_1=O_{r,\varepsilon}(b/N)$. Since $\|\bX\|_1\le N+1$, the linear state terms cancel. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:telescopeblock}
 \sum_{\text{paths}}\ee^{\btheta(\tau+b/N)\cdot\bX'}
 \le\ee^{\btheta(\tau)\cdot\bX}(N\mu_r/\ee)^b
       \exp\{C_{r,\varepsilon}(b^2/N+1)\}.
\end{equation}
This is uniform even at zero coordinates.

Iterate, using $\btheta(1)=0$. There are at most $k!$ initial prefixes: at position $i$ the ascent bound allows at most $i$ choices, so every ascent sequence is bounded by this elementary factorial count. The initial mark is at most $\exp((k+1)\|\btheta(k/N)\|_1)$. For a fixed partition with $J$ blocks,
\[
 c_{N,r}\le k!\exp((k+1)\|\btheta(k/N)\|_1)
       (N\mu_r/\ee)^{N-k}\exp\{C_{r,\varepsilon}(N\delta+J)\}.
\]
After division by $N!$ and Stirling's formula,
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\frac{c_{N,r}}{N!}
 \le\varepsilon\log\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)\log\mu_r
   +\varepsilon\|\btheta(\varepsilon)\|_1+C_{r,\varepsilon}\delta.
\]
First let $\delta\downarrow0$ at fixed $\varepsilon$, then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:origin} makes the prefix term vanish. This proves the matching upper bound and the root assertion of Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:growth}.

\section{Weighted growth and endpoint laws}\label{amc:bm:sec:ratio}
Define the histogram polynomial
\[
 H_N(\bu)=\sum_{\text{length-}N\text{ compact paths}}\prod_{j=1}^r u_j^{X_j}.
\]
By Proposition~\ref{amc:bm:prop:recurrence}, this is also the corresponding original-word histogram polynomial. All marks in this section are fixed positive real numbers, independent of $N$.

\begin{theorem}[Full weighted growth]\label{amc:bm:thm:pressure}
For every $\bu\in(0,\infty)^r$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:pressure}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\log\frac{H_N(\bu)}{N!}
 =-\log\chi(\bu).
\end{equation}
The function $F(\boldsymbol q)=-\log\chi(\ee^{q_1},\ldots,\ee^{q_r})$ is finite and smooth on all of $\R^r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix terminal marks $\bu^0>0$, and put $\nu=1/\chi(\bu^0)$. Replace the earlier mark flow by
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:generalflow}
 u_j(w)=\sum_{\ell=0}^j\frac{u^0_{j-\ell}w^\ell}{\ell!},\quad u^0_0=1,
 \qquad Q(w)=u_r(w)=P_{\bu^0}(w),\quad
 \tau(w)=\nu\int_w^\infty g(Q(z))\dd z.
\end{equation}
All marks stay positive, $u_j'=u_{j-1}$, and $P_{\bu(w)}(s)=Q(w+s)$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:generalpath}
 x_j(w)=-\nu u_j(w)\chi_{u_j}(\bu(w))
 =\frac{\nu u_j(w)}{(r-j)!}\int_w^\infty
 (z-w)^{r-j}[-g'(Q(z))]\dd z>0.
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:pa}--\eqref{amc:bm:eq:globalcal} hold with $\nu$ in place of $\mu_r$, while $\btheta(1)=\log\bu^0$. In particular the legal drift controls satisfy $p_j>0$, $\sum p_j=1$, and $0<a<1$ even when $v=u_r<1$. On every fixed cutoff interval they are bounded away from their respective boundaries.

The leading terms $u_j(w)\sim w^j/j!$ and $Q(w)\sim w^r/r!$ are unchanged. The dominated beta-integral calculation in Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:origin} therefore gives the same initial slopes $\alpha_j$, with $\nu$ replacing $\mu_r$ in the time scale. Averaging the positive controls still gives a strictly feasible positive-drift prefix. The identity used in Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:lem:prefixfeasible} still proves its vanishing action. Although the terminal ascent fraction can be below $1/2$, the initial one tends to one; the Eulerian bin argument permits all interior fractions anyway.

For the upper bound,~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:blockupper} already holds on both sides of $v=1$. The calibration gives
$\rho(\by;\bu)/\nu=-\dot\btheta\cdot\by$. The exact calculation~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:essentiale}--\eqref{amc:bm:eq:telescopeblock} therefore yields
\[
 \sum_{\text{paths}}\ee^{\btheta_{\rm next}\cdot\bX'}
 \le\ee^{\btheta\cdot\bX}(N\nu/\ee)^b
       \exp\{C_{\varepsilon,\bu^0}(b^2/N+1)\}.
\]
No sign condition on $\btheta$ is used: its increments and the initial mark are bounded by absolute values. The terminal weight is now $(\bu^0)^{\bX}$. Summation over the factorially bounded prefix and the same nested limits give the upper bound $-\log\chi(\bu^0)$.

For the lower bound use the explicit seeds, repeated prefix blocks, and buffered later blocks of Section~\ref{amc:bm:sec:growth} on the path~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:generalpath}. At fixed cutoff and fixed prefix size $B$, translate the later path by $\Delta_B=\varepsilon(\boldsymbol\beta_B-\boldsymbol\beta)$. The terminal normalized vector is uniformly $\bx(1)+\Delta_B+o(1)$ over every path selected by this construction. Hence its terminal logarithmic weight divided by $N$ is
\[
 \log\bu^0\cdot(\bx(1)+\Delta_B)+o(1).
\]
This is a two-sided estimate and remains valid for marks below one: the absolute error is bounded by $\|\log\bu^0\|_1$ times the maximum coordinate error. Final deterministic padding changes it by only $O(1/N)$.

The action identity is now $\cL=\log\nu-\btheta\cdot\dot\bx$, so
\[
 \int_\varepsilon^1\cL\dd\tau
 =(1-\varepsilon)(\log\nu-1)
 -\log\bu^0\cdot\bx(1)
 +\btheta(\varepsilon)\cdot\bx(\varepsilon).
\]
The negative terminal term cancels the positive terminal weight. First let $N$ grow with all finite choices fixed, then refine the later partition and improve $B$, then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The origin and prefix terms vanish exactly as before. After factorial normalization, this gives the lower bound $\log\nu$, proving~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:pressure}.

Finally, a mixed derivative of order $k$ of $\chi$ has integrand
\[
 g^{(k)}(P_{\bu}(s))
 \prod_{\ell=1}^k\frac{s^{r-j_\ell}}{(r-j_\ell)!}.
\]
Uniformly on a compact positive mark neighborhood, its tail is
$O(\log s/s^{r+\sum_\ell j_\ell})$, and its bounded-interval part is smooth and dominated. Differentiation of every finite order under the integral is valid. Thus $\chi$ is positive and smooth, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Concentration and the consecutive ratio}
Let $\mathbb P_N$ be the uniform endpoint law and put $Y_N=\bX/N$. Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:pressure} implies
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:logmgf}
 \Lambda(\boldsymbol q):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
 \log\mathbb E_N\ee^{N\boldsymbol q\cdot Y_N}
 =F(\boldsymbol q)-F(0).
\end{equation}
In particular it is a finite differentiable convex function: convexity also follows as the pointwise limit of the finite logarithmic moment-generating functions. At zero,
\[
 \partial_jF(0)=-\mu_r\chi_{u_j}(1)=b_j.
\]
Equation~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:Dchi} at the all-one point gives $\sum_jb_j=\mu_r$.

For any $\eta>0$, differentiability makes
$F(te_j)-F(0)-t(b_j+\eta)<0$ for sufficiently small $t>0$. Markov's inequality gives an exponentially small upper tail for $X_j/N\ge b_j+\eta$. The same argument with $t<0$ gives the lower tail. A finite union bound proves exponential concentration of $Y_N$ at $\boldsymbol b$. Uniform boundedness of $Y_N$ also gives convergence in mean.

From a state there are exactly $M=\sum_jX_j$ possible next labels. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:extension}
 \frac{c_{N+1,r}}{c_{N,r}}=\mathbb E_N M.
\end{equation}
Since $M/N\to\sum_jb_j=\mu_r$ in mean, this proves the ratio limit in Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:growth}. The original-word identities~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:histrelations} yield~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:multlimits}; their fixed affine transformations preserve the exponential concentration. This proves Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:endpoints}.

\subsection{A full large-deviation principle}
The weighted result gives more than concentration. We include an ordinary finite-dimensional proof, so no unstated boundary version of a large-deviation theorem is needed.

\begin{theorem}\label{amc:bm:thm:LDP}
The laws of $Y_N$ satisfy a full large-deviation principle of speed $N$ with good convex rate
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:rate}
 \mathcal I_r(\bx)=\sup_{\boldsymbol q\in\R^r}
 \{\boldsymbol q\cdot\bx-F(\boldsymbol q)+F(0)\}.
\end{equation}
For every open $G$ and closed $C$ in $\R^r$,
\begin{align*}
 \liminf_N N^{-1}\log\mathbb P_N(Y_N\in G)&\ge-\inf_G\mathcal I_r,\\
 \limsup_N N^{-1}\log\mathbb P_N(Y_N\in C)&\le-\inf_C\mathcal I_r.
\end{align*}
Its unique zero is $\boldsymbol b=\nabla F(0)$. Its effective domain lies in
$S=\{\bx\ge0:\sum_jx_j\le1\}$; no claim that all of $S$ has finite rate is made.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $\Lambda=F-F(0)$. The conjugate~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:rate} is lower semicontinuous and convex. The finite supports satisfy $Y_N\ge0$ and $\sum_jY_{N,j}\le1+1/N$. Thus
$\Lambda(\boldsymbol q)\le\sup_{\bx\in S}\boldsymbol q\cdot\bx$.
Separation followed by positive scaling shows $\mathcal I_r=+\infty$ outside $S$. Its closed sublevel sets are therefore compact.

For the upper bound, fix $K<\infty$ and $\eta>0$. At each $\bx$ in the compact set $C\cap[0,2]^r$, choose $\boldsymbol q$ with
\[
 \boldsymbol q\cdot\bx-\Lambda(\boldsymbol q)
 \ge\min(\mathcal I_r(\bx),K)-\eta.
\]
A neighborhood $U_{\bx}$ satisfies the same inequality with $2\eta$ for every point in it. Markov's inequality and~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:logmgf} bound the probability of $Y_N\in U_{\bx}$ by the corresponding exponential rate. A finite subcover exists; taking the largest exponential term yields
\[
 \limsup_N N^{-1}\log\mathbb P_N(Y_N\in C)
 \le-\min(\inf_C\mathcal I_r,K)+2\eta.
\]
All supports lie in $[0,2]^r$, so no probability was omitted. Send $\eta\downarrow0$ and $K\to\infty$.

For the lower bound, first take $\bx=\nabla\Lambda(\boldsymbol q)$. Tilt the law by density
$\ee^{N\boldsymbol q\cdot Y_N}/\mathbb E_N\ee^{N\boldsymbol q\cdot Y_N}$.
Its limiting logarithmic moment-generating function is
$\Lambda(\boldsymbol q+\boldsymbol h)-\Lambda(\boldsymbol q)$, differentiable at zero. The coordinate Chernoff argument just given proves concentration under the tilt at $\bx$. Changing back to the original law on a radius-$\delta$ ball around $\bx$ gives
\[
 \liminf_N N^{-1}\log\mathbb P_N(Y_N\in B_\delta(\bx))
 \ge\Lambda(\boldsymbol q)-\boldsymbol q\cdot\bx
       -\|\boldsymbol q\|\delta.
\]
Convexity makes $\mathcal I_r(\bx)=\boldsymbol q\cdot\bx-\Lambda(\boldsymbol q)$. If an open set contains $\bx$, send $\delta\downarrow0$ inside that set.

It remains to cover finite-rate points that might not themselves be gradients. Fix $\bx$ with $\mathcal I_r(\bx)<\infty$. For $\epsilon>0$ maximize
\[
 \boldsymbol q\cdot\bx-\Lambda(\boldsymbol q)
        -\tfrac\epsilon2\|\boldsymbol q\|^2.
\]
A maximizer $\boldsymbol q_\epsilon$ exists: the affine tangent lower bound on $\Lambda$ makes the negative quadratic dominate at infinity. Let its maximum be $J_\epsilon$. Then $J_\epsilon\uparrow\mathcal I_r(\bx)$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$, and
\[
 \tfrac\epsilon2\|\boldsymbol q_\epsilon\|^2
 \le\mathcal I_r(\bx)-J_\epsilon\longrightarrow0.
\]
The first-order equation gives
$\bx_\epsilon=\nabla\Lambda(\boldsymbol q_\epsilon)
=\bx-\epsilon\boldsymbol q_\epsilon\to\bx$. At these gradient points,
\[
 \mathcal I_r(\bx_\epsilon)
 =J_\epsilon-\tfrac\epsilon2\|\boldsymbol q_\epsilon\|^2
 \longrightarrow\mathcal I_r(\bx).
\]
Every open neighborhood of $\bx$ eventually contains $\bx_\epsilon$, so its lower bound follows from the gradient case. Taking the infimum over an arbitrary open set proves the full lower bound, including finite-rate boundary points.

The tangent inequality for $\Lambda$ gives $\mathcal I_r(\boldsymbol b)=0$. If $\bx\ne\boldsymbol b$, a sufficiently small dual vector in the direction $\bx-\boldsymbol b$ makes $\boldsymbol q\cdot\bx-\Lambda(\boldsymbol q)>0$, by differentiability at zero. This proves uniqueness.
\end{proof}

For a fixed terminal tilt $\bu^0=\ee^{\boldsymbol q^0}$, the same proof gives the rate
\[
 \mathcal I_{r,\boldsymbol q^0}(\bx)
 =\mathcal I_r(\bx)-\boldsymbol q^0\cdot\bx+F(\boldsymbol q^0)-F(0),
\]
with unique typical endpoint
\[
 b_j(\bu^0)=\partial_jF(\boldsymbol q^0)
 =-\frac{u_j^0\chi_{u_j}(\bu^0)}{\chi(\bu^0)}=x_j(1).
\]
Continuous affine images give rate functions obtained by minimizing $\mathcal I_r$ over their fibers, for the original multiplicities, distinct values, and ascents. This follows directly by applying the open- and closed-set bounds to inverse images; compact support handles closed images. The $2/N$ correction for ascents vanishes deterministically and does not change the bounds.

Smooth limiting pressure alone does not establish convergence of its finite-size second derivatives, a central limit theorem, a local limit theorem, or a coefficient prefactor. Those are separate problems.

\section{Monotonicity and inversion}\label{amc:bm:sec:inverse}
\begin{lemma}\label{amc:bm:lem:monotonicity}
One has $c_{n+1,r}>c_{n,r}$ for every $n\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The maximum of an ascent sequence is at most its total number of ascents. This follows inductively: a new maximum is necessarily reached by an ascent, and its value is at most one plus the previous ascent count. If a word has maximum $M$, append the fresh value $M+1$. The ascent bound permits it, and it cannot create an occurrence beyond the cap of any value. Removing the last entry recovers the original word, so this is an injection $\cA_{n,r}\to\cA_{n+1,r}$. Its image ends in a positive new maximum. The valid word
\[
 0,1,\ldots,n-1,0
\]
of length $n+1$ ends in zero, and is outside the image. For $n=1$ this word is $00$. Thus the inequality is strict. The equality $c_{0,r}=c_{1,r}=1$ is the only initial tie relevant here.
\end{proof}

Fix $r\ge2$. For real $y>1$ define the threshold
\[
 N_r(y)=\min\{n\ge0:c_{n,r}\ge y\},\qquad Y=\log y.
\]
Let $W_0$ denote the positive real branch of Lambert's function, characterized by $W_0(z)\ee^{W_0(z)}=z$ for $z>0$.
\begin{corollary}\label{amc:bm:cor:inverse}
As $y\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:inverseW}
 N_r(y)=\frac{Y}{W_0(\mu_r Y/\ee)}
      +o\left(\frac{Y}{(\log Y)^2}\right).
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:inversetwo}
 N_r(y)=\frac{Y}{\log Y}
 +\frac{Y(\log\log Y+1-\log\mu_r)}{(\log Y)^2}
 +o\left(\frac{Y}{(\log Y)^2}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 F(x)=x\log(\mu_r x/\ee).
\]
Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:growth} and Stirling's formula give $\log c_{n,r}=F(n)+o(n)$. If $N=N_r(y)$, the threshold inequalities
\[
 \log c_{N-1,r}<Y\le\log c_{N,r}
\]
and $F(N)-F(N-1)=O(\log N)$ imply
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:eq:thresholdresidual}
 Y=F(N)+o(N).
\end{equation}
This does not require a coefficient-ratio estimate. It follows first that $N\sim Y/\log Y$.

The positive solution $\nu$ of $F(\nu)=Y$ is exactly
\[
 \nu=Y/W_0(\mu_r Y/\ee).
\]
Indeed, writing $w=W_0(\mu_r Y/\ee)$ gives $\mu_r\nu/\ee=\ee^w$ and $\nu w=Y$. Also $\nu\sim Y/\log Y$, so $N/\nu\to1$. On the interval between them,
\[
 F'(x)=\log(\mu_r x)\sim\log Y.
\]
The mean value theorem applied to~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:thresholdresidual} now gives
\[
 N-\nu=o(N/\log Y)=o(Y/(\log Y)^2),
\]
proving~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:inverseW}. Finally, its defining relation $w+\log w=\log Y+\log\mu_r-1$ first gives $w\sim\log Y$. Substituting this estimate once more gives
\[
 W_0(\mu_r Y/\ee)
 =\log Y-\log\log Y+\log\mu_r-1
   +O\left(\frac{\log\log Y}{\log Y}\right).
\]
Expanding the reciprocal yields~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:inversetwo}; its next explicit error is
$O(Y(\log\log Y)^2/(\log Y)^3)$, which is smaller than the error already allowed in~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:inverseW}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The factor in the Lambert argument is $\mu_r Y/\ee$. It follows directly from $F(x)=x\log(\mu_r x/\ee)$; replacing it by $Y/(\ee\mu_r)$ would change the second term incorrectly. The error in~\eqref{amc:bm:eq:inverseW} is generally much larger than one, so neither a floor nor a ceiling of the displayed approximation is justified as an exact threshold rule.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Derivative bounds and a continuous cap parameter}
Recall $g(v)=\log v/(v-1)$, with its removable value $g(1)=1$. Its integral representation gives, for $v>0$ and integers $m\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:deriv-integral}
 \frac{(-1)^m}{m!}g^{(m)}(v)
 =\int_0^1\frac{t^m}{[1+t(v-1)]^{m+1}}\dd t>0.
\end{equation}
For $v\ge1$, $tv\le1+t(v-1)$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:deriv-bound}
 \frac{|g^{(m)}(v)|}{m!}\le\frac{g(v)}{v^m}
 \le\frac{1+\log v}{v^{m+1}}.
\end{equation}
The second inequality follows from
$vg(v)=\log v+\log v/(v-1)\le1+\log v$.
These estimates include $v=1$ and require no exception near $x=0$.

It is useful to extend the parameter to real $R>2$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:continuous}
 \mathcal E(R,x)=\ee^x\frac{\Gamma(R,x)}{\Gamma(R)},\qquad
 \delta_R(x)=\ee^x\frac{\gamma(R,x)}{\Gamma(R)},\qquad
 \mathcal J(R)=\int_0^\infty g(\mathcal E(R,x))\dd x.
\end{equation}
Here $\Gamma(R,x)$ and $\gamma(R,x)$ are the upper and lower incomplete gamma functions. At integer $R=r+1$, $\mathcal E(R,x)=E_r(x)$ and $\mathcal J(R)=J_r$.
For $R\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:E-lower}
 \mathcal E(R,x)=\frac1{\Gamma(R)}\int_0^\infty(x+u)^{R-1}\ee^{-u}\dd u
 \ge\max\left(1,\frac{x^{R-1}}{\Gamma(R)}\right).
\end{equation}
For each fixed $R$, $\mathcal E(R,x)\sim x^{R-1}/\Gamma(R)$ as $x\to\infty$, by dominated convergence in this integral. Thus $g(\mathcal E(R,x))=O(\log x/x^{R-1})$, and $\mathcal J(R)$ is finite for $R>2$.

The gamma convolution identity couples a gamma random variable of shape $R+h$ as the sum of independent gamma variables of shapes $R$ and $h$. Consequently $\Gamma(R,x)/\Gamma(R)$, a gamma upper-tail probability, is strictly increasing in $R$ for every $x>0$. Hence $\mathcal J(R)$ is strictly decreasing. Continuity follows by dominated convergence on compact $R$-intervals, dominated by the integrable value at the smallest parameter. Also $\mathcal E(R,x)<\ee^x$, so $\mathcal J(R)>A$. As $R\to\infty$, the gamma lower tail at fixed $x$ tends to zero, whence
\[
 \mathcal J(R)\downarrow\int_0^\infty\frac{x}{\ee^x-1}\dd x
 =\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-2}=A.
\]
The interchange in the last equality is Tonelli's theorem. The continuous extension is an analytic device; no combinatorial interpretation of a noninteger cap is asserted.

Expanding $\ee^{x(1-t)}$ in a beta integral gives the identity
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:F-factor}
 \delta_R(x)=\frac{x^R}{\Gamma(R+1)}F_R(x),\qquad
 F_R(x)=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{x^k}{(R+1)_k}.
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
 \delta_R(x)=\frac{x^R}{\Gamma(R)}\int_0^1t^{R-1}\ee^{x(1-t)}\dd t,
\]
and the termwise integrals are beta integrals. For any fixed $0<\alpha<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:F-bound}
 F_R(x)\le(1-\alpha)^{-1}\qquad(0\le x\le\alpha R).
\end{equation}
Moreover, if $G\sim\GammaDist(R,\text{ rate }1)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:gamma-lower}
 \ee^{-x}\delta_R(x)=\Pr(G\le x)
 \le\exp[-R(\alpha-1-\log\alpha)]\quad(x\le\alpha R).
\end{equation}
To prove this, apply Markov's inequality to $\ee^{-sG}$ and use
$\mathbb E\ee^{-sG}=(1+s)^{-R}$ with $s=1/\alpha-1$. Thus $\mathcal E(R,x)\ge\ee^x/2$ uniformly below $\alpha R$ for all sufficiently large $R$.

\subsection{Exact positive sectors and their finite remainders}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:T-def}
 T_m(R)=\frac{(-1)^m}{m!}\int_0^\infty
 g^{(m)}(\ee^x)\delta_R(x)^m\dd x\qquad(m\ge1).
\end{equation}
Each sector is positive and finite: its integrand is at most $(1+x)\ee^{-x}$ by \eqref{amc:bm:cap:deriv-bound} and $\delta_R\le\ee^x$.
For $v=\ee^x$, $D=1+t(v-1)$, and $\delta=\delta_R(x)$,
\[
 \frac1{D-t\delta}=\frac1D\sum_{m\ge0}\left(\frac{t\delta}{D}\right)^m,
 \qquad 0\le\frac{t\delta}{D}\le\frac\delta v<1.
\]
All terms are nonnegative. Integrating first in $t$ and then in $x$, Tonelli's theorem proves the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:positive-series}
 \mathcal J(R)-A=\sum_{m\ge1}T_m(R).
\end{equation}
In particular every finite remainder is nonnegative and is bounded pointwise by $g(\mathcal E(R,x))$ before integration.

The first sector is explicit for every real $R>0$. Put
$P(R,x)=\gamma(R,x)/\Gamma(R)$. Integration by parts gives
\begin{align}
 T_1(R)
 &=-\int_0^\infty\frac\dd{\dd x}g(\ee^x)P(R,x)\dd x\notag\\
 &=\frac1{\Gamma(R)}\int_0^\infty
       \frac{x^R\ee^{-x}}{\ee^x-1}\dd x
 =R\bigl(\zeta(R+1)-1\bigr)=:L(R).\label{amc:bm:cap:linear}
\end{align}
The boundary terms vanish because $P(R,0)=0$ and $g(\ee^x)\to0$ at infinity; expanding $(\ee^x-1)^{-1}$ then gives the zeta identity by Tonelli. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:L0}
 L(R)=L_0(R)+O(R3^{-R}),\qquad L_0(R)=R2^{-R-1}.
\end{equation}

Set
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:a-C}
 a_m=\left(\frac m{m+1}\right)^m,\qquad
 C_m=(2\pi)^{(1-m)/2}m^{3/2}(m+1)^{m-2}.
\end{equation}
The bases $a_m$ decrease to $\ee^{-1}$ and are all strictly larger than $\ee^{-1}$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-sector remainder]\label{amc:bm:cap:remainder-prop}
For every fixed $M\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:remainder}
 \mathcal J(R)-A=\sum_{m=1}^M T_m(R)
 +O_M\!\left(R^{(2-M)/2}a_{M+1}^{R}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\alpha<1$ so close to $1$ that $1/(\alpha\ee)<a_{M+1}$. Below $\alpha R$, Taylor's theorem and \eqref{amc:bm:cap:deriv-bound}, with $\mathcal E(R,x)\ge\ee^x/2$, bound the remainder after $M$ terms by
\[
 2^{M+2}(1+x)\ee^{-(M+2)x}\delta_R(x)^{M+1}.
\]
Use \eqref{amc:bm:cap:F-factor}--\eqref{amc:bm:cap:F-bound} and extend the resulting integral to infinity. Up to a fixed multiplicative constant its integral is
\[
 \frac1{\Gamma(R+1)^{M+1}}
 \int_0^\infty(1+x)x^{(M+1)R}\ee^{-(M+2)x}\dd x
 =O_M\!\left(R^{(2-M)/2}a_{M+1}^{R}\right),
\]
by the gamma integral and Stirling's formula.
For the upper tail, \eqref{amc:bm:cap:deriv-bound} and \eqref{amc:bm:cap:E-lower} imply, when $R>3$ and $\alpha R\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:upper-tail}
 \int_{\alpha R}^\infty g(\mathcal E(R,x))\dd x
 \le\frac{2\Gamma(R)(\alpha R)^{3-R}}{R-3}
 =O_\alpha\!\left(R^{3/2}(\alpha\ee)^{-R}\right).
\end{equation}
Here $\log\mathcal E(R,x)\le x$ was used. The strict exponential inequality in the choice of $\alpha$ absorbs this tail into the required bound. Positivity of the exact sector series bounds the tail of the remainder by this same integral.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform tail-factor expansions to every order}
Let $h_\ell(1,\ldots,k)$ denote the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree $\ell$ in $1,\ldots,k$, with $h_0=1$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:f-coeff}
 f_\ell(y)=(-1)^\ell\sum_{k\ge0}h_\ell(1,\ldots,k)y^k.
\end{equation}
For the empty alphabet, $h_\ell=0$ when $\ell>0$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform expansion of the tail factor]\label{amc:bm:cap:F-expand}
For fixed $0<\alpha<1$ and nonnegative integers $q,d$, as real $R\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:F-allorders}
 F_R(Ry)=\sum_{\ell=0}^qf_\ell(y)R^{-\ell}
 +O_{\alpha,q,d}(R^{-q-1})
\end{equation}
uniformly in the $C^d$ norm on $0\le y\le\alpha$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $p_k(z)=\prod_{j=1}^k(1+jz)^{-1}$ and $z\ge0$, the product rule gives
\[
 \frac{(-1)^u p_k^{(u)}(z)}{u!}
 =\sum_{b_1+\cdots+b_k=u}\prod_{j=1}^k
   \frac{j^{b_j}}{(1+jz)^{b_j+1}}
 \le h_u(1,\ldots,k).
\]
Taylor's theorem at $z=0$ therefore bounds the error through degree $q$, at $z=1/R$, by
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:F-error}
 R^{-q-1}h_{q+1}(1,\ldots,k)
 \le R^{-q-1}\left(\frac{k(k+1)}2\right)^{q+1}.
\end{equation}
Multiply by $y^k$ and sum. Differentiating $d$ times in $y$ adds only a fixed polynomial in $k$ to the geometrically summable majorant. This proves the stated uniform remainder.
\end{proof}
The coefficient $h_\ell(1,\ldots,k)$ is a polynomial in $k$ of degree at most $2\ell$, computable from
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:h-recursion}
 \ell h_\ell=\sum_{u=1}^\ell S_u(k)h_{\ell-u},\qquad
 S_u(k)=\sum_{j=1}^k j^u.
\end{equation}
Thus $f_\ell$ is rational, with denominator dividing $(1-y)^{2\ell+1}$. An effective construction replaces each power of $k$ by the same power of the Euler operator $y\,\dd/\dd y$ acting on $(1-y)^{-1}$, and multiplies by $(-1)^\ell$. For example,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:first-f}
 f_0=\frac1{1-y},\qquad f_1=-\frac y{(1-y)^3},\qquad
 f_2=\frac{y(1+2y)}{(1-y)^5}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Gamma concentration and the sector saddle}
For $v>1$, differentiate the normally convergent series $g(v)=\sum_{k\ge1}(\log v)v^{-k}$. The differentiated summand is
\[
 \frac{(-1)^m}{m!}\frac{\dd^m}{\dd v^m}[(\log v)v^{-k}]
 =\binom{k+m-1}{m}v^{-k-m}
 \left(\log v-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac1{k+j}\right).
\]
The $k=1$ term contains $H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m1/j$. The remaining coefficients grow only polynomially in $k$, so for $x\ge\log2$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:deriv-asym}
 \frac{(-1)^m}{m!}g^{(m)}(\ee^x)
 =\ee^{-(m+1)x}(x-H_m)
 +O_m((1+x)\ee^{-(m+2)x}).
\end{equation}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:gamma-scale}
 \begin{gathered}
 I_{R,m}=\frac{\Gamma(mR+2)}{(m+1)^{mR+2}\Gamma(R+1)^m},\qquad
 y_m=\frac m{m+1},\\
 X\sim\GammaDist(mR+2,\text{ rate }m+1),\qquad Y=X/R.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The gamma moment-generating function yields exponential concentration of $Y$ at $y_m$. More explicitly, for $c\ne y_m$, the appropriate one-sided probability is at most
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:gamma-concentration}
 K_{m,c}\ee^{-R\mathcal I_m(c)},\qquad
 \mathcal I_m(c)=(m+1)c-m-m\log\frac{(m+1)c}{m}>0.
\end{equation}
Use the exponential parameter $t=(m+1)-m/c$, positive for the upper tail and negative for the lower tail, in $(1-t/(m+1))^{-mR-2}$. The extra shape parameter $2$ contributes a fixed factor. Fixed polynomial moments on a discarded tail are likewise exponentially small, by this exponential-moment bound or by Cauchy--Schwarz with a slightly weakened exponent.

Choose $0<\beta<y_m<\alpha<1$, with $\alpha>-\log a_m$. Such a choice exists since $a_m>\ee^{-1}$. On $\beta R\le x\le\alpha R$, the expansions \eqref{amc:bm:cap:F-allorders} and \eqref{amc:bm:cap:deriv-asym} are uniform. Below $\beta R$, the derivative bound and the bound on $F_R$ reduce the integral to lower gamma tails of shapes $mR+1$ and $mR+2$. Above $\alpha R$, the sector integrand is at most $(1+x)\ee^{-x}$, whose integral is $O(R\ee^{-\alpha R})$. These errors, as well as the last term in \eqref{amc:bm:cap:deriv-asym}, are smaller than every inverse power of $R$ relative to $I_{R,m}$. It follows first that
\[
 T_m(R)\sim(m+1)^m I_{R,m},
\]
and hence, by Stirling's formula,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:sector-leading}
 T_m(R)\sim C_m R^{(3-m)/2}a_m^R.
\end{equation}
Combining this with Proposition~\ref{amc:bm:cap:remainder-prop} at $M$ and at $M+1$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:remainder-equivalent}
 \mathcal J(R)-A-\sum_{m=1}^MT_m(R)
 \sim T_{M+1}(R)
 \sim C_{M+1}R^{(2-M)/2}a_{M+1}^R.
\end{equation}
This holds for every fixed $M$; it does not resolve the accumulation of bases at $\ee^{-1}$ when the number of sectors grows.

\subsection{All inverse-cap orders and an effective coefficient algorithm}
\begin{theorem}[Every fixed sector to all algebraic orders]\label{amc:bm:cap:allorders-theorem}
For each fixed $m\ge1$ and $q\ge0$, there are rational coefficients $c_{m,j}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:allorders}
 T_m(R)=C_mR^{(3-m)/2}a_m^R
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^q c_{m,j}R^{-j}+O_{m,q}(R^{-q-1})\right).
\end{equation}
They are effectively computable by finite polynomial and rational operations.
\end{theorem}
All expectations of rational functions in the following proof are restricted to a fixed compact saddle interval inside $(0,1)$. Their unrestricted gamma expectations need not exist because of the pole at $Y=1$. Full gamma moments enter only after expansion into polynomials.

\begin{proof}
Let $z$ be a formal variable and define
\begin{align}
 A_{m,\ell}(y)&=[z^\ell]\left(\sum_{u\ge0}f_u(y)z^u\right)^m,\notag\\
 B_{m,\ell}(y)&=A_{m,\ell}(y)-\frac{H_m}{y}A_{m,\ell-1}(y),
 \qquad A_{m,-1}=0.\label{amc:bm:cap:AB}
\end{align}
Only finitely many coefficients are used at a specified order. The exact centered gamma moment polynomial is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:moment-poly}
 M_{m,d}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^d\binom dj(-y_m)^{d-j}
 \frac{\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}[m+(i+2)z]}{(m+1)^j}.
\end{equation}
At $z=1/R$ this equals $\mathbb E[(Y-y_m)^d]$. For $d\ge1$ its first possible power is $z^{\lceil d/2\rceil}$, as seen from
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:moment-mgf}
 \mathbb E\ee^{t(Y-y_m)}
 =\exp\left[\sum_{u\ge1}z^u
 \left(\frac{2t^u}{u(m+1)^u}
 +\frac{mt^{u+1}}{(u+1)(m+1)^{u+1}}\right)\right].
\end{equation}
For the requested order $q$, form the coefficients through degree $q$ of
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:expectation-algorithm}
 \mathcal P_{m,q}(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^q z^\ell
 \sum_{d=0}^{2(q-\ell)}
 \frac{B_{m,\ell}^{(d)}(y_m)}{d!}M_{m,d}(z).
\end{equation}
Let $\mathrm B_{2k}$ denote Bernoulli numbers and form the formal Stirling correction
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:stirling-series}
 \mathcal S_m(z)=\left(1+\frac zm\right)
 \exp\left[\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\mathrm B_{2k}}{2k(2k-1)}
 (m^{1-2k}-m)z^{2k-1}\right].
\end{equation}
The coefficient algorithm is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:coefficient-algorithm}
 c_{m,j}=[z^j]\frac{\mathcal S_m(z)\mathcal P_{m,q}(z)}{(m+1)^m},
 \qquad 0\le j\le q.
\end{equation}
Its inputs are rational, so its outputs are rational.

To prove the remainder, raise the uniform expansion in Lemma~\ref{amc:bm:cap:F-expand} to the fixed power $m$ on the compact saddle interval. Equation~\eqref{amc:bm:cap:deriv-asym} then reduces $T_m/I_{R,m}$, up to exponentially small errors, to the localized expectation of
$\sum_{\ell=0}^qR^{-\ell}B_{m,\ell}(Y)$ with uniform error $O(R^{-q-1})$.
For each $\ell$, Taylor-expand $B_{m,\ell}$ about $y_m$ through degree $2(q-\ell)+1$. The remainder has expectation bounded by a constant times
$\mathbb E|Y-y_m|^{2(q-\ell)+2}=O(R^{-q+\ell-1})$.
The signed moment of degree $2(q-\ell)+1$ is itself of that order, by \eqref{amc:bm:cap:moment-mgf}, and may be omitted. The difference between localized and full polynomial moments is exponentially small by \eqref{amc:bm:cap:gamma-concentration}. This proves \eqref{amc:bm:cap:expectation-algorithm} with the required uniform error.

Finally, Stirling's formula with its finite-order remainder gives
\[
 I_{R,m}=\frac{C_m}{(m+1)^m}R^{(3-m)/2}a_m^R
 \left(\mathcal S_m(1/R)\text{ through degree }q+O_{m,q}(R^{-q-1})\right).
\]
The factor $1+z/m$ comes from $\Gamma(mR+2)=(mR+1)\Gamma(mR+1)$; subtracting $m$ copies of $\log\Gamma(R+1)$ gives the Bernoulli coefficients in \eqref{amc:bm:cap:stirling-series}. This proves the theorem. Uniform majorants are supplied by \eqref{amc:bm:cap:F-error}, \eqref{amc:bm:cap:gamma-concentration}, Taylor's theorem on the fixed compact interval, and Stirling's remainder. No convergence of the infinite inverse-$R$ series is asserted.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Derivative control for threshold inversion}
For every fixed $m$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:T-derivative}
 T_m'(R)\sim(\log a_m)T_m(R),\qquad |T_m'(R)|=O_m(T_m(R)).
\end{equation}
Here is a justification which does not differentiate an unqualified asymptotic error. At fixed $x\le\alpha R$, differentiate the absolutely convergent series \eqref{amc:bm:cap:F-factor}:
\[
 \partial_RF_R(x)=-\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{x^k}{(R+1)_k}
      \sum_{j=1}^k\frac1{R+j},\qquad
 |\partial_RF_R(x)|\le R^{-1}\sum_{k\ge0}k\alpha^k=O_\alpha(R^{-1}).
\]
Since $F_R\ge1$ and $\psi(R+1)=\log R+O(R^{-1})$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:delta-derivative}
 \partial_R\log\delta_R(x)=\log(x/R)+O_\alpha(R^{-1}).
\end{equation}
On the compact saddle interval, differentiation of the sector integrand introduces the factor $m\log(x/R)+O(R^{-1})$, tending at the saddle to $m\log y_m=\log a_m$.

For the upper tail, differentiation of the gamma density gives
\[
 \partial_RP(R,x)=\mathbb E[(\log G-\psi(R))\ind{G\le x}],
 \qquad G\sim\GammaDist(R,\text{ rate }1).
\]
The variance of $\log G$ is $\psi_1(R)=O(R^{-1})$, so Cauchy--Schwarz bounds this derivative uniformly in $x$ by $O(R^{-1/2})$. By \eqref{amc:bm:cap:deriv-bound}, the absolute derivative of the sector integrand is at most
$O_m(R^{-1/2})(1+x)\ee^{-x}$. Its tail beyond $\alpha R$ is negligible when $\alpha>-\log a_m$. On the lower tail, \eqref{amc:bm:cap:delta-derivative} adds only $1+|\log(x/R)|$ to the previous gamma bound; use $|\log y|\le C(y^{-1/2}+y^{1/2})$ and gamma tails with shapes shifted by $1/2$. These integrable estimates justify differentiation and prove \eqref{amc:bm:cap:T-derivative}.
The derivative $\partial_RP(R,x)$ is strictly negative: it is the covariance of the strictly increasing function $\log G$ and the decreasing nonconstant indicator $\ind{G\le x}$ under a nondegenerate gamma density.

For fixed $K\ge1$, write
\[
 \Delta_K(R)=\sum_{m=1}^KT_m(R),\qquad \lambda=\log2.
\]
Direct differentiation of the exact linear term, together with \eqref{amc:bm:cap:T-derivative}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:partial-derivative}
 \Delta_K'(R)=-(\lambda-1/R)L_0(R)
 \left[1+O_K\!\left(R^{-1/2}(8/9)^R\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Indeed, differentiating the nonleading zeta modes gives an error $O(R3^{-R})$, and all remaining fixed sectors and their derivatives are dominated by the quadratic sector. For $K=1$ the stated common bound can be improved.

\[
 \Bigl|\sum_{k\ge3}k^{-R-1}(1-R\log k)\Bigr|
 \le R\,3^{-R-1}\sum_{k\ge3}\log k\Bigl(\frac3k\Bigr)^{R+1}
 \le27S\,R\,3^{-R-1}.
\]
Since $\lambda-1/R\ge\lambda-1/2>0$, dividing by $(\lambda-1/R)L_0(R)$ gives $|\varepsilon_1(R)|\le\frac{27S}{\lambda-1/2}(2/3)^{R+1}=\frac{18S}{\lambda-1/2}(2/3)^R$. Numerically $S=0.11148\ldots$ and $\lambda-1/2=0.19314\ldots$, so the constant is below $10.4$. \qed

\smallskip\noindent Thus the common bound of \eqref{amc:bm:cap:partial-derivative} improves at $K=1$ from relative order $R^{-1/2}(8/9)^R$ to $(2/3)^R$, the order of the first nonleading zeta mode. For $K\ge2$ the error term is set by the quadratic sector and is not improved by this argument.
\end{writenotedated}

\subsection{Canonical envelopes for the minimum cap}
Given $0<\epsilon<1/A$, set
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:tau}
 \tau(\epsilon)=\frac{A^2\epsilon}{1-A\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
The desired inequality is equivalent to
\[
 A^{-1}-\mathcal J(R)^{-1}\le\epsilon
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Delta(R)\le\tau(\epsilon).
\]
For all sufficiently small $\epsilon$ there is a unique real $R_*(\epsilon)>2$ with $\Delta(R_*)=\tau(\epsilon)$, and strict monotonicity yields the exact integer answer
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:integer-exact}
 r_{\min}(\epsilon)=\lceil R_*(\epsilon)\rceil-1.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $K\ge1$, let $R_K(\epsilon)$ be the unique sufficiently large root
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:envelope-roots}
 \Delta_K(R_K)=\tau(\epsilon).
\end{equation}
These envelopes use exact sectors. In particular $R_1$ solves
$R(\zeta(R+1)-1)=\tau$, incorporating the full linear zeta term and the exact reciprocal transformation; $R_2$ adds the exact quadratic integral. Positivity gives
$R_1<R_2<\cdots<R_K<R_*$.

\begin{proposition}[Displacement from an exact-sector envelope]\label{amc:bm:cap:inverse-envelope}
For every fixed $K\ge1$ and $q\ge0$, with $m=K+1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:inverse-allorders}
 R_*-R_K=
 \frac{2C_m}{\lambda-1/R_K}\,
 R_K^{(1-m)/2}(2a_m)^{R_K}
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^qc_{m,j}R_K^{-j}+O_{K,q}(R_K^{-q-1})\right).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:inverse-cubic}
 R_*-R_2\sim\frac{12\sqrt3}{\pi\lambda R_2}
 \left(\frac{27}{32}\right)^{R_2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The omitted tail at $R_K$ is asymptotic to $T_{K+1}(R_K)$ by \eqref{amc:bm:cap:remainder-equivalent}. On a bounded neighborhood of $R_K$, \eqref{amc:bm:cap:partial-derivative} is uniformly asymptotic to $-\lambda L_0$, while the omitted tail is uniformly $o(L_0)$. Evaluating at $R_K$ and at $R_K$ plus a sufficiently large constant times $T_{K+1}(R_K)/L_0(R_K)$ first brackets $R_*$, proving that its displacement tends to zero. The mean value theorem then applies on this shrinking interval. Equation~\eqref{amc:bm:cap:T-derivative} shows that evaluating the leading omitted sector at $R_*$ rather than $R_K$ makes only a relative exponentially small change. Subsequent sectors, the error in \eqref{amc:bm:cap:partial-derivative}, and the displacement effect are all exponentially smaller than every fixed inverse power of $R_K$. Insert Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:cap:allorders-theorem} and divide by $(\lambda-1/R_K)L_0(R_K)$ to obtain \eqref{amc:bm:cap:inverse-allorders}.
\end{proof}
The first inverse-$R_2$ correction in \eqref{amc:bm:cap:inverse-cubic} is
\[
 \frac{12\sqrt3}{\pi(\lambda-1/R_2)R_2}
 \left(\frac{27}{32}\right)^{R_2}
 \left(1-\frac{43}{3R_2}+O(R_2^{-2})\right).
\]
Because $R_2$ uses the exact quadratic sector, this genuinely isolates the cubic exponential scale. A finite inverse-$R$ approximation to $T_2$ alone cannot do so.

\subsection{Lambert W inversion and the integer boundary}
The leading reciprocal gap is $C_0R2^{-R}$, where $C_0=18/\pi^4$. Its inverse on the decreasing branch is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:W-root}
 x_\epsilon=-\frac1\lambda
 W_{-1}\!\left(-\frac{\lambda\epsilon}{C_0}\right).
\end{equation}
If $U=\log(C_0/(\lambda\epsilon))$, then
\[
 \lambda x_\epsilon=U+\log U+\frac{\log U}{U}
 +O((\log U)^2/U^2).
\]
The Lambert expression itself is exact for the leading envelope.

Uniformly for large real $R$,
\[
 \frac{A^{-1}-\mathcal J(R)^{-1}}{C_0R2^{-R}}
 =1+\frac4{\sqrt\pi}R^{-1/2}(8/9)^R
 \left(1-\frac{31}{8R}+O(R^{-2})\right).
\]
The exact-zeta correction has relative order $O((2/3)^R)$ and the reciprocal nonlinearity has relative order $O(R2^{-R})$, both exponentially smaller than the positive quadratic correction. Thus the true gap eventually exceeds the leading envelope. The shrinking-interval argument used above gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:W-correction}
 R_*-x_\epsilon=
 \frac4{\sqrt\pi(\lambda-1/x_\epsilon)}x_\epsilon^{-1/2}
 (8/9)^{x_\epsilon}
 \left(1-\frac{31}{8x_\epsilon}+O(x_\epsilon^{-2})\right).
\end{equation}
In particular the displacement is positive and tends to zero. With denominator $\lambda$ factored out, its first relative inverse-$x_\epsilon$ coefficient is $1/\lambda-31/8$. At any fixed order the last factor may be replaced by the corresponding finite series of coefficients $c_{2,j}$ with its proved remainder.

\begin{corollary}[At most two eventual integer candidates]\label{amc:bm:cap:two-candidates}
For all sufficiently small positive $\epsilon$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:bm:cap:two-integers}
 r_{\min}(\epsilon)\in
 \{\lceil x_\epsilon\rceil-1,\ \lceil x_\epsilon\rceil\}.
\end{equation}
If $x_\epsilon$ is an integer, the larger candidate is the answer. Otherwise put $d_\epsilon=\lceil x_\epsilon\rceil-x_\epsilon$; the smaller candidate is correct exactly when $R_*-x_\epsilon\le d_\epsilon$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For sufficiently small $\epsilon$, \eqref{amc:bm:cap:W-correction} gives
$0<R_*-x_\epsilon<1$. Apply \eqref{amc:bm:cap:integer-exact}. Equality at an integer $R_*$ is included because the original gap inequality is non-strict.
\end{proof}

\begin{writenotedated}{5 October 2026}
\emph{Relation to Section~\ref{amc:sec:capinverse}.} With $\lambda=\log2$ and $C_0=18/\pi^4=2D$, where $D=9/\pi^4$ is the constant of \eqref{amc:eq:gap}, the Lambert root \eqref{amc:bm:cap:W-root} is $x_\epsilon=b_0+1$ for the $b_0$ of \eqref{amc:eq:capinverse}, and $r_{\min}(\epsilon)$ is Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}'s $b_\varepsilon$. So the corollary says $b_\varepsilon\in\{\lceil b_0\rceil,\lceil b_0\rceil+1\}$ for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}'s bracket \eqref{amc:eq:explicitround} is effective for $b_0\ge13$; this corollary is eventual only. When $b_0$ is not an integer, Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}'s lower bracket $\lfloor b_0\rfloor+1$ is the smaller candidate here. Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}'s counterexamples at $\varepsilon=h(n)$ are the case of an integer $x_\epsilon$, where the corollary selects the larger candidate. That the true gap exceeds the leading envelope, eventually here, is proved in Section~\ref{amc:sec:capinverse} for every $b\ge3$ ($d_b>h(b)$). New here: the exact selection criterion $R_*-x_\epsilon\le d_\epsilon$ and the displacement asymptotics \eqref{amc:bm:cap:W-correction}.
\end{writenotedated}
An analogous, narrower two-candidate bracket uses $R_2$ in place of $x_\epsilon$. For any fixed $0<\eta<1$, \eqref{amc:bm:cap:inverse-cubic} gives the eventual interval
\[
 R_2+(1-\eta)H_2(R_2)<R_*<R_2+(1+\eta)H_2(R_2),\qquad
 H_2(u)=\frac{12\sqrt3}{\pi\lambda u}(27/32)^u.
\]
Once a rigorous validity bound for this interval has been established at a particular $\epsilon$, equal ceilings of its endpoints certify the integer cap. The eventual asymptotic statement alone does not provide a numerical certificate at a specified finite $\epsilon$. If validity is not certified, or an integer lies between certified endpoints, a certified direct bound on the gap at the candidate is needed. At an exact threshold the corresponding integer cap qualifies; arbitrarily close approaches to such thresholds cannot be decided from a fixed asymptotic truncation alone.

\begin{remark}[Limits of exponential inversion]
Formula~\eqref{amc:bm:cap:inverse-allorders} is centered at the exact envelope root $R_K$. It does not permit linear superposition of arbitrarily many exponential shifts about $x_\epsilon$. Nonlinear inversion products interleave the sector scales: for example $(8/9)^2$ lies strictly between $2(6/7)^6$ and $2(7/8)^7$. Neither these fixed-sector formulas nor the all-orders algebraic expansions resolve a growing number of sectors or the accumulation layer at exponential base $\ee^{-1}$.
\end{remark}

\section{Reproducible finite verification}
\label{amc:bm:sec:checks}
The accompanying package contains an offline, standard-library-only exact
verification suite.  Python~3.9 or later and a POSIX shell suffice.  From any
working directory, run
\begin{center}
\texttt{sh /path/to/bounded\_ascent\_report/run\_checks.sh}.
\end{center}
The script exits unsuccessfully on any failed check.  It runs the full suite
both normally and under \texttt{python -O}, then runs the negative-regression
harness itself under optimization.  No correctness condition relies on a
removable Python assertion statement.  Setup, data provenance, and detailed
coverage are documented in \texttt{checks/README.md}.

\paragraph{Three finite models and complete marginals.}
For each $r=2,3,4,5,6$, the suite independently computes $c_{n,r}$ by literal
original-word enumeration, an ordered residual-capacity dynamic program that
never sorts, and the compact category-count recurrence.  All three agree for
$0\le n\le10$, and every nonempty endpoint histogram of
$X=(X_1,\ldots,X_r)$ agrees for $1\le n\le10$.  The 55 count values and all
50 endpoint histograms are also compared with frozen integer fixtures; the
latter contain 985 nonzero bins in total.  This is the complete category-count
marginal, not an assertion that sorted and unsorted last-threshold marginals
coincide.  For $r=2$ the three-model and full-histogram comparison additionally
extends through $n=11$, although the frozen endpoint fixtures stop at $n=10$.
The literal enumerations visit 919,973 nonempty prefixes altogether.

The literal enumerator also records the full joint distribution of
$(N_1,\ldots,N_r,D,A)$, where $D$ counts distinct values and $A$ counts ascents.
The endpoint identities in the report recover this distribution exactly from
the compact histograms.  In addition, the finite identity
\[
 c_{n+1,r}=\sum_X\left(\sum_{j=1}^r X_j\right)h_{n,r}(X)
\]
is checked for $n=1,\ldots,9$ when $r=3,\ldots,6$, and for
$n=1,\ldots,10$ when $r=2$.

\paragraph{Published overlap.}
The package transcribes $c_{n,2}$ for $0\le n\le11$ from
\href{https://oeis.org/A202058}{OEIS A202058}, including $c_{11,2}=77451$,
and the displayed square-array entries for $r=2,\ldots,6$, $0\le n\le8$ from
\href{https://oeis.org/A294220}{OEIS A294220}.  This gives 57 source entries,
with overlap retained deliberately.  The pages were checked on
2~October~2026.  A294220 is the whole array; its $r=3$ column is A317784.
The package does not label its locally computed $r=3$, $n=9,10$ values as
independently verified published terms.  The nonpublished frozen histograms
are regression data generated by a separate literal-word audit implementation,
not independent proof certificates.

\paragraph{Local structural checks.}
The adjacent-swap tests exhaust 12,220 cases: $r=2,3,4$; initial capacity-list
length $d=2,3,4$; all lists in $\{1,\ldots,r\}^d$; all thresholds
$L=2,\ldots,d$; all adjacent pairs with upper index below $L$; and suffix
lengths $0,\ldots,4$.  Each test compares the full joint distribution of final
$X$ and new ascent count, not merely a total.  Equal-capacity swaps and empty
suffixes are included in this count.

The positive-block tests check 78,570 deterministic buffered paths, covering
$r=2,\ldots,6$, block lengths $3,\ldots,8$, six specified category-usage
patterns, every middle permutation, and three initial thresholds.  They verify
category retention, exact coordinate drift, the permutation-ascent formula,
and final-threshold closure; the complete middle-permutation ascent bins are
also compared with independently computed Eulerian numbers.  Five further
blocks, one for each tested $r$, have strictly positive drift in every
coordinate.  These tests use no random sampling.

\paragraph{Fail-closed data handling.}
The validators require exact fields, fixed coverage, integer types excluding
booleans, valid dimensions and indices, positive bin counts, feasible endpoint
statistics, and the correct histogram totals.  Duplicate JSON keys, duplicate
rows or bins, missing data, extra or shortened value lists, altered declared
coverage, and nonfinite constants are rejected.  Required coverage is fixed
in the validator rather than inferred from whatever data happen to be present.
The 64 negative-regression cases run in 128 separate processes, half under
\texttt{-O}.  They include syntactically valid but false count values with
matching corrupted histogram totals, false histogram bins preserving the
correct total, and a false published value.  Thus merely retaining correct
schemas or aggregate totals cannot make those corruptions pass.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
All checks above use exact integer arithmetic, but their coverage is finite.
They corroborate the recurrence and its implementations; they do not prove
the exchange lemma in general, the asymptotic root or ratio limit,
concentration, a coefficient equivalent, or a convergence rate.  The proofs
in the report supply the mathematical arguments.
An optional, separately invoked \texttt{mpmath} program evaluates the
characteristic integrals and finite-difference residuals.  It is not part of
\texttt{run\_checks.sh}, has no interval-certified error bounds, and is
explicitly labeled numerical diagnostics rather than proof.

\paragraph{Optional exact large-cap algebra.}
The separate \texttt{checks/analytics/} directory requires SymPy and is excluded
from the standard-library runner.  Its own runner checks, both normally and
under \texttt{-O}, all printed rational $c_{m,j}$ for $m=1,2,3$ and
$j=0,1,2,3$, consistency under changing the finite truncation order, and
$c_{m,1}=\kappa_m$ for $m=1,2,3,4,5,8,12$.  It also checks the first three
rational tail-factor functions and rejects 26 invalid index/order inputs.
These are exact finite algebraic comparisons; the large-cap remainder estimates
and inversion results rely on the analytic arguments, not on the replay.
An additional optional \texttt{mpmath} sector-quadrature diagnostic is likewise
excluded from both exact runners and supplies no certified error bounds.

\section{Further questions and limits of the results}\label{amc:bm:sec:questions}
\subsection{Subexponential word length asymptotics}
Theorem~\ref{amc:bm:thm:growth} identifies $N!\mu_r^N$ only at exponential accuracy. Determining a factor $B_r(N)$ in an equivalent $c_{N,r}\sim N!\mu_r^N B_r(N)$ is a separate problem. Even for $r=2$, a precise amplitude and a complete correction hierarchy require estimates beyond the block action. A useful next step is a controlled discrete transport analysis around the characteristic, with particular care near its singular origin. Numerical evidence or a formal transport equation alone would not prove such a factor.

\subsection{Fluctuations and local enumeration}
The smooth limiting pressure gives exponential concentration and the full large-deviation principle, but does not justify differentiating finite-size limits twice. A central limit theorem would require additional uniform control of the weighted coefficients near a fixed mark. One could seek a nondegenerate covariance matrix from the Hessian of $F$, then prove that it is the actual covariance limit and establish a local limit theorem. The latter would refine the enumeration of words with prescribed multiplicity histograms.

\subsection{The feasible endpoint region}
The rate function is specified by a finite-dimensional convex dual, but its finite-rate boundary is not classified here. Besides nonnegativity and $\sum_jx_j\le1$, the exact multiplicity identity imposes $\sum_{j<r}(r-j)x_j\le1$. Identifying all attainable limiting endpoints, and determining which have finite rate, would give a more geometric interpretation of the pressure and of atypical ascent counts. Such a classification is unnecessary for the full large-deviation proof above.

\subsection{A cap growing with the word length}
The fixed-$r$ theorem and the large-$r$ expansion are two successive results, not a uniform joint limit. They do not allow substitution of $r=r(N)$ into a coefficient asymptotic. The exponentially small gap $6/\pi^2-\mu_r$ suggests investigating logarithmic cap scales, but even the correct transition window requires estimates uniform in $r$ and control of the unrestricted maximum multiplicity. The constants in the present seeds, block dimensions, and error estimates depend on $r$.

\subsection{Sector accumulation and effective inversion}
For every fixed sector and every fixed inverse-cap order, the analytic expansion is rigorous. The sector bases accumulate at $\ee^{-1}$, so letting the number of sectors grow with the cap requires new estimates. A finite inverse-power truncation of one sector also cannot isolate an exponentially smaller sector. The exact-sector threshold envelopes avoid that loss, while concrete decisions arbitrarily close to an integer threshold still need certified evaluation rather than asymptotic rounding.

Certified integral bounds and faster compact enumeration are practical next steps that do not require a full word-length expansion.
