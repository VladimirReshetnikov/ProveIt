\begin{abstract}
Let $a_n^{(b)}$ count ascent sequences of length $n$ in which each value occurs at most $b$ times (the array OEIS A294220; cap three is A317784, cap two A202058). This report prints two manuscripts as two Parts. Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} proves, for every fixed $b\ge3$, that $(a_n^{(b)}/n!)^{1/n}\to\mu_b=1/T_b$ with $T_b=\int_0^\infty\log E_b(v)/(E_b(v)-1)\,dv$ and $E_b$ the truncated exponential, with a coarse error $O_b(n/\log n)$ in $\log a_n^{(b)}$, a controlled Lambert-$W$ inverse in the length, the large-cap expansion $T_b=\pi^2/6+(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1)+O(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b)$ with explicit constants, and a rounding-safe inverse in the cap with counterexamples to naive rounding; the cap-two input $T_2=3\pi^2/8$ is taken from the sibling report on A202058. Part~\ref{amc:part:sec} writes $T_b-\pi^2/6$ exactly as a convergent sum of positive Taylor sectors $A_{r,b}$, proves that the remainder after any fixed number $K$ of sectors is asymptotic to sector $K+1$, and expands every fixed sector to all algebraic orders in $1/b$; in particular the remainder of Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} is $\sim\frac8{9\sqrt\pi}\sqrt b\,(4/9)^b$. No prefactor, ratio limit or all-orders expansion in $n$ for $b\ge3$, no joint regime $b=b(n)$, and no uniformity in a growing number of sectors is claimed. The report is unrefereed, and none of it is formalized.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{Status, scope and non-claims}
\phantomsection\label{amc:guide:status}
Neither manuscript states how it was produced; both reached the repository through its incoming channel for AI-assisted research deliveries and are treated as AI-assisted. Both are unrefereed: source 47 calls its audits ``mathematical checks of this argument, not journal peer review'', and source 52's verification records are research reviews, not formal verification. Their quadratures are high-precision numerical checks, not interval-certified enclosures. Nothing in this report is formalized in Lean or Rocq, and placing it in this repository confers no formal status.

The union of the sources' non-claims, all kept in the Parts, is as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item No fixed-cap prefactor, ratio limit or all-orders coefficient expansion in $n$ for $b\ge3$; the error $O_b(n/\log n)$ is deliberately coarse and is not the true subexponential scale; $a_n^{(b)}\sim C_bn!\mu_b^n$ is not implied.
\item The limits in length and cap are kept separate: the large-cap expansion concerns the growth constant after the length limit; no uniform asymptotics for $b=b(n)$ and no exchange of limits.
\item The length inverse is a controlled leading inverse, not an exact rounding rule. The cap inverse is an inverse for the cap of the limiting growth constant, not a cap-selection guarantee for finite-length counts; near integer transitions no unconditional rounding is justified.
\item The sector results hold for a fixed number of sectors and a fixed algebraic order. No optimal truncation, summability, uniformity in $r=r(b)$, justified sum of all sector expansions, or theorem at the accumulation scale $e^{-b}$; finite truncations of an earlier sector do not resolve later sectors; the reciprocal remainder is positive only eventually for each fixed $K$; no new inverse. The word ``transseries'' in Part~\ref{amc:part:sec} means only the exact positive-sector decomposition.
\item Priority is not established; the literature checks are scoped. The cap-two compacted recurrence and the constant $8/(3\pi^2)$ (conjectured) are due to Conway, Conway, Elvey Price and Guttmann; source 47's involution generalizes their compaction mechanism. The standalone OEIS page of A317784 could not be retrieved by source 47; its identification rests on A294220's cross-reference.
\end{itemize}

\part{Factorial growth of ascent sequences with bounded multiplicity}\label{amc:part:fc}

\begin{partsource}
Batch-77 manuscript 47, archive \texttt{oeis-bounded-multiplicity-family-report.zip}, main file \texttt{bounded-multiplicity-report.tex} (612 lines, 13-page PDF), dated 1 October 2026. Author line: ``A proof and reproducible research report''. No commit pin; it identifies the cap-two report by file hashes. Printed in full, with labels prefixed \texttt{amc:}; one section label and the \textbf{[write]} notes were added at the merge.
\end{partsource}

\begin{partabstract}
Let $a_n^{(b)}$ count ascent sequences of length $n$ in which each value occurs at most $b$ times. For every fixed $b\ge3$ we prove the exact factorial root constant
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{a_n^{(b)}}{n!}\right)^{1/n}
 =\left[\int_0^\infty\frac{\log E_b(v)}{E_b(v)-1}\,dv\right]^{-1},
 \qquad E_b(v)=\sum_{j=0}^b\frac{v^j}{j!}.
\]
The separate cap-two proof gives the same formula. We establish exact compaction by a suffix involution, uniform positive-operator barriers, and a coefficient argument for every $n$. A non-optimal logarithmic error supports a controlled inverse for the required length. We also prove a large-cap expansion of the constant, including an exact zeta first variation and an exponentially smaller remainder, and obtain a rounding-safe cap inverse. This report does not determine fixed-cap prefactors, ratio limits, or all-orders coefficient expansions. The limits in length and cap are kept separate.
\end{partabstract}

\section{Results and interpretation}\label{amc:sec:intro}
An ascent sequence is a word $w_1\cdots w_n$ of nonnegative integers with $w_1=0$ and
\[
 0\le w_i\le1+\asc(w_1\cdots w_{i-1})\quad(i\ge2),\qquad
 \asc(w)=\#\{i:w_i<w_{i+1}\}.
\]
Write $a_0^{(b)}=1$ and let $a_n^{(b)}$ count the words with every label used at most $b$ times. Equivalently, these words avoid the constant pattern of length $b+1$. This is the array in OEIS A294220; cap two is A202058 and cap three is A317784 \cite{array,cap3}.

\begin{theorem}\label{amc:thm:fixed}
For every fixed integer $b\ge3$, set
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:Tb}
 T_b=\int_0^\infty\frac{\log E_b(v)}{E_b(v)-1}\,dv,
 \qquad \mu_b=T_b^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The integrand is interpreted as $1$ at zero. The exponential generating function
$A_b(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n^{(b)}t^n/n!$ has radius $T_b$, and
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:main}
 \log a_n^{(b)}=\log(n!)-n\log T_b+O_b\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently $(a_n^{(b)}/n!)^{1/n}\to\mu_b$ and
\[
 a_n^{(b)}=n!\,\mu_b^n\exp\!\left(O_b\!\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)\right).
\]
\end{theorem}
The subscript $b$ permits constants and starting indices to depend on the fixed cap. The logarithmic error is deliberately coarse; it is not an estimate of the true subexponential scale. In particular, the theorem does not imply $a_n^{(b)}\sim C_bn!\mu_b^n$ or $a_{n+1}^{(b)}/((n+1)a_n^{(b)})\to\mu_b$.

The separately audited cap-two report \cite{cap2report} proves $T_2=3\pi^2/8$ and a stronger logarithmic error $O(n^{9/13}(\log n)^3)$, so \eqref{amc:eq:main} also holds for $b=2$. Its analytic proof is not repeated here. For $b=1$, the unique word at each length is strictly increasing, $T_1=\infty$, and the normalized root is zero.
\begin{writenote}
\emph{The cap-two report in this repository.} The report cited here is batch-77 manuscript 37, printed as Part~I of the sibling report \repopath{a202058-ascent-000-growth} in this collection directory; its main theorem (label \texttt{a58:fd:thm:main} there) proves that the exponential generating function of A202058 has radius $T=3\pi^2/8$, the root limit $(a_n/n!)^{1/n}\to8/(3\pi^2)$, and the error $O(n^{9/13}(\log n)^3)$. The SHA-256 values that source 47 records for that report's delivered \texttt{a202058-report.tex} and \texttt{.pdf} (\repopath{data/47-caps-provenance.json}) match the files of the arrival commit \texttt{096ee7b87}; this was checked at the merge. Later Parts of the sibling report sharpen the cap-two error and prove the cap-two ratio limit (see its README). The two reports are kept separate, by the coordinator's decision at the merge: the sibling treats the fine asymptotics of the single cap $b=2$, this one the growth constants of every cap $b\ge3$ and their large-cap behaviour, and this report takes from the sibling only $T_2$ and the $b=2$ case of \eqref{amc:eq:main}.
\end{writenote}

\begin{theorem}\label{amc:thm:cap}
As $b\to\infty$ through integers,
\begin{align}
 T_b&=\frac{\pi^2}{6}+(b+1)\bigl(\zeta(b+2)-1\bigr)
       +O\!\left(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b\right),\label{amc:eq:capstrong}\\
 T_b-\frac{\pi^2}{6}&\sim\frac{b+1}{2^{b+2}},\qquad
 \frac6{\pi^2}-\mu_b\sim\frac{9(b+1)}{\pi^4 2^b}.\label{amc:eq:caplead}
\end{align}
Moreover $T_b$ strictly decreases to $\pi^2/6$, and $\mu_b$ strictly increases to $6/\pi^2$.
\end{theorem}
This is an expansion of the fixed-cap growth constant after the length limit. It does not assert uniform coefficient asymptotics when $b=b(n)$ grows. Sections~\ref{amc:sec:lengthinverse} and~\ref{amc:sec:capinverse} give different inverses for the length and the cap.
\begin{writenote}
Part~\ref{amc:part:sec} (source 52) sharpens the remainder in \eqref{amc:eq:capstrong}. The exact identity $T_b-\pi^2/6=\sum_{r\ge1}A_{r,b}$ (Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact}) has first sector $A_{1,b}=(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1)$, the first variation of \eqref{amc:eq:capstrong}, and its remainder after one sector is asymptotic to the second sector:
\[
 T_b-\frac{\pi^2}{6}-(b+1)\bigl(\zeta(b+2)-1\bigr)\sim A_{2,b}\sim\frac8{9\sqrt\pi}\sqrt b\left(\frac49\right)^b
\]
(equations \eqref{amc:sec:eq:equivalence} and \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r2}). So the exponential rate $(4/9)^b$ of \eqref{amc:eq:capstrong} is sharp, while its power $b^{5/2}$ is not: the true power is $b^{1/2}$. The two statements are consistent. The bound of this Part has explicit constants and holds for every $b\ge3$ (Section~\ref{amc:sec:capinverse}); Part~\ref{amc:part:sec}'s is sharp but asymptotic, with constants that are not explicit.
\end{writenote}

\section{Exact compaction and the counting operator}\label{amc:sec:compaction}
For any ascent-sequence prefix, $\max(w)\le\asc(w)$. Indeed a new maximum creates an ascent, whereas a nonascent cannot raise the maximum. Thus the top admissible label $1+\asc(w)$ is unused and strictly larger than the last value.

Delete labels whose budgets have been exhausted. The remaining active labels, in numerical order, have positive budgets $\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{m-1}\in\{1,\ldots,b\}$. Let $k$ be the number of active labels at or below the actual last value. This can be a virtual boundary when that value has just been deleted. Choosing active rank $i$ is an ascent exactly when $i\ge k$. Decrease $\beta_i$ by one; remove it and set $k'=i$ if it reaches zero, and otherwise set $k'=i+1$. An ascent creates precisely one additional top label of budget $b$.

\begin{lemma}[Adjacent-budget interchange]\label{amc:lem:swap}
Suppose $x<y$ are adjacent active labels with both at or below the last value. Exchanging their positive remaining budgets, while retaining the last boundary, preserves the number of legal suffixes of every length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In a suffix, reverse each maximal contiguous block consisting only of $x,y$, and then interchange $x$ and $y$ throughout that block. This is an involution that exchanges their total usages. Every intermediate usage is at most the total, so the exchanged quotas hold at every stage.

Inside a binary block, a comparison $p<q$ becomes the same comparison between the complemented pair in reverse order. Its total number of ascents is preserved. No possible outside label lies strictly between $x$ and $y$: the two labels were adjacent among the initially active labels, exhausted labels never return, and future labels are all above the initial alphabet. Thus boundary comparisons with outside letters are unchanged. Entry into an initial binary block is nonascending both before and after the map, since the initial last value is at least $y$.

Ascent totals therefore agree at each block end and before every unchanged outside letter. The timing inside a block can change, but both block labels were already admissible initially, and the admissible upper bound never decreases. This proves legality as well as invertibility.
\end{proof}
The lemma allows permutations below the boundary, not arbitrary sorting above it. Start with a canonical state in which budgets increase with rank. After choosing budget $r>1$, move its decremented entry left to the beginning of the old budget-$r$ group. Every swap lies among the first $i+1$ entries, hence below the new boundary $i+1$, which stays fixed. Exhaustion leaves an already sorted tuple and boundary $i$. A new top label is appended. This gives an exact reduction at every step. The last boundary does not follow the moved budget.

Write $N_0=u$ for the number of unused labels and $N_j=s_j$ for those used $j$ times, $1\le j<b$. Groups occur in increasing rank order as
\[
 N_{b-1},N_{b-2},\ldots,N_1,N_0,\qquad
 m=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}N_j,\quad u\ge1,\quad0\le k<m.
\]
If rank $i$ belongs to group $j$, put $\alpha=\one_{i\ge k}$. Its child is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:transition}
 N'=N-e_j+\one_{j+1<b}e_{j+1}+\alpha e_0,
 \qquad
 k'=\begin{cases}i,&j=b-1,\\i+1,&j<b-1.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
These transitions preserve $u\ge1$ and $k<m$: a choice of the final unused top label is always ascending and creates another. They satisfy $m'\le m+1$.
Define the positive operator and its series by
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:operator}
 (\TT f)(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(x_i),\qquad
 F(t,x)=\sum_{r\ge0}\TT^r\one(x)\frac{t^r}{r!}.
\end{equation}
Let $x_1$ have $s_1=u=k=1$ and all other $s_j=0$. Then
\[
 \TT^r\one(x_1)=a_{r+1}^{(b)},\qquad F(t,x_1)=A_b'(t).
\]
All series identities below allow $+\infty$ and use nonnegative sums when necessary.

\section{Characteristic curve and a uniform barrier}\label{amc:sec:barrier}
For this section fix $b\ge3$ and write $\theta=(b-1)/b>1/2$. Parameterize time by $v\ge0$:
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:characteristic}
 q=\log E_b(v),\quad p_j=\log E_{b-j}(v)\ (1\le j<b),\quad
 p_0=q,\ p_b=0,\quad
 t(v)=\int_0^v\frac{\log E_b(z)}{E_b(z)-1}\,dz.
\end{equation}
Its endpoint is $T_b<\infty$: the integrand is $O_b(\log v/v^b)$ at infinity. A dot denotes a time derivative. Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:ODE}
 \dot q=e^{p_1}\frac{1-e^{-q}}q,
 \qquad \dot p_j=\frac{e^q-1}{q}e^{p_{j+1}-p_j}.
\end{equation}
At zero the removable values are all $1$, so every $p_j,q$ is strictly increasing. Polynomial asymptotics give
\begin{align}
 p_j&=\frac{b-j}{b}q+c_j+O_b(e^{-q/b}),\label{amc:eq:poly}\\
 e^{p_1}&\sim c_b e^{\theta q},\qquad
 \dot q\sim c_b e^{\theta q}/q,\qquad
 c_b=\frac{(b!)^{\theta}}{(b-1)!}.
\end{align}
Fixed-order differentiated estimates follow from the underlying rational functions of $v$, rather than differentiation of an arbitrary error term.

Define
\[
 \rho_0=1,\quad\rho_b=0,\quad
 \rho_j=\frac{\dot p_j}{\dot q}
 =\frac{E_b(v)E_{b-j-1}(v)}{E_{b-1}(v)E_{b-j}(v)}\quad(1\le j<b).
\]
They satisfy $1=\rho_0\ge\rho_1\ge\cdots\ge\rho_{b-1}>0$. To see this, put $d_r=v^r/r!$ and note
\[
 E_r^2-E_{r-1}E_{r+1}
 =d_r\left(E_r-\frac{v}{r+1}E_{r-1}\right)\ge0;
\]
the bracket has positive coefficients. Also $\rho_j\to(b-j)/b$. Positivity, continuity, and rational asymptotics yield fixed-cap constants $c>0,K<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:weights}
 c\le\rho_j\le1,\qquad |(\rho_j)_q|\le K\quad(0\le j<b, q\ge0).
\end{equation}

For a state $x$, let
\[
 D=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}\rho_jN_j,\quad
 h(y)=\int_0^y\rho_{\mathrm{group}(z)}\,dz,\quad
 r(y)=h(y)/D,\quad r=r(k),
\]
where group weights are constant on unit rank intervals. Put
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:psi}
 \psi(t,x)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{b-1}p_js_j+qu-qr\right).
\end{equation}
Here $D\ge u\ge1$, $cm\le D\le m$, $0\le r\le1$, and $\psi(0,x)=1$.

\subsection{Exact child rank and its bounds}
If rank $i$ belongs to group $j$, write $H=h(i)$ and $d_j=\rho_j-\rho_{j+1}\in[0,1]$. Every compaction swap is below the new boundary, so its weight sum is unchanged. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:rank}
 D_i=D-d_j+\alpha,\qquad
 h_{x_i}(k_i)=H+\rho_{j+1},\qquad
 r_i=\frac{H+\rho_{j+1}}{D-d_j+\alpha}.
\end{equation}
This includes saturation through $\rho_b=0$. Since $D_i\ge1$ and $D_i\ge D-1$, we have $D_i\ge D/2$ and
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:rankbound}
 |r_i-r(i)|\le\frac4D.
\end{equation}
For an ascent, $i\ge k$ and $D_i\le D+1$, whence the crucial one-sided bound
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:ascentbound}
 r(k)-r_i\le\frac HD-\frac H{D+1}\le\frac1{D+1}\le\frac12.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Frozen integral and residual}
For real $y$ in group $j$ set
\[
 g(y)=\exp\left(p_{j+1}-p_j+q\one_{y\ge k}-q(r(y)-r(k))\right).
\]
The exact transition ratio $\psi(t,x_i)/\psi(t,x)$ replaces $r(i)$ by $r_i$ in $g(i)$. The characteristic equations imply
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:frozen}
 \int_0^m g(y)\,dy=\dot qD=\sum_{j=1}^{b-1}\dot p_js_j+\dot q u=:\lambda.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $\phi(y)=e^{-qh(y)/D}$ one has
$\phi_y=-(q\rho_j/D)\phi$ and
$e^{p_{j+1}-p_j}=q\dot q\rho_j/(e^q-1)$.
Using $\phi(0)=1,\phi(m)=e^{-q}$, the integrals before division by $\phi(k)$ are
\[
 \frac{\dot qD}{e^q-1}(1-\phi(k)),\qquad
 \frac{\dot qD e^q}{e^q-1}(\phi(k)-e^{-q}),
\]
whose sum is $\dot qD\phi(k)$. Continuity handles $q=0$.

By \eqref{amc:eq:poly}, every frozen ratio is at most $C_be^{\theta q}$. On each of at most $b+1$ intervals cut at group boundaries and $k$, $g$ decreases. All endpoints are integers; hence the left-sum error is bounded by the sum of left endpoint values:
\[
 \left|\sum_i g(i)-\lambda\right|\le C_be^{\theta q}.
\]
Exact descending ratios obey the same bound; exact ascending ratios are at most $C_be^{(\theta+1/2)q}$ by \eqref{amc:eq:ascentbound}. Apply
$|e^z-e^w|\le|z-w|\max(e^z,e^w)$, \eqref{amc:eq:rankbound}, and $m/D\le1/c$ to obtain
\[
 \left|\frac{\TT\psi}{\psi}-\sum_i g(i)\right|
 \le C_b q e^{(\theta+1/2)q}.
\]
Finally $|h_q|,|D_q|\le Km$ imply $|r_q|\le2K/c$. Thus
\[
 \frac{\partial_t\psi}{\psi}=\lambda-\dot q(r+qr_q),
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{\partial_t\psi}{\psi}-\lambda\right|
 \le C_b(1+q)e^{\theta q}.
\]
Together these give the state-uniform estimate
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:residual}
 \left|\frac{\partial_t\psi}{\psi}-\frac{\TT\psi}{\psi}\right|
 \le C_b(1+q)e^{(\theta+1/2)q}=:C(q).
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:Err}
 \Err(q)=\int_0^q\frac{C(z)}{\dot q(z)}\,dz
 =O_b((1+q)^3e^{q/2}),\qquad g_\pm=e^{\pm\Err}\psi.
\end{equation}
Then $\partial_tg_-\le\TT g_-$ and $\partial_tg_+\ge\TT g_+$, and both start at $1$. Since $\theta>1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:smaller}
 \Err(q)=o(e^{p_1(q)}).
\end{equation}
This strict inequality explains why this particular proof excludes $b=2$.

\section{Comparison on the infinite state space}\label{amc:sec:comparison}
Let $X$ be the continuous-time chain with each rank-choice edge at rate $1$. Its generator is $L=\TT-mI$ and its total rate is $m$. Because $m$ grows by at most one per jump, comparison with a linear pure-birth process proves nonexplosion. Expanding by finite jump paths cancels survival factors against the potential factor and gives the extended-real identity
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:FK}
 F(t,x)=\EE_x\exp\left(\int_0^t m(X_s)\,ds\right).
\end{equation}
Let $\tau_M$ be the first exit from $m\le M$ and $Y_s=\exp(\int_0^s m(X_r)\,dr)$. The stopped region is finite. Applying stopped Dynkin comparison to $Y_sg_+(t-s,X_s)$ and discarding its nonnegative exit term gives $F(t,x)\le g_+(t,x)$ upon letting $M\to\infty$.

For the lower bound fix $\delta>0$ with $t+\delta<T_b$. Strict increase of all $p_j,q$ gives an $\eta>0$, uniform for $0\le v\le t$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:exitratio}
 g_-(v,y)\le C e^{-\eta m(y)}g_+(v+\delta,y).
\end{equation}
Indeed the population terms each decrease by at least $\eta$ in the log ratio; its rank term is bounded above by $q(t+\delta)$, and the $\Err$ terms have favorable signs. The stopped subsolution inequality is
\[
 g_-(t,x)\le\EE_x[Y_t;\tau_M>t]
 +\EE_x[Y_{\tau_M}g_-(t-\tau_M,X_{\tau_M});\tau_M\le t].
\]
On exit $m(X_{\tau_M})=M+1$. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:exitratio} and the stopped supersolution inequality at horizon $t+\delta$ bound the second term by
$Ce^{-\eta(M+1)}g_+(t+\delta,x)\to0$. Nonexplosion handles the first term. We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:comparison}
 e^{-\Err(q)}\psi(t,x)\le F(t,x)\le e^{\Err(q)}\psi(t,x),
 \qquad0\le t<T_b.
\end{equation}
No uniqueness assertion for an infinite differential system is required.

\section{The radius and the coefficient limit}\label{amc:sec:coefficients}
Let $x_N$ have $s_1=N,u=1,k=N$ and all other populations zero; the increasing prefix of length $N$ reaches it. Its rank formula yields
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:growing}
 \log\psi(t,x_N)=Np_1(q)+\frac q{\rho_1N+1},\qquad
 \log F(t,x_N)=Np_1(q)+O_b(\Err(q)+q),
\end{equation}
with error independent of $N$. In particular $F(t,x_N)\ge e^{Np_1-\Err}$.
The positive semigroup identity and one increasing-prefix path at every length give, for $h>0$,
\begin{align*}
 F(t+h,x_1)&=\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{h^r}{r!}(\TT^rF(t,\cdot))(x_1)\\
 &\ge\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{h^r}{r!}F(t,x_{r+1})
 \ge\exp(p_1-\Err+h e^{p_1}).
\end{align*}
As $t\uparrow T_b$, \eqref{amc:eq:smaller} makes the last expression diverge. Monotonicity forces $F(T_b+h,x_1)=\infty$. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:comparison} gives finiteness below $T_b$. Thus $F(t,x_1)$ and $A_b$ have exact radius $T_b$, proving the normalized-root limsup. The next argument is needed for the full limit.

\subsection{A concentration estimate from barrier values}
Put $p=p_1$, $d=(\log t)_q$ and $M(q)=p_q/d=t\dot p$. Direct characteristic asymptotics give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:M}
 p_q\to\theta,\quad p_{qq}\to0,\quad
 d\sim\frac{qe^{-\theta q}}{c_bT_b},\quad
 d'/d\to-\theta,\quad
 M(q)\sim\frac{\theta c_bT_b e^{\theta q}}q.
\end{equation}
These limits are uniform after bounded shifts in $q$. For $c_j=\TT^j\one(x_N)$ define
\[
 \PP(J=j)=\frac{c_jt(q)^j}{j!F(t(q),x_N)},\qquad K=NM(q).
\]
The deterministic center $K$ need not be the exact mean. We use values of \eqref{amc:eq:growing}, never derivatives of its error.
Set
\[
 H_q(v)=p(q+v)-p(q)-M(q)(\log t(q+v)-\log t(q)).
\]
For $|v|\le1$, $H_q(0)=H_q'(0)=0$ and
\[
 H_q''(v)\to\theta^2e^{-\theta v},\qquad
 M(q)d(q+v)\to\theta e^{-\theta v}
\]
uniformly. Consequently there are fixed-cap constants $B,c>0$ with
\[
 |H_q(v)|\le Bv^2,\qquad
 M(q)|\log t(q+v)-\log t(q)|\ge c|v|.
\]
The exponential Markov bounds at $q\pm\gamma\varepsilon$, with fixed small $\gamma>0$, now give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:chernoff}
 \PP(|J-K|>\varepsilon K)
 \le2\exp\{-c_b' N\varepsilon^2+2\Err(q+1)+O_b(q+1)\}
\end{equation}
for sufficiently large $q$ and $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Explicitly the main exponent is at most
$N(B\gamma^2\varepsilon^2-c\gamma\varepsilon^2)$; choosing $\gamma$ small makes it negative. Uniformity permits shrinking $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{Extraction for each sufficiently large length}
For every sufficiently large integer $n$, choose
\[
 q=\tfrac12\log n,\qquad\varepsilon=(\log n)^{-2},\qquad
 N=\left\lfloor\frac{(1-4\varepsilon)n}{M(q)}\right\rfloor.
\]
Then
\begin{align*}
 N&\asymp_b n^{1-\theta/2}\log n,&
 N\varepsilon^2&\asymp_b\frac{n^{1-\theta/2}}{(\log n)^3},\\
 K&=(1-4\varepsilon)n+O_b(n^{\theta/2}/\log n),&
 \Err(q+1)&=O_b(n^{1/4}(\log n)^3).
\end{align*}
Since $1/2<\theta<1$, $N\varepsilon^2$ dominates the error and both $N$ and the floor loss in $K$ are $o(\varepsilon n)$. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:chernoff} puts probability at least $1/2$ in $|J-K|\le\varepsilon K$. Some integer $j$ there satisfies
\[
 \frac{c_jt(q)^j}{j!}\ge\frac{F(t(q),x_N)}{4\varepsilon K+6}.
\]
For large $n$ it obeys $(1-6\varepsilon)n\le j$ and $N+j\le n$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:lowercoef}
 \log c_j\ge\log(j!)-j\log t(q)+Np(q)-\Err(q)-O_b(\log n).
\end{equation}
The increasing prefix reaches $x_N$ in $N-1$ transitions, so $a_{N+j}^{(b)}\ge c_j$. Appending the new maximum $1+\asc(w)$ repeatedly gives an injective extension to length $n$, so $a_n^{(b)}\ge c_j$.

\subsection{A quantitative logarithmic error}\label{amc:sec:logerror}
From \eqref{amc:eq:ODE},
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:tailt}
 T_b-t(q)=O_b((1+q)e^{-\theta q}).
\end{equation}
In \eqref{amc:eq:lowercoef} the term $Np(q)$ is nonnegative. Also
$\log(n!/j!)\le6\varepsilon n\log n=6n/\log n$ and
\[
 |-j\log t(q)+n\log T_b|
 =O_b(\varepsilon n+n(1+q)e^{-\theta q}).
\]
All latter errors, and $\Err(q)$, are $o_b(n/\log n)$ for $q=\frac12\log n$. Hence
\[
 \log a_n^{(b)}\ge\log(n!)-n\log T_b-O_b(n/\log n).
\]
For the matching upper estimate use a single coefficient of $F(t,x_1)$:
\[
 a_n^{(b)}\le (n-1)!F(t(q),x_1)t(q)^{-(n-1)}.
\]
Equations~\eqref{amc:eq:growing} and~\eqref{amc:eq:tailt} bound its excess logarithm above $\log(n!)-n\log T_b$ by
\[
 O_b\bigl(\Err(q)+q+\log n+n(1+q)e^{-\theta q}\bigr)
 =o_b(n/\log n).
\]
This proves \eqref{amc:eq:main}, and therefore the full root limit. The proof uses no unproved ratio asymptotic.

\section{Inverse in the sequence length}\label{amc:sec:lengthinverse}
For fixed $b\ge2$ and $X>1$, define the integer threshold
\[
 N_b(X)=\min\{n\ge0:a_n^{(b)}\ge X\},\qquad L=\log X.
\]
The injective extension above makes the counts nondecreasing, and Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed} makes them unbounded. Let $W_0$ denote the principal real branch of Lambert $W$, characterized by $W_0(z)e^{W_0(z)}=z$ for $z>0$. As $X\to\infty$ with $b$ fixed,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:lengthinverse}
 n_0=\frac{L}{W_0(L/(eT_b))},\qquad
 N_b(X)=n_0+O_b\left(\frac{L}{(\log L)^3}\right)
 =n_0\left(1+O_b((\log L)^{-2})\right).
\end{equation}
To prove it, Stirling and \eqref{amc:eq:main} give
$\log a_n^{(b)}=G(n)+O_b(n/\log n)$ for
$G(x)=x\log(x/(eT_b))$. The threshold inequalities at $N=N_b(X)$ and $N-1$, together with $G(N)-G(N-1)=O_b(\log N)$, imply
$L=G(N)+O_b(N/\log N)$.
Thus $N\sim L/\log L$, while $G(n_0)=L$ exactly. Since
$G'(x)=\log(x/T_b)\asymp\log L$ between comparable $N$ and $n_0$, the mean value theorem gives \eqref{amc:eq:lengthinverse}.

This is a controlled leading inverse, not an exact rounding rule: its error tends to infinity. One may expand $W_0$ itself in logarithms, but such a formal expansion cannot improve the error inherited from the unknown coefficient correction. For $b=2$, the separate report gives a sharper inverse using its stronger logarithmic bound.

\section{Large caps and an exact first variation}\label{amc:sec:largecap}
Put $f(x)=\log x/(x-1)$, continuously extended at $1$, and $\tau=\pi^2/6$. The representation
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:frep}
 f(x)=\int_0^1\frac{dt}{1+t(x-1)}
\end{equation}
shows $f'<0$ and $f''>0$ on $[1,\infty)$. Thus $E_b$ increasing strictly in $b$ implies $T_b$ decreasing strictly. The cap-two integrand dominates all later integrands and is integrable. Dominated convergence and the nonnegative geometric series give
\[
 T_b\downarrow\int_0^\infty\frac{v}{e^v-1}\,dv
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k^2}=\tau.
\]
Write
\[
 R_b(v)=e^v-E_b(v),\quad
 \Delta_b(v)=f(E_b(v))-f(e^v),\quad
 L_b=\int_0^\infty[-f'(e^v)]R_b(v)\,dv.
\]
Convexity gives $\Delta_b(v)\ge[-f'(e^v)]R_b(v)\ge0$. We will prove
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:variation}
 L_b=(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1),\qquad
 0\le T_b-\tau-L_b=O\!\left(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Evaluation of the linear term}
For $v>0$,
\[
 -f'(e^v)=\sum_{h\ge2}\bigl((h-1)v-1\bigr)e^{-hv},\qquad
 R_b(v)=\sum_{k\ge b+1}\frac{v^k}{k!}.
\]
Although individual terms can have either sign near zero, their integrated absolute values are bounded by
\[
 \sum_{k\ge b+1}\sum_{h\ge2}
 \left(\frac{(h-1)(k+1)}{h^{k+2}}+\frac1{h^{k+1}}\right)<\infty
 \quad(b\ge2).
\]
Fubini is therefore legitimate. Integration and then summation in $k$ yield
\[
 L_b=\sum_{h\ge2}\sum_{k\ge b+1}\frac{(h-1)k-1}{h^{k+2}}
 =\sum_{h\ge2}\frac{b+1}{h^{b+2}}
 =(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1).
\]

\subsection{A uniform bound for the nonlinear remainder}
There is an absolute constant $C$ with
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:fsecond}
 0\le f''(z)\le C\frac{1+\log z}{z^3}\quad(z\ge1).
\end{equation}
This follows from smoothness at $1$ and the derivative asymptotics at infinity, or directly from \eqref{amc:eq:frep}. For $0\le v\le b$, the function $e^{-v}E_b(v)$ decreases, since its derivative is $-e^{-v}v^b/b!$. Stirling therefore gives
\[
 e^{-v}E_b(v)\ge e^{-b}E_b(b)\ge e^{-b}b^b/b!\ge c b^{-1/2}
\]
with absolute $c>0$. Taylor's theorem on $[E_b(v),e^v]$, using $\log z\le v$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:remainderpoint}
 0\le\Delta_b(v)-[-f'(e^v)]R_b(v)
 \le C b^{3/2}(1+v)e^{-3v}R_b(v)^2.
\end{equation}
Let $m=b+1$. Expanding the square, grouping $S=k+\ell$, and using
$\sum_{k=m}^{S-m}\binom Sk\le2^S$, we obtain
\begin{align*}
 \int_0^\infty(1+v)e^{-3v}R_b(v)^2\,dv
 &\le\sum_{S\ge2m}2^S
 \left(\frac1{3^{S+1}}+\frac{S+1}{3^{S+2}}\right)\\
 &=O\!\left(b(4/9)^b\right).
\end{align*}
All these integrands and terms are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies. This bounds the integrated remainder on $[0,b]$ by $O(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b)$.

For $v\ge b\ge3$, $E_b(v)\ge v^b/b!\ge2$ and $\log E_b(v)\le v$. Convexity implies the remainder is at most $\Delta_b(v)\le f(E_b(v))$, and hence its tail is at most
\[
 2b!\int_b^\infty v^{1-b}\,dv
 =\frac{2b!b^{2-b}}{b-2}
 =O(b^{3/2}e^{-b})
 =O\!\left(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b\right).
\]
This proves \eqref{amc:eq:variation} and \eqref{amc:eq:capstrong} with constants independent of $b$.

Since
\[
 L_b=\frac{b+1}{2^{b+2}}+O(b3^{-b}),
\]
the remainder is smaller than the leading term by an exponentially decaying factor. The reciprocal identity
\[
 \mu_\infty-\mu_b=\frac{T_b-\tau}{\tau T_b},\qquad\mu_\infty=\frac1\tau=\frac6{\pi^2},
\]
then gives the useful quantified form
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:gap}
 \mu_\infty-\mu_b
 =D(b+1)2^{-b}\left[1+O\!\left(b^{3/2}(8/9)^b\right)\right],
 \qquad D=\frac9{\pi^4}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the additive contributions $O(b3^{-b})$ and $O(b^24^{-b})$ are absorbed by $O(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b)$. This also proves \eqref{amc:eq:caplead}. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:capstrong} is a rigorously controlled first-variation expansion; it is not an all-orders transseries.

\section{Inverse in the multiplicity cap}\label{amc:sec:capinverse}
For small $\varepsilon>0$ let
\[
 b_\varepsilon=\min\{b\ge2:\mu_b\ge\mu_\infty-\varepsilon\}.
\]
Monotonicity proves existence. The large real solution of the leading model
$D(b+1)2^{-b}=\varepsilon$ is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:capinverse}
 b_0=-\frac1{\log2}W_{-1}\!\left(-\frac{\varepsilon\log2}{2D}\right)-1,
\end{equation}
where $W_{-1}$ is the real branch with $W_{-1}(z)\le-1$ on $[-1/e,0)$. The branch is essential; $W_0$ gives the irrelevant small root.

There are constants $C$ and $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, for $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, with
\[
 \eta=C b_0^{3/2}(8/9)^{b_0},
\]
one has the integer-safe bracket
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:rounding}
 \left\lceil b_0-\eta\right\rceil
 \le b_\varepsilon\le
 \left\lceil b_0+\eta\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
No interpolation of the exact sequence $T_b$ to real $b$ is assumed.

Here is the discrete justification. For the real leading model $h(x)=D(x+1)2^{-x}$,
\[
 (\log h)'(x)=\frac1{x+1}-\log2
\]
is negative and bounded away from zero for large $x$. Equation~\eqref{amc:eq:gap} first places $b_\varepsilon$ within a fixed bounded distance of $b_0$: at integers several units below or above $b_0$, the fixed change of $h$ dominates the vanishing relative error. In that bounded window the relative error is uniformly $O(b_0^{3/2}(8/9)^{b_0})$. Increasing $C$ if necessary, every integer $b<b_0-\eta$ fails the threshold and every integer $b\ge b_0+\eta$ meets it. Monotonicity handles all integers outside the window, giving \eqref{amc:eq:rounding}.

A useful explicit strengthening, proved by the same estimates, is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:eq:explicitround}
 \lfloor b_0\rfloor+1\le b_\varepsilon\le
 \left\lceil b_0+2000b_0^{3/2}(8/9)^{b_0}\right\rceil
 \qquad(b_0\ge13).
\end{equation}
Here are details to make its constants and strict lower bound reproducible. The bound
$f''(z)\le16(1+\log z)/z^3$ and $b!\le3\sqrt b(b/e)^b$ make
\eqref{amc:eq:remainderpoint} valid with $216$. Its integrated central bound is
$64b^{3/2}(b+4)(4/9)^b$, and its tail is at most $6b^{5/2}(4/9)^b$.
Thus $0\le H_b:=T_b-\tau-L_b\le160b^{5/2}(4/9)^b$ for every $b\ge3$.
\begin{writenote}
In Part~\ref{amc:part:sec}'s notation, $L_b=A_{1,b}$ and $H_b=\mathcal R_{1,b}$, and Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact} with \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r2} gives $H_b\sim A_{2,b}\sim\frac8{9\sqrt\pi}\sqrt b\,(4/9)^b$. The explicit bound $160b^{5/2}(4/9)^b$ above is therefore valid but not sharp in its power of $b$; it is kept because it is explicit, and the integer brackets below use it. ($H_b$ is not Part~\ref{amc:part:sec}'s harmonic number $H_r$.)
\end{writenote}
Put $M_b=(b+1)2^{-b-2}$ and $V_b=L_b-M_b$. Integral comparison gives
\[
 L_b\le (b/2)2^{-b},\qquad
 (b+1)3^{-b-2}\le V_b\le(b+4)3^{-b-2}.
\]
Hence $M_bL_b/V_b\le(9b/8)(3/4)^b\le729/512<\tau$, so
\[
 d_b:=\mu_\infty-\mu_b\ge\frac{L_b}{\tau(\tau+L_b)}>
 \frac{M_b}{\tau^2}=h(b).
\]
The upper bounds above also give
$0<d_b/h(b)-1\le644\phi(b)$, where $\phi(x)=x^{3/2}(8/9)^x$.
For $x\ge13$, $\phi$ decreases, and for $x\ge2$,
$(\log h)'(x)<-1/3$. Every integer $b\le b_0$ fails the threshold, proving
its strict lower bracket. For $m=\lceil b_0+2000\phi(b_0)\rceil$,
\[
 \frac{d_m}{\varepsilon}\le
 e^{-2000\phi(b_0)/3}(1+644\phi(b_0))<1,
\]
proving the upper bracket. The constants are conservative for moderate caps.
In particular, at $\varepsilon=h(n)$ for an integer $n\ge3$, one has
$b_0=n$ but $b_\varepsilon\ge n+1$. This supplies actual counterexamples
to unconditional rounding by $\lceil b_0\rceil$ at arbitrarily small tolerances.

Thus $b_\varepsilon=b_0+O(1)$, and away from vanishingly small neighborhoods of integer transitions the threshold is the expected ceiling. Exactly near those transitions, an unconditional rounding prescription is not justified by an asymptotic equivalence. In particular this is an inverse for the cap of the limiting growth constant, not a cap selection guarantee for finite-length counts.

\begin{partabstract}
The cap integral has exact positive Taylor sectors and an exponentially sharp remainder after any fixed number of sectors. Each fixed sector has an algebraic expansion to arbitrary fixed order; its coefficients are rational and algorithmically computable. We give the leading constants, the first three corrections in sectors two and three, and a precise reciprocal corollary for the growth constant. This supplements the separately delivered bounded-multiplicity report [Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} of this report]: its combinatorial identification and inversion results are prerequisites, not reproved here. \end{partabstract}

\section{Objects and relationship to the main report}\label{amc:sec:sec:objects}
For integer $b\ge2$, define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:objects}
 E_b(v)=\sum_{j=0}^{b}\frac{v^j}{j!},\qquad
 f(x)=\frac{\log x}{x-1},\qquad
 T_b=\int_0^\infty f(E_b(v))\,dv,
\end{equation}
with $f(1)=1$. Set $\tau=\pi^2/6$, $m=b+1$, $R_b(v)=e^v-E_b(v)$, and $H_r=\sum_{j=1}^r1/j$.
The integral is finite for $b\ge2$ because its integrand is $O_b(\log v/v^b)$ at infinity. Also
$\int_0^\infty f(e^v)\,dv=\int_0^\infty v/(e^v-1)\,dv=\tau$.

The main report, Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} of this report (Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed}), proves, for each fixed cap $b\ge3$,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{a_n^{(b)}}{n!}\right)^{1/n}=\mu_b=T_b^{-1},
\]
where $a_n^{(b)}$ counts ascent sequences in which every value occurs at most $b$ times. Its cited cap-two theorem gives the same identification for $b=2$. The present addendum refines the large-$b$ analysis of $T_b$ after that fixed-cap length limit. It does not strengthen the main report's error in $n$ or give joint $b,n$ asymptotics.

The main report established the exact first variation and a nonlinear bound $O(b^{5/2}(4/9)^b)$ (Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}, \eqref{amc:eq:variation}). The theorem below sharpens that remainder to a full fixed-sector hierarchy, starting with $A_{2,b}\asymp b^{1/2}(4/9)^b$. The main report's controlled length inverse and rounding-safe cap inverse (Sections~\ref{amc:sec:lengthinverse} and~\ref{amc:sec:capinverse}) remain the supplied inversion results; no sharper inverse is claimed in this addendum.

\section{Exact positive sectors and the sharp remainder}
Define, for $r\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:sector}
 a_r(x)=\frac{(-1)^r}{r!}f^{(r)}(x)
       =\int_0^1\frac{t^r\,dt}{(1-t+tx)^{r+1}},\qquad
 A_{r,b}=\int_0^\infty a_r(e^v)R_b(v)^r\,dv.
\end{equation}
These sectors are strictly positive. Define their primary exponential bases by
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:beta}
 \beta_r=\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^r.
\end{equation}
They strictly decrease to $e^{-1}$; in particular every fixed $\beta_r$ is greater than $e^{-1}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{amc:sec:thm:exact}
For each integer $b\ge2$, the following is an exact convergent identity:
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:infinite}
 T_b-\tau=\sum_{r\ge1}A_{r,b}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed integer $K\ge1$, as $b\to\infty$ through integers,
\begin{align}
 T_b-\tau&=\sum_{r=1}^K A_{r,b}+\RR_{K,b},\label{amc:sec:eq:remainder}\\
 0<\RR_{K,b}&=O_K\!\left(b^{(2-K)/2}\beta_{K+1}^b\right),\label{amc:sec:eq:order}\\
 \RR_{K,b}&=A_{K+1,b}
 \left[1+O_K\!\left(b^{-1/2}
 \left(\frac{\beta_{K+2}}{\beta_{K+1}}\right)^b\right)\right].\label{amc:sec:eq:equivalence}
\end{align}
In particular $\RR_{K,b}\sim A_{K+1,b}$, with a positive exact remainder. The estimates hold for fixed $K$; the identity \eqref{amc:sec:eq:infinite} does not license summing sector asymptotic formulas.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The integral representation $f(x)=\int_0^1(1-t+tx)^{-1}\,dt$ gives \eqref{amc:sec:eq:sector}. For $x=e^v$, $R=R_b(v)$, $E=x-R$, and $D=1-t+tx$, the finite geometric identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:positive-remainder}
 f(E)-f(x)-\sum_{r=1}^K a_r(x)R^r
 =R^{K+1}\int_0^1\frac{t^{K+1}\,dt}{D^{K+1}(D-tR)}.
\end{equation}
Since $0\le tR/D\le R/x<1$, this lies between
$a_{K+1}(x)R^{K+1}$ and $(x/E)a_{K+1}(x)R^{K+1}$. Expanding the infinite geometric series and using monotone convergence proves \eqref{amc:sec:eq:infinite}; in particular the sectors are integrable.

There is an absolute $c>0$ such that $e^{-v}E_b(v)\ge c$ for $0\le v\le b$. Indeed $e^{-v}E_b(v)$ is decreasing, while Stirling's bounds show that the $\lfloor\sqrt b/2\rfloor+1$ summands of $e^{-b}E_b(b)$ immediately below degree $b$ are each at least a constant times $b^{-1/2}$. For those terms use
\[
 e^{-b}\frac{b^{b-j}}{(b-j)!}
 =\frac{e^{-b}b^b}{b!}\prod_{h=0}^{j-1}(1-h/b),
 \qquad \log(1-u)\ge-2u\quad(0\le u\le1/2).
\]
For sufficiently large $b$ and $v\ge b$, $E_b(v)\ge v^b/b!\ge2$ and $\log E_b(v)\le v$. Thus
\[
 0\le\int_b^\infty[f(E_b(v))-f(e^v)]\,dv
 \le\frac{2b!\,b^{2-b}}{b-2}=O(b^{3/2}e^{-b}).
\]
The integrated geometric bounds now give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:sandwich}
 A_{K+1,b}\le\RR_{K,b}
 \le c^{-1}A_{K+1,b}+O(b^{3/2}e^{-b}).
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:algebraic} below, together with $\beta_{K+1}>e^{-1}$, proves \eqref{amc:sec:eq:order}. Applying the same bound with $K+1$ and using
$\RR_{K,b}=A_{K+1,b}+\RR_{K+1,b}$ gives \eqref{amc:sec:eq:equivalence}. Finally, the derivative of $-r\log(1+1/r)$ is negative, by $\log(1+x)>x/(1+x)$, proving the asserted ordering of the bases.
\end{proof}

\section{Every algebraic order within a fixed sector}
\begin{theorem}\label{amc:sec:thm:algebraic}
For every fixed integer $r\ge1$ and every fixed $N\ge0$, there are rational coefficients $c_{r,j}$, with $c_{r,0}=1$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:allorders}
 A_{r,b}=C_r b^{(3-r)/2}\beta_r^b
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^N\frac{c_{r,j}}{b^j}+O_{r,N}(b^{-N-1})\right),
 \qquad C_r=\frac{r^{r+3/2}}{(r+1)^2}(2\pi)^{(1-r)/2}.
\end{equation}
The first correction is
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:first-coefficient}
 c_{r,1}=\frac{3-r}{2}-\frac{r^2(r+1)}2
          +\frac{23r}{12}+\frac{13}{12r}-\frac{r+1}{r}H_r.
\end{equation}
In particular, the first three corrections in sectors two and three are
\begin{align}
 A_{2,b}&=\frac8{9\sqrt\pi}\sqrt b\left(\frac49\right)^b
 \left(1-\frac{27}{8b}+\frac{5669}{128b^2}
             -\frac{962949}{1024b^3}+O(b^{-4})\right),\label{amc:sec:eq:r2}\\
 A_{3,b}&=\frac{81\sqrt3}{32\pi}\left(\frac{27}{64}\right)^b
 \left(1-\frac{43}{3b}+\frac{33952}{81b^2}
             -\frac{42671116}{2187b^3}+O(b^{-4})\right).\label{amc:sec:eq:r3}
\end{align}
The first sector has the exact formula
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:r1}
 A_{1,b}=(b+1)(\zeta(b+2)-1).
\end{equation}
Consequently its algebraic coefficients are $c_{1,1}=1$ and $c_{1,j}=0$ for $j\ge2$; its smaller exponential terms are retained by \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r1}.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Separate the derivative correction}
Changing variables in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:sector} gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:derivative}
 a_r(e^v)=e^{-(r+1)v}
 \frac{v-\sum_{j=1}^r(1-e^{-v})^j/j}{(1-e^{-v})^{r+1}},
\end{equation}
with continuous value $1/(r+1)$ at $v=0$. For $v\ge1$ this equals
$e^{-(r+1)v}(v-H_r)+O_r((1+v)e^{-(r+2)v})$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:J}
 J_{r,b}=\int_0^\infty(v-H_r)e^{-(r+1)v}R_b(v)^r\,dv.
\end{equation}
The integrand of $J_{r,b}$ need not be positive. Coefficientwise,
$R_b(v)^r\le\sum_{k\ge rm}r^kv^k/k!$. Hence, for fixed $h>r$,
\[
 \int_0^\infty(1+v)e^{-hv}R_b(v)^r\,dv
 \le\sum_{k\ge rm}\frac{r^k}{h^{k+1}}
                  \left(1+\frac{k+1}{h}\right)
 =O_{r,h}\!\left(m(r/h)^{rm}\right).
\]
The bounded derivative factors on $[0,1]$ and $R_b(v)\le e/m!$ give
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:derivative-error}
 A_{r,b}=J_{r,b}
 +O_r\!\left(m\left(\frac r{r+2}\right)^{rm}+(m!)^{-r}\right).
\end{equation}
The bases $(r/(r+2))^r$ are at most $1/3<e^{-1}$, so these corrections are below every fixed primary sector. All constants here may depend on the fixed $r$.

For completeness, \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r1} can be evaluated without asymptotics. Put
$F(k)=\int_0^\infty e^{-kv}R_b(v)\,dv=1/[(k-1)k^m]$ for $k\ge2$.
Expanding $a_1(e^v)=\sum_{k\ge2}((k-1)v-1)e^{-kv}$ and integrating gives
\[
 A_{1,b}=\sum_{k\ge2}\bigl(-(k-1)F'(k)-F(k)\bigr)
        =m\sum_{k\ge2}k^{-m-1}.
\]
Interchanging the sum and integral is valid by absolute integrability: replacing each linear factor by $(k-1)v+1$ gives an integrated summable bound. This also yields $J_{1,b}=m/2^{m+1}$.
\begin{writenote}
Since $A_{1,b}$ is Part~\ref{amc:part:fc}'s $L_b$, this is a second proof of the first identity in \eqref{amc:eq:variation}. Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} (Section~\ref{amc:sec:largecap}, ``Evaluation of the linear term'') integrates the double series in $(h,k)$ term by term; this Part integrates against $F(k)=1/((k-1)k^m)$ and differentiates in $k$.
\end{writenote}

\subsection{Uniform tail expansion and saddle localization}
Write $s=v/m$ and
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:Sm}
 R_b(ms)=\frac{(ms)^m}{m!}S_m(s),\qquad
 S_m(s)=\sum_{\ell\ge0}s^\ell\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(1+i/m)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
For every fixed $d<1$, to arbitrary fixed order and with any fixed number of derivatives,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:sigma}
 S_m(s)=\sum_{j=0}^N\frac{\sigma_j(s)}{m^j}+O_{N,d}(m^{-N-1}),
 \quad \sigma_0(s)=\frac1{1-s},\quad
 \sigma_j(s)=-\frac{s}{1-s}\sigma_{j-1}'(s),
\end{equation}
uniformly on $0\le s\le d$. To justify the remainder, expand
$\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(1+iu)^{-1}$ at $u=0$. Its absolute order-$N$ remainder at $u=1/m$ is bounded by
$m^{-N-1}(\ell(\ell+1)/2)^{N+1}$. This follows by bounding its alternating derivative at positive $u$ by its value at zero. Summing the polynomial bound against $s^\ell$ proves uniformity, also after fixed derivatives. The exact differential equation
$(1-s)S_m+(s/m)S_m'=1$ then proves the recurrence in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:sigma}.

Substitution in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:J} gives
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:laplace}
 J_{r,b}=\frac{m^{rm+2}}{(m!)^r}
 \int_0^\infty (s-H_r/m)S_m(s)^r e^{m\phi_r(s)}\,ds,
 \qquad\phi_r(s)=r\log s-(r+1)s.
\end{equation}
Its unique nondegenerate saddle is $s_r=r/(r+1)$, with
$\lambda_r=-\phi_r''(s_r)=(r+1)^2/r$. Choose fixed $0<a<s_r<d<1$.
On $[0,a]$, use $S_m\le(1-a)^{-1}$ and the phase gap below the maximum. On $[d,1]$, the term ratios in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:Sm} give $S_m\le m+1$, and another phase gap defeats that polynomial. Finally the original integral over $v\ge m$ is $O_r(me^{-m})$, since $R_b(v)\le e^v$. These contributions are exponentially smaller than the saddle term after Stirling's bounds are included.

On $[a,d]$, \eqref{amc:sec:eq:sigma} and the interior Laplace expansion therefore apply to every fixed order. The leading term is
\[
 J_{r,b}\sim D_r m^{(3-r)/2}\beta_r^m,
 \qquad D_r=r^{3/2}(r+1)^{r-2}(2\pi)^{(1-r)/2}.
\]
Since $D_r\beta_r=C_r$, this proves the leading constant in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:allorders}. The same argument supplies a genuine remainder at each algebraic order, not merely a formal series. Together with \eqref{amc:sec:eq:derivative-error}, it establishes the all-orders assertion of Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:algebraic}.

\subsection{A finite algorithm for all coefficients}
The included author script uses the following exact operations to find $c_{r,0},\ldots,c_{r,N}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Set $z=m^{-1/2}$ and $s=s_r+uz$. Expand
$(s-H_rz^2)(\sum_{j=0}^N\sigma_j(s)z^{2j})^r$ through degree $2N$ in $z$.
\item Multiply by the truncated exponential of the higher phase and Stirling terms
\[
 \sum_{k\ge3}\frac{r(-1)^{k-1}}{k}(u/s_r)^kz^{k-2}
 -r\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k-1)}z^{4k-2},
\]
retaining only the terms needed through $z^{2N}$.
\item Apply normalized Gaussian moments: $u^{2j}\mapsto(2j-1)!!/\lambda_r^j$, $u^{2j+1}\mapsto0$. Divide by $s_r(1-s_r)^{-r}$ to obtain relative coefficients $d_{r,j}$ in powers of $m^{-1}$.
\item Put $p=(3-r)/2$. The coefficients in $b$ are the coefficients of $q^j$ in
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^N d_{r,j}q^j(1+q)^{p-j}\pmod{q^{N+1}},\qquad q=b^{-1}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
All these quantities are rational except for the already factored leading constant. In particular the first-order calculation gives
\[
 d_{r,1}=-\frac{r^2(r+1)}2+\frac{23r}{12}
          +\frac{13}{12r}-\frac{r+1}{r}H_r,
\]
and $c_{r,1}=d_{r,1}+p$, proving \eqref{amc:sec:eq:first-coefficient}. The explicit coefficients in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r2}--\eqref{amc:sec:eq:r3} are independently reproduced as described in Section~\ref{amc:sec:sec:checks}.

\section{Reciprocation and the limits of exponential resolution}
\begin{corollary}\label{amc:sec:cor:reciprocal}
Use the combinatorial identification $\mu_b=T_b^{-1}$ from the main report (Theorem~\ref{amc:thm:fixed}), and write $\mu_\infty=1/\tau=6/\pi^2$. For every fixed $K\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:mu}
 \mu_\infty-\mu_b=\tau^{-2}\sum_{r=1}^K A_{r,b}+\widetilde\RR_{K,b},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:mu-rem}
 \widetilde\RR_{K,b}=\tau^{-2}A_{K+1,b}
 \left[1+O_K\!\left(b^{-1/2}
 \left(\frac{\beta_{K+2}}{\beta_{K+1}}\right)^b\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Thus $\widetilde\RR_{K,b}>0$ for all sufficiently large $b$ for each fixed $K$. No all-$b$ positivity of this reciprocal remainder is asserted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $\delta_b=T_b-\tau$. The exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:reciprocal-exact}
 \mu_\infty-\mu_b=\frac{\delta_b}{\tau^2}
            -\frac{\delta_b^2}{\tau^2(\tau+\delta_b)}
\end{equation}
shows the scope of the linear sector transfer. Theorem~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact} with $K=1$ and \eqref{amc:sec:eq:r1} give $\delta_b=O(b2^{-b})$, so the second term is $O(b^2 4^{-b})$.
For fixed $K$, this is exponentially smaller even than $A_{K+2,b}$, since $\beta_{K+2}>e^{-1}>1/4$. Substituting \eqref{amc:sec:eq:equivalence} in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:reciprocal-exact} therefore gives \eqref{amc:sec:eq:mu-rem}. This proof uses no expansion with a growing number of sectors.
\end{proof}
In particular,
\[
 \frac6{\pi^2}-\mu_b\sim\frac{9(b+1)}{\pi^4 2^b}.
\]
Within each fixed primary sector, the coefficients of the deficit are $\tau^{-2}C_r c_{r,j}$. The exact reciprocal correction in \eqref{amc:sec:eq:reciprocal-exact} still exists on smaller exponential scales and must not be dropped in a purported complete transseries.

\paragraph{The essential scale warning.}
Theorems~\ref{amc:sec:thm:exact} and \ref{amc:sec:cor:reciprocal} require exact sectors, or approximations with errors controlled on the next exponential scale. If each $A_{r,b}$, $1\le r\le K$, is replaced by only $N_r+1$ terms of \eqref{amc:sec:eq:allorders}, one must add the error
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:truncation}
 O\!\left(\sum_{r=1}^K
       b^{(3-r)/2-N_r-1}\beta_r^b\right).
\end{equation}
For any fixed truncation order, such a remainder in an earlier nonterminating sector generally exceeds all later exponential sectors. For example, subtracting only the displayed four terms of $A_{2,b}$ does not isolate $A_{3,b}$. Subtracting the exact sector does. The exact first sector is a useful exceptional simplification, not a cure for this issue in the later sectors.

The word ``transseries'' here describes an exact positive-sector decomposition with fixed-sector all-orders expansions. It does not assert optimal truncation, summability, uniform estimates for $r=r(b)$, a justified sum of all sector asymptotic expansions, or a theorem at the accumulation scale $e^{-b}$. Reciprocation and any subsequent inversion must preserve these distinctions.
\begin{writenote}
The word is used here in this narrow sense only. This Part constructs no transseries in the sense of the repository's transseries volume (\repopath{Analysis/Transseries/}), no Borel sum and no Stokes data, which is why it stays beside Part~\ref{amc:part:fc} in this collection.
\end{writenote}

\section{Independent checks and reproducibility}\label{amc:sec:sec:checks}
The fixed-sector derivation \cite{source} received an independent PASS audit \cite{audit} before this addendum was assembled. That audit verified the exact positive remainder, localization and uniform tail expansion, every leading constant, and the coefficients through order $b^{-3}$. It is an audit of the integral analysis; the growth-constant identification remains the separate theorem in the main report.

The independent coefficient calculation expands an exact tail-index sum against independent geometric moments, rather than importing the author's local Gaussian calculation. It reproduces the coefficient arrays for $r=1,2,3,4$ through degree three. A third included script checks $r=1,2,3$ using Gamma-centered moments. These are computational cross-checks of the finite coefficients; the all-orders remainder is justified by the proof above.

For numerical independence, the audit expands
\[
 R_b(v)^r=\sum_{k=0}^r(-1)^k\binom rk e^{(r-k)v}E_b(v)^k.
\]
If $E_b(v)^k=\sum_n d_{k,n}v^n/n!$, exact binomial convolution gives integers $d_{k,n}$ and the finite rational identity
\begin{equation}\label{amc:sec:eq:rational}
 J_{r,b}=\sum_{k=0}^r(-1)^k\binom rk\sum_n
 d_{k,n}\frac{(n+1)-(k+1)H_r}{(k+1)^{n+2}}.
\end{equation}
Exact rational arithmetic avoids cancellation and uses no quadrature. Its normalized values for $r=2,3$ and $b=50,100,200$ agree with the author's saved values to at least 44 decimal places. The script also checks the derivative formula for $r=1,\ldots,6$, $J_{1,b}=m/2^{m+1}$ for $b=0,\ldots,30$, and three exact $A_1$ values. The full author quadrature run additionally covers $b=500$ and exact $A_2,A_3$ values; it reproduces the saved JSON byte for byte in the recorded environment.

\section{Questions left open}\label{amc:sec:sec:questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item How do the fixed-sector bounds behave as $r$ grows with $b$? The saddle $r/(r+1)$ approaches the endpoint $1$, where the present compact-interval expansion loses uniformity. Identifying the correct joint regime is necessary before analyzing the accumulation near $e^{-b}$.
\item Can an optimally truncated or summable expansion for an earlier sector give a certified error below the next sector, and how do derivative exponentials and reciprocal products enter a complete smaller-scale description?
\item Can the refined exact-sector bounds yield a sharper, rounding-safe inverse for the cap at a prescribed deficit in $\mu_b$? Such an inverse needs explicit, appropriately scaled errors; finite coefficient substitution alone does not establish it.
\item For the original counting problem, can one determine fixed-cap prefactors or normalized ratio limits, and then obtain estimates uniform in a growing cap? None of the constant-level expansions here resolves those length-asymptotic questions.
\end{enumerate}
