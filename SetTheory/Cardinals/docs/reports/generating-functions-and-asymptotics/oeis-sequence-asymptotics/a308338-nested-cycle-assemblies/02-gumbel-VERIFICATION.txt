MATHEMATICAL CHECKS

The proofs were developed and checked using separate AI-agent derivations
and audits. Two final consistency reviews of the assembled TeX sections
found no substantive mathematical discrepancy. The reviewed points were:

  - Existence, uniqueness, and expansion of the size saddle.
  - A common block of consecutive positive weights bounding the entire
    Fourier circle for both the full and truncated Poisson laws.
  - The normalized central local probability and the shifted Edgeworth
    estimate used for the largest-atom theorem.
  - Cancellation of the shared O(tau) term when the Fourier integrals
    are subtracted before their errors are estimated.
  - The negative skewness contribution -B_3*S_1/(2*V^2).
  - The O(tau^(3/2)*L^3) paired remainder and both remote-tail bounds.
  - Exact lattice sign and constant terms in the universal formula.
  - The ordinary Kolmogorov constant and the positive affine obstruction.
  - The direct single-occupancy proof of the largest atom and the
    exact half-largest-jump minimax identity for continuous CDFs.
  - Global monotonicity of the explicit nonlinear calibration.
  - Uniform compact-probability quantile inversion.
  - The E1 harmonic tail, residual coefficient error, and sharp
    |a_*|*tau/(c*e*L) equivalent.
  - The nested-cycle constants and the two-term explicit saddle.
  - Restriction of the approximate-saddle substitution to continuous
    formulas; the exact phase is retained for the finer lattice formulas.

The final reviews led to corrected section references, unified notation,
and a qualification of the generic log^2(n) quantile shift: at probability
p=exp(-1), its leading quadratic term cancels. These checks are not
specialist peer review, machine-checked formalization, or an independent
publication-level validation of priority.

EXACT ARITHMETIC AND INDEPENDENT NUMERICAL CHECKS

Nested-cycle model:
  The initial 22 A308338 counts match the inspected OEIS data exactly.
  Rational finite-product coefficients and integer assembly recurrences
  were used in these checks.

  At n=50, six exact rational CDF values were compared with 2048-node
  full-circle Gauss-Legendre Fourier integration. The maximum absolute
  difference was 1.824029208924438e-14. Exact fractions are preserved in
  data/verification.json.

  At n=1000, a separate tilted coefficient recurrence was compared with
  the central Fourier integral at cutoffs 65, 90, 115, 140, 180, and 250.
  The maximum absolute difference was 1.844130930914959e-9. This recurrence
  uses double precision, not exact fractions. The check is included in
  code/nested_numerics.py and its result in data/verification.json.

  The larger experiments use n=1000,10000,100000. Replacing 256 quadrature
  nodes with 384 at the checked thresholds changed values by at most
  1.45e-15. The finite coefficient cutoff is 18,992. Node agreement does
  not bound the omitted Fourier arc or floating-point error; it is one
  convergence check. The recurrence comparison independently tests that
  arc truncation at the smallest displayed n.

Path-forest model:
  A separate implementation evaluates a rational component EGF and its
  truncated counterpart, with no nested-cycle product coefficients.
  It checks n=10^4 through n=10^12.

  Replacing 512 Gauss-Legendre nodes with 1024 changed evaluated CDFs by
  at most 2.56e-15. The largest numerically evaluated analytic omitted-arc
  bound was 1.26e-16. At n=10^4, comparison with a separate 60-digit sparse
  coefficient recurrence at three cutoffs differed by at most 3.34e-16.
  Full details are in data/forest_verification.txt.

INTERPRETATION

The general theorems are proved analytically. Computation checks finite
instances, signs, and predicted behavior; it is not their logical basis.

The retained error columns are numerical maxima over the examined
thresholds. Continuous CDF comparisons include both ends of a lattice
step. Lattice and E1 residuals in the nested-cycle table are evaluated
at lattice points. Larger path-forest cases use a dense sampled grid.
No directed-rounding interval certificate or certified global numerical
supremum is claimed. O-constants are asymptotic, with no effective finite
threshold supplied. Moderate sizes can be far from a leading equivalent.
