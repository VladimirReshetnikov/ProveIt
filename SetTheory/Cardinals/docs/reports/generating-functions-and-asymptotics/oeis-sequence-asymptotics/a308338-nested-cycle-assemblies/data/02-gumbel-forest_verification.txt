INDEPENDENT PATH-FOREST NUMERICAL VERIFICATION

Model
  p_1=1 and p_k=1/2 for k>=2; C(z)=z/(2(1-z))+z/2.
  The saddle solves n=r/(2(1-r)^2)+r/2, with tau=-log(r).
  L=log((1/2)/tau), x=tau*m-L, beta=exp(-x), s=L+x.

Compared continuous curves
  G(x)=exp(-exp(-x)).
  G(h_n(x)), where
  h_n(x)=x+tau/(4c)*(s^2-s-1-beta*(s+1)^2), c=1/2.

Reproduction
  From the project root run:
    python code/forest_numerics.py --recurrence-check
  Dependencies used: Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5, SciPy 1.17.0.
  The generated table is data/forest_errors.csv.

Method
  The numerator is the Fourier integral of exp(C(z)-z^(m+1)/(2(1-z)))
  on the saddle circle. The denominator is the integral of exp(C(z)).
  Both integrands are centered at n and expressed algebraically to avoid
  subtracting large nearly equal imaginary quantities. No asymptotic CDF
  formula is used to evaluate the conditional probability.

  Gaussian coordinates u=sqrt(B_2)*theta are integrated over [-40,40].
  Computations are repeated using 512 and 1024 Gauss-Legendre nodes.
  The maximal absolute difference between these quadratures over all
  reported sizes and thresholds is 2.56e-15. An analytic bound using the
  monotone Fourier damping bounds the omitted arc; the largest evaluated
  bound is 1.26e-16. Bounds below the smallest positive normal floating-
  point value are conservatively reported at that value, not as zero.
  These are numerical evaluations of analytic bounds, not directed-
  rounding interval certificates.

  A distinct 60-decimal-digit coefficient recurrence checks the Fourier
  computation at n=10000 and three thresholds near x=-1,0,1. The largest
  absolute difference is 3.34e-16. The recurrence follows by multiplying
  F_m'=C_m'F_m by (1-z)^2, and does not use Fourier quadrature.

Threshold coverage and interpretation
  The normalized interval [-3,8] is examined. All integer thresholds in
  this interval are used at n=10000 and n=100000. Larger sizes use 6001
  uniformly spaced normalized positions, rounded to integer thresholds,
  plus fine meshes around the limiting extremal positions. The table
  records the number of distinct thresholds actually evaluated.

  Both the left endpoint and the right limit of every sampled CDF step
  are compared with each continuous curve. The error columns are sampled
  maxima on this interval, not certified global suprema. The mesh is
  intended to check asymptotic predictions, not to replace their proofs.

Results
  kappa=((3+sqrt(5))/2)*((1+sqrt(5))/2)*exp(-(3+sqrt(5))/2)
       =0.309004785987676.
  The lattice-optimal smooth-approximation constant 1/(2e)
       =0.183939720585721.

  n         raw error/(delta*kappa)   calibrated error/(tau/(2e))
  10^4             1.861831                    5.010529
  10^5             1.569553                    3.800560
  10^6             1.417043                    2.644926
  10^7             1.328232                    1.774722
  10^8             1.271558                    1.247681
  10^9             1.232456                    1.037303
  10^10            1.203718                    1.014012
  10^11            1.181558                    1.006283
  10^12            1.163850                    1.002911

  Here delta=tau*L^2/(4c). At n=10^12 the sampled raw error is
  2.30666223e-5, and the calibrated error is 1.30443656e-7.
  The raw leading-constant convergence is slow because its next relative
  term is of order 1/L. The finite-size calibrated ratios at modest n
  are appreciably above one; the data should not be described as already
  near the limiting constant there.

Scope
  This is an independent verification of the formulas in one member of
  the weight class. It neither proves the theorem nor establishes novelty.
