SOURCE PROVENANCE AND RESEARCH STATUS
Prepared 8 October 2026 (UTC)

1. PURPOSE AND STATUS

This package was prepared in an AI-assisted mathematical research session
in response to a request for sharper asymptotic bounds related to the
openai/math and VladimirReshetnikov/ProveIt repositories. The accompanying
article contains proofs developed in this session, together with explicit
citations for the prior results it uses. It is an unrefereed research
manuscript. Its proposed contributions have not been independently
validated by a specialist referee, and its priority has not been settled
by an exhaustive literature review.

The package is a standalone deliverable intended for subsequent repository
integration. No remote repository file was changed, no commit was pushed,
and no issue, pull request, or message to a third party was created during
its preparation. Inclusion here does not imply approval or endorsement by
either repository's maintainers or by any cited author.

No Lean, Coq, Isabelle, or other machine-checked formal proof is supplied
or claimed. The numerical and symbolic checks are supporting checks, not
formal certificates for the infinite asymptotic estimates. See the article
and numerical verification files for their precise scope.

4. LITERATURE REVIEW BOUNDARY

The directed review covered expansive assemblies and multisets, largest-
component Gumbel laws, random Lah assemblies and sets of lists, path
forests, second-order extreme-value expansions, convergence rates, and
the component coefficients for permutations with distinct cycle lengths.
The repository documents and primary sources above were the substantive
inputs. Search-result absence was not treated as proof of absence from
the literature. Searches were bounded in time and scope and did not
systematically inspect every work citing these papers, every thesis, or
every non-English source.

The qualitative Gumbel laws and the component coefficient estimate are
explicitly prior results. The universal conditioning profile, the affine
normalization obstruction, the continuous calibration at the leading
lattice lower bound, and the further lattice/component corrections are
proved in the accompanying manuscript as proposed contributions of this
session. The reviewed sources did not supply these exact statements, but
this fact alone does not establish worldwide priority or justify calling
the work a confirmed breakthrough. Specialist review and additional
priority checking remain appropriate before publication.

5. PROOF AND COMPUTATION BOUNDARY

The general probability arguments start from the article's stated
nonnegative weight hypothesis p_k=c+O(1/k), c>0, and use conditioned
Poisson identities, Fourier inversion, Taylor estimates and elementary
sum bounds. The more refined model-dependent result has its own stronger
weight hypothesis; it must not be applied under the weaker hypothesis
without the additional assumption.

The nested-cycle specialization uses the cited published coefficient
estimate. Exact finite coefficient checks and numerical Fourier inversion
check examples and constants. They do not prove a uniform asymptotic
bound for all sufficiently large n, provide an effective threshold for
the O-constants, or establish a computer-assisted proof. Numerical
quadrature comparisons, grid limitations, and the independent path-
forest coefficient check are documented in the data and code artifacts.

The package's sources, bibliography, proofs, numerical code, and proposed
research questions were assembled for review. They should be assessed
on their explicit arguments and reproducible evidence, with the above
source and status boundaries preserved during repository integration.
