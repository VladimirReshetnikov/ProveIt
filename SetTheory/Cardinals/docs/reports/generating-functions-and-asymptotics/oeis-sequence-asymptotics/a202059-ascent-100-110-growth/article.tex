{\large Report \ReportNumber}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\LARGE\bfseries The Sharp Linear Logarithmic Constant\\[5pt] for 100 Avoiding Ascent Sequences}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\large Triangular comparisons and controlled inverse bounds}\par\vspace{7pt}
5 October 2026
\end{center}
\begin{abstract}
Let $a_n$ count ordinary ascent sequences avoiding the arbitrary-subsequence pattern $100$, and let $e_n$ count those avoiding both $000$ and $100$. For either $u_n=a_n$ or $u_n=e_n$, we prove
\[
 \log u_n=n\bigl(\log n-2\log\log n+\log2-1\bigr)
       +O\!\left(\frac{n(\log\log n)^2}{\log n}\right).
\]
Consequently $(\log n)^2(u_n/n!)^{1/n}\to2$. The upper comparison refines an earlier marked-occurrence encoding by retaining the exact increasing-run index. The lower comparison converts triangular weak compositions into positive-block words, repairing empty rows and explicitly bounding three many-to-one fibers. An elementary localization proof supplies the required logarithmic estimate for the historically known triangular sum. A doubled seed gives normalized-root liminf at least $1/2$ for $110$ avoidance and for simultaneous $000,100,110$ avoidance; both have limsup at most $2$. We also prove two-term first-crossing inverses, with a bracket rather than a determined second coefficient for these latter classes. No relative counting equivalent or existence of their linear logarithmic constants is claimed.
\end{abstract}
