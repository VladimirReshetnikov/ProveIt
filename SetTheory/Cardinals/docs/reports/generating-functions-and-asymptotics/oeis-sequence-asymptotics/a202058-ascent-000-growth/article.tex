\begin{abstract}
Let $a_n$ count ascent sequences in which every value occurs at most twice (OEIS A202058), put $T=3\pi^2/8$, $\mu=1/T=8/(3\pi^2)$, and $c_n=a_nT^n/n!$. This report prints four research manuscripts as Parts~I--IV. Part~I proves that the exponential generating function has radius $T$ and that $(a_n/n!)^{1/n}\to\mu$, the constant conjectured by Conway, Conway, Elvey Price and Guttmann, with a coarse error $O(n^{9/13}(\log n)^3)$ in $\log c_n$. Part~II proves $-O(n^{2/3}(\log n)^{5/3})\le\log c_n\le O((\log n)^2)$, a controlled Lambert~$W$ inverse, exact counts through $400$, and the conjecture $\log c_n\sim\frac23(\log n)^2$. Part~III proves $\log A(t)\sim\frac23\log^2\frac1{T-t}$, the cumulative law $\log\sum_{j\le N}c_j\sim\frac23(\log N)^2$ and $\limsup\log c_n/(\log n)^2=\frac23$, so Part~II's conjecture holds in limsup and cumulative form. Part~IV proves $a_{n+1}/((n+1)a_n)=\mu+O(n^{-1/4}(\log n)^{3/4})$ and improves the lower bound to $-O(\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2})$. The pointwise law $\log c_n\sim\frac23(\log n)^2$, normalized log-concavity, a full asymptotic equivalent, an amplitude and an all-orders expansion remain open. The report is AI-assisted and unrefereed, and nothing in it is formalized.
\end{abstract}

\part{Factorial growth of ascent sequences avoiding 000}\label{a58:part:fd}
\partsource{Batch-77 manuscript 37, archive \file{oeis-a202058-report.zip} (delivered as \file{a202058-report.tex}, 9 pages), dated 1 October 2026. Delivered author line: ``A proof and reproducible research report''. Printed as delivered, with \textbf{[write]} notes; section, statement and equation numbers are those of the delivery.}
\begin{partabstract}
We prove that the number $a_n$ of ascent sequences in which each value occurs at most twice satisfies
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}(a_n/n!)^{1/n}=\frac{8}{3\pi^2}.
\]
This is the factorial exponential constant conjectured by Conway, Conway, Elvey Price and Guttmann. The proof starts from their exact compacted recurrence. A positive transfer operator admits uniform exponential barriers whose characteristic curve has endpoint $3\pi^2/8$. Stopped Feynman--Kac comparison proves the exact exponential-generating-function radius. A growing-state coefficient argument upgrades the resulting limsup to a full limit. We also obtain a non-optimal logarithmic error bound and a controlled leading Lambert $W$ inverse. The ratio limit, prefactor, and possible stretched-exponential corrections remain unresolved.
\end{partabstract}

\section{Result and scope}
An ascent sequence is a word $w_1\cdots w_n$ of nonnegative integers such that $w_1=0$ and
\[
 w_i\le1+\asc(w_1\cdots w_{i-1})\quad(i\ge2),\qquad
 \asc(w)=\#\{i:w_i<w_{i+1}\}.
\]
Avoidance of the pattern 000 means that no value occurs three times. Let $a_n$ count these words, with $a_0=1$. The sequence is OEIS A202058 \cite{OEIS}.

\begin{theorem}\label{a58:fd:thm:main}
Put
\[
 T=\frac{3\pi^2}{8},\qquad \mu=T^{-1}=0.27018982304623405718\ldots.
\]
Then the exponential generating function $A(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nt^n/n!$ has radius $T$, and
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:limit}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{a_n}{n!}\right)^{1/n}=\mu.
\end{equation}
More quantitatively,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:coarse}
 \log a_n=\log(n!)-n\log T+O\bigl(n^{9/13}(\log n)^3\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Conway--Conway--Elvey Price--Guttmann \cite{CCEG} conjectured the constant $8/(3\pi^2)$ from long exact series. Their Section 5 also leaves a possible stretched-exponential factor unresolved; its numerical discussion is more cautious than the simplified introduction. The recurrence and its compaction argument are theirs. The contribution proved here is the exact factorial growth constant, including existence of the normalized root limit, together with the coarse error and inverse below. A scoped literature search found no later proof; this is not a comprehensive novelty certification before publication.

The exponent $9/13$ in \eqref{a58:fd:eq:coarse} comes from coarse uniform bounds. It is not a prediction of the actual subexponential scale. In particular, this report does not establish $a_n\sim Cn!\mu^n$, a ratio limit, a power prefactor, or an all-orders expansion.

\section{The exact compacted recurrence}\label{a58:fd:sec:recurrence}
Twice-used labels can no longer occur, and may be deleted from the active alphabet. The remaining once-used labels can be compacted to an initial segment. In the notation of \cite[Sections 2.2 and 2.6]{CCEG}, if $F_0(a,\ell,s)=1$, then
\begin{align}\label{a58:fd:eq:paperrec}
F_n(a,\ell,s)={}&\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}
 F_{n-1}(a+\one_{i>\ell}-1,i-1,s-1)\notag\\
&+\sum_{i=s}^{a+1}F_{n-1}(a+\one_{i>\ell},i,s+1),
\end{align}
and $a_n=F_{n-1}(0,0,1)$ for $n\ge1$. The adjusted quantities $a$ and $\ell$ may equal $-1$.

For completeness, the compaction mechanism is as follows. If adjacent labels $i,i+1$ have different remaining capacities and $i<\ell$, reverse every maximal suffix block using only these two labels, and interchange the two labels. This interchanges their multiplicities and preserves the total number of ascents in each block, including the comparisons with outside labels. Both labels are already allowed because $i+1\le\ell$. Thus the ascent bounds inside a block remain valid, and those after the block see the same number of preceding ascents. The operation is an involution. In the binary-capacity setting needed here, it exchanges a once-used and an unused label below the last value. After appending a previously unused $i$, bubble it down through unused labels until the once-used set is again an initial segment; after a repetition, delete the exhausted label. This gives \eqref{a58:fd:eq:paperrec}. The canonical states need not themselves retain every property of an original prefix; they retain its suffix counts.

Set $m=a+2$, $k=\ell+1$, and $u=m-s$. The following slightly larger invariant state space is convenient:
\[
 \mathcal S=\{(s,u,k):s\ge0,\ u\ge1,\ 0\le k<s+u\},\qquad m=s+u.
\]
For $x=(s,u,k)$, choose each integer $i\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$ once and form the child
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:children}
 x_i=\begin{cases}
 (s-1,u,i),&i<s,\ i<k,\\
 (s-1,u+1,i),&i<s,\ i\ge k,\\
 (s+1,u,i+1),&i\ge s,\ i\ge k,\\
 (s+1,u-1,i+1),&i\ge s,\ i<k.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Call the first two types $D$ and the latter two $N$, for a duplicate or new value. An ascent means $i\ge k$. The last case requires $u\ge2$, so $\mathcal S$ is invariant. Every step changes $m$ by at most one.

Define the positive operator
\[
 (\TT f)(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(x_i),\qquad x_0=(1,1,1).
\]
Then $\TT^n\one(x_0)=a_{n+1}$. Write
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:F}
 \mathcal F(t,x)=\sum_{n\ge0}\TT^n\one(x)\frac{t^n}{n!},
\end{equation}
allowing the value $+\infty$ for $t\ge0$. We have $\mathcal F(t,x_0)=A'(t)$, so the two series have the same radius.

\section{A characteristic curve and rank profile}
For $q\ge0$ define
\begin{align}\label{a58:fd:eq:parameters}
 p(q)&=\tfrac12\log(2e^q-1),& R(q)&=2-e^{-q},\notag\\
 b(q)&=\frac{e^{p(q)}(1-e^{-q})}{q},&
 t(q)&=\int_0^q\frac{dv}{b(v)},
\end{align}
with $b(0)=1$. Put $T_*=t(\infty)$ and use $q=q(t)$ for $0\le t<T_*$. Along this curve,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:flow}
 \frac{dq}{dt}=\frac{e^p(1-e^{-q})}{q},\qquad
 \frac{dp}{dt}=\frac{e^{-p}(e^q-1)}{q}.
\end{equation}
The identity $e^{2p}=2e^q-1$ verifies both equations and their initial values.

For a state $x=(s,u,k)$ let
\begin{align}\label{a58:fd:eq:profile}
 D&=s+Ru,& h(y)&=\min(y,s)+R\max(y-s,0),\notag\\
 r(y)&=h(y)/D,&
 \psi(t,x)&=\exp\{ps+qu-qr(k)\}.
\end{align}
In particular $0\le r(k)\le1$, $\psi(0,x)=1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:psibounds}
 e^{ps+qu-q}\le\psi(t,x)\le e^{ps+qu}.
\end{equation}
Write
\[
 A_0=e^{-p}(e^q-1),\qquad B_0=e^p(1-e^{-q})=RA_0,
 \qquad \lambda=\frac{A_0s+B_0u}{q}=\frac{A_0D}{q}.
\]
The removable value of $\lambda$ at $q=0$ is $m$.

\begin{lemma}\label{a58:fd:lem:residual}
Uniformly over $x\in\mathcal S$ and $0\le t<T_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:residual}
 \left|\frac{\partial_t\psi(t,x)}{\psi(t,x)}
       -\frac{\TT\psi(t,x)}{\psi(t,x)}\right|
 \le C(q),\qquad C(q)=8(1+q)e^{5q/8}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We keep all three sources of error uniform in the state.

\paragraph{The frozen rank integral.}
For real $y\in[0,m]$ put
\[
 w(y)=\begin{cases}
 e^{-p+q\one_{y\ge k}},&y<s,\\
 e^{p-q\one_{y<k}},&y\ge s,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad g(y)=w(y)e^{-q(r(y)-r(k))}.
\]
Direct integration gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:integral}
 \int_0^m g(y)\,dy=\lambda.
\end{equation}
For a verification, set $\phi(y)=e^{-qh(y)/D}$. On the two intervals,
$\lambda\phi'=-A_0\phi$ and $\lambda\phi'=-B_0\phi$, respectively, while
$\phi(m)=e^{-q}\phi(0)$. If $J(k)=\phi(k)\int g$, these derivative identities show that $J-\lambda\phi$ is constant. At $k=0$, writing $X=e^{-qs/D}$ and using $e^p/R=e^{q-p}$ gives
\[
 J(0)=\frac Dq\left[e^{q-p}(1-X)+\frac{e^p}{R}(X-e^{-q})\right]
     =\frac Dq e^{-p}(e^q-1)=\lambda.
\]
This proves \eqref{a58:fd:eq:integral}, including the continuous value at $q=0$.

The inequalities
\[
 q/2\le p\le q/2+(\log2)/2
\]
show that $g(y)\le\sqrt2e^{q/2}$. On an ascent interval use $r(y)\ge r(k)$; on a descent interval use $r(k)-r(y)\le1$. On each of the at most three intervals separated by the integers $s,k$, $g$ decreases. The left Riemann sum therefore differs from its integral by at most
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:quaderror}
 \left|\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}g(i)-\lambda\right|\le3\sqrt2e^{q/2}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The shifted states.}
Let $r_i$ be the rank fraction at the exact child $x_i$. In the order of the four cases in \eqref{a58:fd:eq:children},
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:rankchildren}
 r_i=\frac{i}{D-1},\quad
 \frac{i}{D+R-1},\quad
 \frac{h(i)+1}{D+1},\quad
 \frac{h(i)+1}{D+1-R}.
\end{equation}
The relevant denominators are positive. Subtraction yields $|r_i-r(i)|\le4/D$. Except in the $D$-ascent case, $r_i\ge r(i)$, so the exact transition ratio $\psi(x_i)/\psi(x)$ is no greater than its frozen counterpart $g(i)$.

For a $D$ ascent, $k\le i<s$ and
\[
 r(k)-r_i\le\frac{k(R-1)}{D(D+R-1)}
 \le\frac{D-2}{D^2}\le\frac18.
\]
Here $k\le s-1$, $D\ge s+1$, and $1\le R\le2$. Thus every exact or frozen ratio is at most $\sqrt2e^{5q/8}$. The exponential mean-value inequality gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\psi(x_i)}{\psi(x)}-g(i)\right|
 \le\frac{4q}{D}\sqrt2e^{5q/8}.
\]
Since $m\le D$, its sum is at most $4\sqrt2q e^{5q/8}$.

\paragraph{The time derivative.}
At fixed state, differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{\partial_t\psi}{\psi}
 =\lambda-q'\{r(k)+qe^{-q}\partial_Rr(k)\}.
\]
The two expressions for $\partial_Rr(k)$ are $-ku/D^2$ for $k\le s$ and $-s(m-k)/D^2$ for $k\ge s$. Both lie in $[-1/(4R),0]$. The expression in braces has absolute value at most one. Also $q'\le\sqrt2e^{q/2}$. Combining these bounds with \eqref{a58:fd:eq:quaderror} proves
\[
 |\partial_t\psi/\psi-\TT\psi/\psi|
 \le4\sqrt2e^{q/2}+4\sqrt2q e^{5q/8}
 \le8(1+q)e^{5q/8}.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Comparison for the infinite state space}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:E}
 E(q)=\int_0^q\frac{C(v)}{b(v)}\,dv,\qquad
 G_\pm(t,x)=e^{\pm E(q(t))}\psi(t,x).
\end{equation}
Lemma \ref{a58:fd:lem:residual} gives $\partial_tG_-\le\TT G_-$ and $\partial_tG_+\ge\TT G_+$, with initial values one.

\begin{lemma}\label{a58:fd:lem:comparison}
For every state and $0\le t<T_*$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:comparison}
 e^{-E(q)}\psi(t,x)\le\mathcal F(t,x)\le e^{E(q)}\psi(t,x).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider the continuous-time chain which takes each of the $m$ transitions in \eqref{a58:fd:eq:children} at rate one. Its generator is $\mathcal L=\TT-mI$. Its jump rate is $m$, and each jump increases $m$ by at most one, so a Yule-process domination proves nonexplosion. Let $\tau_N$ be the first exit from $m\le N$. Each stopped state space is finite.

Summing over its finite jump paths gives the extended Feynman--Kac identity
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:FK}
 \mathcal F(t,x)=\E_x\exp\left\{\int_0^t m(X_v)\,dv\right\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the holding-time exponential is canceled by the displayed weight. Each $n$-step path then contributes the simplex volume $t^n/n!$, exactly as in \eqref{a58:fd:eq:F}. Nonexplosion and Tonelli justify the sum even when it is infinite.

For the supersolution, stopped Dynkin comparison applied to
\[
 \exp\left\{\int_0^v m(X_z)\,dz\right\}G_+(t-v,X_v)
\]
gives
\[
 \E_x\left[\exp\left\{\int_0^t m(X_v)\,dv\right\};\ \tau_N>t\right]
 \le G_+(t,x).
\]
Letting $N\to\infty$ proves the upper bound and finiteness before $T_*$.

The lower comparison requires control of exit terms. Choose $\varepsilon>0$ such that $t+\varepsilon<T_*$. Uniformly in $v\in[0,t]$ and states $y$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:tailratio}
 \frac{G_-(v,y)}{G_+(v+\varepsilon,y)}
 \le e^{q(t+\varepsilon)}e^{-\eta m(y)},
\end{equation}
where $\eta>0$ is the minimum, on this compact interval, of both
$p(v+\varepsilon)-p(v)$ and $q(v+\varepsilon)-q(v)$. These functions are strictly increasing. Inequality \eqref{a58:fd:eq:tailratio} follows directly from \eqref{a58:fd:eq:psibounds} and $E\ge0$.

Stopped Dynkin comparison for $G_-$ bounds $G_-(t,x)$ above by the terminal expectation in \eqref{a58:fd:eq:FK}, restricted to $\tau_N>t$, plus the exit contribution. Use \eqref{a58:fd:eq:tailratio} and then the later supersolution to bound the latter by
\[
 e^{q(t+\varepsilon)}e^{-\eta(N+1)}G_+(t+\varepsilon,x).
\]
It tends to zero. Passing to the limit gives the lower bound. This step avoids an unjustified comparison of an arbitrary subsolution with an unbounded positive operator.
\end{proof}

For $q\ge1$,
\[
 b(q)\ge(1-e^{-1})e^{q/2}/q,
 \qquad E(q)=O\bigl((1+q)^3e^{q/8}\bigr)=o(e^p).
\]
This deliberately loose estimate suffices for all subsequent arguments.

\section{The exact radius}\label{a58:fd:sec:radius}
The upper comparison proves that the radius of $\mathcal F(t,x_0)$ is at least $T_*$. Positivity and Tonelli give the semigroup identity
\[
 \mathcal F(t+\delta,x_0)
 =\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{\delta^j}{j!}
       (\TT^j\mathcal F(t,\cdot))(x_0),
\]
with infinity allowed. There is a path of $j$ successive new-maximal-label steps from $x_0$ to $(j+1,1,j+1)$. At this state the $qu-qr$ term is nonnegative. Hence
\[
 \mathcal F(t+\delta,x_0)
 \ge e^{p-E(q)}\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{(\delta e^p)^j}{j!}
 =\exp\{p-E(q)+\delta e^p\}.
\]
For any fixed $\delta>0$ the right side tends to infinity as $t\uparrow T_*$, because $E(q)=o(e^p)$. Monotonicity gives $\mathcal F(T_*+\delta,x_0)=\infty$. Its radius is therefore exactly $T_*$.

To evaluate the endpoint, put $y=\sqrt{2e^q-1}$ in \eqref{a58:fd:eq:parameters}:
\[
 T_*=\int_1^\infty\frac{2\log((y^2+1)/2)}{y^2-1}\,dy.
\]
Integrate by parts using $\log((y-1)/(y+1))$ as an antiderivative of $2/(y^2-1)$, and then set $z=(y-1)/(y+1)$. Both endpoint terms vanish, and
\begin{align*}
 T_*&=-2\int_0^1\log z\,\frac{1+z}{(1-z)(1+z^2)}\,dz\\
 &=-2\int_0^1\frac{\log z}{1-z}\,dz
   -2\int_0^1\frac{z\log z}{1+z^2}\,dz
 =\frac{\pi^2}{3}+\frac{\pi^2}{24}=\frac{3\pi^2}{8}.
\end{align*}
The geometric-series integrations are absolutely integrable. We have proved the radius assertion and the corresponding factorial-normalized limsup. The next section establishes the full limit.

\section{From the radius to the full coefficient limit}\label{a58:fd:sec:fulllimit}
For $x_N=(N,1,N)$, $N\ge1$, the uniform comparison becomes
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:growing}
 \log\mathcal F(t(q),x_N)=Np(q)+O(E(q)+q),
\end{equation}
uniformly in $N$, since the extra rank term is $qR/(N+R)\in[0,q]$.
Put
\[
 d(q)=\frac{d}{dq}\log t(q)=\frac1{t(q)b(q)},\qquad
 M(q)=\frac{p_q(q)}{d(q)}=t(q)p_t(t(q)).
\]
As $q\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:largescales}
 p_q=\tfrac12+O(e^{-q}),\qquad
 d(q)\sim\frac{q e^{-q/2}}{\sqrt2T},\qquad
 M(q)\sim\frac{T e^{q/2}}{\sqrt2q}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{a58:fd:lem:concentration}
Let $c_j=\TT^j\one(x_N)$, and define
\[
 \Prb(J=j)=\frac{c_jt(q)^j}{j!\mathcal F(t(q),x_N)},\qquad K=NM(q).
\]
For all sufficiently large $q$ and $0<\epsilon<1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:chernoff}
 \Prb(|J-K|>\epsilon K)
 \le2\exp\left\{-\frac{3N\epsilon^2}{128}
                    +2E(q+1)+O(q+1)\right\},
\end{equation}
uniformly in $N$ and $\epsilon$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $|v|\le1$ set
\[
 H_q(v)=p(q+v)-p(q)-M(q)\{\log t(q+v)-\log t(q)\}.
\]
We have $H_q(0)=H_q'(0)=0$. The exact formulas
\[
 p_q=1/R,\qquad
 \frac{d'}d=-d-\frac1R-\frac1{e^q-1}+\frac1q
\]
show, uniformly for bounded $v$, that
\[
 H_q''(v)\longrightarrow\tfrac14e^{-v/2},\qquad
 M(q)d(q+v)\longrightarrow\tfrac12e^{-v/2}.
\]
Thus for all sufficiently large $q$, $|H_q(v)|\le v^2/2$ on $[-1,1]$. Also, for $0<\eta<1/2$,
\[
 M(q)|\log t(q\pm\eta)-\log t(q)|\ge\eta/4.
\]
Choose $\eta=\epsilon/8$. The Chernoff upper-tail bound, with the generating function tilted from $t(q)$ to $t(q+\eta)$, has logarithm at most
\[
 NH_q(\eta)-\epsilon NM(q)\{\log t(q+\eta)-\log t(q)\}
       +2E(q+1)+O(q+1).
\]
It is at most $-3N\epsilon^2/128+2E(q+1)+O(q+1)$. The tilt $q-\eta$ gives the same bound for the lower tail. All error constants follow uniformly from \eqref{a58:fd:eq:growing}.
\end{proof}

For each sufficiently large integer $n$, take
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:choices}
 q=\log n,\qquad \epsilon=(\log n)^{-2},\qquad
 N=\left\lfloor\frac{(1-4\epsilon)n}{M(q)}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 N\sim\frac{\sqrt2}{T}\sqrt n\log n,\qquad
 K=(1-4\epsilon)n+O(\sqrt n/\log n),
\]
while $N\epsilon^2$ has order $\sqrt n/(\log n)^3$ and
$E(q+1)=O(n^{1/8}(\log n)^3)=o(N\epsilon^2)$. Lemma \ref{a58:fd:lem:concentration} puts at least half the probability in $|j-K|\le\epsilon K$. Some integer $j$ in this interval therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:extract}
 \log c_j\ge\log(j!)-j\log t(q)+Np(q)-E(q)-O(\log n).
\end{equation}
Indeed, there are at most $2\epsilon K+3$ integers in the interval, and the lower comparison gives $\log\mathcal F\ge Np-E$.

The floor error in \eqref{a58:fd:eq:choices} is $o(\epsilon n)$, and $N=o(\epsilon n)$. Consequently
\[
 (1-6\epsilon)n\le j,\qquad N+j\le n
\]
for large $n$. The initial state reaches $x_N$ after $N-1$ new-maximal-label steps, so $a_{N+j}\ge c_j$. Every valid ascent sequence extends injectively by appending one plus its current number of ascents. The appended label is new: induction gives $\max w\le\asc(w)$ for every ascent sequence. Hence $a_n\ge a_{N+j}\ge c_j$.

It follows that
\[
 \log(a_n/n!)\ge-j\log t(q)-\log(n!/j!)+Np(q)-E(q)-O(\log n)
             \ge-n\log T-o(n).
\]
Here $j/n\to1$, $t(q)\to T$, and
$\log(n!/j!)\le6\epsilon n\log n=o(n)$. The radius theorem supplies the matching limsup, proving \eqref{a58:fd:eq:limit}.

\section{A coarse error and a threshold inverse}\label{a58:fd:sec:coarse}
For a quantitative version, repeat the coefficient argument with
\[
 q=\frac8{13}\log n,\qquad
 \epsilon=n^{-4/13}(\log n)^2.
\]
Now $N$ has order $n^{9/13}\log n$, $E(q+1)=O(n^{1/13}(\log n)^3)$, and $N\epsilon^2$ has order $n^{1/13}(\log n)^5$. Thus the same Chernoff estimate applies, and $N=o(\epsilon n)$. The factorial loss is
\[
 O(\epsilon n\log n)=O\bigl(n^{9/13}(\log n)^3\bigr),
\]
which proves the lower half of \eqref{a58:fd:eq:coarse}.

For the upper half, coefficient positivity and monotonicity give
\[
 \frac{a_n}{n!}\le\frac{A(t)}{t^n},\qquad
 A(t)=1+\int_0^t\mathcal F(v,x_0)\,dv\le1+t\mathcal F(t,x_0).
\]
The superbarrier yields $\log A(t(q))\le E(q)+O(q+1)$, whereas
$T-t(q)=O(qe^{-q/2})$. At the same choice of $q$ this gives
\[
 \log(a_n/n!)+n\log T
 \le O\{E(q)+q+nq e^{-q/2}\}
 =O(n^{9/13}\log n).
\]
This completes Theorem \ref{a58:fd:thm:main}.

\begin{corollary}\label{a58:fd:cor:inverse}
For $y\to\infty$ let $L=\log y$ and
\[
 N(L)=\frac{L}{W(L/(eT))},
\]
where $W$ is the positive real Lambert function. The integer threshold
$\nu(y)=\min\{n\ge0:a_n\ge y\}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fd:eq:inverse}
 \nu(y)=N(L)+O\bigl(N(L)^{9/13}(\log N(L))^2\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Stirling's formula and \eqref{a58:fd:eq:coarse} give
\[
 \log a_n=f(n)+O(n^{9/13}(\log n)^3),\qquad
 f(x)=x\log\frac{x}{eT}.
\]
The chosen $N(L)$ is the positive solution of $f(N)=L$. Since
$f'(x)=\log(x/T)\sim\log N$ near $N$, evaluating at
$N\pm C N^{9/13}(\log N)^2$ brackets $L$ for a sufficiently large fixed $C$. The error envelope is of the same order throughout this $o(N)$ interval. Monotonicity of $a_n$ and integer rounding prove \eqref{a58:fd:eq:inverse}.
\end{proof}

The inverse is a controlled leading approximation. This does not provide unit-level rounding accuracy or an all-orders inverse, nor a multiplicative asymptotic equivalent for $a_n$.

\part{Finer bounds for ascent sequences avoiding 000}\label{a58:part:fn}
\partsource{Batch-77 manuscript 36, archive \file{oeis-a202058-fine-report.zip} (delivered as \file{a202058-fine-addendum.tex}, 18 pages), dated 2 October 2026. Delivered author line: ``A mathematical addendum with reproducible computations''. The delivered ``1 October report'' and ``frozen foundation'' are Part~\ref{a58:part:fd}. Delivered Section $k$ is Section $k+8$ here; the delivered Appendix~A is Section~\ref{a58:fn:sec:bundle}.}
\begin{partabstract}
Let $a_n$ count ascent sequences in which each value occurs at most twice, and let $\mu=8/(3\pi^2)$. Building on the exact characteristic endpoint and normalized root limit in the bundled 1 October report, we prove
\[
 -O\bigl(n^{2/3}(\log n)^{5/3}\bigr)
 \le \log\frac{a_n}{n!\mu^n}
 \le O\bigl((\log n)^2\bigr).
\]
A signed, weighted residual estimate and an exact state-padding inequality give uniform quadratic bounds along the characteristic curve. Positivity yields the coefficient upper bound; a growing-state Chernoff argument yields the weaker lower bound. We give a controlled asymmetric Lambert $W$ inverse. Exact counts through $400$, floating computations through $1000$, and a formal frozen-kernel calculation motivate a separate conjecture with leading coefficient $2/3$ on the logarithmic-square scale. That coefficient, a matching fine lower bound, a ratio limit, and a full asymptotic equivalent remain unproved here.
\end{partabstract}

\section{Statements and scope}
An ascent sequence of length $n\ge1$ is a word $w_1\cdots w_n$ of nonnegative integers with $w_1=0$ and
\[
 w_i\le1+\asc(w_1\cdots w_{i-1})\quad(i\ge2),\qquad
 \asc(w)=\#\{i:w_i<w_{i+1}\}.
\]
The classical pattern $000$ is present when a value appears in three positions, not necessarily consecutive. Thus avoidance means that every value occurs at most twice. Let $a_n$ count these words and put $a_0=1$. This is OEIS A202058 \cite{OEIS}; its first terms are
\[
1,1,2,4,10,27,83,277,1015,4007,17047.
\]
Throughout this addendum all logarithms are natural. Set
\[
 T=\frac{3\pi^2}{8},\qquad \mu=T^{-1},\qquad
 \beta_n=\frac{a_n}{n!},\qquad h_n=\log\frac{\beta_n}{\mu^n}.
\]
The symbol $b(q)$ below is a characteristic speed and is unrelated to $\beta_n$.

\begin{theorem}[Asymmetric coefficient bounds]\label{a58:fn:thm:main}
There are constants $C_-,C_+>0$ and an integer $n_0$ such that for every $n\ge n_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:main}
 -C_-n^{2/3}(\log n)^{5/3}
 \le h_n\le C_+(\log(n+2))^2.
\end{equation}
In particular, there is no asymptotic equivalent of the form
\[
 a_n\sim C_0 n!\mu^n\exp(cn^\sigma)n^g(\log n)^h
\]
with $C_0,c,\sigma>0$ and fixed real $g,h$.
\end{theorem}
The last assertion concerns a \emph{positive} stretched-exponential correction. The theorem does not exclude all negative stretched-exponential corrections and does not identify the true size or sign of $h_n$. The exponent $2/3$ on the lower side of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:main} is a loss in a broad-window extraction argument, not a proposed stretched-exponential exponent.

\begin{corollary}[Controlled inverse]\label{a58:fn:cor:maininverse}
For real $y\to\infty$, define
\[
 N(y)=\min\{n\ge1:\log a_n\ge y\},\qquad
 x=x(y)=\frac{y}{W(y/(eT))},
\]
where $W$ is the positive real Lambert function. Then
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:inverse}
 x-O(\log x)\le N(y)\le
 x+O\bigl(x^{2/3}(\log x)^{2/3}\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
Here the threshold variable is the \emph{logarithm} of a count. For the threshold $\min\{n:a_n\ge Y\}$, substitute $y=\log Y$. This convention avoids confusion with the differently named variables in the frozen report. Neither side gives unit-level rounding accuracy or an all-orders inverse.

The present argument improves that error asymmetrically. The original report, its TeX, and its proof notes are bundled unchanged. The exact compacted recurrence is due to Conway, Conway, Elvey Price and Guttmann \cite{CCEG}. \section{Exact states and the frozen characteristic foundation}\label{a58:fn:sec:foundation}
\subsection{The compacted positive operator}
Once a label has been used twice it cannot recur. Delete such labels from the active alphabet and compact once-used labels into an initial segment, as justified in \cite[Sections 2.2 and 2.6]{CCEG} and explained in Section~\ref{a58:fd:sec:recurrence} (Part~\ref{a58:part:fd}). The resulting suffix-count states lie in the invariant space
\[
 \calS=\{(s,u,k):s\ge0,\ u\ge1,\ 0\le k<s+u\},\qquad m=s+u.
\]
Here $s$ is the number of once-used active labels, $u$ the number of unused active labels, and $k$ is one plus the adjusted last label. Canonical states retain suffix counts; they need not retain every property of an original uncompacted prefix.

For $x=(s,u,k)$ and each integer $0\le i<m$, form exactly one child
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:children}
 x_i=\begin{cases}
 (s-1,u,i),&i<s,\ i<k,\quad D\text{ descent},\\
 (s-1,u+1,i),&i<s,\ i\ge k,\quad D\text{ ascent},\\
 (s+1,u,i+1),&i\ge s,\ i\ge k,\quad N\text{ ascent},\\
 (s+1,u-1,i+1),&i\ge s,\ i<k,\quad N\text{ descent}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The letters $D,N$ mean duplicate and new. The last case requires $u\ge2$: if $u=1$ then $i\ge s$ forces $i=s$ and $k\le s$. Thus every child remains in $\calS$. The value of $m$ changes by at most one at a step.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:operator}
 (\TT f)(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(x_i),\qquad x_0=(1,1,1),\qquad
 \FF(t,x)=\sum_{j\ge0}(\TT^j\one)(x)\frac{t^j}{j!}.
\end{equation}
For $t\ge0$ the series initially permits $+\infty$. Exactly,
\[
 (\TT^j\one)(x_0)=a_{j+1},\qquad
 \FF(t,x_0)=A'(t),\qquad A(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{a_n}{n!}t^n.
\]
These identities are combinatorial; no limiting approximation has entered.

\subsection{Characteristic curve and rank}
For $q\ge0$ set
\begin{align}\label{a58:fn:eq:params}
 p(q)&=\tfrac12\log(2e^q-1),& R(q)&=2-e^{-q},\notag\\
 b(q)&=\frac{e^{p(q)}(1-e^{-q})}{q},&
 t(q)&=\int_0^q\frac{dv}{b(v)},
\end{align}
where $b(0)=1$ by continuity. For $0\le t<T$ write $q=q(t)$. Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:flow}
 q_t=b,\qquad p_q=\frac1R,\qquad p_t=\frac bR,
 \qquad R_q=e^{-q}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed state define
\begin{align}\label{a58:fn:eq:profile}
 D&=s+Ru,& h(y)&=\min(y,s)+R\max(y-s,0),\notag\\
 r(y)&=h(y)/D,& \psi(q,x)&=\exp\{ps+qu-qr(k)\},\qquad
 \lambda=Db/R.
\end{align}
Thus $m\le D\le2m$, $0\le r(k)\le1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:roughpsi}
 e^{ps+qu-q}\le\psi(q,x)\le e^{ps+qu},\qquad \psi(0,x)=1.
\end{equation}
At $q=0$ all formulas with a removable denominator are interpreted continuously, and $\lambda=m$.

The characteristic endpoint from the frozen foundation is
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:endpoint}
 t(\infty)=T=\frac{3\pi^2}{8},\qquad
 T-t(q)\sim\sqrt2(q+2)e^{-q/2}.
\end{equation}
We recall enough of its verification to specify exactly what is being used. Setting $v=\sqrt{2e^q-1}$ gives
\[
 T=\int_1^\infty\frac{2\log((v^2+1)/2)}{v^2-1}\,dv
   =-2\int_0^1\log z\left(\frac1{1-z}+\frac z{1+z^2}\right)\,dz.
\]
Integration by parts and $z=(v-1)/(v+1)$ give the second expression; the boundary terms vanish. Geometric-series integration yields $\pi^2/3$ and $\pi^2/24$ for its two terms. This proves the endpoint. Moreover,
\[
 t_q=\frac q{\sqrt2}e^{-q/2}\{1+O(e^{-q})\},
\]
whose integral from $q$ to infinity gives the tail in \eqref{a58:fn:eq:endpoint}.
The frozen radius and root-limit proofs use this same curve and operator. Those proofs are accessible in the bundled foundation; none is inferred here merely from a coefficient fit.

\subsection{The exact frozen integral}
For real $y\in[0,m)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:frozeng}
 w(y)=\begin{cases}e^{-p+q\one_{y\ge k}},&y<s,\\
 e^{p-q\one_{y<k}},&y\ge s,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad g(y)=w(y)e^{-q(r(y)-r(k))},\qquad
 S=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}g(i).
\end{equation}
The identity
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:frozenintegral}
 \int_0^m g(y)\,dy=\lambda
\end{equation}
has a useful direct verification. Put $r'=h(y)/D$ and $r=r(k)$. On duplicate labels $dy=D\,dr'$ and on new labels $dy=(D/R)\,dr'$. The relation $e^p/R=e^{q-p}$ makes both pieces agree, giving
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:circle}
 \frac{g(y)\,dy}{\lambda}
 =\kappa_q((r'-r)\bmod1)\,dr',\qquad
 \kappa_q(v)=\frac{qe^{-qv}}{1-e^{-q}}\quad(0\le v<1).
\end{equation}
The density integrates to one. At $q=0$ it is the uniform density. This proves \eqref{a58:fn:eq:frozenintegral} exactly for the continuous frozen kernel, without asserting that it is the evolving discrete chain.

\section{Signed weighted residual estimates}\label{a58:fn:sec:residual}
The improvement over the frozen report is to retain signs and normalize by the characteristic speed before bounding the residual.

\begin{lemma}[Signed quadrature and shifted ranks]\label{a58:fn:lem:shifts}
For every $q\ge0$ and $x\in\calS$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:signedsum}
 0\le S-\lambda\le3\sqrt2e^{q/2}.
\end{equation}
Let $r_i$ denote the rank fraction at the actual child $x_i$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:childranks}
 r_i=\frac i{D-1},\quad\frac i{D+R-1},\quad
 \frac{h(i)+1}{D+1},\quad\frac{h(i)+1}{D+1-R}
\end{equation}
in the four cases of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:children}. In every case $|r_i-r(i)|\le4/D$. Except for a duplicate ascent, $r_i\ge r(i)$; in that exceptional case
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:negative}
 0\le r(i)-r_i=\frac{i(R-1)}{D(D+R-1)}\le\frac1D.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The inequalities $q/2\le p\le q/2+(\log2)/2$ imply $g(y)\le\sqrt2e^{q/2}$. For ascents use $r(y)\ge r(k)$; for descents use $r(k)-r(y)\le1$. On every interval separated by the integer breakpoints $s,k$, the function $g$ decreases. Each unit interval has its corresponding summand at its left endpoint. Hence its sum dominates its integral. On each of the at most three nonempty pieces the excess is at most the starting value, proving \eqref{a58:fn:eq:signedsum}. At a breakpoint the formula for the following interval is used; a value at one integration endpoint has no effect on the integral.

Substitution of each child into \eqref{a58:fn:eq:profile} gives \eqref{a58:fn:eq:childranks}. The denominators in the cases that occur are positive. For a duplicate descent,
\[
 r_i-r(i)=\frac{i}{D(D-1)}\in[0,1/D].
\]
For a duplicate ascent, subtraction gives \eqref{a58:fn:eq:negative}, since $i<D$ and $0\le R-1\le1$. For a new ascent,
\[
 r_i-r(i)=\frac{D-h(i)}{D(D+1)}\in[0,1/D].
\]
For a new descent the difference is
\[
 \frac{D+(R-1)h(i)}{D(D+1-R)}\ge0.
\]
Here $u\ge2$, so $D\ge2R$ and $D+1-R\ge D/2$; also $h(i)\le D$ and $R\le2$. The last difference is therefore at most $4/D$.
\end{proof}

Differentiating the profile at a fixed state gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:derivative}
 \frac{\partial_t\psi}{\psi}=\lambda-bB,\qquad
 B=r(k)+qe^{-q}\partial_Rr(k),\qquad |B|\le1.
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
 \partial_Rr(k)=\begin{cases}-ku/D^2,&k\le s,\\
 -s(m-k)/D^2,&k\ge s,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad -\frac1{4R}\le\partial_Rr(k)\le0.
\]
The bound follows from $sRu/(s+Ru)^2\le1/4$, $k\le s$ in the first case, and $m-k\le u$ in the second. Since $0\le r(k)\le1$ and $qe^{-q}\le1/e$, it also implies $|B|\le1$.

\begin{proposition}[Weighted residual]\label{a58:fn:prop:residual}
Uniformly over all states and all $q\ge0$,
\begin{align}\label{a58:fn:eq:upperresidual}
 \frac{\TT\psi-\partial_t\psi}{b\psi}
 &\le qe^{q/D}+3\sqrt2(q+1)e^{q/D}+1,\\
 \frac{\partial_t\psi-\TT\psi}{b\psi}
 &\le4q+1.\label{a58:fn:eq:lowerresidual}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The exact ratio is
\[
 \frac{\psi(q,x_i)}{\psi(q,x)}=g(i)e^{-q(r_i-r(i))}.
\]
Lemma \ref{a58:fn:lem:shifts} and positivity give the two signed estimates
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:weightedS}
 \frac{\TT\psi}{\psi}\le e^{q/D}S,\qquad
 \frac{\TT\psi}{\psi}\ge e^{-4q/D}S\ge e^{-4q/D}\lambda.
\end{equation}
Because $\lambda/b=D/R$, \eqref{a58:fn:eq:derivative} implies
\begin{align*}
 \frac{\TT\psi-\partial_t\psi}{b\psi}
 &\le\frac DR(e^{q/D}-1)
       +3\sqrt2\frac{e^{q/2}}b e^{q/D}+1\\
 &\le qe^{q/D}+3\sqrt2(q+1)e^{q/D}+1.
\end{align*}
We used $e^z-1\le ze^z$, $R\ge1$, and
\[
 \frac{e^{q/2}}b
 =\frac q{\sqrt{2-e^{-q}}(1-e^{-q})}
 \le\frac q{1-e^{-q}}\le q+1.
\]
The last inequality is equivalent to $e^q\ge1+q$; its limiting value at zero is valid. Similarly,
\[
 \frac{\partial_t\psi-\TT\psi}{b\psi}
 \le\frac DR(1-e^{-4q/D})+1\le4q+1,
\]
using $1-e^{-z}\le z$. No lower bound on the state size was used for this second estimate.
\end{proof}

\section{Padding and comparison on the infinite state space}\label{a58:fn:sec:comparison}
\subsection{An exact padding inequality}
For an integer $L\ge0$ put
\[
 I_L(s,u,k)=(s+L,u,k+L).
\]
This map takes $\calS$ into itself. Replacing $i$ by $i+L$ preserves the comparisons with $s,k$, and direct substitution into each case of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:children} shows that the corresponding child of $I_Lx$ is $I_Lx_i$. There are additionally $L$ children indexed by $0,\ldots,L-1$. Therefore every nonnegative $f$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:padding}
 \TT(f\circ I_L)(x)\le(\TT f)(I_Lx).
\end{equation}
This is an inequality, not a commutation identity.

Fix $Q>0$ and, for the entire interval $0\le q\le Q$, fix the integer $L=\lceil Q\rceil$. Then $D(I_Lx)\ge L+1\ge Q$, so $e^{q/D(I_Lx)}\le e$. Define
\begin{align}\label{a58:fn:eq:barriers}
 H(q)&=\frac{e(1+3\sqrt2)}2q^2+(3\sqrt2e+1)q,\notag\\
 G_Q(t,x)&=e^{H(q(t))}\psi(q(t),I_Lx),\qquad
 g_-(t,x)=e^{-2q(t)^2-q(t)}\psi(q(t),x).
\end{align}
The derivative $H'(q)$ dominates the right side of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:upperresidual} at every padded state throughout this interval. Since $dq/dt=b$, Proposition \ref{a58:fn:prop:residual} and \eqref{a58:fn:eq:padding} give
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:diffbarriers}
 \partial_tG_Q\ge\TT G_Q,\qquad
 \partial_tg_-\le\TT g_-,\qquad G_Q(0,x)=g_-(0,x)=1.
\end{equation}
The lower derivative follows because $(2q^2+q)'=4q+1$.

\subsection{Stopped upper comparison}
Formal comparison with an unbounded positive operator is insufficient. We give the stopping argument, including the boundary term needed for the lower bound.

Let $X_v$ be the continuous-time chain with one rate-one transition for every child of its current state. Its total jump rate is $m$ and its generator is $\mathcal L=\TT-mI$. Since each jump increases $m$ by at most one, its successive holding rates are bounded above by those of a pure-birth process with rates $m(x),m(x)+1,\ldots$. That process is nonexplosive, as $\sum_j1/(m(x)+j)=\infty$; hence $X$ is nonexplosive. Let
\[
 \tau_M=\inf\{v:m(X_v)>M\},\qquad
 V_v=\int_0^v m(X_w)\,dw.
\]
States with $m\le M$ form a finite set. By expansion over jump paths and holding times,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:FK}
 \FF(t,x)=\E_x e^{V_t},
\end{equation}
with infinity allowed: the holding exponentials cancel the factor $e^{V_t}$, and a fixed $j$-step path contributes the time-simplex volume $t^j/j!$. Nonexplosion accounts for all paths; Tonelli justifies the nonnegative sums.

For $t\le t(Q)$, stopped Dynkin comparison applied to
$e^{V_v}G_Q(t-v,X_v)$ gives
\[
 \E_x\left[e^{V_t};\tau_M>t\right]\le G_Q(t,x).
\]
One may obtain this by stopping at $t\wedge\tau_M$ and discarding the nonnegative exit term. The drift is
$e^{V_v}(\TT G_Q-\partial_tG_Q)(t-v,X_v)\le0$.
Let $M\to\infty$. Nonexplosion and monotone convergence, followed by \eqref{a58:fn:eq:FK}, give
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:upperF}
 \FF(t(q),x)\le e^{H(q)}\psi(q,I_Lx),\qquad 0\le q\le Q.
\end{equation}
In particular the minimal positive series is finite before $T$.

\subsection{The lower comparison and its exit term}
Fix $t<T$ and choose $\delta>0$ with $t+\delta<T$. Choose one $Q$ with $t+\delta\le t(Q)$ and keep its padding integer $L$ fixed. From the opposite drift in \eqref{a58:fn:eq:diffbarriers},
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:lowerstop}
 g_-(t,x)\le
 \E_x[e^{V_t};\tau_M>t]
 +\E_x[e^{V_{\tau_M}}g_-(t-\tau_M,X_{\tau_M});\tau_M\le t].
\end{equation}
Write $p(v)=p(q(v))$ in this paragraph, and similarly for the other time-dependent functions. Uniformly for $0\le v\le t$ and all $y=(s,u,k)$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:ratioexit}
 \frac{g_-(v,y)}{G_Q(v+\delta,y)}
 \le C_{t,\delta,Q}
 \exp\{-[p(v+\delta)-p(v)]s-[q(v+\delta)-q(v)]u\}
 \le C_{t,\delta,Q}e^{-\eta m(y)}
\end{equation}
for some $\eta>0$. To see this, use \eqref{a58:fn:eq:roughpsi} in the numerator and denominator. Rank exponents have bounded magnitude on the compact time interval; the extra term $Lp(v+\delta)$ is harmless since $L$ is fixed. Both $p,q$ are strictly increasing and continuous, so the two positive increments have positive compact minima.

On exit, $m(X_{\tau_M})=M+1$, because an upward jump has size at most one. Apply the supersolution once more, now to the time horizon $t+\delta$, stopping at $t\wedge\tau_M$. It bounds the exit part with the later function by
\[
 \E_x[e^{V_{\tau_M}}G_Q(t+\delta-\tau_M,X_{\tau_M});\tau_M\le t]
 \le G_Q(t+\delta,x).
\]
Thus the second term in \eqref{a58:fn:eq:lowerstop} is at most
$C_{t,\delta,Q}e^{-\eta(M+1)}G_Q(t+\delta,x)$, which tends to zero. Letting $M\to\infty$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:lowerF}
 \FF(t(q),x)\ge e^{-2q^2-q}\psi(q,x).
\end{equation}
This is the essential justification of the infinite-state lower comparison.

\begin{corollary}[Uniform characteristic bounds]\label{a58:fn:cor:uniform}
For every $q\ge0$ and every state,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:uniform}
 -2q^2-q\le\log\FF(t(q),x)-\log\psi(q,x)
 \le H(q)+\lceil q\rceil p(q)+q.
\end{equation}
In particular the difference is $O((q+1)^2)$ uniformly in $x$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use \eqref{a58:fn:eq:upperF} with $Q=q$ and
\[
 \log\psi(q,I_Lx)-\log\psi(q,x)
 =Lp(q)-q\{r(I_Lx)-r(x)\}\le Lp(q)+q.
\]
For $q=0$ use the initial values. The lower assertion is \eqref{a58:fn:eq:lowerF}.
\end{proof}

\section{The logarithmic square coefficient upper bound}\label{a58:fn:sec:upper}
At the root, $I_Lx_0=(L+1,1,L+1)$, so
\[
 \log\FF(t(Q),x_0)\le H(Q)+(L+1)p(Q)+Q=O((Q+1)^2).
\]
The constants are independent of $Q$ since $L=\lceil Q\rceil$ and $p(Q)\le Q/2+(\log2)/2$. Nonnegative coefficients imply
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:coefficientupper}
 \beta_n\le\frac{A(t)}{t^n},\qquad
 A(t)=1+\int_0^t\FF(v,x_0)\,dv\le1+t\FF(t,x_0).
\end{equation}
Choose $Q=2\log(n+2)$. Then \eqref{a58:fn:eq:endpoint} gives
\[
 n\log\frac{T}{t(Q)}=O\bigl(n(Q+2)e^{-Q/2}\bigr)=O(\log(n+2)),
\]
where $t(Q)$ is bounded away from zero for large $n$. Also
$\log A(t(Q))=O((\log(n+2))^2)$. Taking logarithms in \eqref{a58:fn:eq:coefficientupper} proves the upper side of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:main}. If the equivalent excluded in Theorem \ref{a58:fn:thm:main} held, then
\[
 h_n=cn^\sigma+g\log n+h\log\log n+\log C_0+o(1),
\]
contradicting the upper bound because $c,\sigma>0$.

\section{The growing state lower bound}\label{a58:fn:sec:lower}
\subsection{A uniform tilted coefficient estimate}
Let $x_N=(N,1,N)$ for an integer $N\ge1$. Since $r(k)=N/(N+R)$ at this state,
\[
 \log\psi(q,x_N)=Np(q)+\frac{qR}{N+R}.
\]
Corollary \ref{a58:fn:cor:uniform} therefore gives an absolute constant $C_0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:growing}
 \left|\log\FF(t(q),x_N)-Np(q)\right|\le C_0(q+1)^2
 \qquad(N\ge1,\ q\ge0).
\end{equation}
Put
\[
 d(q)=\frac{d}{dq}\log t(q),\qquad
 M(q)=\frac{p_q(q)}{d(q)}=t(q)p_t(t(q))=\frac{t(q)b(q)}{R(q)}.
\]
These formulas are used for $q>0$. As $q\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:Masympt}
 p_q(q)=\tfrac12+O(e^{-q}),\qquad
 d(q)\sim\frac{qe^{-q/2}}{\sqrt2T},\qquad
 M(q)\sim\frac{Te^{q/2}}{\sqrt2q}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating the explicit formulas also gives
$d'(q)/d(q)\to-1/2$ and $M'(q)/M(q)\to1/2$.
In particular $M$ is eventually strictly increasing and tends to infinity.

For fixed $N,q$, write $c_j=(\TT^j\one)(x_N)$ and define a probability law
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:tilt}
 \Prb(J=j)=\frac{c_jt(q)^j}{j!\FF(t(q),x_N)},\qquad j\ge0.
\end{equation}
Its reference location $NM(q)$ need not be its exact mean.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform Chernoff window]\label{a58:fn:lem:chernoff}
There are absolute constants $c,C>0$ and $q_0$ such that for every $N\ge1$, $q\ge q_0$, and $0<\epsilon<1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:chernoff}
 \Prb\bigl(|J-NM(q)|>\epsilon NM(q)\bigr)
 \le2\exp\{-cN\epsilon^2+C(q+1)^2\}.
\end{equation}
One may take $c=3/128$ after increasing $C$ and $q_0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $|v|\le1$ define
\[
 H_q(v)=p(q+v)-p(q)-M(q)\{\log t(q+v)-\log t(q)\}.
\]
Then $H_q(0)=H_q'(0)=0$. Uniformly for bounded $v$, explicit differentiation of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:params} and \eqref{a58:fn:eq:Masympt} yields
\[
 H_q''(v)\longrightarrow\tfrac14e^{-v/2},\qquad
 M(q)d(q+v)\longrightarrow\tfrac12e^{-v/2}.
\]
Consequently, by taking one sufficiently large $q_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:calculus}
 |H_q(v)|\le v^2/2\quad(|v|\le1),\qquad
 M(q)|\log t(q\pm\eta)-\log t(q)|\ge\eta/4
\end{equation}
for $0\le\eta\le1$. The first inequality follows by integrating the uniform second-derivative bound twice; the second follows by integrating the positive lower bound for $M(q)d$.

Set $K_q=NM(q)$ and $\eta=\epsilon/8$. Markov's inequality applied to $(t(q+\eta)/t(q))^J$ bounds the upper tail by
\[
 \frac{\FF(t(q+\eta),x_N)}{\FF(t(q),x_N)}
 \left(\frac{t(q)}{t(q+\eta)}\right)^{(1+\epsilon)K_q}.
\]
By \eqref{a58:fn:eq:growing}, the logarithm is at most
\begin{align*}
 &NH_q(\eta)-\epsilon NM(q)\{\log t(q+\eta)-\log t(q)\}
     +C(q+1)^2\\
 &\hspace{1cm}\le N\eta^2/2-\epsilon N\eta/4+C(q+1)^2
 =-\frac3{128}N\epsilon^2+C(q+1)^2.
\end{align*}
The bounded shifts change $(q+1)^2$ by only an absolute factor. For the lower tail use $q-\eta$ and the decreasing random variable $(t(q-\eta)/t(q))^J$. Its logarithmic bound is
\[
 NH_q(-\eta)+\epsilon NM(q)\{\log t(q-\eta)-\log t(q)\}
 +C(q+1)^2,
\]
which satisfies the same inequality. Adding the two tail bounds proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A fixed choice of the growing state}
Choose once and for all a sufficiently large constant $K>0$, and for each sufficiently large integer $n$ set
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:choice}
 N=\left\lceil K n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3}\right\rceil,
 \qquad \epsilon=\frac{2N}{n}.
\end{equation}
Then $\epsilon\to0$. Choose $q$ on the eventually increasing branch of $M$ by
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:center}
 NM(q)=(1-4\epsilon)n.
\end{equation}
The right side divided by $N$ tends to infinity, so the choice exists uniquely on that branch for all large $n$. Taking logarithms in \eqref{a58:fn:eq:Masympt} gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:qchoice}
 q=\tfrac23\log n+\tfrac23\log\log n+O_K(1).
\end{equation}
For clarity, first $q\sim(2/3)\log n$ follows from
$q/2-\log q=\log(n/N)+O(1)$; inserting this estimate into $\log q$ gives \eqref{a58:fn:eq:qchoice}. In particular, for any fixed $K$ and all sufficiently large $n$, $q\le\log n$ and $(q+1)^2\le4(\log n)^2$.

There is no $n$-dependent choice of $K$ hidden in the tail estimate. Indeed,
\[
 N\epsilon^2=\frac{4N^3}{n^2}\ge4K^3(\log n)^2.
\]
Choose the fixed $K$ so that $4cK^3>4C+1$, where $c,C$ are from Lemma \ref{a58:fn:lem:chernoff}. Its bound is then at most $2e^{-(\log n)^2}$ for all sufficiently large $n$, hence at most $1/2$. Thus probability at least $1/2$ lies in
\[
 |j-NM(q)|\le\epsilon NM(q).
\]
This interval has at most $2\epsilon NM(q)+3$ integer points. Some such $j$ therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:onecoefficient}
 \frac{c_jt(q)^j}{j!}
 \ge\frac{\FF(t(q),x_N)}{4\epsilon NM(q)+6}.
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{a58:fn:eq:center} and \eqref{a58:fn:eq:choice}, every $j$ in the window has
\begin{align}\label{a58:fn:eq:margins}
 j&\ge(1-\epsilon)(1-4\epsilon)n\ge n-6\epsilon n,\notag\\
 j+N&\le(1+\epsilon)(1-4\epsilon)n+N
       \le n-3\epsilon n+N\le n.
\end{align}
The last inequality uses $N=\epsilon n/2$. There is thus ample space for the initial path and for extension to the desired length.

\subsection{Moving the extracted coefficient to the target index}
The root $x_0=x_1$ reaches $x_N$ by $N-1$ successive new-maximal-label transitions. Hence $a_{N+j}\ge c_j$. Every cap-two ascent word can be extended injectively by appending one plus its current number of ascents. This label is new, since the largest label already used is at most the current number of ascents, and it obeys the next ascent bound. Repeating this deterministic extension gives $a_n\ge a_{N+j}$ whenever $n\ge N+j$. Thus \eqref{a58:fn:eq:margins} gives $a_n\ge c_j$.

By \eqref{a58:fn:eq:onecoefficient} and \eqref{a58:fn:eq:growing},
\begin{align*}
 h_n&\ge-\log\frac{n!}{j!}-j\log t(q)+n\log T
           +Np(q)-C_0(q+1)^2-O(\log n)\\
 &\ge-6\epsilon n\log n-C_0(q+1)^2-O(\log n).
\end{align*}
For the second line, $n-j\le6\epsilon n$ bounds the factorial loss, $Np(q)\ge0$, and eventually $1<t(q)<T$ with $j\le n$ gives $-j\log t(q)\ge-n\log T$. The denominator in \eqref{a58:fn:eq:onecoefficient} is $O(n)$ and contributes only $O(\log n)$. Finally, $\epsilon n=2N=O(n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3})$ and $q=O(\log n)$. This proves
\[
 h_n\ge-O\bigl(n^{2/3}(\log n)^{5/3}\bigr),
\]
completing Theorem \ref{a58:fn:thm:main}.

\section{Proof of the asymmetric inverse}\label{a58:fn:sec:inverse}
Let $f(v)=v\log(v/(eT))$. Stirling's formula and Theorem \ref{a58:fn:thm:main} imply, after adjusting constants, that for all sufficiently large integers $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:fbound}
 f(n)-C_1n^{2/3}(\log n)^{5/3}
 \le\log a_n\le f(n)+C_2(\log n)^2.
\end{equation}
The term $\tfrac12\log(2\pi n)+O(1/n)$ is absorbed into the respective envelopes. The value
$x=y/W(y/(eT))$ solves $f(x)=y$: if $w=W(y/(eT))$, then $x=eT e^w$ and $xw=y$.

For $v$ in any $o(x)$ neighborhood of $x$,
\[
 f'(v)=\log(v/T)\sim\log x.
\]
Take $n_- =\lfloor x-A\log x\rfloor$ for a sufficiently large fixed $A$. The mean value theorem gives
\[
 f(x)-f(n_-)\ge\tfrac A2(\log x)^2
\]
for large $x$. The upper envelope in \eqref{a58:fn:eq:fbound} is at most $2C_2(\log x)^2$ there. Choose $A>4C_2$ to obtain $\log a_{n_-}<y$. Monotonicity then gives $N(y)>n_-$, proving the lower side of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:inverse}.

Next take
\[
 n_+=\left\lceil x+B x^{2/3}(\log x)^{2/3}\right\rceil
\]
with a sufficiently large fixed $B$. This displacement is $o(x)$, and
\[
 f(n_+)-f(x)\ge\tfrac B2x^{2/3}(\log x)^{5/3}
\]
for large $x$. The lower error envelope in \eqref{a58:fn:eq:fbound} is at most
$2C_1x^{2/3}(\log x)^{5/3}$ there. Choose $B>4C_1$ to obtain $\log a_{n_+}\ge y$. Thus $N(y)\le n_+$. Integer rounding is absorbed in the stated errors, proving Corollary \ref{a58:fn:cor:maininverse}.

\section{Exact computation and numerical evidence}\label{a58:fn:sec:numerics}
The numerical investigation is independent of the analytic proof. The bundled sources and data distinguish exact integers from floating approximations explicitly.

\subsection{A reproducible positive recurrence}
Let $P_n(s,u,k)$ count compacted states after $n$ letters, starting from $P_1(1,1,1)=1$. At fixed $(s,u)$, put
\[
 A_i=\sum_{k\le i}P_n(s,u,k),\qquad
 B_i=\sum_{k>i}P_n(s,u,k),\qquad 0\le i<s+u.
\]
Using prefix and suffix sums, the four updates are
\begin{align*}
 P_{n+1}(s-1,u+1,i)&\mathrel{+}=A_i,&
 P_{n+1}(s-1,u,i)&\mathrel{+}=B_i&& (i<s),\\
 P_{n+1}(s+1,u,i+1)&\mathrel{+}=A_i,&
 P_{n+1}(s+1,u-1,i+1)&\mathrel{+}=B_i&& (i\ge s).
\end{align*}
Invalid target contributions vanish. Reachability implies
\[
 0\le s\le n,\quad s\equiv n\pmod2,\quad
 1\le u\le1+(n-s)/2,\quad 0\le k<s+u.
\]
Summing target states gives $a_{n+1}$. Through length $N$ this requires $O(N^4)$ big-integer additions and $O(N^3)$ stored integers; the operation count does not include the growth of integer bit lengths.

The C++17/GMP implementation reproduces all $177$ published b-file terms, indexed $0$ through $176$, and extends the exact counts through $400$. The positive-sum long-double implementation rescales each transition by $1/((n+1)\mu)$, builds suffix sums without subtraction, and uses Kahan summation for its scalar totals. It reaches $1000$ and agrees with exact normalized values through $400$ to about $3.22\times10^{-17}$ maximum relative error. This comparison is an empirical floating-point diagnostic, not an interval certificate for terms $401$ through $1000$.

\section{The coefficient regularity obstruction}\label{a58:fn:sec:regularity}
Uniform function estimates such as \eqref{a58:fn:eq:uniform} bound a positive weighted sum of coefficients. They give upper bounds on every individual coefficient but provide no comparable lower bound at a prescribed index. The growing-state proof extracts one coefficient in a broad window, then pays an $O(N\log n)$ factorial loss to move to the requested index. This is exactly why its lower estimate is much weaker than $O((\log n)^2)$.

One possible additional input is log-concavity of $\beta_n=a_n/n!$. Under the compacted-state distribution of a uniformly selected length-$n$ word, let $\E_n$ denote expectation and put
\[
 M_n=\E_n[m]=a_{n+1}/a_n.
\]
Directly summing \eqref{a58:fn:eq:children} gives
\begin{align}\label{a58:fn:eq:moments}
 \TT m&=m^2+m-s-k=m^2+u-k,\notag\\
 \TT s&=ms+m-2s,\qquad
 \TT k=\frac{m(m+1)}2-s,\\
 \TT(m^2)&=m^3+2m(m-s-k)+m-|s-k|.\notag
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:variance}
 M_{n+1}=M_n+\frac{\Var_n(m)+\E_n(u-k)}{M_n}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the next-step expectation of $m$ is $\E_n[\TT m]/\E_n[m]$, because a state is weighted by its number of children. Log-concavity of $\beta_j$ at $j=n+1$ is therefore equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:regularitytarget}
 (n+1)\{\Var_n(m)+\E_n(u-k)\}\le M_n^2.
\end{equation}
The absolute value in the last identity of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:moments} obstructs a straightforward closed polynomial moment recursion. No proof of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:regularitytarget} is supplied here.
\begin{writenote}[2 October 2026]
Part~\ref{a58:part:rt} restates the identity \eqref{a58:fn:eq:variance} as \eqref{a58:rt:eq:rawidentity}, writing $R_n$ for $M_n$, without citing this Part. It deduces the exact inequality $R_{n+1}\ge R_n-1$ and from it the ratio limit. The target \eqref{a58:fn:eq:regularitytarget} reappears there as \eqref{a58:rt:eq:varianceconjecture} and is still unproved. Part~\ref{a58:part:rt}, Section~\ref{a58:rt:sec:open}, records two further exact scans: factorial-normalized and rising-factorial log-concavity of $(\TT^r\one)(x)$ at every state with $H=s+2u$ and $1\le r\le179$, $H+r+1\le200$, each over $48{,}510{,}450$ inequalities with no violation. Together they extend the all-state check below. Part~\ref{a58:part:rt}'s counterexample concerns a stronger transition-wise inequality that this Part does not state.
\end{writenote}

Exact integer tests give
\[
 (j+1)a_j^2>j a_{j-1}a_{j+1}\qquad(2\le j\le399),
\]
with equality at $j=1$. The stronger all-state property that $(\TT^j\one)(x)/j!$ is log-concave also passes all $3060$ tested centers $1\le j\le15$ across the $204$ states with $s+u\le8$. Neither finite check is a proof.

If global normalized log-concavity were established, the ratios $\beta_{n+1}/\beta_n$ would decrease. The known root limit forces their limit to be $\mu$: the arithmetic means of their logarithms tend to the logarithm of their decreasing limit, and are $\log(\beta_n)/n$. Thus each ratio would be at least $\mu$, giving $\beta_n\ge\mu^n$ and
\[
 0\le h_n\le O((\log n)^2).
\]
This conditional consequence would exclude negative as well as positive stretched-exponential factors, but would still not identify $2/3$.

Several stronger shortcuts are demonstrably unavailable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The normalized sequence is not a P\'olya-frequency sequence of order three. Since $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta_2=1$ and $\beta_3=2/3$,
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}\beta_1&\beta_2&\beta_3\\
 \beta_0&\beta_1&\beta_2\\0&\beta_0&\beta_1\end{pmatrix}=-\frac13.
\]
Thus full Toeplitz total positivity is false. A PF$_2$-specific argument remains possible.
\item The length-seven doubled-letter polynomial is
$1+21z+126z^2+129z^3$. Its discriminant is $-84159$, so it has two nonreal roots. Real-rootedness of this natural refinement cannot be assumed.
\item The words $001$ and $010$ are cap-two ascent words but their coordinatewise minimum $000$ is not. The raw-word class is not closed under the usual lattice meet.
\end{enumerate}
These exact counterexamples concern particular methods; they do not disprove normalized log-concavity or Conjecture \ref{a58:fn:conj:twothirds}.

\section{A formal route toward the conjectured constant}\label{a58:fn:sec:formal}
This section describes a research mechanism. The exact frozen identities are distinguished from asymptotic transport assumptions; no additional coefficient theorem is claimed.

\subsection{Exact frozen averaging}
By \eqref{a58:fn:eq:circle}, the frozen Markov operator on the unit circle is
\[
 (K_qf)(r)=\int_0^1\kappa_q((r'-r)\bmod1)f(r')\,dr'.
\]
Uniform measure is exactly stationary. On the Fourier mode $e^{2\pi i\ell r}$ its multiplier is $q/(q-2\pi i\ell)$. On mean-zero periodic functions the corresponding inverse is
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:poisson}
 (K_q-I)^{-1}=q\partial_r^{-1}-I,
\end{equation}
where $\partial_r^{-1}$ is the mean-zero periodic antiderivative. Since $\lambda/b=D/R$, the inverse frozen generator on the $q$ time scale is $(R/D)(q\partial_r^{-1}-I)$. A forcing of size $q$ thus has a frozen rank corrector of size $O(q^2/D)$. This does not imply a uniform bound for the actual discrete evolving chain.

For some additional precision, let $z=s/D$ and write
$V=\TT\psi/\psi-\partial_t\log\psi$. In the limit $D\to\infty$ at fixed $q$, with limiting ranks away from coincident breakpoints, the first-order continuum residual has rank average
\begin{equation}\label{a58:fn:eq:average}
 \left\langle V/b\right\rangle
 =qI(q)+\frac{q(z-1)}{2R}+\frac12,
 \qquad
 I(q)=\frac{\tfrac12-[1-(1+q)e^{-q}]/q^2}{1-e^{-q}}.
\end{equation}
The identity is for that first-order continuum limit, not for a finite state. Its derivation retains the rank-boundary layers: the Euler--Maclaurin contribution is
\[
 \frac q{2R}\one_{r<z}+\frac q2\one_{r>z}
 +\frac{R-1}{2R}\{\kappa_q((z-r)\bmod1)-\kappa_q((-r)\bmod1)\},
\]
and the last two kernel terms integrate to zero. If $a(r,r')$ is the leading value of $D(r_i-r(i))$, its four values are
\[
 r',\quad -(R-1)r',\quad 1-r',\quad 1+(R-1)r'
\]
in the order of \eqref{a58:fn:eq:children}. Under stationary rank measure, conditional on $r'=x$, the probability of $r\le r'$ is
$P_q(x)=(1-e^{-qx})/(1-e^{-q})$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
 \langle a\rangle&=1-z+\frac{R-1}{2}+\frac{2-R}{2}z^2-RI(q),\\
 \langle B\rangle&=\frac12-\frac{qe^{-q}}{2R}z(1-z).
\end{align*}
Adding the averaged quadrature, shifted-rank term $-(q/R)\langle a\rangle$, and $\langle B\rangle$ gives \eqref{a58:fn:eq:average}, using $2-R=e^{-q}$. It follows that
\[
 \left\langle V/b\right\rangle
 =\frac q4(1+z)+\frac12-\frac1q+O(qe^{-q}).
\]
The bundled formal-kernel audit gives the full continuum calculation and an independent numerical check. The limiting calculation does not bound its finite-state remainder uniformly in joint $q,D$ regimes.

\subsection{Formal transport and the scalar exponent}
Away from the wrap and type-interface layers, the large-$q$ bulk residual suggested by the discrete expansion is
\[
 V/b\approx q f(r,z),\qquad
 f(r,z)=\begin{cases}\tfrac14+\tfrac r2,&r<z,\\
 \tfrac r2,&r>z.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its rank average is $(1+z)/4$. At fixed $q$, write the profile-transformed jump generator as
\[
 (\mathcal L^\psi f)(x)=\sum_i\frac{\psi(q,x_i)}{\psi(q,x)}\{f(x_i)-f(x)\}.
\]
Under the same formal large-state dynamics, the composition drift on the $q$ scale is pointwise
\[
 \mathcal L^\psi z/b\approx
 \begin{cases}-(1+z)/2,&r<z,\\(1-z)/2,&r>z,
 \end{cases}
\]
whose rank average is $(1-3z)/2$. The factor $1/b$ converts the jump rates to the $q$ time scale. Only the \emph{averaged} composition drift equals $(1-3z)/2$.

Multiplying the profile by $\exp(qz/6)$ would formally combine these averaged contributions as
\[
 q\frac{1+z}{4}+q\frac{1-3z}{12}=\frac q3.
\]
Integrating this scalar forcing suggests a factor $\exp(q^2/6)$. More explicitly, after the composition correction the leading mean-zero rank forcing is
\[
 g(r,z)=\frac r2-\frac14+\frac{\one_{r<z}-z}{12}.
\]
The frozen inverse \eqref{a58:fn:eq:poisson} supplies a candidate rank correction of size $O(q^2/D)$. Its sign depends on the convention for conjugating the generator; its existence in the frozen model does not justify exponentiating it across all finite-state transitions.

If a sharp singular estimate $\log\FF(t(q),x_0)\sim q^2/6$ and suitable coefficient transfer could both be proved, the scale $q\sim2\log n$ would suggest $h_n\sim(2/3)(\log n)^2$. This explains why the numerical candidate has structural motivation. Neither of those two required theorems has been established here.

\subsection{The nearly trapped root and rare growth paths}\label{a58:fn:sec:trap}
A naive rapid-mixing argument at the original root is false. For $x_0=(1,1,1)$, the original $\psi$ profile has rank tending to $1/3$. Its new-ascent child is $(2,1,2)$, with rank tending to $1/2$. Thus
\[
 \frac{\psi(q,(2,1,2))}{\psi(q,x_0)}=\exp(q/3+O(1)),\qquad
 \frac1{b(q)}\frac{\psi(q,(2,1,2))}{\psi(q,x_0)}
 =O(qe^{-q/6}).
\]
The duplicate-descent child's rate on this $q$ scale is even smaller, $O(qe^{-2q/3})$. At a large frozen parameter, the transformed chain is therefore nearly trapped at its fixed root. It cannot be treated as if it were already in the large-state stationary rank regime, nor can unweighted typical trajectories be assumed to reach $D\gg q^2$ quickly.

A Feynman--Kac expectation could nevertheless be dominated by rare early growth paths rewarded by later potential. A useful proof would have to control their weighted contribution, possibly by a different conjugation or a quantitative seeding construction. It would also have to control returns to small states. The existing quadratic barriers are too coarse to show that this boundary contribution is $o(q^2)$.

The missing steps are therefore concrete: a uniform evolving-state residual through rank and type boundaries; control of finite differences of the exponentiated corrector; a weighted root-to-bulk estimate with subquadratic logarithmic cost; control of repeated small-state returns; and an individual-coefficient transfer theorem. A real-axis singular estimate by itself is not that transfer theorem.

\section{Literature scope and further questions}\label{a58:fn:sec:literature}
The literature checks bundled with the research were made on 2 October 2026. They are scoped negative evidence, not an exhaustive novelty certificate.

Conway--Conway--Elvey Price--Guttmann \cite{CCEG} provide the recurrence, compaction, and numerical conjecture for the factorial constant. Their detailed Section 5 retains unresolved stretched-exponential and power factors, including numerical estimates $0.06\le\sigma\le0.1$ within the models investigated there. Those estimates are not rigorous bounds. The shorter expression in the introduction must not be promoted to an established equivalent. The upper theorem here rules out a positive fixed-power stretched-exponential factor, while leaving the limitations already stated.

Later results on additional forbidden patterns \cite{CallanMansour}, selected length-four avoidance classes \cite{LiuKitaevZhang}, and Fishburn matrix asymptotics \cite{HwangJin} do not directly settle this single-$000$ restriction. In particular, no applicable multiplicity-preserving correspondence or suitable product generating function was identified in the checked sources.

The targeted regularity search likewise found no retrieved theorem proving log-concavity of $a_n/n!$. Real-rooted $s$-Eulerian polynomials \cite{SavageVisontai} concern inversion sequences with prescribed coordinate bounds; the adaptive ascent bound and global letter cap here require an additional argument. Distributive-lattice methods \cite{LiangSagan} do not apply immediately to the non-meet-closed raw-word class. Generating series tracking total repetitions \cite{JinSchlosser} or consecutive run lengths \cite{Dukes} do not record the complete multiplicity of every letter: for example, $0001$ and $0011$ have the same total repetitions, and a run-length cap would allow $01010$ although one value appears three times.

An authorized indexed search of the default branch of the ProveIt repository \cite{ProveIt} found no direct overlap under the sequence identifiers and the checked ascent-sequence, bounded-multiplicity, Fishburn, and constant keywords. The detailed queries and indexed commit are recorded in the bundled literature note. This is not a semantic audit of the entire repository or proof that the indexed commit was the live tip. No publication or external repository change is part of this addendum.

The most focused next mathematical questions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can one prove normalized log-concavity, or a weaker local coefficient regularity sufficient to reduce the lower extraction loss?
\item Can the circle-kernel Poisson inverse yield a uniform discrete corrector for rank and composition, with controlled boundary layers?
\item Can a weighted rare-seed estimate overcome the fixed-root trapping and control repeated excursions back to small states?
\item Can one establish a local limit or complex saddle estimate strong enough to transfer a sharp singular law to every coefficient?
\item After those steps, what are the next logarithmic, constant, and inverse corrections? A nonzero limiting amplitude and an all-orders expansion require additional estimates beyond a leading logarithmic-square law.
\end{enumerate}

\part{Logarithmic square growth for ascent sequences avoiding 000}\label{a58:part:ln}
\partsource{Batch-77 manuscript 07, archive \file{a202058-lognormal-report.zip} (delivered as \file{a202058-lognormal.tex}, 23 pages), dated 2 October 2026. Delivered author line: ``A self contained mathematical and reproducibility report''. Delivered Section $k$ is Section $k+20$ here.}
\begin{partabstract}
Let $a_n$ count ascent sequences in which every value occurs at most twice,
let $A(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nt^n/n!$, and put $T=3\pi^2/8$.
We prove
\[
 \log A(t)\sim\frac23\log^2\frac1{T-t}\qquad(t\uparrow T).
\]
For $c_n=a_nT^n/n!$, positivity then gives
\[
 \log\sum_{j\le n}c_j\sim\frac23(\log n)^2,
 \qquad \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log c_n}{(\log n)^2}=\frac23.
\]
The proof uses the exact compacted enumeration recurrence, an explicit
circle-kernel Poisson correction uniform at both interfaces, a global
slow-profile patch, and a first-exit integral whose beta factor cancels the
large-state seed cost. The nonlinear residual is controlled for weighted
state size $D\ge Kq$, even when the corrector itself is not small.
All necessary characteristic and infinite-state comparison foundations
are included. A parameter-uniform refinement gives the root remainder
$O(q^{3/2}\sqrt{\log q})$. The full individual limit, a coefficient
equivalent, a ratio limit, and an amplitude remain unproved.
The article therefore resolves the leading real-axis and cumulative
logarithmic-square scales, while leaving a specific coefficient-regularity
problem open.
\end{partabstract}

\section{The result and its precise scope}
An ascent sequence is a word $w_1\cdots w_n$ of nonnegative integers with
$w_1=0$ and
\[
 0\le w_i\le1+\asc(w_1\cdots w_{i-1})\quad(i\ge2),\qquad
 \asc(w)=\#\{i:w_i<w_{i+1}\}.
\]
Classical pattern avoidance of $000$ means that no value occurs three
times, whether or not those occurrences are consecutive. Let $a_n$ count
these words and set $a_0=1$. This is OEIS A202058, the cap-two column of
A294220 \cite{OEIS,OEIStriangle}. Its first terms are
\[
 1,1,2,4,10,27,83,277,1015,4007,17047.
\]
Throughout, logarithms are natural. Define
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:normalization}
 T=\frac{3\pi^2}{8},\quad \mu=T^{-1}=\frac8{3\pi^2},\quad
 A(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\frac{t^n}{n!},\quad
 c_n=\frac{a_nT^n}{n!},\quad S_N=\sum_{n=0}^N c_n.
\end{equation}
The characteristic clock is
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:clock}
 t(q)=\int_0^q
 \frac{v e^{-v/2}}{\sqrt{2-e^{-v}}(1-e^{-v})}\dd v,
 \qquad q\ge0,
\end{equation}
where the integrand has value $1$ at zero.

\begin{theorem}\label{a58:ln:thm:main}
The function $t(q)$ increases from $0$ to $T$, and
\begin{align}
 \log A'(t(q))&=\frac{q^2}{6}+o(q^2),\label{a58:ln:eq:mainroot}\\
 \log A(t)&\sim\frac23\left(\log\frac1{T-t}\right)^2
       &&(t\uparrow T),\label{a58:ln:eq:mainA}\\
 \log S_N&\sim\frac23(\log N)^2,\label{a58:ln:eq:maincum}\\
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log c_n}{(\log n)^2}&=\frac23.
       \label{a58:ln:eq:mainlimsup}
\end{align}
In particular $A$ has radius $T$. No coefficient monotonicity or
log-concavity hypothesis is used in these conclusions.
\end{theorem}

The recurrence and its compaction are due to Conway, Conway, Elvey Price
and Guttmann \cite[Sections 2.2 and 2.6]{CCEG}. Their Section 5 conjectured
the factorial exponential constant $8/(3\pi^2)$ from numerical series
analysis, while leaving finer factors unresolved. The present advance is
the logarithmic-square theorem. The principal new work is a uniform
corrected residual, its globalization to the \emph{full} continuation
function, and the boundary-to-root cancellation. The characteristic
barriers are foundations from the preceding investigation, reproduced
here so that the proof has no dependency on inaccessible companion notes.

\section{The exact positive transfer model}\label{a58:ln:sec:model}
Twice-used labels can be deleted from the active alphabet. The remaining
once-used labels can be compacted to an initial segment without changing
suffix counts. In the variables of \cite{CCEG}, with $G_0(a,\ell,s)=1$,
the compacted recurrence is
\begin{align}\label{a58:ln:eq:priorrec}
 G_j(a,\ell,s)={}&\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}
 G_{j-1}(a+\one_{i>\ell}-1,i-1,s-1)\notag\\
 &+\sum_{i=s}^{a+1}G_{j-1}(a+\one_{i>\ell},i,s+1),
 \qquad a_n=G_{n-1}(0,0,1)\quad(n\ge1).
\end{align}
The adjusted values $a,\ell$ may equal $-1$. To recall why compaction is
legitimate, take adjacent labels $i,i+1$ below or at the last value, with
$i<\ell$, and interchange their remaining capacities. Reverse each
maximal suffix block consisting of these two labels and interchange the
labels inside it. This involution interchanges their multiplicities and
preserves ascent counts within each block and at its outside comparisons.
Both labels were already permitted, since $i+1\le\ell$; subsequent
bounds see unchanged ascent totals. Thus a once-used and an unused
label below the last value can be exchanged. After a new label is
appended, it can be moved through unused labels until the once-used set
is initial; after a repetition, the exhausted label is deleted. This is
the binary-capacity instance of the compaction in \cite{CCEG}, and gives
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:priorrec}. Canonical states preserve continuation counts; they
need not encode every literal property of the original prefix.

Set $m=a+2$, $k=\ell+1$, $u=m-s$. We use the invariant state space
\[
 \Sstate=\{x=(s,u,k):s\ge0,\ u\ge1,\ 0\le k<s+u\}.
\]
For every integer $0\le i<m=s+u$, the child is
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:children}
 x_i=\begin{cases}
 (s-1,u,i),&i<s,\ i<k,\\
 (s-1,u+1,i),&i<s,\ i\ge k,\\
 (s+1,u,i+1),&i\ge s,\ i\ge k,\\
 (s+1,u-1,i+1),&i\ge s,\ i<k.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The first two cases are duplicate ($D$) steps, and the last two are new
($N$) steps. An ascent corresponds to $i\ge k$. An $N$ descent from $u=1$
would require $s\le i<k\le s$, so it cannot occur. There are no $D$ steps
when $s=0$. Thus all children are valid states.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:transfer}
 (\TT f)(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}f(x_i),\quad x_0=(1,1,1),\quad
 F(t,x)=\sum_{j\ge0}(\TT^j\one)(x)\frac{t^j}{j!}.
\end{equation}
Transition multiplicities are retained. The series defines the minimal
nonnegative continuation function, initially allowing $+\infty$.
The recurrence gives $\TT^j\one(x_0)=a_{j+1}$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:rootidentity}F(t,x_0)=A'(t).
\end{equation}
The integer resource
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:H}H(x)=s+2u
\end{equation}
changes by $+1$ on an ascent and $-1$ on a descent. This exact property
will be essential: using a clock-dependent resource at the first-exit
step would lose the cancellation.

\section{Characteristic foundations and global coarse bounds}\label{a58:ln:sec:foundation}
\subsection{The characteristic profile and endpoint}
For $q\ge0$ put
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:pars}
 E=e^{-q},\quad R=2-E,\quad p=\frac{q+\log R}{2},\quad
 b=\frac{e^p(1-E)}q,
\end{equation}
with $b(0)=1$. Then $t'(q)=1/b(q)$ and $p'(q)=1/R(q)$.
For a state $x=(s,u,k)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:rank}
 D=s+Ru,\quad h_0(y)=\min(y,s)+R\max(y-s,0),\quad
 r(y)=h_0(y)/D,\quad r=r(k),\quad z=s/D,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:psi}
 \psi(q,x)=\exp\{ps+qu-qr\}.
\end{equation}
Here $H/2\le D\le H$, $0\le r<1$, $0\le z<1$, and
$\psi(0,x)=1$. We use one-sided limits when formulas extend to $z=1$.

The substitution $y=\sqrt{2e^q-1}$ gives
\[
 t(\infty)=\int_1^\infty
 \frac{2\log((y^2+1)/2)}{y^2-1}\dd y.
\]
Integration by parts, followed by $v=(y-1)/(y+1)$, yields
\[
 t(\infty)=-2\int_0^1\log v\,
 \frac{1+v}{(1-v)(1+v^2)}\dd v
 =-2\int_0^1\log v\left(\frac1{1-v}+\frac{v}{1+v^2}\right)\dd v.
\]
The boundary terms vanish. The first integral is $\pi^2/3$, and the
second is $\pi^2/24$, by integration of the geometric series (or its
Abel-regularized alternating version). Therefore $t(\infty)=3\pi^2/8=T$.

The explicit derivative in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:clock} also proves the two-sided tail
estimate
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:tail}
 c(q+1)e^{-q/2}\le\delta(q):=T-t(q)\le C(q+1)e^{-q/2}
 \qquad(q\ge0),
\end{equation}
with fixed positive constants. Indeed, for $q\ge1$ the derivative is
between constant multiples of $qe^{-q/2}$; integrate to infinity and
adjust on a compact interval. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:logtail}
 \log\frac1{\delta(q)}=\frac q2-\log(q+1)+O(1).
\end{equation}
Both directions in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:tail} are needed later.

\subsection{Frozen weights and exact identities}
For real $y\in[0,m)$ set
\[
 w(y)=\begin{cases}e^{-p+q\one_{y\ge k}},&y<s,\\
 e^{p-q\one_{y<k}},&y\ge s,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad g(y)=w(y)e^{-q(r(y)-r)}.
\]
Direct integration on the intervals separated by $s,k$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:frozenintegral}
 \int_0^m g(y)\dd y=\lambda:=Db/R.
\end{equation}
One can also verify this identity by setting $f(y)=e^{-qh_0(y)/D}$:
on the two type intervals $\lambda f'=-e^{-p}(e^q-1)f$ and
$\lambda f'=-e^p(1-e^{-q})f$, respectively, and $f(m)=e^{-q}f(0)$.
The resulting integral eigen-equation at $k=0$ is
\[
 \frac Dq\left[e^{q-p}(1-e^{-qs/D})+
 \frac{e^p}{R}(e^{-qs/D}-e^{-q})\right]=\lambda,
\]
since $e^{q-p}=e^p/R$; differentiation then verifies every $k$.

Each piece of $g$ decreases, the breakpoints are integers, and
$g\le\sqrt2e^{q/2}$. Thus, with $S=\sum_i g(i)$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:riemann}
 0\le S-\lambda\le3\sqrt2e^{q/2}.
\end{equation}
In the order of \eqref{a58:ln:eq:children}, the exact child ranks are
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:childrank}
 r_i=\frac{i}{D-1},\quad \frac{i}{D+R-1},\quad
 \frac{h_0(i)+1}{D+1},\quad \frac{h_0(i)+1}{D+1-R}.
\end{equation}
All relevant denominators are positive. Subtraction shows
$|r_i-r(i)|\le4/D$; except for a duplicate ascent, $r_i\ge r(i)$.
For that exceptional case,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:negativechild}
 0\le r(i)-r_i=\frac{i(R-1)}{D(D+R-1)}\le\frac1D.
\end{equation}
The exact transition ratio is $g(i)e^{-q(r_i-r(i))}$.

For a fixed integer state,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:derivative}
 \partial_q\log\psi=D/R-B,\qquad B=r+qE\partial_R r,
 \qquad |B|\le1,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:rR}
 \partial_R r=\begin{cases}-r(1-z)/R,&r\le z,\\
 -z(1-r)/R,&r\ge z.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The formulas agree at the type interface, and
$-1/(4R)\le\partial_Rr\le0$.

\subsection{Padding and infinite-state comparison}\label{a58:ln:sec:comparison}
Equations \eqref{a58:ln:eq:riemann}--\eqref{a58:ln:eq:derivative} imply
\begin{align}
 \frac{\TT\psi}{b\psi}-\partial_q\log\psi
 &\le q e^{q/D}+3\sqrt2(q+1)e^{q/D}+1,\label{a58:ln:eq:coarseupper}\\
 \partial_q\log\psi-\frac{\TT\psi}{b\psi}&\le4q+1.
 \label{a58:ln:eq:coarselower}
\end{align}
For the first inequality use $\TT\psi/\psi\le e^{q/D}S$,
$\lambda/b=D/R$, $e^x-1\le xe^x$, and
$e^{q/2}/b\le q+1$. For the second use
$\TT\psi/\psi\ge e^{-4q/D}\lambda$ and $1-e^{-x}\le x$.

For an integer $L\ge0$, define
$I_L(s,u,k)=(s+L,u,k+L)$. Each original transition $i$ maps exactly to
the padded transition $i+L$, and the remaining $L$ transitions are
nonnegative. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:padding}
 \TT(f\circ I_L)\le(\TT f)\circ I_L\qquad(f\ge0).
\end{equation}
Fix $Q$, take $L=\lceil Q\rceil$, and put
\[
 V(q)=\frac{e(1+3\sqrt2)}2q^2+(3\sqrt2e+1)q.
\]
On $0\le q\le Q$, padded states have $D\ge L+1$, so
$e^{V(q)}\psi(q,I_Lx)$ is a supersolution. Also
$e^{-2q^2-q}\psi(q,x)$ is a subsolution. We claim
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:coarsebounds}
 e^{-2q^2-q}\psi(q,x)\le F(t(q),x)
 \le e^{V(q)}\psi(q,I_Lx),\qquad 0\le q\le Q.
\end{equation}

Here and below an infinite positive operator needs an actual comparison
argument. Give each of the $m$ transitions rate $1$. The associated
continuous-time chain has generator $\TT-mI$. Since each jump increases
$m$ by at most one, a Yule-process domination shows nonexplosion.
Cancellation of the holding-time factors against the potential gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:fk}
 F(t,x)=\E_x\exp\left(\int_0^t m(X_v)\dd v\right),
\end{equation}
with infinity allowed: summing a fixed $j$-jump path contributes exactly
$t^j/j!$. Stop on exiting $m\le N$. Finite-state Dynkin comparison for
a nonnegative supersolution, followed by discarding the nonnegative
exit term and then letting $N\to\infty$, proves the upper inequality
in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarsebounds} and finiteness for $t<T$.

For a subsolution at a fixed time $t<T$, choose $\varepsilon>0$ with
$t+\varepsilon<T$ and a single padded supersolution valid through that
later time. Uniformly for $0\le v\le t$,
\[
 \frac{e^{-2q(v)^2-q(v)}\psi(q(v),y)}
 {e^{V(q(v+\varepsilon))}\psi(q(v+\varepsilon),I_Ly)}
 \le C_{t,\varepsilon}e^{-\eta_{t,\varepsilon}m(y)}.
\]
Indeed both $p(q(v+\varepsilon))-p(q(v))$ and
$q(v+\varepsilon)-q(v)$ have positive compact minima; rank terms and
fixed padding contribute bounded factors. The stopped exit contribution
is at most $C e^{-\eta N}$ times the later supersolution at the starting
state, and vanishes. This proves the lower inequality.

Since
$\log\psi(q,I_Lx)-\log\psi(q,x)\le Lp(q)+q$, we obtain the useful
state-uniform comparison
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:coarseuniform}
 -2q^2-q\le\log F(t(q),x)-\log\psi(q,x)
 \le V(q)+\lceil q\rceil p(q)+q=O((q+1)^2).
\end{equation}
The same argument permits any later subsolution whose growth, on each
compact $q$ interval, is bounded by a finite multiple of $\psi$.
For comparison initialized at a positive time, apply the stopped formula
with terminal data $F$ at that time; the positive semigroup identity
follows coefficientwise from \eqref{a58:ln:eq:transfer}. No uniqueness theorem
for an unbounded system is being assumed.

\section{The complete continuum forcing}\label{a58:ln:sec:forcing}
The frozen continuum kernel becomes especially simple in weighted rank.
Define
\[
 \kappa_q(d)=\frac{qe^{-qd}}{1-E}\quad(0\le d<1),\qquad
 (K_qf)(r)=\int_0^1\kappa_q((\xi-r)\bmod1)f(\xi)\dd\xi.
\]
For a frozen target rank $\xi=r(y)$ put
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:coordinates}
 \nu=\one_{\xi\ge z},\quad a_*=\one_{\xi\ge r},\quad
 c_*=1+(R-1)\nu,\quad \sigma=2\nu-1,\quad
 \Delta=-1+(2-R)\nu+Ra_*.
\end{equation}
Also write $a=\nu-\xi\Delta$, $c=\sigma-z\Delta$.
At an actual lattice target $y=i$, direct substitution into
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:children} gives the exact identities
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:childcoordinates}
 D_i=D+\Delta,\qquad z_i-z=\frac{c}{D+\Delta},\qquad
 r_i-\xi=\frac{a}{D+\Delta},\qquad |\Delta|\le1.
\end{equation}
The weak inequalities in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coordinates} agree with the transition
at $i=s$ and $i=k$, including coincidences. The last active label has
rank strictly less than $1$; the right endpoint is excluded.

The exact change of variables is
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:kernelchange}
 \frac{g(y)}b=\frac{c_*}{R}\kappa_q((\xi-r)\bmod1),
 \qquad \dd y=\frac D{c_*}\dd\xi.
\end{equation}
Thus $g(y)\dd y/(Db/R)=\kappa_q\dd\xi$, proving directly that
Lebesgue measure is stationary for $K_q$. In particular no exponential
factor in $q$ is lost when moving between label and rank coordinates.
The rank mesh, including its closing gap, has spacing in $[1/D,2/D]$.

The composition correction is $\exp(qz/6)$. At a fixed integer state,
\[
 z_q=-Ez(1-z)/R.
\]
Define the full first-order forcing by
\begin{align}\label{a58:ln:eq:forcing}
 h(q,r,z)&=\frac qR\int_0^1\kappa_q((\xi-r)\bmod1)
 \left(\frac{c_*}{2}-a+\frac c6\right)\dd\xi
       +B-\frac z6-\frac{qz_q}6.
\end{align}
The first term includes the entire lattice Euler contribution, the exact
rank shift, and the composition shift; the final terms subtract the
fixed-state derivative. We next compute its mean without discarding
rank-wrap or type-interface layers.

\begin{lemma}\label{a58:ln:lem:mean}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:meanfunctions}
 P_q(\xi)=\frac{1-e^{-q\xi}}{1-E},\quad
 I(q)=\int_0^1\xi P_q(\xi)\dd\xi
 =\frac{1/2-[1-(1+q)E]/q^2}{1-E},\quad
 m(q)=qI(q)-\frac q{3R}+\frac12.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:mean}
 \bar h(q,z):=\int_0^1h(q,r,z)\dd r
 =m(q)-\frac{qEz}{6R(1-E)}
 =\frac q3+\frac12-\frac1q+O(qe^{-q}),
\end{equation}
uniformly for $q\ge1$ and $0\le z\le1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Under the joint measure $\dd r\,\kappa_q((\xi-r)\bmod1)\dd\xi$,
the target $\xi$ is uniform and
$\Pp(a_*=1\mid\xi)=P_q(\xi)$. Integrating the two indicator types gives
\begin{align*}
 \langle a\rangle&=1-z+\frac{R-1}{2}
                 +\frac{2-R}{2}z^2-RI(q),\\
 \langle B\rangle&=\frac12-\frac{qE}{2R}z(1-z),\\
 \langle\Delta\rangle&=1-Ez+\frac{RE}{1-E}-\frac Rq,\\
 \langle c\rangle&=1-3z+Ez^2-\frac{RzE}{1-E}+\frac{Rz}q.
\end{align*}
Since $\langle c_*\rangle=R-(R-1)z$, the uncorrected part
$q\langle c_*/2-a\rangle/R+\langle B\rangle$ simplifies to
$qI(q)+q(z-1)/(2R)+1/2$.
Adding $q\langle c\rangle/(6R)-z/6-qz_q/6$ proves the exact formula.
Expanding only exponentially small rational factors proves the final
uniform estimate. This calculation uses the frozen continuum measure
exactly, not a stationarity assumption for the discrete state process.
\end{proof}

\section{A uniform rank corrector}\label{a58:ln:sec:corrector}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:correctordef}
 v=h-\bar h,\qquad Jv(r)=\int_0^r v(u)\dd u,\qquad
 \eta(q,D,r,z)=-\frac RD\{qJv(r)-v(r)\},\qquad
 \phi=\psi\exp(qz/6+\eta).
\end{equation}
The suppressed parameters of $Jv$ are $q,z$. Since $v$ has mean zero,
$Jv$ is continuous and periodic, with $Jv(0)=Jv(1)=0$. Its own mean
need not vanish. Use the right-hand value at the periodic seam $r=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform residual]\label{a58:ln:thm:residual}
There are absolute constants $K_0,C$ such that, for $q\ge1$ and
$D\ge K_0q$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:sharpresidual}
 \left|\frac{\TT\phi}{b\phi}-\partial_q\log\phi-m(q)\right|
 \le C\left(\frac{q^2}D+qe^{-q}\right).
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:roughsharp}
 \left|\frac{\TT\phi}{b\phi}-\partial_q\log\phi-\frac q3\right|
 \le C\left(1+\frac{q^2}D\right).
\end{equation}
The derivative fixes the integer state. The bounds hold at rank zero,
at the type interface, through wrap, and as composition approaches either
endpoint. They do not require $\eta=o(1)$ or $D\gg q^2$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Bounded variation and the moving interface}
We give the regularity details because an unquantified smooth bulk
expansion would not imply Theorem~\ref{a58:ln:thm:residual}. Write
\[
 w_z(\xi)=\frac13+\frac{3R-7}{6}\one_{\xi\ge z}
 +[-1+(2-R)\one_{\xi\ge z}](\xi-z/6),
\]
and
\[
 A_0(q,r)=\frac{1-e^{-q(1-r)}}{1-E},\qquad
 A_1(q,r)=\frac{r+1/q-(1+1/q)e^{-q(1-r)}}{1-E}.
\]
Then \eqref{a58:ln:eq:forcing} becomes exactly
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:hdecomposition}
 h=\frac qR K_qw_z+q(A_1-zA_0/6)
       +B-z/6+\frac{qEz(1-z)}{6R}.
\end{equation}
The supremum and periodic total variation of $w_z$ are uniformly bounded
for $R\in[1,2]$, $z\in[0,1]$. Its distributional $z$ derivative is a
bounded density plus one atom of bounded mass at $z$. The kernel obeys
\[
 \|\kappa_q\|_\infty\le Cq,\quad \|\kappa_q\|_1=1,\quad
 \|\partial_q\kappa_q\|_1\le C/q,\quad \TV(\kappa_q)\le Cq.
\]
Convolving the finite measure $Dw_z$ gives both a supremum $O(q)$ and
an $L^1$ norm $O(1)$ for $\partial_rK_qw_z$. The same two bounds hold
for $\partial_zK_qw_z$, including the moving atom. Translation continuity
of the $L^1$ kernel proves continuity across $r=z$.

The explicit derivatives
\[
 \partial_r A_0=-\frac{qe^{-q(1-r)}}{1-E},\qquad
 \partial_r A_1=\frac{1-(q+1)e^{-q(1-r)}}{1-E}
\]
have supremum $O(q)$ and $L^1$ norm $O(1)$; also
$|\partial_qA_j|\le C/q$. The last bound follows by integrating
$\partial_q\kappa_q$ against $1$ or $\xi$ over $[r,1]$.
The piecewise bilinear factor in $B$ has bounded one-sided derivatives
and agrees at $r=z$; its multiplier $qE/R$ and its $q$ derivative are
bounded. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:hregularity}
 |h|\le Cq,\quad |h_q|\le C,\quad |h_r|+|h_z|\le Cq^2,
 \qquad \int_0^1(|h_r|+|h_z|)\dd r\le Cq.
\end{equation}
The integral bound is essential for controlling $\partial_zJv$.

At the periodic seam, direct evaluation of \eqref{a58:ln:eq:hdecomposition}
gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:seam}
 h(q,1-,z)-h(q,0,z)=qz/6+\frac{qE}{1-E}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the convolution has no jump, the $qA$ terms have jump
$-1+qE/(1-E)+qz/6$, and $B$ has jump $1$. The interface $r=z$ is
continuous, including coincident endpoints by one-sided limits.

It follows from \eqref{a58:ln:eq:correctordef}--\eqref{a58:ln:eq:seam} that
\begin{align}
 |\eta|&\le Cq^2/D,& |\eta_r|+|\eta_z|&\le Cq^2/D,\notag\\
 |\eta(1-)-\eta(0)|&\le Cq/D,&
 \left|\frac{d\eta}{dq}\right|_{\rm fixed\ state}&\le Cq/D.
 \label{a58:ln:eq:etabounds}
\end{align}
For the derivative, first hold $(D,r,z)$ fixed. Equation
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:hregularity} gives $\eta_q=O(q/D)$. Along a fixed integer
state, $D_q/D,r_q,z_q=O(E)$, and their extra contributions are
$O(q^2E/D)\le Cq/D$. The sign of $r-z$ is the sign of $k-s$ and never
changes as $q$ varies. If $k=s$, the path stays on the continuous common
interface and the tangential derivatives agree.

If $d=(\xi-r)\bmod1$, integration along the clockwise arc, adding at
most one seam jump, gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:etaincrement}
 |\eta(q,D,\xi,z)-\eta(q,D,r,z)|
 \le C\left(\frac{q^2d}D+\frac qD\right).
\end{equation}
The $q/D$ term is needed only across the seam, but retaining it everywhere
is harmless and convenient.

\subsection{The pointwise Poisson identity}
Integration by parts in the forward kernel, using the continuity and
periodicity of $Jv$, gives
\[
 K_qv=q(K_q-I)Jv.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:poisson}
 (K_q-I)(qJv-v)=v,\qquad
 \frac DR(K_q-I)\eta=-v.
\end{equation}
This is the concrete form of $(K_q-I)^{-1}=q\partial_r^{-1}-I$ on
mean-zero functions. It is valid pointwise with the selected right-hand
representative at zero: convolution ignores individual point values,
and the same value of $v$ is used in both identity terms. Thus no
almost-everywhere assertion is substituted at an actual lattice state.

\subsection{Euler correction and linear discrete errors}
On an original-label interval of integer length, all type and ascent
indicators are fixed. The frozen weight is geometric with exponent
$\alpha=qc_*/D$. Its left sum divided by its integral is exactly
$\alpha/(1-e^{-\alpha})$. Uniformly for $D\ge K_0q$,
\[
 \frac{\alpha}{1-e^{-\alpha}}=1+\frac\alpha2+O(\alpha^2).
\]
After summing intervals and using \eqref{a58:ln:eq:kernelchange},
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:euler}
 \frac Sb=\frac DR+\frac q{2R}K_q[c_*](r)+O(q^2/D).
\end{equation}
The remainder is at most $C(q^2/D^2)(D/R)$. Empty intervals or
coincident integer breakpoints contribute no extra term. In particular
all wrap and interface Euler terms are retained in the convolution in
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:euler}.

Let $\tau=-a+c/6$. The logarithmic change of $\psi e^{qz/6}$ relative
to the frozen ratio is exactly
\[
 \ell_i=\frac{q\tau_i}{D+\Delta_i}
      =\frac qD\tau_i+O(q/D^2).
\]
The function $\tau$ is bounded and piecewise affine. On the original
label intervals the variation of $(g/b)\tau$ is $O(q)$, including the
finite jumps. The left Riemann error is bounded by that variation.
Since the total frozen weight is $O(D)$, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:linearxi}
 \sum_i\frac{g_i}b\ell_i
 =\frac qR\int_0^1\kappa_q((\xi-r)\bmod1)\tau(\xi)\dd\xi
   +O(q^2/D).
\end{equation}
Replacing $D+\Delta$ by $D$ costs only $O(q/D)$ in the summed
expression, which is absorbed for $q\ge1$.

For the frozen rank increment $\Delta_r\eta=\eta(q,D,\xi,z)-\eta(q,D,r,z)$,
the smooth variation of $(g/b)\Delta_r\eta$ is bounded by
\[
 C\int_0^1\left(q\kappa_q(d)|\Delta_r\eta|
                      +\kappa_q(d)|\eta_r(\xi)|\right)\dd\xi
 \le Cq^2/D.
\]
Here \eqref{a58:ln:eq:etaincrement} and the exponential moments of $\kappa_q$
were used. A type-weight jump obeys the same bound, since
\[
 \sup_{0\le d<1}\kappa_q(d)(q^2d/D+q/D)\le Cq^2/D.
\]
At the interior kernel jump $\xi=r$, the increment itself is zero.
At the seam, the corrector jump is $O(q/D)$ and the kernel endpoint is
$O(q)$, again giving $O(q^2/D)$. Thus the genuine lattice estimate is
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:lineareta}
 \sum_i\frac{g_i}b[\eta(q,D,\xi_i,z)-\eta(q,D,r,z)]
 =\frac DR(K_q-I)\eta+O(q^2/D),
\end{equation}
where $\xi_i=r(i)$ denotes the frozen target rank and $\ell_i$ the logarithmic increment.

Changing $(D,\xi_i,z)$ to the actual child's coordinates in
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:childcoordinates} changes $\eta$ by $O(q^2/D^2)$:
$\eta_D=-\eta/D$, and the rank and composition changes are $O(1/D)$.
The straight segment between the two ranks stays in $[0,1)$, so it does
not cross the seam. The type interface is continuous with bounded
one-sided derivatives. Summing against $O(D)$ frozen weight gives
another $O(q^2/D)$ error.

For clarity, the Riemann principle just used is elementary. For each
unit interval with its right-continuous endpoint convention,
\[
 \left|f(i)-\int_i^{i+1}f(y)\dd y\right|
 \le\operatorname{Var}(f;[i,i+1)).
\]
All discontinuities here are at integer breakpoints. Summing smooth
variations and one-sided jumps proves the bounds even when type and
rank breakpoints coincide.

\subsection{The nonlinear exponential error}
Let
\[
 \chi_i=\ell_i+\eta(q,x_i)-\eta(q,x).
\]
The preceding estimates imply, for $d_i=(r(i)-r)\bmod1$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:chi}
 |\chi_i|\le Cq/D+Cq^2d_i/D.
\end{equation}
Choose $K_0$ large enough that $Cq^2/D\le q/2$ when $D\ge K_0q$.
For $j=0,1,2$ the weighted rank mesh gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:mesh}
 \sum_i\frac{g_i}b d_i^j e^{qd_i/2}\le CDq^{-j}.
\end{equation}
To prove this, each bin $[\ell/q,(\ell+1)/q)$ contains at most
$C(D/q+1)$ points; $g_i/b\le Cqe^{-qd_i}$ by
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:kernelchange}. Sum
$Cq(D/q+1)q^{-j}(\ell+1)^je^{-\ell/2}$ over $\ell\ge0$ and use
$D\ge q$. This includes the bin containing $d_i=0$.

Now $|e^x-1-x|\le x^2e^{|x|}/2$ and \eqref{a58:ln:eq:chi}--\eqref{a58:ln:eq:mesh}
yield
\begin{align}\label{a58:ln:eq:nonlinear}
 \sum_i\frac{g_i}b|e^{\chi_i}-1-\chi_i|
 &\le C\left(\frac{q^2}{D^2}D+
             \frac{q^4}{D^2}\frac D{q^2}\right)
 \le Cq^2/D.
\end{align}
This is the decisive uniform estimate. A supremum bound
$e^{O(q^2/D)}$ would not suffice in the range needed for root transfer.
The exponentially decaying clockwise kernel instead controls rare
large rank increments.

Combining \eqref{a58:ln:eq:euler}, \eqref{a58:ln:eq:linearxi}, \eqref{a58:ln:eq:lineareta},
and \eqref{a58:ln:eq:nonlinear}, and subtracting the fixed-state derivative of
$\log\phi$, gives
\[
 \frac{\TT\phi}{b\phi}-\partial_q\log\phi
 =h+\frac DR(K_q-I)\eta-\frac{d\eta}{dq}+O(q^2/D)
 =\bar h-\frac{d\eta}{dq}+O(q^2/D).
\]
Equations \eqref{a58:ln:eq:mean} and \eqref{a58:ln:eq:etabounds} prove
Theorem~\ref{a58:ln:thm:residual}.

\section{Global barriers for the full continuation function}\label{a58:ln:sec:global}
A local sharp estimate does not bound the full function if paths can
return to small states. We now construct global barriers, including those
excursions, rather than compare only with a killed process.

\begin{lemma}[Slow profile]\label{a58:ln:lem:slow}
There is an absolute $q_*$ such that for every $A\ge1$,
\[
 S_A(q,x)=e^{Aq^2}\psi(q/2,x),\qquad
 (\partial_q-\TT/b(q))S_A\ge(q+H/8)S_A\quad(q\ge q_*).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At a parameter $v$, every frozen ratio is at most $\sqrt2e^{v/2}$.
Only a duplicate ascent lowers the true rank below the frozen rank, and
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:negativechild} sharpens to
\[
 \frac{i(R-1)}{D(D+R-1)}
 \le\frac{s(R-1)}{(s+R)^2}\le\frac{R-1}{4R}\le\frac18.
\]
Thus each exact child ratio at $v$ is at most $\sqrt2e^{5v/8}$.
There are $m\le H$ children, and $b(q)\asymp e^{q/2}/q$, so
\[
 \frac{\TT\psi(q/2,x)}{b(q)\psi(q/2,x)}
 \le CHq e^{-3q/16}\le H/8
\]
for large absolute $q$. Meanwhile \eqref{a58:ln:eq:derivative} gives
$\partial_q\log\psi(q/2,x)\ge H/4-1/2$. Hence the residual of
$S_A$ is at least $2Aq+H/8-1/2\ge q+H/8$ after enlarging $q_*$.
\end{proof}
The slow profile alone does not dominate $F$ initially over arbitrarily
large states. It is used only as a patch combined with a full-$q$ term.

Fix $0<\varepsilon<1/6$ and set
\[
 \kappa_\pm=1/6\pm\varepsilon,\qquad
 P_\pm=e^{\kappa_\pm q^2}\phi.
\]
By \eqref{a58:ln:eq:roughsharp}, choose a fixed $K$ sufficiently large and then
$q_0\ge q_*$ sufficiently large, depending only on $\varepsilon$, so
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:strictsharp}
 (\partial_q-\TT/b)P_+\ge\varepsilon qP_+,
 \qquad (\partial_q-\TT/b)P_-\le-\varepsilon qP_-
 \quad\hbox{when }H\ge Kq.
\end{equation}
Here $D\ge H/2$ has absorbed a fixed factor into $K$.

\subsection{The upper cutoff}
Choose a smooth function $0\le\theta\le1$, zero on $[0,1]$ and one
on $[2,\infty)$, with bounded derivative. Write
$\theta_q(x)=\theta(H/(Kq))$, and try
\[
 U(q,x)=C_+\{\theta_q(x)P_+(q,x)+S_A(q,x)\}.
\]
Outside the enlarged strip $Kq-1\le H\le2Kq+1$, every child cutoff lies
on the same plateau as its parent. Inside the strip, the exact defect is
\begin{align*}
 (\partial_q-\TT/b)(\theta P_+)
 ={}&\theta(\partial_q-\TT/b)P_+
 +(\partial_q\theta)P_+\\
 &-\sum_i\frac{P_+(x_i)}b[\theta(x_i)-\theta(x)].
\end{align*}
The first term is nonnegative whenever $\theta>0$, by
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:strictsharp}; it vanishes when $\theta=0$.

Here is a uniform bound for the remaining terms that does not assume
any mixing. On the strip, $H_i\le2Kq+2$, $D_i\ge H_i/2$, and
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:etabounds} is global. Thus
\begin{align*}
 \log P_+(q,x_i)&\le\kappa_+q^2+H_iq/2
                       +C(H_i+q+q^2/D_i),\\
 \log S_A(q,x)&\ge Aq^2+Hq/4-q/2.
\end{align*}
The ratios of $P_+(x)$ and $P_+(x_i)$ to $S_A(x)$ are at most
\[
 \exp\{(\kappa_+-A+K/2)q^2+O_K(q)\}.
\]
There are at most $2Kq+1$ children,
$|\partial_q\theta|=O(1/q)$, and $1/b\le e^{O(q)}$.
Choose $A>\kappa_++K/2+2$ and then enlarge $q_0$.
The entire negative cutoff defect is at most $qS_A/2$, which the strict
slow margin absorbs. Hence $U$ is a global supersolution.

\subsection{The positive-part lower barrier}
Increase $A$ if necessary. For $H\le Kq$, using $D\ge H/2\ge1$ and
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:etabounds},
\[
 \log(P_-/S_A)
 \le(\kappa_--A+K/4+C/2)q^2+O_K(q).
\]
Thus $P_-\le S_A$ on the small-state region for $q\ge q_0$.
Set
\[
 L(q,x)=c_-\{P_-(q,x)-S_A(q,x)\}_+.
\]
Where $L>0$, the sharp inequality applies and
\[
 \partial_q(P_--S_A)\le\TT(P_--S_A)/b
                         \le\TT(P_--S_A)_+/b.
\]
The positive part is locally absolutely continuous. Almost everywhere
on its zero set its derivative is zero, while its transfer is
nonnegative. Hence $L$ is a global subsolution in the absolutely
continuous comparison sense, including zero crossings.

\subsection{Initial data and comparison at infinity}
At fixed $q_0$, \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarseuniform} shows that $F/\psi$ is bounded
above and below uniformly in all states. The same is true of $\phi/\psi$
by \eqref{a58:ln:eq:etabounds}. Choose $c_->0$ small enough that $L(q_0)\le F$.
For $H\ge2Kq_0$ the sharp term lets a fixed $C_+$ dominate $F$;
there are only finitely many states with $H<2Kq_0$, and increasing $C_+$
covers them by the positive slow term. Thus $U(q_0)\ge F$ globally.

On every compact $q$ interval, $|\eta|\le Cq^2$,
$e^{qz/6}\le e^{q/6}$, and
$\psi(q/2,x)/\psi(q,x)\le e^q$. Every trial function is consequently
bounded by a finite scalar multiple of $\psi(q,x)$. A padded
supersolution at a strictly later original time dominates these trial
functions by an additional $e^{cH}$ factor. The stopped comparison and
vanishing-exit argument of Section~\ref{a58:ln:sec:comparison} applies verbatim,
including the positive part and cutoff. We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:globalbarriers}
 c_-(P_--S_A)_+\le F(t(q),x)
 \le C_+(\theta_qP_++S_A)\qquad(q\ge q_0).
\end{equation}
These are bounds for full $F$ and already include returns to small states.

\begin{corollary}[Large-state theorem]\label{a58:ln:cor:largestates}
Uniformly as $q\to\infty$ and $H(x)/q\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:largestates}
 \log F(t(q),x)-\log\psi(q,x)=q^2/6+o(q^2).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The identity $p(q)=q/2+\log R(q)/2$ and monotonicity of $R$ give
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:slowratio}
 \log\psi(q,x)-\log\psi(q/2,x)\ge qH/4-q.
\end{equation}
Thus
\[
 \log(S_A/P_\pm)\le(A-\kappa_\pm)q^2-qH/4+q+Cq^2/D,
\]
which tends to $-\infty$ uniformly in the specified joint limit.
Moreover $qz/6+\eta=O(q)+O(q^2/D)=o(q^2)$, and the cutoff eventually
equals one. Squeeze in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:globalbarriers} and then let
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\section{First-exit transfer and the fixed root}\label{a58:ln:sec:root}
A large-state bound alone does not prove a fixed-root result. In
particular ordinary typical growth of the root-conjugated process is
not required here. Instead we use the exact resource $H$ and a positive
first-exit decomposition.

\begin{proposition}[Finite boundary transfer]\label{a58:ln:prop:transfer}
Let $B\ge5$ be an integer, $Q\ge2$, $K\ge0$, $E_0\ge0$.
If for every state $H(x)=B$ and all $0\le v\le Q$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:upperhyp}
 F(t(v),x)\le e^{Kv^2+E_0}\psi(v,x),
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:rootupper}
 \log F(t(Q),x_0)\le KQ^2+E_0+CB\log(Q+2)+CQ+C.
\end{equation}
If $x_B=(B-2,1,B-2)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:lowerhyp}
 F(t(Q-1),x_B)\ge e^{K(Q-1)^2-E_0}\psi(Q-1,x_B),
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:rootlower}
 \log F(t(Q),x_0)\ge KQ^2-E_0-B\log\frac B{Q+1}
                                  -C_K(B+Q+1).
\end{equation}
The upper constant is absolute; the lower one may depend on fixed $K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $M$ be the transfer matrix restricted to the finite set $H<B$, and
$C_B$ its exit matrix to $H=B$. Splitting paths at their first exit gives,
coefficientwise and hence for all $t<T$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:firstexit}
 F_{\rm in}(t)=e^{tM}\one+
     \int_0^t e^{sM}C_BF_{\rm boundary}(t-s)\dd s.
\end{equation}
The boundary function is full $F$, counting arbitrary subsequent paths,
including returns below $B$. All outgoing row sums inside are at most
$B$. Since $H(x_0)=3$, a first exit requires at least $h=B-3$ edges.
Thus $(M^jC_B\one)(x_0)=0$ for $j<h-1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:exitkernel}
 (e^{sM}C_B\one)(x_0)
 \le B\sum_{j\ge h-1}\frac{(Bs)^j}{j!}
 \le\frac{B^hs^{h-1}e^{Bs}}{(h-1)!}.
\end{equation}
No norm depending on the growing matrix dimension enters this estimate.
The unexited term is at most $e^{BT}$.

On $H=B$,
\[
 \psi(v,x)\le e^{CB}e^{Bv/2},\qquad
 e^{v/2}\le\frac{C(v+1)}{\delta(v)},
\]
by \eqref{a58:ln:eq:tail}. At $t=t(Q)$, let $t-s=t(v)$; then
$0\le v\le Q$ and $\delta(v)=\delta(Q)+s$ exactly. Keeping $s$ as the
integration variable entails no time-change Jacobian. Hypothesis
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:upperhyp} gives
\[
 F_{\rm boundary}(t-s)\le
 e^{KQ^2+E_0+CB}\left[\frac{C(Q+1)}{\delta(Q)+s}\right]^B.
\]
Insert this and \eqref{a58:ln:eq:exitkernel} into \eqref{a58:ln:eq:firstexit}, bound
$e^{Bs}\le e^{BT}$, and extend the positive integral to infinity. Its
value is exact:
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:beta}
 \int_0^\infty\frac{s^{h-1}}{(\delta+s)^B}\dd s
 =\delta^{-3}\frac{\Gamma(h)\Gamma(3)}{\Gamma(B)}.
\end{equation}
The $\Gamma(h)=(h-1)!$ factor cancels the path denominator. The exit
contribution is at most
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:betacancel}
 e^{KQ^2+E_0+CB}[C(Q+1)]^B
 \frac{2B^{B-3}}{\Gamma(B)}\delta(Q)^{-3}.
\end{equation}
Stirling gives
$\log(B^{B-3}/\Gamma(B))=B-\tfrac52\log B+O(1)=O(B)$.
The \emph{lower} bound in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:tail} ensures
$\log(1/\delta(Q))=O(Q)$. Add the unexited term to prove
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:rootupper}. This beta cancellation removes the otherwise
prohibitive $BQ/2$ cost.

For the lower bound, repeatedly choose the largest new label. The root
reaches $x_B$ by $h=B-3$ unit-weight edges, so
$\partial_t^hF(t,x_0)\ge F(t,x_B)$. Taylor positivity between
$t(Q-1)$ and $t(Q)$ gives
\[
 F(t(Q),x_0)\ge\frac{\Delta_t^h}{h!}F(t(Q-1),x_B),
 \qquad \Delta_t=t(Q)-t(Q-1).
\]
The explicit derivative gives
$\log\Delta_t\ge-Q/2+\log(Q+1)-C$, and
$\log\psi(Q-1,x_B)\ge(B/2-1)(Q-1)$.
With $\log h!\le h\log h$, the logarithm of this lower bound is at least
\[
 KQ^2-E_0+h\log\frac{Q+1}{h}-Ch-B/2+1
                       +(1/2-2K)Q+K.
\]
Finally
\[
 h\log\frac{Q+1}{h}-B\log\frac{Q+1}{B}
 =B\log B-(B-3)\log(B-3)-3\log(Q+1)
 \ge-3\log(Q+1),
\]
which proves \eqref{a58:ln:eq:rootlower} with all constants uniform in $B,Q$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Boundary uniformity on the entire time interval}
Choose $B=B(Q)=\lceil Q^{3/2}\rceil$ and fix $\varepsilon$ and all the
constants in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:globalbarriers}. Every boundary state has $D\ge B/2$.
For $0\le v\le q_0$, use the fixed padding $L=\lceil q_0\rceil$ in
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarsebounds}. It gives
\[
 \log F(t(v),x)-\log\psi(v,x)\le C_\varepsilon
\]
uniformly in all states, with no factor depending on $B$.

For $q_0\le v\le Q$, the sharp cutoff is one for sufficiently large
$Q$. Equation \eqref{a58:ln:eq:slowratio} gives, uniformly on $H=B$,
\[
 \log(S_A/P_\pm)
 \le(A-\kappa_\pm)v^2-Bv/4+v+Cv^2/D\le-Bv/8
\]
for all large $Q$, because $B/Q\to\infty$. Also
$|vz/6+\eta|\le Q/6+2CQ^2/B$. Consequently
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:upperhyp} holds throughout $0\le v\le Q$ with
$K=\kappa_+$ and $E_\varepsilon(Q)=O_\varepsilon(Q)=o(Q^2)$.
At $v=Q-1$, $x=x_B$, the slow term is exponentially negligible in
the lower barrier, so \eqref{a58:ln:eq:lowerhyp} holds with
$K=\kappa_-$ and the same harmless error size.

Both losses $B\log(Q+2)$ and $B|\log(B/(Q+1))|$ are $o(Q^2)$.
Proposition~\ref{a58:ln:prop:transfer} therefore gives
\[
 \frac16-\varepsilon\le
 \liminf\frac{\log F(t(Q),x_0)}{Q^2}
 \le\limsup\frac{\log F(t(Q),x_0)}{Q^2}
 \le\frac16+\varepsilon.
\]
First let $Q\to\infty$, then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This proves
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:mainroot}. Constants in this construction can deteriorate with $\varepsilon$;
the following separate argument supplies the uniform parameter control
needed for a quantitative remainder.

\section{A parameter uniform quantitative refinement}\label{a58:ln:sec:quantitative}
The fixed-$\varepsilon$ construction proves the leading constant but does
not itself provide a rate. A separate uncut patch keeps the dependence
on the accuracy parameter uniform and yields a quantitative bound.

\begin{theorem}\label{a58:ln:thm:quantitative}
There are absolute finite constants $C,K_*$ such that whenever
$K_*\le K\le Q-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:parameterbound}
 \left|\log F(t(Q),x_0)-\frac{Q^2}{6}\right|
 \le C\left(\frac{Q^2}K+KQ\log(Q+2)+K^2\right).
\end{equation}
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:quantroot}
 \log A'(t(Q))=\frac{Q^2}{6}
                    +O\bigl(Q^{3/2}\sqrt{\log Q}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The constants are independent of $K,Q$; no numerical effective value or
optimality is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Global bounds for the corrector and the small state defect}
Let $C_r,C_\eta,K_0$ be absolute constants such that
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:roughsharp} holds with $C_r$ and
\[
 |\eta|\le C_\eta q^2/D,\qquad
 |d\eta/dq|_{\rm fixed\ state}\le C_\eta q/D.
\]
The latter estimates hold for every state and $q\ge1$, irrespective of
$D\ge K_0q$: they follow from the explicit forcing, the $L^1$ derivative
bounds and the chain rule in Section~\ref{a58:ln:sec:corrector}. Only the sharp
residual requires the large-size hypothesis.

Fix an absolute $a\ge\max(1,4C_r)$, take
$K_*\ge\max(1,q_*,2K_0,12a)$, and for $K\ge K_*$ define
\[
 \kappa_\pm=1/6\pm a/K,\qquad P_\pm=e^{\kappa_\pm q^2}\phi,
 \qquad q_0=K.
\]
Then $\kappa_\pm\in[1/12,1/4]$. When $q\ge K$, $H\ge Kq$,
$D\ge Kq/2$ and $C_r(1+q^2/D)\le3C_rq/K$, so with
$\mathcal L=\partial_q-\TT/b$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:quantsharp}
 \mathcal LP_+\ge(aq/K)P_+,\qquad
 \mathcal LP_-\le-(aq/K)P_-.
\end{equation}
Additional absolute lower bounds on $K_*$ can be imposed below.

In the complementary region $H\le Kq$ we need only a coarse defect
bound, with an absolute constant independent of $K$:
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:coarsedefect}
 \mathcal LP_+\ge-e^{C_dq^2}P_+\qquad(q\ge K\ge K_*).
\end{equation}
Indeed the individual child ratio of $\psi$ is at most
$\sqrt2e^{5q/8}$, $b^{-1}\le Cqe^{-q/2}$, and the composition and
corrector changes give
\[
 \frac{\TT P_+}{bP_+}=\frac{\TT\phi}{b\phi}
 \le CHq\exp(7q/24+2C_\eta q^2).
\]
The exponent $7q/24$ is $5q/8-q/2+q/6$. Moreover
$|\partial_q\log P_+|\le C(H+q)$ by the global derivative bound,
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:derivative}, and $z_q=-Ez(1-z)/R$.
Since $H\le Kq\le q^2$, all polynomial factors can be absorbed in
$e^{C_dq^2}$ with absolute $C_d$, proving \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarsedefect}.

\subsection{The uncut patch and its initial normalization}
The exact characteristic identity gives both inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:twoslow}
 Hq/4-q\le\log\frac{\psi(q,x)}{\psi(q/2,x)}
 \le Hq/4+(H/2)\log2+q/2.
\end{equation}
Choose absolute $C_b=C_\eta+2$ and
$c_A\ge C_d+C_b+2$, and set
$A=c_AK$, $S=e^{Aq^2}\psi(q/2,x)$. On $H\le Kq$, $q\ge K$,
\begin{align*}
 \log(S/P_+)
 &\ge(A-\kappa_+-K/4-C_\eta)q^2
                   -(\log2)Kq/2-2q/3\\
 &\ge[(c_A-1/4)K-C_b]q^2\ge(C_d+1)q^2.
\end{align*}
Together with the slow margin and \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarsedefect}, this proves
$\mathcal L(P_++S)\ge0$ in the small-state region. In the large-state
region both summands are supersolutions by \eqref{a58:ln:eq:quantsharp}.
Thus $P_++S$ is a global supersolution \emph{without a cutoff}.
Also $P_-\le P_+\le S$ in the small-state region. The same positive-part
argument as before makes $(P_--S)_+$ a global subsolution.

At $q=K$, the global corrector bound implies
\[
 e^{-C_pK^2}\le P_\pm(K,x)/\psi(K,x)\le e^{C_pK^2}
\]
uniformly over every state. The coarse comparison
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarseuniform} therefore yields an absolute $C_i$ such that
\[
 e^{-C_iK^2}P_-(K,x)\le F(t(K),x)
                  \le e^{C_iK^2}P_+(K,x).
\]
Initial comparison uses the sharp components alone. Although $S$ itself
contains $e^{c_AK^3}$ at $q=K$, its large positive size never forces a
multiplicative normalization $e^{O(K^3)}$.

Full-state comparison now gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:quantglobal}
 e^{-C_iK^2}(P_--S)_+\le F(t(q),x)
                 \le e^{C_iK^2}(P_++S),\qquad q\ge K.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $K$ and compact clock interval these functions are bounded
by a finite multiple of $\psi$, so the later padded supersolution
eliminates stopped exit contributions exactly as before. Its auxiliary
finite constants vanish with the exit limit; they do not alter $C_i$.
The positive semigroup identity justifies starting at the positive time
$t(K)$.

\subsection{Moving boundary estimates with uniform constants}
Choose once and for all
$\beta\ge8(c_A+C_\eta+4)$ and $B=\lceil\beta KQ\rceil$.
For $H=B$ we have $D\ge B/2$. For $K\le v\le Q$,
\begin{align*}
 \log(S/P_\pm)
 &\le(c_AK-\kappa_\pm)v^2-Bv/4+v+2C_\eta v^2/B\\
 &\le-(\beta/4-c_A)KQv+(1+2C_\eta/(\beta K))v
 \le-KQv.
\end{align*}
Hence $S/P_\pm<1/2$ on this whole interval. The sharp-profile logarithm
satisfies $|vz/6+\eta|\le Q/6+2C_\eta Q^2/B\le C(Q+Q/K)$.
It follows from \eqref{a58:ln:eq:quantglobal} that with
$E_0=C_e(K^2+Q)$, for an absolute $C_e$,
\[
 F(t(v),x)\le e^{\kappa_+v^2+E_0}\psi(v,x)
 \quad(H=B,\ K\le v\le Q).
\]
For $0\le v\le K$ the same assertion follows from the uniform coarse
bound with error $O(K^2)$, since $\kappa_+\ge0$.
At $v=Q-1\ge K$, the lower half of \eqref{a58:ln:eq:quantglobal} also gives
\[
 F(t(Q-1),x_B)\ge
 e^{\kappa_-(Q-1)^2-E_0}\psi(Q-1,x_B).
\]
Thus both hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{a58:ln:prop:transfer} hold with
constants genuinely independent of growing $K$.

Its lower-transfer constant is uniform for
$\kappa_-\in[1/12,1/4]$, as the explicit proof only adds a term linear
in that parameter. Since $B\le CKQ$ and $K\le Q-1$,
\[
 B\log\frac B{Q+1}\le CKQ\log(K+2)\le CKQ\log(Q+2).
\]
The upper and lower transfer bounds prove \eqref{a58:ln:eq:parameterbound}.
Finally $K=\sqrt{Q/\log(Q+2)}$ is admissible for large $Q$ and balances
$Q^2/K$ with $KQ\log(Q+2)$; its $K^2$ term is smaller.
This proves \eqref{a58:ln:eq:quantroot}.

The refinement quantifies the root real-axis theorem. It does not give
an all-index lower bound at the logarithmic-square scale or imply any
coefficientwise regularity.

\section{What positivity transfers to coefficients}\label{a58:ln:sec:coefficients}
Because $F(t,x_0)=A'(t)$ is nondecreasing,
\[
 A(t(Q))\le1+t(Q)F(t(Q),x_0),\qquad
 A(t(Q))\ge[t(Q)-t(Q-1)]F(t(Q-1),x_0).
\]
The interval length has logarithm $-Q/2+O(\log Q)$, so
$\log A(t(Q))\sim Q^2/6$. Equation \eqref{a58:ln:eq:logtail} proves
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:mainA}. It also proves divergence at $T$. Finiteness below
$T$ and positivity then establish that the radius is exactly $T$.

Set $C(z)=A(Tz)=\sum c_nz^n$ and $\alpha=2/3$. For
$s=-\log(t(Q)/T)$, we have $s\sim\delta(Q)/T$ and
$\log(1/s)=Q/2-\log Q+O(1)$. The increasing characteristic covers all
sufficiently small positive $s$, not merely a sequence. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:laplace}
 \log C(e^{-s})\sim\alpha(\log(1/s))^2\qquad(s\downarrow0).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Positive coefficient transfer]\label{a58:ln:prop:positivity}
For any nonnegative sequence satisfying \eqref{a58:ln:eq:laplace},
\[
 \log S_N\sim\alpha(\log N)^2,
 \qquad \limsup\frac{\log c_n}{(\log n)^2}=\alpha.
\]
If in addition $c_n$ is eventually nondecreasing, the limsup is a full
limit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Positivity gives $S_N\le eC(e^{-1/N})$, proving the upper cumulative
bound. For the lower bound take $s=(\log N)^3/N$. The weighted tail is
at most
\[
 \sum_{k>N}c_ke^{-sk}\le e^{-sN/2}C(e^{-s/2})=o(1),
\]
since its logarithm is bounded above by
$-\tfrac12(\log N)^3+O((\log N)^2)$. The weighted prefix is at most
$S_N$, so $S_N\ge C(e^{-s})-o(1)$. Since
$\log(1/s)=\log N-3\log\log N$ and $C(e^{-s})\to\infty$, this
proves the matching lower cumulative asymptotic.

Also $c_n\le eC(e^{-1/n})$, giving the limsup upper bound. If the
limsup were less than $\alpha$, choose
$\max(0,\limsup)<\beta<\alpha$. For all large $n$,
$c_n\le e^{\beta(\log n)^2}$, whence
$S_N\le O(1)+(N+1)e^{\beta(\log N)^2}$, a contradiction. All the
coefficients here are positive; the general proof may set $\log0=-\infty$.
Finally eventual nondecreasingness gives
$S_N\le O(1)+(N+1)c_N$, and $\log(N+1)=o((\log N)^2)$ supplies the
individual lower bound.
\end{proof}

The quantitative refinement propagates to the same positive-coefficient
statements. If $L=\log(1/s)$, then \eqref{a58:ln:eq:logtail} gives
$Q=2L+O(\log L)$, so the integration bounds and
Theorem~\ref{a58:ln:thm:quantitative} give
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:quantlaplace}
 \log C(e^{-s})=\frac23L^2+O(L^{3/2}\sqrt{\log L}).
\end{equation}
In the preceding proof, the lower choice $s=(\log N)^3/N$ changes $L$
from $\log N$ by $O(\log\log N)$. The induced
$O(\log N\log\log N)$ error is smaller than the remainder in
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:quantlaplace}. Hence, as $N\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:quantcum}
 \log S_N=\frac23(\log N)^2
       +O\bigl((\log N)^{3/2}\sqrt{\log\log N}\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is still a cumulative statement; it does not provide a lower bound
for an arbitrary individual $c_n$.

This completes Theorem~\ref{a58:ln:thm:main}. The sufficient extra regularity
condition has a concrete form:
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:monotonicity}
 c_{n+1}\ge c_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\ge(n+1)\mu.
\end{equation}
Neither this eventual inequality nor eventual log-concavity of $c_n$
has been proved. The latter would be sufficient, once the normalized
root limit is known: decreasing eventual ratios cannot ever fall below
one, since then all later ratios are bounded above by a fixed number below one,
contradicting
$c_n^{1/n}\to1$. This implication is not a proof of its premise.

\subsection{A weaker one sided regularity criterion}
Eventual monotonicity is stronger than necessary. The following abstract
criterion identifies a more permissive sufficient target.
\begin{proposition}\label{a58:ln:prop:weakregularity}
Let $c_n>0$, $S_n=\sum_{j\le n}c_j$, and
$\log S_n\sim\alpha(\log n)^2$ with $\alpha>0$.
If for some fixed $C\ge0$ and all sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:weakregularity}
 \log(c_{n+1}/c_n)\ge-C\log n/n,
\end{equation}
then $\log c_n\sim\alpha(\log n)^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows from $c_n\le S_n$. If the lower conclusion fails,
there are $\delta>0$ and infinitely many $n$ with
$\log c_n\le(\alpha-\delta)(\log n)^2$. Choose fixed $0<\beta<1$
so close to one that $\tfrac C2(1-\beta^2)<\delta/2$.
Summing \eqref{a58:ln:eq:weakregularity} backwards, uniformly for
$n^\beta\le k\le n$,
\begin{align*}
 \log c_k&\le\log c_n+C\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\frac{\log j}{j}\\
 &\le[\alpha-\delta+\tfrac C2(1-\beta^2)+o(1)](\log n)^2
 \le[\alpha-\delta/2+o(1)](\log n)^2.
\end{align*}
Integral comparison for $(\log x)/x$ makes the error uniform on this
whole interval. Therefore
\[
 S_n\le S_{\lfloor n^\beta\rfloor}
 +(n+1)\exp\{[\alpha-\delta/2+o(1)](\log n)^2\}.
\]
The first summand has logarithmic coefficient $\alpha\beta^2<\alpha$;
the second has coefficient at most $\alpha-\delta/2<\alpha$.
Taking the logarithm of their sum adds only $O(1)$, and its factor
$n+1$ adds only $O(\log n)$, contradicting the cumulative asymptotic.
\end{proof}
For A202058 a sufficient model-specific bound is consequently
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:weakraw}
 \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\ge\mu(n+1)-L\log n
 \quad\hbox{eventually, for some fixed }L\ge0.
\end{equation}
Divide by $\mu(n+1)$ and use $\log(1-x)\ge-2x$ for sufficiently small
$x\ge0$ to obtain \eqref{a58:ln:eq:weakregularity}. This bound is unproved for
the model. A ratio limit alone, or a finite numerical fit, would not
supply its quantitative one-sided premise.
\begin{writenote}[2 October 2026]
Still unproved after Part~\ref{a58:part:rt}. Its raw-ratio error $O(n^{3/4}(\log n)^{3/4})$ is far from the $O(\log n)$ that \eqref{a58:ln:eq:weakraw} requires; see Part~\ref{a58:part:rt}, Section~\ref{a58:rt:sec:open}.
\end{writenote}

\section{All index coarse control and safe inversions}\label{a58:ln:sec:inverse}
The new cumulative theorem cannot simply be inverted as though it
were an individual coefficient equivalent. We include the older
all-index control, with its proof, to make the distinction explicit.

\subsection{An all index lower enclosure}\label{a58:ln:sec:allindex}
\begin{writenote}[2 October 2026]

The root limit derived at the end of this subsection is Theorem~\ref{a58:fd:thm:main} of Part~\ref{a58:part:fd}. The argument is the growing-seed Chernoff extraction of Part~\ref{a58:part:fn}, Section~\ref{a58:fn:sec:lower}, built on this Part's own comparison \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarseuniform}. Part~\ref{a58:part:rt} improves the lower side of the enclosure to $-O(\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2})$.

For $x_N=(N,1,N)$, \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarseuniform} gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:growing}
 \log F(t(q),x_N)=Np(q)+O((q+1)^2),
\end{equation}
uniformly in $N\ge1$. Put $d(q)=(\log t(q))'$ and
$M(q)=p'(q)/d(q)=t(q)p_t(t(q))$. Explicit differentiation gives
\[
 M(q)\sim\frac{T e^{q/2}}{\sqrt2q},\qquad
 d(q)=\frac{qe^{-q/2}}{\sqrt2T}(1+o(1)),
\]
and $M$ is eventually strictly increasing.
For $b_j=(\TT^j\one)(x_N)$, use the tilted distribution
\[\Pp(J=j)=\frac{b_jt(q)^j}{j!F(t(q),x_N)}.\]
Then for all large $q$, $0<\epsilon<1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:chernoff}
 \Pp(|J-NM(q)|>\epsilon NM(q))
 \le2\exp\{-cN\epsilon^2+C(q+1)^2\}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, with
$H_q(v)=p(q+v)-p(q)-M(q)[\log t(q+v)-\log t(q)]$,
we have $H_q(0)=H'_q(0)=0$ and, uniformly for $|v|\le1$,
\[
 H_q''(v)\to\tfrac14e^{-v/2},\qquad M(q)d(q+v)\to\tfrac12e^{-v/2}.
\]
Thus $|H_q(v)|\le v^2/2$ and
$M(q)|\log t(q\pm\eta)-\log t(q)|\ge\eta/4$ for large $q$ and
$0\le\eta\le1$. Comparing the tilted series at $q\pm\eta$ using
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:growing}, with $\eta=\epsilon/8$, bounds each tail exponent by
$-3N\epsilon^2/128+C(q+1)^2$, proving \eqref{a58:ln:eq:chernoff}.

Take
\[
 N=\lceil K n^{2/3}(\log n)^{2/3}\rceil,\qquad
 \epsilon=2N/n,\qquad NM(q)=(1-4\epsilon)n,
\]
with fixed sufficiently large $K$. Then
$q=\tfrac23\log n+\tfrac23\log\log n+O(1)$ and
$N\epsilon^2=4N^3/n^2\ge4K^3(\log n)^2$.
The central window in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:chernoff} has mass at least $1/2$.
It contains an index $j$ with
\[
 b_j\frac{t(q)^j}{j!}\ge
 \frac{F(t(q),x_N)}{4\epsilon NM(q)+6},\quad
 j\ge n-6\epsilon n,\quad N+j\le n.
\]
The root reaches $x_N$ by $N-1$ new-maximal steps, hence
$a_{N+j}\ge b_j$. Any ascent sequence extends injectively by appending
one plus its current number of ascents. This value is new: inductively
the maximum previously used value is at most the current ascent count.
Thus $a_n\ge a_{N+j}$, and Stirling or the factorial product bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:coarseenclosure}
 -C_1n^{2/3}(\log n)^{5/3}\le\log c_n
 \le C_2(\log(n+2))^2.
\end{equation}
For the lower bound the factorial-extension loss is
$O(N\log n)$, while the quadratic error and the pigeonhole loss are
smaller; eventually $1<t(q)<T$, so $-j\log t(q)\ge-n\log T$.
The upper bound follows already from the main theorem and coefficient
positivity (or from \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarseuniform}). In particular
$c_n^{1/n}\to1$, proving the full factorial-normalized root limit.
The lower exponent in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarseenclosure} reflects the broad-window
method and is not a proposed true correction scale.

\subsection{Three justified inverse statements}
For the cumulative sequence define
$N_S(y)=\min\{N:S_N\ge e^y\}$. Since $S_N$ is increasing and diverges,
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:quantcum} and the adjacent-integer squeeze give
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:cuminverse}
 \log N_S(y)=\sqrt{3y/2}+O(y^{1/4}\sqrt{\log y}).
\end{equation}
For the strictly increasing real-axis function, let $t_y$ solve
$A(t_y)=e^y$. Then \eqref{a58:ln:eq:mainA} gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:realinverse}
 \log\frac1{T-t_y}\sim\sqrt{3y/2},\qquad
 T-t_y=\exp\{-\sqrt{3y/2}(1+o(1))\}.
\end{equation}
The same quantitative argument gives
$\log(1/(T-t_y))=\sqrt{3y/2}+O(y^{1/4}\sqrt{\log y})$.
To see either inverse error, the leading scale first implies its logarithmic
variable is of order $\sqrt y$; divide the error
$O(y^{3/4}\sqrt{\log y})$ by the leading quadratic derivative of order
$\sqrt y$. Adjacent integer values in the cumulative inverse have a
vanishing logarithmic gap and obey the same bound. These are bounds on
logarithmic variables, not equivalents after exponentiation or exact
integer rounding formulas.

For the original integer counts put
$N_a(y)=\min\{n\ge1:\log a_n\ge y\}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:lambert}
 x=\frac{y}{W(y/(eT))},\qquad x\log\frac{x}{eT}=y,
\end{equation}
where $W$ is the positive real Lambert function. Stirling and
\eqref{a58:ln:eq:coarseenclosure} give
\begin{equation}\label{a58:ln:eq:ainverse}
 x-O(\log x)\le N_a(y)
 \le x+O\bigl(x^{2/3}(\log x)^{2/3}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Indeed the main smooth term is $f(x)=x\log(x/(eT))$ with derivative
$f'(x)=\log(x/T)$. The upper coefficient error $O(\log^2x)$ divided by
$f'(x)$ gives the left displacement $O(\log x)$; the lower error gives
the right displacement in \eqref{a58:ln:eq:ainverse}. Taylor's theorem on these
$o(x)$ windows and integer rounding complete the monotone inverse
comparison. There is no justified logarithmic-square correction to
$N_a$ from the limsup theorem alone.

\section{Results and the dependency boundary}
An ascent sequence is a word $w_1\cdots w_n$ of nonnegative integers with
$w_1=0$ and
\[
 0\le w_i\le1+\asc(w_1\cdots w_{i-1}),\qquad
 \asc(w)=\#\{i:w_i<w_{i+1}\}.
\]
Avoiding $000$ means that each value occurs at most twice, with no
requirement that equal occurrences be consecutive. Let $a_0=1$ and
let $a_n$ count these words. The sequence is OEIS A202058, also the
cap-two column of A294220 \cite{OEIS,OEIStriangle}. Its initial values are

 T=\frac{3\pi^2}{8},\qquad \mu=\frac1T=\frac8{3\pi^2},\qquad

\end{equation}

\section{The exact transfer model}\label{a58:rt:sec:transfer}
Twice-used labels are removed from the active alphabet, and the
once-used labels are compacted to an initial segment. In the published
recurrence, with $G_0(a,\ell,s)=1$,
\begin{align*}
 G_j(a,\ell,s)={}&\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}
 G_{j-1}(a+\one_{i>\ell}-1,i-1,s-1)\\
 &+\sum_{i=s}^{a+1}G_{j-1}(a+\one_{i>\ell},i,s+1),
 \qquad a_n=G_{n-1}(0,0,1)\quad(n\ge1).
\end{align*}
The adjusted $a$ and $\ell$ may equal $-1$. Compaction preserves suffix
counts and transition multiplicities. Its elementary bijection exchanges
the remaining capacities of adjacent labels $i,i+1$ below or at the
last value $\ell$, with $i<\ell$: reverse each maximal suffix block in the two labels and interchange
the labels within it. Multiplicities are exchanged while ascent counts
and the outside comparisons are preserved. Both labels are already
permitted, so all subsequent ascent-sequence bounds remain valid.
Repeated exchanges place once-used labels first, and exhausted labels
can be deleted. See \cite{CCEG} for the original compacted recurrence.

Set $m=a+2$, $k=\ell+1$, and $u=m-s$. The invariant state space is
\[
 \Sstate=\{(s,u,k):s\ge0,\ u\ge1,\ 0\le k<s+u\}.
\]
Write $m(x)=s+u$. For each integer $0\le i<m$, the child is
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:children}
 x_i=\begin{cases}
 (s-1,u,i),&i<s,\ i<k,\\
 (s-1,u+1,i),&i<s,\ i\ge k,\\
 (s+1,u,i+1),&i\ge s,\ i\ge k,\\
 (s+1,u-1,i+1),&i\ge s,\ i<k.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The first two cases repeat a label; the last two use a new label.
An ascent corresponds to $i\ge k$. The potentially invalid last case
with $u=1$ would require $s\le i<k\le s$, and therefore never occurs.
There are no repeat transitions when $s=0$.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:operator}
 (\TT f)(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m(x)-1}f(x_i),\qquad
 F(t,x)=\sum_{j\ge0}(\TT^j\one)(x)\frac{t^j}{j!}.
\end{equation}
The formal pre-empty state $x_*=(0,1,0)$ has the unique child $(1,1,1)$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:root}
 a_n=(\TT^n\one)(x_*),\qquad n\ge0.
\end{equation}
Each transition changes $m$ by at most one, and changes the integer
resource $H=s+2u$ by exactly $1$ or $-1$. The state
\[
 x_N=(N,1,N)
\]
is reached from $x_*$ by $N$ successive largest-new-label steps.
We shall use the continuation series
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:GN}
 G_N(t)=F(t,x_N)=\sum_{j\ge0}b_j\frac{t^j}{j!},
 \qquad b_j=(\TT^j\one)(x_N).
\end{equation}
Paths, rather than distinct terminal states, are always counted. A
merger of states therefore does not identify different prefixes.

\section{Two exact moment identities}\label{a58:rt:sec:moments}
Put $d(x)=u-k$. The number of children with degree $m+1$ minus the
number with degree $m-1$ is
\[
 [m-\max(s,k)]-\min(s,k)=m-s-k=u-k.
\]
There are $m$ children in total. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:degreeidentity}
 \TT m=m^2+d,\qquad d\ge2-m,\qquad |d|\le m.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fixed length paths and the slow decrease of ratios}
Choose one of the $a_n$ transfer paths of length $n$ from $x_*$ uniformly,
with multiplicity. Write $\E_n$ and $\Var_n$ for the terminal-state
expectation and variance. Since every terminal state has $m$ children,
\[
 R_n=\E_n m,
 \qquad R_{n+1}=\frac{\E_n(\TT m)}{\E_n m}.
\]
Substitution of \eqref{a58:rt:eq:degreeidentity} gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:rawidentity}
 R_{n+1}=R_n+\frac{\Var_n(m)+\E_n d}{R_n}.
\end{equation}
In particular, for every $n\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:slowdecrease}
 1\le R_n\le n+1,\qquad
 R_{n+1}\ge R_n-1+\frac2{R_n}\ge R_n-1.
\end{equation}
The degree starts at $1$ and can increase by at most one per step, which
proves the upper bound; every state has at least one child, which proves
the lower bound. This exact local regularity property is sufficient to
turn a sublinear bound on $\log c_n$ into a ratio limit.

The variance form \eqref{a58:rt:eq:varianceconjecture} of Section~\ref{a58:rt:sec:open} is Part~\ref{a58:part:fn}'s \eqref{a58:fn:eq:regularitytarget}. New here are the consequence $R_{n+1}\ge R_n-1$ in \eqref{a58:rt:eq:slowdecrease} and its use in Lemma~\ref{a58:rt:lem:slow}.

\subsection{Tilted paths and terminal degree}
Fix any starting state $x$ and $0<t<T$. Assign each individual length-$j$
path probability $t^j/(j!F(t,x))$. Let $J$ be its length and let $m,d$
refer to its terminal state. Finiteness of $F$ below $T$ is proved in
Section~\ref{a58:rt:sec:analytic}. All differentiations below are justified by
positivity and finiteness at any slightly later time $t'<T$:
$m\le m(x)+J$, $|d|\le m$, and all moments of $J$ are finite in the
interior of the convergence disk.

The transfer identities and termwise differentiation give
\begin{align}
 \E m&=F'/F,& \E J&=tF'/F,\notag\\
 \E(m^2+d)&=F''/F,& \E(Jm)&=tF''/F,\label{a58:rt:eq:tiltedmoments}\\
 &&\E J^2&=t^2F''/F+tF'/F.\notag
\end{align}
Expanding the square cancels the second derivatives exactly:
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:tiltedidentity}
 \E(J-tm)=0,\qquad
 \E[(J-tm)^2]=\E J-t^2\E d\le(1+t)\E J.
\end{equation}
The last inequality uses $d\ge-m$ and $t\E m=\E J$.
No independence, log-concavity assumption, Gaussian approximation or
local limit theorem is involved.

\section{An elementary slow decrease lemma}\label{a58:rt:sec:slow}
\begin{lemma}\label{a58:rt:lem:slow}
Let $0<\mu<1$, $a_n>0$, $c_n=a_n\mu^{-n}/n!$, and
$R_n=a_{n+1}/a_n$. Suppose $R_n\le n+1$ and $R_{n+1}\ge R_n-1$.
If $|\log c_n|\le E(n)$ eventually, where $E(n)\ge1$, $E(n)=o(n)$,
and $E(k)=O(E(n))$ uniformly for $n/2\le k\le2n$, then
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:slowerate}
 R_n=\mu(n+1)+O\!\left(\sqrt{nE(n)}+1\right).
\end{equation}
For the qualitative conclusion $R_n/[\mu(n+1)]\to1$, it is enough that
$\log c_n=o(n)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $D_n=R_n-\mu(n+1)$ and $L=1+\mu$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:defect}
 D_{n+1}\ge D_n-L,\qquad
 \log c_{k+1}-\log c_k
 =\log\left(1+\frac{D_k}{\mu(k+1)}\right).
\end{equation}
First suppose $D_n=D>0$, and set $h=\lfloor D/(2L)\rfloor$.
If $D$ is bounded there is nothing to prove. Otherwise $h\ge cD$,
and, for $0\le j<h$,
\[
 D_{n+j}\ge D-Lj\ge D/2.
\]
Because $D\le(1-\mu)(n+1)$, all these indices lie in $[n/2,2n]$
for large $n$. Also $D_{n+j}/[\mu(n+j+1)]$ belongs to a fixed bounded
nonnegative interval, since $R_{n+j}\le n+j+1$. On that interval
$\log(1+v)\ge c_\mu v$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:posexcursion}
 \log c_{n+h}-\log c_n
 \ge c\frac{hD}{n}\ge c'\frac{D^2}{n}.
\end{equation}
The left side is $O(E(n))$, giving the required bound.

If $D_n=-D<0$, set the same $h=\lfloor D/(2L)\rfloor$.
Telescoping the one-sided increment bound backwards gives
\[
 D_{n-j}\le-D+Lj\le-D/2,\qquad1\le j\le h.
\]
Positivity of $R_n$ implies $D<\mu(n+1)$, so $n-h\ge n/2$ for
large $n$. For these indices the argument of the logarithm in
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:defect} is positive. Using $\log(1-v)\le-v$ for $0\le v<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:negexcursion}
 \log c_{n-h}-\log c_n
 =-\sum_{k=n-h}^{n-1}\log\left(1+\frac{D_k}{\mu(k+1)}\right)
 \ge c'\frac{D^2}{n}.
\end{equation}
Again the left side is $O(E(n))$. Bounded $D$ and the floor cases
contribute only the additional $O(1)$ in \eqref{a58:rt:eq:slowerate}.

For the qualitative assertion, any subsequence with $|D_n|\ge\eta n$
for fixed $\eta>0$ would, by the same forward or backward excursion,
force a difference of order $n$ between logarithms at comparable
indices. This contradicts $\log c_k=o(k)$.
\end{proof}

Applying the lemma to \eqref{a58:rt:eq:oldenclosure} and
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:slowdecrease} already proves
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:oldrateratio}
 \frac{a_{n+1}}{(n+1)a_n}
 =\mu+O\!\left(n^{-1/6}(\log n)^{5/6}\right).
\end{equation}
Thus the ratio limit itself does not depend on the new lower enclosure.
The remainder of the proof improves its quantitative rate.

\subsection{The clock and its exact endpoint}
For $q\ge0$, set
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:clockpars}
 E=e^{-q},\qquad \mathcal R=2-E,\qquad
 p=\frac{q+\log\mathcal R}{2},\qquad
 b=\frac{e^p(1-E)}q,
\end{equation}
with $b(0)=1$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:clock}
 t(q)=\int_0^q\frac{v e^{-v/2}}{\sqrt{2-e^{-v}}(1-e^{-v})}\dd v.
\end{equation}
The integrand at zero is its continuous value $1$.
Direct differentiation gives $t'=1/b$ and $p'=1/\mathcal R$.
The substitution $y=\sqrt{2e^q-1}$ yields
\[
 t(\infty)=\int_1^\infty
 \frac{2\log((y^2+1)/2)}{y^2-1}\dd y.
\]
Integration by parts followed by $v=(y-1)/(y+1)$ gives
\begin{align*}
 t(\infty)
 &=-2\int_0^1\log v\,
     \frac{1+v}{(1-v)(1+v^2)}\dd v\\
 &=-2\int_0^1\log v
       \left(\frac1{1-v}+\frac{v}{1+v^2}\right)\dd v
 =\frac{\pi^2}{3}+\frac{\pi^2}{24}=\frac{3\pi^2}{8}=T.
\end{align*}
Boundary terms vanish; integrating the geometric and alternating
geometric series gives the two displayed values. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:clocktail}
 T-t(q)\asymp(q+1)e^{-q/2},\qquad
 \log\frac1{T-t(q)}=\frac q2-\log(q+1)+O(1),
\end{equation}
since for $q\ge1$ the derivative in \eqref{a58:rt:eq:clock} is between fixed
positive multiples of $qe^{-q/2}$. Compact intervals are absorbed into
the constants.

\subsection{A state uniform comparison}
For $x=(s,u,k)$, introduce
\[
 D=s+\mathcal R u,\qquad
 h_0(y)=\min(y,s)+\mathcal R\max(y-s,0),\qquad
 r(y)=\frac{h_0(y)}D,
\]
and write $r=r(k)$, $z=s/D$. Define the characteristic profile
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:psi}
 \psi(q,x)=\exp\{ps+qu-qr\}.
\end{equation}
Here $H/2\le D\le H$, $0\le r,z<1$, and $\psi(0,x)=1$.
For real $0\le y<m$ set
\[
 w(y)=\begin{cases}e^{-p+q\one_{y\ge k}},&y<s,\\
 e^{p-q\one_{y<k}},&y\ge s,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad g(y)=w(y)e^{-q(r(y)-r)}.
\]
Direct integration on the intervals separated by $s,k$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:frozen}
 \int_0^m g(y)\dd y=\lambda:=Db/\mathcal R.
\end{equation}
For example, at $k=0$ the integral is
\[
 \frac Dq\left[e^{q-p}(1-e^{-qs/D})
 +\frac{e^p}{\mathcal R}(e^{-qs/D}-e^{-q})\right]=\lambda,
\]
using $e^{q-p}=e^p/\mathcal R$; differentiating in the cut point $k$
verifies the same identity for every $k$. Each smooth piece of $g$ is
decreasing, breakpoints are integers, and $g\le\sqrt2 e^{q/2}$.
Thus, with $S=\sum_i g(i)$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:riemann}
 0\le S-\lambda\le3\sqrt2e^{q/2}.
\end{equation}
In the order of the four transitions \eqref{a58:rt:eq:children}, the child ranks
computed at the same $q$ are
\[
 r_i=\frac{i}{D-1},\quad \frac{i}{D+\mathcal R-1},\quad
 \frac{h_0(i)+1}{D+1},\quad
 \frac{h_0(i)+1}{D+1-\mathcal R}.
\]
All denominators in transitions that actually occur are positive.
Subtraction gives $|r_i-r(i)|\le4/D$. Except for a repeat ascent,
$r_i\ge r(i)$; in that exceptional case,
\[
 0\le r(i)-r_i
 =\frac{i(\mathcal R-1)}{D(D+\mathcal R-1)}\le\frac1D.
\]
The exact transition ratio is
$\psi(q,x_i)/\psi(q,x)=g(i)e^{-q(r_i-r(i))}$.
For the derivative at a fixed integer state,
\begin{align*}
 \partial_q\log\psi&=D/\mathcal R-B,\qquad
 B=r+qE\partial_{\mathcal R}r,\qquad |B|\le1,\\
 \partial_{\mathcal R}r&=
 \begin{cases}-r(1-z)/\mathcal R,&r\le z,\\
 -z(1-r)/\mathcal R,&r\ge z.
 \end{cases}
\end{align*}
The formulas agree at the interface, and
$-1/(4\mathcal R)\le\partial_{\mathcal R}r\le0$.
Combining these exact expressions with \eqref{a58:rt:eq:riemann} yields
\begin{align}
 \frac{\TT\psi}{b\psi}-\partial_q\log\psi
 &\le q e^{q/D}+3\sqrt2(q+1)e^{q/D}+1,\label{a58:rt:eq:coarseupper}\\
 \partial_q\log\psi-\frac{\TT\psi}{b\psi}
 &\le4q+1.\label{a58:rt:eq:coarselower}
\end{align}
Indeed $\TT\psi/\psi\le e^{q/D}S$ and
$\TT\psi/\psi\ge e^{-4q/D}\lambda$; use
$e^x-1\le xe^x$, $1-e^{-x}\le x$ and $e^{q/2}/b\le q+1$.

For $L\ge0$ an integer, put $I_L(s,u,k)=(s+L,u,k+L)$.
Each original transition $i$ is exactly the padded transition $i+L$,
with $L$ additional nonnegative transitions. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:padding}
 \TT(f\circ I_L)\le(\TT f)\circ I_L\qquad(f\ge0).
\end{equation}
Fix $Q$, choose $L=\lceil Q\rceil$, and set
\[
 V(q)=\frac{e(1+3\sqrt2)}2q^2+(3\sqrt2e+1)q.
\]
For $0\le q\le Q$, padded states have $D\ge L+1$, so
$e^{V(q)}\psi(q,I_Lx)$ is a supersolution of $\partial_qF=\TT F/b$.
Equation \eqref{a58:rt:eq:coarselower} makes
$e^{-2q^2-q}\psi(q,x)$ a subsolution. Both equal $1$ at $q=0$.

For completeness, comparison on the infinite state space requires more
than a finite matrix inequality. Give each of the $m$ transitions rate
$1$. The resulting chain has generator $\TT-mI$, and is nonexplosive
by domination of its degree by a Yule process. Expanding jump paths and
cancelling holding-time factors gives the positive representation
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:fk}
 F(t,x)=\E_x\exp\left(\int_0^t m(X_v)\dd v\right),
\end{equation}
with infinity initially allowed. Stop at the exit from $m\le M$.
Finite-state comparison, discarding a nonnegative supersolution exit
term and then letting $M\to\infty$, proves the upper bound below and
finiteness for $t<T$.

For the lower comparison at fixed $t<T$, choose $\eta>0$ with
$t+\eta<T$ and a padded supersolution valid through that later time.
Uniformly for $0\le v\le t$, the ratio of the subsolution at $v$ to
this supersolution at $v+\eta$ is at most $C e^{-c m(x)}$.
To see this, both $p(q(v+\eta))-p(q(v))$ and
$q(v+\eta)-q(v)$ have strictly positive compact minima. Rank and fixed
padding terms contribute bounded factors, while the $s,u$ terms give
the exponential decay. In the stopped representation the lower exit
term is therefore at most $Ce^{-cM}$ times the later supersolution at
the initial state. It vanishes as $M\to\infty$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:barriers}
 e^{-2q^2-q}\psi(q,x)\le F(t(q),x)
 \le e^{V(q)}\psi(q,I_{\lceil Q\rceil}x),\qquad0\le q\le Q.
\end{equation}
There is no appeal to uniqueness for an unbounded differential system.
Since
$\log\psi(q,I_Lx)-\log\psi(q,x)\le Lp(q)+q$, taking $Q=q$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:coarseuniform}
 \log F(t(q),x)=\log\psi(q,x)+O((q+1)^2),
\end{equation}
with constants independent of the state $x$.

For $x_N=(N,1,N)$, the profile logarithm is
$Np+q\mathcal R/(N+\mathcal R)$, so the uniform estimate becomes
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:growing}
 \log G_N(t(q))=Np(q)+O((q+1)^2),\qquad N\ge1.
\end{equation}
The same comparison at $x_*$, where $F(t,x_*)=A(t)$ and
$\log\psi(q,x_*)=q$, also gives the upper coefficient bound directly.
Choose $q=2\log n+2\log\log n+O(1)$, so that
$T-t(q)=O(1/n)$ by \eqref{a58:rt:eq:clocktail}. Positivity implies
\[
 c_n\le A(t(q))(T/t(q))^n,
\]
whose logarithm is $O(q^2)+O(1)=O((\log n)^2)$.
Thus the upper half of \eqref{a58:rt:eq:mainenclosure} uses only the coarse
comparison, not the earlier sharp cumulative theorem.

\subsection{Concentration for the large seed}
Let $\ell(q)=(\log t(q))'$ and
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:M}
 M(q)=\frac{p'(q)}{\ell(q)}=t(q)p_t(t(q))
 =\frac{t(q)e^{p(q)}(1-e^{-q})}{q\mathcal R(q)}.
\end{equation}
Explicit differentiation and \eqref{a58:rt:eq:clocktail} give
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:Masymp}
 M(q)\sim\frac{T e^{q/2}}{\sqrt2 q},\qquad
 \ell(q)\sim\frac{q e^{-q/2}}{\sqrt2 T},
\end{equation}
and $M$ is eventually strictly increasing. Under the tilted law
\[
 \Pp(J=j)=\frac{b_jt(q)^j}{j!G_N(t(q))},
\]
we claim that, for all sufficiently large $q$ and $0<\epsilon<1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:chernoff}
 \Pp\bigl(|J-NM(q)|>\epsilon NM(q)\bigr)
 \le2\exp\{-cN\epsilon^2+C(q+1)^2\}.
\end{equation}
Here and below constants are independent of $N$.

To prove this, define
\[
 H_q(v)=p(q+v)-p(q)-M(q)[\log t(q+v)-\log t(q)].
\]
Then $H_q(0)=H'_q(0)=0$ and, uniformly for $|v|\le1$,
\[
 H_q''(v)\longrightarrow\tfrac14e^{-v/2},\qquad
 M(q)\ell(q+v)\longrightarrow\tfrac12e^{-v/2}.
\]
For large $q$, these imply $|H_q(v)|\le v^2/2$ and
\[
 M(q)|\log t(q\pm\eta)-\log t(q)|\ge\eta/4,
 \qquad0\le\eta\le1.
\]
Apply the exponential Markov inequality by comparing the positive series
at $q$ and $q\pm\eta$. Formula \eqref{a58:rt:eq:growing} bounds either tail
exponent by
\[
 N\eta^2/2-N\epsilon\eta/4+C(q+1)^2.
\]
With $\eta=\epsilon/8$ this is
$-3N\epsilon^2/128+C(q+1)^2$, proving \eqref{a58:rt:eq:chernoff}.

\section{A quadratic cost extension bound}\label{a58:rt:sec:extension}
A path ending at degree $m$ has at least
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:continuations}
 (m-h)^h
\end{equation}
continuations of length $h$ whenever $h$ is a nonnegative integer and
$m>h$. Indeed the degree before each successive extension is at least
$m-h$, because a transition decreases it by at most one. The stronger
falling product $m(m-1)\cdots(m-h+1)$ is available but unnecessary.
The useful point is to retain all these continuations, rather than one
prescribed continuation at each step.

\subsection{Choosing the seed and controlling the mean length}
For large $n$, choose
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:seedchoice}
 N=\left\lceil\sqrt{n\log n}\right\rceil,\qquad
 \epsilon=\frac{D_0\log n}{\sqrt N},\qquad
 NM(q)=(1-4\epsilon)n,
\end{equation}
where $D_0$ is a fixed sufficiently large constant. Eventual monotonicity
of $M$ gives a solution $q$ on its increasing branch. Equation
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:Masymp} implies
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:scales}
 q=\log n+\log\log n+O(1),\quad \epsilon\to0,\quad
 N=o(\epsilon n),\quad (q+1)^2=o(N).
\end{equation}
The Chernoff interval $(1\pm\epsilon)(1-4\epsilon)n$ lies inside
$[n-5\epsilon n,n-3\epsilon n]$. Since
$N\epsilon^2=D_0^2(\log n)^2$, choosing $D_0$ sufficiently large in
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:chernoff} ensures that
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:central}
 \mathcal C=\{n-5\epsilon n\le J\le n-3\epsilon n\}
 \quad\hbox{has probability at least }7/8.
\end{equation}

A high-probability central event by itself does not bound the mean.
For that additional step, $v\mapsto\log G_N(e^v)$ is convex, with
derivative $\E J$ at $v=\log t(q)$. The forward secant and
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:growing} give
\begin{align}
 \E J
 &\le\frac{N[p(q+1)-p(q)]+C(q+2)^2}
               {\log t(q+1)-\log t(q)}\notag\\
 &\le C[N+(q+1)^2]M(q)=O(n).\label{a58:rt:eq:meanbound}
\end{align}
Here $p(q+1)-p(q)=O(1)$ and the denominator is comparable to $1/M(q)$,
as follows directly by integrating \eqref{a58:rt:eq:Masymp} over a unit interval.
The last equality uses \eqref{a58:rt:eq:seedchoice} and $q^2=o(N)$.

Now \eqref{a58:rt:eq:tiltedidentity}, \eqref{a58:rt:eq:meanbound} and Markov's inequality
for the square imply, for a fixed sufficiently large $A_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:degreeevent}
 \mathcal D=\{|J-t(q)m|\le A_0\sqrt n\}
 \quad\hbox{has probability at least }7/8.
\end{equation}
Therefore $\Pp(\mathcal C\cap\mathcal D)\ge3/4$.

\subsection{Extracting a useful coefficient and retaining its degree}
Write $t=t(q)$. For each integer $j$ in the central interval, let $K_j$
count length-$j$ paths from $x_N$ ending at degree
$m\ge j/t-A_0\sqrt n/t$. As $t$ is eventually bounded away from zero,
a fixed $B_0$ can replace $A_0/t$. There are at most $n+1$ possible
central integers. Hence some $j$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:goodmass}
 \frac{K_jt^j}{j!}\ge\frac{G_N(t)}{2(n+1)},\qquad
 m\ge j/t-B_0\sqrt n
 \quad\hbox{for every counted path}.
\end{equation}
This follows by summing the tilted mass over $\mathcal C\cap\mathcal D$
and then applying the pigeonhole principle.

Set $h=n-N-j$. Equations \eqref{a58:rt:eq:scales} and \eqref{a58:rt:eq:central} show
$h\ge0$ and $h\le5\epsilon n=o(n)$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
Also $j\sim n$ and $t\to T$, so the base below is positive.
Prefix each path by the fixed length-$N$ increasing path and then use
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:continuations}. This gives the exact counting inequality
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:extensioncount}
 a_n\ge K_j\left(j/t-B_0\sqrt n-h\right)^h.
\end{equation}
Different transfer paths remain distinct after prefixing and extension,
even when their terminal states agree. Thus \eqref{a58:rt:eq:extensioncount}
does not assume individual coefficient regularity or extract a local
coefficient asymptotic from the cumulative theorem.

\subsection{Cancellation of factorial costs}
Since $j=n-N-h$, write
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:z}
 j/t-B_0\sqrt n-h=\frac nt(1-z),\qquad
 z=\frac{N+(1+t)h+B_0t\sqrt n}{n}=o(1).
\end{equation}
Use $\log(1-z)\ge-2z$ eventually and
$\log(n!/j!)\le(N+h)\log n$. Combining
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:growing}, \eqref{a58:rt:eq:goodmass} and \eqref{a58:rt:eq:extensioncount}
gives
\begin{align}
 \log c_n\ge{}&Np(q)-N\log n+n\log(T/t)+N\log t\notag\\
 &-C\left((q+1)^2+\log(n+1)
             +\frac{hN}{n}+\frac{h^2}{n}+\frac h{\sqrt n}\right).
 \label{a58:rt:eq:cancelled}
\end{align}
For clarity, before simplification the lower bound is
\[
 \log G_N(t)-\log(2(n+1))+\log(j!/n!)
 +n\log T-j\log t+h\log(j/t-B_0\sqrt n-h).
\]
The $h\log n$ term from the many extensions cancels that portion of the
factorial loss, leaving only $-N\log n$ in \eqref{a58:rt:eq:cancelled}.
The last positive terms $n\log(T/t)$ and $N\log t$ may be discarded,
since eventually $1<t<T$.

The remaining seed cost has a useful exact cancellation as well.
Equations \eqref{a58:rt:eq:M} and \eqref{a58:rt:eq:seedchoice} give
\begin{align}
 p(q)-\log n
 &=\log(q/N)
 +\log\frac{(1-4\epsilon)\mathcal R(q)}{t(q)(1-e^{-q})}\notag\\
 &=\log(q/N)+O(1).\label{a58:rt:eq:seedcancel}
\end{align}
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:losses}
 \log c_n\ge-C\left(N\log(N/q)+N+q^2+\log n
             +\frac{hN}{n}+\frac{h^2}{n}+\frac h{\sqrt n}\right).
\end{equation}
Every loss is at most a constant multiple of
$\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2}$. More explicitly,
\begin{align*}
 N\log(N/q)&=O\!\left(\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2}\right),\\
 h^2/n&=O(\epsilon^2n)
       =O\!\left(n(\log n)^2/N\right)
       =O\!\left(\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2}\right),\\
 hN/n&=O(\sqrt N\log n)
       =O\!\left(n^{1/4}(\log n)^{5/4}\right),\\
 h/\sqrt n&=O\!\left(\sqrt{n/N}\log n\right)
       =O\!\left(n^{1/4}(\log n)^{3/4}\right),\\
 q^2+\log n&=O((\log n)^2).
\end{align*}
Together with the upper bound from Section~\ref{a58:rt:sec:analytic}, this proves
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:mainenclosure}. Lemma~\ref{a58:rt:lem:slow} with
$E(n)=C\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2}$ now gives
\[
 R_n=\mu(n+1)+O\!\left(n^{3/4}(\log n)^{3/4}\right).
\]
Dividing by $n+1$ proves \eqref{a58:rt:eq:mainratio}.

\section{The improved inverse enclosure}\label{a58:rt:sec:inverse}
The counts $a_n$ are nondecreasing because each transfer state has at
least one child. They tend to infinity by the lower enclosure and
Stirling's formula. Write
\[
 f(v)=v\log\frac{v}{eT},\qquad
 \log a_n=f(n)+e_n.
\]
Stirling's formula and \eqref{a58:rt:eq:mainenclosure} imply
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:inverseerror}
 -C\sqrt n(\log n)^{3/2}\le e_n\le C'(\log n)^2
\end{equation}
for sufficiently large $n$; the Stirling correction $O(\log n)$ is
absorbed. Equation \eqref{a58:rt:eq:lambert} gives $f(x)=y$, and
$f'(v)=\log(v/T)$.

For fixed sufficiently large $K_1$, put
$n_- =\lfloor x-K_1\log x\rfloor$. On this $o(x)$ window,
$f'(v)\sim\log x$, and therefore
\[
 f(n_-)\le y-(K_1+o(1))\log^2x.
\]
The upper bound in \eqref{a58:rt:eq:inverseerror} makes $\log a_{n_-}<y$
when $K_1$ exceeds the corresponding error constant. Monotonicity gives
$N_a(y)>n_-$.

Similarly, for a fixed sufficiently large $K_2$, put
$n_+=\lceil x+K_2\sqrt{x\log x}\rceil$. On this $o(x)$ window,
\[
 f(n_+)\ge y+(K_2+o(1))\sqrt x(\log x)^{3/2}.
\]
The lower error bound in \eqref{a58:rt:eq:inverseerror} then gives
$\log a_{n_+}\ge y$, and $N_a(y)\le n_+$. This proves
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:maininverse}, including integer rounding.
It improves the previous right displacement
$O(x^{2/3}(\log x)^{2/3})$; the left displacement remains $O(\log x)$.
It does not justify a logarithmic-square correction to the integer
inverse, because the full individual law \eqref{a58:rt:eq:open} is still open.

\section{The remaining pointwise problem}\label{a58:rt:sec:open}
One sufficient route from the earlier cumulative law
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:importedcum} to \eqref{a58:rt:eq:open} would be the sharper one-sided
estimate
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:desiredratio}
 R_n\ge\mu(n+1)-O(\log n).
\end{equation}
It would imply
$\log(c_{n+1}/c_n)\ge-C\log n/n$ eventually.
For completeness, this weak regularity condition forces the individual
logarithmic-square law for any positive sequence with
$\log S_n\sim\alpha(\log n)^2$, $\alpha>0$.
If along a subsequence
$\log c_n\le(\alpha-\delta)(\log n)^2$, choose fixed $\beta<1$
sufficiently close to $1$ that $C(1-\beta^2)/2<\delta/2$.
Summing the one-sided increment bound backwards gives, uniformly for
$n^\beta\le k\le n$,
\[
 \log c_k\le[\alpha-\delta/2+o(1)](\log n)^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
 S_n\le S_{\lfloor n^\beta\rfloor}
 +(n+1)\exp\{[\alpha-\delta/2+o(1)](\log n)^2\},
\]
whose logarithmic-square coefficient is smaller than $\alpha$, a
contradiction. The present raw-ratio error
$O(n^{3/4}(\log n)^{3/4})$ is much larger than $O(\log n)$,
so it supplies no such implication.

There is also an exact variance formulation. Factorial-normalized
log-concavity at adjacent indices is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:varianceconjecture}
 \Var_n(m)+\E_n(u-k)\le\frac{R_n^2}{n+1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $a_n/n!$ is log-concave at the center $n+1$ exactly when
$R_{n+1}/(n+2)\le R_n/(n+1)$; substitute
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:rawidentity}. If \eqref{a58:rt:eq:varianceconjecture} held eventually,
$R_n/(n+1)$ would be nonincreasing and, by the proved ratio limit,
never below $\mu$ thereafter. Thus $c_n$ would be eventually
nondecreasing. Then $S_n\le O(1)+(n+1)c_n$, and
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:importedcum} would prove \eqref{a58:rt:eq:open}.
The premise \eqref{a58:rt:eq:varianceconjecture} remains unproved.

\subsection{An exact counterexample to a childwise strengthening}
Let $f_r(x)=(\TT^r\one)(x)$. One tempting invariant would require,
for every transition $x\to y$,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:falsechild}
 r f_r(y)f_{r-1}(x)
 \le(r+1)f_{r-1}(y)f_r(x).
\end{equation}
It is false. Take $r=19$, $x=(5,1,4)$ and $y=(4,2,4)$, where the
transition selects the repeat label $i=4$. Exact recurrence evaluation
gives
\begin{align*}
 f_{18}(x)&=328610526895548,&
 f_{19}(x)&=2835761210180706,\\
 f_{18}(y)&=1125633749884382,&
 f_{19}(y)&=10225957430377930.
\end{align*}
The two sides of \eqref{a58:rt:eq:falsechild} are respectively
\begin{align*}
 L&=63846787924950777938074214657160,\\
 R&=63840570495847624656576542673840,
\end{align*}
and
\[
 L-R=6217429103153281497671983320>0.
\]
This is a reachable-state obstruction: six largest-new-label steps
reach $(6,1,6)$; selecting the repeat label $i=4$ reaches $x$, and
selecting it again reaches $y$. The archive's independent memoized
recurrence reproduces all four counts and the exact integer difference.
The counterexample rules out this childwise assertion. It does not
refute the statewise log-concavity conjectures below.

The counterexample therefore refutes no delivered and no repository claim. \subsection{Finite evidence and its limits}
Two exact integer scans found no violation, each in $48{,}510{,}450$
inequalities, over all valid states and center indices satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:finitescope}
 1\le r\le179,\qquad H(x)+r+1\le200,\qquad H=s+2u.
\end{equation}
The first tested factorial-normalized log-concavity,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:factorialtest}
 (r+1)f_r(x)^2\ge r f_{r-1}(x)f_{r+1}(x).
\end{equation}
The second tested the stronger rising-factorial normalization,
\begin{equation}\label{a58:rt:eq:risingtest}
 (r+m)f_r(x)^2\ge(r+m-1)f_{r-1}(x)f_{r+1}(x),
 \qquad m=s+u.
\end{equation}
Here $(m)_r=m(m+1)\cdots(m+r-1)$, so \eqref{a58:rt:eq:risingtest} is
log-concavity of $f_r(x)/(m)_r$. For $m\ge1$ it is at least as strong
as \eqref{a58:rt:eq:factorialtest}; the two agree when $m=1$, in particular
at $x_*$. At $r=1$, the slack in \eqref{a58:rt:eq:risingtest} is exactly
$m(s+k)$, with equality when $s=k=0$.

The resource restriction is a safe finite domain, not an uncontrolled
truncation. Every step changes $H$ by $\pm1$. To compute all $f_j(x)$
with $H(x)\le200-j$, the preceding layer through $H\le201-j$
therefore contains every child needed. At layer $j=r+1$, the three
values in a test have been computed exactly. All arithmetic in these
two scans is Python integer arithmetic. Their root arrays through
$a_{180}$ agree. These finite results suggest useful targets, but they
prove neither eventual log-concavity nor any asymptotic estimate.

\section{Reproduction and source scope}\label{a58:rt:sec:reproduction}
The archive contains this PDF and editable TeX source, the unchanged
predecessor PDF and TeX, portable build and replay entry points,
verification scripts, frozen exact outputs and SHA-256 checksums.
It contains no third-party paper or private working correspondence.
Its README gives the required software and exact commands.

Run \texttt{bash replay.sh core} after extraction. The core replay
checks the manifest, exact transfer identities on a finite state set,
forward moments and root counts, the childwise counterexample, and a
coefficientwise version of the tilted square identity. The separate
full replay, \texttt{bash replay.sh full}, regenerates both scans in
\eqref{a58:rt:eq:finitescope} and compares the outputs with the frozen files.
Replay outputs go to a separate build directory; they do not overwrite
frozen results. All mathematical verification scripts use only the
Python standard library. Rebuilding the PDF uses standard pdfLaTeX
packages listed in the source and requires no network access.

The new moment identities, the excursion proof, and the improved
extension argument have received independent analytical review. This
is not a formal proof-assistant certificate. Computational checks
corroborate the exact recurrence implementations and finite examples;
the uniform and asymptotic estimates are proved by the arguments in
the text.
