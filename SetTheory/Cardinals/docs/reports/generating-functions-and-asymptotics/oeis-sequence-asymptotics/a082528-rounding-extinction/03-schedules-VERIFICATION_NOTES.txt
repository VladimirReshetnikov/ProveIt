VERIFICATION NOTES
Microscopic Schedules in Rounding Extinction
5 October 2026

1. DELIVERABLE AND SCOPE

The package contains a 29-page mathematical article, its complete LaTeX
source, four vector PDF figures with PNG previews, reproducible Python
code, and the output of a completed default computation.

The article supplies mathematical proofs. The computation checks finite
instances of its backward-threshold mechanism, constructs exact rational
enclosure endpoints, and illustrates limiting formulas. It does not
constitute formal verification of the article, and it does not prove the
asymptotic convergence statements from finitely many observations.

The finite Mobius-inversion theorem for every bounded increment law was
added to the mathematical manuscript. Its proof is not part of the finite
computation tests described below.

2. EXACT DYNAMICS AND INDEXING

Every weight sequence starts at w_1=1. Write r_k=w_k/w_(k-1), for k>=2.
The forward integer quotient Q_k=x_k/w_k (using the article's notation
for the remaining mass x_k) follows

    Q_k = floor(Q_(k-1) / r_k).

The least initial state that reaches a specified integer target at stage K
is obtained by the backward recursion

    q_K = target,
    q_(k-1) = ceil(r_k * q_k).

For survival, target=1, and T_w(K)=q_1. All tested r_k are rational. Their
numerators and denominators are handled as Python integers; both the
forward floors and backward ceilings use exact integer division.

For a periodic tuple (alpha_0,...,alpha_(p-1)), the transition indexed by k
uses alpha_(k mod p). A positive phase has r_k=1+alpha_(k mod p)/k. The
two zero-phase choices are an exact plateau r_k=1 and an active weak
increment r_k=1+1/k^2.

The separate explicit perturbation models use

    w_(2j-1)=j,    w_(2j)=j+epsilon/j,

with epsilon=0, 1/2, 1/100, and 1/10000. Their ratios are derived exactly
from these weights, rather than substituted with an asymptotic expansion.

3. THE TEN WEIGHT MODELS

The six periodic models are:

    (1)
    (1/2,3/2)
    (0,2), with the zero phase an exact plateau
    (0,2), with the zero phase active
    (1/2,1,3/2)
    (1/2,3/2,1)

The four explicit perturbation models are the epsilon values listed in
Section 2. Thus the permuted tuples are separate finite-dynamics models,
even though their limiting drift is the same.

4. COMPLETED FINITE CHECKS

Inverse-boundary checks: 3,200 passed.

For each of ten models, every K from 1 through 40 and every terminal
target from 1 through 4 were tested. The computed backward threshold was
run forward and shown to attain the target. One less than that threshold
was run forward and shown not to attain the target. Counting both sides
gives 10 * 40 * 4 * 2 = 3,200 checks.

Exhaustive small-survival checks: 2,847 passed.

For each model and each K from 1 through 15, every initial integer from
zero through T_w(K)+2 was tested. The forward survival decision agreed
with the criterion initial >= T_w(K) on every tested input.

Threshold diagnostics: 310 records.

Each of the ten models was evaluated at 31 selected terminal stages,
including K=100, 1000, 10000, and 100000. The threshold field in every
record is an exact integer. The selected K values and results are recorded
in data/thresholds.csv. Larger K values can be requested through the
script's --max-K option, but they are not part of the delivered default run.

The data/verification.json and data/run_log.txt files record the outcome.

5. EXACT RATIONAL CERTIFICATES

For rational phase data and rational z, the code enumerates the exact
breakpoints of the piecewise constant drift D. On an interval (a,b) where
D=d, its contribution to t is exactly

    (a+d)/(b+d).

Consequently t(z) is constructed as a product of rational numbers using
fractions.Fraction, without floating-point breakpoint comparisons.

All certificate examples in the delivered run have mean m=1. With eta the
active-zero frequency and rho the total active frequency, the article's
enclosure theorem gives

    (z + eta/(m+1)) * t(z)^(m+1)
        <= L <=
    (z + rho/(m+1)) * t(z)^(m+1).

Because m+1=2 here, both endpoints are exact rational numbers. The file
data/certificates.json stores their complete numerators and denominators,
as well as those of t(z).

There are 22 certificate records: z=100 and z=1000 for eleven drift
entries. These comprise the six periodic drift entries and five density
entries theta=0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1. Some entries coincide: permuting the
positive phases preserves the drift, and the endpoint density models
coincide with the plateau and fully active cases. Thus 22 records should
not be read as 22 distinct mathematical laws.

The arithmetic construction of each endpoint is exact. That the endpoints
enclose the mathematical limiting constant is a consequence of the proof
in the article. The code also checks that a numerical evaluation of the
proposed constant lies between them; this is a numerical compatibility
check, not an independently certified evaluation of the Gamma functions.

6. ROUNDED DIAGNOSTICS

Weights and normalized thresholds are evaluated with 70-digit Decimal
arithmetic. The explicit perturbation weights have exact rational formulas
before their conversion to Decimal. General periodic weights are evaluated
by successive Decimal products.

The constants 1/pi, 1/(2*pi), and pi/8 use a Decimal evaluation of pi from
Machin's identity. Other commensurable constants use the standard-library
math.lgamma function on exact rational arguments converted to floating
point. The stored arguments are exact; the evaluations are not.

The CSV decimal displays are rounded values. They are not guaranteed
outward-rounded endpoints and should not be quoted as such. For a fully
exact enclosure, use the rational fields in data/certificates.json.

The profile samples, plotting coordinates, and rendered curves use
floating-point values. Their role is illustration. The exact q_k values
underlying the sampled backward trajectories are included separately in
data/profile_samples.csv.

8. CLAIMS NOT MADE BY THE COMPUTATION

The finite checks do not establish an asymptotic error rate. Observed
convergence at K<=100000 is not a certified bound on the remaining error.
No K^(-2/3) rate, bounded-error claim, or other rate is asserted as a result
of these computations.

The finite checks do not prove the full averaging theorem, the sharp
spectrum theorem, sparse-modification invariance, nonexistence examples,
or the finite Mobius-inversion theorem. Those claims rest on their stated
hypotheses and mathematical proofs in the manuscript.

Neither the Python checks nor the accompanying mathematical proofs have
been checked by a proof assistant. This package makes no formal-
verification claim and no claim of exhaustive historical priority.

9. REPRODUCTION

From the package directory, use:

    python3 code/verify.py

or, without regenerating figures:

    python3 code/verify.py --no-figures

Python 3.10 or later is required. The delivered run used Python 3.12.
The checks and data generation use only the standard library. NumPy and
Matplotlib are needed only for figure generation. See README.txt for the
LaTeX reproduction commands and a complete file guide.
