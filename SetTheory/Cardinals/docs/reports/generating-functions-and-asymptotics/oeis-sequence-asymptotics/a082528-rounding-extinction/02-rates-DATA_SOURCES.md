# Sources and attribution for Report188

The mathematical article is self-contained. Its references are supplied for
attribution and model-level comparison; third-party papers, raw records, and
third-party program text are not bundled.

## Bounded OEIS fixtures

Exactly 342 displayed integer terms are extracted into `data/oeis_fixtures.json`:

- [A002491](https://oeis.org/A002491), 53 terms at indices 1–53, interpreted as
  the first-power survival thresholds. Revision 109, 20 August 2026
- [A073047](https://oeis.org/A073047), 79 terms at indices 1–79, interpreted as
  first-power first-zero indices. Revision 15, 7 June 2020
- [A082527](https://oeis.org/A082527), 105 terms at indices 0–104, square-power
  first-zero indices. Revision 8, 11 December 2025
- [A082528](https://oeis.org/A082528), 105 terms at indices 0–104, cube-power
  first-zero indices. Revision 6, 30 March 2012

The fixed official `oeis/oeisdata` records were inspected on 3–4 October 2026.
Their extraction hashes, contributor names, URLs, exact offsets, and counts are
stored in the JSON. See `data/README.md` for OEIS Foundation attribution and
Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licensing. The transformation is
bounded integer extraction with minimal added provenance, not redistribution
of raw exports or comments. The package checker verifies all fixture values.

The square-power record's explanatory example contains an arithmetic typo:
`4 floor(10/4)` is 8. The defining recurrence and listed values agree; the typo
is not used. The cube-power record contains the real-positive-power leading
conjecture addressed in the article. The first-power `O(1)` inverse-error
conjecture is not resolved here.

## Historical and analytic references

- P. Erdős and E. Jabotinsky, *On sequences of integers generated by a sieving
  process*, Parts I and II (1958),
  [Part I](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1958-08.pdf) and
  [Part II](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1958-09.pdf).
  Their first-power specialization supplies the historical leading and
  `O(k^(4/3))` threshold result. General nested-ceiling methodology is classical
- D. M. Broline and D. E. Loeb, *The combinatorics of Mancala-type games: Ayo,
  Tchoukaillon, and 1/pi* (1995),
  [arXiv:math/9502225](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9502225).
  Its stronger claimed remainder is not used. The current A002491 record's
  June 2021 Knuth comment explains the summation objection
- S. Li, *Asymptotics of the Tchoukaillon array and a conjecture of Beluhov*,
  [arXiv:2608.17517v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17517v1), 18 August 2026.
  This studies a two-coordinate first-power array, rather than grids `j^p`.
  No improvement or correction to its independent interior theorem is claimed
- [Generalized rounding up to pi](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/4698076/how-to-prove-this-recurrence-relation-for-generalized-rounding-up-to-pi/4705436),
  original Math.StackExchange question and answer (2023). Its parameter changes
  the selected multiple on linear grids, yielding a different Gamma-ratio model
- NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions,
  [Gamma inequalities](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.6) and
  [log-Gamma expansion, Equation 5.7.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.7#E3).
  Log-convexity and the convergent parameter series are classical inputs.
  The specific Gamma-ratio bounds needed for the proof are derived in the article

These sources were inspected at the relevant definitions, statements, and proof
scope. No directly matching arbitrary-power-grid theorem was located in this
bounded source check. This is not a worldwide priority or novelty certification.

## Exact checks and floating diagnostics

The original mandatory Python suite uses exact integer and Fraction arithmetic.
Its integer threshold samples are computed from the defined recursion, rather
than imported floating estimates. Gamma-product formulas are tested as finite
rational identities. The optional diagnostic script uses ordinary floating
Gamma evaluation, clearly labeled; it does not provide interval certificates.
No source reference is used to imply a stronger error bound than the article
actually proves.
