# First thermal correction and refined Lambert-W inverse for A022629

Proposed ordinary deduction from EXACT_SADDLE_EXPANSION.md, 1 October 2026. Independent review pending.

Write m_0=√(2n), L_0=log m_0, and C=π²/6. Then

log a_n=m_0(L_0−1)+C m_0/(L_0−1)+O(m_0/L_0³).

Consequently, for s=log y, m_*=s/W(s/e), L_*=log m_*, and N(y)=min{n:a_n≥y},

N(y)=m_*²/2 · [1−π²/(3L_*(L_*−1))+O(L_*^(−4))].

The order and coefficient of this new correction are deduced analytically, not fitted.

## 1. A uniform soft-cutoff integral estimate

For t=log m/m, L=log m→∞, let g(x)=log x−tx. Sum–integral comparison for the unimodal function log(1+exp g(x)) has error O(L): its total variation is at most twice its maximum, which is O(L). Thus

f(t)=∫_0^∞log(1+exp g(x))dx+O(L).

Subtract ∫_0^m g(x)dx=m(L/2−1). On x≤m/2, the remaining correction is O(sqrt(m)log m): x∈(0,2) contributes O(1), and on [2,m/2] use log(1+exp(−g))≤exp(tx)/x≤sqrt(m)/x. On x≥2m the correction is bounded by ∫_2m^∞x exp(−tx)dx=O(1/L). These errors are smaller than m/L^K for every fixed K.

On m/2≤x≤2m put v=tx−log x. This is strictly increasing for large L. Write x=x(v) for its inverse branch through x(0)=m. The correction there is

∫ log(1+exp(−|v|)) x'(v)dv.

Its tails |v|≥L/4 contribute O(m exp(−L/4)/L), again smaller than m/L^K for each fixed K. On the remaining symmetric interval,

x'(0)=m/(L−1), x''(0)=−m/(L−1)³,
x'''(v)=O(m/L⁴), uniformly for |v|≤L/4.

The last assertion follows either by implicit differentiation, or from

x'''=(3(h'')²−h'''h')/(h')⁵,
 h(x)=tx−log x,
 h'≍L/m, h''≍1/m², h'''=O(1/m³)

on this interval. The linear term in v integrates to zero. Since

∫_(−∞)^∞ log(1+exp(−|v|))dv=π²/6,

Taylor's theorem and its finite second moment prove

f(t)=m(L/2−1)+C m/(L−1)+O(m/L⁴).             (1)

## 2. Saddle localization

Similarly, μ(t)=Σ k p_k differs by O(m) from the integral of x/(1+exp(tx−log x)); the integrand is unimodal and has maximum O(m). Subtract the hard integral m²/2. Under the same variable change, the remaining kernel is

[1/(1+exp v)−1_{v<0}] x(v)x'(v).

The bracket is odd and exponentially decreasing. The derivative of x(v)x'(v) is O(m²/L²), and the noncentral parts are negligible compared with m²/L² by the same bounds as above. Therefore

μ(t)=m²/2+O(m²/L²).

At the exact saddle μ(t_n)=n this implies m=m_0[1+O(L_0^(−2))]. Define

Q_0(m)=n log(m)/m+m(log(m)/2−1).

Its derivative vanishes at m_0 and Q_0''(m_0)=(L_0−1)/m_0. Uniformly in the just-localized interval, Q_0(m)−Q_0(m_0)=O(m_0/L_0³), while the variation of C m/(log m−1) is also O(m_0/L_0³). Equation (1) therefore gives

f(t_n)+nt_n=m_0(L_0−1)+C m_0/(L_0−1)+O(m_0/L_0³).

The relative saddle theorem gives log a_n=f(t_n)+nt_n−½log(2πV)+O(1/w). Since log V=O(L_0), this proves the asserted correction.

## 3. Inversion

Write H(m)=m(log m−1), so H(m_*)=s. Set

m=m_*[1−C/(L_*(L_*−1))].

Taylor expansion of H and of C m/(log m−1) gives

H(m)+C m/(log m−1)=s+O(m_*/L_*³).

The coefficient theorem has an error of the same order, and H'(m)~L_*. Thus the inverse m differs from this expression by O(m_*/L_*⁴). Strict monotonicity, as in LEADING_AND_INVERSE.md, supplies the identical bound for the integer inverse after rounding. Squaring m and dividing by two yields the displayed formula; the square of its order-L_*^(−2) correction is itself part of the allowed O(L_*^(−4)) remainder.

## 4. Route to every inverse-logarithmic order

This same proof gives a constructive hierarchy. For fixed J, Taylor-expand x'(v) to arbitrary finite order; odd powers vanish. The coefficient attached to x^(2j+1)(0) is

2(1−2^(−2j−1)) ζ(2j+2),

because the 2jth moment of log(1+exp(−|v|)) is 2(2j)!(1−2^(−2j−1))ζ(2j+2). All inverse derivatives are rational functions of L times m and are generated by implicit differentiation of tx−log x=v. For example

x'''(0)=m(2L+1)/(L−1)⁵.

The tails and sum–integral discrepancy are smaller than every fixed m/L^K. The exact saddle and this uniform expansion can then be minimized or formally inverted to any fixed inverse-logarithmic order, with remainder control from Taylor's theorem. A separate coefficient generator and explicit higher-order statement remain to be checked; this paragraph does not claim a completed exponentially small transseries.
