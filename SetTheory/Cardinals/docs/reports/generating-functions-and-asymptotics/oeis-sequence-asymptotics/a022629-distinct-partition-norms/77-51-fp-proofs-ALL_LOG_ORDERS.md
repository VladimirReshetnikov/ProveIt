# Constructive inverse-logarithmic hierarchy for A022629

Proposed extension for independent review, 1 October 2026. The exact-saddle and first thermal notes remain unchanged. This result is an all-orders inverse-logarithmic expansion; exponentially small sectors are not claimed.

Put m=√(2n), L=log m and P=π². There are explicitly generated constants d_j such that, for every fixed K≥1,

log a_n=m[L−1+Σ_(j=1)^K d_j L^(−j)+O_K(L^(−K−1))].       (1)

The first four are

d_1=P/6, d_2=P/6, d_3=P/6−P²/72, d_4=P/6+P²/40.

For s=log y, m_*=s/W_0(s/e), L_*=log m_*, the strictly monotone integer inverse satisfies

N(y)=m_*²/2 · [1+Σ_(j=2)^(K+1) b_j L_*^(−j)+O_K(L_*^(−K−2))].       (2)

In particular

b_2=−P/3, b_3=−P/3, b_4=P(P−3)/9, b_5=P(P−10)/30.

All constants and arbitrary further orders are generated by the following finite formal algorithm. The attached generator uses exact rational arithmetic in P; its finite output corroborates these formulas rather than replacing the analytic proof below.

## 1. Soft-cutoff expansion at every order

Define rational functions

R_1(x)=1/(x−1), R_(r+1)(x)=[R_r(x)+xR_r'(x)]/(x−1).

For the inverse branch x(v) of tx−log x=v with x(0)=M and t=log M/M, implicit differentiation gives

x^(r)(0)=M R_r(log M).

Indeed, with t held fixed, differentiation in v acts as x/(tx−1) times differentiation in x; applying this to xR_r(tx) proves the recurrence. Also R_r(L)=O_r(L^(−r−1)) for r≥2, whereas R_1(L)=O(L^(−1)). The same bounds hold uniformly when |v|≤cL and x remains in a fixed neighborhood of M.

Let η(s)=(1−2^(1−s))ζ(s). Repeating the symmetric-kernel Taylor proof of THERMAL_CORRECTION.md to any even degree gives, for each fixed J≥0,

f(log M/M)=M[log M/2−1+Σ_(j=0)^J 2η(2j+2) R_(2j+1)(log M)]
 +O_J(M/(log M)^(2J+4)).                              (3)

The next even amplitude derivative is x^(2J+3), of size O(M/L^(2J+4)), and the kernel has every finite moment. The discarded nonsymmetric tails, small-size region and sum–integral discrepancy are smaller than M/L^A for every fixed A, as established in the thermal note. Odd Taylor terms cancel. This proves (3), including its uniformity for M/m in a fixed small neighborhood of one.

Write the resulting formal Laurent series as

T(L)=Σ_(j≥0)2η(2j+2)R_(2j+1)(L).

Every coefficient of L^(−k) receives contributions from only finitely many j, so this is a well-defined formal series.

## 2. Formal minimization and coefficients d_j

Let h=1/L and use ρ=M/m. Define the formal expression

Q(ρ,h)=(ρ+ρ^(−1))(h^(−1)+log ρ)/2−ρ+ρ T(h^(−1)+log ρ).

There is a unique formal solution ρ(h)=1+O(h²) to

0=h∂_ρQ
 = h(1−ρ^(−2))(h^(−1)+log ρ−1)/2
   +h[T(h^(−1)+log ρ)+T'(h^(−1)+log ρ)].               (4)

After multiplication by h this equation has constant derivative one with respect to ρ at (ρ,h)=(1,0). Thus formal implicit inversion determines its coefficients recursively, one linear equation per coefficient. Define d_j by

Q(ρ(h),h)=h^(−1)−1+Σ_(j≥1)d_j h^j.

These are the claimed constants. To justify the formal computation asymptotically, truncate T to sufficient order in (3). On the localized saddle interval M/m=1+O(L^(−2)), its real Q has second ρ derivative comparable to L and a unique critical point near one. The polynomial truncations of ρ(h) approximate that critical point to arbitrary order, by Taylor's theorem or the ordinary implicit function theorem applied after multiplication by h. The uniform error in (3) changes the minimum by at most that error. The exact-saddle theorem then contributes only O(log m) to log a_n, smaller than m/L^A for every fixed A. This proves (1) at every fixed order without interchanging infinite asymptotic series.

## 3. Formal inversion and coefficients b_j

Set D(h)=Σ_(j≥1)d_jh^j. Let τ(h)=1+O(h²) solve

τ[h^(−1)+log τ−1+D((h^(−1)+log τ)^(−1))]=h^(−1)−1.   (5)

Multiplication by h again makes the derivative with respect to τ equal to one at (1,0), so formal inversion is unique. Define

τ(h)²=1+Σ_(j≥2)b_j h^j.

A truncation of (1) through L^(−K) has an entropy error O(m/L^(K+1)); dividing by the entropy derivative ~L gives a relative m error O(L^(−K−2)). Strict monotonicity and integer rounding yield the same order for N(y), because a single integer changes its relative size by O(m_*^(−2)), smaller than every fixed inverse-logarithmic power. This proves (2).
