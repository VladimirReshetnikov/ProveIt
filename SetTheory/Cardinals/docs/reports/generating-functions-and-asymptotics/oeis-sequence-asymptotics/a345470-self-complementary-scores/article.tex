\begin{abstract}
A tournament score sequence $s_1\le\cdots\le s_n$ is self-complementary when $s_i+s_{n+1-i}=n-1$. Let $C_n$ count these sequences (OEIS A345470) and $D_n$ the strong ones (A351869). This report prints two manuscripts as Parts~I and~II. Part~I (Report~116, the base) proves $C_n\sim A\,2^nn^{-3/4}$ and $D_n\sim e^{-\lambda}A\,2^nn^{-3/4}$ with one amplitude $A>0$ for both parities. Here $\lambda=0.33023754\ldots$ is the constant of the ordinary score sequences ($S(1/4)=e^\lambda$, Kolesnik), so the limiting strong fraction is $e^{-\lambda}=0.71875297\ldots$. Part~I also proves a harmonic identity for the Denisov--Wachtel harmonic function, a Lambert-$W$ smooth inverse with vanishing-width integer brackets, and an eventual zero-or-one gap between the strong and the all thresholds. Part~II (Report~114) proved the bounds $\Theta(2^nn^{-3/4})$ first, by a bounded-bridge argument, with an $O(1)$ inverse, and shows that $C_n$ is nondecreasing for all $n$ and $D_n$ for $n\ge2$. Both manuscripts copy the limit density of the Denisov--Wachtel conditional limit theorem as printed (2015, p.~170). That display integrates to $0.1154552960\ldots$, not~$1$. The write identifies the misprint: the display is not the density $\kappa^{-1}\bar h(1,x,-y)$ that the same paper's Remark~2 takes from Groeneboom, Jongbloed and Wellner; a factor $s^{-2}$ and the constant are lost. The write proves that the corrected density has total mass exactly~$1$ and velocity marginal $\sqrt{2/3}\,\Gamma(3/4)^2/\pi=0.3902762\ldots$ at~$0$, about ten times Part~I's printed value $0.0377237\ldots$. Hence $A=\sqrt6\,\Gamma(3/4)\pi^{-3/2}\Vdw(1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2)$, and the one proof step that used the misprinted shape is repaired. Exact counts suggest $A\approx0.387$ (uncertified). A certified value of $A$, rates, correction terms and a direct proof of the harmonic identity remain open. The report is unrefereed, and nothing in it is formalized.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Relation to the ordinary score sequences (a000571)}\label{sct:sec:neighbours}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Relation to the ordinary score sequences}
The collection's report \file{a000571-tournament-score-sequences} (same directory) treats ordinary score sequences: $S_n$ (A000571), the strong ones $I_n$, $S(z)=e^{A(z)}$ and $I(z)=1-e^{-A(z)}$ with the singularity at $\rho=1/4$. Its constant $\lambda=A(1/4)=0.3302375439859293$ is exactly the $\lambda$ of \eqref{sct:eq:lambda}: Part~I's $T_k$ and $S_k$ are its $I_k$ and $S_k$, and \eqref{sct:eq:ordinary-recurrence} is its $S=e^A$. Its value lies inside Part~I's certified enclosure $[0.3302375420437401,0.3302375453489038]$, and $e^{-\lambda}=0.71875297770\ldots$ at its value lies inside both of Part~I's intervals for $r$.

\emph{Why the strong fraction is $e^{-\lambda}$ here but $e^{-2\lambda}$ there.} a000571 records Kolesnik's $S_n\sim e^{\lambda}4^nn^{-5/2}/(2\sqrt\pi)$ and $I_n\sim e^{-\lambda}4^nn^{-5/2}/(2\sqrt\pi)$, so $I_n/S_n\to e^{-2\lambda}$ (its equation \texttt{tss:eq:known}). Both are transforms of one singular series: if $a_n=[z^n]A(z)\sim4^nn^{-5/2}/(2\sqrt\pi)$, then $[z^n]e^{\pm A(z)}\sim\pm e^{\pm A(1/4)}a_n$, because $A(1/4)$ is finite and the singular part of $e^{\pm A}$ is $\pm e^{\pm A(1/4)}$ times that of $A$. So $S_n\sim e^{\lambda}a_n$ and $I_n=-[z^n]e^{-A(z)}\sim e^{-\lambda}a_n$ ($n\ge1$): the all count gains one factor $e^{\lambda}$ and the strong count loses one, and their ratio is $e^{-2\lambda}$. Here instead $D(z)=C(z)\,(1-T(z^2))=C(z)\,e^{-A(z^2)}$ by Stockmeyer's \eqref{sct:eq:SC-gf}. The two factors are not transforms of one series: $C_n$ is of order $2^nn^{-3/4}$, while the coefficients of $e^{-A(z^2)}$ are of order $2^nn^{-5/2}$ (on even $n$) and summable at the radius $1/2$. The heavier factor dominates and the lighter one contributes only its value $e^{-A(1/4)}=1/S(1/4)=e^{-\lambda}$, which is the convolution argument of Section~\ref{sct:sec:D}. In particular $r=1-I(1/4)$ in a000571's notation.

\emph{Inverses.} Part~I's Lambert $W_{-1}$ inverse \eqref{sct:eq:Lambert} is the same mechanism as a000571's \texttt{tss:eq:lambert}, there for $4^xx^{-5/2}$ and here for $2^tt^{-3/4}$: a second instance, not a re-proof.

\emph{Other reports.} No other report of the repository treats self-complementary score sequences, A345470, A351869, integrated random walks or the Denisov--Wachtel exit-time theorem (searched 5~October 2026); \file{a377922-corner-polyhedra-schnyder} and \file{a215561-fixed-composition-excursions} cite Denisov--Wachtel's paper on random walks in cones, a different paper. Nothing in this report is formalized in Lean or Rocq, and its placement confers no formal status.

\begin{partabstract}
Let $C_n$ count self-complementary tournament score sequences of length $n$
(OEIS A345470), and let $D_n$ count the strong ones (A351869). We derive
\[
 C_n\sim A\,2^n n^{-3/4},\qquad
 D_n\sim e^{-\lambda}A\,2^n n^{-3/4},
\]
with one positive amplitude $A$ for both parities. The amplitude has an exact
harmonic-function characterization; no numerical value for it is established.
The limiting strong fraction satisfies
$0.7187529762<e^{-\lambda}<0.7187529792$, using Kolesnik's published bounds.
The new probabilistic step upgrades a survival weak limit to a fixed-velocity
endpoint equivalent by smoothing with a short final block. The proof treats
marginal regularity, the killing error, final slack and convolution tails.
It also gives a harmonic identity, a Lambert $W$ smooth inverse, sharp
vanishing-width ceiling brackets for integer thresholds, and an eventual
zero-or-one threshold gap, with both values occurring infinitely often.
No convergence rate or higher-order counting correction is proved.
\end{partabstract}

\section{Result and conventions}\label{sct:sec:result}
A tournament score sequence is the sorted list of vertex outdegrees.
Self-complementarity of a score sequence means
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:complement}
 s_i+s_{n+1-i}=n-1\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\end{equation}
We count distinct sorted sequences, not labeled tournaments or tournament
isomorphism classes. A sequence is \emph{strong} when its tournament
realizations are strongly connected. Landau's criterion and its strict
version characterize these two classes by nonnegative and positive proper
partial-sum excesses, respectively; see \cite[Propositions 1 and 7]{stockmeyer}.

Let $C_n$ and $D_n$ denote the all and strong self-complementary counts.
Set $C_0=D_0=1$ for the empty terms in generating functions. The small values
are $C_1=D_1=1$ and $C_2=1,D_2=0$; empty-term conventions do not assert that an
empty tournament is strongly connected.

\begin{theorem}\label{sct:thm:main}
There exists $A>0$ such that, through all positive integers $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:main}
 C_n\sim A\,2^n n^{-3/4},\qquad
 D_n\sim rA\,2^n n^{-3/4},\qquad r=e^{-\lambda},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 \lambda&=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{N_k}{k4^k},\label{sct:eq:lambda}\\
 N_k&=\frac1{2k}\sum_{d\mid k}(-1)^{k+d}\varphi(k/d)\binom{2d}{d}.
 \label{sct:eq:EGZ}
\end{align}
Here $\varphi$ is Euler's totient. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:ratio-bounds}
 0.7187529762<r<0.7187529792.
\end{equation}
For the variance-one geometric-minus-one walk defined in
Section~\ref{sct:sec:DW}, with its Denisov--Wachtel normalization,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:amplitude}
 A=2^{1/4}\kappa\Vdw(1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2)\fY(0)>0.
\end{equation}
Thus the same leading constants hold on even and odd lengths.
\end{theorem}

 =\frac{\sqrt6\,\Gamma(3/4)}{\pi^{3/2}}\Vdw(1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2)

\]

The two equivalents, their common amplitude and $r=e^{-\lambda}$ do not depend on the value of $\fY(0)$ (Remark~\ref{sct:rem:scope}).

\subsection{Proof outline and scope}
An exact geometric-gap encoding gives $2^{-n}C_n$ as a survival probability
with a final velocity constraint. Denisov--Wachtel Theorem 1 supplies survival
of order $n^{-1/4}$ and a conditional weak limit. A free last block supplies
one-dimensional endpoint mass of order $n^{-1/2}$. The substantive step is
to obtain its \emph{constant} while controlling the paths killed in that block.
Bounded continuity of the limiting velocity marginal and an endpoint-weighted
maximal estimate make this possible. A summable slack mixture then proves
the first equivalent. Stockmeyer's exact convolution and the ordinary-score
tail bound give the second. The proof uses no killed local-limit formula,
and specifically does not use equation (12) of \cite{dw}.

\section{Exact half-score and slack encoding}\label{sct:sec:encoding}
Write $n=2m$ or $n=2m+1$ and
\[
 M=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
 =\begin{cases}m-1,&n=2m,\\m,&n=2m+1.\end{cases}
\]
For $m\ge1$, every self-complementary candidate is uniquely determined by
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:half}
 0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_m\le M.
\end{equation}
Reflect by \eqref{sct:eq:complement}, inserting $s_{m+1}=m$ in the odd case.
The result is nondecreasing and its total is $\binom n2$.
For the full sequence put
\[
 A_j=\sum_{i=1}^j s_i-\binom j2\qquad(0\le j\le n).
\]
The last $j$ scores sum to $j(n-1)-\sum_{i\le j}s_i$, and therefore
\[
 A_{n-j}=\binom n2-j(n-1)+\sum_{i\le j}s_i-\binom{n-j}2=A_j.
\]
Consequently all Landau inequalities reduce exactly to
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:Landau}
 A_j\ge0\quad(1\le j\le m);
 \qquad\text{strongness replaces $\ge0$ by $>0$.}
\end{equation}
This includes the middle inequality, and both middle inequalities for odd $n$.

Define the gaps and the \emph{last slack}
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:gaps}
 G_1=s_1,\quad G_j=s_j-s_{j-1}\ (2\le j\le m),\quad
 G_{m+1}=M-s_m.
\end{equation}
This is a bijection between \eqref{sct:eq:half} and nonnegative compositions
$\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}G_j=M$. Take the $G_j$ independent with
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:geom}
 \Pp(G_j=g)=2^{-g-1},\qquad g\in\Z_{\ge0},
 \qquad X_j=G_j-1.
\end{equation}
Every such composition has probability $2^{-M-m-1}=2^{-n}$.
With initial velocity $V_0=1$ and area $A_0=0$, set
\[
 V_j=1+\sum_{i=1}^jX_i,\qquad A_j=\sum_{i=1}^jV_i.
\]
For $j\le m$, $V_j=s_j-j+1$, so $A_j$ is precisely the Landau excess.
The composition condition is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:rn}
 V_{m+1}=r_n,\qquad
 r_n=\begin{cases}-1,&n=2m,\\0,&n=2m+1.\end{cases}
\end{equation}

For a general state $z=(a,v)$ we henceforth use
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:process}
 V_j=v+\sum_{i=1}^jX_i,\qquad
 I_j=a+\sum_{i=1}^jV_i,\qquad
 \tau=\min\{j\ge1:I_j\le0\}.
\end{equation}
Because $A_j$ is integer, $A_j\ge0$ is equivalent to $I_j=A_j+1>0$.
Thus weak inequalities correspond to the start $(I_0,V_0)=(1,1)$.
Conditioning on the independent last slack $G_{m+1}=h$ gives the exact formula
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:C-exact}
 2^{-n}C_n=\sum_{h\ge0}2^{-h-1}
 \Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>m,\ V_m=r_n+1-h).
\end{equation}
The area at time $m+1$ is deliberately untested. For $n=2$, the valid weak
half sequence $(0)$ would fail a new test on its final unshifted area; for
$n=4$, the strong half sequence $(1,1)$ has final unshifted area zero and
would fail a strict extra test. The slack is not another score.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{sct:gr:part}, Proposition~\ref{sct:gr:prop:exact}, proves the same encoding, with the same two slack counterexamples; \eqref{sct:eq:C-exact} refines it by conditioning on the last slack. Part~\ref{sct:gr:part} writes the excess $A_r$ and the endpoint as $V_{m+1}$; here the endpoint is moved to time~$m$ by the slack mixture.
\end{writenote}

\section{The imported survival and weak-limit theorem}\label{sct:sec:DW}
For \eqref{sct:eq:geom},
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:moments}
 \Ee X=0,\qquad \Ee X^2=\sigma^2=2,\qquad
 \Ee e^{\theta|X|}<\infty\quad(0<\theta<\log2).
\end{equation}
Its support $\{-1,0,1,\ldots\}$ has maximal lattice span one.
Apply Denisov--Wachtel \cite[Theorem 1, (7)--(8), pp.~169--170]{dw} to
$X/\sigma$. Its assumptions are mean zero, variance one and a finite
$(2+\delta)$-moment for some $\delta>0$, all verified by \eqref{sct:eq:moments}.
The theorem's positive harmonic function $\Vdw$ is normalized as in that paper;
its universal survival constant is
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:kappa}
 \kappa=\frac{3\Gamma(1/4)}{2^{3/4}\pi^{3/2}}.
\end{equation}
For a fixed point in
\[
 K_+=\{z\in(0,\infty)\times\R:
 \Pp_z(Z_j\in(0,\infty)^2,\tau>j)>0\text{ for some }j\ge0\},
\]
the theorem provides the survival equivalent and the conditional endpoint
weak limit. In this definition $Z_j$ denotes the area--velocity pair of the
variance-one process. Every positive-coordinate start belongs to $K_+$ at
$j=0$.

Divide \emph{both} coordinates of \eqref{sct:eq:process} by $\sigma$. For any
fixed positive-coordinate integer state $z=(a,v)$ this gives
\begin{align}
 \Pp_z(\tau>n)&\sim c_z n^{-1/4},\qquad
 c_z=\kappa\Vdw(a/\sigma,v/\sigma)>0,\label{sct:eq:survival}\\
 \mathcal L\left(\frac{I_n}{\sigma n^{3/2}},
       \frac{V_n}{\sigma\sqrt n}\ \middle|\ \tau>n\right)
 &\Longrightarrow\mu.\label{sct:eq:weak-limit}
\end{align}
The measure $\mu$ is universal for these standardized walks. Theorem 1 has no
lattice condition; the span-one calculations below remain in the original
integer coordinates. In particular, the endpoint mass will contribute
$1/\sigma$, not a two-dimensional lattice-cell factor.

For precision, define $\Vdw$ using the paper's normalization
$\Vdw(z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Ee_z[h(Z_n);\tau>n]$, where $h$ is its Brownian
harmonic function \cite[equation (4)]{dw}. This fixes the scale of
\eqref{sct:eq:amplitude}; it is not an arbitrary positive harmonic function.
Equivalently, the product $\kappa\Vdw(z)$ is fixed by \eqref{sct:eq:survival}.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
The density of $\mu$ is displayed in \cite[Theorem~1]{dw} with a misprint, and Section~\ref{sct:sec:marginal} copies that display. The paper's own Remark~2 identifies the density as $\kappa^{-1}\bar h_{\mathrm{GJW}}(1,x,-y)$ with $\bar h_{\mathrm{GJW}}$ from \cite[(2.24)]{gjw}; this is the corrected density $\hstar$ of Section~\ref{sct:sub:density}. The survival equivalent \eqref{sct:eq:survival}, the weak limit \eqref{sct:eq:weak-limit} and the normalization of $\Vdw$ are not affected.
\end{writenote}

\section{Regularity of the limiting velocity density}\label{sct:sec:marginal}
We must localize at velocity zero. Abstract weak convergence alone does not
supply this step. We verify the required regularity from the density displayed
in \cite[Theorem 1]{dw}. Write
\begin{align}
 \bar h(x,y)&=c_{\mathrm{DW}}\int_0^1\int_0^\infty
    w^{3/2}s^{-1/2}e^{-2w^2/s}
    q_{1-s}(x,-y;0,-w)\,dw\,ds,\label{sct:eq:density}\\
 c_{\mathrm{DW}}&=\frac{2^{9/4}\sqrt\pi}{\sqrt3\Gamma(1/4)},\qquad
 q_t(x,y;u,v)=p_t(x,y;u,v)-p_t(x,y;u,-v),\nonumber
\end{align}
where the free Gaussian density is
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:gaussian}
 p_t(x,y;u,v)=\frac{\sqrt3}{\pi t^2}
 \exp\left(-\frac{6(u-x-ty)^2}{t^3}
 +\frac{6(u-x-ty)(v-y)}{t^2}-\frac{2(v-y)^2}{t}\right).
\end{equation}
For $a\ge0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:marginal}
 F_a(y)=\int_{x>a}\bar h(x,y)\,dx,\qquad \fY(y)=F_0(y).
\end{equation}

\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026, correction]
The display \eqref{sct:eq:density} copies \cite[p.~170]{dw} exactly, and that display is misprinted: it integrates to $0.1154552960\ldots$, so it is not the density of $\mu$ (Remark~\ref{sct:rem:misprint}). The density of $\mu$ is $\hstar$ of Proposition~\ref{sct:prop:corrected}: $s^{-1/2}$ must read $s^{-5/2}$, and $c_{\mathrm{DW}}$ must read $\Kstar=2^{9/4}/\Gamma(1/4)$. The proof of Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:marginal} below is correct for the display as printed. For $\hstar$ the lemma remains true, but its majorant must be replaced; see Remark~\ref{sct:rem:lemmafix}.
\end{writenote}
\begin{lemma}\label{sct:lem:marginal}
The functions $F_a(y)$ are jointly continuous in $(a,y)\in[0,\infty)\times\R$
and bounded by a single finite constant. Moreover $0<\fY(0)<\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Completing the square in the starting area $x$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:gaussian-integral}
 \int_\R p_t(x,-y;0,\pm w)\,dx
   =(2\pi t)^{-1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{(y\pm w)^2}{2t}\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
 \int_{x>a}|q_t(x,-y;0,-w)|\,dx\le2(2\pi t)^{-1/2}.
\]
Multiplying by the weight in \eqref{sct:eq:density} and integrating over $w$
yields a constant times
\[
 s^{-1/2}(1-s)^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty w^{3/2}e^{-2w^2/s}\,dw
 =C s^{3/4}(1-s)^{-1/2}.
\]
This is integrable over $0<s<1$. Thus Fubini's theorem applies absolutely to
the integrals defining $F_a$, and they share a bound independent of $a,y$.
For fixed $s,w$, the integral over $x>a$ of either Gaussian in
\eqref{sct:eq:gaussian-integral} is a continuous Gaussian-tail function of $a,y$.
The same integrable majorant and dominated convergence prove joint continuity,
including at $a=0$.

Finally, for $x,w,t>0$,
\[
 \frac{p_t(x,0;0,-w)}{p_t(x,0;0,w)}
 =\exp(12wx/t^2)>1.
\]
All outer weights in \eqref{sct:eq:density} are positive, so
$\bar h(x,0)>0$ for $x>0$. Hence $F_a(0)>0$ for every finite $a$, and the
claimed assertion at zero follows.
\end{proof}
This argument selects continuous representatives of the marginal densities;
it does not rely on their almost-everywhere values at one chosen point.


\subsection{An explicit universal factor}
The factor $\fY(0)$ admits a convergent special-function expression independent
of the discrete harmonic function:
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:fY-explicit}
 \fY(0)=\frac{5\Gamma(7/4)^2}{96\sqrt{2\pi}}
 {}_3F_2\left(\begin{matrix}1,7/4,9/4\\3/2,4\end{matrix};\frac34\right).
\end{equation}
Here ${}_pF_q$ has its usual series definition
$\sum_{j\ge0}(a_1)_j\cdots(a_p)_jz^j/
((b_1)_j\cdots(b_q)_j j!)$, where $(a)_j$ is the rising factorial.
The series in \eqref{sct:eq:fY-explicit} is positive and convergent.

To derive the formula, integration of the two completed Gaussian squares
over $x>0$ gives, with $u=1-s$,
\[
 \int_0^\infty q_u(x,0;0,-w)\,dx
 =\frac{e^{-w^2/(2u)}}{\sqrt{2\pi u}}
       \operatorname{erf}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac3{2u}}\,w\right).
\]
For $P,Q>0$, the resulting $w$ integral is
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:erf-integral}
 \int_0^\infty w^{3/2}e^{-Pw^2}\operatorname{erf}(Qw)\,dw
 =\frac{Q\Gamma(7/4)}{\sqrt\pi P^{7/4}}
 {}_2F_1\left(\frac12,\frac74;\frac32;-\frac{Q^2}{P}\right).
\end{equation}
For example, insert
$\operatorname{erf}(Qw)=2Qw\pi^{-1/2}\int_0^1e^{-Q^2w^2t^2}\,dt$
and first evaluate the nonnegative gamma integral in $w$.
Substitute $P=(4-3s)/(2s(1-s))$, $Q=\sqrt{3/(2(1-s))}$.
Pfaff's transformation changes the hypergeometric factor to
\[
 \left(\frac{4-3s}{4}\right)^{7/4}
 {}_2F_1\left(1,\frac74;\frac32;\frac{3s}{4}\right).
\]
After cancellation of powers, \eqref{sct:eq:density} therefore gives
\[
 \fY(0)=\frac{\Gamma(7/4)}{\sqrt{2\pi}\Gamma(1/4)}
 \int_0^1s^{5/4}(1-s)^{3/4}
 {}_2F_1\left(1,\frac74;\frac32;\frac{3s}{4}\right)\,ds.
\]
Termwise integration is justified by positivity and convergence at $3/4$.
The beta integral contributes $B(9/4,7/4)$ and the additional hypergeometric
parameters $9/4$ and $4$. Finally
$\Gamma(9/4)=5\Gamma(1/4)/16$ and $\Gamma(4)=6$ give
\eqref{sct:eq:fY-explicit}. This exact evaluation of the universal factor does
not evaluate $A$, which still contains $\Vdw(1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2)$.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026, correction]
This evaluation is correct for the printed display: its value is $0.0377237238918081803966\ldots$, and a quadrature of \eqref{sct:eq:density} at the placement agreed to 30 digits. It is not the velocity marginal of $\mu$ at~$0$. The same steps with $s^{-5/2}$ in place of $s^{-1/2}$ give $\fstar(0)=\sqrt{2/3}\,\Gamma(3/4)^2/\pi=0.3902762188691763\ldots$, which is $10.3456\ldots$ times larger (Proposition~\ref{sct:prop:corrected}(b)). The ratio is not the factor $8.6614$ by which the mass is off, because the misprint changes the shape as well as the constant.
\end{writenote}
\subsection{The limit density: a misprint and its correction}\label{sct:sub:density}
\wtag\ (5 October 2026.) This subsection was added in the write. It records a claim, shared by both Parts, that is demonstrably wrong (under Vladimir's standing rule of 4~October 2026), identifies its source and corrects it. Throughout, $u=1-s$, and for $v\in\R$
\[
 G_a(v)=\int_{x>a}p_u(x,-y;0,v)\,dx,\qquad
 \int_{x>a}q_u(x,-y;0,-w)\,dx=G_a(-w)-G_a(w).
\]

\begin{remark}[\wtag, wrong claim]\label{sct:rem:misprint}
The display \eqref{sct:eq:density}, copied from \cite[Theorem~1, p.~170]{dw} by this Part and, with an unspecified constant $c_0>0$, by Part~\ref{sct:gr:part} as \eqref{sct:gr:eq:density}, is not a probability density. Its total mass is
\[
 \int_0^\infty\!\!\int_\R\bar h(x,y)\,dy\,dx=0.11545529600527263025801512135\ldots .
\]
Consequently the value $0.0377237\ldots$ of \eqref{sct:eq:fY-explicit} is not the density at~$0$ of the velocity marginal of $\mu$, and \eqref{sct:eq:amplitude} evaluated with it does not give~$A$. The statements are kept as printed.

\emph{Proof of the counterexample.} Write $d=uy-x$ and $m=u(v+y)/2$. Completing the square in $d$ in \eqref{sct:eq:gaussian} gives
\[
 p_u(x,-y;0,v)=\frac{\sqrt3}{\pi u^2}
 \exp\Bigl(-\frac{(v+y)^2}{2u}-\frac{6(d-m)^2}{u^3}\Bigr),
\]
and integrating over $x>a$, that is over $d<uy-a$,
\[
 G_a(v)=\frac{e^{-(v+y)^2/(2u)}}{2\sqrt{2\pi u}}
 \Bigl(1+\erf\Bigl(\sqrt6\,\frac{u(y-v)/2-a}{u^{3/2}}\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
For $a=0$ the factor $e^{-(v+y)^2/(2u)}/\sqrt{2\pi u}$ is the density in $y$ of $N(-v,u)$, under which $y-v\sim N(-2v,u)$. The Gaussian identity $\Ee\erf(cZ)=\erf\bigl(c\,\Ee Z/\sqrt{1+2c^2\operatorname{Var}Z}\bigr)$ with $c=\sqrt{3/(2u)}$, so that $1+2c^2u=4$, gives $\int_\R G_0(v)\,dy=\frac12\bigl(1-\erf(v\sqrt{3/(2u)})\bigr)$, hence
\[
 \int_\R\int_{x>0}q_u(x,-y;0,-w)\,dx\,dy=\erf\bigl(w\sqrt{3/(2u)}\bigr).
\]
The integrand of \eqref{sct:eq:density} is absolutely integrable over $(s,w,x,y)$ by the majorant in the proof of Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:marginal} (with the bound $\int\int|q_u|\,dx\,dy\le2$). By Fubini the mass is therefore
\[
 c_{\mathrm{DW}}\int_0^1s^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty w^{3/2}e^{-2w^2/s}
 \erf\Bigl(w\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{2(1-s)}}\Bigr)\,dw\,ds,
\]
whose inner integral is \eqref{sct:eq:erf-integral} with $P=2/s$ and $Q^2=3/(2(1-s))$. Quadrature gives the value above (40 digits with mpmath at the placement; 12 digits by an independent scipy quadrature in the write). It would have to be~$1$.

\emph{Counts against the prediction.} By Section~\ref{sct:sec:C}, $A=2^{3/4}c_{(1,1)}\fY(0)/\sigma=2^{1/4}c_{(1,1)}\fY(0)$ with $c_{(1,1)}=\lim n^{1/4}\Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>n)$, which neither Part evaluates. A Monte Carlo of the geometric-minus-one walk at the placement (480{,}000 paths each) gave $n^{1/4}\Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>n)=0.8355$ at $n=500$ and $0.8426$ at $n=2000$. With \eqref{sct:eq:fY-explicit} the formula then predicts $A\approx0.038$. The exact counts (floating-point dynamic programming at the placement) give $C_nn^{3/4}2^{-n}=0.3606$ and $0.4249$ at $n=40,41$, and $0.3762$ and $0.3989$ at $n=320,321$; the even values increase, the odd values decrease, with a gap of order $n^{-1/2}$, and the parity average extrapolates to $A\approx0.387$. Both extrapolations are uncertified, but the factor of ten is far outside their uncertainty.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[\wtag]\label{sct:prop:corrected}
Let $\Kstar=2^{9/4}/\Gamma(1/4)$ and, for $x>0$ and $y\in\R$,
\[
 \hstar(x,y)=\Kstar\int_0^1\int_0^\infty
 w^{3/2}s^{-5/2}e^{-2w^2/s}\,q_{1-s}(x,-y;0,-w)\,dw\,ds .
\]
Then $\hstar(x,y)=\kappa^{-1}\bar h_{\mathrm{GJW}}(1,x,-y)$, where $\bar h_{\mathrm{GJW}}$ is the function \cite[(2.24)]{gjw} named in \cite[Remark~2]{dw}. The integral converges absolutely for almost every $(x,y)$, and:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\int_0^\infty\int_\R\hstar(x,y)\,dy\,dx=1$;
\item[(b)] the velocity marginal $\fstar(y)=\int_{x>0}\hstar(x,y)\,dx$ has the value
 \[\fstar(0)=\sqrt{2/3}\,\Gamma(3/4)^2/\pi=0.3902762188691763\ldots;\]
\item[(c)] the integrand of $\hstar(x,y)$ is positive for $x>0$ and $y\le3x$; in particular $\int_{x>a}\hstar(x,0)\,dx>0$ for every $a\ge0$.
\end{enumerate}
The display \eqref{sct:eq:density} differs from $\hstar$ in the power of $s$ ($s^{-1/2}$ for $s^{-5/2}$: the factor $s^{-2}$ of $p_s(0,w;0,0)$ is lost) and in the constant ($c_{\mathrm{DW}}=\sqrt{\pi/3}\,\Kstar$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Identification.} By \eqref{sct:eq:gaussian}, $p_s(0,w;0,0)=\sqrt3\,e^{-2w^2/s}/(\pi s^2)$. Groeneboom, Jongbloed and Wellner define
\[
 \bar h_{\mathrm{GJW}}(t,x,y)=\frac{4\sqrt3}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_0^t\int_0^\infty
 w^{3/2}s^{-1/2}p_s(0,w;0,0)\,q_{t-s}(x,y;0,-w)\,dw\,ds
\]
\cite[(2.24)]{gjw} (the same formula is restated in \cite[Section~6]{bdw}). At $t=1$, with $y$ replaced by $-y$, this is $\frac{12}{\pi\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_0^1\int_0^\infty w^{3/2}s^{-5/2}e^{-2w^2/s}q_{1-s}(x,-y;0,-w)\,dw\,ds$, and $\kappa^{-1}\cdot12/(\pi\sqrt{2\pi})=2^{9/4}/\Gamma(1/4)$ by \eqref{sct:eq:kappa}.

\emph{Two bounds on $q$.} The formula for $G_a$ in Remark~\ref{sct:rem:misprint} gives $0\le G_a(v)\le(2\pi u)^{-1/2}$, and $\int_\R\int_\R p_u\,dx\,dy=1$. Moreover $\partial_v\log p_u(x,-y;0,v)=6(d-m)/u^2-(v+y)/u$, and $\int_\R e^{-6z^2/u^3}dz=(\pi u^3/6)^{1/2}$, $\int_\R|z|e^{-6z^2/u^3}dz=u^3/6$, $\sup_z|z|e^{-z^2/(2u)}=(u/e)^{1/2}$. Hence
\[
 \int_\R|\partial_vp_u(x,-y;0,v)|\,dx\le\frac{3}{u},\qquad
 \int_\R\int_\R|\partial_vp_u(x,-y;0,v)|\,dx\,dy\le\frac{3}{\sqrt u}.
\]
Since $q_u(x,-y;0,-w)=-\int_{-w}^{w}\partial_vp_u(x,-y;0,v)\,dv$, for all $a\ge0$, $y\in\R$, $w>0$ and $u\in(0,1)$,
\[
 \int_{x>a}|q_u(x,-y;0,-w)|\,dx\le\min\Bigl\{\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi u}},\frac{6w}{u}\Bigr\},\qquad
 \int_\R\int_{x>0}|q_u(x,-y;0,-w)|\,dx\,dy\le\min\Bigl\{2,\frac{6w}{\sqrt u}\Bigr\}.
\]

\emph{An integrable majorant.} Put $W(s,w)=w^{3/2}s^{-5/2}e^{-2w^2/s}$ and
\[
 M(s,w)=W(s,w)\Bigl(\frac{6w}{1-s}\ind_{\{s\le1/2\}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi(1-s)}}\ind_{\{s>1/2\}}\Bigr).
\]
For $s\le1/2$, $\int_0^\infty M\,dw\le12s^{-5/2}\int_0^\infty w^{5/2}e^{-2w^2/s}dw=12\cdot2^{-11/4}\Gamma(7/4)\,s^{-3/4}$. For $s>1/2$, $\int_0^\infty M\,dw\le2^{7/2}(2\pi(1-s))^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty w^{3/2}e^{-2w^2}dw$. Both are integrable, so $\int_0^1\int_0^\infty M<\infty$, and $M$ dominates $W(s,w)\int_{x>a}|q_{1-s}(x,-y;0,-w)|\,dx$ for every $a\ge0$ and $y$. In the same way $W(s,w)\min\{2,6w(1-s)^{-1/2}\}$, which dominates $W(s,w)\int_\R\int_{x>0}|q_{1-s}|\,dx\,dy$, is integrable. By Tonelli the integral defining $\hstar$ converges absolutely for almost every $(x,y)$, and every integral of $\hstar$ over $x>a$, or over $x>0$ and $y\in\R$, may be computed in any order.

\emph{(a)} By the identity in Remark~\ref{sct:rem:misprint}, the mass is $\Kstar\int_0^1s^{-5/2}J(s)\,ds$, where $Q=(3/(2(1-s)))^{1/2}$ and
\[
 J(s)=\int_0^\infty w^{3/2}e^{-2w^2/s}\erf(Qw)\,dw,\qquad
 \erf(Qw)=\frac{2Qw}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^1e^{-Q^2w^2t^2}dt.
\]
Inserting the second formula in the first and integrating in $w$ (all terms positive),
\[
 s^{-5/2}J(s)=\frac{\Gamma(7/4)\,Q}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^1\frac{s^{-5/2}\,dt}{(2/s+Q^2t^2)^{7/4}}
 =\frac{\Gamma(7/4)}{2^{7/4}\sqrt\pi}\sqrt{\frac32}\int_0^1
 \frac{s^{-3/4}(1-s)^{5/4}}{(1-s(1-3t^2/4))^{7/4}}\,dt.
\]
For fixed $t\in(0,1]$ put $z=1-3t^2/4\in[1/4,1)$. Euler's integral $\int_0^1s^{b-1}(1-s)^{c-b-1}(1-zs)^{-a}ds=B(b,c-b)\,F(a,b;c;z)$ with $(a,b,c)=(7/4,1/4,5/2)$, followed by Euler's transformation $F(a,b;c;z)=(1-z)^{c-a-b}F(c-a,c-b;c;z)$, gives
\[
 \int_0^1\frac{s^{-3/4}(1-s)^{5/4}}{(1-zs)^{7/4}}\,ds
 =B\bigl(\tfrac14,\tfrac94\bigr)\,\frac{\sqrt3\,t}{2}\,F\bigl(\tfrac34,\tfrac94;\tfrac52;z\bigr).
\]
Since $\frac{d}{dz}F(-\frac14,\frac54;\frac32;z)=-\frac5{24}F(\frac34,\frac94;\frac52;z)$ and $t\,dt=-\frac23dz$,
\[
 \int_0^1\frac{\sqrt3\,t}{2}F\bigl(\tfrac34,\tfrac94;\tfrac52;1-\tfrac34t^2\bigr)dt
 =\frac1{\sqrt3}\int_{1/4}^1F\bigl(\tfrac34,\tfrac94;\tfrac52;z\bigr)dz
 =\frac{24}{5\sqrt3}\Bigl[F\bigl(-\tfrac14,\tfrac54;\tfrac32;\tfrac14\bigr)-F\bigl(-\tfrac14,\tfrac54;\tfrac32;1\bigr)\Bigr];
\]
the integrand has an integrable singularity $(1-z)^{-1/2}$ at $z=1$, and $F(-\frac14,\frac54;\frac32;\cdot)$ is continuous on $[0,1]$ because $\frac32+\frac14-\frac54>0$. The quadratic case $F(\alpha,1-\alpha;\frac32;\sin^2\theta)=\sin((2\alpha-1)\theta)/((2\alpha-1)\sin\theta)$ \cite[\S15.4(i)]{dlmf} with $\alpha=-\frac14$ gives $2\sqrt2/3$ at $\theta=\pi/6$ and $\sqrt2/3$ at $\theta=\pi/2$ (Gauss's sum gives the same). So the $t$-integral is $8\sqrt2/(5\sqrt3)$. With $B(\frac14,\frac94)=\Gamma(\frac14)\Gamma(\frac94)/\Gamma(\frac52)=5\Gamma(1/4)^2/(12\sqrt\pi)$,
\[
 \int\!\!\int\hstar=\Kstar\,\frac{\Gamma(7/4)}{2^{7/4}\sqrt\pi}\sqrt{\frac32}\cdot\frac{5\Gamma(1/4)^2}{12\sqrt\pi}\cdot\frac{8\sqrt2}{5\sqrt3}
 =\Kstar\,\frac{2\,\Gamma(7/4)\Gamma(1/4)^2}{3\cdot2^{7/4}\pi}
 =\Kstar\,2^{-9/4}\Gamma(1/4)=1,
\]
using $\Gamma(7/4)=\frac34\Gamma(\frac34)$ and $\Gamma(\frac14)\Gamma(\frac34)=\pi\sqrt2$.

\emph{(b)} For $a=y=0$ the formula for $G_0$ gives the inner integral of Section~\ref{sct:sec:marginal},
\[
 G_0(-w)-G_0(w)=(2\pi u)^{-1/2}e^{-w^2/(2u)}\erf\bigl(w\sqrt{3/(2u)}\bigr).
\]
The steps of that section, with $s^{-5/2}$ in place of $s^{-1/2}$ (so with \eqref{sct:eq:erf-integral} for $P=(4-3s)/(2s(1-s))$ and Pfaff's transformation), give
\[
 \fstar(0)=\Kstar\,\frac{\sqrt3\,\Gamma(7/4)}{2^{11/4}\pi}\int_0^1s^{-3/4}(1-s)^{3/4}
 F\bigl(1,\tfrac74;\tfrac32;\tfrac{3s}4\bigr)\,ds
 =\Kstar\,\frac{\sqrt3\,\Gamma(7/4)}{2^{11/4}\pi}\,B\bigl(\tfrac14,\tfrac74\bigr)\,
 {}_3F_2\Bigl(\begin{matrix}\frac14,1,\frac74\\[1pt]\frac32,2\end{matrix};\frac34\Bigr),
\]
by termwise integration of positive terms. Because two parameters are $1$ and $2$,
${}_3F_2(a,1,b;c,2;z)=\frac{c-1}{(a-1)(b-1)z}\bigl(F(a-1,b-1;c-1;z)-1\bigr)$, and $F(-\frac34,\frac34;\frac12;\sin^2\theta)=\cos(\frac32\theta)$ \cite[\S15.4(i)]{dlmf} vanishes at $\sin^2\theta=\frac34$. Hence the ${}_3F_2$ equals $-\frac{32}{27}(0-1)=\frac{32}{27}$, and with $B(\frac14,\frac74)=\Gamma(\frac14)\Gamma(\frac74)$,
\[
 \fstar(0)=\frac{2^{9/4}}{\Gamma(1/4)}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3\,\Gamma(7/4)^2\Gamma(1/4)}{2^{11/4}\pi}\cdot\frac{32}{27}
 =\sqrt{\frac32}\cdot\frac{9}{16}\cdot\frac{32}{27}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(3/4)^2}{\pi}
 =\sqrt{\frac23}\,\frac{\Gamma(3/4)^2}{\pi}.
\]

\emph{(c)} For $0<t<1$ and $w>0$, $p_t(x,-y;0,-w)/p_t(x,-y;0,w)=\exp(4w(3x-yt)/t^2)$ (the ratio of Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:starts} and of \eqref{sct:gr:eq:ratio}), which exceeds~$1$ when $x>0$ and $y\le3x$; all weights are positive. For $y=0$ the integrand $W(s,w)\,(G_a(-w)-G_a(w))$ of $\int_{x>a}\hstar(x,0)\,dx$ is therefore positive.

\emph{Numerical checks} (write, 5 October 2026; uncertified floating point): an independent scipy quadrature of $\hstar$ gives mass $1.000000000000$ and $\fstar(0)=0.390276218869$, both to 12 digits; mpmath confirms the Euler-integral step, the two quadratic values and ${}_3F_2=32/27$ to 40 digits.
\end{proof}

\section{Free endpoint masses and a maximal estimate}\label{sct:sec:bridge}
Put $T_j=X_1+\cdots+X_j$. The ordinary span-one lattice local central limit
theorem gives, uniformly over integers $d$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:LCLT}
 \sqrt\ell\,\Pp(T_\ell=d)=
 \frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \exp\left(-\frac{d^2}{2\sigma^2\ell}\right)+o(1).
\end{equation}
In particular $\sup_d\Pp(T_\ell=d)\le C\ell^{-1/2}$ for $\ell\ge1$.
The hypotheses here are just the finite variance and span one already checked.
For this particular increment law the exact atom is also explicit:
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:NB}
 \Pp(T_\ell=d)=\binom{2\ell+d-1}{\ell-1}2^{-2\ell-d}\quad(d\ge-\ell),
\end{equation}
and it is zero otherwise. The ratio of consecutive positive masses is
$(2\ell+d)/(2(\ell+d+1))$, so $d=-1$ is a mode and the maximum is
$\frac12\binom{2\ell-2}{\ell-1}4^{-(\ell-1)}=O(\ell^{-1/2})$.
Thus the uniform atom bound can alternatively be checked directly by Stirling.

\begin{lemma}\label{sct:lem:max}
There is a constant $C$, depending only on the increment law, such that for
$\ell\ge4$, $H>0$, and an integer $d$ with $|d|\le H/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:max}
 \Pp\left(T_\ell=d,\ \max_{j\le\ell}|T_j|\ge H\right)
 \le C\frac{\sqrt\ell}{H^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split at $h=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor$. For a violation in the first half, condition
on the first $h$ increments and bound the remaining independent endpoint atom
by $C/\sqrt{\ell-h}$. Kolmogorov's inequality bounds the first-half maximum
probability by $\sigma^2h/H^2$, giving $C\sqrt\ell/H^2$.

For a violation at a time $j>h$, the equality $T_\ell=d$ implies
$|T_\ell-T_j|\ge H-|d|\ge H/2$. The reversed last $\ell-h$ increments
therefore have a partial sum of absolute value at least $H/2$. Condition on
that suffix, use the first-half endpoint-atom bound $C/\sqrt h$, and then
Kolmogorov's inequality for its reversed partial sums. This contributes
another $C\sqrt\ell/H^2$. Their sum proves the estimate.
\end{proof}
The estimate concerns joint endpoint mass. Dividing by an endpoint probability
would introduce an unnecessary, possibly nonuniform bridge constant.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{sct:gr:part}, Lemma~\ref{sct:gr:lem:bridge} (bounded bridge), proves the complementary lower bound by the same Kolmogorov split; Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:max} is the endpoint-weighted upper form. The atom formula \eqref{sct:eq:NB} and its mode are Part~\ref{sct:gr:part}'s \eqref{sct:gr:eq:nb} and Lemma~\ref{sct:gr:lem:atoms}, which adds a local lower bound.
\end{writenote}

\section{Smoothing to one fixed terminal velocity}\label{sct:sec:smoothing}
\begin{theorem}\label{sct:thm:endpoint}
For every fixed positive-coordinate integer state $z=(a,v)$ and every fixed
integer $b$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:endpoint}
 \Pp_z(\tau>n,V_n=b)\sim\frac{c_z\fY(0)}{\sigma}\,n^{-3/4}.
\end{equation}
Also, uniformly over integer $b$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:uniform-endpoint}
 \Pp_z(\tau>n,V_n=b)\le C_z(n+1)^{-3/4}\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \eqref{sct:eq:uniform-endpoint}, split at $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, retain survival
only in the first block, and use the free endpoint-atom bound in the second.
The survival equivalent \eqref{sct:eq:survival}, enlarged to a global upper bound,
then gives $C_z n^{-1/4}n^{-1/2}$. Enlarge $C_z$ for small times.

For the equivalent fix $0<\varepsilon<1/2$ and put
\[
 k=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)n\rfloor,\quad \ell=n-k,
 \quad \alpha=1-\varepsilon.
\]
All limits in $n$ below keep $\varepsilon$ fixed.

\textbf{Upper bound.}
Dropping the area tests after $k$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:upper-Markov}
 \Pp_z(\tau>n,V_n=b)
 \le\Ee_z[\ind_{\{\tau>k\}}\Pp(T_\ell=b-V_k)].
\end{equation}
The uniform error in \eqref{sct:eq:LCLT} is $o(\ell^{-1/2})$; after averaging it is
multiplied by $\Pp_z(\tau>k)=O(k^{-1/4})$. Under the scaling
$V_k=\sigma\sqrt k\,y$, the Gaussian test functions converge uniformly on
$\R$ to the Gaussian with exponent $-\alpha y^2/(2\varepsilon)$.
Thus \eqref{sct:eq:survival}--\eqref{sct:eq:weak-limit} show that $n^{3/4}$ times the
right side of \eqref{sct:eq:upper-Markov} converges to
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:Ueps}
 U_\varepsilon=\frac{c_z\alpha^{-1/4}}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi\varepsilon}}
 \int_\R e^{-\alpha y^2/(2\varepsilon)}\fY(y)\,dy.
\end{equation}
Writing $t=\varepsilon/\alpha$ and
$\phi_t(y)=(2\pi t)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/(2t)}$, this is
\[
 U_\varepsilon=\frac{c_z}{\sigma}\alpha^{-3/4}
 \int_\R\phi_t(y)\fY(y)\,dy\longrightarrow\frac{c_z\fY(0)}{\sigma}
 \quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0)
\]
by Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:marginal}. This proves the limsup bound.

\textbf{Lower bound before the killing error.}
Fix $\delta,K>0$ and at time $k$ retain only the states
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:retained}
 I_k>\delta n^{3/2},\qquad |V_k|<K\sqrt n.
\end{equation}
First ignore killing in the last block. The same calculation gives the limit
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:Leps}
 L_{\varepsilon,\delta,K}=
 \frac{c_z\alpha^{-1/4}}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi\varepsilon}}
 \int_{|y|<K/(\sigma\sqrt\alpha)}
 e^{-\alpha y^2/(2\varepsilon)}
 F_{\delta/(\sigma\alpha^{3/2})}(y)\,dy.
\end{equation}
Indeed, in conditional scaled coordinates the retained set has area threshold
$\delta/(\sigma(k/n)^{3/2})$ and velocity cutoff $K/(\sigma\sqrt{k/n})$.
The three limiting boundary lines are $\mu$-null because $\mu$ has a density.
Weak convergence therefore applies to the bounded Gaussian times this
indicator. Moving boundary strips caused by floors have vanishing mass by
Portmanteau. No uniform weak-limit rate in the parameters is being used.

\textbf{Endpoint mass of paths killed in the last block.}
For a retained state $(a,v)$, the area after $j$ more steps is
\[
 a+jv+\sum_{i=1}^jT_i.
\]
For large $n$, $\ell\le2\varepsilon n$. If
$\varepsilon\le\delta/(8(K+1))$, then a failure of positivity implies
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:H}
 \max_{i\le\ell}|T_i|\ge H,\qquad
 H=\frac{\delta}{4\varepsilon}\sqrt n.
\end{equation}
In fact $a-\ell|v|>\delta n^{3/2}-2\varepsilon Kn^{3/2}
\ge\delta n^{3/2}/2$, so a nonpositive area requires
$\max|T_i|\ge\delta n^{3/2}/(2\ell)\ge H$.
The endpoint condition is $T_\ell=b-v$ and eventually
$|b-v|\le(K+1)\sqrt n\le H/2$. Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:max} therefore gives,
uniformly over all retained states,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:kill-error}
 \Pp_{(a,v)}(\tau\le\ell,V_\ell=b)
 \le C\varepsilon^{5/2}\delta^{-2}n^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
The constant here does not grow as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$; the threshold
from which $n$ is sufficiently large may depend on $b,\delta,K,\varepsilon$.
Multiplying by $\Pp_z(\tau>k)$ shows that the error in $n^{3/4}$-scaled mass
is at most $C_z\alpha^{-1/4}\varepsilon^{5/2}\delta^{-2}$ in the limit.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:liminf}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{3/4}\Pp_z(\tau>n,V_n=b)
 \ge L_{\varepsilon,\delta,K}
      -C_z\alpha^{-1/4}\varepsilon^{5/2}\delta^{-2}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Taking the limits in order.}
In \eqref{sct:eq:Leps} substitute $y=\sqrt{\varepsilon/\alpha}\,u$ to obtain
\[
 L_{\varepsilon,\delta,K}=\frac{c_z}{\sigma}\alpha^{-3/4}
 \int_{|u|<K/(\sigma\sqrt\varepsilon)}
 \frac{e^{-u^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 F_{\delta/(\sigma\alpha^{3/2})}
       (\sqrt{\varepsilon/\alpha}\,u)\,du.
\]
For fixed $\delta,K>0$, joint continuity and the common bound of
Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:marginal} give

\end{proof}

\section{The strong equivalent from an exact convolution}\label{sct:sec:D}
Let $S_k$ and $T_k$ count ordinary all and strong tournament score sequences,
with $S_0=1,T_0=0$. Write their ordinary generating functions as $S(z),T(z)$,
and use $C(z),D(z)$ for the self-complementary counts. Stockmeyer's
Theorems 11 and 13 \cite{stockmeyer} give
\begin{align}
 S(z)&=\frac1{1-T(z)},\label{sct:eq:ordinary-gf}\\
 D_n&=C_n-\sum_{1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor}T_k C_{n-2k}\quad(n\ge1),
 \label{sct:eq:Stock-conv}\\
 D(z)&=C(z)(1-T(z^2)),\qquad C(z)=D(z)S(z^2).
 \label{sct:eq:SC-gf}
\end{align}
These are formal power-series identities, so no analytic assumption is needed
at this stage. The established ordinary-score asymptotic in \cite{kolesnik}
in particular yields
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:ordinary-tail}
 0\le T_k/4^k\le S_k/4^k\le C(k+1)^{-5/2}.
\end{equation}
This ordinary-score input is not a new result of the present report.

\subsection{Justifying the convolution limit}
Put $c_n=C_n/2^n$ and $t_k=T_k/4^k$. We have
$c_n\sim A n^{-3/4}$ and hence $c_n\le B(n+1)^{-3/4}$ for all $n\ge0$.
For fixed $K$,
\[
 n^{3/4}\sum_{k=1}^K t_k c_{n-2k}
 \longrightarrow A\sum_{k=1}^K t_k.
\]
For $K<k\le n/4$, the bound on $c_{n-2k}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:conv-tail-one}
 n^{3/4}\sum_{K<k\le n/4}t_k c_{n-2k}
 \le C\sum_{k>K}t_k\longrightarrow0\quad(K\to\infty),
\end{equation}
uniformly in large $n$. The remaining terms satisfy
\begin{align}
 n^{3/4}\sum_{n/4<k\le n/2}t_k c_{n-2k}
 &\le C n^{3/4}n^{-5/2}
       \sum_{0\le j\le n/2}(j+1)^{-3/4}\nonumber\\
 &=O(n^{-3/2}).\label{sct:eq:conv-tail-two}
\end{align}
Only one parity of $j=n-2k$ occurs, and extending the sum only enlarges it.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:conv-limit}
 n^{3/4}\sum_{1\le k\le n/2}t_k c_{n-2k}
 \longrightarrow A\sum_{k\ge1}t_k.
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{sct:eq:Stock-conv},
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:D-amplitude}
 D_n\sim A(1-T(1/4))\,2^n n^{-3/4}
       =\frac{A}{S(1/4)}\,2^n n^{-3/4}.
\end{equation}
The ordinary tail bound makes $S(1/4)$ finite. Taking increasing real limits
in \eqref{sct:eq:ordinary-gf} gives $1-T(1/4)=1/S(1/4)>0$.

\section{A direct strong amplitude and a harmonic identity}\label{sct:sec:harmonic}
This section gives an independent expression for the strong amplitude.
It is optional for Theorem~\ref{sct:thm:main}, whose proof is already complete.
It also checks that a mixture over unbounded starting states can be justified
without assuming a uniform version of \eqref{sct:eq:survival}.

\begin{lemma}\label{sct:lem:starts}
There is a constant $C$ such that, for all integers $g,m\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 \Pp_{(g,g)}(\tau>m)&\le C\sqrt g\,m^{-1/4},\label{sct:eq:start-surv}\\
 \sup_{b\in\Z}\Pp_{(g,g)}(\tau>m,V_m=b)
   &\le C\sqrt g\,m^{-3/4}.\label{sct:eq:start-atom}
\end{align}
In particular $c_{(g,g)}\le C\sqrt g$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From $(1,1)$, use time $N=g^2$ and the normalized rectangle
\[
 2<I_N/(\sigma N^{3/2})<3,\qquad
 1<V_N/(\sigma\sqrt N)<2.
\]
It has positive $\mu$-mass. Indeed,
\[
 \frac{p_t(x,-y;0,-w)}{p_t(x,-y;0,w)}
   =\exp\left(\frac{4w(3x-yt)}{t^2}\right)>1
\]
for $0<t<1$ on this rectangle, so \eqref{sct:eq:density} is positive there.
By \eqref{sct:eq:survival}--\eqref{sct:eq:weak-limit}, the probability of reaching it
while surviving is at least $c g^{-1/2}$ for all sufficiently large $g$.
Every state there dominates $(g,g)$ coordinatewise. For the finitely many
smaller $g$, force $G_1=g$ and $G_j=1$ for $2\le j\le g^2$.
Starting from $(1,1)$ the velocity then equals $g$ from time one onward and
the area stays positive; its time-$g^2$ state dominates $(g,g)$.
Each forced path has positive probability, so decreasing $c$ covers all $g$.

The common-increment coupling is monotone in both starting coordinates.
The Markov property therefore gives
\[
 \Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>g^2+m)
 \ge c g^{-1/2}\Pp_{(g,g)}(\tau>m).
\]
The global upper bound following from \eqref{sct:eq:survival} proves
\eqref{sct:eq:start-surv}. Split an endpoint event at halfway and use the free
atom bound to obtain \eqref{sct:eq:start-atom}, enlarging $C$ for $m=1$.
Taking $m\to\infty$ in the first bound gives the assertion about $c_{(g,g)}$.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
The positivity on the rectangle uses only the ratio displayed in the proof and positive weights, so it holds for the true density $\hstar$ (Proposition~\ref{sct:prop:corrected}(c)); the rectangle lies in $y\le3x$.
\end{writenote}

For strict Landau inequalities the first geometric value must be $g\ge1$.
At time one its unshifted state is $(A_1,V_1)=(g,g)$.
Decomposing this first value and the final slack yields, for $m\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:D-direct}
 2^{-n}D_n=\sum_{g\ge1}\sum_{h\ge0}2^{-g-1}2^{-h-1}
 \Pp_{(g,g)}(\tau>m-1,V_{m-1}=r_n+1-h).
\end{equation}
Here the probabilities use the state after that first step as their new start.
Theorem~\ref{sct:thm:endpoint} applies for each fixed $g,h$;
Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:starts} and $\sum_{g\ge1}2^{-g-1}\sqrt g<\infty$ justify
summation. Hence the direct strong amplitude is
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:B-direct}
 B=\frac{2^{3/4}\fY(0)}{\sigma}
     \sum_{g\ge1}2^{-g-1}c_{(g,g)}.
\end{equation}
Comparing with $B=rA$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:harmonic-identity}
 \sum_{g\ge1}2^{-g-1}\Vdw(g/\sqrt2,g/\sqrt2)
   =e^{-\lambda}\Vdw(1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2).
\end{equation}
The first-step weights are unconditional; their sum over $g\ge1$ is $1/2$.
Renormalizing them would incorrectly double the amplitude. The identity is a
consequence of two independently justified amplitude representations; whether
it has a shorter direct harmonic or combinatorial proof is a further question.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]

\end{writenote}

\section{Smooth inverses and integer thresholds}\label{sct:sec:inverse}
All logarithms in this section are natural. Put $a=3/4$, $\gamma=\log2$,
and $A_C=A$, $A_D=rA$. For $E=C$ or $D$, define
\[
 F_E(t)=A_E e^{\gamma t}t^{-a}\quad(t>0),\qquad
 N_E(X)=\min\{n\ge1:E_n\ge X\}.
\]
The counts tend to infinity, so the minimum exists. Their equivalents imply
$E_{n+1}/E_n\to2$, hence eventual strict increase; a finite initial exception
such as $D_1>D_2$ has no effect for large $X$.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{sct:gr:part}, Proposition~\ref{sct:gr:prop:mono}, proves more for all $n$: $C_n$ is nondecreasing for $n\ge0$ and $D_n$ for $n\ge2$ (not strictly: $C_1=C_2$ and $C_3=C_4$).
\end{writenote}

\begin{proposition}\label{sct:prop:inverse}
For all sufficiently large $X$, the large real solution of $F_E(t)=X$ is
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:Lambert}
 q_E(X)=-\frac a\gamma W_{-1}\left(
       -\frac\gamma a\left(\frac{A_E}{X}\right)^{1/a}\right),
\end{equation}
where $W_{-1}$ is the real branch with values at most $-1$. It satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:inverse-expand}
 q_E(X)=\frac{\log X}{\gamma}
 +\frac a\gamma\log\log X
 -\frac a\gamma\log\gamma
 -\frac{\log A_E}{\gamma}+o(1).
\end{equation}
Moreover $q_E(E_n)=n+o(1)$. There exists $\eta_E(X)\ge0$ with $\eta_E(X)\to0$ such that, for all
sufficiently large $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:ceil-brackets}
 \left\lceil q_E(X)-\eta_E(X)\right\rceil
 \le N_E(X)\le
 \left\lceil q_E(X)+\eta_E(X)\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
In particular $N_E(X)=q_E(X)+O(1)$, but a global claim
$N_E(X)=q_E(X)+o(1)$ is not justified and is false.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The function $F_E$ is strictly increasing for $t>a/\gamma$.
Rearranging its equation gives
$t e^{-\gamma t/a}=(A_E/X)^{1/a}$, from which \eqref{sct:eq:Lambert} follows.
The branch $W_{-1}$ gives the large solution; $W_0$ would give a solution
approaching zero.

Let $L=\log X$ and set
\[
 u=\frac1\gamma(L+a\log L-a\log\gamma-\log A_E).
\]
Then $u\sim L/\gamma$ and
$\log F_E(u)=\log A_E+\gamma u-a\log u=L+o(1)$.
Since $(\log F_E)'(t)=\gamma-a/t$ is bounded away from zero at large $t$,
the mean value theorem gives $q_E(X)-u=o(1)$, proving
\eqref{sct:eq:inverse-expand}. Similarly
$\log E_n-\log F_E(n)=o(1)$ proves $q_E(E_n)=n+o(1)$.

For the integer statement write $e_n=q_E(E_n)-n\to0$ and, for large $X$,
let $m=N_E(X)$. Eventual strict increase and removal of the finite initial
range give $E_{m-1}<X\le E_m$, hence
\[
 m-1+e_{m-1}<q_E(X)\le m+e_m.
\]
Take, for example, $\eta_E(X)=\max(|e_{m-1}|,|e_m|)$.
It tends to zero because $m\to\infty$, and
$q_E(X)-\eta_E(X)\le m<q_E(X)+1+\eta_E(X)$.
The two ceiling inequalities follow, including the case when a bound is an
integer. These brackets are qualitative and not an effective computable error
bound. To see why an $o(1)$ error for $N_E$ cannot hold for all real $X$, put
$X=F_E(j+1/2)$. Then $q_E(X)=j+1/2$, while its distance from any integer is
$1/2$. For large $j$, the brackets in fact force $N_E(X)=j+1$.
\end{proof}

In base-two form the smooth expansion is
\[
 q_E(X)=\log_2X+\frac34\log_2\log_2X-\log_2A_E+o(1).
\]
In particular
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:smooth-gap}
 q_D(X)-q_C(X)\longrightarrow\frac\lambda{\log2}\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
The nonintegral limiting smooth displacement must not be confused with the
integer threshold gap.

\begin{corollary}\label{sct:cor:gap}
For every sufficiently large real $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:eq:threshold-gap}
 N_C(X)\le N_D(X)\le N_C(X)+1.
\end{equation}
Both possible gaps, zero and one, occur for infinitely many $X$ tending to
infinity, even if thresholds are restricted to integers.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Always $D_n\le C_n$, and \eqref{sct:eq:main} gives
$D_{n+1}/C_n\to2r>1$. For $m=N_C(X)$ large, therefore
$D_{m+1}>C_m\ge X$, so $N_D(X)\le m+1$; the other inequality is immediate.
At $X=D_n$ the eventual inequalities
$C_{n-1}<D_n\le C_n$ and strict growth of both counts give
$N_C(X)=N_D(X)=n$. At $X=C_n$, we have eventually
$D_n<C_n<D_{n+1}$, giving $N_C(X)=n$ and $N_D(X)=n+1$.
Both sequences of thresholds are integer and tend to infinity.
\end{proof}
Neither a rate for \eqref{sct:eq:main} nor a higher-order coefficient expansion is
used in these arguments. Further terms of the smooth Lambert inverse alone
would not supply corresponding further terms for the actual count thresholds.

\section{The problem and the result}\label{sct:gr:sec:problem}

The score of a tournament vertex is its outdegree. A score sequence is the
nondecreasing list of these scores. Landau's criterion says that an integer
sequence $0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_n$ is a tournament score sequence precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:landau}
 \sum_{i=1}^r s_i\ge \binom{r}{2}\quad(1\le r<n),
 \qquad \sum_{i=1}^n s_i=\binom{n}{2}.
\end{equation}
A score sequence is \emph{strong} precisely when all the proper inequalities
in \eqref{sct:gr:eq:landau} are strict. These criteria are recorded in Stockmeyer
\cite[Propositions 1 and 7]{stockmeyer}.

We call the score sequence self-complementary when
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:complement}
 s_i+s_{n+1-i}=n-1\qquad(1\le i\le n).
\end{equation}
Let $C(n)$ count score sequences satisfying \eqref{sct:gr:eq:complement}, and let
$D(n)$ count those that are also strong. With the empty sequence convention,
$C(0)=D(0)=1$; also $C(1)=D(1)=1$.

\textbf{The objects being counted.}
Each sorted score sequence is counted once, regardless of how many tournaments
realize it. We do not count labeled tournaments or isomorphism classes of
self-complementary tournaments. Complement symmetry of a score sequence does
not imply that every tournament realizing it is isomorphic to its reverse.
Stockmeyer discusses this distinction in Section 3 \cite{stockmeyer}.

\begin{theorem}\label{sct:gr:thm:main}
There are positive constants $c_C,C_C,c_D,C_D$ and an integer $n_0$ such that,
for every $n\ge n_0$,
\[
 c_C2^n n^{-3/4}\le C(n)\le C_C2^n n^{-3/4},\qquad
 c_D2^n n^{-3/4}\le D(n)\le C_D2^n n^{-3/4}.
\]
For $E=C$ or $E=D$, define
\[
 N_E(x)=\min\{n\ge1:E(n)\ge x\}.
\]
Then, as $x\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:inverse}
 N_E(x)=\log_2 x+\frac34\log_2\log_2 x+O(1).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Stockmeyer conjectured the two $\Theta$ estimates in
\cite[Section 6, p.~8]{stockmeyer}. The proof here uses a published probability
theorem with its hypotheses checked below. This report makes no claim about
publication, peer review, priority, or the absence of other proofs.

\subsection{Proof map}

The exact encoding in Section~\ref{sct:gr:sec:encoding} expresses each count as $2^n$
times an integrated-walk probability with a fixed terminal velocity. The
probability has order $n^{-3/4}$: survival to a halfway time costs $n^{-1/4}$,
and hitting one specified velocity over the remaining block costs $n^{-1/2}$.
The lower bound must couple these two requirements without losing survival.
A large positive halfway area and a bounded-velocity bridge accomplish that.
Sections~\ref{sct:gr:sec:bridge}--\ref{sct:gr:sec:endpoint} supply the argument, and
Section~\ref{sct:gr:sec:application} transfers it back to both counting problems.

\section{Exact encoding by a half sequence}\label{sct:gr:sec:encoding}

Write $n=2m$ or $n=2m+1$ and set
\[
 M=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
 =\begin{cases}m-1,&n=2m,\\m,&n=2m+1.\end{cases}
\]
For $m\ge1$, a self-complementary candidate is uniquely determined by
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:half}
 0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_m\le M.
\end{equation}
Reflect these entries using \eqref{sct:gr:eq:complement}; for odd $n$, insert the
central score $s_{m+1}=m$. The bound $M$ is exactly what makes the full reflected
sequence nondecreasing. Its total is automatically $\binom{n}{2}$.

For the full sequence define the Landau excess
\[
 A_r=\sum_{i=1}^r s_i-\binom{r}{2},\qquad 0\le r\le n.
\]
The last $r$ scores sum to $r(n-1)-\sum_{i=1}^r s_i$, so
\begin{align*}
 A_{n-r}
 &= \binom{n}{2}-r(n-1)+\sum_{i=1}^r s_i-\binom{n-r}{2}\\
 &=\sum_{i=1}^r s_i-\binom{r}{2}=A_r.
\end{align*}
Consequently all the Landau inequalities are equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:half-landau}
 A_r\ge0\quad(1\le r\le m),
\end{equation}
and strongness is equivalent to $A_r>0$ on exactly the same index set.
This includes the even midpoint and, by symmetry, both odd midpoint
inequalities. There is no omitted middle condition.

\subsection{Differences and the final slack}

Introduce nonnegative integers
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:gaps}
 G_1=s_1,\qquad G_j=s_j-s_{j-1}\ (2\le j\le m),
 \qquad G_{m+1}=M-s_m.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:composition}
 G_1+\cdots+G_{m+1}=M.
\end{equation}
Conversely, each nonnegative composition \eqref{sct:gr:eq:composition} gives
\eqref{sct:gr:eq:half} by $s_r=G_1+\cdots+G_r$. Thus this is a bijection,
not an approximation or a weighting of score sequences by realizations.

Now take $G_1,\ldots,G_{m+1}$ independently with
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:geom}
 \Pp(G_j=g)=2^{-g-1},\qquad g=0,1,2,\ldots.
\end{equation}
Every composition \eqref{sct:gr:eq:composition} has probability
\[
 2^{-(M+m+1)}=2^{-n}
\]
for either parity. Define an ordinary walk and its integrated area by
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:walk}
 X_j=G_j-1,\qquad V_0=1,\qquad
 V_r=1+\sum_{j=1}^r X_j,\qquad A_0=0,\qquad
 A_r=\sum_{j=1}^r V_j.
\end{equation}
For $r\le m$, $V_r=s_r-r+1$, so this $A_r$ is precisely the Landau excess.
The total-composition condition becomes
\[
 V_{m+1}=1+M-(m+1)=M-m.
\]
We have proved the following exact identities.

\begin{proposition}\label{sct:gr:prop:exact}
For every $m\ge1$,
\begin{align}
 C(2m)&=2^{2m}\Pp(A_r\ge0\ (1\le r\le m),\ V_{m+1}=-1),\label{sct:gr:eq:Ceven}\\
 C(2m+1)&=2^{2m+1}\Pp(A_r\ge0\ (1\le r\le m),\ V_{m+1}=0).\label{sct:gr:eq:Codd}
\end{align}
Replacing $\ge0$ by $>0$ gives the corresponding identities for $D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
The same encoding is Part~\ref{sct:part}, Section~\ref{sct:sec:encoding}, which adds the slack mixture \eqref{sct:eq:C-exact}. Here the endpoint is the composition condition at time $m+1$; there it is moved to time $m$ by conditioning on the last gap.
\end{writenote}

\textbf{The final step is not tested for area positivity.}
The last geometric variable is a slack variable, rather than an additional
score in the half sequence. Imposing a positivity condition on $A_{m+1}$
would change the count. For example, at $n=2$ the valid weak half sequence
$(0)$ has $(A_1,V_2)=(0,-1)$ and $A_2=-1$. At $n=4$ the valid strong half
sequence $(1,1)$ has $A_1=A_2=1$ and $A_3=0$. Both would be wrongly excluded
by an extra test at the slack step.

\subsection{Why the exponential factor is two}

Before Landau inequalities are imposed, the number of half sequences is
$\binom{M+m}{m}$. This is $\binom{2m-1}{m}$ for even length and
$\binom{2m}{m}$ for odd length, each of order $2^n n^{-1/2}$.
The additional integrated positivity constraint accounts for another
$n^{-1/4}$ factor. This observation explains the exponent but is not itself
a proof: survival and the terminal-velocity condition are dependent. The next
two sections handle that dependence.

\section{An elementary bounded bridge estimate}\label{sct:gr:sec:bridge}

Let $T_j=X_1+\cdots+X_j$, with $T_0=0$ and the increment law in
\eqref{sct:gr:eq:geom}--\eqref{sct:gr:eq:walk}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:moments}
 \Ee X=0,\qquad \Ee X^2=2,\qquad
 \Ee e^{\theta|X|}<\infty\quad(0<\theta<\log2).
\end{equation}
The support is $\{-1,0,1,\ldots\}$, of lattice span one. All endpoint estimates
in this section use these original integer coordinates.

\begin{lemma}\label{sct:gr:lem:atoms}
There is a finite constant $C$ such that
\[
 \sup_{d\in\Z}\Pp(T_\ell=d)\le C\ell^{-1/2}\qquad(\ell\ge1).
\]
For each fixed $B>0$, there are $c_B>0$ and $\ell_B$ such that
\[
 \Pp(T_\ell=d)\ge c_B\ell^{-1/2}
 \quad\text{if }\ell\ge\ell_B,\ d\in\Z,\ |d|\le B\sqrt\ell.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sum of $\ell$ independent geometric variables has a negative-binomial
law, so, for $d\ge-\ell$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:nb}
 \Pp(T_\ell=d)=\binom{2\ell+d-1}{\ell-1}2^{-2\ell-d}.
\end{equation}
For $d<-\ell$ it is zero. Consecutive positive masses have ratio
\[
 \frac{\Pp(T_\ell=d+1)}{\Pp(T_\ell=d)}
 =\frac{2\ell+d}{2(\ell+d+1)}.
\]
Thus $d=-1$ is a mode (also $d=-2$ when $\ell\ge2$), and
\[
 \sup_d\Pp(T_\ell=d)
 =\frac12\binom{2\ell-2}{\ell-1}4^{-(\ell-1)}
 =O(\ell^{-1/2}).
\]
Stirling's formula, uniformly over $|d|\le B\sqrt\ell$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:stirling}
 \Pp(T_\ell=d)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\ell}}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{d^2}{4\ell}\right)
 \bigl(1+O_B(\ell^{-1/2})\bigr).
\end{equation}
For clarity, the uniformity follows by writing
$\binom{2\ell+d-1}{\ell-1}=\frac{\ell}{2\ell+d}\binom{2\ell+d}{\ell}$,
applying Stirling to its three factorials, and expanding their logarithms
about $d=0$. In this window $d/\ell=O_B(\ell^{-1/2})$; the cubic remainder is
$O_B(\ell^{-1/2})$, and the factorial error terms are $O(\ell^{-1})$.
The Gaussian factor in \eqref{sct:gr:eq:stirling} is at least $e^{-B^2/4}$, which
proves the lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded bridge]\label{sct:gr:lem:bridge}
For every fixed $B>0$ there are $K>B$, $c>0$, and $\ell_0$ such that,
for every $\ell\ge\ell_0$ and every integer $|d|\le B\sqrt\ell$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:bridge}
 \Pp\!\left(T_\ell=d,\ \max_{0\le j\le\ell}|T_j|\le K\sqrt\ell\right)
 \ge c\ell^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $h=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor$ and $q=\ell-h$. For $\ell\ge3$, both are
comparable to $\ell$. On a violation during the first $h$ steps, condition
on those steps, apply the uniform endpoint bound to the remaining $q$
increments, and use Kolmogorov's maximal inequality. Since
$\operatorname{Var}(T_h)=2h$, this gives
\begin{align*}
 &\Pp\!\left(T_\ell=d,\ \max_{j\le h}|T_j|>K\sqrt\ell\right)\\
 &\hspace{1cm}\le \frac{C}{\sqrt q}
      \Pp\!\left(\max_{j\le h}|T_j|>K\sqrt\ell\right)
 \le\frac{C}{\sqrt q}\frac{2h}{K^2\ell}.
\end{align*}
For a violation at some $j>h$, the terminal condition $T_\ell=d$ implies
\[
 |T_\ell-T_j|\ge |T_j|-|d|>(K-B)\sqrt\ell.
\]
The reversed suffix sums
$R_i=X_\ell+X_{\ell-1}+\cdots+X_{\ell-i+1}$, $0\le i\le q$,
depend only on the last $q$ increments and have the same maximal inequality.
Condition on that block and use the atom bound on the first $h$ increments:
\begin{align*}
 &\Pp\!\left(T_\ell=d,\ \max_{h<j\le\ell}|T_j|>K\sqrt\ell\right)\\
 &\hspace{1cm}\le\frac{C}{\sqrt h}\frac{2q}{(K-B)^2\ell}.
\end{align*}
Hence, for a constant $C_0$ depending only on the increment law,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:bridge-tail}
 \Pp\!\left(T_\ell=d,\ \max_{j\le\ell}|T_j|>K\sqrt\ell\right)
 \le\frac{C_0}{\sqrt\ell}
 \left(\frac1{K^2}+\frac1{(K-B)^2}\right).
\end{equation}
Choose $K$ so large that the parenthesis times $C_0$ is at most $c_B/2$,
where $c_B$ is from Lemma~\ref{sct:gr:lem:atoms}. Subtracting
\eqref{sct:gr:eq:bridge-tail} from the lower endpoint mass proves
\eqref{sct:gr:eq:bridge} with $c=c_B/2$.
\end{proof}

The bridge is controlled by a deterministic velocity bound, so no conditioned
bridge invariance principle or two-dimensional local limit theorem is needed.

Part~\ref{sct:part}, Lemma~\ref{sct:lem:max}, proves the endpoint-weighted maximal upper bound $C\sqrt\ell/H^2$ by the same split; Lemma~\ref{sct:gr:lem:bridge} is the lower-bound form used only in this Part.
\end{writenote}

\section{The integrated walk with a fixed final velocity}\label{sct:gr:sec:endpoint}

For a starting state $(a,v)$, write
\[
 V_j=v+T_j,\qquad I_j=a+\sum_{i=1}^j V_i,
 \qquad \tau=\min\{j\ge1:I_j\le0\}.
\]
Probabilities with this starting state are denoted by $\Pp_{(a,v)}$.
The main probabilistic step is the following lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{sct:gr:lem:endpoint}
For every fixed integer $b$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:endpoint}
 \Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>k,\ V_k=b)=\Theta(k^{-3/4})
 \qquad(k\to\infty).
\end{equation}
The constants may depend on $b$. The applications below require only $b=0,1$.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{The imported theorem and its hypotheses}

Denisov and Wachtel \cite[Theorem 1, equations (7)--(8), pp.~169--170]{dw}
consider independent identically distributed increments $Y$ satisfying
\[
 \Ee Y=0,\qquad \Ee Y^2=1,\qquad
 \Ee|Y|^{2+\delta}<\infty\quad\text{for some }\delta>0.
\]
For the integrated process killed when its area ceases to be positive, their
theorem supplies a positive harmonic function on
\[
 K_+=\{z\in(0,\infty)\times\R:
 \Pp_z(Z_j\in(0,\infty)^2,\ \tau>j)>0
 \text{ for some }j\ge0\}.
\]
For a fixed starting point in $K_+$, it gives a positive-constant survival
asymptotic of order $k^{-1/4}$ and weak convergence of the conditional endpoint,
with area divided by $k^{3/2}$ and velocity divided by $k^{1/2}$, to a probability
measure $\mu$ whose density is displayed in that theorem.

Here $\sigma=\sqrt2$ and $Y=X/\sigma$. Equation~\eqref{sct:gr:eq:moments}
checks all the moment assumptions. Divide \emph{both} area and velocity by
$\sigma$, so the start becomes $(1/\sigma,1/\sigma)$. This point belongs to
$K_+$ already at time zero. Scaling by a positive number preserves the exit
event. Consequently, for a constant $c_*>0$,
\begin{align}
 \Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>t)&\sim c_*t^{-1/4},\label{sct:gr:eq:DWsurvival}\\
 \mathcal L\!\left(
 \frac{I_t}{\sigma t^{3/2}},\frac{V_t}{\sigma\sqrt t}
 \ \middle|\ \tau>t\right)&\Longrightarrow\mu.
 \label{sct:gr:eq:DWlimit}
\end{align}
Theorem 1 has no lattice hypothesis. The normalized increments need not be
integer valued; the exact lattice calculations of Section~\ref{sct:gr:sec:bridge}
remain in the unscaled coordinates. We use only Theorem 1, not any killed
local-limit statement elsewhere in that paper.

\subsection{Positive limiting mass at large area}

We need more than survival alone: some surviving paths must have a large
positive area and a bounded velocity at halfway time. The density formula in
\cite[Theorem 1]{dw} has the form
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:density}
 \bar h(a,b)=c_0\int_0^\infty\int_0^1
 w^{3/2}s^{-1/2}e^{-2w^2/s}\,
 q_{1-s}(a,-b;0,-w)\,ds\,dw,
 \qquad c_0>0,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 q_u(x,y;z,v)=p_u(x,y;z,v)-p_u(x,y;z,-v)
\]
and the Gaussian transition density is
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:gaussian}
 p_u(x,y;z,v)=\frac{\sqrt3}{\pi u^2}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{6(z-x-uy)^2}{u^3}
 +\frac{6(z-x-uy)(v-y)}{u^2}
 -\frac{2(v-y)^2}{u}\right).
\end{equation}
Taking the ratio of the two Gaussian terms gives
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:ratio}
 \frac{p_u(a,-b;0,-w)}{p_u(a,-b;0,w)}
 =\exp\!\left(\frac{4w(3a-bu)}{u^2}\right).
\end{equation}
If $a>0$ and $b<3a$, the right side exceeds one for all $0<u<1$, $w>0$.
Thus the integrand in \eqref{sct:gr:eq:density} is positive and $\bar h(a,b)>0$
in this region. In particular, every sufficiently high-area open rectangle
with velocity close to zero has positive $\mu$-mass.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026, correction]

The argument of this subsection uses only the positivity of the outer weights and the ratio \eqref{sct:gr:eq:ratio}, both unchanged for the true density. Its conclusion, positive $\mu$-mass of the rectangle, holds verbatim (Proposition~\ref{sct:prop:corrected}(c): the integrand is positive where $a>0$ and $b\le3a$).
\end{writenote}

Fix any sufficiently large $L$. In the normalized coordinates of
\eqref{sct:gr:eq:DWlimit}, the open rectangle
\[
 (L/\sigma,(L+1)/\sigma)\times(-1/\sigma,1/\sigma)
\]
lies in that positive-density region. The Portmanteau theorem and
\eqref{sct:gr:eq:DWsurvival} imply that, for some $c_L>0$ and all large $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:halfway}
 \Pp_{(1,1)}\!\left(\tau>t,
 Lt^{3/2}<I_t<(L+1)t^{3/2},\ |V_t|<\sqrt t\right)
 \ge c_Lt^{-1/4}.
\end{equation}
The constant can be small, but it is fixed and strictly positive. No
continuity of the density or local endpoint theorem is required for this use
of weak convergence.

\subsection{Proof of the endpoint lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{sct:gr:lem:endpoint}]
Put $t=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ and $\ell=k-t$. The upper bound follows by
forgetting every area condition after time $t$. The Markov property,
Lemma~\ref{sct:gr:lem:atoms}, and \eqref{sct:gr:eq:DWsurvival} yield
\begin{align*}
 \Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>k,V_k=b)
 &\le \Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>t)\sup_d\Pp(T_\ell=d)\\
 &=O(t^{-1/4}\ell^{-1/2})=O(k^{-3/4}).
\end{align*}

For the lower bound, take $B=2$ in Lemma~\ref{sct:gr:lem:bridge}, and fix its
constant $K$. Then choose $L=4(K+1)$ in \eqref{sct:gr:eq:halfway}. Given a halfway
state $(a,v)$ in that event, $|v|<\sqrt t\le\sqrt\ell$. Since $b$ is fixed,
$|b-v|\le2\sqrt\ell$ for all large $k$. The independent remaining increments
therefore have probability at least $c/\sqrt\ell$ of satisfying
\[
 T_\ell=b-v,\qquad \max_{j\le\ell}|T_j|\le K\sqrt\ell.
\]
During this event every remaining velocity is at least
$-(K+1)\sqrt\ell$. For every $j\le\ell$, the area obeys
\[
 I_{t+j}=a+jv+\sum_{i=1}^jT_i
 \ge a-(K+1)\ell^{3/2}
 >Lt^{3/2}-(K+1)\ell^{3/2}>0.
\]
The last inequality holds for all sufficiently large $t$, since
$\ell\in\{t,t+1\}$ and $L=4(K+1)$. Thus the entire final block survives,
and $V_k=b$. Multiplying the conditional bridge bound by
\eqref{sct:gr:eq:halfway} gives
\[
 \Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>k,V_k=b)
 \ge c' t^{-1/4}\ell^{-1/2}\ge c''k^{-3/4}.
\]
This proves both bounds.
\end{proof}

\section{Application to the two counting sequences}\label{sct:gr:sec:application}

\subsection{Weak upper bound and the extra endpoint step}

In Proposition~\ref{sct:gr:prop:exact}, put $I_r=A_r+1$. Since the areas are integers,
$A_r\ge0$ if and only if $I_r>0$. The shifted walk has starting state
$(I_0,V_0)=(1,1)$, so \eqref{sct:gr:eq:DWsurvival} applies.

Set $t=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, and let $b=-1$ for even length or $b=0$ for
odd length. Dropping all area constraints after $t$ and retaining the endpoint
at time $m+1$ gives
\begin{align*}
 &\Pp(A_r\ge0\ (1\le r\le m),\ V_{m+1}=b)\\
 &\hspace{1cm}\le\Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>t)
              \sup_d\Pp(T_{m+1-t}=d)
 =O(m^{-3/4}).
\end{align*}
There is no new area condition at time $m+1$ in this bound. Multiplication by
$2^n$ gives $C(n)=O(2^nn^{-3/4})$, and $D(n)\le C(n)$ gives the same upper
bound for the strong count.

\subsection{Strong lower bound from two fixed gaps}

For the strong event, force
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:forced}
 G_1=1,\qquad G_{m+1}=0.
\end{equation}
These independent requirements cost exactly $(1/4)(1/2)=1/8$.
The first step gives $(A_1,V_1)=(1,1)$, so strict positivity is already
satisfied. The next $m-1$ tested steps are an integrated walk from this
strictly interior starting state. This avoids applying the imported theorem
to the boundary starting point $(0,1)$.

The final slack increment is $X_{m+1}=-1$. Therefore the tested-time endpoint
needed at time $m$ is
\[
 V_m=\begin{cases}0,&n=2m,\\1,&n=2m+1.\end{cases}
\]
Let $b$ denote the indicated tested-time target. Independence and
Lemma~\ref{sct:gr:lem:endpoint} show that the event in the strong counting identity
has probability at least
\[
 \frac18\Pp_{(1,1)}(\tau>m-1,V_{m-1}=b)
 =\Omega(m^{-3/4}).
\]
Here the time index $m-1$ belongs to the walk restarted after the forced first
step. The endpoint velocity is still the absolute velocity, not a displacement.
The final forced slack restores the required original terminal velocity
$-1$ or $0$, respectively.

Thus $D(n)=\Omega(2^nn^{-3/4})$. Since $C(n)\ge D(n)$, the same lower bound
holds for $C$. This completes the growth part of Theorem~\ref{sct:gr:thm:main},
with constants valid across both parities.
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{sct:part}, Section~\ref{sct:sec:harmonic}, decomposes the strong count over the first gap $g\ge1$, \eqref{sct:eq:D-direct}, instead of forcing $G_1=1$. It obtains the strong amplitude, and comparison with $D_n\sim rC_n$ gives the harmonic identity \eqref{sct:eq:harmonic-identity}. The forced-gap class here keeps only a subset of the paths, as Section~\ref{sct:gr:sec:scope} says.
\end{writenote}

\section{Monotonicity and threshold inversion}\label{sct:gr:sec:inverse}

\begin{proposition}\label{sct:gr:prop:mono}
$C(n)$ is nondecreasing for all $n\ge0$. $D(n)$ is nondecreasing for $n\ge2$.
The initial exception is $D(1)=1>D(2)=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From length $2m$ to length $2m+1$, keep the same half sequence. The cap increases
from $m-1$ to $m$, while all tested excesses remain unchanged. Reflection
recovers the original half sequence, so the map is injective and preserves
weak or strict inequalities.

From length $2m+1$ to length $2m+2$, send
\[
 (s_1,\ldots,s_m)\longmapsto(s_1,\ldots,s_m,m).
\]
The new cap is $m$. Earlier excesses are unchanged, and the new one is
\[
 A_{m+1}=A_m+m-m=A_m.
\]
For $m\ge1$ this preserves both weak and strict inequalities; removing the
last half score proves injectivity. For $m=0$ it preserves only the weak
condition, because $A_0=0$. Directly checking $n=0,1,2$ gives the asserted
initial behavior.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Not in Part~\ref{sct:part}, which has only eventual strict increase of both counts (Section~\ref{sct:sec:inverse}). Together: $C_n$ is nondecreasing for all $n$ and $D_n$ for $n\ge2$, both eventually strictly increasing; the table of Section~\ref{sct:gr:sec:checks} shows equalities $C(1)=C(2)$ and $C(3)=C(4)=2$, $D(3)=D(4)$, $D(5)=D(6)$.
\end{writenote}

\pagebreak[3]
\begin{proof}[Proof of the inverse formula]
Fix $E=C$ or $E=D$. The growth theorem makes $N_E(x)$ finite for all large
$x$ and ensures $N_E(x)\to\infty$. Write $n=N_E(x)$ and $y=\log_2x$.
Minimality gives $E(n-1)<x\le E(n)$. For sufficiently large $x$, both
$n-1$ and $n$ lie beyond the finite exceptions in the two-sided growth bounds,
so
\[
 c\,2^{n-1}(n-1)^{-3/4}<x
 \le C\,2^n n^{-3/4}
\]
for fixed positive $c,C$. Taking logarithms yields
\begin{equation}\label{sct:gr:eq:invert-middle}
 y=n-\frac34\log_2n+O(1).
\end{equation}
First $n\asymp y$ and then $n=y+O(\log y)$. Hence
$\log_2n=\log_2y+O((\log y)/y)$. Substituting in
\eqref{sct:gr:eq:invert-middle} proves \eqref{sct:gr:eq:inverse}.
The isolated decrease in $D$ is irrelevant: minimality, eventual bounds, and
unboundedness suffice for this argument.
\end{proof}
\begin{writenote}[5 October 2026]
Part~\ref{sct:part}, Proposition~\ref{sct:prop:inverse}, sharpens the $O(1)$ for the smooth inverse to the constant $-\log_2A_E$ plus $o(1)$ and gives vanishing-width ceiling brackets for $N_E$; Corollary~\ref{sct:cor:gap} adds the eventual zero-or-one gap between $N_C$ and $N_D$. Its proof also shows that a global $o(1)$ error for $N_E$ is false. This is consistent with Section~\ref{sct:gr:sec:scope}: a $\Theta$ estimate alone gives no constant, and the constant comes from the equivalents.
\end{writenote}

\section{What is proved and what remains open}\label{sct:gr:sec:scope}

The proof establishes two-sided growth bounds, with positive finite constants,
and an inverse accurate to a bounded additive error. It also gives an
integrated-walk endpoint estimate valid for every fixed integer terminal
velocity from the interior start $(1,1)$.

It does not identify a limit for $C(n)/(2^nn^{-3/4})$ or
$D(n)/(2^nn^{-3/4})$, nor prove that such limits exist on either parity.
The two-sided bounds imply that $D(n)/C(n)$ stays bounded away from zero
for all large $n$, but they do not prove convergence of this ratio or the
numerical proportion suggested by finite data. An $O(1)$ error in the inverse
cannot be sharpened to a fixed constant from a $\Theta$ estimate alone.

Three natural next steps are:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Endpoint-conditioned density estimates.}
 Establish a local estimate for a fixed terminal velocity, with integrated
 area ranging over its natural $n^{3/2}$ scale, and prove tail bounds strong
 enough to sum it. Conditional weak convergence alone does not determine
 the probability of a single velocity value.
 \item \textbf{Leading constants and parity.}
 Use such an estimate, together with the exact initial and final gap
 constraints, to determine whether the even and odd normalized counts
 converge and whether their limits agree. The fixed-gap lower bound used
 here intentionally retains only a subset of valid paths, so it cannot
 recover a leading constant.
 \item \textbf{Strong-to-all ratios and corrections.}
 Analyze the weak and strict boundary conditions sharply enough to compare
 their amplitudes, then justify any lower-order powers, logarithmic terms,
 or other correction expansions. No transseries or full correction
 expansion is established in this report.
\end{enumerate}

No inference about differential finiteness or analytic continuation is drawn
from the order-of-growth estimates. Such conclusions require additional
arguments, not numerical fitting of the exponent.

Steps~1 and~2 are answered there for the purposes stated: Theorem~\ref{sct:thm:endpoint} gives the fixed-velocity endpoint equivalent (without localizing the area), and Section~\ref{sct:sec:C} shows that both parities have one limit with no parity multiplier. The strong-to-all limit of step~3 is $e^{-\lambda}$ (Section~\ref{sct:sec:D}). Correction terms, logarithmic terms and transseries remain open in both Parts. The non-claims above remain true of this Part as printed. See Section~\ref{sct:gr:sec:further}.

\section{Exact checks and reproducibility}\label{sct:gr:sec:checks}

The accompanying program is a finite consistency check of the combinatorial
encoding and its indexing. It is not a numerical proof of an asymptotic claim,
and the probabilistic lemmas above do not depend on the program's output.

\subsection{Independent finite formulations}

The checker uses four representations:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item A half-score dynamic program on the last score and the running score sum,
 extending nondecreasing half sequences directly.
 \item A composition dynamic program on the used gap total and weighted gap sum,
 appending the uniquely determined final slack without another area test.
 \item An integrated-walk dynamic program on velocity and area, using endpoint
 reachability to bound transitions and including the final untested slack step.
 \item Brute enumeration of full nondecreasing score lists, imposing reflection
 symmetry and every proper Landau inequality in the full list.
\end{enumerate}
The first three agree for both weak and strict inequalities at every
$n=0,\ldots,38$ (156 pairwise comparisons); the full enumeration agrees for
$n=0,\ldots,9$ (20 comparisons). The computed prefixes match 35 reference
terms of A345470 and 39 of A351869, independently checked against the OEIS
entry pages on 2 October 2026, starting at $n=0$.

Exhaustive tests for $n=2,\ldots,17$ compare 26,364 half candidates with
26,364 independently generated compositions. They check reflection of
excesses, the gap bijection, equal geometric mass, 22,987 valid monotonicity
injections, the fixed-gap lower-bound class, and both final-slack
counterexamples. Exact rational and symbolic checks also cover the
negative-binomial identities, increment moments, Gaussian density-ratio
algebra, and finite threshold bookkeeping. These checks support the formulas
and indexing; they do not certify the probability limit theorem or replace
any proof above.

checker, fixed reference prefixes with provenance, generated exact counts and
check summary, a deterministic PDF build script, a replay driver, and a strict
SHA-256 manifest. No downloaded source PDFs are bundled. Bibliographic links below identify
the primary sources.

Run \code{python3 replay.py} from the extracted package directory. The replay
first validates the exact manifest path set, file types, sizes, and hashes.
It runs the finite checker both normally and under \code{python3 -O} and
compares both outputs with the archived results. The mathematical and schema
mutation campaign rejects 37 named mutants in 74 normal and optimized runs,
each with its prescribed diagnostic rather than a syntax error or crash. The suite also rejects 17 damaged, absent, unexpected, or unsafe manifest
and package variants, with the same protections active under optimization. All requirements use
explicit exceptions rather than Python \code{assert}, so optimized execution
cannot silently disable checks.

the PDF twice in clean temporary directories and compares the bytes with each
other and with the archived PDF. Byte-for-byte reproduction requires the same
TeX engine, packages, and fonts. The recorded toolchain is included in
\code{build-environment.txt}; a different toolchain may typeset an equivalent
PDF with different bytes. Arithmetic verification uses only Python's standard
library and does not require network access or external source downloads.
