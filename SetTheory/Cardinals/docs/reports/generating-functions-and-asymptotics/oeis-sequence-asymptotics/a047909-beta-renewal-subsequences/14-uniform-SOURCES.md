# Source scope for the transition uniform addendum

Audit date: 2 October 2026. Scope: attribution of the general method in the accompanying article; this is not an exhaustive novelty search. Only primary articles and their publisher-hosted material support the conclusions below.

## Bottom line

The general method should be attributed to the classical exponential-tilting/Edgeworth (Esscher–Cramér) approach. The real half-line normal–Laplace-kernel expression is not literally the Lugannani–Rice formula. A safe contribution statement is: **a self-contained, family-specific, arbitrary-fixed-order uniform remainder proof and matching calculation for the Beta-renewal triangular family**. Do not claim that transition-uniform tail approximation, integrating a tilted Edgeworth expansion, or all-order saddlepoint expansions are new general methods. This limited audit does not establish priority for the family-specific theorem either.

## Verified primary sources

### Daniels (1987): closest explicit attribution

H. E. Daniels, “Tail Probability Approximations,” *International Statistical Review* **55**(1), 37–48. [Publisher archive / DOI](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1403269), DOI 10.2307/1403269.

**Inspected:** original p. 37 scan and publisher abstract in the cloud browser. The introduction attributes tilted/recentered Edgeworth tail approximation to Esscher (1932) and Cramér (1938). It distinguishes its half-integer central ordering from the integer-order large-deviation reduction, and contrasts the LR organization. Thus this is the central citation for the method and the distinction. **Access limit:** pages 38–48 were not inspected; do not cite an equation or exact remainder theorem from those pages as verified by this audit.

### Lugannani–Rice (1980): classical uniform tail-series precedent

R. Lugannani and S. Rice, “Saddle point approximation for the distribution of the sum of independent random variables,” *Advances in Applied Probability* **12**(2), 475–490. [Original publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/70A031DB905980CA675021C6D9BFFD21/S0001867800050278a.pdf/saddle-point-approximation-for-the-distribution-of-the-sum-of-independent-random-variables.pdf), DOI 10.2307/1426607.

**Inspected:** publisher PDF text, especially pp. 478–483. Equation (11) introduces an exponentially tilted associated density. Section 3(a) gives a coefficient recurrence; §3(b) treats coalescence with the origin; §3(d) discusses integration of Daniels’ density series. Their proposed equivalence of general error orders there is a conjecture supported by an exponential example. Sections 4–5 give conditions and error analysis for their contour construction. This verifies substantial classical precedent, but does not by itself verify the precise two-norm triangular-family theorem used here.

### Robinson (1982): tilted normal/Edgeworth tail construction

J. Robinson, “Saddlepoint Approximations for Permutation Tests and Confidence Intervals,” *JRSS B* **44**(1), 91–101. [Publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/44/1/91/7027643), DOI 10.1111/j.2517-6161.1982.tb01191.x.

**Inspected:** publisher abstract and search-indexed text of the publisher PDF, pp. 91 and 94. Robinson describes obtaining tail approximations from normal approximation of an exponentially shifted distribution, improving relative error using another Edgeworth term, and relating the result to integrated saddlepoint densities. This is a useful additional classical citation. The full formulas and hypotheses were not independently checked here; no precise Robinson theorem is invoked as the proof of (U).

### Jensen (1988): relevant uniformity literature

J. L. Jensen, “Uniform saddlepoint approximations,” *Advances in Applied Probability* **20**(3), 622–634. [Publisher abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-applied-probability/article/abs/uniform-saddlepoint-approximations/3B79D8194C481B2CB4792CC054DD6116), DOI 10.2307/1427038.

**Inspected:** abstract only. It concerns validity of density and tail expansions as the evaluation point tends to infinity, with general conditions and distribution classes. Relevant context, but not evidence that it proves the specific compact-saddle triangular-family statement here. The original publication is 1988, despite the publisher’s later online-posting date.

## The article and its proof

The family-specific weighted-density lemma, transition-uniform relative tail theorem, matching calculation, and separated inverse are proved in the accompanying article. No precise theorem from any partially inspected source is invoked as a replacement for that proof. All orders are fixed before taking a limit; constants depend on the fixed order and compact ratio range.
