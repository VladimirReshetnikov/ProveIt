\begin{abstract}
The OEIS entries A284365 and A284366 conjecture that two complementary
position sequences associated with the substitution $0\mapsto1$,
$1\mapsto101010$ have linear-approximation errors smaller than $2$.
Both upper bounds are false. Their first counterexamples occur at ranks
$8\,933\,313$ and $2\,977\,771$, respectively, and have the same error
$2.0048721733261288\ldots$. We give exact integer certificates for these
values and for their minimality. More substantially, we determine the
complete discrepancy limit sets for every substitution
$0\mapsto1$, $1\mapsto(10)^m$, $m\ge1$. For the OEIS case $m=3$, the sharp
common upper bound is $(6+\sqrt{21})/5=2.116515138991168\ldots$.
The proof uses two state-dependent prefix-weight intervals and a contracting
set recursion. We also prove the companion density conjectures, construct
explicit infinite families of counterexamples, derive rational generating
functions for their indices, and provide exact-arithmetic random-access
and verification software. The mathematical proofs are self-contained;
finite experiments are supporting checks, not substitutes for the proofs.

Part~II, added on October 1, 2026 (batch 73O1), applies the same method to
the disjoint family $0\mapsto1$, $1\mapsto1\,0^a1^b$ with $a,b\ge1$. It
proves that prefix discrepancy is bounded exactly when $a\le b+1$
(logarithmic growth at $a=b+2$, power growth beyond), determines the four
exact state extrema and the sharp, unattained position-error envelopes
throughout the bounded regime, and specializes them to the OEIS words
A284368 ($1\mapsto1011$) and A284369 ($1\mapsto1001$). For A284369 this
proves the conjectured bounds of A284370 and A284371 with sharper constants;
for A284368 it recovers, as a calibration, the known $s$-Wythoff floor
formulas of A184484--A184485.

Part~III, added on October 6, 2026 (batch 114), answers Part~II's question
on the unbounded regimes. At $a=b+2$ the discrepancy $\#0-\#1$ divided by
$\log N$ has the cluster interval $[-(a-1)/(2\log a),(a-1)/(2\log a)]$, with
explicit first extremal prefixes; for $a\geq b+3$ the eigenweight
$\#0-q\#1$ divided by $N^{\log_\lambda q}$ has a closed-form symmetric
cluster interval and a continuous graph-directed phase profile (endpoints
$\pm\sqrt{10}$ and the bound $X(N)^2+5X(N)\leq10N$ at every prefix for
$1\mapsto1\,0^81$). For every word with $a$ zeros and $a-1$ ones beginning
with $1$, an exact positive convolution formula for complete-block prefix
histograms yields a random-sum law, exact moments, Gaussian, local and
large-deviation laws, sharp variance and pressure comparisons, and a
classification of the zero-height profiles, counted by $(a-1)2^{a-2}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The selected problem and its resolution}\label{sec:problem}

Let $\sigma$ be the substitution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:original-sub}
\sigma(0)=1,\qquad \sigma(1)=101010.
\end{equation}
Its fixed word beginning with $1$ is
\[
\word=101010110101011010101\cdots.
\]
This is \OEIS{A284364} \cite{oeis364}. Number positions starting at $1$.
Let $u(n)$ and $v(n)$ be the positions of the $n$th zero and the $n$th one,
respectively. Thus $u$ is \OEIS{A284365} and $v$ is \OEIS{A284366}
\cite{oeis365,oeis366}. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rs3}
r=\frac{9+\sqrt{21}}6,\qquad
s=\frac{\sqrt{21}-1}2,\qquad \frac1r+\frac1s=1.
\end{equation}
The position-sequence entries conjecture
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjectures}
-2<rn-u(n)<2,\qquad -1<sn-v(n)<2\qquad(n\ge1).
\end{equation}
They also conjecture their respective densities to be
$(9-\sqrt{21})/10$ and $(1+\sqrt{21})/10$. The density comments are dated
April 13, 2025; the sequence entries credit Clark Kimberling, March 26,
2017. We use the unambiguous position-sequence formulations: the parent
entry's comment uses count terminology alongside position slopes, which
would be a different and immediately incompatible statement.

\begin{theorem}[Resolution for the OEIS sequences]\label{thm:original}
Write $E_0(n)=rn-u(n)$ and $E_1(n)=sn-v(n)$. For every $n\ge1$,
\begin{align}
-\frac{12+2\sqrt{21}}{15}&<E_0(n)<\frac{6+\sqrt{21}}5,\label{eq:zero3}\\
\frac{\sqrt{21}-9}{5}&<E_1(n)<\frac{6+\sqrt{21}}5.\label{eq:one3}
\end{align}
Each endpoint is a subsequential limit, and indeed the full set of
subsequential limits of each error sequence is the corresponding closed
interval. In particular, both conjectured lower bounds in
\eqref{eq:conjectures} hold, but both upper bounds fail infinitely often.

The first counterexamples are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-values}
v(2\,977\,771)=5\,334\,043,\qquad
u(8\,933\,313)=20\,222\,898.
\end{equation}
Their common error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-error}
\frac{2\,977\,771\sqrt{21}-13\,645\,857}{2}
=2.00487217332612884794005732278\ldots>2.
\end{equation}
The two asserted densities are correct.
\end{theorem}

The bounds are not consequences of extrapolating the observed maxima.
Sections~\ref{sec:family}--\ref{sec:positions} prove them for an entire
family. Section~\ref{sec:certificate} supplies a finite certificate for
\eqref{eq:first-values} and its minimality. Section~\ref{sec:infinite}
gives an explicit sequence of errors increasing to the sharp upper
endpoint. The method belongs to the established study of the relation
between substitution spectra and discrepancy \cite{adamczewski}; all
specific interval and sharpness arguments needed here are proved below.

\section{A parameterized family and its discrepancy theorem}\label{sec:family}

Fix an integer $m\ge1$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:family-sub}
\sigma_m(0)=1,\qquad \sigma_m(1)=(10)^m.
\end{equation}
The image of $1$ begins with $1$ and has length at least $2$, so the words
$\sigma_m^k(1)$ form nested prefixes of an infinite fixed word
$\word^{(m)}$. The substitution contains both letters in an iterate of
either letter; no additional initial condition is needed beyond the
initial $1$.

Throughout the general argument let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
q=\frac{\sqrt{m^2+4m}-m}{2},\quad
\lambda=m+q=\frac mq,\quad
s=1+q,\quad r=1+\frac1q,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CH}
t=1-q,\qquad C=\frac{q}{1-q^2},\qquad
H=qC=\frac{q^2}{1-q^2}.
\end{equation}
The quantities depend on $m$, even when the subscript is suppressed.
The following identities will be used repeatedly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identities}
0<q<1,\quad q^2=m(1-q),\quad
q(1+H)=C,\quad H(1+q)=m,\quad
\frac1r+\frac1s=1.
\end{equation}
For example, $H(1+q)=q^2/(1-q)=m$. Also $q$ is irrational:
$(m+1)^2<m^2+4m<(m+2)^2$ for $m\ge1$.

Let $Z(N)$ and $O(N)$ count the zeros and ones in the first $N$ letters,
with $Z(0)=O(0)=0$. Let $u_m(n)$ and $v_m(n)$ denote the corresponding
position sequences, and set
\[
E_0(n)=rn-u_m(n),\qquad E_1(n)=sn-v_m(n).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sharp error intervals for every $m$]\label{thm:general}
For every integer $m\ge1$ and $n\ge1$,
\begin{align}
C-m&<E_1(n)<C,\label{eq:general-one}\\
\frac{1-C}{q}&<E_0(n)<C.\label{eq:general-zero}
\end{align}
The closures of the two error-value sets are exactly these intervals
with their endpoints included. These are also their full subsequential
limit sets. No endpoint is attained at a finite index.

For every $N\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-density}
-\frac{H}{1+q}<O(N)-\frac{N}{1+q}<\frac{C}{1+q}.
\end{equation}
Again, the two endpoints are sharp, and the closure of the centered
counting errors is the full closed interval. In particular,
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{O(N)}N=\frac1s,\qquad
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{Z(N)}N=\frac1r.
\]
\end{theorem}

A useful equivalent form of the lower zero-position endpoint is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-lower-alt}
\frac{1-C}{q}=C-1-\frac{m-1}{q}.
\end{equation}
For $m=3$, substituting $q=(\sqrt{21}-3)/2$ gives
\[
C=\frac{6+\sqrt{21}}5,\qquad
H=\frac{3+3\sqrt{21}}{10},
\]
and \eqref{eq:general-one}--\eqref{eq:general-zero} become
\eqref{eq:one3}--\eqref{eq:zero3}.

\section{A contracting weight and two prefix states}\label{sec:weight}

For a finite binary word $P$, define its weight by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight}
X(P)=|P|_0-q|P|_1.
\end{equation}
This is additive under concatenation. The incidence matrix of the
substitution, with row order $0,1$ and column order $0,1$, is
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&m\\1&m\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are $\lambda$ and $-q$. More directly, the letter weights
give the identity on which the proof rests:
\begin{lemma}[Weight contraction]\label{lem:contraction}
For every finite word $P$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-contract}
X(\sigma_m(P))=-qX(P).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $P=0$ the two sides equal $-q$. For $P=1$, the image has $m$ letters
of each type and hence weight $m(1-q)=q^2=-q(-q)$. Additivity proves the
claim for an arbitrary concatenation of letters.
\end{proof}

The next letter is crucial. For $a\in\{0,1\}$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state-def}
S_a=\{X(w_1\cdots w_N):N\ge0,\ w_{N+1}=a\}.
\end{equation}
Thus $0\in S_1$, from the empty prefix. A prefix ending immediately
before a zero belongs to state $0$, not to a state determined by its last
letter.

\begin{lemma}[Exact state equations]\label{lem:state}
The sets in \eqref{eq:state-def} satisfy
\begin{align}
S_0&=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}(-qS_1+jt-q),\label{eq:state0}\\
S_1&=(-qS_0)\ \cup\!
\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}(-qS_1+jt).\label{eq:state1}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
View the fixed word as the concatenation of images of its own letters.
If a parent prefix $P$ is followed by $0$, its image is followed by the
single letter $1$, giving the transition $-qS_0$ in \eqref{eq:state1}.
If $P$ is followed by $1$, its image is followed by $(10)^m$.
Immediately before the $(j+1)$st one in that image, the internal prefix is
$(10)^j$, whose weight is $jt$. Immediately before the corresponding
zero, the internal prefix is $(10)^j1$, whose weight is $jt-q$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:contraction} gives all the stated affine images.
Conversely, every location in the fixed word lies in one of these image
blocks. Thus there are no missing transitions and the equations are
set equalities.
\end{proof}

Using a single state and adding all possible offsets independently would
forget which parent letter permits which offset. The two-state system
retains precisely the information required for the sharp constants.

\section{Solving the interval recursion, including sharpness}\label{sec:intervals}

Define two closed intervals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intervals}
I_0=[-C,H-1],\qquad I_1=[-qH,H].
\end{equation}
They are nondegenerate for every $m\ge1$.

\begin{lemma}[Invariant intervals]\label{lem:intervals}
The pair $(I_0,I_1)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:state0}--\eqref{eq:state1}
with $S_a$ replaced by $I_a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $J_j=-qI_1+jt$. Each $J_j$ has length
\[
qH(1+q)=mq,
\]
while successive left endpoints are separated by $t=1-q=q^2/m$.
If $m\ge2$, then $mq\ge t$, so adjacent intervals overlap. For $m=1$
there is only one interval. The extreme endpoints yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unionJ}
\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}J_j=[-qH,H-t].
\end{equation}
Indeed, the upper endpoint is $q^2H+(m-1)t=H-t$, since
$q^2(H+1)=H$ and $mt=q^2$.

Subtracting $q$ from \eqref{eq:unionJ} gives
\[
\bigcup_{j=0}^{m-1}(J_j-q)=[-qH-q,H-t-q]=[-C,H-1]=I_0.
\]
For state $1$, the other image is
\[
-qI_0=[q-qH,H].
\]
It overlaps or touches the interval in \eqref{eq:unionJ}, because
\[
H-t\ge q-qH
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H(1+q)\ge1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\ge1.
\]
Their union is therefore $[-qH,H]=I_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Strict inclusion of all actual prefixes]\label{lem:strict}
Every element of $S_a$ lies in the interior of $I_a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We induct on the length $N$ of the prefix. The empty prefix has weight
$0$, which lies strictly inside $I_1$.

For a nonempty prefix, locate its next letter in an image block. The
associated parent prefix has some length $k<N$. To see strict decrease,
if $k>0$ then that parent prefix begins with $1$, so its image has length
at least $k+(2m-1)>k$; this image is contained in the original prefix.
If $k=0$, strict decrease follows from $N>0$.

By induction, the parent weight belongs to the interior of its appropriate
interval. Its actual transition is an affine map with nonzero slope
$-q$. The image of an interior point is an interior point of the image
interval, and Lemma~\ref{lem:intervals} puts that image interval inside
the appropriate target interval. Such a point cannot be an endpoint of
the target interval. This completes the induction.
\end{proof}

Bounds alone do not prove sharpness. We next show that the intervals
contain no gap in the closures of the actual prefix weights.

\begin{lemma}[Uniqueness for the compact set recursion]\label{lem:hausdorff}
Let $K_0,K_1$ be nonempty compact subsets of $\R$ satisfying the state
equations. Then $K_0=I_0$ and $K_1=I_1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For compact nonempty subsets of $\R$, write $d_H$ for Hausdorff distance.
An affine map of slope $-q$ multiplies this distance by $q$.
Also, for finite indexed families,
\[
d_H\left(\bigcup_i A_i,\bigcup_i B_i\right)
\le\max_i d_H(A_i,B_i).
\]
Let $D=\max\{d_H(K_0,I_0),d_H(K_1,I_1)\}$. Apply these observations to
the two state equations for $K$ and $I$. They imply $D\le qD$.
Since $D$ is finite and $0<q<1$, it follows that $D=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact closures]\label{prop:closures}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closures}
\cl S_0=[-C,H-1],\qquad \cl S_1=[-qH,H].
\end{equation}
The closure of all prefix weights is $[-C,H]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:strict} makes $S_0,S_1$ bounded, and both are nonempty.
Take closures in their exact finite-union equations. Closure commutes
with each affine map and with a finite union, so $(\cl S_0,\cl S_1)$ is
a compact fixed pair. Lemma~\ref{lem:hausdorff} proves \eqref{eq:closures}.
Finally $I_0$ and $I_1$ overlap or touch, since
$H-1\ge-qH$ is again equivalent to $m\ge1$. Their union is $[-C,H]$.
\end{proof}

Distinct prefix lengths have distinct weights. Indeed, equality would
give $\Delta Z=q\Delta O$, impossible for irrational $q$ unless both
integer differences vanish, in which case the lengths agree. A set dense
in a nondegenerate interval has infinitely many distinct points in every
relative neighborhood of every point of that interval. Consequently its
density statements remain valid after deleting any finite number of
prefixes. This proves that the closures just obtained are also the full
sets of limits along prefix lengths tending to infinity.

\section{Position errors, densities, and exact transfer}\label{sec:positions}

\subsection{Passing from prefix weights to position errors}
Immediately before the $n$th one, the prefix has $n-1$ ones and
$v_m(n)-n$ zeros. Its weight is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-one}
X=v_m(n)-n-q(n-1)=q-E_1(n).
\end{equation}
Immediately before the $n$th zero, the prefix has $n-1$ zeros and
$u_m(n)-n$ ones, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weight-zero}
X=n-1-q(u_m(n)-n)=qE_0(n)-1.
\end{equation}
Apply the strict state bounds and Proposition~\ref{prop:closures}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:weight-one} transforms $I_1$ into
\[
[q-H,q+qH]=[C-m,C],
\]
and \eqref{eq:weight-zero} transforms $I_0$ into
$[(1-C)/q,C]$. The identities follow from \eqref{eq:identities}.
The state sets contain exactly the prefixes immediately before the
respective ranked letters, so no additional selection restriction is
needed. This proves the position part of Theorem~\ref{thm:general},
including strictness and the full subsequential limit sets.

For ordinary prefix counts,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-weight}
X=N-(1+q)O(N),\qquad
O(N)-\frac{N}{1+q}=-\frac{X}{1+q}.
\end{equation}
The total prefix-weight interval $[-C,H]$ proves
\eqref{eq:general-density} and its sharpness. Dividing the bounded
counting error by $N$ establishes the densities without invoking an
ergodic theorem. In the case $m=3$, this yields the quantitative estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density3}
-\frac{33+3\sqrt{21}}{50}
<O(N)-\frac{1+\sqrt{21}}{10}N
<\frac{27+7\sqrt{21}}{50}.
\end{equation}
Thus the density conjectures in all three relevant OEIS entries follow,
and the error in the unnormalized counting function is uniformly bounded.

\subsection{A relation between the two position sequences}
\begin{proposition}[Exact position transfer]\label{prop:transfer}
For $n\ge1$ and $1\le h\le m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
u_m(m(n-1)+h)=v_m(n)+(2m-1)n-2m+2h.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error-transfer}
E_0(m(n-1)+h)=E_1(n)-(m-h)(r-2).
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mn}
u_m(mn)=v_m(n)+(2m-1)n,\qquad E_0(mn)=E_1(n).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Before the parent word's $n$th one there are $n-1$ ones and
$v_m(n)-n$ zeros. The total length of their images is
$2m(n-1)+v_m(n)-n$. The image of the next one contains $m$ zeros, at
internal positions $2,4,\ldots,2m$, and no zero comes from the image of a
parent zero. Thus the $h$th such zero has global rank $m(n-1)+h$ and the
position in \eqref{eq:transfer}.

Subtract this expression from $r(m(n-1)+h)$. The identity
$m(r-2)=q$ reduces the result to \eqref{eq:error-transfer}.
\end{proof}

For $m=3$, $C<r$; explicitly
\[
r-C=\frac{9-\sqrt{21}}{30}>0.
\]
Since $r-2>0$, \eqref{eq:error-transfer} shows that the errors at
zero ranks $3(n-1)+1$ and $3(n-1)+2$ are strictly less than
$C-(r-2)<2$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:violations-bijection}
\{k:E_0(k)>2\}=\{3n:E_1(n)>2\}.
\end{equation}
Equality to $2$ is impossible, because $r$ and $s$ are irrational.
This bijection will make the zero-position minimality result immediate
once the first one-position violation has been certified.

\section{An exact finite certificate and the first counterexamples}\label{sec:certificate}

\subsection{Recursive blocks}
Define auxiliary finite words
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blocks}
W_{-1}=0,\quad W_0=1,\quad
W_k=(W_{k-1}W_{k-2})^m\quad(k\ge1).
\end{equation}
For $k\ge0$, induction gives $W_k=\sigma_m^k(1)$. In particular,
$W_k$ is a prefix of $\word^{(m)}$. Write $L_k,Z_k,O_k$ for its length,
zero count, and one count. Each satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-recurrence}
a_k=m(a_{k-1}+a_{k-2}).
\end{equation}
For the remainder of this section $m=3$.

Consider the finite word
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P-witness}
P=W_{11}\,W_{10}\,W_{10}\,W_8\,W_6\,W_4\,W_2.
\end{equation}
This is a prefix of $W_{12}$, followed by a $1$. Here is an explicit
way to follow the recursive decomposition. In
$W_{12}=(W_{11}W_{10})^3$, take the first $W_{11}W_{10}$, then enter the
next $W_{11}=(W_{10}W_9)^3$ and take its first $W_{10}$. The residual
word begins with $W_9$. In $W_9=(W_8W_7)^3$, take $W_8$, leaving a word

\section{Infinite extremal families and their generating functions}\label{sec:infinite}

The compact-set proof gives sharpness nonconstructively in terms of
closures. A specific family approaching the common upper endpoint can
also be written down and evaluated by short recurrences.

\begin{proposition}[Explicit extremal prefixes]\label{prop:extremal}
For general $m\ge1$, let $P_0$ be the empty word and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extremal-prefix}
P_{j+1}=\sigma_m^2(P_j)(10)^m,\qquad U_j=\sigma_m(P_j).
\end{equation}
Every $P_j$ and every $U_j$ is a prefix of the fixed word, followed by a
$1$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extremal-np}
n_j=|U_j|_1+1,\qquad p_j=|U_j|+1.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extremal-errors}
v_m(n_j)=p_j,\qquad
E_1(n_j)=C(1-q^{2j+2})\nearrow C.
\end{equation}
The zero-position errors at ranks $mn_j$ are equal to these errors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $P_j$ is a prefix followed by $1$. Its image is followed by
$(10)^m$. Therefore $\sigma_m(P_j)1$ is a prefix followed by $0$.
Apply the substitution again: the resulting prefix is
$\sigma_m^2(P_j)(10)^m=P_{j+1}$, followed by $\sigma_m(0)=1$.
This proves the prefix assertion inductively from $P_0$. It also shows
that $U_j$ is followed by $1$.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:contraction} and $m(1-q)=q^2$,
\[
X(P_{j+1})=q^2X(P_j)+q^2.
\]
Starting at zero and summing the geometric progression gives
$X(P_j)=H(1-q^{2j})$. Thus
\[
E_1(n_j)=q-X(U_j)=q+qH(1-q^{2j})
=C-Cq^{2j+2},
\]
where $q+qH=C$ and $qH=Cq^2$. The errors increase strictly because
$0<q<1$. The zero-position assertion follows from \eqref{eq:mn}.
\end{proof}

Let $(z_j,o_j)=(|P_j|_0,|P_j|_1)$. The preceding construction is fully
integer-recursive:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counts-extremal}
\begin{pmatrix}z_{j+1}\\o_{j+1}\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}m&m^2\\m&m^2+m\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}z_j\\o_j\end{pmatrix}
+\begin{pmatrix}m\\m\end{pmatrix},\qquad (z_0,o_0)=(0,0),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:np-fromcounts}
n_j=z_j+mo_j+1,\qquad p_j=z_j+2mo_j+1.
\end{equation}
Eliminating the two counts yields an especially simple result.

\begin{corollary}[Scalar recurrences and rational generating functions]
\label{cor:gf}
Put $T=m(m+2)$. Then
\begin{align}
n_{j+2}&=Tn_{j+1}-m^2n_j+1-m,
& n_0&=1,& n_1&=m^2+m+1,\label{eq:nrec}\\
p_{j+2}&=Tp_{j+1}-m^2p_j+m^2-m+1,
& p_0&=1,& p_1&=2m^2+m+1.\label{eq:prec}
\end{align}
Their ordinary generating functions are
\begin{align}
\sum_{j\ge0}n_jz^j
&=\frac{1-mz}{(1-z)(1-m(m+2)z+m^2z^2)},\label{eq:ngf}\\
\sum_{j\ge0}p_jz^j
&=\frac{1+m(m-1)z}{(1-z)(1-m(m+2)z+m^2z^2)}.\label{eq:pgf}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The linear part in \eqref{eq:counts-extremal} is $M^2$, with trace
$T$ and determinant $m^2$. Its characteristic identity implies the two
inhomogeneous recurrences after substitution in \eqref{eq:np-fromcounts};
alternatively, one may eliminate $z_j,o_j$ directly. The constant terms
and initial values follow by evaluating $j=0,1,2$.

For a sequence obeying $a_{j+2}=Ta_{j+1}-m^2a_j+c$, multiplying its
series by $1-Tz+m^2z^2$ gives
$a_0+(a_1-Ta_0)z+cz^2/(1-z)$. For \eqref{eq:nrec}, the numerator after
multiplication by $1-z$ reduces to $1-mz$; for \eqref{eq:prec}, it reduces
to $1+m(m-1)z$. This proves both generating functions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact asymptotics along the extremal family}
The denominator in \eqref{eq:ngf}--\eqref{eq:pgf} factors as
\[
(1-z)(1-\lambda^2z)(1-q^2z).
\]
Here $\lambda>1$ and $0<q<1$, so partial fractions give an exact
three-term expansion. For either sequence $a_j=n_j$ or $a_j=p_j$, let
\[
d=\frac{c}{1-2m},\quad
A=\frac{a_1-d-q^2(a_0-d)}{\lambda^2-q^2},\quad B=a_0-d-A,
\]
where $c$ is the corresponding constant in its recurrence. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-expansion}
a_j=A\lambda^{2j}+d+Bq^{2j}.
\end{equation}
The leading coefficient is positive, as follows either from these
expressions or the positive count recurrence. In particular,
$n_j,p_j=\Theta(\lambda^{2j})$, whereas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-rate}
C-E_1(n_j)=Cq^{2j+2}=\Theta(p_j^{-\kappa}),\qquad
\kappa=-\frac{\log q}{\log\lambda}.
\end{equation}
For $m=3$, $\kappa=0.17565278149\ldots$. This is a statement along the
constructed subsequence, not a claim about the optimal record-error
convergence rate over every prefix. The comparatively small exponent
explains why substantial growth in the rank can coexist with a slow
approach to the limiting extremum.

\section{Exact algorithms and executed checks}\label{sec:algorithms}

\subsection{Quadratic arithmetic without rounding decisions}
Every prefix weight and every one-position error is of the form $a+bq$
with integer $a,b$. Since $q^2=m-mq$, addition and multiplication stay
in this representation. To compare $a+bq$ with zero, put
\[
A=2a-mb,\qquad D=m^2+4m.
\]
Its sign is the sign of $A+b\sqrt D$. If $A$ and $b$ have the same
nonzero sign, that sign is the answer. Zero cases are immediate. If
they have opposite signs, the answer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign-rule}
\sgn(A)\,\sgn(A^2-Db^2).
\end{equation}
As $D$ is nonsquare, there is no unhandled nontrivial equality case.
Thus all block-extremum decisions and counterexample inequalities in the
software reduce to integer arithmetic. Zero-position errors may contain
$1/q$; multiplying their comparisons by the positive number $q$ puts
them in the same representation.

\subsection{Associative summaries and random access}
A block summary stores its zero and one counts, and for each following
letter state the minimum and maximum prefix weight, together with a
location attaining the extremum. Under concatenation $UV$, the extrema
from $V$ are translated by $X(U)$ and their positions by $|U|$; the
appropriate minimum or maximum is then taken with the extrema from $U$.
This is the same identity as \eqref{eq:min-concat} in both states and
both directions.

Precompute summaries for $W_{-1},W_0,\ldots,W_k$ using
\eqref{eq:blocks}. To count the first $N$ letters, find the first block
long enough, skip whole child pairs by their lengths, and descend into
the residual child. To find the $n$th specified letter, use its block
counts instead of lengths. At a fixed $m$, there are $O(\log N)$ levels,
and each level involves only a bounded number of arithmetic operations.
The library groups repeated child pairs for these operations.

To find the first error at least a threshold $B$, a block beginning after
a prefix of weight $x$ can be discarded exactly when its appropriate
extremum cannot reach the threshold. For ones the feasibility condition is
\[
x+\mu_1(W_k)\le q-B.
\]
For zeros, using the maximum prefix weight $\nu_0(W_k)$ before a zero,
the condition is
\[
x+\nu_0(W_k)\ge qB-1.
\]
Find a first feasible enclosing block, then inspect children from left to
right and descend into the first feasible child, skipping the others by
their counts. The returned leaf is the first violation, and the skipped
block extrema certify that no earlier one was missed.

For fixed $m$ and a counterexample at a position of size $N$, the
precomputation and descent use $O(\log N)$ arithmetic operations on
$O(\log N)$-bit integers. This is an arithmetic-operation count, not a
unit-cost bit-complexity assertion. With $\mathsf M(b)$ denoting the cost
of multiplying $b$-bit integers, the sign tests give the conservative
bound $O(\log N\,\mathsf M(\log N))$ bit operations, alongside additions
and small-parameter divisions. Storing all summaries uses
$O((\log N)^2)$ bits at fixed $m$. Searching only up to a prescribed
level and finding nothing is a finite negative result, not a proof of
nonexistence in the infinite word.

\section{Further consequences and boundaries of the result}\label{sec:consequences}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$m=1$}{m=1} endpoint is a Beatty pair}
At $m=1$, $q=(\sqrt5-1)/2$ and $C=1$. Both error intervals become
$(0,1)$. Since the positions are integers and the slopes are irrational,
\[
v_1(n)=\lfloor(1+q)n\rfloor,\qquad
u_1(n)=\left\lfloor\left(1+\frac1q\right)n\right\rfloor.
\]
Thus the familiar complementary Beatty description is recovered directly
from the theorem. For $m\ge2$, no fixed real shift $\gamma$ can give
$v_m(n)=\lfloor sn+\gamma\rfloor$ for every $n$: such an expression would
put all its errors in an interval of length $1$, whereas the error
closure in \eqref{eq:general-one} has length exactly $m$. The density
already forces any putative Beatty slope to be $s$.

\subsection{A finite factor-balance bound}
The centered prefix-counting errors have range width
\[
\frac{C+H}{1+q}=C.
\]
For a factor of length $L$, its number of ones minus $L/s$ is the
difference of two prefix errors, so it lies strictly between $-C$ and
$C$. Two factors of the same length consequently differ in their numbers
of ones by an integer whose absolute value is strictly less than $2C$.
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balance-bound}
\bigl||A|_1-|B|_1\bigr|\le\lceil2C\rceil-1
\qquad\text{whenever }|A|=|B|.
\end{equation}
For $m=3$ this gives a balance bound of $4$. The article does not assert
that this bound is optimal: sharp prefix extrema do not automatically
occur at two endpoints separated by the same prescribed factor length.
The exact optimal factor-balance constant is a separate question.

\subsection{The word itself has a nonrational generating function}
Although the extremal-index series \eqref{eq:ngf}--\eqref{eq:pgf} are
rational, the characteristic series
\[
F_m(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}w^{(m)}_n z^n
\]
is not rational. Indeed, a rational power series has coefficients obeying
an eventual fixed linear recurrence. If those coefficients take values
in a finite set, the vector of a fixed number of consecutive coefficients
has only finitely many states and deterministically produces its successor.
A state eventually repeats, making the sequence eventually periodic.
An eventually periodic binary sequence has rational density, contradicting
$1/(1+q)$, which is irrational. This elementary observation concerns the
full word, not the rational generating functions of the sparse families.

\subsection{What remains outside the claims}
The conjectured upper bounds and density statements selected in
Section~\ref{sec:problem} are resolved by the preceding proofs. The
article does not determine the frequency distribution of discrepancy
values within their interval, the density of the ranks with error above
$2$, or the exact best factor-balance constant. Knowing the support of
subsequential limits does not by itself answer those distributional
questions. Nor is the constructed extremal subsequence claimed to enumerate
all record errors. These distinctions prevent stronger statements from
being inferred merely from the observed finite data or the interval
closure result.

\section{Conclusion}
A conjecture about an error below $2$ can survive a substantial finite
check while failing only slightly at a much larger index. Here the first
one-position violation occurs at rank $2\,977\,771$, and its transfer
produces the first zero-position violation at rank $8\,933\,313$.
The finite disproof is certified by a short recursive prefix and two
integer square comparisons. The structural analysis goes further: the
correct common upper bound is $(6+\sqrt{21})/5$, and a two-state contraction
proves exact discrepancy intervals for every $0\mapsto1$,
$1\mapsto(10)^m$. Density, explicit extremal families, rational
index-generating functions, and efficient exact verification then follow
from the same framework.

\paragraph{Summary (the manuscript's abstract).}
We study the fixed point of the binary substitution
\[
  \tau_{a,b}(0)=1,\qquad \tau_{a,b}(1)=1\,0^a1^b,
  \qquad a,b\geq1.
\]
Let
\[
q=\frac{\sqrt{(b+1)^2+4a}-(b+1)}2.
\]
The non-Perron eigenvalue of the incidence matrix is $-q$.  We prove a
sharp phase transition: prefix discrepancy, and hence the errors in the
positions of zeros and ones, is bounded exactly when $a\leq b+1$.
Throughout that bounded regime we determine the four exact state-dependent
extrema.  Writing
\[
 {\widehat C}=\frac{a-q}{1-q^2},
\]
the closures of the position-error sets have endpoints
\[
q-{\widehat C},\quad q(1+{\widehat C})
\]
for positions of ones, and
\[
\frac1q-{\widehat C}-1,\quad \frac Cq
\]
for positions of zeros.  None of these endpoints is attained, and all are
subsequential limits.

The specializations $(a,b)=(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$ are OEIS A284368 and A284369.
For A284368 we recover the sharper bounds
\[
\frac{\sqrt{13}-5}{3}<E_1(n)<\frac{\sqrt{13}-2}{3},\qquad
\frac{1+\sqrt{13}}3<E_0(n)<\frac{4+\sqrt{13}}3.
\]
For A284369 we prove
\[
-2<E_1(n)<1+\sqrt3,\qquad
-\frac{3+\sqrt3}{2}<E_0(n)<2+\sqrt3.
\]
The latter resolves the still-posted conjectures for its position sequences
A284370 and A284371, with strictly stronger constants.  In both OEIS cases
the complete state attractors are intervals, so the displayed endpoints are
not merely estimates but the exact error-value envelopes.  We also give
constructive extremal subsequences, rational generating functions for their
indices, a standard-library exact verifier, and a research program for the
remaining interval-versus-Cantor and distribution questions.

\begin{remark}[The two families, compared]\label{qmw:rem:partI}
\emph{The families are disjoint.} The word $(10)^m$ has the form
$1\,0^a1^b$ only for $m=1$, with $(a,b)=(1,0)$, which Part~II excludes
($a,b\geq1$). So Part~II neither contains, re-proves nor refines Part~I's
theorems for A284364--A284366, and it does not say it does.

\emph{The same quadratic fields occur.} For $(a,b)=(m,m-1)$ the incidence
matrix of $\tau_{a,b}$ is $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&m\\1&m\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
the matrix of $\sigma_m$, so $q$, $\lambda$, $r$ and $s$ coincide; the words
and the envelopes differ. For $m=3$, Part~I gives one-position errors in
$(C-3,C)\approx(-0.8835,2.1165)$ and zero-position errors in
$((1-C)/q,C)\approx(-1.4110,2.1165)$, while Theorem~\ref{qmw:thm:general}
for $(a,b)=(3,2)$ gives $(q-\widehat C,q(1+\widehat C))\approx(-5.1165,5.4661)$
and $(1/q-\widehat C-1,\widehat C/q)\approx(-5.6440,7.4661)$.

\emph{A formal consistency check.} Setting $(a,b)=(1,0)$ in the formulas of
Theorem~\ref{qmw:thm:general}, outside its hypotheses, gives
$q=(\sqrt5-1)/2$, $\widehat C=q$, and the interval $(0,1)$ for both letters:
the complementary Beatty pair of Part~I's case $m=1$
(Section~\ref{sec:consequences}). Part~II's proof does not cover this case
(it uses $\widehat C-q^2(\widehat C+1)=qb>0$).

\emph{Method.} Both Parts rest on the contracting weight $X=|P|_0-q|P|_1$
with $X(\text{image})=-qX$ and on states recording the next letter (Part~I,
Sections~\ref{sec:weight}--\ref{sec:intervals}; Part~II,
Sections~\ref{qmw:sec:states}--\ref{qmw:sec:extrema}). Part~II solves a
four-variable Bellman system for the hulls and treats the question whether
the attractors fill them separately (Section~\ref{qmw:sec:intervals}),
whereas in Part~I the intervals are invariant for every $m$.

\emph{Open questions.} Part~II's research question~3 (densities of ranks with
error above a threshold) is the question Part~I leaves open in
Section~\ref{sec:consequences}; it is not answered in either Part.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp OEIS envelopes]\label{qmw:thm:oeis-main}
For the word A284368, put
\[
 E_0(n)=n\frac{5+\sqrt{13}}2-u(n),\qquad
 E_1(n)=n\frac{\sqrt{13}-1}{2}-v(n).
\]
Then, for every $n\geq1$,
\begin{align}
\frac{1+\sqrt{13}}3&<E_0(n)<\frac{4+\sqrt{13}}3,
\label{qmw:eq:sharp368zero}\\
\frac{\sqrt{13}-5}{3}&<E_1(n)<\frac{\sqrt{13}-2}{3}.
\label{qmw:eq:sharp368one}
\end{align}
The closures of the two error-value sets are the corresponding closed
intervals, and every displayed endpoint is a subsequential limit.
Consequently both conjectures in \eqref{qmw:eq:oeis368} hold, with substantial
room to spare.

For the word A284369, put
\[
 F_0(n)=n\frac{3+\sqrt3}{2}-u(n),\qquad
 F_1(n)=n\sqrt3-v(n).
\]
Then, for every $n\geq1$,
\begin{align}
-\frac{3+\sqrt3}{2}&<F_0(n)<2+\sqrt3,
\label{qmw:eq:sharp369zero}\\
-2&<F_1(n)<1+\sqrt3.
\label{qmw:eq:sharp369one}
\end{align}
Again the closures are exactly the corresponding closed intervals, with all
four endpoints occurring as subsequential limits.  Hence
\eqref{qmw:eq:oeis369-zero}--\eqref{qmw:eq:oeis369-one} are true, and the constants
in them can be sharpened to those above.
\end{theorem}

The A284368 intervals have width exactly one, so they also recover explicit
floor formulas.

\section{The two-parameter family}\label{qmw:sec:family}

Fix integers $a,b\geq1$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:tauab}
\tau_{a,b}(0)=1,
\qquad
\tau_{a,b}(1)=1\,0^a1^b.
\end{equation}
Because the image of $1$ begins with $1$, the iterates
$\tau_{a,b}^k(1)$ are nested prefixes of a fixed infinite word
$\word^{(a,b)}$.  The substitution is primitive.  The relation between
subdominant substitution eigenvalues and discrepancy is classical
\cite{adam2003,adamczewski}; the point here is to solve the legal-prefix state
system exactly for this family.

Its incidence matrix, with rows and columns ordered $0,1$, is
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:matrix}
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\1&b+1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:q-lambda}
q=\frac{\sqrt{(b+1)^2+4a}-(b+1)}2,
\qquad
\lambda=b+1+q=\frac aq.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:qpoly}
q^2+(b+1)q=a,
\end{equation}
and the eigenvalues of $M$ are $\lambda$ and $-q$.  A positive right
Perron eigenvector is $(q,1)^T$, so the zero and one frequencies are
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:frequencies}
\delta=\frac q{1+q},
\qquad
\rho=\frac1{1+q}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding position slopes are
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:slopes}
r=\frac1\delta=1+\frac1q,
\qquad
s=\frac1\rho=1+q.
\end{equation}

The simple inequality
\[
q<1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sqrt{(b+1)^2+4a}<b+3
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a<b+2
\]
gives, for integer parameters,
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:q-threshold}
q<1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\leq b+1.
\end{equation}
This is exactly the bounded-discrepancy regime.

\begin{theorem}[General sharp envelope theorem]\label{qmw:thm:general}
Assume $a,b\geq1$ and $a\leq b+1$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:Cdef}
{\widehat C}=\frac{a-q}{1-q^2}.
\end{equation}
Let $u(n)$ and $v(n)$ be the positions of the $n$th zero and one in
$\word^{(a,b)}$, and let
\[
E_0(n)=\left(1+\frac1q\right)n-u(n),
\qquad
E_1(n)=(1+q)n-v(n).
\]
Then for every $n\geq1$,
\begin{align}
\frac1q-{\widehat C}-1&<E_0(n)<\frac Cq,
\label{qmw:eq:generalE0}\\
q-{\widehat C}&<E_1(n)<q(1+{\widehat C}).
\label{qmw:eq:generalE1}
\end{align}
Each endpoint is sharp and is approached by an explicit infinite
subsequence.  No endpoint is attained at a finite index.

The centered one-count discrepancy satisfies, for every prefix length
$N\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:count-discrepancy}
-\frac{{\widehat C}}{1+q}
< O(N)-\frac{N}{1+q}
< \frac{q({\widehat C}+1)}{1+q},
\end{equation}
where $O(N)$ counts ones in the first $N$ letters.  Both constants are sharp.
In particular the densities in \eqref{qmw:eq:frequencies} exist.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral phase transition]\label{qmw:thm:phase}
For the family \eqref{qmw:eq:tauab}, the prefix discrepancy is bounded if and
only if $a\leq b+1$.  If $a=b+2$, there are prefixes whose discrepancy grows
linearly with substitution depth, hence at least logarithmically with prefix
length.  If $a\geq b+3$, there are prefixes whose discrepancy grows
geometrically with substitution depth.
\end{theorem}

Sections~\ref{qmw:sec:states}--\ref{qmw:sec:extrema} prove these theorems.  The
specific interval attractors needed for Theorem~\ref{qmw:thm:oeis-main} are
resolved in Section~\ref{qmw:sec:intervals}.

\section{Contracting weights and exact prefix states}\label{qmw:sec:states}

For a finite binary word $P$, define the quadratic weight
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:weight}
X(P)=\zeros{P}-q\ones{P}.
\end{equation}
It is additive under concatenation.

\begin{lemma}[Weight eigenrelation]\label{qmw:lem:weight}
For every finite word $P$,
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:contraction}
X(\tau_{a,b}(P))=-qX(P).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the letter $0$, the two sides both equal $-q$.  For the letter $1$,
\[
X(1\,0^a1^b)=a-q(b+1)=q^2=-qX(1),
\]
using \eqref{qmw:eq:qpoly}.  Additivity proves the identity for any word.
\end{proof}

A prefix must remember the letter immediately following it.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:states}
S_i=\{X(P):P\text{ is a prefix of }\word^{(a,b)}
\text{ and the next letter is }i\},
\qquad i\in\{0,1\},
\end{equation}
and let $K_i=\cl S_i$.

\begin{lemma}[Graph-directed state equations]\label{qmw:lem:state-equations}
If $q<1$, the compact sets $K_0,K_1$ are the unique nonempty compact solution
of
\begin{align}
K_0&=\bigcup_{j=0}^{a-1}\bigl(-qK_1+j-q\bigr),
\label{qmw:eq:K0}\\
K_1&=(-qK_0)\ \cup\ (-qK_1)\ \cup\!
\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{b}\bigl(-qK_1+a-\ell q\bigr).
\label{qmw:eq:K1}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $P$ be a parent prefix and suppose the next parent letter is $1$.
Inside its image $1\,0^a1^b$, the prefixes immediately before the zeros are
\[
1,\ 10,\ \ldots,\ 10^{a-1},
\]
with weights $j-q$ for $0\leq j<a$.  The prefixes immediately before the
initial one and the $b$ trailing ones have weights
\[
0,\quad a-q,\quad a-2q,\quad\ldots,\quad a-bq.
\]
If the next parent letter is $0$, its image is the single letter $1$, so the
only transition is to state $1$ with internal weight $0$.  Combining these
internal weights with Lemma~\ref{qmw:lem:weight} gives
\eqref{qmw:eq:K0}--\eqref{qmw:eq:K1}.

Conversely, every position lies in one of these image blocks, so no
transitions are missing.  Since all affine maps have contraction ratio $q<1$,
the graph-directed Hutchinson operator is a contraction in the maximum
Hausdorff metric.  It therefore has a unique compact fixed pair, and the
closures of the actual prefix-state sets form that pair.
\end{proof}

The relation
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:min-digit}
a-bq=q(q+1)>0
\end{equation}
follows from \eqref{qmw:eq:qpoly}.  Thus the smallest state-$1$ internal digit is
$0$ and the largest is $a-q$.  This observation makes the extrema solvable in
closed form even when the attractors themselves have gaps.

\section{Solving the Bellman extremum system}\label{qmw:sec:extrema}

Write
\[
\ell_i=\min K_i,
\qquad
u_i=\max K_i.
\]
Because multiplication by $-q$ reverses order, equations
\eqref{qmw:eq:K0}--\eqref{qmw:eq:K1} give
\begin{align}
\ell_0&=-qu_1-q,
&u_0&=-q\ell_1+a-1-q,
\label{qmw:eq:bellman0}\\
\ell_1&=\min\{-qu_0,-qu_1\},
&u_1&=\max\{-q\ell_0,-q\ell_1+a-q\}.
\label{qmw:eq:bellman1}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Exact state extrema]\label{qmw:prop:extrema}
In the bounded regime $q<1$, with ${\widehat C}$ as in \eqref{qmw:eq:Cdef},
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:state-extrema}
\boxed{
\ell_0=-q({\widehat C}+1),\qquad
u_0={\widehat C}-1,
\qquad
\ell_1=-qC,
\qquad
u_1={\widehat C}.}
\end{equation}
None of these four values belongs to the corresponding unclosed state set
$S_i$, but each is a limit of values in that state.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The extremum map defined by the right sides of
\eqref{qmw:eq:bellman0}--\eqref{qmw:eq:bellman1} is $q$-Lipschitz in the supremum
norm, because minima and maxima are nonexpansive.  It therefore has at most
one fixed point.

We verify the displayed candidate.  Since $u_1={\widehat C}> {\widehat C}-1=u_0$,
\[
\ell_1=-qu_1=-qC.
\]
Also
\[
-q\ell_1+a-q=q^2{\widehat C}+a-q={\widehat C}
\]
by ${\widehat C}(1-q^2)=a-q$.  The competing maximum branch is
\[
-q\ell_0=q^2({\widehat C}+1)<{\widehat C},
\]
because
\[
{\widehat C}-q^2({\widehat C}+1)=a-q-q^2=qb>0.
\]
Thus $u_1={\widehat C}$.  The formulas for $\ell_0$ and $u_0$ now follow directly;
for the latter,
\[
-q\ell_1+a-1-q=q^2{\widehat C}+a-q-1={\widehat C}-1.
\]
Uniqueness proves \eqref{qmw:eq:state-extrema}.

For nonattainment, suppose first that an actual state-$1$ prefix had weight
${\widehat C}$.  The strict inequality between the two maximum branches forces its
parent to have state-$1$ weight $-qC$ and to use the digit $a-q$.  Attaining
$-qC$ in turn forces a parent of state $1$ and weight ${\widehat C}$, using digit $0$.
Two desubstitutions therefore return to the same extremal requirement while
strictly shortening a nonempty prefix, an infinite-descent contradiction.
The same argument starts from $-qC$.  Attainment of either state-$0$ endpoint
would force attainment of the corresponding state-$1$ endpoint through
\eqref{qmw:eq:K0}.  Finally, iterating the two extremizing transitions from the
empty prefix constructs sequences converging to the endpoints, so they are
sharp.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counting discrepancy}

If $P$ has length $N$, then
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:Xcount}
X(P)=N-(1+q)O(N),
\qquad
O(N)-\frac{N}{1+q}=-\frac{X(P)}{1+q}.
\end{equation}
Across both states, Proposition~\ref{qmw:prop:extrema} gives
\[
-q({\widehat C}+1)<X(P)<{\widehat C},
\]
which is exactly \eqref{qmw:eq:count-discrepancy}.

\subsection{Position errors}

Suppose a prefix $P$ is immediately followed by the $n$th one.  Then
$\ones P=n-1$ and $\abs P=v(n)-1$, so
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:E1X}
E_1(n)=(1+q)n-v(n)=q-X(P).
\end{equation}
Because $X(P)\in S_1$, Proposition~\ref{qmw:prop:extrema} yields
\[
q-{\widehat C}<E_1(n)<q+qC=q(1+{\widehat C}).
\]

Similarly, immediately before the $n$th zero one has
$\zeros P=n-1$ and $\abs P=u(n)-1$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:E0X}
E_0(n)=\left(1+\frac1q\right)n-u(n)=\frac{X(P)+1}{q}.
\end{equation}
Using $X(P)\in S_0$ gives
\[
\frac1q-{\widehat C}-1<E_0(n)<\frac Cq.
\]
This proves Theorem~\ref{qmw:thm:general}.

\section{The bounded-discrepancy phase transition}\label{qmw:sec:phase}

The preceding contraction proof establishes bounded discrepancy for $q<1$.
To prove that this condition is necessary, one explicit state-$1$ cycle is
enough.

Let $P_0$ be the empty prefix and recursively define
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:Pk}
P_{k+1}=\tau_{a,b}^2(P_k)\,1\,0^a.
\end{equation}
Every $P_k$ is a prefix immediately before a one.  If $x_k=X(P_k)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:xrec}
x_0=0,
\qquad
x_{k+1}=q^2x_k+a-q.
\end{equation}
The two steps are: use the zero internal digit before the initial one, then
use the largest state-$1$ digit $a-q$ before the first trailing one.

When $q<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:xclosed}
x_k={\widehat C}(1-q^{2k})\longrightarrow {\widehat C},
\end{equation}
which also gives an explicit sharpness sequence.  When $q=1$,
\[
x_k=k(a-1)=k(b+1),
\]
so discrepancy is unbounded linearly in depth.  When $q>1$, the solution of
\eqref{qmw:eq:xrec} grows geometrically like $q^{2k}$.  Together with
\eqref{qmw:eq:q-threshold}, this proves Theorem~\ref{qmw:thm:phase}.

At the boundary $q=1$, substitution lengths grow exponentially in $k$ while
$x_k$ grows linearly, giving a logarithmic lower bound in the physical prefix
length.  A matching $O(\log N)$ upper bound follows by desubstituting any
prefix: every twofold image has length at least $3$, each internal correction
is bounded, and the multiplier has absolute value one.  For $q>1$, the
constructed prefixes satisfy $\abs{P_k}\asymp\lambda^{2k}$ and
$X(P_k)\asymp q^{2k}$, hence exhibit the power law

\section{When the state attractors are full intervals}\label{qmw:sec:intervals}

Proposition~\ref{qmw:prop:extrema} determines the hulls of $K_0,K_1$ for every
bounded parameter pair.  It does not assert that the compact sets fill their
hulls.  Indeed, for some larger parameters the first-level images already
leave gaps.  The two OEIS cases are especially tractable: one is an exact
tiling and the other has ample overlap.

\subsection{A284368: an exact interval tiling}

Here $(a,b)=(1,2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:q13C13}
q=\frac{\sqrt{13}-3}{2},
\qquad
{\widehat C}=\frac{\sqrt{13}+1}{6}.
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{qmw:prop:extrema} gives the candidate intervals
\begin{align}
I_0&=\left[\frac{2-\sqrt{13}}3,\frac{\sqrt{13}-5}{6}\right],
\label{qmw:eq:I0-13}\\
I_1&=\left[\frac{\sqrt{13}-5}{6},\frac{\sqrt{13}+1}{6}\right].
\label{qmw:eq:I1-13}

\subsection{A284369: overlapping interval covers}

Here $(a,b)=(2,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:q3C3}
q=\sqrt3-1,
\qquad
{\widehat C}=1+\sqrt3.
\end{equation}
The state hulls are
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:I3}
I_0=[-1-\sqrt3,\sqrt3],
\qquad
I_1=[-2,1+\sqrt3].
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:zero-images3}
[-1-\sqrt3,\sqrt3-1],
\qquad

\end{equation}

\section{Constructive extremal subsequences and generating functions}
\label{qmw:sec:constructive}

The cycle \eqref{qmw:eq:Pk} not only proves sharpness but gives explicit indices.
Let
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:countvector}
c_k=\begin{pmatrix}z_k\\o_k\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\zeros{P_k}\\\ones{P_k}\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Since the suffix $10^a$ has count vector $(a,1)^T$,
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:vectorrec}
c_0=0,
\qquad
c_{k+1}=M^2c_k+\begin{pmatrix}a\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The next one has rank $o_k+1$ and position $z_k+o_k+1$.  Its error is
$q-x_k$, which decreases to $q-{\widehat C}$.  To exhibit all four endpoints explicitly,
put
\[
 Q_k=\tau_{a,b}(P_k),\qquad
 R_k=\tau_{a,b}(P_k)1,\qquad
 T_k=\tau_{a,b}(Q_k)1\,0^{a-1}.
\]
Then $Q_k$ is in state $1$ with weight $-qx_k\to-qC$, so its one-position
errors tend to $q(1+{\widehat C})$.  The prefixes $R_k$ and $T_k$ are in state $0$ and
have respective weights
\[
 -qx_k-q\longrightarrow-q({\widehat C}+1),
 \qquad
 -qX(Q_k)+a-1-q\longrightarrow {\widehat C}-1.
\]
Their zero-position errors therefore tend to $1/q-{\widehat C}-1$ and ${\widehat C}/q$.
This supplies concrete infinite subsequences for every endpoint in
Theorem~\ref{qmw:thm:general}.

The vector generating function is rational:
\begin{equation}\label{qmw:eq:vectorGF}
\sum_{k\geq0}c_kz^k
=\frac{z}{1-z}(I-zM^2)^{-1}
\begin{pmatrix}a\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Let $N_k=z_k+o_k=\abs{P_k}$.  In the two OEIS cases,
\begin{align}
\sum_{k\geq0}N_kz^k
&=\frac{z(2-5z)}{(1-z)(1-11z+z^2)}
&&\text{for A284368},
\label{qmw:eq:GF368}\\
\sum_{k\geq0}N_kz^k
&=\frac{3z(1-2z)}{(1-z)(1-8z+4z^2)}
&&\text{for A284369}.
\label{qmw:eq:GF369}
\end{align}
The first terms are
\begin{align*}
\text{A284368: }&0,2,19,204,2222,24235,264360,2883722,\ldots,\\
\text{A284369: }&0,3,21,153,1137,8481,63297,472449,\ldots.
\end{align*}
Thus the corresponding homogeneous recurrences are
\begin{align*}
N_k&=12N_{k-1}-12N_{k-2}+N_{k-3}
&&\text{for A284368},\\
N_k&=9N_{k-1}-12N_{k-2}+4N_{k-3}
&&\text{for A284369}.
\end{align*}
These formulas make the sharpness sequences reproducible without building the
full fixed words.

\section{Further research questions}\label{qmw:sec:further}

The two-state reduction exposes several problems that are both concrete and
substantially broader than the four resolved inequalities.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=5pt]
\item \textbf{Interval-versus-Cantor classification.}
Determine necessary and sufficient conditions on $(a,b)$, with $a\leq b+1$,
for $K_0$ and $K_1$ to equal their convex hulls.  First-level overlap gives
explicit inequalities, but deeper exact overlaps can close apparent gaps.
A finite-state classification may exist because all translations lie in the
same quadratic field.

\item \textbf{Invariant error distributions.}
The present paper identifies the support and its endpoints.  Determine the
limiting empirical measures of $E_0(n)$ and $E_1(n)$, their continuity or
singularity, moments, and local dimensions.  In interval cases, decide
whether the measures are absolutely continuous or have self-similar density
singularities.

every word with $a$ zeros and $a-1$ ones beginning with $1$, Part~III
determines the law of the prefix weight under uniform prefix sampling:
exact complete-block histograms (Theorem~\ref{bmf:thm:histogram}),
Gaussian and local limit laws at every cutoff
(Theorems~\ref{bmf:thm:block-normal} and~\ref{bmf:thm:all-cutoff}) and
large deviations (Theorem~\ref{bmf:thm:ldp}). The bounded regime $a\leq b+1$
asked about here remains open (Question~1 of
Section~\ref{bmf:sec:research}).]

\item \textbf{Threshold densities and record statistics.}
For a fixed threshold $t$, determine the natural density of ranks with
$E_i(n)>t$, when it exists.  This directly continues an open direction in
Part~I (A284364--A284366; Section~\ref{sec:consequences}), where sharp supports are known but violation
densities are not.
[Added 6 October 2026, batch~114: at $a=b+2$, where the errors are
unbounded, \eqref{bmf:eq:threshold-density} gives the frequency of prefixes
with $X(n)\geq x\log_aN$ up to a factor $N^{o(1)}$, namely
$N^{-I_a(x)/\log a+o(1)}$. The densities asked for here, in the bounded
regime, remain open.]

\item \textbf{Exact first-hitting algorithms.}
Develop automata or Ostrowski-type numeration systems that return the first
rank at which a prescribed algebraic error interval is entered.  The graph
system suggests a branch-and-bound algorithm with exact quadratic endpoints.

makes a single ordinary Beatty formula impossible, but structured finite
combinations may remain possible.

Compute the optimal constant controlling the difference in letter counts
between arbitrary equal-length factors.  Prefix envelopes give immediate
coarse bounds, but the sharp factor constant requires the difference set
$K_i-K_j$ together with legal endpoint states.

\item \textbf{Critical and supercritical normalization.}
At $a=b+2$, determine the exact limsup and liminf after division by

\section{Conclusion}

The position conjectures for A284369 are not isolated numerical accidents.
They are the $(a,b)=(2,1)$ specialization of a two-parameter theorem whose
constants are forced by a four-variable contracting Bellman system.  The same
theorem gives an independent, sharper resolution of the neighboring A284368
bounds and reveals a clean spectral transition at $a=b+2$.

The main structural lesson is that the non-Perron eigenvalue gives the
contraction rate, but sharp constants require legal prefix states.  Once those
states are retained, the exact extrema collapse to the single algebraic
constant ${\widehat C}=(a-q)/(1-q^2)$.  Whether the resulting compact sets fill their
hulls is a separate overlap problem: it succeeds by exact tiling for A284368,
by overlap for A284369, and fails already for some nearby parameters.  That
separation between sharp envelopes and internal fractal geometry provides a
natural next research program.

\section{The question, the answer, and its provenance}\label{bmf:sec:intro}

A morphism replaces each letter of a finite word by another finite
word and concatenates the replacements. If the image of $1$ begins
with $1$ and grows, its iterates give longer and longer prefixes of
one infinite word. Such a word can be completely deterministic while
its prefix counts display substantial statistical variation.

The starting point is the ProveIt report \emph{Sharp discrepancy for
two families of binary substitutions}, specifically its Part~II
(Sections~\ref{qmw:sec:oeis}--\ref{qmw:sec:further} of this report, read
by the manuscript at commit \texttt{112d6bed}; see
Section~\ref{bmf:sec:provenance}). Its family is
\eqref{qmw:eq:tauab}, repeated here:
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:source-family}
 \tau_{a,b}(0)=1,\qquad \tau_{a,b}(1)=1\,0^a1^b,\qquad a,b\geq1.
\end{equation}
Writing
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:source-spectrum}
 q=\frac{\sqrt{(b+1)^2+4a}-(b+1)}2,\qquad
 \lambda=b+1+q,
\end{equation}
the incidence eigenvalues are $\lambda$ and $-q$. The predecessor
establishes bounded discrepancy for $q<1$, logarithmic extremal growth
at $q=1$, and power growth for $q>1$ (Theorem~\ref{qmw:thm:phase} and
Section~\ref{qmw:sec:phase}). Its research question 7
(Section~\ref{qmw:sec:further}) asks for
the exact critical limsup and liminf and the supercritical normalized
limit sets. Our principal critical results answer the first part
exactly and extend it to every arrangement with the same incidence
matrix. Section~\ref{bmf:sec:supercritical} answers the supercritical
part as well, including a closed formula for its endpoints.

\subsection{The main concrete conclusions}

Let $X_a(N)$ be the number of zeros minus the number of ones among
the first $N$ letters of the fixed point of \eqref{bmf:eq:run-family}.
We prove
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:intro-extremes}
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{X_a(N)}{\log N}
 =-\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{X_a(N)}{\log N}
 =\frac{a-1}{2\log a}.
\end{equation}
Every value between these two endpoints is a subsequential limit.
If $U_N$ is uniform on $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:intro-clt}
 \frac{X_a(U_N)}
 {\sqrt{((a^2-1)/12)\log_a N}}
 \ \Longrightarrow\ {\cal N}(0,1).
\end{equation}
Thus typical errors are of order $\sqrt{\log N}$, whereas extremal
errors are of order $\log N$. These are different assertions about
the same deterministic word.

The exact pressure controlling deviations on the larger scale is
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:intro-pressure}
 \chi_a(s)=
 \log\!\left(\frac{\sinh(as/2)}{a\sinh(s/2)}\right),
 \qquad \chi_a(0)=0.
\end{equation}
In particular, the frequency of a prescribed positive
logarithmic-size deviation is a power of $N$ with an explicitly
determined exponent; see Section~\ref{bmf:sec:pressure}.

All three conclusions arise from one exact finite identity, rather
than extrapolation from sampled prefixes.

In the supercritical range $a\geq b+3$, set
$\alpha=\log q/\log\lambda$. For the eigenweight
$X_q(N)=\#0-q\#1$, we obtain the complete cluster interval
\[
 \operatorname{Clust}_{N\to\infty}\frac{X_q(N)}{N^\alpha}
 =[-\mathcal C_{a,b},\mathcal C_{a,b}],
\]
with the explicit constant
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:intro-superconstant}
 \mathcal C_{a,b}=
 \frac{\lambda-1}{q^2-1}
 \left(\frac{(\lambda+q)(\lambda^2-1)}{(q+1)^2}\right)^\alpha.
\end{equation}
For example, $0\mapsto1$, $1\mapsto1\,0^8\,1$ has
$q=2$, $\lambda=4$, $\alpha=1/2$ and sharp endpoints
$\pm\sqrt{10}$.

\subsection{What is established theory and what is supplied here}

Adamczewski's work supplies the general relation between substitution
spectra and bounded or logarithmic discrepancy
\cite{adam2003,adamczewski}. Paquette and Son, Theorem 3.3, already prove
the existence of a Gaussian limit for noncoboundary eigenfunctions
with eigenvalue of modulus one, using a uniformly selected prefix
and arbitrary cutoffs \cite{paquette}. It applies to our class.
Our Gaussian limit is therefore an explicit evaluation and refinement
of that theorem, with an elementary independent-summand proof.

The telescoping identity used below is a one-variable specialization
of the invariant-section identity attributed to Rajabzadeh and
Safaee in Solomyak's Proposition 3.1 \cite{solomyak,rajabzadeh}.
We do not claim the general invariant-section mechanism as new.
The exact positive histogram mixture, its reduction to the finite
zero-height distribution, and the resulting sharp constants are the
specific results developed here. We have not located these exact
statements in the primary sources surveyed; that is a bounded
literature assessment, not a certification of publication priority.

The related work of Bressaud, Bufetov, and Hubert
\cite{bressaud} concerns a different sampling framework in its
constant-length limit theorems: a random initial point at prescribed
times. We keep it distinct from a uniform prefix of one fixed word.

\section{A general critical binary class}\label{bmf:sec:class}

Fix an integer $a\geq2$. Let $w$ be any binary word that begins with
$1$ and has $a$ zeros and $a-1$ ones. Define
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:general-sub}
 \sigma(0)=1,\qquad \sigma(1)=w.
\end{equation}
The substitution is primitive, and its nested iterates on $1$
define a fixed point $\mathbf u=u_1u_2\cdots$. For a finite word $P$ set
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:weight}
 X(P)=|P|_0-|P|_1,\qquad
 X(N)=X(u_1\cdots u_N),\qquad X(0)=0.
\end{equation}
The incidence matrix and its eigenweight identity are
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:matrix}
 M=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\1&a-1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad X(\sigma(P))=-X(P).
\end{equation}
The eigenvalues are $a$ and $-1$. The eigenweight identity follows
already from $X(\sigma(0))=-1$ and $X(\sigma(1))=1$.

For $r\geq0$ let $W_r=\sigma^r(1)$ and $L_r=|W_r|$.
The order of the letters in $w$ does not affect these lengths:
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:length}
 L_r=\frac{2a^{r+1}+(-1)^{r+1}(a-1)}{a+1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $L_0=1$, $L_1=2a-1$, and
$L_{r+1}=(a-1)L_r+aL_{r-1}$. Its characteristic roots are $a,-1$.
For $r\geq1$, $|\sigma^r(0)|=L_{r-1}$.

\begin{definition}[Zero-height distribution]\label{bmf:def:zero-height}
For each occurrence of a zero in $w$, record the value of $X$
immediately before that occurrence. Let $Z$ be a uniformly selected
one of these $a$ values, with repetitions retained. Define
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:ST}
 S(t)=\sum_{\substack{p0\text{ is a prefix}\\\text{of }w}}t^{X(p)},
 \qquad
 T(t)=\sum_{\substack{p1\text{ is a prefix}\\\text{of }w}}t^{X(p)}.
\end{equation}
Thus $S(t)/a$ is the Laurent probability generating function of $Z$.
We write $\mu=\E Z$, $v=\Var Z$, and
$d=\max\supp Z-\min\supp Z$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[The zero heights have no gaps]\label{bmf:lem:gaps}
The support of $Z$ is a consecutive interval of integers containing
$-1$ and $0$. In particular $v>0$, $d\geq1$, and $Z-Z'$ has lattice
span one for an independent copy $Z'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The prefix weights trace a nearest-neighbor path from $0$ to $1$.
The first step is downward. The path must later cross upward from
$-1$ to $0$ and from $0$ to $1$, so both starting heights occur.
If a negative height is reached, the subsequent return to the final
height $1$ includes every intervening upward crossing. If a
nonnegative height is reached by an upward crossing, the path from
its initial height $0$ must include all intervening upward crossings.
The set of starting heights of the upward steps is consequently
an integer interval. Each such step is exactly a zero occurrence.
\end{proof}

For \eqref{bmf:eq:run-family}, the zero heights are
$-1,0,\ldots,a-2$, each once, so
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:run-muv}
 \mu=\frac{a-3}{2},\qquad
 v=\frac{a^2-1}{12},\qquad d=a-1.
\end{equation}

\section{An exact positive formula for every prefix histogram}\label{bmf:sec:histogram}

\begin{definition}[Proper-prefix histogram]
For $c\in\{0,1\}$, let
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:Fdef}
 F_{r,c}(t)=
 \sum_{0\leq n<|\sigma^r(c)|}
 t^{X(\pref_n(\sigma^r(c)))}.
\end{equation}
The empty prefix is included and the complete word is excluded.
Consequently $F_{0,0}=F_{0,1}=1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Positive convolution formula]\label{bmf:thm:histogram}
Let $S^-(t)=S(t^{-1})$ and $T^-(t)=T(t^{-1})$, and put
\begin{align}
 \Delta(t)&=S(t)S^-(t),\label{bmf:eq:Delta}\\
 K_0(t)&=S(t)+T(t)-1,\label{bmf:eq:K0}\\
 K_1(t)&=K_0(t^{-1})+T^-(t)K_0(t).\label{bmf:eq:K1}
\end{align}
Both $K_c$ have nonnegative integer coefficients, and
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:Kmass}
 K_0(1)=2(a-1),\qquad K_1(1)=2a(a-1).
\end{equation}
For every $m\geq0$ and $c\in\{0,1\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:positive-hist}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 F_{2m,c}(t)=1+K_c(t)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\Delta(t)^j.}
\end{equation}
The sum is empty when $m=0$. Odd depths for $W_r$ are recovered by
$F_{r,1}=F_{r+1,0}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An upward step from height $h$ changes $t^h$ by
$(t-1)t^h$; a downward step changes it by
$(t^{-1}-1)t^h$. Summing these increments along $w$, whose final
height is $1$, gives the Laurent-polynomial identity
\[
 t-1=(t-1)S(t)+(t^{-1}-1)T(t).
\]
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:telescoping}
 T(t)=t(S(t)-1).
\end{equation}
This identity holds formally, including at $t=1$ by evaluation.

Every proper prefix of $\sigma^{r+1}(c)$ selects one letter in
$\sigma(c)$ and then a proper prefix in its $\sigma^r$ image.
The preceding complete images contribute
$(-1)^r X(p)$. With column vectors
$F_r=(F_{r,0},F_{r,1})^{\mathsf T}$, this gives
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:one-step}
 F_{r+1}(t)=B(t^{(-1)^r})F_r(t),
 \qquad
 B(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\S(t)&T(t)\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Pairing two steps therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:paired}
 F_{2m}=C(t)^m\binom11,\qquad
 C(t)=B(t^{-1})B(t)
 =\begin{pmatrix}S&T\\T^-S&S^-+T^-T\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{bmf:eq:telescoping} implies
$T^-T=(S^--1)(S-1)$, so
\[
 \operatorname{tr}C=\Delta+1,\qquad \det C=\Delta.
\]
The Cayley--Hamilton identity becomes
$(C-I)(C-\Delta I)=0$. Induction, entirely in the Laurent-polynomial
ring, now gives
\[
 C^m=I+(C-I)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\Delta^j.
\]
Multiplication by $(1,1)^{\mathsf T}$ yields
\eqref{bmf:eq:positive-hist}, because
\[
 (C-I)\binom11
 =\binom{S+T-1}{S^--1+T^-(S+T)}
 =\binom{K_0}{K_1}.
\]
Finally $w$ begins with $1$, so $T$ includes the constant term
of its empty prefix. Subtracting this one contribution leaves
$K_0\geq0$ coefficientwise. Formula \eqref{bmf:eq:K1} then proves
$K_1\geq0$. Their total masses follow from $S(1)=a$ and $T(1)=a-1$.
The odd-depth assertion is the word identity
$\sigma^{r+1}(0)=\sigma^r(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why positivity matters]
The factorization of the two eigenvalues by itself would give a
spectral formula. Equation \eqref{bmf:eq:positive-hist} gives more:
it expresses the integer histogram as a sum of positive convolutions.
It therefore has an exact probability interpretation with no signed
weights, and no spectral remainder has to be guessed or fitted.
The telescoping mechanism is related to the general invariant section
in \cite[Proposition 3.1]{solomyak}; the positivity and probability
interpretation here use the specified binary class.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A complete small example}

Take $a=3$ and $w=10001$. Then
\[
 S=t^{-1}+1+t,\qquad T=1+t^2,
\]
\[
 \Delta=t^{-2}+2t^{-1}+3+2t+t^2,\qquad
 K_0=t^{-1}+1+t+t^2,
\]
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:a3K}
 K_1=t^{-3}+2t^{-2}+3t^{-1}+3+2t+t^2.
\end{equation}
The depth-two word is the concatenation
$10001\,|\,1\,|\,1\,|\,1\,|\,10001$, of length $13$.
Its proper-prefix weights are
\[
 0,-1,0,1,2,1,0,-1,-2,-3,-2,-1,0.
\]
Their histogram is $1+K_1$, exactly as the theorem says. The same
identity computes the entire histogram at depth $256$, even though
the corresponding word has on the order of $3^{256}$ letters.

\section{The exact random-sum law and finite moments}\label{bmf:sec:mixture}

Let $Z_i,Z_i'$ be independent copies of $Z$, and set
$Y_i=Z_i-Z_i'$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:digit-law}
 \E t^{Y_i}=\frac{\Delta(t)}{a^2},\qquad
 \E Y_i=0,\qquad \Var Y_i=2v.
\end{equation}
The distribution of $Y_i$ is symmetric and has span one.

\begin{corollary}[Exact geometric mixture]\label{bmf:cor:mixture}
Fix $m\geq1$, $c\in\{0,1\}$, and let $L=|\sigma^{2m}(c)|$.
A uniformly chosen proper-prefix weight has the following law:
with probability $1/L$ it is $0$; with probability
$\omega=(L-1)/L$ it is
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:mixture-law}
 V_c+\sum_{i=1}^{J}Y_i,
\end{equation}
where all variables on the right are independent,
\[
 \E t^{V_c}=\frac{K_c(t)}{K_c(1)},\qquad
 \Prob(J=j)=\frac{a^{2j}}{\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}a^{2h}},
 \quad 0\leq j<m.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Divide each summand of \eqref{bmf:eq:positive-hist} by its total mass.
The mass of the summand $K_c\Delta^j$ is $K_c(1)a^{2j}$.
\end{proof}

The depth loss $m-1-J$ has a truncated geometric distribution.
All of its fixed moments are bounded independently of $m$.
In particular, with $R=a^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:EJ}
 \E J=m-\frac{R}{R-1}+\frac{m}{R^m-1}.
\end{equation}
The random sum therefore contains essentially one independent
digit difference for every two substitution levels.

\subsection{Exact moments for every word in the class}

The first two moments can be evaluated using only $\mu=\E Z$ and
$v=\Var Z$. Define
\begin{align}
 m_r&=
 \begin{cases}
 -\dfrac12-\dfrac{\mu}{a-1},&r\text{ even},\\[2mm]
 \dfrac12+\dfrac{a\mu}{a-1},&r\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}\label{bmf:eq:parity-mean}\\
 \beta&=\frac{v}{a+1}-\frac{a\mu^2}{(a-1)^2}+\frac14,
 \qquad
 \omega_r=1-\frac1{L_r},\label{bmf:eq:beta}\\
 \eta_r&=
 \begin{cases}ar,&r\text{ even},\\r+1,&r\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}\label{bmf:eq:eta}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Finite mean and variance]\label{bmf:thm:moments}
For $U_r$ uniform on $\{0,\ldots,L_r-1\}$ and
$X_r=X(U_r)$, the following identities hold for every $r\geq0$:
\begin{align}
 \E X_r&=\omega_r m_r,\label{bmf:eq:exact-mean}\\
 \Var X_r&=
 \omega_r(rv+\beta)
 +\frac{2v\eta_r}{(a+1)L_r}
 +\omega_r(1-\omega_r)m_r^2.\label{bmf:eq:exact-var}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:moment-asym}
 \E X_r=m_r+O_w(a^{-r}),\qquad
 \Var X_r=rv+\beta+O_w(ra^{-r}).
\end{equation}
The variance correction $\beta$ is the same in both parity classes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate the probability generating functions at $t=e^s$,
so derivatives at $s=0$ give moments. From \eqref{bmf:eq:telescoping}
and \eqref{bmf:eq:K0}--\eqref{bmf:eq:K1} one obtains
\begin{align*}
 \E V_0&=\frac12+\frac{a\mu}{a-1},&
 \Var V_0&=\frac{av}{a-1}-\frac{a\mu^2}{(a-1)^2}+\frac14,\\
 \E V_1&=-\frac12-\frac{\mu}{a-1},&
 \Var V_1&=\frac{(2a-1)v}{a-1}
                 -\frac{a\mu^2}{(a-1)^2}+\frac14.
\end{align*}
For example $S(e^s)'|_0=a\mu$ and
$S(e^s)''|_0=a(v+\mu^2)$; substitution in
$K_0=(1+t)S-t-1$ gives the first row directly.
Substitution in $K_1=K_0^-+T^-K_0$ gives the second.

Conditional on the non-atomic part of \eqref{bmf:eq:mixture-law},
the mean is $\E V_c$ and the variance is
$\Var V_c+2v\E J$. The zero atom changes these to
$\omega\E V_c$ and
$\omega(\Var V_c+2v\E J)+\omega(1-\omega)(\E V_c)^2$.
Use \eqref{bmf:eq:EJ}. For even $r=2m$, use $c=1$; for odd
$r=2m-1$, use $c=0$ at depth $2m$. The lengths from
\eqref{bmf:eq:length} reduce the resulting expressions to
\eqref{bmf:eq:exact-mean}--\eqref{bmf:eq:exact-var}.
For $r=0$, $\omega_0=\eta_0=0$, so both sides vanish.
\end{proof}

For the critical run family \eqref{bmf:eq:run-family}, these constants are
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:run-moments}
 m_{\rm even}=-\frac{a-2}{a-1},\qquad
 m_{\rm odd}=\frac{a-1}{2}-\frac1{a-1},\qquad
 \beta=\frac{7-a}{6}-\frac1{(a-1)^2}.
\end{equation}
At $a=3$, the limiting means are $-1/2$ and $1/2$,
$v=2/3$, and $\beta=5/12$. At depth two the exact values are
$\E X_2=-6/13$ and $\Var X_2=302/169$.

\section{Gaussian laws, local expansions, and arbitrary cutoffs}\label{bmf:sec:gaussian}

Throughout this section $w$ is fixed. Constants in $O_w(\cdot)$ may
depend on that finite word, but never on the cutoff or substitution
depth. Write $\ph(x)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}$ and let $\Phi$ be
its distribution function.

\subsection{A bounded independent-summand estimate}

The exact mixture makes the relevant probability input elementary.
For $S_j=Y_1+\cdots+Y_j$, the usual Berry--Esseen estimate for
bounded independent summands gives
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:iid-be}
 \sup_x\left|
 \Prob\left(\frac{S_j}{\sqrt{2vj}}\leq x\right)-\Phi(x)
 \right|=O_w(j^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
This is a standard probability theorem, not a new general estimate;
see, for example, \cite[Chapter XVI]{feller}.
The lattice local estimate can be seen directly. The characteristic
function is
\[
 \psi(t)=\E e^{itY_1}=|\E e^{itZ}|^2
       =1-vt^2+O_w(t^4).
\]
Lemma~\ref{bmf:lem:gaps} gives $|\psi(t)|<1$ for
$0<|t|\leq\pi$. On any closed interval separated from $0$ the
bound is uniform. Fourier inversion, with a small neighborhood of
zero separated from its complement, therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:iid-local}
 \sup_{k\in\Z}\left|
 \Prob(S_j=k)-\frac1{\sqrt{2vj}}
       \ph\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{2vj}}\right)
 \right|=O_w(j^{-3/2}).
\end{equation}
For completeness, in the small neighborhood the integrand difference
is bounded by
$Cj t^4e^{-cjt^2}$; its integral is $O(j^{-3/2})$.
The complementary integral is exponentially small. This also
explains the absence of a bulk skewness term.

\begin{theorem}[Full-block normal and local laws]\label{bmf:thm:block-normal}
For $X_r$ as in Theorem~\ref{bmf:thm:moments}, as $r\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \sup_x\left|
 \Prob\left(\frac{X_r-m_r}{\sqrt{rv}}\leq x\right)-\Phi(x)
 \right|&=O_w(r^{-1/2}),\label{bmf:eq:block-be}\\
 \sup_{k\in\Z}\left|
 \Prob(X_r=k)-\frac1{\sqrt{rv}}
       \ph\left(\frac{k-m_r}{\sqrt{rv}}\right)
 \right|&=O_w(r^{-3/2}).\label{bmf:eq:block-local}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \eqref{bmf:eq:block-be}, apply \eqref{bmf:eq:iid-be} conditional on
$J$ in Corollary~\ref{bmf:cor:mixture}. The event $J<m/2$ is exponentially
small. On its complement, the bounded seed changes a normal
distribution function by $O(m^{-1/2})$, and the scale change from
$J$ to $m$ is bounded by $C(m-J)/m$. The geometric depth loss has
bounded moments. The atom contributes only $O(a^{-r})$.
Odd depths use state $0$ at the next even depth.

Here are details yielding the sharper centered local estimate.
On a complex neighborhood of $s=0$, choose the analytic logarithm
and square root that are real at $0$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:chi-def}
 \chi(s)=\frac12\log\frac{S(e^s)S(e^{-s})}{a^2}.
\end{equation}
The finite geometric identity implies
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:block-analytic}
 \E e^{sX_r}
 =e^{r\chi(s)}g_{\epsilon}(s)(1+O_w(\rho^r)),
 \qquad 0<\rho<1,\quad \epsilon=r\bmod2,
\end{equation}
uniformly on a smaller fixed neighborhood, where
\begin{align}
 g_0(s)&=\frac{(a+1)K_1(e^s)}
                   {2a(\Delta(e^s)-1)},\label{bmf:eq:g-even}\\
 g_1(s)&=\frac{(a+1)K_0(e^s)\sqrt{\Delta(e^s)}}
                   {2a(\Delta(e^s)-1)}.\label{bmf:eq:g-odd}
\end{align}
Both are $1$ at $0$. The moment identities give
$(\log g_\epsilon)'(0)=m_r$ and
$(\log g_\epsilon)''(0)=\beta$. Also
$\chi'(0)=\chi'''(0)=0$ and $\chi''(0)=v$.
Consequently, after removal of the bounded mean,
the small-frequency characteristic function differs from
$e^{-rvt^2/2}$ by an integrable error bounded by
$C(t^2+rt^4)e^{-crt^2}$, whose integral is $O(r^{-3/2})$.
Away from zero, use the original finite geometric sum, so that
possible points $\Delta(e^{it})=1$ create no denominator problem:
its normalized modulus is bounded by $Cr\rho_1^r$ for some
$\rho_1<1$. Fourier inversion proves \eqref{bmf:eq:block-local}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The first local correction}

Let $\kappa_4(Z)=\E(Z-\mu)^4-3v^2$ and set
$H_2(y)=y^2-1$, $H_4(y)=y^4-6y^2+3$.
Keeping the next terms in the preceding Fourier proof gives,
uniformly for $y=(k-m_r)/\sqrt{rv}$ in any fixed compact interval,
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:edgeworth}
 \Prob(X_r=k)=\frac{\ph(y)}{\sqrt{rv}}
 \left[
 1+\frac1r\left\{
 \frac{\beta}{2v}H_2(y)
 +\frac{\kappa_4(Z)}{24v^2}H_4(y)
 \right\}+O_w(r^{-3/2})
 \right].
\end{equation}
Indeed $\chi^{(4)}(0)=\kappa_4(Z)$; the second derivative
of $\log g_\epsilon$ supplies the $H_2$ term. Higher odd
derivatives of $\chi$ vanish. The bounded third derivative of
$\log g_\epsilon$ first enters at order $r^{-3/2}$ in the bracket.
The same analytic expansion gives every fixed local correction
order. This statement concerns fixed orders and does not assert
convergence of an infinite asymptotic expansion.

\end{equation}

\subsection{Why every cutoff has the same leading law}

A theorem about $L_r$ alone does not automatically imply a theorem
about all $N$. The required passage uses a precise prefix
decomposition.

\begin{lemma}[Decomposition with a geometric loss of depth]\label{bmf:lem:decompose}
Let $h$ be the least integer with $N\leq L_h$.
The prefix of length $N$ can be partitioned into complete blocks
$\sigma^r(c)$ with at most $2a-2$ blocks at each level $r<h$,
or into the single block $W_h$ if it is complete.
For a block at level $r=h-j$, its mass under uniform sampling
of the $N$ prefix positions is $O_w(a^{-j})$, summed over
all blocks at that level. Its starting weight has absolute value
$O_w(j+1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand the one unfinished block into the images of its letters.
Accept the complete children and continue only inside the next
unfinished child. There are at most $2a-2$ accepted children at
a stage. By \eqref{bmf:eq:length}, a level-$r$ child has length
comparable to $a^r$, while $N$ is comparable to $a^h$.
The mass estimate follows. At an ancestor level, every preceding
complete sibling has total weight $1$ or $-1$ by
\eqref{bmf:eq:matrix}; a bounded number of siblings is passed at
each of the $j+1$ levels. Their sum is the stated starting weight.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative normal laws at every cutoff]\label{bmf:thm:all-cutoff}
Let $N\geq2$, let $U_N$ be uniform on $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$,
and put $b_N=\log_a N$. As $N\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 \E X(U_N)&=O_w(1),&
 \Var X(U_N)&=vb_N+O_w(1),\label{bmf:eq:all-moments}\\
 \sup_x\left|
 \Prob\left(\frac{X(U_N)}{\sqrt{vb_N}}\leq x\right)-\Phi(x)
 \right|&=O_w(b_N^{-1/2}),\label{bmf:eq:all-be}\\
 \sup_{k\in\Z}\left|
 \sqrt{vb_N}\Prob(X(U_N)=k)
 -\ph\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{vb_N}}\right)
 \right|&=O_w(b_N^{-1/2}).\label{bmf:eq:all-local}
\end{align}
These conclusions also hold for the one-based convention
$U_N\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Condition on the block of Lemma~\ref{bmf:lem:decompose}.
Its internal mean is bounded and its variance is $vr+O_w(1)$.
The lost depth $j$ and the starting weight have uniformly bounded
moments under the geometric mass bound. Conditional expectation and
variance therefore give \eqref{bmf:eq:all-moments}, using
$h=b_N+O_w(1)$.

For the normal estimates first retain $j\leq h/2$.
There $r$ is comparable to $b_N$. The full-block error is
$O_w(b_N^{-1/2})$. The bounded seed and starting shift change the
normal comparison by at most $C_w(j+1)/\sqrt{b_N}$,
and the variance-scale change contributes at most
$C_w(j+1)/b_N$. Summation over the geometric level masses proves
the asserted errors. The discarded mass is $O_w(a^{-h/2})$;
for the local assertion multiply it by $\sqrt{b_N}$ before
discarding it. This is still negligible. Replacing the sampled
interval $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ by $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ changes the
law in total variation by at most $1/N$.
Since $X(N)=O_w(\log N)$, as also proved in
Section~\ref{bmf:sec:critical-extremes}, the corresponding changes
in the first and second moments are $O_w(\log N/N)$ and
$O_w((\log N)^2/N)$. Thus the moment conclusions transfer too.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the existing central limit theorem]
The existence of the limit in \eqref{bmf:eq:all-be} is already implied
by Paquette--Son \cite[Theorem 3.3]{paquette}. In this class their
noncoboundary condition can also be checked directly: $w$ must
contain $00$, since its first letter is $1$ and there are more
zeros than ones. Their Proposition 3.1 applied to adjacent equal
letters excludes a coboundary for the weight $(1,-1)$.
Theorem~\ref{bmf:thm:all-cutoff} adds an evaluated variance and
quantitative, locally resolved statements through the positive
representation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cumulants at arbitrary cutoffs}

For each fixed positive integer $j$, the same decomposition gives
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:all-cumulants}
 \kappa_{2j}(X(U_N))=b_N\kappa_{2j}(Z)+O_{w,j}(1),
 \qquad
 \kappa_{2j-1}(X(U_N))=O_{w,j}(1).
\end{equation}
One must justify differentiating here; a pointwise pressure limit
alone would not suffice. To do so, combine the block decomposition
and the exact random-sum law to write, conditional on the selected
block and depth,
$X(U_N)=A_N+\sum_{i=1}^{J_N}Y_i$. Both $A_N$ and
$J_N-b_N/2$ have uniformly bounded exponential moments in
a neighborhood of zero. This follows from the geometric depth
tails and the $O(j+1)$ offset bound, including the exponentially
rare base component. Therefore
\[
 \E e^{sX(U_N)}
 =e^{b_N\chi(s)}
 \E\exp\left\{sA_N+
       (J_N-b_N/2)\log\E e^{sY_1}\right\}.
\]
The last factor is analytic, uniformly bounded, and uniformly
nonzero on a sufficiently small disk about $0$; it is $1$ at $0$,
and uniform analyticity bounds its derivative there and nearby.
All fixed derivatives of its logarithm are bounded. Differentiating
proves \eqref{bmf:eq:all-cumulants}.
For the run family, with Bernoulli numbers defined by
$s/(e^s-1)=\sum B_j s^j/j!$,
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:bernoulli-cumulants}
 \kappa_{2j}(X(U_N))
 =b_N\,\frac{B_{2j}(a^{2j}-1)}{2j}+O_{a,j}(1).
\end{equation}

\section{Exact pressure and the rarity of logarithmic errors}\label{bmf:sec:pressure}

\begin{theorem}[Pressure for every cutoff]\label{bmf:thm:pressure}
For every real $s$,
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:pressure-asym}
 \log\E e^{sX(U_N)}
 =(\log_a N)\chi(s)+O_{w,s}(1),
 \qquad
 \chi(s)=\frac12\log\frac{S(e^s)S(e^{-s})}{a^2}.
\end{equation}
The function $\chi$ is even, analytic on the real line, strictly
convex, and satisfies $\chi(0)=\chi'(0)=0$, $\chi''(0)=v$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For real $s$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\Delta(e^s)\geq a^2>1$. The positive finite formula, and its
odd-depth counterpart, give
\[
 \log F_{r,1}(e^s)=\frac r2\log\Delta(e^s)+O_{w,s}(1).
\]
Let $L_{h-1}<N\leq L_h$. Since $W_{h-1}$ is a prefix of $W_h$,
positivity gives the decisive squeeze
\[
 F_{h-1,1}(e^s)
 \leq \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}e^{sX(n)}
 \leq F_{h,1}(e^s).
\]
Subtract $\log N$ and use $h=\log_a N+O_w(1)$.
Strict convexity follows because
$\chi''(s)$ is one half the sum of the variances of the
nondegenerate finite distribution $Z$ under tilts $s$ and $-s$.
\end{proof}

Define the rate function
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:rate}
 I_w(x)=\sup_{s\in\R}\{sx-\chi(s)\}.
\end{equation}
Let $z_-=\min\supp Z$, $z_+=\max\supp Z$,
$d=z_+-z_-$, and let $c_-,c_+$ be the multiplicities of
$z_-,z_+$ among the $a$ zero heights.

\begin{theorem}[A full large-deviation law]\label{bmf:thm:ldp}
The variables $X(U_N)/\log_a N$ satisfy a large-deviation principle
with speed $\log_a N$ and rate \eqref{bmf:eq:rate}. Thus for a closed
set $F\subseteq\R$ and an open set $G\subseteq\R$,
\begin{align}
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac1{\log_a N}
  \log\Prob\!\left(\frac{X(U_N)}{\log_a N}\in F\right)
 &\leq-\inf_F I_w,\label{bmf:eq:ldp-upper}\\
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac1{\log_a N}
  \log\Prob\!\left(\frac{X(U_N)}{\log_a N}\in G\right)
 &\geq-\inf_G I_w.\label{bmf:eq:ldp-lower}
\end{align}
Its effective domain is $[-d/2,d/2]$ and its endpoint values are
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:rate-endpoint}
 I_w(d/2)=I_w(-d/2)
 =\log a-\frac12\log(c_-c_+).
\end{equation}
For $|x|<d/2$, the supremum is attained at the unique solution
$\chi'(s)=x$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Exponential Markov bounds from \eqref{bmf:eq:pressure-asym} give the
upper bound on an interval; finitely many such intervals cover
a compact closed set. Exponential tightness also follows either
from the same bounds or from the deterministic logarithmic bound
proved in Section~\ref{bmf:sec:critical-extremes}.

For the lower bound at $x=\chi'(s)$, tilt the distribution by
$e^{sX}/\E e^{sX}$. Under the tilted measure the scaled
logarithmic moment generating function is
$\chi(s+t)-\chi(s)$. Strict convexity and exponential Markov
bounds with small positive and negative $t$ show concentration
of $X/\log_a N$ within any fixed neighborhood of $x$.
Undoing the tilt on this neighborhood gives the lower exponent
$\chi(s)-sx-|s|\varepsilon$; let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
This proves the lower bound for all interior points, and hence
for all open sets meeting the interior.

As $s\to+\infty$,
\[
 \chi(s)=\frac d2s-\log a+\frac12\log(c_-c_+)+o(1).
\]
The analogous formula holds at $-\infty$. It determines the
effective domain and the endpoint values. The rate approaches
these finite endpoint values from the interior. An open
neighborhood of an endpoint contains interior points, completing
the lower bound there.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Endpoint events]
The endpoint assertion concerns neighborhoods in a large-deviation
principle. It does not assert a single asymptotic formula for one
exact extremal integer at every cutoff. Moreover the endpoint
rate is $\log a$ only when both extreme zero heights have
multiplicity one.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Explicit formulas in the run family}

For $Z$ uniform on $-1,0,\ldots,a-2$, the translation cancels and
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:run-pressure}
 \chi_a(s)=\log\left(\frac{\sinh(as/2)}{a\sinh(s/2)}\right).
\end{equation}
For $0<|x|<(a-1)/2$, the tilting parameter is determined by
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:saddle}
 x=\frac a2\coth\frac{as}{2}-\frac12\coth\frac{s}{2},
 \qquad I_a(x)=sx-\chi_a(s).
\end{equation}
The continuation at $s=0$ has $x=0$. The rate is even and
\[
 I_a(0)=0,\qquad I_a\!\left(\pm\frac{a-1}{2}\right)=\log a.
\]
Near zero,
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:rate-near0}
 I_a(x)=\frac{6x^2}{a^2-1}
       +\frac{36(a^2+1)x^4}{5(a^2-1)^3}+O_a(x^6).
\end{equation}
This follows by reverting
$\chi_a'(s)=vs+\kappa_4(Z)s^3/6+O(s^5)$.

For every fixed $0<x<(a-1)/2$, the large-deviation law yields
the exact logarithmic exponent
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:threshold-density}
 \frac1N\#\{0\leq n<N:X_a(n)\geq x\log_a N\}
   =N^{-I_a(x)/\log a+o(1)}.
\end{equation}
To see the matching lower bound, use a small interval strictly
above $x$ and then let its left endpoint decrease to $x$.
The upper bound follows from the closed half-line and the
strict increase of $I_a$ on its positive half-domain.

Equation \eqref{bmf:eq:threshold-density} describes how exceptional
prefixes interpolate between the Gaussian scale and the extremal
logarithmic scale. It gives a frequency exponent, not a leading
multiplicative constant.

\section{Which arrangement has the largest fluctuations?}\label{bmf:sec:arrangements}

The incidence matrix \eqref{bmf:eq:matrix} is the same for all choices
of $w$ at fixed $a$. It determines the frequencies and the
substitution growth, but not the magnitude of the fluctuations.
The zero-height representation makes the comparison exact.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp variance and pressure comparison]\label{bmf:thm:arrangements}
For every admissible $w$,
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:variance-extrema}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \frac{a-1}{a^2}\leq v\leq\frac{a^2-1}{12}.}
\end{equation}
The upper equality holds exactly for
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:max-arrangements}
 w=1^h0^a1^{a-1-h},\qquad 1\leq h\leq a-1.
\end{equation}
The lower equality holds exactly for
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:min-arrangements}
 w=(10)^{a-1}0
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 w=10(01)^{a-2}0.
\end{equation}
They coincide when $a=2$.

For every real $s$ the entire pressure is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:pressure-comparison}
 \frac12\log\left(1+\frac{2(a-1)}{a^2}(\cosh s-1)\right)
 \leq\chi_w(s)\leq
 \log\left(\frac{\sinh(as/2)}{a\sinh(s/2)}\right).
\end{equation}
At any $s\ne0$, its equality cases are exactly the corresponding
words in \eqref{bmf:eq:min-arrangements} and
\eqref{bmf:eq:max-arrangements}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sort the $a$ zero heights, with multiplicity, as
$z_1\leq\cdots\leq z_a$. By Lemma~\ref{bmf:lem:gaps}, each
successive gap is either $0$ or $1$. Hence
$0\leq z_j-z_i\leq j-i$, and
\[
 v=\frac1{a^2}\sum_{i<j}(z_j-z_i)^2
 \leq\frac1{a^2}\sum_{i<j}(j-i)^2
 =\frac{a^2-1}{12}.
\]
Equality requires all successive gaps to be $1$, so all heights
are distinct. An internal run of ones between two zeros forces
a repetition of a zero height: its downward move either lands
inside the already crossed interval, or lands below that interval
and must later cross it again to end at height $1$.
Therefore distinct heights force all zeros into one run.
Conversely, \eqref{bmf:eq:max-arrangements} gives $a$ consecutive
distinct heights and attains the bound.

A nonconstant list of $a$ integers has at least $a-1$ unequal
unordered pairs, each contributing at least $1$ to the variance
sum. This gives the lower bound. Equality requires two adjacent
heights with multiplicities $1$ and $a-1$. Since $-1$ and $0$
are both present, these are exactly the two heights.
Such a path consists of excursions $10$ below height $0$ and
$01$ above it, followed by its last $0$. It starts with a $10$
excursion. Requiring one of the two heights to occur only once
gives exactly \eqref{bmf:eq:min-arrangements}.

Finally,
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:cosh-pairs}
 \Delta(e^s)=a^2+
 2\sum_{i<j}\bigl(\cosh(s(z_j-z_i))-1\bigr).
\end{equation}
The same pairwise upper and lower comparisons give
\[
 a^2+2(a-1)(\cosh s-1)
 \leq\Delta(e^s)
 \leq\left(\frac{\sinh(as/2)}{\sinh(s/2)}\right)^2.
\]
Take one half the logarithm after division by $a^2$.
For $s\ne0$, strict increase of $\cosh(sx)$ in $x\geq0$
gives precisely the already identified equality cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ordering the deviation costs]\label{bmf:cor:rate-order}
Let $I_{\rm run}$ be the rate function for any word in
\eqref{bmf:eq:max-arrangements}, and $I_{\rm alt}$ that for either
word in \eqref{bmf:eq:min-arrangements}. Then, with extended-real
values allowed,
\[
 I_{\rm run}(x)\leq I_w(x)\leq I_{\rm alt}(x)
 \qquad(x\in\R).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Taking a Legendre transform reverses pointwise inequalities.
\end{proof}

Thus a single zero run maximizes more than the variance: it
maximizes the complete logarithmic moment generating function.
It minimizes the exponential cost of every deviation on the
common part of the effective domains. The critical family selected
by the repository is an extremal member of a much larger class.

\section{Which height profiles occur? A second OEIS connection}
\label{bmf:sec:realization}

The distribution of the zero heights is sufficient to recover the
complete histograms: it determines $S$, then $T=t(S-1)$, the seeds,
and every expression in Theorem~\ref{bmf:thm:histogram}. This makes the
inverse problem natural. Are there constraints beyond positivity,
total mass, and the no-gap condition?

\begin{theorem}[Complete realization and profile count]
\label{bmf:thm:realization}
Fix $a\geq2$. A Laurent polynomial is the zero-height polynomial of
an admissible word if and only if it has the form
\[
 S(t)=\sum_{h=\ell}^{H}c_h t^h,\qquad
 c_h\in\{1,2,\ldots\},\qquad
 \sum_{h=\ell}^{H}c_h=a,\qquad \ell\leq-1<0\leq H.
\]
The number of distinct such polynomials is
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:profile-count}
 \sum_{k=2}^{a}(k-1)\binom{a-1}{k-1}
 =(a-1)2^{a-2}.
\end{equation}
Thus these profiles give an interpretation of
$\mathrm{A001787}(a-1)$, where A001787 is $n2^{n-1}$
with its usual value $0$ at $n=0$ \cite{oeis1787}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity is Lemma~\ref{bmf:lem:gaps}. For sufficiency, construct a
directed multigraph on the integers $\ell,\ldots,H+1$.
For each $h$ put $c_h$ upward edges $h\to h+1$ and
$c_h-\mathbf1_{\{h=0\}}$ downward edges $h+1\to h$.
There are $a$ upward and $a-1$ downward edges. The difference
between outdegree and indegree is $1$ at $0$, $-1$ at $1$,
and zero at every other vertex.

There is a downward edge $0\to-1$, because $c_{-1}>0$.
Prescribe this as the first edge and temporarily remove it.
All upward edges remain, so the underlying undirected graph is
still connected. The new degree imbalance is $1$ at $-1$ and
$-1$ at $1$. The directed Euler-trail criterion therefore gives
a trail from $-1$ to $1$ using all remaining edges. Prepending
the removed edge gives a trail from $0$ to $1$ beginning with a
downward step. Read upward edges as $0$ and downward edges as
$1$. The resulting word begins with $1$, has the required letter
counts, and has exactly $c_h$ zero occurrences beginning at height
$h$.

If the support has $k$ values, there are $k-1$ positions for a
consecutive integer interval containing both $-1$ and $0$.
Its positive coefficient vectors are the
$\binom{a-1}{k-1}$ compositions of $a$ into $k$ parts. Summing
over $k$ proves the first equality in \eqref{bmf:eq:profile-count}.
The second is the standard binomial identity
$\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}j\binom{a-1}{j}=(a-1)2^{a-2}$.
\end{proof}

This counts profiles, not words. There are
$\binom{2a-2}{a}$ admissible words, and different words can
have the same profile. Such words have identical complete-block
prefix histograms at every depth, even when their internal
prefix order differs. Arbitrary-cutoff bounded corrections may
retain information that the profile forgets.
For $a=2,\ldots,9$ the profile counts are
\[
 1,\ 4,\ 12,\ 32,\ 80,\ 192,\ 448,\ 1024.
\]
The constructive proof also supplies an algorithm for generating
a representative word for every profile.

\section{Sharp logarithmic envelopes and exact first hits}\label{bmf:sec:critical-extremes}

\subsection{A sharp constant for every arrangement}

\begin{theorem}[General critical cluster interval]\label{bmf:thm:general-extremes}
For an arbitrary admissible $w$, let $d$ be the diameter of its
zero-height support. Then
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:general-extremes}
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{X(N)}{\log N}
 =-\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{X(N)}{\log N}
 =\frac{d}{2\log a}.
\end{equation}
The complete cluster set is the closed interval between these
endpoints. In particular
$|X(N)|\leq(d/2)\log_a N+O_w(1)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The support of $\Delta$ is the full integer interval $[-d,d]$,
and both endpoint coefficients are positive. Since each $K_c$
is a fixed nonzero positive polynomial,
\eqref{bmf:eq:positive-hist} shows that the maximum and minimum
proper-prefix weights at depth $r$ are
$dr/2+O_w(1)$ and $-dr/2+O_w(1)$.
The odd-depth identity supplies the same statement at odd $r$.
Enclosing an arbitrary prefix between adjacent full-word lengths
proves the uniform bound.

Choose positions $p_r,q_r$ attaining the maximum and minimum
in a depth-$r$ block. The uniform bound applied to their attained
values forces
$\log_a p_r=r+O_w(1)$ and $\log_a q_r=r+O_w(1)$.
The opposite inequalities follow from $p_r,q_r\leq L_r$.
These positions attain both constants in \eqref{bmf:eq:general-extremes}.
Between $p_r$ and $q_r$ the walk changes by $\pm1$ at every
step and therefore crosses every intermediate integer height.
All these positions have logarithm $r\log a+O_w(1)$.
Selecting crossings of any prescribed proportional height gives
every intermediate normalized limit.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact finite extrema in the repository's critical family}

Now specialize to \eqref{bmf:eq:run-family}, so $a\geq3$ and
$d=a-1$. Set $W_{-1}=0$. Its word recursion is
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:wordrec}
 W_{r+1}=W_rW_{r-1}^{\,a}W_r^{\,a-2},\qquad
 X(W_r)=(-1)^{r+1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite envelopes]\label{bmf:prop:finite-extrema}
Including both empty and complete prefixes of $W_r$, the maximum
and minimum weights are
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:finite-extrema}
 A_r=\left\lceil\frac r2\right\rceil(a-1),\qquad
 B_r=-\left\lfloor\frac r2\right\rfloor(a-1)-1
 \qquad(r\geq0).
\end{equation}
Every integer in $[B_r,A_r]$ occurs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The initial values are $A_0=0$, $B_0=-1$,
$A_1=a-1$, and $B_1=-1$.
The offsets of the blocks in \eqref{bmf:eq:wordrec} give, for odd
$r\geq1$,
\[
 A_{r+1}=\max(A_r,A_{r-1}+1),\qquad
 B_{r+1}=\min(B_r+1-a,B_{r-1}+2-a),
\]
and for even $r\geq1$,
\[
 A_{r+1}=\max(A_r+a-1,A_{r-1}+a-2),\qquad
 B_{r+1}=\min(B_r,B_{r-1}-1).
\]
Substitution proves \eqref{bmf:eq:finite-extrema} by induction.
The nearest-neighbor property proves the interval assertion.
Alternatively, at positive even depths the outer exponents of
the histogram give the same extrema directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit first extremal prefixes]\label{bmf:thm:first-hit}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:PQ}
 P_0=\varnothing,\qquad P_{k+1}=\sigma_a^2(P_k)\,1\,0^a,
 \qquad Q_k=\sigma_a(P_k)1.
\end{equation}
Then $P_k$ and $Q_k$ are the first prefixes of the infinite word
with weights $k(a-1)$ and $-k(a-1)-1$, respectively.
Writing
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:ab-length}
 A=\frac{4a}{(a-1)(a+1)^2},\qquad
 B=\frac{(a-1)^2}{a+1},
\end{equation}
their exact lengths are
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:pq-length}
 p_k=A(a^{2k}-1)+Bk,\qquad
 q_k=aA(a^{2k}-1)-Bk+1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $P_k$ is a legal prefix immediately before a one; when
$k\geq1$ it ends in zero. The word $1\,0^a$ is a prefix
immediately before a one in $\sigma_a^2(1)$, so induction
proves legality. Every $Q_k$ ends in a one immediately before
a zero. The eigenweight identity gives the stated weights.

For $k\geq1$, $P_k$ is a proper prefix of $W_{2k-1}$,
and $Q_k$ is a proper prefix of $W_{2k}$. The extreme
coefficients of $\Delta$ and $K_1$ are all $1$ in this family.
Equation \eqref{bmf:eq:positive-hist} therefore shows that each
extreme of $W_{2k}$ is attained at exactly one proper prefix.
The full word has weight $-1$ and adds no duplicate extreme.
The exhibited prefixes consequently are the first occurrences
of their weights in the infinite fixed point.

For a finite word $P$,
\[
 |\sigma_a(P)|=a|P|-(a-1)X(P),\qquad
 |\sigma_a^2(P)|=a^2|P|-(a-1)^2X(P).
\]
Thus
\[
 p_{k+1}=a^2p_k-(a-1)^3k+a+1,\qquad
 q_k=ap_k-(a-1)^2k+1.
\]
Solve the first recurrence with $p_0=0$ and substitute into
the second to obtain \eqref{bmf:eq:pq-length}.
\end{proof}

In particular $p_1=a+1$, $q_1=3a$,
$p_k\sim Aa^{2k}$ and $q_k\sim aAa^{2k}$.
At $a=3$ the first positive hitting lengths are
$4,32,276,\ldots$ and the negative ones are
$9,89,817,\ldots$. These formulas answer an explicit extremal
slice of the predecessor's first-hitting question as well as
its critical normalization question.

\subsection{Counting and position errors}

Let $O(N)$ count ones and $Z_0(N)$ count zeros.
Then
\[
 Z_0(N)-N/2=X(N)/2,\qquad O(N)-N/2=-X(N)/2.
\]
Both frequencies are $1/2$. For the run family their normalized
counting-error cluster intervals are
$[-(a-1)/(4\log a),(a-1)/(4\log a)]$.

If $u(n)$ and $v(n)$ are the positions of the $n$th zero and
one, respectively, their errors satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:position-exact}
 E_0(n)=2n-u(n)=X(u(n)),\qquad
 E_1(n)=2n-v(n)=-X(v(n)).
\end{equation}
Thus $u(n)=2n+O_a(\log n)$ and $v(n)=2n+O_a(\log n)$.
Both $E_i(n)/\log n$ have the complete cluster interval
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:position-cluster}
 \left[-\frac{a-1}{2\log a},\frac{a-1}{2\log a}\right].
\end{equation}
To justify transfer of every intermediate value, both letters
occur within a bounded distance of every position: each of
the two fixed finite words $\sigma_a^2(0),\sigma_a^2(1)$
contains both letters, and the fixed point is their
concatenation. Move a chosen prefix endpoint to the next
required letter. The length and weight change by $O_a(1)$,
which does not change the logarithmic limit.

The four explicit extremal rank sequences, with $h_k=k(a-1)$,
are as follows.

\subsection{An exact letter-conditioned refinement}

The paired matrix $C(t)^m$ also records the histogram separately
according to the next letter. Its $(c,j)$ entry counts the proper
prefixes of $\sigma^{2m}(c)$ immediately followed by $j$.
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:rankone}
 C-I=
 \binom{S-1}{T^-S}\begin{pmatrix}1&t\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
All its entries are nonnegative: $S$ contains the zero-height
upward crossing, so $S-1\geq0$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:conditioned}
 C^m=I+(C-I)\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}\Delta^h
\end{equation}
is a positive histogram formula even after conditioning on the
next letter. The two seed laws in each row differ by a shift
of one. This supplies exact distributions for the position
errors at complete substitution cutoffs, through
$E_0=X(\hbox{preceding prefix})+1$ and
$E_1=1-X(\hbox{preceding prefix})$.
Their bulk variance and pressure are the same as above.

\section{An exact supercritical amplitude and its self-affine profile}
\label{bmf:sec:supercritical}

In this section we return to the two-parameter family of the predecessor.
The weight is now $\#0-q\#1$, and $L(P)$ denotes geometric length;
ordinary word length remains $|P|$.

Let $a,b\geq1$ be integers with $a\geq b+3$, and let $w$ be the
fixed point starting with $1$ of
\[
 \tau(0)=1,\qquad \tau(1)=1\,0^a1^b.
\]
Write
\[
 q=\frac{\sqrt{(b+1)^2+4a}-(b+1)}2>1,\qquad
 \lambda=q+b+1=\frac aq,\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{\log q}{\log\lambda}\in(0,1).
\]
For a finite word $P$, define
\[
 X(P)=|P|_0-q|P|_1,\qquad
 L(P)=|P|_0+\lambda |P|_1.
\]
Both are additive, and
\[
 X(\tau(P))=-qX(P),\qquad L(\tau(P))=\lambda L(P).
\]
For the first $N$ letters of $w$, abbreviate their weight and geometric
length by $X(N)$ and $L_N$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact supercritical normalization]
\label{bmf:thm:supercritical-amplitude}
Set
\[
 \mathcal C_{a,b}=
 \frac{\lambda-1}{q^2-1}
 \left(\frac{(\lambda+q)(\lambda^2-1)}{(q+1)^2}\right)^\alpha.
\]
Then
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{X(N)}{N^\alpha}=\mathcal C_{a,b},
 \qquad
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{X(N)}{N^\alpha}=-\mathcal C_{a,b}.
\]
The full cluster set is $[-\mathcal C_{a,b},\mathcal C_{a,b}]$.
There is a continuous function $\Theta:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ satisfying
$\Theta(s+1)=-\Theta(s)$ such that, with $c=(\lambda+q)/(q+1)$,
\[
 X(N)=c^\alpha N^\alpha\Theta(\log_\lambda(cN))
       +O_{a,b}(N^{\alpha^2}).
\]
The function is obtained from a two-state graph-directed system with
explicit affine maps. Its amplitude is
\[
 \max_s|\Theta(s)|=
 \frac{\lambda-1}{q^2-1}
 \left(\frac{\lambda^2-1}{q+1}\right)^\alpha.
\]
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Continuous block profiles}

Assign geometric letter lengths $\ell_0=1,\ell_1=\lambda$ and vertical
increments $\chi_0=1,\chi_1=-q$. Let $H_{i,k}$ be the polygonal path of
$\tau^k(i)$ with these increments. Define
\[
 f_{i,k}(t)=(-q)^{-k}H_{i,k}(\lambda^k t),
 \qquad 0\leq t\leq\ell_i.
\]
The initial profiles are $f_{0,0}(t)=t$ and $f_{1,0}(t)=-qt/\lambda$.
The pair of continuous functions with endpoint data
$f_i(0)=0,f_i(\ell_i)=\chi_i$ is a complete metric space in the maximum
uniform norm. On the interval corresponding to a letter $j$ of $\tau(i)$,
preceded by a prefix $P$ of that image, set
\[
 (Tf)_i\left(\frac{L(P)+u}{\lambda}\right)
    =-\frac{X(P)+f_j(u)}q,
 \qquad 0\leq u\leq\ell_j.
\]
Endpoint compatibility makes this a continuous function. The operator $T$
is a contraction of ratio $1/q$, and $f_{\cdot,k+1}=Tf_{\cdot,k}$.
Consequently the profiles converge uniformly to a unique pair $(f_0,f_1)$.
Its graph maps are exactly
\[
 (u,y)\longmapsto
 \left(\frac{L(P)+u}{\lambda},-\frac{X(P)+y}{q}\right).
\]
In particular they contract in both coordinates.

For later verification, their branch equations are
\begin{align*}
 f_0(t)&=-q^{-1}f_1(\lambda t), &&0\leq t\leq1,\\
 f_1(t)&=-q^{-1}f_1(\lambda t), &&0\leq t\leq1,\\
 f_1\left(1+\frac{j+u}{\lambda}\right)
   &=1-\frac jq-\frac{f_0(u)}q,
   &&0\leq j<a,\quad0\leq u\leq1,\\
 f_1\left(q+1+\ell+\frac u\lambda\right)
   &=1-\lambda+\ell-\frac{f_1(u)}q,
   &&0\leq\ell<b,\quad0\leq u\leq\lambda.
\end{align*}
The first-level difference from the initial line is linear on each piece.
For state $0$ it vanishes. For state $1$ its largest absolute value occurs
after the zero block and equals
\[
 D_0=\frac{b(\lambda+q)}\lambda.
\]
Therefore, with
\[
 E=\frac{bq(\lambda+q)}{\lambda(q-1)},
\]
the uniform error satisfies
\[
 \max_i\|f_{i,k}-f_i\|_\infty\leq E q^{-k}.
\]

Let $H:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ be the corresponding polygonal path of
the infinite fixed point. Because $\tau^k(1)$ is a prefix of $w$,
$f_{1,k}(t)=(-q)^{-k}H(\lambda^k t)$ for $0\leq t\leq\lambda$.
Extend the limiting profile by
\[
 F(t)=(-q)^k f_1(t/\lambda^k)
\]
for any sufficiently large integer $k\geq0$. The first branch equation
makes this independent of $k$. It gives a continuous function satisfying
\[
 F(\lambda t)=-qF(t),\qquad |H(t)-F(t)|\leq E
 \quad(t\geq0).
\]
Every refinement preserves all existing polygon vertices, since the child
profiles have fixed endpoint weights. Thus $f_1=f_{1,k}$ at the depth-$k$
vertices. Choosing a substitution prefix long enough to contain a given
integer prefix gives the exact interpolation identity
\[
 F(L_N)=X(N)\qquad(N\geq0).
\]
The bounded-error comparison with $H$ is only needed between these vertices.

The function $F$ is globally H\"older continuous of exponent $\alpha$.
Indeed, at depth $k$ the profile intervals have lengths
$\ell_i\lambda^{-k}\geq\lambda^{-k}$ and oscillations bounded by
$Dq^{-k}$, where $D=\max_i\operatorname{osc}(f_i)$. An interval of length
at most $\lambda^{-k}$ meets at most two interiors of depth-$k$ cells;
allowing three cells avoids all endpoint conventions. Choosing $k$ at the
scale of $|x-y|$ proves a uniform $\alpha$-H\"older bound on $[0,\lambda]$.
The relation $F(\lambda t)=-qF(t)$ extends the same bound to all positive
arguments, since $q=\lambda^\alpha$.

\subsection{A concave polygon envelope: proof of the exact amplitude}

This is the substantive sharp-constant argument. Put
\[
 d=q+1,\quad e=\lambda-1,\quad
 r=\lambda^{-2},\quad s=q^{-2},\quad
 t_* =\frac d{1-r},\quad A=\frac e{1-s}.
\]
Thus $0<r<s<1$, $s=r^\alpha$, and
\[
 d=(1-r)t_*,\qquad e=(1-s)A.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{t_*}{\lambda}<1<t_*,\qquad rt_*<1,
\]
because $\lambda-q-1=b\geq1$ and
$\lambda^2-1-\lambda(q+1)=b\lambda-1>0$.

Define $P:[0,\infty)\to[0,A]$ by
\[
 P(0)=0,\qquad P(t_*r^n)=As^n\quad(n\geq0),
\]
using linear interpolation between consecutive displayed points and setting
$P(t)=A$ for $t\geq t_*$. Its successive slopes are
\[
 m(s/r)^n,\qquad
 m=\frac{A(1-s)}{t_*(1-r)}=\frac ed.
\]
Thus $P$ is increasing and concave, with slope at most $m$ on $[1,\infty)$.
Because it interpolates the concave power curve at its breakpoints,
\[
 P(t)\leq \frac A{t_*^\alpha}t^\alpha\quad(t\geq0).
\]
It also satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:polygon-main}
 P(d+rv)\geq e+sP(v)\qquad(v\geq0).
\end{equation}
For $v\geq t_*$ both sides equal $A$. For $v<t_*$, the argument
$d+rv$ lies in the last linear segment $[rt_*,t_*]$, because
$d>rt_*$ follows from $\lambda^2>2$. Hence
\[
 P(d+rv)=A-mr(t_*-v).
\]
Concavity gives $A-P(v)\geq m(t_*-v)$; multiplying by $s>r$ proves
\eqref{bmf:eq:polygon-main}. We shall also use
\[
 q^{-2}P(\lambda^2t)\leq P(t),\qquad
 \frac m\lambda<\frac1q.
\]
The first follows from the exact small-scale relation below the final
breakpoint and monotonicity above it. The second is
$q(\lambda-1)<\lambda(q+1)$.

We claim that $T$ preserves the following closed bounds:
\begin{align*}
 -P(t)\leq f_1(t)&\leq q^{-1}P(\lambda t),\\
 -q^{-2}P(\lambda^2t)\leq f_0(t)&\leq q^{-1}P(\lambda t).
\end{align*}
The state-$0$ equation proves its bounds directly. The first state-$1$
branch then follows using $q^{-2}P(\lambda^2t)\leq P(t)$.

For a zero-block branch let $v=\lambda u$ and
$t=1+j/\lambda+rv$. The lower bound is equivalent to
\[
 P(t)\geq j/q-1+sP(v).
\]
Since $t\geq1$ and $d+rv-t=(a-j)/\lambda$, the slope bound and
\eqref{bmf:eq:polygon-main} give
\begin{align*}
 P(t)&\geq P(d+rv)-m(a-j)/\lambda\\
     &\geq e+sP(v)-(a-j)/q\\
     &=j/q-1+sP(v).
\end{align*}
For the upper bound, $t\geq1>t_*/\lambda$, so its desired value is the
constant $A/q$. The old lower bound on $f_0$ gives
\[
 1-j/q-f_0(u)/q\leq1+A/q^3\leq A/q,
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to $e/q\geq1$, true since
$e=q+b$.

For a trailing-one branch write $v=\lambda u$ and
$t=d+\ell+rv$. Its lower bound follows from
\[
 P(t)\geq P(d+rv)\geq e+sP(v)\geq e-\ell+sP(v).
\]
Its upper bound follows from
$-e+\ell+P(u)/q\leq A/q$, since $\ell\leq b-1<e$.
This verifies every branch.

The initial straight profiles belong to these bounds: $P$ is concave,
$P(\lambda)=A$, and $A/q>1$, so $P(\lambda t)\geq At\geq qt$
for $0\leq t\leq1$; the state-$1$ lower bound follows in the same way
on $[0,\lambda]$. Contraction therefore gives the asserted bounds for
the limit profiles. In particular,
\[
 |f_1(t)|\leq Kt^\alpha,
 \qquad K=\frac A{t_*^\alpha}.
\]
In fact the same bound holds for every finite profile $f_{1,k}$, since the
bounds contain the initial profiles and are invariant under $T$. Rescaling
a sufficiently long substitution prefix therefore gives the useful exact
finite inequality
\[
 |H(t)|\leq Kt^\alpha\quad(t\geq0),
 \qquad |X(N)|\leq K L_N^\alpha.
\]
This is an inequality in geometric length; the corresponding symmetric
physical-length constant need not hold without a lower-order correction at
every finite $N$.

The constant is attained. Set $s_*=t_*/\lambda<1$. The first and first
trailing branch equations give
\[
 f_1(s_*)=-q^{-1}f_1(t_*),\qquad
 f_1(t_*)=-e-q^{-1}f_1(s_*).
\]
Here $t_*=d+t_*/\lambda^2$ ensures that the second evaluation is legal.
Solving gives $f_1(t_*)=-A$ and $f_1(s_*)=A/q$. Consequently
\[
 \sup_{t>0}\frac{|F(t)|}{t^\alpha}
 =K
 =\frac{\lambda-1}{q^2-1}
   \left(\frac{\lambda^2-1}{q+1}\right)^\alpha.
\]
Both signs attain this normalized amplitude at geometrically rescaled
copies of $t_*$ and $s_*$.

\subsection{Conversion from geometric time to prefix length}

Solving for the letter counts gives the exact identities
\[
 N=\frac{q+1}{\lambda+q}L_N
    +\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda+q}X(N),
 \qquad
 L_N=cN-\frac{\lambda-1}{q+1}X(N).
\]
The identity $X(N)=F(L_N)$ and the sharp bound on $F$ first
give $X(N)=O(N^\alpha)$, because $N\leq L_N\leq\lambda N$.
The H\"older bound then yields
\[
 X(N)=F(cN)+O(N^{\alpha^2}).
\]
Define
\[
 \Theta(s)=\lambda^{-\alpha s}F(\lambda^s).
\]
This function is continuous and anti-periodic with step $1$. Its range
over a full period is $[-K,K]$: it is a continuous interval, the sharp
bound places it inside that interval, and the two explicitly exhibited
points attain both endpoints. Every phase is approached by integer
subsequences $N_k$ nearest to $c^{-1}\lambda^{2k+s}$. Therefore the full
cluster set of $X(N)/N^\alpha$ is $[-c^\alpha K,c^\alpha K]$.
The identity $c^\alpha K=\mathcal C_{a,b}$ proves the theorem.

\subsection{Explicit extremizing prefixes and a second check of constants}

Define legal prefixes immediately before a one by
\[
 P_0=\varnothing,\qquad P_{k+1}=\tau^2(P_k)\,1\,0^a.
\]
Their exact weights and geometric lengths are
\[
 X(P_k)=\frac{q(\lambda-1)}{q^2-1}(q^{2k}-1),\qquad
 L(P_k)=\frac{\lambda(q+1)}{\lambda^2-1}(\lambda^{2k}-1).
\]
Their physical lengths consequently are
\begin{align*}
 |P_k|={}&
 \frac{\lambda(q+1)^2}{(\lambda+q)(\lambda^2-1)}
       (\lambda^{2k}-1)\\
 &+\frac{q(\lambda-1)^2}{(\lambda+q)(q^2-1)}
       (q^{2k}-1).
\end{align*}
Since $q<\lambda$ and $\lambda^\alpha=q$, direct division verifies
\[
 \frac{X(P_k)}{|P_k|^\alpha}\longrightarrow\mathcal C_{a,b}.
\]
The legal prefixes $Q_k=\tau(P_k)$ satisfy
$X(Q_k)=-qX(P_k)$ and $|Q_k|/|P_k|\to\lambda$, so their normalized
weights tend to $-\mathcal C_{a,b}$.

\paragraph{Scope and priority.}
The continuous substitution-profile construction and broad spectral
power-law phenomenon have general predecessors. The result established
here is the exact amplitude for this specified family, with a branchwise
concave-envelope certificate and complete physical-length cluster set.
No claim of a previously unknown general limit-profile theorem is required.
The graph is a graph-directed self-affine object; its scalar normalized
cluster set is an interval and should not be described as a Cantor set.


\subsection{A square-root example with an exact finite inequality}
\label{bmf:sec:sqrt-example}

Take
\[
 \tau(0)=1,\qquad \tau(1)=1\,0^8\,1 .
\]
Then $q=2$, $\lambda=4$, $\alpha=1/2$, $c=2$, and
$K=\sqrt5$. The sharp asymptotic statement becomes
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:sqrt-ten}
 \operatorname{Clust}_{N\to\infty}
 \frac{\#0-2\#1}{\sqrt N}=[-\sqrt{10},\sqrt{10}].
\end{equation}
There is also a particularly simple statement valid at every
finite prefix. Since $L_N=2N-X(N)$, the geometric inequality
$X(N)^2\leq5L_N$ is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:finite-quadratic}
 \boxed{X(N)^2+5X(N)\leq10N.}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
$|X(N)+5/2|\leq\sqrt{10N+25/4}$.
This explains why a finite negative ratio $X(N)/\sqrt N$
can lie below $-\sqrt{10}$ while converging to the proved
asymptotic envelope.

The positive extremizing prefixes have
\[
 X(P_k)=2(4^k-1),\qquad
 |P_k|=\frac{2\cdot16^k+5\cdot4^k-7}{5}.
\]
The first lengths are $9,117,1701,26469,420453$.
Their ratios increase toward $\sqrt{10}$; the asymptotic
limit follows directly from these formulas, without a numerical
fit.

More generally, for an integer $q\geq2$, the parameters
$a=q^3$, $b=q^2-q-1$ give $\lambda=q^2$ and $\alpha=1/2$.

\[
 \mathcal C_{a,b}=\sqrt{q(q-1)(q^2+1)}
\]
and the exact finite inequality
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:sqrt-family}
 X(N)^2+(q-1)^2(q^2+1)X(N)
 \leq q(q-1)(q^2+1)N .
\end{equation}
Indeed here $K^2=(q-1)(q^2+1)$, $c=q$, and
$L_N=qN-(q-1)X(N)$. This is an infinite family of

\subsection{Consequences for phase-dependent empirical distributions}
\label{bmf:sec:phase-distributions}

The continuous profile determines more than the cluster interval.
For $n\geq1$ put $Y(n)=X(n)/n^\alpha$ and
$G(s)=c^\alpha\Theta(s)$, a continuous function of period $2$.
Theorem~\ref{bmf:thm:supercritical-amplitude} gives
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:phase-approx}
 Y(n)=G(\log_\lambda(cn))+O_{a,b}(n^{-\alpha(1-\alpha)}).
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Logarithmic sampling and phase subsequences]
\label{bmf:cor:phase-laws}
Let $H_M=\sum_{n=1}^{M}1/n$. The logarithmically weighted
empirical probability measures satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:log-law}
 \frac1{H_M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}\frac1n\,\delta_{Y(n)}
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \nu,\qquad
 \int h\,\mathrm d\nu=\frac12\int_0^2h(G(s))\,\mathrm ds
\end{equation}
for every bounded continuous function $h$.
The limit is symmetric and has support
$[-\mathcal C_{a,b},\mathcal C_{a,b}]$.

If $M_j\to\infty$ and
$\log_\lambda(cM_j)\bmod2\to s$, then ordinary uniform sampling
has the subsequential law
\begin{equation}\label{bmf:eq:phase-law}
 \frac1{M_j}\sum_{n=1}^{M_j}\delta_{Y(n)}
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 \operatorname{Law}\bigl(G(s+\log_\lambda U)\bigr),
 \qquad U\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1).
\end{equation}
These formulas describe all subsequential laws, allowing that
different phases might induce the same measure.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The error in \eqref{bmf:eq:phase-approx} tends to zero and all the
variables are bounded, so it can be removed against a continuous
test function. In the logarithmic average, uniform continuity
allows replacement of the sum by
\[
 \frac1{\log M}\int_1^M
     h(G(\log_\lambda(cu)))\,\frac{\mathrm du}{u}.
\]
The substitution $s=\log_\lambda(cu)$ turns this into an average
of a continuous function of period $2$ over an interval whose
length tends to infinity. This proves \eqref{bmf:eq:log-law}.
Anti-periodicity gives symmetry. Continuity and the attained
extreme values show that every open interval meeting the range
has a nonempty open preimage and therefore positive measure.

For \eqref{bmf:eq:phase-law}, first restrict $n/M_j$ to $[\varepsilon,1]$.
There the phase approximation is uniform and the sum is a Riemann
sum for $h(G(s+\log_\lambda u))$. The omitted proportion is at
most $\varepsilon$. Let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
Finally every sequence of phases has a convergent subsequence
modulo $2$, and every prescribed phase is approached by integer
cutoffs nearest to $c^{-1}\lambda^{2j+s}$.
\end{proof}

No absolute continuity or absence of atoms is asserted for these
pushforward measures. Those properties depend on the level sets
and regularity of the profile and are natural next questions.

\subsection{An economical histogram algorithm}

For a fixed word, compute the finite Laurent polynomials $S$, $T$,
$K_0$, $K_1$, and $\Delta$. To obtain the even-depth histogram
for state $c$, accumulate
\[
 H\leftarrow1,\quad Q\leftarrow K_c;\qquad
 H\leftarrow H+Q,\quad Q\leftarrow Q\Delta
 \quad\hbox{for }j=0,\ldots,m-1 .
\]
The support width grows linearly in $m$, whereas the literal word
length grows like $a^{2m}$. For a fixed substitution, multiplication
by the fixed-support polynomial $\Delta$ gives
$O_w(m^2)$ integer coefficient operations for this elementary
implementation. Coefficient bit lengths are $O_w(m)$; this
operation count does not treat arbitrary-size integer arithmetic
as having a fixed bit cost. Odd depths use the state-$0$ identity.
The supplied programs implement the formulas independently of
literal word generation whenever possible.

\label{bmf:sec:research}

The exact representation closes several questions while making
others precise. The following projects have distinct mathematical
content; none is claimed solved by the present computations.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=7pt]
\item \textbf{Bounded-regime value distributions.}
For the repository's original cases $a\leq b+1$, determine the
limiting distribution, if any, of $\#0-q\#1$ under uniform prefix
sampling. For $(a,b)=(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$, obtain explicit laws
for the OEIS words A284368 and A284369 and transfer them to
the position sequences. Decide whether the laws have densities,
atoms, or a singular continuous part. Our critical positive
mixture does not automatically extend across $q=1$.

\item \textbf{Local large deviations with a leading constant.}
For the critical class, prove a saddle-point formula for
$\Prob(X_r=k)$ uniformly when $k/r$ lies in a compact subset
of $(-d/2,d/2)$, evaluating the prefactor from $g_0$ and $g_1$.
Then characterize the additional cutoff dependence when
$N$ is not a substitution length. The present pressure gives
the exponential cost; the arithmetic phase and exact leading
constant remain to be organized.

\item \textbf{Extreme atoms and near-extreme multiplicities.}
The endpoint rate depends only on $c_-c_+$, while the run family
has unique even-block extremal prefixes. Classify the exact
multiplicities and locations of the first $j$ levels inward
from each extreme for a general profile. Determine how much
of this information is determined by $S$, and what further
data are needed to recover first-hit positions in the word.

\item \textbf{Counting words with a prescribed profile.}
Theorem~\ref{bmf:thm:realization} counts distinct profiles and constructs
one representative. Determine the number of admissible words
realizing each coefficient vector $(c_h)$. The Euler-trail
description suggests an exact formula using directed trail
enumeration, with care about indistinguishable parallel edges
and the prescribed first letter. This would refine the
A001787 interpretation and identify the largest histogram
equivalence classes.

\item \textbf{Inverse problems from fluctuation data.}
Determine when two distinct profiles have the same pressure,
that is,
$S_1(t)S_1(t^{-1})=S_2(t)S_2(t^{-1})$.
Translations and reflection are immediate sources of agreement,
but factorization can produce further coincidences.
Classify which additional seed or parity data recover $S$.
This separates equality of bulk laws from equality of complete
finite histograms.

\item \textbf{Regularity and distribution of the supercritical profile.}
Determine the sharp local H\"older exponents and the sizes of
level sets of $\Theta$. Is the logarithmic-sampling law
\eqref{bmf:eq:log-law} absolutely continuous, atomless, or singular
for particular $(a,b)$? Obtain a usable density equation where
one exists. The scalar cluster set is already known to be an
interval; the unresolved geometry concerns the graph and
its induced measures.

\item \textbf{Sharper physical-time asymptotics.}
The conversion from geometric length to letter count gives an
error $O(N^{\alpha^2})$ using global H\"older continuity.
Find whether that exponent is sharp, and identify parameters
or phases where a smaller error holds. A second-order
expansion may require local information about $\Theta$ rather
than a derivative, because differentiability is not assumed.

\item \textbf{Optimizing supercritical arrangements.}
Keep the incidence matrix fixed but rearrange the zeros and
ones in the long image. Determine which arrangements maximize
or minimize the normalized supercritical amplitude. The
critical variance comparison uses ordered zero heights;
its supercritical analogue involves a discounted branch
optimization. Seek an algorithm that outputs a verifiable
polygon or other finite envelope certificate.

\item \textbf{Higher alphabets and roots of unity.}
For primitive substitutions with an eigenvalue that is a
root of unity of order greater than two, group that many
levels and ask when the histogram transfer matrix admits a
nonnegative low-rank remainder. A useful target is an exact
finite mixture of independent vector increments. The
invariant-section identity by itself does not establish
this positivity.

\item \textbf{Formal proof and certified computation.}
Formalize the positive histogram identity and its finite
moment consequences first: they involve finite words,
Laurent polynomials, and elementary sums. A separate
formalization could check the finitely many branch inequalities
of the supercritical envelope. For publication-quality
numerical constants beyond the closed forms here, add
interval-certified saddle-point calculations.

from substitution import Substitution

model = Substitution(3)

\section{A compact proof checklist}
\label{qmw:app:checklist}

For ease of independent checking, the argument can be reduced to the
following algebraic chain.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item $q^2+(b+1)q=a$ and $X=\#0-q\#1$ imply
      $X(\tau(P))=-qX(P)$.
\item Legal internal prefixes give exactly
      \eqref{qmw:eq:K0}--\eqref{qmw:eq:K1}.
\item Since $a-bq=q(q+1)>0$, the extreme state-$1$ digits are $0$ and $a-q$.
\item The extremum operator is $q$-contractive, and
      \[
      (-q({\widehat C}+1),\ {\widehat C}-1,\ -qC,\ {\widehat C})
      \]
      is its fixed point because ${\widehat C}(1-q^2)=a-q$ and
      ${\widehat C}-q^2({\widehat C}+1)=qb>0$.
\item Immediately before the $n$th one and zero, the error maps are
      $q-X$ and $(X+1)/q$, respectively.
\item The two-step extremal cycle has affine map
      $x\mapsto q^2x+a-q$, proving sharpness and the phase transition.
\end{enumerate}
