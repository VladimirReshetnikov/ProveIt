PROOF STATUS
4 October 2026

The article contains conventional mathematical proofs. It is not a
Lean/Rocq formalization and is not a report of peer review.

Independently reviewed conclusions:

- Boolean degree bound for adaptive decision trees.
- Self-contained fourth-moment hypercontractive estimate and the
  resulting two-sided sign bound.
- Conditional-cylinder argument giving probability zero for divergent
  excess under uniformly bounded depth, without a visibility-fanout
  or locality assumption.
- Reachable-path stochastic domination and the simultaneous lower
  frontier for every nonnegative nondecreasing query cost.
- Eldredge block implementation, truncated geometric query law, and
  simultaneous attainment of the complete cost frontier.
- Stage schedule, all-prefix rates, finite almost-sure loss, central
  limit theorem with nonconvergent moments, mean population query
  cost, and maximum query cost.
- Exact covariance/influence formula, sharp weighted variance bound,
  mean-square and almost-sure density results, and spectral escape.
- Finite saturation lower bounds, Fourier purity, uniform projections,
  adaptive leaf parity constraints, affine rigidity, exact parameter
  criterion, and replicated trace sufficiency.

Review corrections incorporated:

- The expected-query threshold concerns a uniform supremum equal to
  two, not a common strict upper bound below two.
- The all-black construction belongs to Eldredge; it is not presented
  as newly invented here.
- Affine Hamming perfect-code conclusions at strict majority are
  restricted to odd team sizes; even sizes can have replicated columns.
- The final constant-weight linear-code classification is credited to
  Bonisoli and rederived in the binary case.
- Eventual strict positivity is explicitly distinguished from
  divergence to positive infinity.
- External randomness and its cost convention are explicitly scoped.

Finite verification:

19 exhaustive cases; 59,546 hat assignments.
838,498 own-hat flip comparisons.
30,927 nonnegative pair increments and 30,927 pair prefixes.

The included program is ordinary Python using exact integer arithmetic.
Its code has been inspected and its run completed successfully.
These checks do not prove the general or infinite statements.
