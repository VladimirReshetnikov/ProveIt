5. DIRECT GLAZER CONNECTION AND EXTERNAL PRIOR ART

Nathaniel Eldredge:
A probabilistic look at the infinite hat-guessing game.
arXiv:2508.02828v2, revised 24 October 2025.
https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02828v2
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02828

The landing page lists v1 of 4 August 2025 and v2 of 24 October 2025.
The relevant full-text discussion in Section 6 was inspected.

The paragraph immediately before Proposition 6.6 explicitly credits
Elliot Glazer and Charles Wang with suggesting the study of the number
of excess correct guesses. This is direct evidence for the research
intersection; no inference about Glazer's current employment, affiliations,
or private interests is needed.

Proposition 6.6 ALREADY supplies the all-black exceptional-block strategy,
the singleton-white success mechanism, and the Borel-Cantelli argument
for D_n -> +infinity almost surely. The present manuscript does not
claim to invent this block rule.

The query implementation used here observes that:
- a D player always guesses opposite its mate, using exactly one query;
- an S player queries its mate first, then scans other hats only if the
  mate is black, stopping at the first white hat.

For an even block size t >= 4, this gives:
E Q_D = 1;
E Q_S = 2 - 2^(-(t-2)) < 2;
P(Q_S > k) <= 2^(-k), k >= 1.

The query distribution approaches the positive geometric law as block
sizes grow. The cost frontier is obtained from this efficient execution
and from the new obstruction/lower-bound argument. The existence of an
almost-surely divergent strategy itself is Eldredge's result.

Remark 6.8 asks about logarithmic or faster excess. The ProveIt predecessor
already addresses those growth questions. They are not newly resolved
again by the current manuscript.

6. NEW-CLAIM BOUNDARY

The manuscript's proposed contributions are the bounded-query obstruction
at probability-zero strength, its conditional-cylinder formulation, the
sharp deterministic adaptive-query cost frontier, and the finite
consequences proved from these arguments.

The final finite result goes beyond a parameter-family construction.
For first-moment saturation at threshold s > n/2, it proves a minimum
uniform inspection depth s-1, Fourier purity and uniform projections
at that depth, and an adaptive leaf argument forcing the all-wrong set
to be affine. Once affinity is established, its constant-weight dual
is classified using the binary case of Bonisoli's classical result.
The resulting necessary and sufficient parameters are
n = m(2^r-1), s = m*2^(r-1), with integers r,m >= 1.
A replicated trace construction realizes every permitted pair.

The proposed refinement is the implication from adaptive inspection
optimality to affine failure-set structure and its cost consequences.
The underlying constant-weight linear-code classification is not
claimed as new. The theorem classifies parameter pairs and failure
sets at the sharp depth; it does not classify all prediction trees or
all unconstrained probability-optimal strategies. At strict majority,
the affine Hamming-code conclusion is restricted to odd team sizes;
even sizes may have repeated parity-check columns.

The sharp mean threshold is 2 as a supremum across players. Individual
expected costs in the upper construction are strictly below 2, but their
supremum is 2; these are different statements.

The lower expected-cost inference uses deterministic query trees under
independent fair hats. Uncharged external randomization must not be
silently averaged into that theorem: unconditional expected cost under
a random public seed need not bound the depth of each conditional tree.

The finite computational companion checks finite instances and exact
identities only. It does not replace the written infinite proofs and
does not make the manuscript kernel-checked. Execution results belong
to the companion verification record rather than this source-inspection
record.
