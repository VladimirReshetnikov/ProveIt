\begin{resultbox}
Let $\PP_\kappa$ be the site of free filters on $\om$ generated, modulo
the cofinite filter, by fewer than $\kappa$ sets, and let $\C_\kappa$ be
its dense-site Boolean algebra. Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \text{the original completion survives (marked)}&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\kappa\leq\cp,\\
 \text{no atoms and no geometric points}&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\kappa\leq\cu.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For $\kappa>\cp$ no complete Boolean map fixing the original events
exists from the old completion to $\C_\kappa$ (both sources) or from
$\C_\kappa$ back to it (source 10). The distributivity number $\ch$ is
the exact local joint-decidability boundary of the old completion. The
associated reduced-power sheaves are computed by Boolean mixing; they
keep finitary transfer, and at the successor stages their ultrafilter
specializations are countably saturated.
\end{resultbox}

\begin{abstract}
The ProveIt report on two-valued gluing obstructions proves that the
Fr\'echet reduced-power presheaf is not a sheaf even on the site of
countably generated free filters. It computes the associated sheaf on the
full filter site as a product of ultrapowers, but that computation cannot
be transferred to the smaller site: its ultrafilters are absent. That
report explicitly does not assert anything about other filter sites. This
report studies them. It merges two manuscripts written independently
against the same repository snapshot.

Let $\PP_\kappa$ be the free filters on $\om$ generated, modulo the
cofinite filter, by fewer than $\kappa$ sets. Its Boolean completion
$\C_\kappa$ is the regular-open algebra of the topology on $\om^*$
generated by the ultrafilter-extension sets of these filters. The
original $\pow(\om)/\Fin$ remains order dense exactly for
$\kappa\leq\cp$. Above this threshold there is no complete Boolean
homomorphism from the original completion to $\C_\kappa$ fixing all
original events, and none from $\C_\kappa$ back to the original
completion either. The distributivity number $\ch$ is the least size of
a family of completed binary events of the original completion that
cannot be decided jointly on a dense set of regions, and at the
successor stages above $\ch$ the completion changes even abstractly.
Atoms are exactly ultrafilters of character below $\kappa$, so they
first occur at $\cu^+$. We characterize full atomicity, describe the
residual part as the region where forcing adds a new ultrafilter, and
derive a three-stage example from Mill\'an's published model. The
reduced-power sheafification is constructed by Boolean mixing.
Finitary first-order truth is preserved and existential values are
attained, and at the successor stages every ultrafilter specialization
is countably saturated; a $\cp$-fold conjunction of fixed old predicates
separates the completions sharply. Global rings over fields are von
Neumann regular, with the completion as their idempotent algebra. For
$2\leq|M|\leq\cc$ the raw global reduced power, the countably generated
sheafification and the full-site sheafification have $\cc$, $2^\cc$ and
$2^{2^\cc}$ elements. A final program collects the questions of both
manuscripts, re-scoped where one answers the other.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{A gap left by the existing gluing analysis}
Massas's possibility-semantic construction of the hyperreal line uses
reduced powers indexed by proper filters extending the cofinite filter.
His published question after Fact~5.10 asks whether the uncountability
assumption in his failure-of-sheafness argument is necessary
\cite[p.~517]{Massas}. The ProveIt manuscript
\emph{A two-valued obstruction to gluing Fr\'echet powers} gives a
negative answer using two constants, and also proves failure on the
countably generated subsite \cite{Repo}. We do not claim that resolution
again. In Massas's setting the full filter poset and its regular-open
algebra are already part of the literature
\cite[Sections~5.2 and~6.1]{Massas}. The repository report is
unrefereed and is not a Lean theorem, and no result below is justified
merely by its presence in that report.

There is, however, an important distinction between the two associated
sheaves. On the full filter site, maximal filters form a dense family;
in $\ZFC$ the associated sheaf has values
\[
 F\longmapsto\prod_{U\supseteq F} M^\om/U.
\]
On the countably generated free-filter site there are no ultrafilter
objects. Reusing the full-site formula on that subsite would give the
wrong answer. Our starting question is thus precise:
\begin{quote}
What is the Booleanization of the smaller filter site, and exactly how
much filter complexity must be admitted before its truth values and its
points change?
\end{quote}
We answer this question and compute the corresponding reduced-power
sheafification. The two-valued obstruction is background motivation,
not an unproved premise of our results.

\subsection{The non-claim of the earlier report that this report continues}
\label{fbc:sec:nonclaim}
The earlier report ends its hyperreal discussion with a limitation of
scope \cite[Section~10]{Repo}:
\begin{quote}
``The result also depends on the category and its topology. It is not an
assertion that every sheaf-based construction of nonstandard analysis
fails, or that every reduced-power presheaf on a different filter site
has the same behavior. The relative countably generated site considered
in Section~7 was analyzed separately; no unexamined change of topology
is implicit in the main theorem.''
\end{quote}
This report continues exactly that non-claim: it examines the
reduced-power presheaf on a whole cardinal-indexed family of other filter
sites, the bounded-character sites $\PP_\kappa$ of
Section~\ref{fbc:sec:filters}, which interpolate between the countably
generated site of that report's Section~7 ($\PP_{\aleph_1}$) and its full
site ($\PP_{\cc^+}$).

It does \emph{not} re-claim that report's results. Its two-valued
nongluing theorem (its Theorem~1.1), the failure on the countably
generated site (its Section~7.1), separatedness (its Theorem~5.1), finite
pasting (its Theorem~5.2), the local finite-quotient classification (its Theorem~8.5) and

Where the present text reproves one of them (separatedness in

one question that report names, whether the infinite-quotient direction
of its Theorem~8.5 needs dependent choice; see question~R8 in
Section~\ref{fbc:sec:future}.

\subsection{Questions answered and what is classical}
There are several genuinely different meanings of ``complete the
Fr\'echet power.'' The raw binary reduced power is $\pow(\om)/\Fin$. Its
order-dense Boolean completion is atomless. The full filter-site
completion is instead a power-set algebra, hence atomic. This raises an
interpolation problem: how much filter complexity is needed to pass from
one behavior to the other? The report answers three questions about it.
First, exactly when does the original modulo-finite algebra remain order
dense in the completed truth-value algebra, and when does every complete
comparison fixing the original events fail? Second, when do complete
Boolean points appear, and what part of the completion do they see?
Third, how large a family of binary sections can be decided
simultaneously on a dense collection of local regions? The answers
involve $\cp$, $\cu$ and $\ch$, respectively. A change in the complete
algebra of truth values need not destroy transfer or saturation, and the
model-theoretic sections show that at the successor stages it does not:
the mixed Fr\'echet models keep finitary transfer, attained existential
values and a localized countable realization property.

The pseudointersection number $\cp$, the ultrafilter number $\cu$, the
distributivity number $\ch$, and their basic filter interpretations are
classical \cite{Blass}. Likewise, Stone duality, Boolean completion,
closure-to-distributivity arguments, the countable diagonal, and the
passage from Boolean-valued models to sheaves by mixing are established
tools. The recent treatment of Pierobon and Viale explicitly develops the
relationship between mixing and dense-topology sheaves \cite{PV}, and
Ulrich's treatment of full Boolean-valued models covers their ordinary
ultrafilter specializations \cite[Sections~2--3]{Ulrich}. We give the
specialized proofs needed here so that no semantic convention is left
implicit.

The contribution is a single, explicitly marked hierarchy connecting
these tools to the repository's filter site: an exact obstruction to
complete event-preserving comparison, in both directions; the complete
atom and point spectrum; an early-atomicity application; a local-reading
interpretation of $\ch$; a concrete sheafification; a sharp infinitary
truth-value gap; countable saturation of specializations; and exact
cardinalities of the endpoints. The cardinal thresholds are translations
of classical combinatorics, not newly discovered cardinal invariants.
Recovering $\cp\leq\ch$ (Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:abstract}) is a
consistency check, not a new inequality. The full-site power-set
identification is already discussed by Massas, and the full-site product
sheaf is already in the repository report. The resulting statements are
research extensions of the inspected report; targeted literature
searches by both source manuscripts did not locate this exact
bounded-character package, but that is not an exhaustive novelty or
priority determination.

\begin{resultbox}
\textbf{Main distinction.} The same finite Boolean algebraic events can
sit inside different complete algebras. Preserving the old events and
preserving arbitrary joins are separate requirements. The first does not
force the second, and above $\cp$ the two requirements become
incompatible with either canonical direction of comparison.
\end{resultbox}

\section{Filters, generating character, and dense covers}
\label{fbc:sec:filters}

All arguments in this report take place in $\ZFC$; all sets and all
cardinalities are external. Put $\cc=2^{\aleph_0}$ and
\[
 \Fr=\{A\subseteq\om:\om\setminus A\text{ is finite}\},
 \qquad \B=\pow(\om)/\Fin.
\]
A free filter means a proper filter containing $\Fr$; in particular it
has no finite member. The notation $A\subseteq^*D$ means that
$A\setminus D$ is finite, and $[A]$ is the class of $A$ in $\B$.
Boolean order on $\B$ is almost inclusion. A complete Boolean
homomorphism is always unital and preserves \emph{all} joins; it
therefore preserves complements and all meets.

\begin{definition}[Two characters \src{08 Def.~2.1; 10 \S2.1}]\label{fbc:def:characters}
For a free filter $F$, set
\[
 \chi_*(F)=\min\{|\mathcal A|:
 F=\gen{\Fr\cup\mathcal A}\},
\]
the generating character modulo finite sets (source~08), and let
$\chi(F)$ be the least cardinality of a base $\mathcal A\subseteq F$,
meaning that every member of $F$ contains a member of $\mathcal A$
(source~10's ordinary base character, not a $\pi$-character). For an
infinite cardinal $\kappa$ define
\[
 \PP_\kappa=\{F:F\text{ is a free filter and }\chi_*(F)<\kappa\}.
\]
The order is the forcing order: $G\leq F$ means $G\supseteq F$, and a
\emph{stronger} condition is a larger filter. Equivalently, the category
has an arrow $G\to F$ for every such inclusion.
\end{definition}

The use of $\chi_*$ is deliberate. The cofinite filter has
$\chi_*(\Fr)=0$, although its usual inclusion-base character is
countably infinite. For a nonprincipal ultrafilter the two characters
coincide: closing a family under finite intersections and deleting
finite sets multiplies an infinite size only by $\aleph_0$, while
no nonprincipal ultrafilter has a countable base. The latter fact also
follows from Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:cardinal-bounds} below. The next lemma
makes the comparison exact; it is the dictionary between the two
sources' indexings.

\begin{lemma}[The index shift \src{merge}]
\label{fbc:lem:index-shift}
For every free filter $F$,
\[
 \chi_*(F)\leq\chi(F)\leq\max(\chi_*(F),\aleph_0),
 \qquad \chi(F)\geq\aleph_0.
\]
Consequently, for every infinite cardinal $\lambda$, source~10's site
$\mathcal F_\lambda=\{F:\chi(F)\leq\lambda\}$ equals $\PP_{\lambda^+}$.
For a free ultrafilter $U$, $\chi(U)=\chi_*(U)$, so the ultrafilter
number $\cu$ is the same whether defined with $\chi$ (source~10) or with
$\chi_*$ (source~08).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A base of $F$ generates $F$, so $\chi_*(F)\leq\chi(F)$. Conversely, if
$\mathcal A$ generates $F$ modulo $\Fr$, every member of $F$ contains a
set $A_1\cap\dots\cap A_m\cap C$ with $A_i\in\mathcal A$ and $C$
cofinite, hence contains $(A_1\cap\dots\cap A_m)\setminus n$ for some
$n<\om$. These sets lie in $F$ and form a base of size at most
$\max(|\mathcal A|,\aleph_0)$. If $F$ had a finite base, the intersection
$B$ of its members would lie in $F$ and be infinite; then
$B\setminus\{\min B\}\in F$ would contain no member of the base, since
each contains $B$. Hence $\chi(F)\geq\aleph_0$. For infinite $\lambda$
the three inequalities give $\chi(F)\leq\lambda\iff\chi_*(F)\leq\lambda
\iff\chi_*(F)<\lambda^+$. For a free ultrafilter, $\chi(U)$ is
uncountable (Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:cardinal-bounds}), so
$\chi_*(U)$ is uncountable too and the bounds coincide.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The finite-generation endpoint \src{08 Lemma~2.2; 10 eq.~(1)}]
\label{fbc:lem:principal}
For every infinite $A\subseteq\om$, let
\[
 F_A=\{D\subseteq\om:A\subseteq^*D\}.
\]
Then $\PP_\om$ consists exactly of the filters $F_A$, and
$[A]\mapsto F_A$ is an order isomorphism from $\B^+$ onto
$\PP_\om$. For any free filter $F$,
\[
 F_A\supseteq F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A\subseteq^*D\text{ for every }D\in F.
\]
Each $F_A$ has the countable base $A\setminus n$ ($n<\om$), and
$F_{A'}\supseteq F_A$ exactly when $A'\subseteq^*A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A finite generating family can be replaced by its intersection $A$.
Properness implies that $A$ is infinite, and adjoining the cofinite
sets gives exactly $F_A$. Moreover, $F_A\supseteq F_D$ precisely when
$A\subseteq^*D$. The equivalence is just the definition of $F_A$, and
the base is displayed by the definition.
\end{proof}

Source~10 calls the filters $F_A$ the principal-modulo-finite subsite.
``Principal'' is meant modulo finite sets: $F_A$ is a free filter, not
an ordinary principal ultrafilter. For $\kappa=\om_1$, the objects of
$\PP_\kappa$ are precisely the countably generated free filters. Every
free filter has at most $\cc=2^{\aleph_0}$ generators, so $\PP_{\cc^+}$
is the full free-filter site, and $\PP_\kappa=\PP_{\cc^+}$ for every
$\kappa>\cc$ (source~10: $\mathcal F_\lambda=\mathcal F_\cc$ for
$\lambda\geq\cc$).

A sieve $D$ at $F$ is a family of extensions of $F$ closed under further
extension. It is \emph{dense covering} if
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:dense-cover}
 \forall G\supseteq F\ (G\in\PP_\kappa\Rightarrow
 \exists H\in D\ (H\supseteq G)).
\end{equation}
The condition is relative to the allowed filters: the same family need
not have the same covering behavior after the site is enlarged.

\begin{lemma}[Small extensions \src{08 Lemma~2.3; 10 Lemma~2.1}]
\label{fbc:lem:small-extensions}
Let $F$ be a free filter and $A\subseteq\om$. The filter generated by
$F\cup\{A\}$ is proper exactly when $A$ is $F$-positive, meaning
$\om\setminus A\notin F$. If $F\in\PP_\kappa$ and $A$ is $F$-positive,
then $\gen{F\cup\{A\}}$ is a member of $\PP_\kappa$. The filter
generated by two compatible members of $\PP_\kappa$ is again a member.
Thus compatibility can be checked by ordinary filter compatibility
without leaving the site, and it does not depend on the bound once the
two filters are admitted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An improper generated filter would contain an empty intersection
$B\cap A$ with $B\in F$, which is equivalent to $\om\setminus A\in F$.
Otherwise every member of $F$ meets $A$; since $F$ contains all cofinite
sets, every such intersection is in fact infinite. The number of
generators increases by at most one. Likewise, a union of two generating
families has size below $\kappa$, and its filter is proper exactly when
the original filters are compatible. Only a finite union of small
families is involved, so regularity of $\kappa$ is not needed.
\end{proof}

\section{A concrete representation of the Booleanization}
\label{fbc:sec:representation}

\subsection{A topology on the set of free ultrafilters}
Let $X=\Ult(\B)=\om^*$, with its Stone topology $\tau$. If $b=[A]\in\B$,
write
\[
 \widehat b=\{U\in X:A\in U\}.
\]
These sets are clopen, and they form a base for $\tau$. For a free
filter $F$ put
\[
 K_F=\{U\in X:F\subseteq U\}.
\]
The ultrafilter lemma gives $K_F\ne\varnothing$.

\begin{definition}[The small-intersection topology \src{08 Def.~3.1}]\label{fbc:def:topology}
Let $\tau_\kappa$ be the topology on $X$ generated by the sets $K_F$,
$F\in\PP_\kappa$. Equivalently, it is generated by nonempty
intersections of fewer than $\kappa$ original Stone clopens. Put
\[
 \C_\kappa=\RO(X,\tau_\kappa),
 \qquad e_\kappa:\B\longrightarrow\C_\kappa,
 \quad e_\kappa(b)=\widehat b.
\]
We keep this explicit convention instead of using an ambiguous
$G_\kappa$-modification notation.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Recovery and the clopen base \src{08 Lemma~3.2}]
\label{fbc:lem:recovery}
For free filters $F,G$ and $A\subseteq\om$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 A\in F&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K_F\subseteq\widehat{[A]},\\
 G\supseteq F&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K_G\subseteq K_F.
 \end{split}
\]
The sets $K_F$, $F\in\PP_\kappa$, form a base of nonempty
$\tau_\kappa$-clopens. Two such sets intersect exactly when their
filters are compatible, and a nonempty intersection is the set
corresponding to the generated joint filter.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The forward implication in the first equivalence is immediate. If
$A\notin F$, then $F\cup\{\om\setminus A\}$ generates a proper
filter: otherwise some member of $F$ would be contained in $A$.
An ultrafilter extension of this filter belongs to $K_F$ but not to
$\widehat{[A]}$. This proves the equivalence and, by applying it to
every member of $F$, proves the second one.

If $\mathcal A$ generates $F$ modulo $\Fr$, then
$K_F=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal A}\widehat{[A]}$. It is closed in $\tau$
and hence in the finer topology $\tau_\kappa$; it is open by definition.
Finite intersections remain of the same form, by
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:small-extensions}. The compatibility assertion follows
by extending the joint proper filter to an ultrafilter.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regular opens and the dense topology}
For a topological space $Y$, an open set $V$ is regular open when
$V=\Int\Cl V$. Its regular-open algebra is a complete Boolean algebra,
with operations
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:regular-operations}
 \bigvee_i V_i=\Int\Cl\!\bigcup_iV_i,
 \qquad \bigwedge_i V_i=\Int\!\bigcap_iV_i,
 \qquad \neg V=\Int(Y\setminus V).
\end{equation}
For completeness, if $W=\Int\bigcap_iV_i$ and each $V_i$ is regular
open, then $\Int\Cl W\subseteq\Int\Cl V_i=V_i$ for every $i$.
Being open, $\Int\Cl W$ is therefore contained in $W$; the reverse
inclusion holds for every open $W$. This verifies the meet formula.
The other formulas follow by the same regularization argument.

\begin{theorem}[Representation of the filter-site Booleanization \src{08 Thm~3.3}]
\label{fbc:thm:representation}
The map $F\mapsto K_F$ identifies $\PP_\kappa$ with an order-dense
subposet of $\C_\kappa^+$. The algebra $\C_\kappa$ is its Boolean
completion. A sieve $D$ covers $F$ exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:cover-join}
 \bigvee_{G\in D}K_G=K_F\qquad\text{in }\C_\kappa.
\end{equation}
Consequently the dense-site sheaf category is
\[
 \Sh(\PP_\kappa,J_{\rm dense})\simeq\Sh(\C_\kappa),
\]
where the right side is the sheaf category of the complete Boolean
algebra viewed as a locale, with covering families having join equal
to the object covered.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:recovery} gives the order embedding. Every nonempty
regular open set contains a nonempty basic clopen $K_F$, proving
order density. Separativity follows as well: if $K_G\nsubseteq K_F$,
the nonempty open difference $K_G\setminus K_F$ contains a condition
incompatible with $F$.

Let $V=\bigcup_{G\in D}K_G\subseteq K_F$. If $D$ is dense in the
sense of \eqref{fbc:eq:dense-cover}, every nonempty basic open in $K_F$
contains a member $K_H$ with $H\in D$. Thus $V$ is dense in $K_F$,
which is clopen, and its regularization is $K_F$.
Conversely, suppose $V$ is dense in $K_F$ and take $H\supseteq F$.
Then $K_H$ meets some $K_G$ with $G\in D$. The joint filter is allowed
and belongs to $D$ because $D$ is a sieve. It extends $H$, proving
\eqref{fbc:eq:dense-cover}.

Finally, restriction from the Boolean algebra to its dense subposet
preserves covers by \eqref{fbc:eq:cover-join}. It also loses no sheaf data:
every nonzero Boolean element is covered by the conditions below it,
and a section over it is uniquely a matching family on that cover.
This describes an inverse extension functor. The same matching-family
construction extends morphisms, and restricting and extending in
either order gives a natural isomorphism. This proves the claimed
equivalence without assuming that the locale has points.
\end{proof}

In particular, $\tau_\om=\tau$ and $\C_\om=\RO(\om^*)$ is the
ordinary order-dense completion of $\B$; this is source~10's $\mathbb C$,
which it also constructs as $\RO(\B^+)$. At the opposite endpoint,
every ultrafilter is allowed in $\PP_{\cc^+}$, so every singleton
$K_U=\{U\}$ is open. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:full-completion}
 \C_{\cc^+}=\pow(\om^*).
\end{equation}
This power-set algebra is generally \emph{not} the order-dense
completion of the original $\B$.

\subsection{The poset-side description}
\label{fbc:sec:poset-side}
Source~10 works with the regular opens of the filter poset itself.
An open subset of $\PP_\kappa$ is a family closed under stronger
extensions. For an open $V$, put
\begin{align}
 \neg V&=\{F:\text{no }G\supseteq F\text{ belongs to }V\},\label{fbc:eq:neg}\\
 \neg\neg V&=\{F:\text{for every }G\supseteq F\text{ there is }
 H\supseteq G\text{ in }V\},\label{fbc:eq:reg}
\end{align}
where all quantified filters belong to $\PP_\kappa$. A regular open is an
open fixed by $\neg\neg$. The regular opens form a complete Boolean
algebra $\RO(\PP_\kappa)$: its order is inclusion, its complement is
\eqref{fbc:eq:neg}, its finite meet is intersection, and its arbitrary
join is $\neg\neg(\bigcup_iV_i)$. In the extension topology arbitrary
intersections of opens are open; for regular opens they are also
regular, so arbitrary meets are intersections. A sieve at $F$ covers
$F$ exactly when the condition in \eqref{fbc:eq:reg} holds at $F$, which
is \eqref{fbc:eq:dense-cover}. The principal cone of $F$ is
\[
 \up F=\{G\in\PP_\kappa:G\supseteq F\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Cones and old events \src{10 Lemma~2.2}]
\label{fbc:lem:cones}
Every cone $\up F$ is a nonzero regular open. The cones are order
dense in $\RO(\PP_\kappa)$: every nonzero regular open contains one. The
map
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:j}
 j_\kappa:\B\longrightarrow\RO(\PP_\kappa),
 \qquad j_\kappa([A])=\{F:A\in F\},
\end{equation}
is an injective Boolean homomorphism. For infinite $A$ its value is
$\up F_A$, and for every $F$,
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:membership}
 \up F\subseteq j_\kappa([A])\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\in F.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $H$ does not extend $F$, choose $A\in F\setminus H$. By
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:small-extensions}, $H$ has a proper extension
containing $\om\setminus A$; no further extension can extend $F$. Thus
$H$ is not in the double negation of $\up F$. This proves regularity;
nonzeroness follows from $F\in\up F$. A nonempty open containing $F$
contains its cone, which proves order density.

Membership in a free filter is unchanged by a finite symmetric
difference. Also $A\in F$ if and only if $F\supseteq F_A$ when $A$ is
infinite. A filter lies in $\neg j_\kappa([A])$ exactly when it admits no
proper extension containing $A$, equivalently when $\om\setminus A\in F$.
Therefore $\neg j_\kappa([A])=j_\kappa([\om\setminus A])$. Intersections
are preserved by the filter axioms; finite joins then follow from
complements and intersections. If $A$ is infinite, $F_A$ witnesses that
$j_\kappa([A])\ne0$, so the Boolean homomorphism is injective. Finally,
evaluate inclusion of cones at $F$ itself to obtain
\eqref{fbc:eq:membership}.
\end{proof}

Source~10 states this lemma for its stages; the proof uses only
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:small-extensions} and holds verbatim at every stage.

\begin{remark}[Joins are not unions \src{10 Remark~2.3}]\label{fbc:rem:joins}
A filter can contain $A\cup B$ while containing neither $A$ nor $B$.
Consequently $j_\kappa([A\cup B])$ need not be the literal union of the
two membership events. It is their join after regularization. Forgetting
this distinction would invalidate later completion comparisons.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[The two descriptions agree \src{merge}]
\label{fbc:lem:dictionary}
The maps
\[
 \RO(\PP_\kappa)\ni V\longmapsto\bigvee_{F\in V}K_F\in\C_\kappa,
 \qquad
 \C_\kappa\ni b\longmapsto\{F\in\PP_\kappa:K_F\subseteq b\},
\]
are mutually inverse complete Boolean isomorphisms. They send $\up F$ to
$K_F$ and $j_\kappa([A])$ to $e_\kappa([A])$. In particular source~10's
$\mathbb D_\lambda=\RO(\mathcal F_\lambda)$, with its embedding
$j_\lambda$, is $\C_{\lambda^+}$ with $e_{\lambda^+}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $b\in\C_\kappa$ and $V_b=\{F:K_F\subseteq b\}$. It is upward closed
by Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:recovery}. If $F\notin V_b$, then $K_F\nsubseteq b$;
since $K_F$ is open and $b$ is regular open, $K_F\cap\neg b\ne\varnothing$,
so some $K_G\subseteq K_F\cap\neg b$ with $G\supseteq F$, and no extension
of $G$ lies in $V_b$. Hence $V_b$ is regular. Both maps are monotone.
By order density (Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:representation}),
$b=\bigvee\{K_F:K_F\subseteq b\}$. Conversely, if $V$ is regular and
$K_F\subseteq\bigvee_{G\in V}K_G$, then every $K_H\subseteq K_F$ meets
some $K_G$ with $G\in V$, and the joint filter extends $H$ and lies in
$V$; so $F\in\neg\neg V=V$. The maps are therefore inverse order
isomorphisms, hence complete Boolean isomorphisms. The cone $\up F$ goes
to $\bigvee_{G\supseteq F}K_G=K_F$. For infinite $A$,
$j_\kappa([A])=\up F_A$ goes to $K_{F_A}=\widehat{[A]}=e_\kappa([A])$, and
for finite $A$ both sides are $0$. The last sentence is
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:index-shift}.
\end{proof}

\section{The exact pseudointersection threshold}
\label{fbc:sec:p}

\subsection{The two cardinal invariants}
A family $\mathcal A\subseteq[\om]^\om$ has the strong finite
intersection property, or SFIP, if every finite intersection is infinite.
An infinite $A$ is a pseudointersection of $\mathcal A$ if
$A\subseteq^*D$ for every $D\in\mathcal A$. For a filter $F$, $A$ is a
pseudointersection of $F$ exactly when $F\subseteq F_A$. Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 \cp&=\min\{|\mathcal A|:\mathcal A\text{ has SFIP and no infinite
 pseudointersection}\},\\
 \cu&=\min\{\chi_*(U):U\text{ is a free ultrafilter}\}.
 \end{split}
\]
These are the usual cardinal characteristics \cite{Blass}. We use only
these definitions, not any theorem identifying $\cp$ with another
cardinal characteristic; in particular, no result about the tower
number is an input.

\begin{lemma}[\src{08 Lemma~4.1; 10 Lemmas~4.2 and~7.2}]
\label{fbc:lem:cardinal-bounds}
In $\ZFC$, $\aleph_1\leq\cp\leq\cu\leq\cc$. No free ultrafilter has an
infinite pseudointersection. Every free filter generated modulo $\Fr$ by
fewer than $\cp$ sets has an infinite pseudointersection, and a family
witnessing $\cp$ generates a free filter $F$ with $\chi_*(F)\leq\cp$ and
no infinite pseudointersection.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a countable SFIP family $D_0,D_1,\ldots$, recursively choose a
strictly increasing $a_n\in\bigcap_{i\leq n}D_i$. The set of the
$a_n$ is an infinite pseudointersection. Thus $\cp>\aleph_0$.

No free ultrafilter has an infinite pseudointersection. Indeed, if
$A$ were such a pseudointersection, split it into two infinite pieces
$A_0,A_1$. If $A_0\in U$, the inclusion $A\subseteq^*A_0$ fails;
if $A_0\notin U$, then $\om\setminus A_0\in U$, and
$A\subseteq^*(\om\setminus A_0)$ fails. A generating family for
$U$ therefore has no pseudointersection, proving $\cp\leq\cu$.
Finally a free ultrafilter exists, and its size is at most $\cc$.

A generating family of a free filter has SFIP, and a pseudointersection
of it is one of the generated filter, since $F_A$ is a filter containing
$\Fr$. So fewer than $\cp$ generators have a pseudointersection.
Conversely, adjoining the cofinite sets to a witness for $\cp$ preserves
properness, and any pseudointersection of the resulting filter would be
one of the witness family.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A general completion criterion}
\label{fbc:sec:criterion}
The next theorem, from source~10, isolates the order-theoretic mechanism
before any cardinal bound is imposed. An \emph{admissible site} $\cQ$ is
a set of free filters containing every $F_A$ and closed under adjoining
one set whenever the generated filter is proper. The constructions of
Section~\ref{fbc:sec:poset-side} give $\RO(\cQ)$ and
$e_\cQ:\B\to\RO(\cQ)$, $e_\cQ([A])=\{F\in\cQ:A\in F\}$, with the same
proofs. Every $\PP_\kappa$ is admissible, by
Lemmas~\ref{fbc:lem:principal} and~\ref{fbc:lem:small-extensions}.

\begin{theorem}[General completion criterion \src{10 Thm~3.2}]
\label{fbc:thm:criterion}
For an admissible site $\cQ$, the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Every filter in $\cQ$ has an infinite pseudointersection.
\item The filters $F_A$ are dense in $\cQ$.
\item The subalgebra $e_\cQ(\B)$ is order dense in $\RO(\cQ)$.
\item There is a complete Boolean homomorphism $J:\C_\om\to\RO(\cQ)$
with $J\circ e_\om=e_\cQ$.
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold, the homomorphism in \textup{(iv)} is unique
and is an isomorphism.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is the identity
$F\subseteq F_A\Longleftrightarrow A\subseteq^*D$ for every $D\in F$.
If (ii) holds, every cone contains a cone $\up F_A=e_\cQ([A])$,
proving (iii). Conversely, under (iii) choose
$0<e_\cQ([A])\subseteq\up F$; evaluating at $F_A$ gives $F\subseteq F_A$,
so (i) follows.

For (iii)$\Rightarrow$(iv), here is the usual uniqueness-of-completion
argument. For $c\in\C_\om$, define
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:completionmap}
 J(c)=\bigvee\{e_\cQ(b):b\in\B,\,e_\om(b)\leq c\}.
\end{equation}
The analogous construction in the opposite direction, using the
order-dense copy of $\B$ in $\RO(\cQ)$, is inverse to $J$. To check this,
it suffices to test the two compositions on the elements below each
of the two dense copies. Equivalently, for $b\in\B$,
\[
 e_\om(b)\leq c\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad e_\cQ(b)\leq J(c).
\]
The forward implication is immediate. If the reverse failed, choose a
nonzero $a\in\B$ with $e_\om(a)\leq e_\om(b)\wedge\neg c$ by density in
$\C_\om$. Then $e_\cQ(a)$ is nonzero and disjoint from every term of
\eqref{fbc:eq:completionmap}, contradicting $e_\cQ(b)\leq J(c)$.
The same argument on the other side proves that the two maps are inverse
order isomorphisms. Order isomorphisms of complete Boolean algebras
preserve all joins and complements. Uniqueness follows because
$c=\bigvee\{e_\om(b):e_\om(b)\leq c\}$.

It remains to prove (iv)$\Rightarrow$(i); this direction contains the
useful obstruction. Suppose $F\in\cQ$ has no pseudointersection, and put
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:dense-incompatible}
 \cE_F=\{[A]\in\B^+:\om\setminus A\in F\}.
\end{equation}
This set is dense in $\B^+$. Indeed, for any infinite $Y$, the failure
of pseudointersection gives $D\in F$ with $Y\setminus D$ infinite.
Set $A=Y\setminus D$. Then $[A]\leq[Y]$ and $\om\setminus A\supseteq D$,
so $[A]\in\cE_F$. Consequently $\bigvee_{b\in\cE_F}e_\om(b)=1$ in $\C_\om$.
But every $e_\cQ(b)$ with $b\in\cE_F$ is disjoint from the nonzero cone
$\up F$. A complete extension would therefore give
\[
 1=J(1)=\bigvee_{b\in\cE_F}e_\cQ(b)\leq\neg\up F<1,
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is a failed preservation of a particular dense join.
It does not merely say that the evident set-theoretic map is badly
chosen: \emph{no} complete extension of the prescribed old events can
work.

\subsection{Preservation and its sharp failure}
\begin{theorem}[Exact marked-completion threshold \src{08 Thm~4.2; 10 Thm~4.3(a),(b)}]
\label{fbc:thm:p-threshold}
For every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\kappa\leq\cp$.
\item Every $F\in\PP_\kappa$ has an extension of the form $F_A$.
\item $e_\kappa[\B]$ is order dense in $\C_\kappa$.
\item There is a complete Boolean isomorphism
$J:\C_\om\to\C_\kappa$ satisfying $J e_\om=e_\kappa$.
\item There is a complete Boolean homomorphism
$J:\C_\om\to\C_\kappa$ satisfying $J e_\om=e_\kappa$.
\end{enumerate}
When the isomorphism in \textup{(iv)} exists, it is unique.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume $\kappa\leq\cp$, and choose fewer than $\kappa$ generators
for $F$. They have SFIP and hence have an infinite pseudointersection
$A$. Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:principal} gives $F_A\supseteq F$, proving
\textup{(ii)}. Conversely, such an extension is exactly a
pseudointersection of the generators. If $\kappa>\cp$, a family
witnessing $\cp$ generates an allowed filter with no such extension.
Thus \textup{(i)} and \textup{(ii)} are equivalent.

By Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:representation}, every positive Boolean element
contains a $K_F$. Condition \textup{(ii)} says that every such $K_F$
contains an original nonempty clopen $\widehat{[A]}=K_{F_A}$, so
\textup{(ii)} implies \textup{(iii)}. Conversely, if
$\widehat{[A]}\subseteq K_F$, Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:recovery} gives
$F_A\supseteq F$. Hence \textup{(iii)} implies \textup{(ii)}.

An order-dense Boolean embedding has a unique completion up to a
complete isomorphism fixing the embedded algebra. One can see this
directly here: each element of either completion is the join of the
original Boolean elements below it; its set of such lower bounds
is determined by which original Boolean elements are incompatible
with it. The same incompatibility relation holds in both embeddings.
Mapping these cuts to each other gives an order isomorphism and hence
a complete Boolean isomorphism. This proves \textup{(iii)} to
\textup{(iv)}, including uniqueness. The implication from
\textup{(iv)} to \textup{(v)} is immediate.

It remains to show that even \textup{(v)} fails when $\kappa>\cp$.
Let $\mathcal A$ be an SFIP family of size $\cp$ with no infinite
pseudointersection, and let $F=\gen{\Fr\cup\mathcal A}$. The set
\[
 K_F=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal A}\widehat{[A]}
\]
is nonempty and $\tau_\kappa$-clopen. Its original Stone interior is
empty: a nonempty $\widehat{[D]}\subseteq K_F$ would make $D$ a
pseudointersection. Formula~\eqref{fbc:eq:regular-operations} therefore
gives
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:meet-obstruction}
 \bigwedge_{A\in\mathcal A}e_\om([A])=0,
 \qquad
 \bigwedge_{A\in\mathcal A}e_\kappa([A])=K_F>0.
\end{equation}
A complete Boolean homomorphism fixing the original events would
preserve these meets, which is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Second proof of the failure of \textup{(v)} \src{source 10}]
For $\kappa>\cp$, Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:cardinal-bounds} gives a filter in
$\PP_\kappa$ without an infinite pseudointersection. By
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:dictionary}, a map $J$ as in \textup{(v)} is a map as
in Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:criterion}\textup{(iv)} for $\cQ=\PP_\kappa$, so
it cannot exist. The obstruction here is the dense \emph{join} of the
family $\cE_F$ of \eqref{fbc:eq:dense-incompatible}, not the $\cp$-fold
\emph{meet} of \eqref{fbc:eq:meet-obstruction}; the two are dual forms of
one phenomenon, since $\cE_F$ consists of the old events disjoint from
$K_F$.
\end{proof}

Source~08 proves only the direction from the old completion $\C_\om$ to
$\C_\kappa$. Source~10 proves that the reverse direction fails as well.

\begin{theorem}[The reverse barrier \src{10 Thm~4.3(c)}]
\label{fbc:thm:reverse}
If $\kappa>\cp$, there is no complete Boolean homomorphism
$H:\C_\kappa\to\C_\om$ satisfying $H\circ e_\kappa=e_\om$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\kappa>\cp$ and let
\[
 N_\kappa=\{F\in\PP_\kappa:F\text{ has no infinite pseudointersection}\}.
\]
We claim that $N_\kappa$ is dense. Given $F$, there is nothing to do
when $F\in N_\kappa$. Otherwise choose an infinite pseudointersection
$A$, so that $F\subseteq F_A$. Transport a fixed $\cp$-sized SFIP
family without pseudointersection from $\om$ to $A$ along a bijection.
The filter generated by that family, $A$, and $\Fr$ extends $F_A$,
needs at most $\cp<\kappa$ generators modulo $\Fr$, and has no
pseudointersection. The last assertion follows because any such
pseudointersection would be almost contained in $A$ and would pull back
to one of the original witness family. This proves the claim.

Now suppose $H$ exists, and take $F\in N_\kappa$. For every $A\in F$ we
have $K_F\leq e_\kappa([A])$ by Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:recovery}, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:kill-cone}
 H(K_F)\leq\bigwedge_{A\in F} e_\om([A])=0.
\end{equation}
To justify the equality, a nonzero meet in $\C_\om$ would contain a
nonzero $e_\om([X])$, by order density. Then $X\subseteq^*A$ for every
$A\in F$, contrary to $F\in N_\kappa$. Since $N_\kappa$ is dense,
$\bigvee_{F\in N_\kappa}K_F=1$ in $\C_\kappa$
(Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:representation}). Completeness of $H$ and
\eqref{fbc:eq:kill-cone} give $H(1)=0$, contradicting unitality.
\end{proof}

\begin{mergenote}
Source~10 states Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:reverse} for its stages
$\mathcal F_\lambda$ with $\lambda\geq\cp$, that is, for successor
$\kappa=\lambda^+>\cp$. Its proof uses only the density of $N_\kappa$,
and the extension built there needs at most $\cp$ generators, which is
allowed at \emph{every} $\kappa>\cp$. No closure of the site is used.
We therefore state it for every $\kappa>\cp$, limit stages included;
this is the only place where a statement of source~10 is printed for
stages it did not name, and the reason is this re-reading of the proof.
\end{mergenote}

\begin{corollary}[The two-sided comparison boundary \src{10 Thm~4.3}]
\label{fbc:cor:two-sided}
For every infinite cardinal $\kappa$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item If $\kappa\leq\cp$, there is a unique complete Boolean isomorphism
$\C_\om\cong\C_\kappa$ carrying $e_\om([A])$ to $e_\kappa([A])$ for every
$A\subseteq\om$.
\item If $\kappa>\cp$, there is no complete Boolean homomorphism
$J:\C_\om\to\C_\kappa$ satisfying $J\circ e_\om=e_\kappa$.
\item If $\kappa>\cp$, there is also no complete Boolean
homomorphism $H:\C_\kappa\to\C_\om$ satisfying $H\circ e_\kappa=e_\om$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the existence of a complete comparison preserving the original
modulo-finite events, in either direction, has the exact boundary
$\kappa\leq\cp$. Parts \textup{(a)} and \textup{(b)} are in both sources;
part \textup{(c)}, and hence the two-sided statement, is source~10's.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Parts (a) and (b) are Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:p-threshold}; part (c) is
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:reverse}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The countably generated site \src{08 Cor.~4.3; 10 eq.~(9)}]
\label{fbc:cor:countable}
The dense Booleanization of the countably generated free-filter site
is canonically $\RO(\om^*)$ over $\pow(\om)/\Fin$:
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:cgcompletion}
 \C_{\aleph_1}\cong\RO\bigl((\pow(\om)/\Fin)^+\bigr)=\C_\om.
\end{equation}
In particular, it is not $\pow(\om^*)$: the former is atomless and the
latter is atomic. More generally the marked completion is unchanged
throughout $\om\leq\kappa\leq\cp$; its first possible change is at
$\cp^+$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:p-threshold} and
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:cardinal-bounds}. The underlying reason is the
elementary diagonal pseudointersection of a countable base. The algebra
$\B$ is atomless, because every infinite subset of $\om$ splits into two
infinite pieces. Its order-dense completion is consequently atomless as
well.
\end{proof}

The obstruction in \eqref{fbc:eq:meet-obstruction} is stronger than
failure of density. It rules out \emph{any} complete event-preserving
map in the displayed direction, not just an isomorphism, and
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:reverse} rules out the other direction too. Neither
rules out an unmarked abstract isomorphism with a completely different
action on the events $[A]$; Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:abstract} gives an
additional, unlabeled obstruction at the successor stages above $\ch$.
The obstruction also does not assert that the identity on the underlying
set of ultrafilters induces a morphism of Booleanized locales; it
explains exactly why that assumption can fail.

Above $\cp$, the old algebra remains an injective Boolean subalgebra,
but it stops being an order-dense subalgebra. In particular, ``more
filters'' does not mean ``a bigger complete algebra into which the old
completion canonically embeds.'' Nor does it produce a canonical complete
quotient onto the old completion.

\begin{example}[A visible change in a dense cover \src{10 Example~4.4}]\label{fbc:ex:visible}
For a filter $F\in\PP_\kappa$ witnessing the failure above $\cp$, the
family $\cE_F$ of \eqref{fbc:eq:dense-incompatible} covers the weakest
condition in the principal-modulo-finite site $\PP_\om$: its join in
$\C_\om$ is $1$. In the larger site it misses the entire nonzero region
$K_F$. This one family of old binary events already witnesses failure of
complete functoriality. The failure is therefore not confined to
complicated model-theoretic formulas or infinite-valued sections.
\end{example}

\subsection{An exact arity invariant and an explicit inverse map}
\begin{definition}[\src{08 Def.~4.4}]\label{fbc:def:arity}
Let $d_\kappa$ be the least size of a family $\mathcal A\subseteq\B$
whose meet is zero in $\C_\om$ and positive in $\C_\kappa$, with
$d_\kappa=\infty$ if no such family exists. Both meets use the
specified embeddings $e_\om,e_\kappa$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The first zero-meet gap \src{08 Prop.~4.5}]
\label{fbc:prop:arity}
\[
 d_\kappa=\begin{cases}
 \infty,&\kappa\leq\cp,\\
 \cp,&\kappa>\cp.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Below the threshold, the complete isomorphism preserves every meet.
Above it, \eqref{fbc:eq:meet-obstruction} gives $d_\kappa\leq\cp$.
If a family of fewer than $\cp$ old events has a positive meet in
$\C_\kappa$, all of its finite meets are nonzero in $\B$. Choose
subset representatives; they have SFIP. A pseudointersection gives
a nonzero original Boolean lower bound, so the meet in $\C_\om$
cannot be zero. This gives the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[How a small filter is represented in the old completion \src{08 Prop.~4.6}]
\label{fbc:prop:interior}
Assume $\kappa\leq\cp$ and $F\in\PP_\kappa$. Then
\[
 K_F=\Cl_\tau\Int_\tau K_F.
\]
Under the inverse isomorphism $\C_\kappa\to\C_\om$ fixing $\B$,
the element $K_F$ is sent to $\Int_\tau K_F$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every original neighborhood of a point of $K_F$ contains a clopen
$\widehat{[D]}$ meeting $K_F$. The filter generated by $F$ and $D$
has fewer than $\cp$ generators, so it has a pseudointersection $A$.
Then $\widehat{[A]}$ lies in $K_F\cap\widehat{[D]}$. Thus the
interior of $K_F$ is dense in $K_F$, proving the first identity.
The interior of a closed set is regular open. It is the join, in the
old completion, of all original clopens contained in $K_F$, which
is exactly the cut description of the inverse isomorphism.
\end{proof}

For the repository's row--tail filter, the countable intersection
$K_{F_\infty}$ (the earlier report's closed set $K$) is closed but not
originally clopen. It nevertheless has dense original interior, and that
interior, not the raw subset $K_{F_\infty}$, is its representative in
$\RO(\om^*)$. Thus the new calculation agrees with the old gluing
obstruction without treating a closed set as if it were automatically a
regular-open event.

\section{Closure and simultaneous Boolean refinement}
\label{fbc:sec:closure}

\begin{proposition}[\src{08 Prop.~5.5}]\label{fbc:prop:closure}
If $\kappa$ is regular and infinite, then $\PP_\kappa$ is
$<\kappa$-closed: every descending forcing-order chain of length
less than $\kappa$ has a lower bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In filter notation, such a chain is an increasing chain
$(F_\xi)_{\xi<\delta}$, where $\delta<\kappa$. Its union is a
proper filter: any finitely many of its members occur together at
one stage, where their intersection is nonempty. Choose a small
generating family at each stage. Regularity of $\kappa$ ensures
that the union of these $\delta$ families has size below $\kappa$.
It generates the union filter, which is the required lower bound.
The empty chain is bounded by $\Fr$.
\end{proof}

In particular, the countable-base presentation is countably closed.
This assertion concerns a presentation, not a classification of all
its Boolean-algebra invariants. For singular $\kappa$, the union of
fewer than $\kappa$ small bases can have size $\kappa$, so the proof
does not apply. No singular closure conclusion is being smuggled into
the threshold theorems, which did not need regularity.

Source~10 proves the same union argument at its stages and draws
consequences from it. Every successor cardinal is regular, so at
$\kappa=\lambda^+$ Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:closure} is exactly the
first half of the next theorem; source~10 remarks that the argument
needs no regularity of $\lambda$ itself, since a chain of length
$\delta$ with $|\delta|\leq\lambda$ of filters with bases of size at most
$\lambda$ has a union with a base of size at most
$\lambda\cdot\lambda=\lambda$.

\begin{theorem}[Closure at the character bound \src{10 Thm~5.1}]
\label{fbc:thm:closure}
Let $\lambda$ be an infinite cardinal, regular or singular. Every chain
of successively stronger conditions in $\PP_{\lambda^+}$ of length less
than $\lambda^+$ has a common stronger condition. Consequently, any
family of at most $\lambda$ maximal antichains of $\C_{\lambda^+}$ has a
common refining maximal antichain.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion is Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:closure} at the regular
cardinal $\lambda^+$. Now let $(\mathcal A_\xi)_{\xi<\delta}$ be maximal
antichains in $\C_{\lambda^+}$, where $|\delta|\leq\lambda$. Given a
nonzero element $w$, first choose a basic element $K_F\leq w$.
Recursively strengthen its filter to $F_\xi$ so that $K_{F_\xi}$ lies
below a member of $\mathcal A_\xi$. Such a strengthening exists because
the antichain has join $1$ and the sets $K_G$ are order dense. At limit
stages and at the final stage, take the union of the earlier filters.
The first assertion makes this a legal construction. The final $K_G$ is
below $w$ and below one member of each $\mathcal A_\xi$.

Hence the nonzero elements refining all the antichains form an order-dense
set. Choose a maximal pairwise disjoint subfamily of that set. It has join
$1$ by density and maximality, so it is the required common refinement.
\end{proof}

We call the conclusion \emph{partition distributivity for at most
$\lambda$ families}. This explicit terminology avoids conventions about
whether ``$\lambda$-distributive'' means a bound of $<\lambda$ or a bound
of $\leq\lambda$. Equivalently, an intersection of at most $\lambda$
dense open subsets of the forcing poset $\PP_{\lambda^+}$ is dense. The
proof just given is the usual closure-to-distributivity argument,
specialized to filter bases.

\begin{corollary}[Local simultaneous decisions \src{10 Cor.~5.2}]
\label{fbc:cor:decisions}
For every family $(b_\xi)_{\xi<\delta}$ in $\C_{\lambda^+}$ with
$|\delta|\leq\lambda$, there is a maximal antichain $\mathcal A$ such
that each $a\in\mathcal A$ lies below either $b_\xi$ or $\neg b_\xi$
for every $\xi<\delta$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure} to the nonzero members of the binary
partitions $\{b_\xi,\neg b_\xi\}$.
\end{proof}

The antichain in this result is a local cover, not a claim that the
original family has one global constant value. Different regions of the
cover can give different sequences of decisions.

\subsection{At which stages the closure consequences are proved}
\label{fbc:sec:closure-scope}
Source~10's results that go through Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure} are
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:decisions}, the bound $\aleph_0<\ch$ and
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:abstract} in Section~\ref{fbc:sec:h},
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:forcing}, Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:unmix},
Theorems~\ref{fbc:thm:localized} and~\ref{fbc:thm:saturated}, and the
remark that the stages are countably distributive. They are stated
here, as there, at the successor stages $\PP_{\lambda^+}$.

\begin{mergenote}
The other stages of source~08 are covered as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The finite-generation stage $\PP_\om$.} It is countably
closed: a countable chain $F_{A_0}\subseteq F_{A_1}\subseteq\cdots$ is
bounded by $F_X$ for any infinite pseudointersection $X$ of the $A_n$
(Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:cardinal-bounds}). Statements about the complete
algebra $\C_{\aleph_1}$ and its mixing model apply to $\C_\om$ through the
marked isomorphism of Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:countable} and its transport
of mixing models (Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:model-stable}).
\item \emph{Regular limit stages.} Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:closure}
makes $\PP_\kappa$ $<\kappa$-closed. Neither source derives source~10's
consequences there, and this report does not claim them.
\item \emph{Singular stages.} At a singular $\kappa$ of countable
cofinality, for example $\PP_{\aleph_\om}$, an increasing chain
$(F_n)_{n<\om}$ with $\chi_*(F_n)=\aleph_n$ can have a union needing
$\aleph_\om$ generators, which lies outside the site. The union argument
then gives no lower bound, and none of the results listed above is
proved there. Whether such a stage is even countably closed is not
decided here; it is part of question~Q5.
\end{itemize}
The comparison theorems of Section~\ref{fbc:sec:p}, including the reverse
barrier, the atom and point results of Section~\ref{fbc:sec:atoms}, and
the exact sizes of Section~\ref{fbc:sec:sizes} use no closure and hold at
every stage for which they are stated.
\end{mergenote}

\section{The distributivity number as a local-reading boundary}
\label{fbc:sec:h}

This section, from source~10, identifies the first obstruction to
simultaneous binary decisions in the countably generated completion
$\C_\om$ ($=\C_{\aleph_1}$). The cardinal being identified is classical;
the interpretation concerns the completed sections constructed in
Section~\ref{fbc:sec:mixing}.

\begin{definition}[\src{10 Def.~6.1}]\label{fbc:def:h}
Let $\ch$ be the least cardinality of a family of dense open subsets of
$\B^+$ whose intersection is not dense. Here a subset of $\B^+$ is
open when it is closed under passing to smaller nonzero Boolean elements.
A family $(b_\xi)_{\xi<\mu}$ of elements of $\C_\om$ is
\emph{locally jointly decidable} if
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:joint-decision}
 \{a\in\B^+:\text{for every }\xi<\mu,
 \ e_\om(a)\leq b_\xi\text{ or }e_\om(a)\leq\neg b_\xi\}
\end{equation}
is dense in $\B^+$.
\end{definition}

This form of $\ch$ is the usual distributivity number for
$\pow(\om)/\Fin$ \cite{Blass}. One has $\aleph_0<\ch\leq\cc$. The first
inequality follows from the countable case of
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure} (at $\lambda=\aleph_0$) and
\eqref{fbc:eq:cgcompletion}. For the upper bound, take the $\cc$ many
dense sets that decide each element of $\B$. No nonzero element of the
atomless algebra $\B$ can decide all its elements: splitting a
representative infinite set into two infinite pieces disproves that
possibility.

\begin{theorem}[Exact binary local-reading boundary \src{10 Thm~6.2}]
\label{fbc:thm:h}
The least size of a family in $\C_\om$ that is not locally jointly
decidable is $\ch$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $b\in\C_\om$, the set
\[
 \mathcal D_b=\{a\in\B^+:e_\om(a)\leq b\text{ or }e_\om(a)\leq\neg b\}
\]
is dense open. Indeed, at least one of $e_\om(a)\wedge b$ and
$e_\om(a)\wedge\neg b$ is nonzero, and order density supplies a nonzero
old event below that part. Thus fewer than $\ch$ binary events have
a dense set of joint decisions by the definition of $\ch$.

For the converse, choose dense open sets
$(\mathcal D_\xi)_{\xi<\ch}$ with nondense intersection. For each $\xi$,
choose a maximal antichain $\mathcal A_\xi\subseteq\mathcal D_\xi$ in
$\B^+$. It has join $1$ in $\C_\om$. Since $|\B|=\cc$, its members admit
pairwise distinct binary real codes
\[
 \gamma_\xi:\mathcal A_\xi\longrightarrow 2^\om.
\]
For $n<\om$, form the completed binary event
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:bit-event}
 b_{\xi,n}=\bigvee\{e_\om(a):a\in\mathcal A_\xi,
                                      \ \gamma_\xi(a)(n)=1\}.
\end{equation}
There are at most $\ch\cdot\aleph_0=\ch$ such events.

Suppose $a\in\B^+$ decides every event in \eqref{fbc:eq:bit-event}.
Fix $\xi$. The region $e_\om(a)$ can meet at most one member of
$e_\om(\mathcal A_\xi)$. If it met two, some bit of their distinct codes
would differ, so $e_\om(a)$ would meet both $b_{\xi,n}$ and its
complement, contrary to the decision of that bit. It meets at least
one member, since the antichain has join $1$. Finite-meet distribution
over arbitrary joins now implies that $e_\om(a)$ lies below that unique
member. Therefore $a\in\mathcal D_\xi$ because $\mathcal D_\xi$ is
downward closed. This holds for every $\xi$, so every joint decision lies
in $\bigcap_{\xi<\ch}\mathcal D_\xi$, which is not dense. The family
\eqref{fbc:eq:bit-event} is the desired obstruction.
\end{proof}

The coding by reals is important. A dense-set witness initially gives
partitions with potentially many cells, not binary partitions. The
countably many bits of the code reduce each such partition to binary
events without changing the infinite cardinal $\ch$.

\begin{corollary}[An unlabeled nonisomorphism \src{10 Cor.~6.3}]
\label{fbc:cor:abstract}
Let $\kappa=\lambda^+$ be a successor cardinal with $\lambda\geq\ch$
(equivalently, a successor cardinal $\kappa>\ch$). Then $\C_\kappa$ is
not isomorphic to $\C_\om$, even by an isomorphism that need not preserve
the old events. The arguments also recover the classical inequality
$\cp\leq\ch$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure}, every $\ch$ many binary partitions in
$\C_{\lambda^+}$ have a common maximal-antichain refinement, since
$\ch\leq\lambda$. By Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:h}, this property fails in
$\C_\om$: such a refinement would, using the order-dense copy of $\B$,
make the decision set \eqref{fbc:eq:joint-decision} dense. The property
is invariant under Boolean isomorphism. Finally, if $\ch<\cp$, the
successor stage $\kappa=\ch^+$ satisfies $\kappa\leq\cp$, so
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided}\textup{(a)} gives $\C_{\ch^+}\cong\C_\om$,
contradicting the first assertion.
\end{proof}

We emphasize that recovering $\cp\leq\ch$ is a consistency check on
the framework, not a claim of a new cardinal-characteristic inequality.
For a possible successor stage $\cp<\kappa\leq\ch$, the two-sided
\emph{labeled} barrier of Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided} still holds,
while this particular unlabeled argument does not decide isomorphism.
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:abstract} is proved only at successor stages, as
explained in Section~\ref{fbc:sec:closure-scope}.

\begin{mergenote}
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:abstract} partly answers source~08's question Q4
(marked versus unmarked isomorphism): at the successor stages above
$\ch$ the unmarked question has a negative answer. Q4 is re-scoped
accordingly in Section~\ref{fbc:sec:future}.
\end{mergenote}

\section{The ultrafilter threshold: atoms, points, and atomicity}
\label{fbc:sec:atoms}

\subsection{The exact atom spectrum}
Define
\[
 S_\kappa=\{U\in\om^*:\chi_*(U)<\kappa\}.
\]
This is a set of ultrafilters, not a set of hypothetical generic filters
in an extension. Source~10's $\mathcal U_\lambda$, the free ultrafilters
of base character at most $\lambda$, is $S_{\lambda^+}$ by
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:index-shift}. The topology $\tau_\kappa$ is Hausdorff
because it contains the original Stone topology.

\begin{theorem}[Atoms are exactly small-character ultrafilters \src{08 Thm~5.1; 10 Thm~7.3, Cor.~7.4}]
\label{fbc:thm:atoms}
The atoms of $\C_\kappa$ are precisely the singleton sets
$\{U\}$ with $U\in S_\kappa$. Consequently
\[
 \C_\kappa\text{ is atomless}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \kappa\leq\cu.
\]
The first cardinal stage with atoms is $\cu^+$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A nonempty regular open set containing two distinct points can be split
by an original Stone clopen separating those points. Its intersections
with that clopen and its complement are nonzero disjoint regular open
sets. Such a set cannot be an atom. Every atom is therefore a singleton.

A singleton $\{U\}$ is open in $\tau_\kappa$ exactly when a basic
$K_F$ contains $U$ and is contained in $\{U\}$. In that case
$K_F=K_U$, and Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:recovery} gives $F=U$; thus
$\chi_*(U)<\kappa$. Conversely, if $U\in S_\kappa$, its singleton
is the basic clopen $K_U$, and hence is an atom. By the definition of
$\cu$, the set $S_\kappa$ is empty exactly for $\kappa\leq\cu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Second proof, on the poset side \src{source 10}]
A singleton ultrafilter cone $\up U=\{U\}$ is a nonzero regular open with
no nonzero proper part, so it is an atom of $\RO(\PP_\kappa)$. Conversely,
any atom contains a cone $\up F$, and must equal that cone. If $F$ is not
an ultrafilter, choose $A$ with neither $A$ nor $\om\setminus A$ in $F$.
Adjoining these two sets separately produces two proper filters in
$\PP_\kappa$ (Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:small-extensions}), with disjoint
nonzero cones below $\up F$. This contradicts atomicity. Therefore $F$ is
an ultrafilter of $\PP_\kappa$, and Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:dictionary}
transfers the conclusion to $\C_\kappa$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Geometric points of the Booleanization \src{08 Prop.~5.2; 10 Lemma~7.5, Cor.~7.6}]
\label{fbc:prop:points}
The points of the localic topos
$\Sh(\PP_\kappa,J_{\rm dense})$, equivalently the complete Boolean
points of $\C_\kappa$ (unital maps $\C_\kappa\to2$ preserving finite
meets and arbitrary joins), are indexed by $S_\kappa$.
In particular, there are none when $\kappa\leq\cu$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A point of a locale is a map from its frame to $2$ preserving finite
meets and arbitrary joins; such a map preserves complements
automatically. Let $q:\Eb\to2$ be such a map from a nontrivial complete
Boolean algebra $\Eb$. Put
\[
 z=\bigvee\{b\in\Eb:q(b)=0\},\qquad a=\neg z.
\]
Join preservation gives $q(z)=0$, so $q(a)=1$ and $a\ne0$.
If $0<b\leq a$, then $q(b)$ cannot be zero, because that would
imply $b\leq z$. If both $b$ and $a\wedge\neg b$ were nonzero,
they would both map to $1$, contradicting preservation of their
zero meet. Thus $a$ is an atom. Conversely an atom $a$ defines
$q_a(b)=1$ exactly when $a\leq b$; an atom lies below an arbitrary join
exactly when it lies below one of the terms (distribute its meet over
the join), so $q_a$ preserves arbitrary joins. These constructions are
inverse. Apply Theorems~\ref{fbc:thm:representation}
and~\ref{fbc:thm:atoms}.
\end{proof}

The two sources give this same kernel-join proof.

\begin{corollary}[\src{10 Cor.~7.6}]\label{fbc:cor:no-powerset}
There is no unital complete Boolean homomorphism from $\C_\om$ into any
nonzero power-set algebra.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The completion $\C_\om$ is atomless, because below each nonzero element
there is an old infinite-set event that can be split into two nonzero
events. A complete map $\C_\om\to\pow(I)$ with $I\ne\varnothing$,
followed by evaluation at any $i\in I$, would be a complete Boolean point
of $\C_\om$.
\end{proof}

At the full-site endpoint $\C_{\cc^+}=\pow(\om^*)$ this is stronger than
the event-preserving obstruction in the direction from $\C_\om$. In the
other direction, complete maps $\pow(\om^*)\to\C_\om$ certainly exist
abstractly: evaluation at a fixed ultrafilter, followed by the inclusion
$2\hookrightarrow\C_\om$, is one. What Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:reverse}
rules out is preservation of \emph{all} the original events by such a
map.

\begin{remark}[Do not confuse two kinds of points \src{08 \S5.1; 10 Remark~7.7}]
\label{fbc:rem:two-points}
This does not deny that $X=\om^*$ contains many ordinary points.
An atomless complete Boolean algebra still has ordinary ultrafilters in
$\ZFC$. Those are points of its \emph{Stone space} and yield maps to $2$
preserving finite Boolean operations; they generally do not preserve
arbitrary joins. Booleanization can therefore have no geometric points
even though the topological presentation from which it was obtained has
points: a point-free Boolean locale need not have an empty Stone space.
This distinction is essential in interpreting the countable-site model.
The model-theoretic specializations of Section~\ref{fbc:sec:logic} use
ordinary ultrafilters, not complete points.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Full atomicity is an extension property}
A complete Boolean algebra is atomic if every nonzero element contains
an atom. Having one atom, or many atoms, is weaker.

\begin{theorem}[Atomicity and the canonical decomposition \src{08 Thm~5.3; 10 Thm~7.3}]
\label{fbc:thm:atomicity}
For every infinite $\kappa$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\C_\kappa$ is atomic;
\item $S_\kappa$ is dense in $(X,\tau_\kappa)$;
\item every $F\in\PP_\kappa$ extends to an ultrafilter
$U\in\PP_\kappa$.
\end{enumerate}
Let
\[
 a_\kappa=\bigvee_{U\in S_\kappa}\{U\}
          =\Int_{\tau_\kappa}\Cl_{\tau_\kappa}S_\kappa,
 \qquad r_\kappa=\neg a_\kappa.
\]
There is a canonical complete Boolean decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:atomic-split}
 \C_\kappa\cong\pow(S_\kappa)\times
       (\C_\kappa\res r_\kappa),
\end{equation}
and the second factor is atomless. It is the trivial one-element
algebra exactly when the equivalent conditions hold. On the poset side
(Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:dictionary}) the residual element is
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:residual}
 r_\kappa=
 \{F\in\PP_\kappa:\text{no }U\in S_\kappa\text{ extends }F\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every nonempty regular open contains an atom exactly when it meets
$S_\kappa$, by Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atoms}. This is equivalent to
density. Since the $K_F$ form a base, density is equivalent to
$K_F\cap S_\kappa\ne\varnothing$ for every allowed $F$, which
is precisely \textup{(iii)}.

For any complete Boolean algebra and any element $a$, the map
$b\mapsto(b\wedge a,b\wedge\neg a)$ gives the product of its two
relative algebras. Below the join of all atoms, an element is uniquely
the join of the atoms below it. Thus the first relative algebra is
the power set of its atoms, here $S_\kappa$. The complementary
relative algebra contains no atom: an atom in it would be an atom
of the original algebra and hence lie below $a_\kappa$, a contradiction.
Taking the negation \eqref{fbc:eq:neg} of the union of the singleton
cones $\up U$, $U\in S_\kappa$, gives \eqref{fbc:eq:residual}.
\end{proof}

Source~10 states \eqref{fbc:eq:residual} at its stages; the proof uses no
closure and holds at every stage. In source~10's words, $\C_{\lambda^+}$
is wholly atomic if and only if every filter of character at most
$\lambda$ extends to an ultrafilter of character at most $\lambda$.

\begin{corollary}[The full site \src{10 Cor.~7.4}]\label{fbc:cor:full-site}
For $\kappa>\cc$ the algebra $\C_\kappa=\C_{\cc^+}$ is wholly atomic and
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:all-algebra}
 \C_{\cc^+}=\pow(\om^*),
 \qquad e_{\cc^+}([A])=\{U\in\om^*:A\in U\}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At the all-filter stage every proper filter extends to an ultrafilter,
and that ultrafilter has at most $\cc$ generators. Thus the residual
factor is zero. The event formula follows by evaluating membership at
each atom; it is also \eqref{fbc:eq:full-completion}.
\end{proof}

The endpoint \eqref{fbc:eq:all-algebra} is classical in this setting;
Massas identifies it as the canonical extension of the modulo-finite
algebra under the ultrafilter lemma \cite[Section~6.1]{Massas}.
``Canonical extension'' there is not the same construction as the
order-dense Boolean completion $\C_\om$.
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided} and Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atoms}
describe precisely why the two endpoints must be kept distinct.

Mill\'an's notation $\GE(\mathcal U,\kappa)$ expresses that every
filter with a base of size below $\kappa$ extends a member of a
specified ultrafilter class $\mathcal U$ \cite[Definition~2.1]{Millan}.
For uncountable $\kappa$, the criterion above is exactly
\[
 \GE(S_\kappa,\kappa).
\]
This identifies full atomicity with a familiar but stronger extension
problem, rather than silently deducing it from $\cu<\kappa$.

\begin{remark}[Why ordinary density is insufficient \src{08 Remark~5.4}]\label{fbc:rem:density}
If $S_\kappa$ is nonempty, it is dense in the \emph{original} Stone
topology. To see this, transport one small-character free ultrafilter
along a bijection from $\om$ to any prescribed infinite
$A\subseteq\om$. The resulting ultrafilter contains $A$ and has
the same character. But ordinary Stone density says only that the
sets $K_{F_A}$ meet $S_\kappa$; Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atomicity} requires
this for every small-generated filter. We make no assertion that
existence alone implies, or refutes, this stronger density. The least
bound giving a wholly atomic algebra need not be determined by the
point-existence statement of Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atoms}.
\end{remark}

\section{A forcing interpretation of the residual part}
\label{fbc:sec:forcing}

The decomposition \eqref{fbc:eq:atomic-split} also has a direct
set-theoretic meaning, found in source~10. We state it in the usual
language of forcing over a ground model $\bV$ of $\ZFC$. All cardinals
and all posets in the statement are initially computed in $\bV$. A
generic extension is understood externally; no generic filter over the
whole universe is being postulated inside that universe.

\begin{proposition}[Old and new ultrafilter regions \src{10 Prop.~8.1}]
\label{fbc:prop:forcing}
Let $\lambda$ be an infinite cardinal. Forcing with $\PP_{\lambda^+}$
adds no new sequences of ground-model elements of length at most
$\lambda$. If $G$ is generic, then
\[
 U_G=\bigcup_{F\in G}F
\]
is a free ultrafilter on $\om$ in the extension. On the atomic part
$a_{\lambda^+}$ it is a ground-model ultrafilter in $S_{\lambda^+}$, that
is, of character at most $\lambda$. On a nonzero residual part
$r_{\lambda^+}$, it is a new ultrafilter and has character greater than
$\lambda$ in the extension.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a name for a sequence of length at most $\lambda$, recursively
strengthen a condition to decide each entry and take unions at limit
stages and at the end. Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure} supplies all lower
bounds. The final condition decides the whole sequence as a sequence in
$\bV$. This standard decision argument proves the first assertion. In
particular there are no new subsets of $\om$.

The generic family is directed in the strengthening order, so its union
is a proper free filter. For every ground-model $A\subseteq\om$, the
set of filters deciding $A$ is dense by
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:small-extensions}. Thus the union decides every
ground-model subset, and, since there are no new subsets of $\om$, it is
an ultrafilter in the extension. If a generic condition is a singleton
atom $U$, its stronger cone consists only of $U$, and the union is $U$.

Suppose now that we force below the residual region. No ground-model
ultrafilter in $S_{\lambda^+}$ can be the generic union, by
\eqref{fbc:eq:residual}. For a fixed ground-model ultrafilter $W$ with
$\chi^{\bV}(W)>\lambda$, the conditions $F$ not contained in $W$ form a
dense set: if $F\subseteq W$, choose $A\in W\setminus F$ and adjoin
$\om\setminus A$ to $F$. The resulting proper extension is not contained
in $W$. The generic union therefore differs from every ground-model
ultrafilter.

If $U_G$ had a base of size at most $\lambda$ in the extension, enumerate
that base in a sequence of length at most $\lambda$, with repetition if
necessary. Every entry is a ground-model subset of $\om$, and the first
paragraph says the entire sequence belongs to $\bV$. Its generated filter
would then be a ground-model ultrafilter, since the extension has no new
subsets of $\om$. This contradicts newness. Thus its character in the
extension exceeds $\lambda$.
\end{proof}

At the countably generated stage this describes a familiar
modulo-finite forcing behavior: new ultrafilters can be added without
new reals. At the full-filter stage the forcing is atomic and merely
selects an already available ultrafilter. The bounded-character residual
interpolates between these behaviors, without asserting that its
intermediate stages are all isomorphic, and without identifying the
residual forcing uniquely. The proposition uses
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure} and is therefore stated only at the
successor stages (Section~\ref{fbc:sec:closure-scope}).

\section{Early atomicity and genuinely different atomic stages}
\label{fbc:sec:early-atomicity}

The full stage $\C_{\cc^+}=\pow(\om^*)$ is atomic. Is full filter
complexity necessary for atomicity? The answer is no, relative to a
published forcing model. The following is the only imported
relative-consistency input in this report.

\begin{proposition}[Published model input; \textbf{imported} \src{08 Prop.~6.1}]
\label{fbc:prop:model-input}
Mill\'an's proof of Theorem~3.14 \cite[pp.~34--35]{Millan} supplies,
relative to consistency of $\ZFC$, a model with
\[
 \cc=2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_2,
 \qquad \GE(\mathcal P\!t,\cc),
 \qquad |\mathcal P\!t|=\cc,
\]
where $\mathcal P\!t$ is the class of $P$-point ultrafilters, and every
member of $\mathcal P\!t$ has character $\aleph_1$.
\end{proposition}

Here a $P$-point is a free ultrafilter in which every countable family
has a pseudointersection belonging to the ultrafilter. We only use
the displayed properties, not additional special features of $P$-points.
The cited construction is a countable-support iteration of rational
perfect set forcing. We do not claim to have proved that forcing theorem;
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:model-input} is an imported input, not a result
of this report.

\begin{lemma}[The number of free ultrafilters \src{08 Lemma~6.2; 10 Lemma~12.3}]
\label{fbc:lem:ultra-number}
In $\ZFC$, $|\om^*|=2^\cc$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound follows because an ultrafilter is a subset of
$\pow(\om)$. For the lower bound, use the countably infinite set
\[
 I=\{(n,H):n<\om,\ H\subseteq2^n\}.
\]
It is countably infinite because each of its levels is finite. For each
real $x\in2^\om$, put $A_x=\{(n,H):x\res n\in H\}$. Given finitely many
distinct reals, their initial segments are distinct for all sufficiently
large $n$. At every such $n$ choose $H$ to include exactly the initial
segments prescribed by an arbitrary binary pattern. Thus every finite
Boolean combination of the $A_x$ with no repeated index is infinite.

For every function $\varepsilon:2^\om\to2$, the family choosing
$A_x$ when $\varepsilon(x)=1$ and its complement otherwise has
SFIP. Together with the cofinite filter it extends to a free
ultrafilter on $I$. Different functions force different ultrafilters,
since they make opposite decisions about some $A_x$.
Choice allows one extension to be selected for each function, giving
$2^\cc$ distinct ultrafilters. Transfer them to $\om$ by a bijection.
\end{proof}

The two sources give the same independent-family construction (source~10
indexes it by $\Omega$ and writes $I_x$ for $A_x$).

\begin{theorem}[A three-stage hierarchy from the published model \src{08 Thm~6.3}]
\label{fbc:thm:early-atomicity}
In the model of Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:model-input},
\[
 \cp=\cu=\aleph_1,
 \qquad
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{stage}&\text{Booleanization}\\ \hline
 \kappa=\aleph_1&\C_{\aleph_1}\cong\RO(\om^*)\text{, atomless}\\
 \kappa=\aleph_2&\C_{\aleph_2}\cong\pow(\cc)\\
 \kappa=\aleph_3&\C_{\aleph_3}\cong\pow(2^\cc).
 \end{array}
\]
In particular, the last two complete Boolean algebras are atomic but
are not isomorphic, even without marking the original events.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The existence of ultrafilters with character $\aleph_1$ gives
$\cu=\aleph_1$, and Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:cardinal-bounds} then gives
$\cp=\aleph_1$. The first line follows from
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:countable}.

Every $F\in\PP_{\aleph_2}$ has a $P$-point extension, and every
such extension has character $\aleph_1$. Thus the small-character
ultrafilters are dense in this site, and
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atomicity} makes $\C_{\aleph_2}$ atomic.
There are at least $\cc$ such ultrafilters, by the stated number
of $P$-points. There are at most $\cc^{\aleph_1}=\cc$ generating
families of size at most $\aleph_1$: use
$\cc=2^{\aleph_1}$ to compute
$(2^{\aleph_1})^{\aleph_1}=2^{\aleph_1}$. Every member of
$S_{\aleph_2}$ is determined by one such family. Therefore
$|S_{\aleph_2}|=\cc$, proving the middle line.

Finally $\aleph_3=\cc^+$, so the last line follows from
\eqref{fbc:eq:full-completion} and Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:ultra-number}.
The two atomic algebras have respectively $\cc$ and $2^\cc$
atoms; Cantor's theorem distinguishes their atom sets and hence
the algebras.
\end{proof}

The conclusion settles the proposed ``atomic only at the full site''
possibility negatively, with its exact external assumption exposed.
It does not settle the general atomicity criterion in terms of a
short list of cardinal characteristics. Notice also that two atomic
forcing notions can both add no new sets in the usual generic-extension
sense and still have nonisomorphic complete Boolean algebras and
non-equivalent localic sheaf categories. The atom counts here retain
information that the phrase ``trivial forcing'' discards.

For comparison, under CH we have $\cp=\cu=\cc=\aleph_1$.
Then the countable-base stage is atomless, while the very next cardinal
stage is already the full atomic site. The model above separates
atomicity from the admission of all filters.

\begin{mergenote}
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:early-atomicity} answers the second clause of
source~10's research question~14.2 (question~R2 here), ``Can this
extension property hold strictly below $\cc$?'', positively, relative to
the imported model: in source~10's indexing the stage
$\mathcal F_{\aleph_1}=\PP_{\aleph_2}$ is wholly atomic while
$\aleph_1<\cc=\aleph_2$. The answer is a relative-consistency result that
rests on Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:model-input}; it is not a $\ZFC$
theorem. R2 is re-scoped in Section~\ref{fbc:sec:future}.
\end{mergenote}

\section{A countable toy model and the marking issue}
\label{fbc:sec:toy}

The following elementary example isolates the complete-homomorphism
obstruction without requiring uncountable cardinal characteristics.
It is not a claim of a new finite--cofinite algebra phenomenon.
Pierobon and Viale also use that algebra to illustrate delicate
completion behavior \cite[Remark~3.7]{PV}; our purpose is to display
the two filter stages explicitly.

Let $\B_0$ be the Boolean algebra of finite and cofinite subsets of
$\om$. Its Stone space is $Y=\om\cup\{\infty\}$, where each
integer is isolated and neighborhoods of $\infty$ are cofinite.
For an abstract Boolean algebra, repeat the filter construction using
proper filters generated by fewer than $\kappa$ Boolean elements.
At the finite-generation stage, the topology is the original Stone
topology. Since $\om$ is a dense set of isolated points,
\[
 \RO(Y)\cong\pow(\om).
\]
The embedding of $\B_0$ is the ordinary inclusion into $\pow(\om)$.
The isomorphism sends a regular open set to its intersection with
$\om$; its inverse is regularization of that subset in $Y$.

At the countable-generation stage, all proper filters of $\B_0$
are allowed. In particular, the cofinite ultrafilter corresponds to
$\{\infty\}$, which becomes open. The topology is now discrete,
and the completion is $\pow(\om\cup\{\infty\})$. A finite Boolean
element $A$ maps to $A$, while a cofinite one maps to
$A\cup\{\infty\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Abstractly isomorphic, but not compatibly complete \src{08 Prop.~7.1}]
\label{fbc:prop:toy}
The two complete Boolean algebras just described are abstractly
isomorphic. No complete Boolean homomorphism from the first to the
second extends the identity of $\B_0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Their atom sets are both countably infinite, so a bijection of those
sets induces an abstract complete isomorphism. On the other hand,
for $T_n=\{m:m\geq n\}$, the meet of the embedded $T_n$ is empty
in the first algebra and $\{\infty\}$ in the second. A complete
homomorphism extending the original embedding would preserve that
meet, which is impossible. Equivalently, the join of the singletons
$\{n\}$ is $1$ in the first algebra but not in the second.
\end{proof}

Thus ``the completions differ over the original events'' is a genuine
mathematical statement, but it must not be strengthened without proof
to ``the completions are abstractly nonisomorphic.'' The example
supplies a concrete audit test for the wording of
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:p-threshold}.

\section{Reduced powers and their explicit mixing sheaves}
\label{fbc:sec:mixing}

\subsection{The raw presheaf is separated}
Let $M$ be a nonempty set. For a free filter $F$, define
\[
 \R_M(F)=M^\om/{\sim_F},
 \qquad f\sim_Fg\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \eqset{f}{g}:=\{n:f(n)=g(n)\}\in F.
\]
For $G\supseteq F$, restriction sends $[f]_F$ to $[f]_G$.
These definitions make a presheaf on every $\PP_\kappa$. Its global
sections at $\Fr$ are the raw sequences modulo eventual equality.

\begin{proposition}[\src{08 Prop.~8.1; in 10 inside Thm~9.2}]\label{fbc:prop:separated}
The presheaf $\R_M$ is separated for the relative dense topology on
$\PP_\kappa$. Its map into its associated sheaf is injective at
every object.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $\eqset{f}{g}\notin F$, adjoining its complement gives an
allowed proper extension $H$ of $F$. At every further extension
of $H$, the two sequences remain inequivalent. A dense cover of
$F$ must reach some such extension. Hence two inequivalent sections
cannot become equal throughout a dense cover. The same fact gives
injectivity of the sheafification unit; it will also follow directly
from the construction below.
\end{proof}

Separatedness is not sheafness. It says that gluing, when it exists,
is unique; it does not supply a representative for every matching
family. On the full site separatedness is the earlier report's
Theorem~5.1 \cite{Repo}.

\subsection{Mixtures over a Boolean element}
Fix $\kappa$, abbreviate $e=e_\kappa$, and write $\sigma_F=K_F$. An
antichain below $b\in\C_\kappa$ is a set of pairwise disjoint nonzero
elements, each at most $b$. It is a partition of $b$ when its join is
$b$.

\begin{definition}[A mixture \src{08 Def.~8.2; 10 \S9.1}]\label{fbc:def:mixture}
A mixture over $b$ is a partition $P\subseteq\C_\kappa^+$ of $b$,
together with one sequence $f_p\in M^\om$ for each $p\in P$.
We write it as $x=\sum_{p\in P}p\cdot f_p$; the notation denotes
pasting, not numerical addition. Over $0$ the empty partition is
the unique representation.

If $x=\sum p\cdot f_p$ and $y=\sum q\cdot g_q$ are mixtures
on the same support $b$, define their equality value by
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:mix-equality}
 \truth{x=y}=\bigvee_{p,q}
       p\wedge q\wedge e([\eqset{f_p}{g_q}]).
\end{equation}
Identify $x$ and $y$ when this value is $b$. Let $\Mix_M(b)$
be the resulting set of equivalence classes.
\end{definition}

All representations form a set: partitions are subsets of the set
$\C_\kappa$, and labels are chosen from the set $M^\om$. There is no
proper-class collection of names in this construction. The relevant
labels are \emph{sequences in $M$}, not just constant members of $M$;
that distinction is essential for infinite $M$
(Example~\ref{fbc:ex:infinite-labels}).

\begin{lemma}[Equality, refinement, restriction, and flattening \src{08 Lemma~8.3; 10 Lemma~9.1}]
\label{fbc:lem:mix-well-defined}
Equality at support $b$ is an equivalence relation. Replacing a
partition by a refinement and retaining its labels does not change
the mixture. For $c\leq b$, intersecting each partition element
with $c$ and dropping zeros defines a well-defined restriction
$\Mix_M(b)\to\Mix_M(c)$. A mixture of mixtures along a partition of $b$
is represented by a single mixture over $b$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Reflexivity follows because the diagonal terms of
\eqref{fbc:eq:mix-equality} join to $b$; symmetry is immediate.
For transitivity, refine three partitions simultaneously. On a
cell $p\wedge q\wedge r$, the ordinary implication
\[
 \eqset{f_p}{g_q}\cap\eqset{g_q}{h_r}
       \subseteq\eqset{f_p}{h_r}
\]
becomes a Boolean inequality after applying $e$. Taking joins over
all cells gives
$\truth{x=y}\wedge\truth{y=z}\leq\truth{x=z}$.
Thus equality at $b$ is transitive. The same formula shows that
refinement preserves equality values. Intersecting every term with
$c$ multiplies the equality value by $c$, proving that restriction
is independent of representatives. Successive restrictions agree
by associativity of Boolean meet.

For flattening, if the outer cell is $b_i$ and the inner mixture uses
cells $c_{i,j}$ with labels $f_{i,j}$, use all nonzero cells
$b_i\wedge c_{i,j}$ with labels $f_{i,j}$. They are pairwise disjoint
and have join $b$, and the equality value with each intended inner
element is at least its outer cell.
\end{proof}

The use of arbitrary joins here is legitimate in a complete Boolean
algebra. Finite meet distributes over arbitrary joins: for fixed
$a$, the operation $x\mapsto a\wedge x$ is left adjoint to
$x\mapsto\neg a\vee x$. No stronger complete-distributivity
assumption is being made.

\begin{lemma}[The mixing sheaf \src{08 Lemma~8.4; 10 Thm~9.2, first step}]
\label{fbc:lem:mix-sheaf}
The functor $b\mapsto\Mix_M(b)$ is a sheaf on the complete Boolean
algebra $\C_\kappa$ with its join coverage.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $b=\bigvee_i b_i$ and $x_i\in\Mix_M(b_i)$ agree on
all overlaps. Choose a maximal disjoint family of nonzero elements
each below some $b_i$. Its join is $b$: otherwise its nonzero
complement inside $b$ would meet some $b_i$ and permit one more
element. For each cell choose such an $i$, restrict $x_i$ to the
cell, and flatten the resulting partitions
(Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:mix-well-defined}). Their union is a
partition of $b$ labelled by sequences, hence a mixture $x$.

On overlap with any $b_j$, each of these pieces agrees with $x_j$
by compatibility of the original family.
Formula~\eqref{fbc:eq:mix-equality} therefore gives equality value $b_j$,
so $x$ is an amalgam. If two amalgams exist, their equality value
contains every $b_i$ and hence their join $b$. They represent the same
element.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Source~10's presentation of the same object]
\label{fbc:rem:presentations}
Source~10 uses only \emph{global} presentations $\sum_ib_i\cdot f_i$ over
partitions of $1$, calls the quotient by value-$1$ equality $S_\lambda(A)$,
and defines the sections on a region $w$ as the quotient
$S_\lambda(A;w)$ by the relation $w\leq\truth{s=t}$. A presentation on
a partition of $w$ extends globally by an arbitrary fixed constant label
on $\neg w$, which is why global presentations suffice; at $w=0$ there is
one section. Restricting a global presentation to $w$ identifies
$S_\lambda(A;w)$ with $\Mix_M(w)$ at stage $\lambda^+$ (with $A=M$). Its
proof of the associated-sheaf theorem follows the same steps as the one
below (disjoint refinement and flattening, the raw equality test, local
representability by ordinary sequences, and the universal gluing
argument), so it is not printed as a separate route. The general theory
of associated sheaves is standard \cite{Stacks}; the point of both
sources is that here it can be made explicit.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The associated-sheaf theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Explicit sheafification on every complexity stage \src{08 Thm~8.5; 10 Thm~9.2}]
\label{fbc:thm:mix-sheafification}
The associated sheaf of $\R_M$ on $\PP_\kappa$ is
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:associated-sheaf}
 \Ssh_{M,\kappa}(F)=\Mix_M(\sigma_F).
\end{equation}
The unit sends $[f]_F$ to the single-label mixture
$\sigma_F\cdot f$. It is injective. Every completed section is locally
represented by ordinary sequences. Global sections, meaning
sections at the weakest filter $\Fr$, are exactly $\Mix_M(1)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For two sequences on support $\sigma_F$, their equality value is
$\sigma_F\wedge e([\eqset{f}{g}])$. By
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:recovery}, this equals $\sigma_F$ exactly when
$\eqset{f}{g}\in F$. Thus the unit is well-defined and injective;
it commutes with restrictions.

The functor in \eqref{fbc:eq:associated-sheaf} is a sheaf by
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:mix-sheaf} and Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:representation}.
It remains to verify its universal property, rather than merely
exhibiting a larger sheaf. Let $x$ be a mixture on $\sigma_F$.
For every nonzero partition cell $p$ of $x$, take all conditions
$\sigma_G\leq p$. These conditions together cover $\sigma_F$
by order density and the fact that the cells join to $\sigma_F$.
On each such $G$, the mixture is represented by the raw sequence
labelling $p$. Thus the unit is locally surjective.

Now let $\alpha:\R_M\to T$ be a morphism into a sheaf $T$.
On the covering conditions just constructed, apply $\alpha$ to
the local raw representatives of $x$. They agree in $T$ on all
overlaps: their equality as mixtures on any overlap is witnessed
locally by conditions contained in their sequence-equality event,
and Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:recovery} says that on these conditions their
raw reduced-power classes are equal. Separatedness of $T$ gives
equality on the whole overlap. Glue in $T$ to define the image of
$x$.

Refining the local representations does not change the glued
section, by uniqueness. The same uniqueness makes the construction
natural in $F$ and independent of the mixture chosen to represent
$x$. It extends $\alpha$, and any extension must have these local
values, so it is unique. This is the sheafification universal property.
Finally $\sigma_{\Fr}=X=1$, giving the description of globals.
\end{proof}

This is a specialized mixification construction, not a new general
sheafification method. The prior framework in \cite{PV} clarifies
why fullness of a Boolean-valued model must not be confused with
its having all mixtures. The explicit form above makes that
distinction usable for the repository's changing filter sites.

\begin{corollary}[The binary and finite-valued cases \src{08 Cor.~8.6; 10 Prop.~9.3}]
\label{fbc:cor:binary}
For $M=2$, there is a canonical Boolean-algebra identification
\[
 \Ssh_{2,\kappa}(F)\cong\C_\kappa\res\sigma_F,
 \qquad\text{in particular}\quad\Mix_2(1)\cong\C_\kappa.
\]
For a finite nonempty set $M$, sections at $F$ correspond exactly
to $M$-indexed Boolean partitions of $\sigma_F$, with zero parts
allowed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On a raw sequence $f:\om\to2$, the event for value $1$ is
$e([f^{-1}(1)])$. On a mixture it is the join of the corresponding
piecewise events. This determines the mixture: on that event it
is the constant $1$, and on its complement in $\sigma_F$ it is
$0$. Conversely every $c\leq\sigma_F$ is represented by the
mixture $c\cdot1+(\sigma_F\wedge\neg c)\cdot0$.
For a finite set, the same argument splits each sequence according
to its finitely many fibers $f^{-1}\{m\}$ and then joins the pieces
carrying each value. Finite preservation by $e$ makes the resulting
events a partition of the support. Distinct constant labels have
equality value zero, so the partition is recovered from the section $s$
as $d_m=\truth{s=\overline m}$, where $\overline m$ is the constant
sequence.
\end{proof}

In particular, the binary associated sheaf on the countably generated
site has global Boolean algebra $\RO(\om^*)$, while the full-site
binary associated sheaf has global Boolean algebra $\pow(\om^*)$.
This computes the missing subsite object rather than merely showing
that a proposed product formula cannot work.

Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:h} therefore says that $\ch$ is the first size of a
family of completed binary sections on the countably generated site
which is not locally jointly constant. Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure} says
that at most $\lambda$ many completed finite-valued sections on
$\PP_{\lambda^+}$ can always be made jointly constant on a dense cover.

\begin{example}[Infinite labels cannot be discarded \src{10 Example~9.4}]
\label{fbc:ex:infinite-labels}
For $M=\om$, the raw sequence $f(n)=n$ satisfies
$\truth{f=\overline m}=0$ for every standard $m<\om$. It cannot be a
mixture of constant labels: on every nonzero cell with constant label
$m$, its equality with that constant would have to be nonzero.
Consequently $\Mix_M(1)$ is not, in general, just a Boolean-valued
copy of the constant set $M$. The raw sequences retain the nonstandard
part of the construction.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Local simultaneous raw representatives \src{10 Prop.~9.5}]
\label{fbc:prop:unmix}
Let $\lambda$ be infinite. For any family of at most $\lambda$ elements
of $\Mix_M(1)$ at stage $\lambda^+$, there is a Boolean partition of $1$
in $\C_{\lambda^+}$ on each cell of which all members of the family are
represented simultaneously by ordinary sequences.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose one presentation for each member and apply
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure} to their at most $\lambda$ many partitions.
A cell in a common refinement selects a raw label for every member.
\end{proof}

For infinite $M$, this conclusion is weaker than simultaneous constant
labels and is the correct statement. The distinction matters in the
countable type argument of Section~\ref{fbc:sec:saturation}.

\begin{corollary}[Ultrapowers at the atomic stages \src{08 Cor.~8.7; 10 Prop.~12.1 at the full site}]
\label{fbc:cor:atomic-products}
If the equivalent conditions of Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atomicity} hold,
then
\[
 \Ssh_{M,\kappa}(F)\cong
 \prod_{\substack{U\in S_\kappa\\U\supseteq F}}M^\om/U.
\]
In particular, at the full stage,
$\Ssh_{M,\cc^+}(F)\cong\prod_{U\in\om^*,\,U\supseteq F}M^\om/U$ and
$\Mix_M(1)\cong\prod_{U\in\om^*}M^\om/U$; this recovers the product of
all ultrapowers in the repository report.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At the atom $\{U\}$, every mixture has only one nonzero Boolean
piece. Its sequence labels are identified exactly by $U$-equality,
so the value there is $M^\om/U$. Atomicity says that the atoms below
$\sigma_F$ cover it. Distinct atoms have zero overlap, so arbitrary
choices of these local sections glue uniquely. This is precisely
the displayed product. Restriction to $\sigma_F$ keeps exactly the
ultrafilters extending $F$.
\end{proof}

Source~10 proves the full-site case by the same argument (a partition
into singleton atoms assigns one raw label to each ultrafilter).
Source~08 proves it at every atomic stage. This is the product formula
already proved in the repository \cite[Theorem~9.2]{Repo}; the argument
here identifies it with the uniform mixing construction. It is not the
associated-sheaf formula on the countably generated site, whose complete
algebra is atomless rather than atomic.

\section{First-order transfer, specializations, and a sharp infinitary separation}
\label{fbc:sec:logic}

\subsection{Boolean truth on mixtures}
Let $M$ now be a nonempty, set-sized first-order structure in a
set-sized finitary language. Constants are evaluated by constant
sequences, and functions pointwise on sequences and piecewise on
mixtures. If finitely many mixtures have different partitions,
intersect those partitions to obtain a common refinement. A finite
common refinement has join $1$, by repeated distribution of finite meet
over arbitrary joins.

For a first-order formula $\varphi(\bar v)$ and a tuple of raw
sequences $\bar f$, write
\[
 V_\varphi(\bar f)=
 \{n<\om:M\models\varphi(\bar f(n))\}.
\]
The intended value on a raw tuple is $e_\kappa([V_\varphi(\bar f)])$.
For mixed tuples, Boolean logical connectives use the operations of
$\C_\kappa$, and quantifiers use joins and meets over the set of
global mixtures. Atomic equality is \eqref{fbc:eq:mix-equality}; other
atomic relations use the analogous piecewise formula. The construction
belongs to the standard framework of full Boolean-valued models and
mixing; Ulrich's treatment gives a convenient modern account of that
framework and its ordinary ultrafilter specializations
\cite[Sections~2--3]{Ulrich}. We prove the specific facts needed here.

\begin{theorem}[Finitary transfer with an attained existential value \src{08 Thm~9.1; 10 Thm~10.1}]
\label{fbc:thm:transfer}
Suppose $\bar x$ is a finite tuple of global mixtures written on a
common partition $(b_i)$ as
$\bar x=\sum_i b_i\cdot\bar f_i$. Then every finitary first-order
formula satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:los-mixing}
 \truth{\varphi(\bar x)}_\kappa
 =\bigvee_i b_i\wedge
       e_\kappa([V_\varphi(\bar f_i)]).
\end{equation}
The value is independent of all representations and satisfies the
Boolean-valued first-order semantic clauses. For every formula
$\exists v\,\psi(v,\bar x)$, there is a global mixture $y$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:maximum}
 \truth{\exists v\,\psi(v,\bar x)}_\kappa
       =\truth{\psi(y,\bar x)}_\kappa
       =\bigvee_{z\in\Mix_M(1)}\truth{\psi(z,\bar x)}_\kappa.
\end{equation}
For raw sequences, $\truth{\varphi(\bar f)}_\kappa=e_\kappa([V_\varphi(\bar f)])$.
For a tuple $\bar a$ from $M$, the value on its constant copy is $1$ or
$0$ according as $M\models\varphi(\bar a)$ or not; in particular, every
sentence true in $M$ has value $1$, and every sentence false in $M$ has
value $0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For terms and atomic formulas, the statement is built into the
piecewise definitions. Equality substitution is valid because, on
any piece where the coordinate sequences agree, the ordinary
atomic statements in $M$ agree. This proves well-definedness at the
atomic level. Refining partitions leaves the displayed join unchanged.

Suppose the formula identity has been proved for $\varphi$.
Complementing a value piecewise on a partition gives
\[
 \neg\bigvee_i(b_i\wedge a_i)
       =\bigvee_i(b_i\wedge\neg a_i).
\]
This follows by intersecting both sides with each $b_i$, which join
to $1$. Since $e_\kappa$ preserves finite Boolean operations,
ordinary negation in $M$ proves the identity for $\neg\varphi$.
The proof for conjunction is the same after common refinement;
the other finitary connectives follow.

For the existential step, suppose the identity holds for $\psi$.
For each $i$ and each $n$, choose $g_i(n)\in M$ satisfying
$\psi(g_i(n),\bar f_i(n))$ whenever any witness exists; use a fixed
default element of $M$ otherwise. This is a set-indexed choice,
permitted in $\ZFC$. Then, as literal subsets of $\om$,
\[
 V_\psi(g_i,\bar f_i)=V_{\exists v\,\psi}(\bar f_i).
\]
The mixture $y=\sum_i b_i\cdot g_i$ therefore has, by the induction
hypothesis, exactly the right-hand side of
\eqref{fbc:eq:los-mixing} for $\exists v\,\psi$.

For any other candidate mixture $z$, refine its partition with the
$b_i$. On each refined cell, ordinary truth in $M$ gives
$\psi(z(n),\bar f_i(n))\Rightarrow\exists v\,\psi(v,\bar f_i(n))$.
The induction hypothesis bounds its value by that same right-hand
side. Taking the join over all candidates proves both the
existential clause and \eqref{fbc:eq:maximum}. Universal quantification
follows by negation. This completes the induction and establishes
representation independence for all formulas.

The raw formula and the assertion about constants are immediate from
their one-cell presentations. For a sentence, the set $V_\varphi$ is
either $\om$ or $\varnothing$, independently of the partition. Its value
is therefore $1$ or $0$ as asserted.
\end{proof}

The theorem gives an exact internal, Boolean-valued first-order
model of the theory of $M$. It does \emph{not} say that the set of
global sections, equipped with the relation ``has Boolean value
$1$,'' is an ordinary classical model of every sentence of that
theory. For example, a Boolean disjunction can have value $1$
without either disjunct having value $1$. The field example in
Section~\ref{fbc:sec:rings} makes this distinction concrete.

The maximum principle here also does not prove saturation for
infinite types. It produces a witness for one existential
first-order formula. Replacing a single formula by an infinite
family introduces complete meets, which can change with the
filter stage. Countable types are treated by a different argument, from
source~10, in Section~\ref{fbc:sec:saturation}.

\begin{remark}[No illicit complete extension is used \src{10 Remark~10.2}]\label{fbc:rem:no-illicit}
The proof never commutes $e_\kappa$ with an arbitrary join in $\B$. Such
a step would be unavailable, and above $\cp$ it would conflict with
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided}. Existential truth is handled instead
by one pointwise witness on each presentation cell. This is the precise
reason fullness and transfer survive the failure of a complete
comparison map.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ordinary specializations}
\begin{proposition}[Ordinary specializations \src{10 Prop.~10.3}]
\label{fbc:prop:specialize}
Let $\cV$ be an ordinary ultrafilter on $\C_\kappa$. Quotient global
mixtures by
\[
 s\equiv_\cV t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\truth{s=t}_\kappa\in\cV.
\]
With the induced functions and relations, the quotient $M_{\kappa,\cV}$
is an ordinary structure satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:special-los}
 M_{\kappa,\cV}\models\varphi([\bar s]_\cV)
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\truth{\varphi(\bar s)}_\kappa\in\cV.
\end{equation}
The constant copy of $M$ is an elementary substructure of this quotient.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Boolean equality laws and substitution make the quotient operations
and relations well defined. Prove \eqref{fbc:eq:special-los} by
induction. Finite Boolean operations are decided by an ultrafilter. If
an existential truth value belongs to $\cV$, fullness in
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:transfer} supplies a witness with that same value,
giving a witness in the quotient. The converse follows by monotonicity
of truth under existential quantification. The claim about constants
follows from their $0$--$1$ truth values; distinct constants have
equality value zero and hence remain distinct in the quotient.
\end{proof}

The ultrafilter $\cV$ in this proposition need not be a complete point of
$\C_\kappa$ (Remark~\ref{fbc:rem:two-points}). In particular, the
proposition applies when $\kappa\leq\cu$ and the Boolean locale has no
complete points at all. It uses only Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:transfer} and
therefore holds at every stage; source~10 states it at its stages.

\subsection{An explicit infinitary formula detecting the threshold}
\begin{theorem}[A $\cp$-fold conjunction separates the stages \src{08 Thm~9.2}]
\label{fbc:thm:infinitary}
For every $\kappa>\cp$, there are a structure $M$, a tuple consisting
of one fixed old sequence $f$, and an infinitary formula
$\Psi(v)\in L_{\cp^+,\om}$ such that
\[
 \truth{\Psi(f)}_\om=0,
 \qquad
 \truth{\Psi(f)}_\kappa>0.
\]
Every finitary subformula has its old event as the Boolean value
at both stages. Among conjunctions of fixed old events whose value
changes from zero to positive, the arity $\cp$ is minimal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose an SFIP family $(A_\alpha)_{\alpha<\cp}$ with no infinite
pseudointersection. Take the underlying set of $M$ to be $\om$
and give the language unary predicates $P_\alpha$, interpreted as
$A_\alpha$. Let $f(n)=n$, and form
\[
 \Psi(v)=\bigwedge_{\alpha<\cp}P_\alpha(v).
\]
Interpret this infinitary conjunction as the complete Boolean meet
of the values of its conjuncts. The individual values are
$e_\mu([A_\alpha])$ at stage $\mu$, by
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:transfer}. Equation~\eqref{fbc:eq:meet-obstruction}
therefore gives the two claimed values, the second being
$K_{\gen{\Fr\cup\{A_\alpha:\alpha<\cp\}}}$.
The minimality assertion is Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:arity}.
\end{proof}

There is no contradiction with ordinary truth in $M$. The
intersection of all the $A_\alpha$ is finite, since an infinite
intersection would itself be a pseudointersection. Thus the
pointwise event for the whole infinitary conjunction is zero
modulo finite sets. At the larger stage its \emph{Boolean-valued}
infinitary interpretation is positive. Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:transfer}
was explicitly finitary; applying its formula to this infinitary
conjunction would be invalid.

This distinction also explains the complete-homomorphism obstruction
model-theoretically. A complete comparison preserving all old atomic
predicates would preserve their conjunction. The two values prove
that such a comparison cannot exist. We have not changed the
interpretation of a finitary predicate, and we have not proved
anything about the realization of every type of cardinality $\cp$.

\subsection{The stable range includes the whole sheaf model}
\begin{corollary}[\src{08 Cor.~9.3}]\label{fbc:cor:model-stable}
When $\kappa\leq\cp$, the unique complete isomorphism
$\C_\om\cong\C_\kappa$ fixing $\B$ transports the mixing model
at one stage to the mixing model at the other, preserving all old
sequences and all Boolean truth values for which the semantics is
defined by complete Boolean operations.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the isomorphism to every partition coefficient in a mixture,
leaving its sequence labels unchanged. The equality formula,
Boolean operations, and arbitrary joins and meets are preserved.
The inverse isomorphism gives the inverse transport. In particular,
the finitary truth formula and the explicitly defined infinitary
conjunction semantics commute with transport.
\end{proof}

This gives more than an equality of abstract subterminal algebras:
within the stable range the associated models themselves have the
same explicit mixing description. At a small filter $F$, the
support is represented in the original completion by
$\Int_\tau K_F$, as in Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:interior}.

\section{Localized realization and saturated specializations}
\label{fbc:sec:saturation}

This section is source~10's. Its results use
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:unmix}, hence Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure},
and are stated at the successor stages $\kappa=\lambda^+$. Throughout,
$\lambda$ is an infinite cardinal and all mixtures, truth values and
specializations are those of stage $\lambda^+$.

\begin{theorem}[Localized countable realization \src{10 Thm~11.1}]
\label{fbc:thm:localized}
Let $w\in\C_{\lambda^+}$, and let
\[
 \bigl(\varphi_n(x,\bar a_n)\bigr)_{n<\om}
\]
be a countable family of formulas with finite tuples of mixed parameters
$\bar a_n$ from $\Mix_M(1)$. Suppose that for every $m<\om$,
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:local-finsat}
 w\leq\truth{\exists x\,\bigwedge_{n<m}\varphi_n(x,\bar a_n)}.
\end{equation}
There is a mixed element $s$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:local-realize}
 w\leq\truth{\varphi_n(s,\bar a_n)}\qquad(n<\om).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The case $w=0$ is immediate. The countably many parameters can be
simultaneously represented by raw sequences on a Boolean partition,
by Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:unmix}. Intersect this partition with $w$ and
discard zero cells. On a resulting cell $c$, write $\bar f_n$ for the
raw tuple labeling $\bar a_n$, and define
\[
 \Sigma_m=\{k<\om:M\models
                 \exists x\,\bigwedge_{n<m}\varphi_n(x,\bar f_n(k))\}.
\]
These sets decrease with $m$, with $\Sigma_0=\om$. Equations
\eqref{fbc:eq:local-finsat} and \eqref{fbc:eq:los-mixing}, restricted to
$c$, give
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:cell-Em}
 c\leq e_{\lambda^+}([\Sigma_m])\qquad(m<\om).
\end{equation}

At coordinate $k$, let
\[
 \ell(k)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{m\leq k:k\in\Sigma_m\}\bigr).
\]
Choose $g_c(k)$ satisfying the first $\ell(k)$ formulas; such a
witness exists by the definition of $\Sigma_{\ell(k)}$. If $\ell(k)=0$,
choose any fixed member of $M$. For a fixed $n$, whenever
$k\geq n+1$ and $k\in\Sigma_{n+1}$, one has $\ell(k)\geq n+1$ and
hence $M\models\varphi_n(g_c(k),\bar f_n(k))$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:diagonal-inclusion}
 \Sigma_{n+1}\setminus\{0,\ldots,n\}
       \subseteq V_{\varphi_n}(g_c,\bar f_n).
\end{equation}
Finite sets disappear in $\B$, so \eqref{fbc:eq:cell-Em} and
\eqref{fbc:eq:diagonal-inclusion} imply
$c\leq e_{\lambda^+}([V_{\varphi_n}(g_c,\bar f_n)])$ for every $n$.

Mix the sequences $g_c$ over the partition of $w$, and use a default
label outside $w$. On every cell the desired truth values contain that
cell, so their global values contain $w$. This proves
\eqref{fbc:eq:local-realize}.
\end{proof}

For $w=1$ this is a uniform realization theorem for countable types whose
finite parts are satisfiable with Boolean value $1$. The local form is
strictly more useful for specialization, because finite satisfiability
in an ultrafilter quotient usually gives values \emph{in the ultrafilter},
not values equal to $1$.

\begin{theorem}[Every specialization is countably saturated \src{10 Thm~11.2}]
\label{fbc:thm:saturated}
For every ordinary ultrafilter $\cV$ on $\C_{\lambda^+}$, the
specialization $M_{\lambda^+,\cV}$ realizes every countable finitely
satisfiable one-variable type with parameters from the specialization.
In particular, when the language is countable, $M_{\lambda^+,\cV}$ is
$\aleph_1$-saturated.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose mixed representatives of the parameters in a countable type
$(\varphi_n(x,\bar a_n))_{n<\om}$ and put
\[
 \theta_m=\truth{\exists x\,\bigwedge_{n<m}\varphi_n(x,\bar a_n)},
 \qquad \theta_0=1.
\]
Finite satisfiability in the quotient and
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:specialize} say that $\theta_m\in\cV$ for every
$m$. These values form a decreasing sequence. Consider the Boolean
partition, after omitting zero cells,
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:failure-partition}
 \pi_m=\theta_m\wedge\neg\theta_{m+1}\quad(m<\om),
 \qquad \pi_\infty=\bigwedge_{m<\om}\theta_m.
\end{equation}
Its join is $1$: the finite differences exhaust the complement of the
infinite meet. More generally, for each $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:telescoping}
 \theta_k=\pi_\infty\vee\bigvee_{m\geq k}\pi_m.
\end{equation}
These identities hold in every complete Boolean algebra, by taking
complements of the decreasing meets.

On $\pi_m$, fullness supplies an element realizing the first $m$
formulas there. On $\pi_\infty$, Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:localized}
supplies one element realizing every formula there, since
$\pi_\infty\leq\theta_m$ for all $m$. Mix these elements along
\eqref{fbc:eq:failure-partition}, using
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:mix-well-defined}, to obtain $s$. For a fixed $n$,
the formula $\varphi_n(s,\bar a_n)$ holds on $\pi_\infty$ and on every
$\pi_m$ with $m\geq n+1$. Equation~\eqref{fbc:eq:telescoping} therefore
gives
\[
 \truth{\varphi_n(s,\bar a_n)}\geq\theta_{n+1}\in\cV.
\]
By \eqref{fbc:eq:special-los}, the class $[s]_\cV$ realizes the whole
type. In a countable language, all formulas in one variable over a
countable parameter set form a countable set, so this proves the usual
$\aleph_1$-saturation criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{mergenote}
By Corollaries~\ref{fbc:cor:countable} and~\ref{fbc:cor:model-stable},
the stage-$\om$ mixing model is the stage-$\aleph_1$ model transported
along a marked isomorphism, so Theorems~\ref{fbc:thm:localized}
and~\ref{fbc:thm:saturated} hold for $\C_\om$ as well. They are not
claimed at limit stages (Section~\ref{fbc:sec:closure-scope}).
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:saturated} answers the ``useful first target'' of
source~08's question Q6, ``a precise theorem for countable types in a
countable language, with the meaning of Boolean finite satisfiability
fixed in advance'': finite satisfiability is read as membership of the
values $\theta_m$ in $\cV$ (or, in the localized form, as
$\theta_m\geq w$). Q6 is re-scoped in Section~\ref{fbc:sec:future}.
\end{mergenote}

\subsection{Scope of the model-theoretic conclusion}
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:saturated} is proved for the specific completed
Fr\'echet models; it is not a general assertion that arbitrary
Boolean-valued saturation survives arbitrary specialization. The
localized diagonal construction and the failure-level partition
\eqref{fbc:eq:failure-partition} are the two ingredients that make the
assertion work here. Nor is any higher saturation cardinal claimed.
For an uncountable language the theorem still realizes countable types,
but that assertion alone is not the full $\aleph_1$-saturation condition
for all types over countable parameter sets.

The result also clarifies the role of $\ch$. A first failure of
uncountable local joint decisions does not obstruct the countable
refinements used in Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:localized}. All successor
stages, and $\C_\om$, remain countably distributive
(Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure}), even when their complete algebras have
different atoms and admit no event-preserving complete comparison.

\section{Global rings: regularity and the idempotent spectrum}
\label{fbc:sec:rings}

Let $\Fd$ be a field with $0\ne1$, and let
\[
 A_{\Fd,\kappa}=\Ssh_{\Fd,\kappa}(\Fr)
\]
be the ring of global sections. Ring operations are the piecewise
operations from the mixing construction.

\begin{theorem}[Regularity and Boolean idempotents \src{08 Thm~10.1}]
\label{fbc:thm:rings}
The ring $A_{\Fd,\kappa}$ is a commutative von Neumann regular ring:
for every $x$ there is $y$ with $xyx=x$. Its Boolean algebra of
idempotents is canonically $\C_\kappa$:
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:idempotents}
 \Idem(A_{\Fd,\kappa})\cong\C_\kappa.
\end{equation}
Under this identification, primitive nonzero idempotents correspond
exactly to the ultrafilters $U$ with $\chi_*(U)<\kappa$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $x=\sum_i b_i\cdot f_i$. Define the generalized inverse
sequence
\[
 g_i(n)=\begin{cases}
 0,&f_i(n)=0,\\
 f_i(n)^{-1},&f_i(n)\ne0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The mixture $y=\sum_i b_i\cdot g_i$ satisfies $xyx=x$ pointwise
on every piece, hence globally.

For $b\in\C_\kappa$, the element
$\epsilon_b=b\cdot1+(\neg b)\cdot0$ is idempotent. The map
$b\mapsto\epsilon_b$ is injective because its value-$1$ event is
exactly $b$. Products, Boolean complements, and Boolean joins of
these idempotents are respectively
\[
 \epsilon_b\epsilon_c=\epsilon_{b\wedge c},\qquad
 1-\epsilon_b=\epsilon_{\neg b},\qquad
 \epsilon_b+\epsilon_c-\epsilon_b\epsilon_c
       =\epsilon_{b\vee c}.
\]

Conversely suppose $x^2=x$. In a field, $t^2=t$ is equivalent to
$t=0\vee t=1$. By Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:transfer},
$\truth{x=0}\vee\truth{x=1}=1$. The two events are disjoint,
since $0\ne1$. Therefore $x$ agrees with $0$ on one piece and
with $1$ on the other; the equality formula shows
$x=\epsilon_{\truth{x=1}}$. This proves surjectivity.
An idempotent is primitive exactly when it is nonzero and has no
nonzero strictly smaller idempotent in the Boolean order. Hence
primitive idempotents are atoms, now classified by
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atoms}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A non-product theorem for the countable site \src{08 Cor.~10.2}]
\label{fbc:cor:not-product}
For every field $\Fd$, if $\kappa\leq\cu$, the global ring
$A_{\Fd,\kappa}$ is not isomorphic to a full direct product of
nonzero fields. In particular, the associated global ring on the
countably generated free-filter site is not a product of
ultrapower fields. At an atomic stage, by contrast,
\[
 A_{\Fd,\kappa}\cong\prod_{U\in S_\kappa}\Fd^\om/U.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A nonempty product of nonzero fields has primitive idempotents,
one for each coordinate. The ring here has none when
$\kappa\leq\cu$, by Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:rings}. The empty product
is the zero ring and is excluded because $A_{\Fd,\kappa}$ has distinct
constant sections $0,1$. The atomic-stage formula is
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:atomic-products}.
\end{proof}

This is a statement about a \emph{full direct product}; it does
not deny familiar subdirect-product representations of reduced
rings. Nor does it assert that the global ring is a field in the
atomless case. In fact $\B$ has nontrivial events, so
\eqref{fbc:eq:idempotents} supplies nontrivial idempotents at every
stage. For $\Fd=\mathbb R$, the sheaf is internally an ordered field
model with Boolean truth, while its global ring is not a single
hyperreal field. Source~10 makes the same observation (its
Example~11.3): the completed object satisfies the field axioms with
Boolean value $1$, every specialization of
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:specialize} is a field, and yet, for a
nontrivial region $b$, the mixture of $1$ on $b$ and $0$ on $\neg b$ is
an idempotent distinct from both global constants. This is not a
counterexample to transfer: being one of the two constants holds with
Boolean value $1$ as a disjunction, while neither equality separately
has value $1$.

The ring also remembers the marked-completion obstruction. Any ring
map preserves finite Boolean operations on idempotents. If a ring
comparison between the two stages were required additionally to
preserve all joins of idempotents and to fix the characteristic
sequences of subsets of $\om$, its restriction to idempotents would
be the forbidden complete Boolean map of
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided}, in either direction. Thus above $\cp$
no comparison with these combined requirements exists. Ordinary ring
homomorphisms need not preserve arbitrary idempotent joins; without that
requirement this argument gives no prohibition.

\section{The two endpoints and their exact cardinalities}
\label{fbc:sec:sizes}

This section is source~10's. By the index shift, its $S_{\aleph_0}(A)$ is
$\Mix_M(1)$ at stage $\aleph_1$, and its $S_\cc(A)$ is $\Mix_M(1)$ at the
full stage $\cc^+$.

\begin{lemma}[\src{10 Lemma~12.2}]\label{fbc:lem:sizeC}
One has $|\B|=\cc$ and $|\C_\om|=2^{\cc}$. Every antichain in
$\C_\om$ has size at most $\cc$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every equivalence class of subsets of $\om$ modulo finite difference
is countable. Since there are $\cc$ subsets, their quotient has
cardinality $\cc$. A regular open of $\B^+$ is a subset of a set of
size $\cc$, so $|\C_\om|\leq2^{\cc}$.

For the opposite inequality, identify the countable set $2^{<\om}$
of finite binary strings with $\om$. For each real $x\in2^\om$,
take the infinite set of its initial segments. Distinct branches have
only finitely many common initial segments. Their classes therefore
form an antichain of size $\cc$ in $\B^+$. In the completion, joins
of distinct subfamilies of this antichain are distinct: an antichain
member in their symmetric difference is below one join and disjoint
from the other. Thus $|\C_\om|\geq2^{\cc}$.

Finally, below each member of an antichain in $\C_\om$ choose a nonzero
element of the order-dense copy of $\B$. The chosen elements must be
distinct. This injects the antichain into a set of size $\cc$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three exact sizes \src{10 Thm~12.4}]
\label{fbc:thm:sizes}
For every set $M$ with $2\leq|M|\leq\cc$,
\begin{align}
 |\R_M(\Fr)|&=\cc,\label{fbc:eq:size-raw}\\
 |\Ssh_{M,\aleph_1}(\Fr)|&=2^{\cc},\label{fbc:eq:size-cg}\\
 |\Ssh_{M,\cc^+}(\Fr)|&=2^{2^{\cc}}.\label{fbc:eq:size-all}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are at most $|M|^{\aleph_0}\leq\cc$ raw sequences. Fixing
two distinct labels embeds binary sequences modulo finite difference
into $\R_M(\Fr)$, giving the lower bound $|\B|=\cc$ in
\eqref{fbc:eq:size-raw}.

At the countably generated stage the complete algebra is $\C_\om$
(Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:countable}). A mixed presentation uses an
antichain of size at most $\cc$, by Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:sizeC}, with at
most $\cc$ possible raw labels. There are at most
\[
 (2^{\cc})^{\cc}\cdot\cc^{\cc}=2^{\cc}
\]
such presentations, so there are at most that many quotient elements.
Conversely, mix two fixed distinct constant labels along $b,\neg b$
for every $b\in\C_\om$. Distinct $b$ give distinct sections, so the
global sections number at least $|\C_\om|=2^{\cc}$.

At the full-site stage, Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:atomic-products} gives a
product of $|\om^*|=2^{\cc}$ factors (Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:ultra-number}),
each of size at least $2$ and at most $\cc$. Consequently its size lies
between $2^{2^{\cc}}$ and
\[
 \cc^{2^{\cc}}=(2^{\aleph_0})^{2^{\cc}}=2^{2^{\cc}}.
\]
This proves \eqref{fbc:eq:size-all}.
\end{proof}

\begin{mergenote}
By Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:model-stable}, the global sections at every
stage $\kappa\leq\cp$, in particular at stage $\om$, are in bijection
with those at stage $\aleph_1$, so \eqref{fbc:eq:size-cg} gives $2^\cc$
there too.
\end{mergenote}

The increases are strict by Cantor's theorem. They do not depend on CH.
The first increase is the cost of allowing arbitrary Boolean mixing
along the atomless completion. The second is the cost of allowing
independent choices at every free ultrafilter. These are cardinal
comparisons, not a construction of complete embeddings between the two
completed truth-value algebras. Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided}
excludes the canonical complete embeddings one might otherwise expect.

\section{General Boolean algebras and intermediate comparisons}
\label{fbc:sec:general}

The representation theorem is not peculiar to $\pow(\om)/\Fin$.
Let $B$ be any nontrivial Boolean algebra. For each infinite
$\kappa$, take all proper filters of $B$ generated by fewer than
$\kappa$ elements, and put on $\Ult(B)$ the topology generated by
their sets of ultrafilter extensions. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:representation} applies verbatim. This supplies
a convenient general mechanism whose specialized thresholds can
be computed from the algebra itself.

\begin{definition}[\src{08 Def.~11.1}]\label{fbc:def:general-p}
Let $\mathfrak p(B)$ be the least size of a centered family in
$B^+$ with no nonzero common lower bound, and set
$\mathfrak p(B)=\infty$ if no such family exists. Here centered
means that every finite meet is nonzero.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[The abstract threshold mechanism \src{08 Prop.~11.2}]
\label{fbc:prop:general-p}
For the filter hierarchy of $B$, the original $B^+$ is order dense
at stage $\kappa$ exactly when every centered family of size
below $\kappa$ has a nonzero lower bound. Equivalently, this holds
when $\kappa\leq\mathfrak p(B)$, interpreting $\infty$ as larger
than every cardinal. When it fails, a centered family witnessing
the failure gives a zero-to-positive meet and obstructs every
complete homomorphism from the original completion that fixes $B$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A principal filter generated by $b>0$ extends the filter generated
by $\mathcal A$ exactly when $b$ is a lower bound for
$\mathcal A$. Thus the first assertion is the order-density
criterion. For a centered family with no positive lower bound,
the intersection of its Stone clopens is nonempty by the
ultrafilter lemma but has empty original interior. Once the
family is small enough to generate an allowed filter, that
intersection is open at the new stage. The two meet calculations
are exactly those of \eqref{fbc:eq:meet-obstruction}.
\end{proof}

For $B=\pow(\om)/\Fin$ this invariant is the usual $\cp$.
For the finite--cofinite algebra of Section~\ref{fbc:sec:toy}, it is
$\aleph_0$. For a finite Boolean algebra it is $\infty$. The
construction therefore explains both examples within one proof,
without claiming that this abstract reformulation defines an
unprecedented invariant. Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:general-p} concerns
only the forward direction; an analogue of the reverse barrier
(Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:reverse}) for $B$ would also need a density theorem
for filters with no nonzero lower bound, as source~10 points out in its
question on other ideals (R9 in Section~\ref{fbc:sec:future}).

\begin{proposition}[A criterion for comparing arbitrary stages \src{08 Prop.~11.3}]
\label{fbc:prop:intermediate}
Let $\kappa\leq\mu$ be infinite cardinals. The inclusion of
filter posets $\PP_\kappa\subseteq\PP_\mu$ is order dense
exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{fbc:eq:intermediate-criterion}
 \forall F\in\PP_\mu\ \exists G\in\PP_\kappa\quad G\supseteq F.
\end{equation}
When this holds, their completions are canonically completely
isomorphic in a way respecting every condition from
$\PP_\kappa$, and their reduced-power mixing models agree under
this isomorphism.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The displayed condition is precisely order density. Compatibility
of conditions of $\PP_\kappa$ is the same in both posets by
Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:small-extensions}; hence a dense inclusion gives
the unique common completion. A raw sequence-equality event is
represented by a condition from the finite-generation stage, so
these events are fixed. Transporting partition coefficients then
identifies the mixing constructions as in
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:model-stable}.
\end{proof}

The converse in this proposition is asserted for \emph{order density
of the inclusion}, not for the existence of an arbitrary abstract
isomorphism. A single complete algebra can have many inequivalent
presentations and many different embeddings of a common subalgebra.

\subsection{Under the continuum hypothesis}
If $\cc=\aleph_1$, the inequalities proved above give
$\cp=\ch=\cu=\cc=\aleph_1$. There are then only two different
completions at the infinite cardinal stages: the stages $\om$ and
$\aleph_1$ share the countably generated completion $\C_\om$
(Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:countable}), and every stage $\kappa\geq\aleph_2$
is the full stage $\PP_{\cc^+}$. The former has atomless algebra $\C_\om$
and $2^{\aleph_1}$ completed binary sections; the latter has the
power set of $2^{\aleph_1}$ ultrafilters and $2^{2^{\aleph_1}}$
completed binary sections. There is no complete event-preserving
comparison in either direction. This is a conditional example, not an
assumption used elsewhere. In source~10's indexing these are its two
stages $\mathcal F_{\aleph_0}$ and $\mathcal F_{\aleph_1}=\mathcal F_\cc$.

\subsection{When the characteristics separate}
If $\cp=\cc$, then all stages $\kappa\leq\cc$ yield the same completion
over $\B$, by Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:p-threshold}. The inequalities also
force $\ch=\cu=\cc$.

If $\cp<\cu$, every stage in the interval $\cp<\kappa\leq\cu$ produces
an atomless algebra for which the two-sided canonical comparison already
fails. Thus appearance of atoms cannot be the mechanism responsible for
the first failure. If $\cp<\ch$, the labeled obstruction appears before
the particular unlabeled distributivity obstruction. These are
implications under the displayed inequalities; no forcing consistency
result about separating the characteristics is needed for them, and none
is claimed.

\subsection{Why ordinary model theory does not see everything}
For a fixed $M$, all the specializations $M_{\kappa,\cV}$ satisfy the
same first-order theory as $M$, and at the successor stages, in a
countable language, they are countably saturated. Yet the Boolean
algebras before specialization have different point behavior, different
dense joins, and potentially very different global section
cardinalities. Elementary equivalence and countable saturation therefore
do not classify the pre-specialized objects.

This is not a paradox. Specialization decides Boolean events using an
ordinary ultrafilter and forgets most of the completion structure.
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided} concerns preservation of \emph{all}
joins while keeping the original events fixed, a demand far stronger
than ordinary first-order truth preservation.

\section{Proof audit and reproducible finite checks}

\subsection{Foundational and logical boundaries}
The results separate naturally into three groups. The Stone/filter
representation, the comparison theorems in both directions, the closure,
$\ch$, atom and point results, the forcing reading, the mixing
construction, transfer, specialization and saturation, the ring
consequences and the exact sizes are proved here in $\ZFC$. The
properties of the forcing model in Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:model-input}
are imported from Mill\'an; the three-stage consequence is proved from
those properties. The source report supplies the motivating problem and
its previous gluing result, but no theorem in this report depends on
trusting its unformalized proof. The set-theoretic results do not depend
on a large-cardinal hypothesis, on CH, on a reported Lean theorem, or on
a conjectural value of a cardinal characteristic. The proofs of the main
comparison statements are self-contained relative to elementary complete
Boolean algebra, and the model construction is given explicitly so that
the uses of mixing, choice, and countable refinement can be inspected
separately.

Choice is used at visible points: ultrafilter extension; selection
of bases and representatives; maximal antichain refinements;
pointwise witnesses in a general structure; independent ultrafilter
choices; and cardinal arithmetic arguments. This report does not extend
the source report's choice-free guarantees to the new theory. Some parts
require less than full choice, but an exact reverse-mathematical
classification is left as a further task (Q8, R8).

The most important negative checks of source~08 are the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{A\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.8em]
\item \textbf{The bound is strict in the definition of the site.}
The original completion persists at $\kappa=\cp$; change occurs
at $\cp^+$. Atoms first occur at $\cu^+$, not at $\cu$. (Source~10's
``at and above $\cp$'' and ``atoms at $\cu$'' are the same statements in
its non-strict indexing; see Section~\ref{fbc:sec:notation}.)
\item \textbf{A marked obstruction is not an abstract classification.}
The no-homomorphism theorems fix the embedded $\B$.
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:toy} shows why the qualifier is indispensable.
\item \textbf{An atom is not atomicity.}
The latter requires \eqref{fbc:eq:intermediate-criterion} with
ultrafilter targets, as stated in Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atomicity}.
\item \textbf{A Boolean point is not an ordinary ultrafilter.}
A locale point preserves arbitrary joins. This is why the
countable-site Booleanization is pointless.
\item \textbf{Finitary transfer is not infinitary transfer or saturation.}
The explicit $\cp$-conjunction distinguishes these notions.
\item \textbf{An internal field need not have a field of global sections.}
The idempotent algebra is a direct obstruction to that inference.
\item \textbf{Regularity of $\kappa$ is used only where stated.}
The topology and threshold theorems allow singular cardinals;
the closure proof assumes regularity, and source~10's closure
consequences are stated only at the successor stages.
\end{enumerate}
Source~10's checks against tempting invalid arguments, which its proofs
use rather than add afterwards, are these.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{B\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.8em]
\item Boolean joins of membership events are regularized unions.
\item All complete comparison maps are required to be unital.
\item The reverse obstruction uses density of the bad filters, not just
the existence of one.
\item Complete locale points are not ordinary Boolean ultrafilters.
\item Infinite-valued mixed elements require sequence labels.
\item Specialization saturation is proved using
\eqref{fbc:eq:failure-partition}, not inferred from Boolean-$1$ finite
satisfiability alone.
\item A character bound is on an ordinary filter base; all cardinal
multiplication arguments use an infinite cardinal.
\end{enumerate}
The merge adds two: the index shift of Lemma~\ref{fbc:lem:index-shift}
is applied to every imported statement, and no closure-based statement of

\subsection{A formalization path}
A practical formalization should begin with an arbitrary Boolean
algebra and postpone $\om^*$ and cardinal arithmetic. First prove
that proper filters correspond to nonempty Stone closed sets and
that finite unions of small bases preserve compatibility. Next
formalize the dense-site/regular-open representation and the
principal-filter density criterion. The pseudointersection theorem
then becomes an instantiation of a purely order-theoretic lemma.

The mixing construction can be formalized independently over an
abstract complete Boolean algebra with a Boolean-valued sequence
structure. The key finite-support proof obligations are equality
transitivity, finite common refinement, and the existential witness
lemma. Only after these are established should one connect the
locale sheaf construction and the cardinal-indexed sites.

The relative-consistency application should remain a clearly
named external hypothesis until its forcing input has actually
been formalized. A file with an admitted statement is not the same
as a checked derivation of that statement; the theorem dependency
ledger must retain that boundary. Source~10 proposes a different first
target, the two-sided barrier (question R10); both orders are recorded.

\section{Further research questions and proposed projects}
\label{fbc:sec:future}

The questions below are a proposed research program, the union of the
two sources' lists. Except where a specific published problem has been
cited, they are not claims that the literature has established them as
open, and no claimed solution is hidden in their wording. The point is
to state exact next targets and the results already available for
attacking them. Questions Q1--Q10 are source~08's; R1--R10 are
source~10's research questions~14.1--14.10, translated into this

\subsection{Source 08's questions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.},leftmargin=3em]
\item \textbf{Classify intermediate atomicity.}
Determine the consistency possibilities for
$\GE(S_\kappa,\kappa)$ when $\cu<\kappa\leq\cc$.
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atomicity} turns this into an exact extension
question; Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:early-atomicity} gives one positive
intermediate example. Is its truth determined by familiar
cardinal characteristics in any substantial range, or does it
require more detailed ultrafilter-character information?
(Parallel to R2.)

\item \textbf{Determine mixed atomic and atomless regions.}
For specified $\kappa$, analyze when
$0<a_\kappa<1$ in \eqref{fbc:eq:atomic-split}. Equivalently, small
ultrafilters exist but fail to occur above some allowed condition.
Describe the relative completion on the complement of their
regular-open support. The first step is a literature audit of
small-character ultrafilter extension failures, not the unsupported
assumption that $\cu<\kappa$ already decides the issue.
(Parallel to R3; Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:forcing} describes the
residual part as the region where forcing adds a new ultrafilter.)

\item \textbf{Compute the full stabilization relation.}
For arbitrary $\kappa\leq\mu$, decide the extension property
\eqref{fbc:eq:intermediate-criterion}. The special case
$\kappa=\om$ is settled here exactly by $\cp$. Other pairs ask
whether complicated filters can always be strengthened to filters
with smaller bases. Determine whether atomicity, once reached,
can be lost at a later stage; no monotonicity assertion follows
from the theorems proved here. (Parallel to R4.)

\item \textbf{Separate marked and unmarked isomorphism problems.}
Above $\cp$ the completions cannot be compared by a complete map
fixing all original events, in either direction. Determine when they can
nevertheless be abstractly isomorphic, and which invariants distinguish
them. Atom counts settle the two atomic stages in our model, while the
finite--cofinite example shows that marking can matter even when
atom counts coincide. Cellularities, distributivity spectra, and
relative densities are natural additional tests.
\emph{Re-scoped:} Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:abstract} (source~10) settles
the unmarked question negatively at every successor stage $\kappa>\ch$,
using partition distributivity. What remains is the interval
$\cp<\kappa\leq\ch$ (R1) and the limit stages.

\item \textbf{Analyze singular stages.}
At singular $\kappa$, compute closure and distributivity of
$\PP_\kappa$ and its completion. The union-of-bases argument
fails at a clearly identified point. One should distinguish the
poset having a particular closure property from it possessing a
dense closed presentation, and from distributivity of the complete
Boolean algebra. Source~10's closure consequences (local decisions,
the forcing reading, simultaneous raw representatives, countable
saturation) are not proved at these stages
(Section~\ref{fbc:sec:closure-scope}); deciding whether they hold there
is part of this question.

\item \textbf{Develop a genuine type-realization theory.}
Characterize the cardinals $\mu$ for which relevant $\mu$-sized
Boolean-valued types over the mixing model are realized. The maximum
principle proved here handles one existential formula, and the
$\cp$-conjunction gives a sharp fixed-parameter obstruction to
preserving infinitary truth.
\emph{Re-scoped:} the question's ``useful first target'', a precise
theorem for countable types in a countable language with the meaning of
Boolean finite satisfiability fixed in advance, is settled by source~10's
Theorems~\ref{fbc:thm:localized} and~\ref{fbc:thm:saturated} at the
successor stages and at stage $\om$. What remains is types of size at
least $\aleph_1$ (higher saturation, R7), countable types in the
Boolean-valued model itself beyond the localized form, and the limit
stages.

\item \textbf{Replace the finite ideal.}
Apply Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:general-p} to
$\pow(I)/\mathcal I$ for other ideals and index sets. Compute both
the centered-family lower-bound threshold and the character
spectrum of quotient ultrafilters. Distinguish a genuinely new
calculation for a particular ideal from merely renaming a known
ideal pseudointersection invariant. (Parallel to R9.)

\item \textbf{Remove unnecessary choice.}
Develop a point-free version directly from regular downsets of
the filter poset, avoiding an a priori set of Stone points.
Determine which comparison theorems require the Boolean prime
ideal theorem, which use dependent choice, and which require
stronger choice for representative selection. This would connect
the present $\ZFC$ theory more tightly to the source report's
choice-free gluing obstruction. (Parallel to R8.)

\item \textbf{Recover filter complexity from algebraic models.}
The idempotent algebra of $A_{\Fd,\kappa}$ is exactly
$\C_\kappa$. Determine what further information the ring retains
about the marked original sequence algebra, its elementary
ultrapower factors, and the stage parameter. Classify ring maps
that preserve complete idempotent joins separately from ordinary
ring homomorphisms, for which our obstruction is insufficient.

\item \textbf{Build a checked library with explicit assumptions.}
Implement the dependency order in Section~\ref{fbc:sec:audit},
beginning with the abstract Boolean statements. A realistic
first milestone is a kernel-checked proof of the principal-filter
density equivalence and the complete-homomorphism obstruction;
a second is the general mixing sheaf. The forcing model should
be a separately audited assumption until its proof is available.
(Parallel to R10.)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Source 10's questions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*.},leftmargin=3em]
\item \textbf{Unlabeled isomorphism below $\ch$} (source~10, 14.1).
Let $\kappa=\lambda^+$ with $\cp\leq\lambda<\ch$, that is, a successor
stage with $\cp<\kappa\leq\ch$. Can $\C_\kappa$ be abstractly
isomorphic to $\C_\om$, despite the absence of any complete comparison
preserving the original copy of $\B$? Which additional invariants,
such as density, antichain spectra, or homogeneity, decide this?
Corollaries~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided} and~\ref{fbc:cor:abstract} leave
exactly this distinction unresolved by the arguments given here. An
answer should specify whether it concerns an abstract algebra, an algebra
with its distinguished subalgebra, or the associated site; these are
different classification problems. (Parallel to Q4.)

\item \textbf{When are small-character ultrafilters dense?} (source~10,
14.2). For which infinite cardinals $\lambda<\cc$ does every
$F\in\PP_{\lambda^+}$ extend to some $U\in S_{\lambda^+}$? Can this
extension property hold strictly below $\cc$, and how does it depend on
the ultrafilter character spectrum? By Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atomicity},
this is exactly the question when $\C_{\lambda^+}$ is wholly atomic. The
existence of one small-character ultrafilter is insufficient for the
proof. We do not assume that this extension property is monotone in
$\lambda$: increasing the bound adds both possible ultrafilters and new
filters requiring extensions.
\emph{Re-scoped:} the second clause is answered positively, relative to
the imported model of Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:model-input}, by source~08's
Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:early-atomicity}: there $\PP_{\aleph_2}$
($\lambda=\aleph_1$) is wholly atomic while $\aleph_1<\cc=\aleph_2$. This
is a relative-consistency answer, not a $\ZFC$ theorem. The first and
third clauses remain open. (Parallel to Q1.)

\item \textbf{Residual invariants} (source~10, 14.3). When
$r_\kappa\ne0$, determine the density, cellularity, distributivity
spectrum, and forcing equivalence class of $\C_\kappa\res r_\kappa$. How
much information about its isomorphism type is carried by the spectrum
$\{\chi(U):U\in\om^*\}$? Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:forcing} supplies a
concrete constraint at successor stages $\kappa=\lambda^+$: the residual
forcing adds a new ultrafilter of character greater than $\lambda$ while
adding no sequences of ground-model objects of length at most
$\lambda$. It does not identify that forcing uniquely. (Parallel to Q2.)

\item \textbf{Comparisons between two bounded stages} (source~10, 14.4).
For $\cp\leq\lambda<\mu\leq\cc$, characterize complete Boolean
homomorphisms between $\C_{\lambda^+}$ and $\C_{\mu^+}$ that preserve
every event $[A]$. Is there a useful extension or approximation
criterion on filters that replaces the pseudointersection criterion?
The proof at the lowest stage works because an infinite set is exactly
a nonzero element of an order-dense base. At higher stages that base
has already lost order density, so applying the same argument without
modification would beg the question. Source~08's
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:intermediate} supplies a sufficient criterion,
order density of the inclusion, which partly addresses this question.
(Parallel to Q3.)

\item \textbf{The next distributivity failure} (source~10, 14.5). For a
nonzero atomless residual, what is the least size of a family of
Boolean partitions with no common dense refinement? At stage
$\lambda^+$, Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:closure} shows that this least size is
at least $\lambda^+$. Can that bound be sharp, or can there be
substantially more distributivity? The answer should distinguish
partition distributivity from binary decision distributivity if the
algebra has antichains too large to be coded by countably many bits. The
real-code reduction in Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:h} uses the special antichain
bound for $\C_\om$.

\item \textbf{Joint raw labels versus joint constant labels}
(source~10, 14.6). For infinite $M$, determine the least size of a
family of completed sections that cannot be simultaneously represented
by raw sequences on a dense cover. How does this differ from the
joint-constant threshold for finite-valued sections?
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:unmix} gives a lower bound at every successor
stage, and Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:h} completely answers the binary
constant-label version at the initial stage. The identity sequence in
Example~\ref{fbc:ex:infinite-labels} prevents treating these as the same
problem for infinite $M$.

\item \textbf{Higher saturation} (source~10, 14.7). Which larger types
are realized in $M_{\lambda^+,\cV}$? How do the theory of $M$, the
ordinary ultrafilter $\cV$ on $\C_{\lambda^+}$, and the partition
distributivity of $\C_{\lambda^+}$ interact in determining saturation
above $\aleph_1$? Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:saturated} settles the countable
case uniformly. Its coordinate diagonal uses the finite cutoff
$\ell(k)\leq k$ and cannot simply be stretched to uncountably many
requirements. Higher saturation should be compared carefully with the
notions for full Boolean-valued models and specializations in
\cite{Ulrich}. (Parallel to the remainder of Q6.)

\item \textbf{Choice strength} (source~10, 14.8). Which parts of the
hierarchy and its comparison barriers can be proved in $\mathsf{ZF}$,
$\mathsf{ZF}+\mathsf{DC}$, or with only the Boolean prime ideal
theorem? What is the exact choice strength of the explicit mixed sheaf
construction and of the cardinality formulas? The repository's
two-valued nongluing result is choice-free, whereas the present report
deliberately works in $\ZFC$. Maximal antichains, independent ultrafilter
choices, and set-sized witness choices are visible places where its
proofs use choice. The diagonal existence of a pseudointersection for an
explicitly enumerated base is much less demanding. These differences
should be preserved rather than obscured by a single global foundational
slogan. (Parallel to Q8. The earlier report's own question, whether the
infinite-quotient direction of its Theorem~8.5 needs dependent choice,
is not addressed by either source.)

\item \textbf{Other ideals and index sets} (source~10, 14.9). Replace
$\Fin$ by an ideal $\mathcal I$ on an index set $J$. For the quotient
$\pow(J)/\mathcal I$, identify hypotheses under which bounded-character
filter sites admit analogues of Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:criterion},
Corollary~\ref{fbc:cor:two-sided} and Theorem~\ref{fbc:thm:atoms}. What
replaces the countable diagonal realization argument when $J$ is
uncountable? The criterion proof suggests an order-theoretic version
using nonzero lower bounds in the quotient algebra, which source~08's
Proposition~\ref{fbc:prop:general-p} supplies for the forward direction.
The reverse comparison barrier also needs a density theorem for filters
with no such lower bound; that additional requirement should not be
omitted. (Parallel to Q7.)

\item \textbf{A formal proof development} (source~10, 14.10). Formalize
the complete comparison barrier and the atom classification, then the
mixed-model realization theorem, with all imported results listed
explicitly. Can the construction serve as a reusable interface between
filter semantics and complete Boolean-valued structures? A practical
order of development is: finite-modification invariance and positive
adjunction; regular cones and the old-event embedding; the general
completion criterion; the density of filters without
pseudointersections; atoms; and finally partition mixing and the
coordinate diagonal. The two-sided barrier has a relatively small
mathematical dependency graph and is a better initial formalization
target than the entire sheaf-theoretic presentation. No Lean code
implementing these steps exists in this report or in either source
package. (Parallel to Q10.)
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

Restricting the filters is not a harmless implementation choice.
For countably generated filters, the associated binary sheaf has
an atomless regular-open Boolean algebra, and associated field
models have global rings that are not full products of fields.
For all filters, the Boolean algebra is the power set of the
ultrafilter set and the global sections are products of ultrapowers.

The hierarchy between these endpoints has two exact initial
thresholds. The pseudointersection number controls when the
original Boolean events stop being order dense, and even when a
complete event-preserving comparison becomes impossible, in either
direction. The distributivity number measures the first failure of
simultaneous local binary decisions in the old completion and, at the
successor stages above it, separates the completions abstractly. The
ultrafilter number controls when atoms and geometric points first
appear. Full atomicity is governed by a separate generic-extension
property. A published model shows that atomicity can arrive before
the full site, and that a later atomic stage can still be a
different complete Boolean algebra.

The mixing calculation ties these order-theoretic facts to
first-order model theory without confusing internal truth with
ordinary truth of global sections. Transfer and, at the successor
stages, countable saturation of specializations survive the change of
truth-value algebra, while the sharp infinitary counterexample
identifies exactly what is lost at the first threshold, and the exact
sizes $\cc$, $2^\cc$, $2^{2^\cc}$ measure what mixing adds.

The report answers the bounded-character comparison, point-existence,
local binary-reading and countable specialization questions formulated
here. Bibliographical priority, intermediate algebra classification,
higher saturation, the small-character ultrafilter extension property in
$\ZFC$ terms, and the singular stages remain unestablished. The earlier
repository nongluing result is not claimed as new. These are explicit
conclusions about the repository's filter-site construction; they do not
assert a resolution of an unrelated major set-theoretic conjecture or
certify publication priority.
