# Source and open-status record

Checked: September 19, 2026.

## Specific target

Harry Altman, *Bounding finite-image sequences of length omega^k*:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03199v2

The current abstract page listed v2 (March 10, 2026) as the latest version.
The PDF is dated March 9, 2026 and has 16 pages. Example 3.15 on printed
page 10 gives the bound omega^(omega^4) for two inequivalent letters, notes
the smaller upper bound omega^(omega^3) for the chain, and leaves tightness
of the antichain bound unverified. Both the parsed text and a rendered PDF
screenshot of that page were inspected.

The manuscript addresses this precise question and develops a general
finite-alphabet, finite-level exactness theorem. It does not treat every
arbitrary-ordinal-alphabet extension raised later in Altman's paper.

## Closely related work that must not be overlooked

Alakh Dhruv Chopra and Fedor Pakhomov, *Well-quasi-orders on finite trees
and transfinite sequences*:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09830v1

The current record listed v1 (February 10, 2026). Section 4.3 and Theorem 4.26
establish the correspondence of finite leaf-labelled trees with indecomposable
finite-image sequences below omega^omega. The bounded-height normal form in
our manuscript is a self-contained presentation of this known structure,
with antichain reduction. It is not asserted to be a new classification.
Their all-height treatment is distinct from the fixed finite levels addressed
by the proposed exactness proof.

## Classical dependencies

The de Jongh--Parikh maximal-order-type calculus and the finite-word formula
are stated in Altman Section 2 and in the preliminaries of Chopra--Pakhomov.
The article cites the original classical sources and includes its own proof
of the finite-alphabet Higman formula from the standard calculus. The older
DOI landing pages were not accessible through the browser in this session;
the manuscript does not claim a full-text inspection of those originals.

## Scope of the literature check

The two recent full papers and their current arXiv version records were
consulted, and searches were made for the paper identifier and finite-alphabet
transfinite-sequence maximal-order-type results. No source consulted supplied
the protected-separator attainment theorem proved in the manuscript.

This supports treating Example 3.15 as an explicit unresolved target in the
latest source version that was found. It is not a guarantee that no unpublished
proof, differently worded result, or later unindexed source exists. Independent
priority checking remains appropriate before claiming a first publication.

No paper was submitted, no author was contacted, and no external peer review
or formal proof checking was performed.
