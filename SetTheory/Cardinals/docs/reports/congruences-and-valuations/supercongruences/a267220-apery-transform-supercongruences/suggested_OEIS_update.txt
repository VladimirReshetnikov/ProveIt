September 20, 2026

Both supercongruences of October 17, 2024 hold for every integer m.
For every odd prime p, nonzero integer m, and positive N divisible by p,
the differences u_m(N)-u_m(N/p) and v_m(N)-v_m(N/p) are divisible by
p^(2*valuation_p(N)+valuation_p(m)). For m=0 the positive-index terms
vanish. The v_m congruence modulo p^(2*valuation_p(N)) holds also for p=2.

Proof outline:
Let D=x*A'/A and define X_m=y*A(X_m)^m. If
D(X_m)/(1-m*D(X_m)) = Sum_{n>=1} b_m(n)*y^n, then all b_m(n) are integers.
Lagrange inversion gives, for n>=1,

  u_m(n)=m*b_m(n),    v_m(n)=m*b_(m-1)(n),    b_0(n)=A005259(n).

The signed sequence (-1)^(m*n)*b_m(n) satisfies second-order Gauss
congruences by the established framing-integrality principle of
Schwarz–Vologodsky–Walcher. The input second-order Gauss congruences for
the Apéry numbers follow by separating multiples of p in the binomial
sum. The attached mathematical note supplies the full coefficient proof,
including parameter divisibility and the prime 2.

The all-prime signed refinements, for p|N and m!=0, are

  (-1)^(m*N)*u_m(N) == (-1)^(m*N/p)*u_m(N/p),
  (-1)^((m-1)*N)*v_m(N) == (-1)^((m-1)*N/p)*v_m(N/p),

both modulo p^(2*valuation_p(N)+valuation_p(m)). The unsigned u_m
congruence at 2 holds for even m or valuation_2(N)>=2, but not in full
generality: u_1(2)-u_1(1)=118 is not divisible by 4.

Second order cannot uniformly be replaced by third order:
u_1(7)-u_1(1)=49*103720679, and 103720679 == 6 (mod 7).
The identity v_2(n)=2*u_1(n), n>=1, supplies the same obstruction for v.
